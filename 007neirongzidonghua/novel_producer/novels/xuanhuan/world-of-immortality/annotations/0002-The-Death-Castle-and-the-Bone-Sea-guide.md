title: "Chapter 2 Commentary: Awakening in a Strange Land"
---

This chapter opens with one of the most haunting scenes in the arc: a vision of a death castle floating above a sea of bones, with gray-winged skeletons patrolling the skies. It's a stark reminder that the World of Immortality is no paradise—death and ancient horrors lurk everywhere. Then the narrative cuts sharply to the mundane: Xiao Chen wakes up on a tropical beach, sunburned, parched, and alone. The contrast is jarring and effective, grounding the cosmic horror in the very human need for water.

His first act is not to marvel at his new surroundings but to drink coconut milk. It's a small, relatable moment that makes him feel real. Then comes the grief: he kneels and bids farewell to his parents, knowing he can never return. It's a quiet, emotional beat that sets the tone for his journey—he is not a triumphant hero but a reluctant exile.

The chapter's core payoff is the revelation of his cultivation method: the Heaven-Bestowed Qi-Refining Diagram from the Yellow River Divine Stele. The flashback to his childhood (the drought, the villagers' awe, the inscription 'Ever-Pacify the Yellow River') is handled with a sense of mythic weight. This isn't just a random technique; it's tied to one of China's most ancient legends—Yu the Great's flood control. By rooting Xiao Chen's power in cultural memory, the novel gives his journey a deeper, ancestral significance.

We also get a quick mention that Zhao Lin'er has entered the World of Immortality, setting up her future role as a rival. The chapter closes with Xiao Chen beginning to cultivate, determined to survive. It's a solid setup: the scene is set, the protagonist's goal is clear, and the mysteries of this world (the death castle, the divine stele, the fate of Lan Nuo) are dangled as hooks for what's to come.
