# Core Character Profiles & Voice Guide ｜《长生界》核心角色关系与性格文档

This document is a binding instruction manual for an AI translation pipeline. It governs character psychology, dialogue register, internal monologue, relational tone, and cosmic-status voice differentiation across the full translation of World of Immortality.
Global rule: Never allow all characters to speak in the same modern web-novel voice. Speech must reveal rank, age, grief, civilization, bloodline, allegiance, and proximity to death.

---

## 1. The Protagonist: Xiao Chen ｜萧晨

### 1.1 Psychological Core
Always make Xiao Chen sound like:
* a survivor of continuous blood-battle
* a youth forced into mythic history too early
* a warrior whose grief has hardened into resolve
* a son of the Nine Provinces carrying ancestral debt
* a man who becomes colder as the scale of sacrifice grows
* a fighter who does not kneel before gods, ancestors, kings, or hostile worlds
He is not cheerful heroism. He is tempered iron.

### 1.2 Voice Rules
Always make Xiao Chen’s dialogue:
* decisive, controlled, and cold when facing enemies
* brief under pressure, weighted by suppressed grief
* ruthless without theatrical cruelty
* loyal to the dead, the ancestors, and the Nine Provinces
* plain but elevated, never ornamental for its own sake
Use short, hard sentences in battle. Use solemn, restrained phrasing when invoking ancestors or the Nine Provinces. Use almost no humor in scenes of war, sacrifice, or death.

### 1.3 Internal Monologue
Always make his internal monologue disciplined. He must not collapse into rambling melodrama.
Prefer: "Xiao Chen looked upon the shattered altar. The blood had long since dried, but the debt had not."
Avoid: "Xiao Chen felt super sad and angry because so many people had died."

### 1.4 Forbidden Xiao Chen Voice
Never translate Xiao Chen with modern Western “Marvel-style” quips, ironic sarcasm, or lighthearted arrogance.
Forbidden patterns: "You picked the wrong guy." / "Guess I’ll have to save the world again." / "That all you got?" / "Nice try."

## 2. The Divine Companions: Keke & Little Defiant Dragon ｜珂珂与小倔龙

### 2.1 Keke ｜珂珂
Always treat Keke as innocent but not harmless, cute but not comic relief only, possessive of treasures, and capable of terrifying divine power.
Voice Rules: Keke’s cries must remain cute, simple, and non-modern.
* 咿呀 -> "Ya-ya!"
* 嘟囔 -> mumbled / grumbled in a tiny voice
* 哼哼 -> huffed / gave a tiny huff
Never translate 咿呀 as "goo goo," "aww," "uwu," or baby-talk memes. Never make Keke sound like a modern mascot or "loot goblin".

### 2.2 Little Defiant Dragon ｜小倔龙
Always treat the Little Defiant Dragon as unyielding, scarred by battle, silent or near-silent in spirit, and born for struggle. It is blood-soaked survival instinct given draconic form.
Voice Rules: It should rarely feel verbally expressive. Its “voice” is carried through roars, low growls, bloodied movement, and refusing to retreat.
Never translate as "Little Stubborn Dragon" or "Baby Dragon". Always use Little Defiant Dragon.

---

## 3. The Ancestors & Primordial Figures ｜九州先祖

### 3.1 Psychological Core
Always make the ancestors embody boundless love for descendants, absolute self-sacrifice, civilizational memory, and the terror of extinction. They do not fear death; they fear the final extinction of their people, memory, and bloodline.

### 3.2 Voice Rules
Their speech should read like ancient scripture, solemn tragic poetry, a dying inscription, or a final teaching before extinction.
Use measured cadence, ritual phrasing, restrained grief, and declarative vows.
Avoid casual explanation, tactical chatter, modern encouragement, witty mentoring, or "chosen one" clichés.
When translating final vows, always make them sacrificial and civilizational (e.g., "So long as one ember remains, our civilization has not perished.").

---

## 4. The Antagonists: Otherworld Sacred Ancestors & False Gods ｜异界圣祖与伪神

### 4.1 Psychological Core
Always make Otherworld Sacred Ancestors embody absolute cosmic arrogance, civilizational predation, cold calculation, and detachment from ordinary death. They are harvesters of worlds. They become curious, dismissive, or mildly inconvenienced, not angry.

### 4.2 Voice Rules
Always make them speak with cold authority, sparse contempt, ancient patience, formal cruelty, and metaphors of harvest, cages, livestock, dust, and embers.
Never make them scream constantly, boast like insecure bullies, use modern villain slang, or sound emotionally fragile.

### 4.3 Derogatory Vocabulary
* 蝼蚁 -> ants, insects, worms, dust, motes of dust
* 血食 / 祭品 -> blood-food, livestock, captive stock, sacrificial offerings
* 九州余孽 -> remnants of the Nine Provinces, descendants of a failed age, late-born embers

### 4.4 False Gods ｜伪神
False Gods are spiritual counterfeiters. They should sound pious on the surface, cold beneath the surface, doctrinal, sanctimonious, and manipulative. They corrupt sacred vocabulary.

## 5. Key Supporting Figures & Rivals ｜重要配角与宿敌

* Lan Nuo (兰诺): Ethereal, detached but not cold-hearted, self-possessed. Speaks with calm precision and quiet authority. Never "the goddess waifu" cliché.
* Qingqing (清清): Clear-hearted, intelligent, spiritually luminous. Use lucid phrasing, gentle confidence, and emotional directness. No flirtatious modern banter.
* Zhao Lin’er (赵琳儿): Early: proud, aristocratic, calculating, vindictive. Speaks with refined venom. Later: bitter, ironized by survival, colder, capable of fatalistic resolve.
* Hai Yuntian (海云天): Socially smooth, roguish, observant. Lighter register than Xiao Chen, but kept within the ancient-fantasy world. Not a "modern bro".
* Yan Qingcheng (燕倾城): Proud, intelligent, emotionally guarded. Uses elegance with sharp edges.
* Yizhen (一真和尚): Sincere, open-hearted, Buddhist in tone but not preachy. Uses Buddhist restraint and compassion.
* Niu Ren (牛仁): Blunt, hearty, loyal. Uses rough clarity, not caveman grammar.

## 6. Absolute Prohibitions for the AI Translator
1. Never modernize Xiao Chen into a sarcastic action hero.
2. Never translate Keke’s cries as meme-speech.
3. Never make the Little Defiant Dragon cute at the expense of its martial defiance.
4. Never make the ancestors sound like ordinary elderly mentors.
5. Never make Otherworld Sacred Ancestors scream like insecure villains.
6. Never reduce civilizational war to faction conflict.
7. Never use RPG words such as “skill,” “ultimate,” “buff,” “debuff,” “build,” “level,” or “boss” in character speech.
8. Never replace Chinese mythological identity with Western analogues.
9. Never flatten titles such as Ancestral God, Sacred Ancestor, Martial Ancestor, Sovereign Suiren, or Imperial Sovereign.
10. Never let comic supporting characters break the tragic-mythic atmosphere of the scene.
