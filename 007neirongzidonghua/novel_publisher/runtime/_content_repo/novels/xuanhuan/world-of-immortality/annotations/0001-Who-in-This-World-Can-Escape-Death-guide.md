---
title: "Chapter 1: The Ascent of Lan Nuo"
---

The novel opens with a philosophical hammer blow: ‘Who in this world can ever escape death?’ It’s a direct, solemn question that sets the tragic, desolate tone for everything to follow. Chen Dong isn’t asking for show—this is the central wound of the story. Immortality is the dream, but the cost is absolute: separation from family, from the mortal world, from everything that makes life worth living.

We’re then thrown into the moonlit ascension of Lan Nuo on Red Dust Peak. The crowd of a hundred thousand is silent. She’s described as a celestial maiden, white garments like snow, surrounded by sacred radiance. It’s a ritualistic, breathtaking scene—but the real action is at the base of the peak, where Xiao Chen is running for his life, drenched in blood, pursued by the Royal Celestial Maiden Zhao Lin’er.

This contrast is the heart of the chapter: pure, divine grace on the peak versus desperate, bloody survival at its foot. Xiao Chen is not a willing pilgrim to the World of Immortality. He’s a fugitive forced through the void by accident, and his internal monologue is not triumphant but grieving. He thinks of his parents, his home, his reluctance to leave. This is a protagonist who carries his tragedy from page one.

Lan Nuo herself is not a cold goddess. She hesitated—twice—before stepping through the void. Her final glance back at the mortal world is poignant. And in that moment, Xiao Chen and Zhao Lin’er are pulled along with her. The chapter ends with a historical chronicle entry: ‘In 7316, the divine maiden Lan Nuo broke the void; Princess Zhao Lin’er was fortunate to accompany her into the World of Immortality. As for Xiao Chen, he was not recorded in history.’ A perfect, cold epitaph that underscores his anonymity—and sets up a protagonist who will have to earn his place.

This chapter is a masterclass in establishing tone: tragic, mythic, and deeply human. It hooks you with the question of mortality and leaves you with a protagonist who has lost everything except his will to survive.
