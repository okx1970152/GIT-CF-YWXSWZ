---
title: "Chapter 2 Guide: The Death Castle and the Divine Stele"
---

Chapter 2 opens with one of the most iconic images in the entire novel: the Death Castle, an ancient, eerie fortress floating over a vast sea of bones, surrounded by gray-winged humanoid skeletons. This is a direct contrast to the tranquil tropical island where Xiao Chen wakes shortly after. The shift from deathly dread to vibrant, sun-drenched jungle sets up a core theme of the novel: the constant juxtaposition of sacred and savage, life and death.

Xiao Chen’s first actions—drinking coconut water, kneeling to bid farewell to his parents—ground him as a reluctant exile, not a triumphant hero. His grief is palpable but controlled, and he quickly turns to survival. The chapter then reveals the origin of his cultivation: a childhood encounter with the Yellow River Divine Stele during a drought. The stele bears the ancient inscription 'Ever-Pacify the Yellow River' and a mysterious diagram on its reverse—the Heaven-Bestowed Qi-Refining Diagram, which Xiao Chen has practiced for ten years.

This backstory is crucial. It ties Xiao Chen to a legendary artifact and a unique cultivation path that sets him apart from ordinary practitioners. The stele’s connection to Yu the Great and the Nine Heavens hints at a grand cosmic destiny. Xiao Chen is not just a random survivor; he carries the seed of a forgotten myth.

The chapter also mentions Lan Nuo and the legendary void-breakers, establishing the larger world of immortals and the questions that will drive Xiao Chen: Where is Lan Nuo? Do those legends truly walk this world? And most importantly, how will he survive and grow in this brutal, beautiful realm?
