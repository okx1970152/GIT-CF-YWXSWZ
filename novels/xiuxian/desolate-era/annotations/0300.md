## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a chapter that masterfully balances raw cultivation momentum with an emotional reveal. Ji Ning’s Primaltwin breaks through to Primal Perfection and achieves a combat milestone that places him on par with pinnacle Loose Immortals—all while he’s still a young Wanxiang Adept in his main body. But the real hook comes when a mysterious commandery princess summons him. The chapter starts at a blistering pace with resource upgrading and formation crafting, then pivots into a tense, personal encounter. Finally, the emotional weight lands hard: when the green-robed woman turns and their eyes meet, the chapter ends on a wire-thin cliffhanger, leaving you wondering exactly who this is and why she’s been waiting her whole life for this moment. It’s a perfect blend of progression fantasy juice and the kind of familial mystery that keeps you reading deep into the night.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning is invited by Xiyue Commandery Princess, the sole relative of Prince Yan—a recently ascended Celestial Immortal. Yu Qi explains the significance: Celestial Immortals are astronomically rare, with only one appearing every million years in the Grand Xia Dynasty.
- The chapter expands on the scarcity of Celestial Immortals, contrasting them with the countless Loose Immortals and Earth Immortals who burn through generations but almost never reach the final threshold.
- Ji Ning’s Primaltwin, having absorbed 120,000 jin of Primordial Liquid, breaks through to Primal Perfection. His divine soul (at Earth Immortal level) gives him exceptional control over the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation.
- The Primaltwin activates the third layer of the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation using over two hundred Earth-ranked flying swords, including the peak-grade Water-Parting Divine Shark Swords. The result is a condensed golden sword-light of terrifying power, enough to rival a pinnacle Loose Immortal.
- Xiyue Commandery Princess’s inner world is revealed: extreme nervousness, trembling excitement, and a whispered revelation that she considers Ji Ning her “only little brother”—setting up a deep personal connection tied to his lineage.
- Ji Ning enters the tower. The final scene is a silent eye-lock between the two, with the chapter ending on the emotional cliffhanger.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The **Heavenly Tribulation (天劫)** is a pivotal theme in this chapter. In Daoist cultivation lore, ascending from Earth Immortal or Loose Immortal to Celestial Immortal requires passing a trial sent by the Heavenly Dao itself. This is not a gentle exam—it’s a cosmological culling. The novel’s explanation that a million years may pass without a single success mirrors the real mythological severity of the Tribulation. The **Loose Immortals** mentioned are those who failed the Tribulation but survived by abandoning their physical body; they live in constant fear of periodic, escalating Tribulations that will eventually destroy them. This makes Prince Yan’s recent success a monumental event—comparable to a mortal empire suddenly gaining an immortal god-emperor in terms of power shift. The **Eight-Hundred-Earth-Sha Formation** mentioned earlier and the **Lesser Thousand Sword Formation** here both borrow from Daoist numerology (108 as a complete cycle), showing how ancient Chinese numerical symbolism is woven into the magical system of the novel.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in how Xianxia blends its two core engines: **resource progression** and **emotional payoff**. Watch how the story treats Primordial Liquid not just as currency, but as a narrative timer—Ji Ning burns through tens of thousands of jin in a single scene to push his Primaltwin to the next tier. The formation upgrade scene is also worth savoring: the text gives you concrete numbers (200+ Earth-ranked swords, nine peak-grade swords as the core) so you can *feel* the power multiplier. But the real star is the meeting with Xiyue Commandery Princess. Pay close attention to the contrast between Ji Ning’s calm, analytical exterior and the princess’s raw, trembling vulnerability. The chapter trusts you to connect the dots about her identity before the formal reveal—a sign of mature storytelling. If you love chapters where a character walks into a room and the whole world shifts under their feet, this one delivers.

## Deeper Dive: Prince Yan and the Weight of Celestial Immortality
Prince Yan is more than just a new Celestial Immortal—he’s a **narrative anchor** for understanding the Grand Xia’s power structure. In the novel’s terms, Celestial Immortals are so rare that the imperial family instantly enfeoffs anyone who succeeds, granting them one of the highest noble titles available. This is not generosity; it’s strategic necessity. A single Celestial Immortal can serve as a clan’s unmovable foundation for eras, providing protection and influence that outlasts thousands of Loose Immortals who will inevitably die to their recurring Tribulations. The mention that Prince Yan placed his entire personal guard—including two elite Primal-level azure luan divine beasts—under his daughter’s command tells you volumes about both his power and his priorities. This relationship subtext makes the imminent meeting with Ji Ning all the more loaded: if the princess is his only family, then anyone she considers a “brother” becomes, by extension, a ward of the Yan Estate itself. The political and emotional stakes are sky-high even before the door opens.
