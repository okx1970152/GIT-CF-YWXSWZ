## Chapter Overview
The team’s prize-gathering in the Treasure Hall turns into a powder keg when Xue Hongyi walks out with a freshly born Immortal-ranked treasure. Cangjiang Realperson, his old self’s greed burning bright, pushes for murder and a split — but a quiet mental coalition of Ji Ning, Yu Wei, and Nine Lotos cools him down, because killing now would hand Shaoyin Nong a perfect excuse to turn on them later. Xue Hongyi, shrewd as he is, tries to trade his way out of the danger zone by offering the treasure straight to the Young Master… only to find that when the beast is this hungry, a gift only makes him look like tastier prey. Meanwhile, the clock ticks down: the jade bed is refined, and huge news drops that two colossal demon armies are camped outside, ready to explode. This chapter is all about the brittle peace before a bigger storm.

## Key Plot Points
- Cangjiang Realperson advocates killing Xue Hongyi to seize the Immortal-ranked treasure, but Ji Ning and Yu Wei veto the idea, arguing that a premature kill would give Shaoyin Nong a plausible excuse to retaliate once the team is no longer useful.
- Nine Lotos reinforces the political angle: Shaoyin Nong won’t touch her due to her Dongyan inheritance, but he’d gladly use Xue Hongyi’s death as a righteous pretext to wipe out the rest.
- Xue Hongyi attempts to defuse the tension by proactively offering the treasure to Shaoyin Nong, framing it as a virtuous transaction rather than a bribe.
- Shaoyin Nong publicly refuses the gift for now — partly to keep up his noble image — but privately plans to collect it later once the team’s utility expires.
- Xiangliu Fang reveals the post-refinement trap: two supreme demon factions (the tidal-wave Dao-soldier legion and the Yaksha army) have been waiting outside the entire time, blocking all exits.
- The chapter ends on a tightly wound dilemma — the team has nowhere to go but out, and the enemy intends to have them.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The “Excuse” Game** — In Xianxia politics, the letter of a promise matters almost as much as the style of the giver. When a Young Master like Shaoyin Nong says “no internal fighting,” breaking that rule gives him moral cover to strike. A society that prides itself on “face” and “righteousness” means that even a tyrant prefers to have a reason. The team’s refusal to kill Xue Hongyi now isn’t mercy — it’s smart operational security.

**Immortal-ranked treasures** are so rare that even a past-life Loose Immortal like Cangjiang Realperson never held one. They can shift sect alliances and trigger massacres. The fact that Xue Hongyi survives the room with everyone present is a testament to the team’s discipline and political sense.

**The “Offering” Ritual** — Xue Hongyi’s attempt to give away the treasure isn’t real generosity; it’s a classic Xianxia survival move where a weaker party tries to turn a weapon into a shield. By offering it “voluntarily,” he hopes to defuse the threat of being killed for it. Shaoyin Nong’s refusal is equally tactical: accepting now would damage his reputation and make him look desperate.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass of tension without a single sword drawn. Watch how the danger shifts from “who gets the treasure” to “who gets out alive” in just a few pages. The real battlefield here isn’t the Treasure Hall — it’s the power dynamic between the team’s cautious pragmatism, Xue Hongyi’s desperate gambit, and Shaoyin Nong’s cold patience.

The cultural angle of “giving an excuse” — or refusing to — is central. Understanding this social code makes the characters’ restraint feel brilliant rather than cowardly. And honestly? The revelation that two full-scale demon armies have been waiting out there this entire time is a beautiful gut punch.

Get ready for the storm to break in the next chapter.

## Deeper Dive: The Fiendgod Body Vs. Magic Treasures
Xiangliu Fang’s presence highlights a recurring theme in Desolate Era: pure physical cultivation (Fiendgod Body Refining) can exceed even Immortal-ranked treasures in raw power, but it can’t block an army of ten thousand Dao-soldiers working together. The Fiendgod is a raid boss, but the demon horde is an entire server of players. This contrast — solitary might versus coordinated force — is at the heart of the coming confrontation.
