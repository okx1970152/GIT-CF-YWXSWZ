## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow cultivators! Chapter 153 marks a major crossroad for Ji Ning as he finally sets his sights on the legendary Black-White College. This isn’t just another sect visit—it’s the moment where our young genius, fresh off his world-shaking Zifu breakthrough, begins to truly understand the scale of the world he’s stepping into. From learning about the miraculous Black-White Millstone Diagram to getting a first-hand taste of the politics and grudges within the Anchan Beishan clan, this chapter is a masterclass in building anticipation. And if that weren’t enough, we get a tantalizing glimpse of ancient Fiendgod capture operations and a new rival who already looks down on our boy. Buckle up—the real game is about to begin, and the college might be the one place that can keep pace with Ji Ning’s monstrous talent.

## Key Plot Points
Beishan Baiwei finally reveals the true gem in his tour: the Black-White College, a sect so elite it only accepts the rarest of geniuses. He shares the legend of the college’s supreme treasure, the Black-White Millstone Diagram—a naturally formed, unfathomably intricate engraving that makes comprehending the Dao far more accessible. This is *the* reason the college became a powerhouse, and it immediately captures Ji Ning’s imagination, planting a flag in his heart. The two friends then head out for a closer look at the college itself.

On their way out of the Beishan manor, Baiwei spots a high-level operative, Xuan Six, reporting to his father, Beishan Blacktiger. The report is staggering: they captured an ancient Fiendgod alive, at the cost of a Primal Daoist and twelve Wanxiang Adepts. Blacktiger immediately departs by divine dragon chariot to inspect the prize—a scene that quietly reaffirms the terrifying power levels and resources of the ruling Beishan clan.

When Ji Ning and Baiwei finally reach the Black-White College’s gates, they run into Beishan Xuantang, a remote-branch member who clawed his way into the college. His success has gone to his head; he treats Ji Ning with barely concealed pity and condescension, instantly reading him as just another noble hanger-on. Baiwei warns Ji Ning privately that Xuantang is a “wolf in sheep’s clothing.” Ji Ning, however, is undeterred. Standing before the Celestial Immortal’s calligraphy on the college gate, he makes his decision: this is it. Come the twelfth month, he *will* join the Black-White College, and he will rise.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Black-White Millstone Diagram (黑白磨盘图)** is a brilliant piece of Xianxia worldbuilding. The idea of a ‘naturally formed’ diagram that perfectly illustrates the mysteries of the Dao is a classic trope—it’s the ultimate teaching tool, turning abstract comprehension into something almost tangible. The description of “countless black and white lines” where different observation methods yield different insights reinforces the vastness and multi-faceted nature of Dao comprehension. In the world of Xianxia, even the greatest cultivator can only ‘see’ a fraction of the truth in any given moment; a diagram like this is a treasure worth more than mountains of gold.

The chapter also gives a fantastic look at **how major sects manage their elite geniuses**. The Black-White College allows each formal disciple to keep up to ten retainers (随从), creating a built-in support network of lesser cultivators who handle logistics, serve as aides, and can even be promoted in rare cases. This solves the practical problem of idle geniuses—these peerless prodigies shouldn’t be wasting time on mundane chores—while also giving the sect a deeper talent pool to draw from. The contrast with a small tribe like the Ji Clan, where every Xiantian fighter pulls triple duty, is stark.

Finally, the brief mention of the **ancient Fiendgod capture** is a powerful reminder of the world’s deep history. These aren’t just random monsters; they are surviving remnants of the Primordial Fiendgod Era, beings of immense power. The fact that a single ancient Fiendgod requires the sacrifice of a Primal Daoist and twelve Wanxiang Adepts just to be captured alive sets the bar for high-end power incredibly high—and hints at the kind of threats that lie far beyond the petty tribal politics of the Yan Mountains.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is all about **shifting gears**. Ji Ning is mentally leaving the small pond of his home territory and sizing up the vast ocean of the Grand Xia. Watch how quickly he pivots to asking the right questions and making a firm decision once he sees the Millstone Diagram—this is the same ruthlessly pragmatic, goal-oriented mind that got him through the Zifu tribulation. His calm response to Beishan Xuantang’s condescension is also a clear sign of his Dao Heart; he doesn’t waste energy on anger, he just notes the challenge and moves on.

Pay close attention to the background worldbuilding in this chapter. Beishan Blacktiger’s ancient Fiendgod capture and the college’s retainer system both paint a picture of a world that is *organized* on a scale Ji Ning hasn’t encountered before. The Black-White College doesn’t just teach; it hoards resources (the diagram), cultivates an identity (only 100% geniuses), and operates as a self-sustaining institution. For Ji Ning, this is the perfect environment to challenge himself and shed his small-tribe mindset. He’s right—this is where he shall rise.
