## Chapter Overview

This chapter is all about the *planning phase*—the quiet, methodical storm before the action. Ji Ning has just received his trial mission from Mount Innerheart: recruit a disciple radiating a hundred-zhang of golden karmic merit AND kill ten Earth Immortal or Loose Immortal-level great demons whose karmic sin blazes with a hundred-zhang of bloody light. It's a tall order that calls for both a saint and a slaughterer. But instead of rushing off, Ning does what he does best: he goes back to his chamber, sits on his meditation jade bed, and *strategizes*. He ruthlessly triages his options from the Three Realms Palace, locking in the Eight-Nine Arcane Art for its "no-cost indestructibility" and the Houyi Archery for its terrifying long-range kill power—while wistfully eyeing the Candle Dragon's Eye as a bonus. And then, because he can't fight cosmic-level demons with a Primal-stage cultivation, he dumps half a million jin of Primordial Liquid into his Primaltwin to force a late-stage Earth Immortal breakthrough. There are no flashy fights here, just the quiet hum of a cultivator optimizing his build before the carnage begins. It's the Xianxia equivalent of reloading your weapon and checking your ammo before the boss fight.

## Key Plot Points

- **Ji Ning Receives His Trial**: The mission is officially explained: recruit a disciple with a hundred-zhang golden karmic aura (nearly impossible to find, as such beings are already snatched up by major sects), and kill ten Earth Immortal/Loose Immortals with a hundred-zhang bloody karma aura (insanely dangerous, as such demons are the toughest of the tough).
- **The Dao of Specialization**: Back in his quiet chamber, Ning reflects on the flood of techniques from the Three Realms Palace. He adopts a "less is more" philosophy, discarding dozens of shiny options to focus on three core pillars. His reasoning is cold and tactical: the Starseizing Hand for close combat, the Houyi Archery for ranged supremacy, and the **Eight-Nine Arcane Art** for no-cost, passive defense. He even decides the Candle Dragon's Eye might be a bonus request—if Master allows.
- **The Primaltwin's Big Break**: With a battle against sin-saturated immortal demons looming, Ning forces his second body to break through. He dumps 500,000+ jin of Primordial Liquid into his Primaltwin, catalyzing the iconic **"Planting the Golden Lotus in Flames"** process that marks the transition from Primal to Earth Immortal. He stops at late-stage, not peak, to avoid triggering an untimely Heavenly Tribulation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The "Perfect Build" Philosophy**: One of the most enduring tropes in Xianxia is the "build optimization" phase. A cultivator doesn't just collect techniques; they must *prune* them. Ning's ruthless discarding of the *Vairocana Corpse* and *Buddha's Palm Within the Palm* mirrors a core Daoist principle: specialization leads to transcendence. Tripping over too many paths leads to stagnation. This is why top-tier cultivators often have only three to five core divine abilities rather than a hundred gimmicks. Ning's selection of the Eight-Nine Arcane Art + Starseizing Hand + Houyi Archery creates a perfect triangle of Defense, Close Combat, and Long Range—a trinity of strategic invincibility.

**Planting the Golden Lotus in Flames (火中种金莲)**: This is a brilliant visual metaphor derived from Daoist internal alchemy. The Tortoise-Serpent Primal represents the coalesced Yin and Yang of the cultivator's soul and energy. The "Primordial Fire" (元火) is the internal heat of the True Yuan. The "Golden Lotus" is the seed of the Earth Immortal. The process is described with agricultural and botanical precision: the tortoise-shell is the soil, the flame is the sunlight, and the lotus grows from the energy essence. It signifies the birth of an immortal foundation from the ashes of the mortal Primal, and the lotus itself becomes the new vessel for the cultivator's soul—making it far purer and more resilient.

**The Burden of Karmic Merit and Sin**: The trial's difficulty isn't arbitrary. A disciple with a hundred-zhang golden aura is valued by major sects not for their combat power, but because they "stabilize fate" (镇压气运). In the metaphysics of Xianxia, such beings are walking good luck charms. Conversely, a demon with a hundred-zhang bloody light is a player who has deliberately defied the Three Calamities and Nine Tribulations—they are survivors, experts who have mastered their own evil and are incredibly dangerous precisely because they didn't die from the karmic backlash.

### Planting the Golden Lotus in Flames (火中种金莲)

A key Daoist alchemical metaphor in Xianxia for breaking through to the Earth Immortal stage. The Tortoise-Serpent Primal (Yin-Yang coalescence) acts as the soil, the Primordial Fire is the sunlight, and a Golden Lotus sprouts to become the new, more soul-vessel.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *character preparation* before a major story arc. If you're used to Western action fantasies where the hero just picks up a new sword and goes, this slower, more deliberate beat might feel like a pause. But in the rhythm of Xianxia, this is where the real tension is built. Ning's meditation isn't laziness—it's the quiet satisfaction of a plan being forged. Pay close attention to his reasoning for choosing the Eight-Nine Arcane Art: a passive, no-cost body refinement that makes him as tough as a treasure. This is a long-term investment that will pay off in every single battle from now on. Also, note the *weight* of his resources: half a million jin of Primordial Liquid just to push his second body to late-stage Earth Immortal. This is a reminder that in this world, wealth is power, and Ning is burning through his prize money at a terrifying rate. Get ready—the next phase is going to be bloody.
