## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers a masterclass in political Xianxia, shifting from bloody revenge to the cold calculus of noble intrigue. We witness the first real consequence of Ji Ning’s actions: the Mystic Army, an elite cultivator legion of the Northmont clan, is closing in. Beishan Heihu, a seasoned political operator, lays out the brutal arithmetic of choice for his son. Will Beishan Baiwei abandon his friend to save himself, or will he, a direct descendant of the Marquis of Anchan, take the blame for a crime he didn’t commit? The answer reveals the soul of a true friend and sets up a debt that resonates far beyond this chapter. Meanwhile, Ji Ning, oblivious to the storm he’s caused, arrives at the territory of his next target, Shui Yi, and prepares to act with cold, surgical precision.

## Key Plot Points

- The Mystic Army’s intelligence branch has traced the leak back to Beishan Heihu’s estate after investigating the Crippled Moon incident. The danger is real and immediate.
- Beishan Heihu presents two stark choices: abandon Ji Ning, or take the blame himself. He frames the risk in pure power-calculation terms—the Mystic Army is an internal force of the Northmont clan; a direct descendant like Beishan Baiwei can survive the heat, while any outsider would be crushed.
- Beishan Baiwei chooses to shoulder the blame without hesitation, affirming his belief in genuine loyalty over cold strategy. His father approves of the decision but adds a layer of pragmatic advice: make sure Ji Ning *knows* about the sacrifice.
- Beishan Baiwei internally rejects his father’s worldview, asserting his own identity—he is not Beishan Heihu. He will be sincere without scheming.
- The scene cuts to Ji Ning and Mu Northson arriving at the territory of the Shui clan, a weak tribe centered around a self-built city. Ji Ning plans to lure Shui Yi out by creating a crisis without mass slaughter, demonstrating his growing strategic patience and his commitment to his Dao-heart.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“Retired” Cultivator Legions (退役修仙者军队)**: This is a fascinating Xianxia worldbuilding detail. “Retirement” in the immortal world is a rare privilege—most cultivators die in battle or in tribulation. A retired member of a legion is an old brother, a living monument to past campaigns. Their murder is an attack on the legion’s collective honor and memory, triggering an unspoken but absolute code of vengeance that even princes must respect. This is why Beishan Heihu treats the matter with such gravity; he knows the emotional and political weight of the unit’s “brother bond.”

**The “Accidental Discovery” Maneuver (无意中发现真相)**: This is a classic strategy in Chinese political fiction, often called the “gift that reveals itself.” By having Beishan Baiwei’s sacrifice discovered “by accident,” Ji Ning’s gratitude is both pure and overwhelming—he feels *indebtedness*, not obligation. It is a higher form of social engineering, one that binds a talented subordinate with invisible chains of genuine loyalty. Beishan Heihu understands this perfectly; his son finds it distasteful but cannot deny its effectiveness.

**Karmic Caution (不牵连因果)**: Ji Ning’s decision to *not* slaughter the Shui clan indiscriminately is not mere morality. In Xianxia, killing innocents generates karmic sin (业力). While Ji Ning is powerful, he knows that a Dao-heart stained by indiscriminate murder becomes brittle. His refusal to harm the innocent is a form of spiritual investment, preserving the clarity required for his future Dao breakthroughs. This is a hallmark of a mature cultivator.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a palate cleanser and a setup. It steps back from the raw violence of the previous assassinations to examine their consequences in the political theater of the Grand Xia. Watch closely how the Northmont clan’s internal dynamics work: the Mystic Army is a tool of the Marquis, but it has its own institutional pride. Beishan Heihu is not a villain for suggesting strategy; he is a father trying to teach his son survival. The key character moment is Beishan Baiwei’s quiet rebellion against his father’s cynicism—a declaration that he will be a different kind of lord. Meanwhile, Ji Ning’s methodology against the Shui clan shows his growth: he is learning to be a scalpel, not a hammer. The stage is set for the final piece of his revenge plan, but a new, invisible debt has just been written in blood and honor.
