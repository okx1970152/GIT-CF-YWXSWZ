## Chapter Overview

Alright, buckle up, fellow Daoists. The quiet has ended. Ji Ning isn't *planning* revenge anymore—he’s *going* to take it. This chapter is the calm before the absolute storm, a masterclass in controlled fury and strategic resolve. We see Ji Ning shut down his cousin Yuchi Xiyue’s suicidal desire for mass slaughter, reveal the terrifying scale of his enemy (over a million cultivators in one lair alone!), and finally, gather his small, loyal war party at the doorstep of one of the Grand Xia’s ten most ancient clans. The emotional beats hit hard, and the tension is palpable. This isn't a flight; it's a deliberate, high-stakes invasion.

## Key Plot Points

*   **Ji Ning’s Determination:** Ji Ning informs his cousin, Yuchi Xiyue, of his plan to attack the Shaoyin clan’s lair. He exudes absolute confidence, explaining that with his current power (top-tier Celestial Immortal level and the invincible defense of the [Eight-Nine Arcane Art]), the four Celestial Immortals who once ambushed him are no longer a real threat.
*   **Yuchi Xiyue’s Vengeful Plea:** Overcome by decades of hatred, Yuchi Xiyue begs to join the attack, specifically asking to lead an army to slaughter the Shaoyin clan’s *mortals* in retribution for the Yuchi clan’s extinction. Ji Ning firmly refuses, explaining that the karmic sin and hellfire from such mass slaughter would instantly kill her at her Primal Daoist level.
*   **The War Party Assembles:** Back at Yan Mountain, Ji Ning assembles his core team. He allows Little Qing (the Skygreen Serpent) to join after she demonstrates her Primaltwin is already an Earth Immortal and can stay behind to guard the mountain. Mu Northson is ready, his soul scarred but his resolve hardened.
*   **The Scale of the Enemy:** The chapter terrifyingly establishes the might of the Shaoyin clan. They control three entire commanderies, their ancestral lair has over a *million* cultivators and a population in the *billions*, and it is layered with formations that would spell certain death for any ordinary Celestial Immortal. Only Ji Ning’s near-indestructible body can even attempt a frontal assault.
*   **The Final Moment of Peace:** The chapter ends with a poignant scene of Ji Ning sitting alone on a rooftop, drinking under the moon and silently vowing to his parents that he will make the Shaoyin clan pay. The next morning, the small group—two youths, a white hound, and a green serpent—appears in the sky over the Shaoyin clan’s lair, the invasion about to begin.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **Karmic Sin and the Hierarchy of Retribution:** Yuchi Xiyue’s desire to slaughter mortals is a perfect illustration of the Xianxia karma system. The text explicitly breaks down the hierarchy: slight sin creates turbid miasma; greater sin manifests as a bloody light (which characters like the Eastern Flow kings had); and the ultimate penalty is **Karmic Hellfire**. This isn't just a moral punishment; it's a physical, metaphysical flame that burns from the soul outwards. A Primal Daoist would be instantly annihilated by it, which is why Ji Ning forbids his cousin from going, even though her rage is just. This reinforces the cosmic law that the strong must not casually exterminate the weak, or face the universe’s own judgment.
*   **The Eight-Nine Arcane Art as a Siege Engine:** Ji Ning’s reliance on his fifth-stage [Eight-Nine Arcane Art] (八九玄功) is key here. This divine ability doesn't just make him tough; it makes him a one-man siege weapon. A lair with a million cultivators and countless formations would kill any normal Celestial Immortal through sheer attrition. The only reason Ji Ning can even *contemplate* a frontal assault is because his body is virtually indestructible. This showcases a unique Xianxia trope: the "invincible tank" whose special ability allows him to ignore the rules of conventional warfare.
*   **The Heir’s Responsibility:** This chapter is a masterful study in the burden of leadership. Ji Ning must be the cold strategist, denying his beloved cousin’s plea for personal vengeance to save her life. He must be the pillar for his traumatized junior brother. He must be the commander for his spirit-beasts. The quiet moment on the rooftop isn't just reflection; it's a final, solitary reaffirmation of his Dao-heart before he shoulders the weight of an entire clan’s retribution.

### Primaltwin

A second body cultivated by splitting one's soul, allowing a cultivator to be in two places at once. Little Qing uses hers to guard Yan Mountain while joining the attack.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for one of the most anticipated arcs of the novel so far! This chapter is the perfect setup. It manages to be incredibly tense while also being a showcase for just how far Ji Ning has come. We see him acting not as a hot-headed youth, but as a mature, terrifyingly powerful clan leader. The contrast between the overwhelming, galactic-scale power of the Shaoyin clan and Ji Ning’s tiny, loyal war party is fantastic. And that final visual—the four of them appearing in the sky—is pure epic fantasy gold. Enjoy the calm before the storm, because once they descend, all hell is going to break loose.
