## Chapter Overview

The Evil Dragon River rages—but Ji Ning’s patience is about to snap. This chapter is a textbook Xianxia negotiation class: Ji Ning tries to buy an incredibly talented young girl as his disciple, the Qi Kingdom prince stalls and lies, and then—right when the political standoff hits a deadlock—a squad of Firewing Guards crashes the party. It’s a “two birds, one stone” moment: the attackers force the prince’s true hand, and Ji Ning gets to show exactly why you don’t bluff a perfect Primal Daoist. Buckle up for a short, brutal demonstration of absolute power.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning interrogates Qi Xiaoyu, confirming she has great karmic merit, excellent aptitude, and a pure heart. He decides to take her as his disciple.
- The prince (Qi Rufeng) and his two sisters refuse via telepathy; they view Xiaoyu as a priceless fortune-stabilizing pawn and won’t give her up easily.
- Ji Ning escalates his offer: first ten Heaven-ranked top-grade flying swords, then a hundred. The royal siblings are tempted but still refuse.
- The prince lies, claiming only the clan chieftain can approve the transfer (he *is* the chieftain). Ji Ning sees through the lie and challenges him to a Heavenly Dao Oath.
- Before the standoff can be resolved, a twelve-man Firewing Guard squad arrives—the same hunters that have been chasing the Qi remnants for decades.
- The Firewing Guards form their signature Dao-Soldier formation, manifesting a massive Firewing Insect, and attack the ship.
- Ji Ning steps up; the insect charges. With a single burst of his aura, Ji Ning freezes the river, the wind, the guards, and the royal family alike in an instant—absolute suppression.
- The chapter ends with Ji Ning’s cold verdict: “You asked for death.”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Evil Dragon River as a “Forbidden Zone.”**  
The Evil Dragon River follows a classic Xianxia trope: a legendary cursed zone where flight techniques and Five Elements escape arts are completely suppressed. This geographical equalizer forces everyone—from a fleeing princess to a rampaging Firewing Insect—to fight or flee on the river’s terms. It creates a tense, grounded arena where raw power and strategy matter more than mobility.

**The Dao-Soldier Formation: Firewing Insect.**  
The Firewing Guards use a military-standard Dao-Soldier formation: twelve armored cultivators link their Yuan power into a single beast-shaped phantom. Here, they form a massive, venomous Firewing Insect (part centipede, part moth). When the formation is complete, even a perfect Primal Daoist would struggle to break it. Ji Ning’s effortless suppression of the entire formation—including the Primal-level lead guard—signals that he is operating on a completely different tier of power.

**Karmic Merit and the Value of a Disciple.**  
A cultivator with great karmic merit is rare and precious. In Xianxia cosmology, such individuals are considered good-luck charms for any clan: they stabilize fortune, attract blessings, and are often protected by the Heavenly Dao itself. This is why the Qi royal clan hoards Qi Xiaoyu despite their desperate circumstances—and why Ji Ning is willing to pay a fortune in flying swords. The “price” of a disciple is not just about power; it’s about cosmic alignment.

**Heavenly Dao Oaths as Social Contracts.**  
In Xianxia, swearing a Heavenly Dao Oath is not a casual gesture; it is spiritually binding. Breaking it causes instant death. Ji Ning’s demand that the prince swear such an oath reveals his tactical patience: he didn’t want to fight them, but he was fully prepared to call their bluff through the ultimate Xianxia truth-teller.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in “soft power” leading to “hard power.” Watch how Ji Ning handles the negotiation: he starts by offering treasure, escalates with patience, and only turns cold when the other side lies. Then, when the Firewing Guards attack, he doesn’t fight them—he *negates* them with a single, silent display of overwhelming force. This is the mark of a cultivator who has long since outgrown his foes. Also, pay attention to the prince’s panic—it tells us that these three royals are not bad people, just desperate survivors holding onto their last asset. The real question for the next chapter: will Ji Ning’s absolutely terrifying power convince them to let go?
