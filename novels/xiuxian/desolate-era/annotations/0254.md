## Chapter Overview
We interrupt our regularly scheduled lake-side peace for a royal visit that throws Serpentwing Lake’s social temperature from zero to boiling in seconds. Ji Ning has just weathered an ideological storm with Nine Lotos over status and servant treatment (ouch), when an ancient warship with the ‘Shao Yan’ banner—the clan with THREE marquises—docks at his doorstep. Shao Yan Nong, the heir to one of the Grand Xia Dynasty’s top-ten clans, has come calling, and he’s brought Xue Hongyi (remember that guy?) along for the ride. The chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia power-dynamics theater: the clash of domestic philosophy, the weight of ancient clan names, and the subtle art of being “recruited” by someone who can flatten your entire prefecture without blinking.

## Key Plot Points
- **Domestic Clash**: Before anyone flies in, Ji Ning and Nine Lotos argue—hard—about how he treats servants. Nine Lotos argues for strict hierarchy; Ji Ning drops a truth bomb that strength, not etiquette, defines a cultivator’s worth in the Three Realms.
- **The Arrival**: A warship bearing the ‘Shao Yan’ clan insignia appears over Serpentwing Lake. Cangjiang Realperson and Nine Lotos immediately go pale, explaining to Ji Ning and Mu Northson that this clan is among the Grand Xia’s top ten, with three marquis-level fiefs and roots stretching back to the Primordial Fiendgod Era.
- **The Meeting**: Shao Yan Nong steps out with his retinue, including Xue Hongyi (Snowdragon Mountain) and Buyou Realperson. He immediately recognizes Nine Lotos as the Dong Yan clan heir.
- **The Recruitment Pitch**: After pleasantries (and obvious glances at Yu Wei’s beauty), Shao Yan Nong reveals he has a job for them—someone of his station came all the way here, which means the task is no small favor.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The Great Clan Pecking Order**: The Shao Yan clan being “top ten in the Grand Xia” with three separate commandery fiefs means their reach is *absurdly* wide. Each fief is governed by a marquis (侯), a title that grants military and civil authority over territory that can dwarf the entire Anchan Commandery. Having three such marquises in one bloodline is a statement of raw, unapologetic supremacy.
- **“Four Young Masters” (四公子)**: This title is Xianxia shorthand for “the most politically connected and dangerous scions of the capital.” These are not idle rich kids; they are being groomed to inherit and wield real power over millions. When Shao Yan Nong shows up, Ji Ning is looking at the future leader of a monster clan.
- **Domestic vs. Cultivator Ethics**: The argument between Ji Ning and Nine Lotos is more than marital tension—it’s a clash between a “merit-through-strength” worldview (Ji Ning: “True Immortals get worshipped no matter their manners”) and the “born-to-rule” aristocratic ideology of ancient clans (Nine Lotos: “Without hierarchy, you have chaos”). This tension is a recurring theme in Xianxia, where protagonists often have to decide whether to adopt the system or smash it.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the power dynamics**: Shao Yan Nong doesn’t just arrive—he *descends*. Xue Hongyi’s silent rage, Nine Lotos’s instant recognition, and Cangjiang’s freaked-out transmission all paint a picture of someone who can make or break lives without raising a hand.
- **Ji Ning’s philosophy drop**: His rant about strength being the only real currency in the Three Realms is *the* core thesis of his Dao-heart. He doesn’t care about class theater if he can eventually punch through it. Keep this line in mind—it’s his mission statement for the rest of the arc.
- **The recruitment trap**: A top-tier heir coming to personally recruit Wanxiang Adepts means the job is dangerous enough that even his clan’s resources can’t solve it internally. Expect a classic Xianxia “high risk, high reward” mission that will test everyone’s limits.

## Reference: Grand Xia Clan Rankings
The Shao Yan clan sits in the top ten of the Grand Xia Dynasty’s most powerful bloodlines—a club whose members collectively hold the vast majority of the realm’s immortal military, economic, and political power. For context, the Ji clan and even the Dong Yan clan are small fish in this pond. An heir to the Shao Yan clan arriving at Ji Ning’s modest lake-home is like an imperial prince knocking on a rural knight’s door.
