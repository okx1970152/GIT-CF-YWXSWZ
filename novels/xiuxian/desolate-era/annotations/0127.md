## Chapter Overview

Get your tissues ready, fellow Daoists. Chapter 127 is a brutal, emotional gut-punch that proves how devastating a Xianxia battle can be even when both sides are standing still. The Ji Clan, their backs against the wall, resorts to its most desperate gambit: forcing its own tamed spirit-beasts to self-detonate in a bid to slow down the horrific genius Nong Zidao and his formation-breaking. We watch as a proud dragon and a loyal crane are sacrificed for mere seconds of delay, setting the stage for Ji Yichuan’s inevitable, final stand. The chapter’s true masterpiece, however, is the agonizing farewell between Ji Ning and his father. It forces the young genius to make the hardest choice any cultivator can face: not to fight and die with honor, but to run and live with the burden of an entire clan’s hopes.

## Key Plot Points

1. **The Desperate Gambit**: Ji Jiuhuo initiates Plan C, sending the newly-tamed Dilong to self-detonate as a distraction. Xu Li’s Wanxiang-level power easily tanks the blast, buying Nong Zidao only a brief respite.
2. **The Betrayal of Memory**: The Immortal Crane, Nong Zidao’s former mount, is sent next. Her deep bond with her former master causes her to self-detonate *before* reaching the enemy, a sacrifice of love that wounds Nong Zidao’s heart far more than any explosion could.
3. **The Father’s Demand**: As the delay tactics run out, Ji Yichuan doesn’t prepare for a final charge. Instead, he turns to his son and issues a brutal, non-negotiable command: “Go.”
4. **The Hardest Choice**: Ji Ning is torn between his desire for a glorious, suicidal fight and the stark, lonely responsibility of survival. He realizes that his death would waste everyone’s sacrifice.
5. **The Oath**: Trapped by the sealing formation, Ji Ning fails to break it. In a moment of pure, cold fury, he swears a blood oath to annihilate Snowdragon Mountain, Xu Li, and Nong Zidao before activating the Ten-Thousand-Mile Trace-Away Talisman and teleporting away.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Weight of a Self-Detonation (Zifu Lake Self-Detonation)**: This chapter demonstrates a core Xianxia trope—the self-detonation of a cultivator’s energy core (Zifu Lake). It’s not a generic explosion; it is the calculated detonation of a lifetime’s worth of accumulated, compressed energy. A divine beast like Dilong, whose Zifu Lake rivals a Wanxiang Adept’s in sheer volume, becomes a living bomb of terrifying potential. This act is always a point of no return—the soul itself is obliterated (unless strong enough to remain as a ghost), making it the ultimate expression of despair and loyalty.

**The Tragedy of Tamed Beasts**: The Dilong and the Immortal Crane highlight the grim reality of ‘spirit beast slavery’ in this world. Forced to submit after their master’s death, they become tools with no free will. Their only agency is in the *manner* of their death. The Crane’s choice to self-detonate far from Nong Zidao is a tragic act of love—a final, heartbreaking show of loyalty to a master who set her free, performed just to avoid harming his heart. This is a classic, heart-wrenching Xianxia trope: the loyal beast who chooses personal destruction over betraying a beloved bond.

### Delegated Awe

A literary device in Xianxia where the power of an off-screen character is conveyed through the awed reactions of on-screen characters who have witnessed their legacy, without direct demonstration.

### Severed Immortal Path

A permanent, irreversible ceiling on a cultivator's growth, often due to a crippled spiritual foundation, making them unable to advance further.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in building tension through emotional sacrifice. The battle is less about physical damage and more about the psychological warfare of *memory*. Pay close attention to how Xu Li, a cold veteran, remains unfazed, while Nong Zidao, the genius, is clearly shaken. The main event, however, is the farewell scene. The sheer, raw power of Ji Ning’s final oath, “I will erase every last one of you,” isn’t a boast—it’s a grim promise forged from the guilt of survival. This moment is the crucible that forges Ji Ning’s future Dao Heart. Compare his earlier, proud confidence against Tongyu with this newfound, cold resolve. The chapter ends not with a victory, but with the birth of a true cultivator’s vengeance.

### The Art of a Xianxia Exit
Ji Ning’s escape is not cowardice; it is the ultimate act of filial piety. In the stark, survival-of-the-fittest logic of this world, a clan’s only true immortality is its bloodline. By forcing Ji Ning to leave, Ji Yichuan is preserving the future of the Ji Clan. He is choosing to let his son’s potential—a potential that defies the laws of heaven—outlive his own death. This is a common, yet always powerful, Xianxia theme: the transfer of destiny from the old generation to the new, sealed by a promise of future vengeance.
