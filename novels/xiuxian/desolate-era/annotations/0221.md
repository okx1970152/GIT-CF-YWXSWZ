## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is an absolute banger. Ji Ning’s long-simmering revenge plot against Yu Tong finally comes to a head, but not without complications. The Crippled Moon Realperson, Yu Tong’s master, is a wily old Wanxiang Adept at perfection, and he’s not about to let some mysterious Yinglong Guard haul his disciple away without a fight. What follows is a masterclass in Xianxia tension: polite words masking murderous intent, a dangerous political game involving a Beishan clan token, and a breathtakingly brutal assassination that proves Ji Ning’s patience has limits—and his sword has none.

## Key Plot Points

Ji Ning and Mu Northson, posing as Yinglong Guard officers, confront Yu Tong at his master’s estate on Crippled Moon Peak. Yue Wei, Yu Tong’s lover and the Crippled Moon Realperson’s daughter, panics and crushes her signal talisman, summoning the old monster himself.

The Crippled Moon Realperson arrives swiftly and immediately clocks that these aren’t ordinary cultivators. He probes their identities while shielding Yu Tong with his presence. Ji Ning tries to get him to leave by invoking his Yinglong Guard authority, but Yu Tong, sharp as a tack, refuses to let his master go. He even offers to swear a Heavenly Oath, a near-ironclad guarantee in the Xianxia world, to keep the conversation happening in front of his protector.

Things escalate when Mu Northson tries to bully the old man into backing down. The Crippled Moon Realperson responds by pulling out a Beishan Blood Token—a symbol of deep connection with the Anchan Beishan clan. This is a massive power move. Ji Ning knows that even he can’t casually offend the Marquis of Anchan’s clan.

Just when it looks like Ji Ning is forced to retreat, he turns his back… and unleashes a brutal, perfectly timed ambush. Using the Soul-Shattering Art to stun the old man, then combining the Starseizing Hand, Windwing Evasion, and the ‘Thorns Everywhere’ stance of the Three-Foot Sword, he bisects the Wanxiang Adept in a single, devastating strike before his signature knife formation can even form. The chapter ends with Ji Ning staring down a horrified Yu Tong, making it clear that no token, no clan, and no power in the world will stop his revenge.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Beishan Blood Token**: In the Grand Xia Dynasty’s political landscape, a clan token isn’t just a calling card—it’s a loaded weapon. The Beishan clan of Anchan is one of the ruling families in the commandery, and their tokens are a form of ‘delegated awe.’ The Crippled Moon Realperson didn’t earn this token by being a nice guy; he earned it by bleeding for the Beishan clan in life-or-death battles. Using it is a threat that says, “Harm me, and you harm a vassal of a marquis.” It’s the Xianxia equivalent of pulling a nuclear briefcase.

**The Soul-Shattering Art (撼神术)**: This is a divine sense secret art, a rare and highly lethal technique for soul-based combat. Normally, divine sense is used for perception, but secret arts like this weaponize it directly against an enemy’s mind. It’s a sneak-attack that bypasses physical defenses, but it’s only effective if the user’s soul is far stronger than the target’s. Ji Ning, with his anomalously powerful soul, has been training this art, and here it pays off in a massive way.

**The ‘Thorns Everywhere’ Stance (荆棘密布)**: This is the second stance of the legendary Three-Foot Sword (三尺剑), a sword-art created by the fearsome Immortal Beixing. While the first stance, ‘Sword Heart Illumination,’ is a state of clarity, this stance is pure, undiluted killing intent. It manifests the user’s will to pierce through all obstacles. Ji Ning’s strike was a perfect fusion of this sword-intent, the raw power of the Starseizing Hand, and the impossible speed of Windwing Evasion.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterful setup and payoff. Pay close attention to the negotiation—Ji Ning isn’t just angry; he’s *calculating*. He knows he can’t win a straight fight against a veteran Wanxiang Adept with unknown connections, so he engineers a scenario where the old man feels safe, relaxed, and superior. The moment the Crippled Moon Realperson smiles, thinking he’s won, is the exact moment he’s already dead.

Notice the contrast between Ji Ning’s cold, analytical surface and the volcanic fury underneath. When he says “I could kill Yu Tong in an instant,” he’s not bragging. The chapter makes it clear he could have done so before the Realperson arrived. But he *wants* Yu Tong to suffer, to despair, to understand *why* he’s being killed before the blade falls.

Also, this is a major inflection point for Mu Northson. He’s not just a cheerful sidekick here—he’s ready to fight a Wanxiang Adept without hesitation. His loyalty to Ji Ning is absolute, and his anger is real. Keep an eye on how his character grows from this moment forward.
