## Chapter Overview

Holy smokes, fellow cultivators! Welcome back to the Multi-Treasure Peak, where the stakes just went thermonuclear. The seemingly humble material, the Five Elements Peacock Tail, has turned into a full-blown war of attrition between Earth Immortals, a rogue cultivator, and our man Ji Ning. But Ji Ning isn't just a wide-eyed little Wanxiang Adept watching the big boys play—he’s here to play for keeps. In a display of sheer financial dominance, he shocks the entire assembly by leaping the bid to an eye-watering five million jin of Primordial Liquid. This isn't just a purchase; it's a declaration. Get ready for a masterclass in Xianxia economics, a critical turning point in Ji Ning's personal power, and the incredibly tense moment when a store sends in the auditors to check if this kid is actually good for the money. Buckle up, Daoists, the auction just caught fire!

## Key Plot Points

*   Ji Ning makes his explosive entry into the high-stakes bidding war for the Peacock Tail. His voice cuts through the noise of the veteran Immortals, immediately drawing the attention and skepticism of every guest on Multi-Treasure Peak.
*   The price escalates rapidly from four million to a tense four million, seven hundred thousand jin, with the rouge cultivator Dong Chou and the Shaoyan clan's Shaoyan Xiushui locked in a bitter duel for a tribulation-crossing treasure.
*   Heavenly Treasures Mountain, fearing Ji Ning might be a bluffer, dispatches a representative named Yichen to privately verify his ability to pay. This moment highlights the immense scale of the transaction and the store’s risk-mitigation protocol.
*   Ji Ning, ever the pragmatist, solves the verification problem by pulling out the Immortal-ranked 'Green Strand Divine Fire Lamp' from his storage treasure and offering it for auction to cover his bid, proving his financial backbone with a flick of his wrist.
*   The bid caps out at five million jin of Primordial Liquid. Ji Ning’s final offer silences the hall, crushing the will of both Shaoyan Xiushui and Dong Chou Immortal. The Peacock Tail is his.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Treasure Competition Assembly (争宝会)** – This is not just any auction; it's a high-stakes social and financial battlefield. In Xianxia, power is directly tied to resources. An auction is a litmus test of a cultivator's wealth, backing, and, crucially, their track record of slaying. When Ji Ning bids, the crowd’s immediate assumption isn't that he’s rich; it's a forensic calculation—*he killed Shaoyan Nong and a Primal Fiendgod, so he must have their loot.* This isn't envy; it's respected cause-and-effect in the martial world. A man who can kill an Immortal's offspring is assumed to be carrying an Immortal's treasury.

**Primordial Liquid (元液)** – Forget gold coins. In the upper echelons of the Grand Xia, the currency of true power is Primordial Liquid, the ultra-pure essence of natural energy. Five million jin is an astronomical sum, enough to buy a complete Immortal-ranked magic treasure outright. That Ji Ning is spending this much on a *material* (the Peacock Tail) rather than a finished weapon shows he's playing a very long-term, high-risk game.

**Transcending Tribulation (渡劫)** – The desperation of Earth Immortals like Dong Chou and Shaoyan Xiushui to acquire the Peacock Tail underscores the terror of the Three Calamities and Nine Tribulations (三灾九劫). These are periodic, inescapable disasters that batter a cultivator until they either ascend to Celestial Immortal or perish. Any treasure, any material that can bolster their defenses or their power during these tribulations is worth a king's ransom. Their near-murderous rivalry for a "mere" forging material makes perfect sense in this terrifying context.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterful slow-burn of economic and psychological warfare. Watch how Ji Ning plays the room. He doesn't just raise the bid; he *shatters* the bidding rhythm. By jumping from 4M to 4.8M and then to a final, decisive 5M, he isn't just outbidding his rivals. He's delivering a tactical blow to their morale, demonstrating that his resources (or his connnection to the Black-White College) are bottomless compared to theirs. The tension with the Heavenly Treasures Mountain representative is a classic "poor kid at the big boy's table" moment, immediately shut down by Ji Ning’s effortless display of wealth.

Pay close attention to the list of onlookers. The commentary from the Xiyue Princess, Wood Pass Realperson, and Green Willow Immortal creates a vivid chorus of awe and disbelief. This isn't just filler—it’s the novel telling us that Ji Ning has officially stepped out of the local pond and into a vast ocean. His status in the world has fundamentally changed with this single purchase. The 'Peacock Tail' isn't just crafting material; it's a symbol of his ascension.

## Xianxia Economy Deep Dive: The Value of a Life

This chapter offers a grim but fascinating glimpse into the economic engine of the Xianxia world. The root of Ji Ning's purchasing power isn't alchemy or trading; it's murder. Killing Shaoyan Nong (a Wanxiang Adept heir) and a Primal-level Fiendgod effectively liquidated their entire centuries-long accumulation of wealth into one giant pile of liquid assets. The observant crowd at the auction is essentially doing a post-mortem mathematical analysis of that kill. *“How much was Shaoyan Nong's robe worth? That Fiendgod probably had a nice weapon…”*

This concept, widely known as "man-slaughter wealth" (杀人放火金腰带), is a core truth of the cultivation genre. The risks are apocalyptic (annihilation by a Primal Daoist ancestor), but the rewards are equally extreme. Ji Ning isn't just bidding on a feather; he's using the liquidated value of a young master's life to buy his own ticket to the next power tier. It's brutal, transactional, and deeply satisfying to watch unfold. The Xinxia economy runs on blood and ambition.
