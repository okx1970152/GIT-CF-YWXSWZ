## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists—the stakes just got a major upgrade! In this chapter, ShaoYan Nong, one of the Four Young Masters of the Grand Xia Imperial Capital, extends a once-in-a-lifetime invitation to Ji Ning and his Black-White College crew: explore a genuine Celestial Immortal’s estate. Not some backwater Earth Immortal’s shack—a real, dead-for-ages Celestial Immortal’s lair, untouched for millennia. But as any veteran cultivator knows, the most tempting bait hides the sharpest hook. The chapter lays out the classic Xianxia negotiating dance: temptation, caution, team deliberation, and finally, a pact sealed with promises of trust. What follows is a whirlwind two-day voyage that ends with the Tianyou Mountains looming on the horizon—and a whole new world of danger and opportunity waiting inside.

## Key Plot Points

- The invitation: ShaoYan Nong reveals the expedition as a test from his clan, to explore an ancient Celestial Immortal’s estate. Though he has a dangerous attendant, he still needs their help—a red flag that immediately makes Ji Ning suspicious.
- Team deliberation: Cangjiang, Nine Lotos, and Yu Wei are tempted by the prestige and potential gains. But Ji Ning’s cautious instincts slow the rush to accept, highlighting his maturity as the group’s de facto leader.
- The bribe escalates: When the group hesitates, ShaoYan Nong drops the real bait—Immortal Wujiang was a Daemon Sword Immortal, and his sword arts are likely still inside. For a sword fanatic like Ji Ning, that’s game over. The deal is sealed.
- Ji Ning’s preparation: Before departure, he arms himself with two protective treasures from the aquatic palace—strange items crafted by Daoist Threelives himself. The subtext is clear: this is a life-or-death venture, and he’s not taking risks.
- Power flex on the war-galley: ShaoYan Nong’s own spirit-beast, a Redscale Flood Dragon (a top-tier godbeast that won’t take human form until Primal stage), is a subtle reminder of his overwhelming background. Yet he still needs Ji Ning’s crew—which says everything about the estate’s danger.
- Arrival at the Tianyou Mountains, with the Immortal estate looming. The expedition is officially on.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Celestial Immortal vs Earth/Loose Immortal**: This is a crucial power-tier distinction in Xianxia cosmology. A Celestial Immortal has transcended the Five Elements and the cycle of reincarnation—their lifespan is truly boundless. That’s why their estate is a vastly bigger deal than an Earth Immortal’s cave. Immortal Wujiang wasn’t just any Celestial Immortal—he was a *Daemon Sword Immortal*, a rare combination that makes his sword-art inheritance all the more valuable. For a sword-maniac like Ji Ning, this is gold-dust.

**The “Battle of Fame” setup (成名之战)**: Ji Ning’s upcoming clash in this arc carries the aroma of a classic Xianxia “Battle of Fame.” That is, a rising star faces off against an established powerhouse—not to defeat them, but to escape alive or force a draw. The result itself functions as a declaration: “This new name has arrived, and everyone must remember it.” This chapter plants the seed for exactly such a moment.

**Nascent intelligence of treasures (灵智初生)**: Ji Ning’s black armband, forged by Daoist Threelives himself, isn’t just a random magic rock. At a high enough tier, treasures begin to exhibit glimmers of sentience—living eyes, draconic fury. That’s the sign that an artifact is approaching the threshold of giving birth to a true artifact spirit. For now, the armband is merely “strange,” but with the right growth, it could become something far more alive.

### Nascent intelligence (灵智初生)

The state of a high-grade magic treasure approaching sentience; the artifact can exhibit instinctual behavior (living eyes, draconic fury) but has not yet developed a full, independent spirit or personality.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a textbook example of what makes Xianxia negotiation scenes so addictive—the tension is all in what’s *not* being said. ShaoYan Nong is a polished salesman, dangling the sword-art card at the perfect moment. But the real thrill lies in the quiet power dynamics: a young master with a Primal-tier godbeast still needs a bunch of Zifu/Wanxiang kids to back him up. That alone tells you the estate is a death trap dressed in gold. Watch for Ji Ning’s clinical post-battle analysis later—he doesn’t bask in glory, he breaks down his performance into variables: sword-art, treasure grade, resource availability. That’s the Dao-heart of a survivor, not a glory-hunter.
