## Chapter Overview
Wake up, fellow Daoists—Ji Ning is finally back inside the aquatic estate, and the artifact spirits drop some *heavy* lore on him. This chapter pulls back the curtain on the War God Hall's real difficulty curve (spoiler: it's built for Wanxiang-level Fiendgod Body Refiners, not Zifu-stage overachievers) and serves up a gorgeous Star Hall enlightenment sequence that flexes Ji Ning's accumulated growth over four years. Plus, we get a quiet, intriguing setup: Nine Lotos has learned exactly where Ji Ning is staying, and she's already on her way to Winged Serpent Lake. Tension, cultivation philosophy, and a hint of romance—this chapter has it all.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning reports to the aquatic estate's artifact spirits. The yellow-furred bear assesses his progress: Ki Refining has reached late Wanxiang (fast), but Fiendgod Body Refining at the 9th stage is lagging behind.
- Ji Ning asks about challenging the War God Hall's third level. The bear shocks him: less than one in ten chance. He explains the brutal scaling—levels 1–2 test Zifu-level Fiendgod Refining; levels 3–4 test Wanxiang-level. Even Celestial Immortal Juhua couldn't clear level 7 until he became a Loose Immortal.
- The bear and ox elaborate on the Dao hierarchy: minor Daos, Grand Daos, Heavenly Daos. Juhua mastered nine minor Daos but never a single Grand Dao. Daoist Threelives was born with innate mastery of a Grand Dao and later grasped many, but never a Heavenly Dao.
- The spirits praise Ji Ning's potential, saying he might become the first in the Grand Xia Dynasty's major world to fully master the Sword Dao (a Grand Dao). Ji Ning's ambition burns white-hot.
- Ji Ning enters the Star Hall, reads the Star Scroll aloud, and enters a profound enlightenment state. He achieves the Domain level in the Dao of the Gale, and his Windwing Evasion reaches its true form.
- Cut to Anchan City: Mu Northson reports Ji Ning's location (Winged Serpent Lake) to Nine Lotos. She decides to leave the college and head there to gain experience and meet him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Minor Dao, Grand Dao, Heavenly Dao (小道、大道、天道):** This is the novel's core metaphysical hierarchy. A minor Dao governs a single phenomenon (like wind or fire); a Grand Dao is a vast, interconnected domain (like the Sword Dao or the Dao of Spacetime); a Heavenly Dao is a universal law that underpins an entire chaosworld. In Xianxia, the gap between them is not just about power—it's about *comprehension*. You can train for millions of years and never grasp a Grand Dao. This explains why Celestial Immortal Juhua, despite being a legendary Sword Immortal, never *fully* mastered the Sword Dao. Ji Ning is being told he has the *potential* to surpass figures who already shook the entire Grand Xia Dynasty. That's a *monstrously* high ceiling.

**The Nine-Story Terrace Proverb (九层之台，起于累土):** Ji Ning quotes Laozi's *Tao Te Ching* here. The full line is "A nine-story terrace begins with a pile of earth; a journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step." In a Xianxia context, this is a humble, wise reminder that even the greatest cultivators start from nothing. It's classic Daoist philosophy applied to cultivation psychology—ambition must be paired with patience.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a beautiful blend of *lore dump* and *character breath*. If you've been wondering why the War God Hall feels so unbalanced compared to Ji Ning's insane talent—now you know. The artifact spirits aren't being mean; they're being *honest*. The trials were calibrated by a Primordial Fiendgod for absolute monsters. Ji Ning is a monster, but he's still a *young* one.

Pay close attention to the Star Hall's effect. Unlike the War God Hall which tests combat, the Star Hall is a *sanctuary* for enlightenment. Ji Ning didn't just train for four years—he *accumulated* insights. The Star Hall unstops the bottle. This is a classic Xianxia payoff: the quiet, patient foundation-building finally pays off in a burst of comprehension.

And Nine Lotos making her move? That's a subtle but important thread. In Xianxia, a female lead who takes the initiative to travel to the male lead's territory is a big signal. Buckle up—things are about to get more personal.
