## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia social humiliation and financial flexing. Our boy Ji Ning, fresh off his Immortal Fortunes Assembly victory and a 30-year training retreat, strolls into Heavenly Treasures Mountain with his cousin Yuchi Xiyue. The insufferable Young Master Yunpeng, a nouveau riche from the Heavenly Realm's Windstop Isle, tries to one-up him at every turn—only to be utterly demolished when Ji Ning casually whips out a single flame arrow worth more than Yunpeng's entire treasure hoard. But beneath the surface, Xiyue's cold pragmatism about using Yunpeng for resources reveals the brutal economics of rebuilding a fallen clan. Get ready, because this chapter serves up equal parts emotional homecoming, political maneuvering, and the sweetest kind of financial revenge.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiyue Commandery Princess reveals her ruthless strategy: she tolerates Yunpeng's arrogance purely to extract treasure gifts for rebuilding the Yuchi clan, admitting she would give her own life for the cause.
- Ji Ning and Xiyue share a mental conversation where she explains her calculated marriage to the spineless Feng Yungu—she needs a husband she can completely control.
- The group arrives at Heavenly Treasures Mountain headquarters. Yunpeng continues his passive-aggressive taunts, trying to embarrass Ji Ning.
- Uncle White (Whitewater Hound) hands Ji Ning two beast-hide scrolls: the first for formations that can terrify Celestial Immortals, the second adding three supreme slaughter formations from the Tristar Crescent Abode that can threaten True Immortals and Empyrean Gods.
- The Green Willow Immortal is shocked by the lists; the second scroll's enormity requires the intervention of a Celestial Immortal overseer.
- The silver-haired Celestial Immortal reveals the price: the first list costs 8.6 million jin of Primordial Liquid; the second cannot be bought with any amount of Primordial Liquid—it requires ten supreme-grade Immortal treasures or one low-grade Pure Yang treasure.
- Yunpeng is visibly shaken, realizing his entire wealth falls far short.
- Ji Ning produces the Fire Elephant Arrow, a weapon from the Luohou Bow's quiver, equivalent in value to a low-grade Pure Yang treasure.
- The Celestial Immortal accepts the arrow and agrees to also pay Ji Ning an additional 30 million jin of Primordial Liquid.
- Yunpeng's arrogance is completely shattered; his only remaining confidence—wealth and backing—has been utterly eclipsed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Treasure Economics in Xianxia**: This chapter beautifully illustrates a key principle in high-level Xianxia—the diminishing utility of generic currency. For cultivators below the Celestial Immortal level, Primordial Liquid (refined natural energy) is a versatile and valuable resource. But for True Immortals, Empyrean Gods, and Daofathers, it's just "water." This is why top-tier treasures (Pure Yang, innate spirit treasures) are exchanged on a barter system: a Pure Yang treasure for multiple Immortal-ranked treasures, never for raw liquid. The Fire Elephant Arrow being worth a Pure Yang treasure is actually reasonable when you consider that divine archers of the Three Realms must have arrows that can survive being shot from a bow like the Luohou Bow—each arrow is essentially a disposable Pure Yang-grade weapon.

**The Economics of Rebuilding a Fallen Clan**: Xiyue's strategy—tolerating a rich suitor for gifts while giving no real affection—is not merely cynical; it's a survival tactic deeply embedded in the Xianxia clan structure. A ruined clan like the Yuchi has lost its power base, its elders, and its economic engine. The only remaining assets are the bloodline's talent (like Xiyue herself) and sheer will. Using social leverage—especially the dangling hope of a marriage alliance—to extract resources from a wealthy fool is a time-honored tradition among weakened aristocratic families in Chinese literature. It's cold, it's pragmatic, and it's exactly what the genre demands.

**Fortress Formations**: The concept of layering multiple grand formations (each one terrifying in its own right) over a single territory is a classic Xianxia defense strategy. Age equals power—a sect or clan that has existed for millennia will have generation after generation adding new protective arrays on top of the old ones, creating a cumulative maze that even high-level immortals cannot brute-force. The Black-White College's defenses have been built up over eons, and Ji Ning is now adding a layer that can threaten True Immortals. This is how you protect a home when raw firepower alone isn't enough.

### Fortress Formation

The cumulative process of layering multiple grand formations over a single territory, each reinforcing the other, making it exponentially harder for intruders to breach. A classic sect-protection strategy in Xianxia where age equals power.

### Wufeng Immortal

The oldest Loose Immortal of the Black-White College, known for his slovenly appearance. His lifespan was extended by the new scriptures, showing the value of shared resources within a sect.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the payoff for all that tension with Young Master Yunpeng. If you've been waiting for Ji Ning to casually reveal just how far above this petty noble he's risen, this is it. The "Fire Elephant Arrow flex" is one of the most satisfying moments in this arc—not because Ji Ning was trying to show off (he genuinely just needed to pay for the formations), but because Yunpeng's entire identity as a wealthy, privileged bully collapses in an instant. Pay close attention to Xiyue's character here, too. Her cold pragmatism might seem harsh, but it's rooted in a survivor's mentality. She's playing a long game that Ji Ning, fresh from his cultivation retreat, is only beginning to understand. And those three supreme slaughter formations? They're not just a flex—they're Ji Ning preparing for a coming storm. The Black-White College is about to face something big, and our boy is making sure his home won't fall.
