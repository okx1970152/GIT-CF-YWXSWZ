## Chapter Overview

Holy hell, folks. After all these chapters of Ji Ning the cold, calculating infiltrator—the master of disguise, the ghost who slips through eighteen formations as a speck of dust—we finally get to the real, soul-crushing payload of this Eastwood Mountain arc. And it hits you right in the gut. Ji Ning’s mission to rescue his parents’ true bodies takes a backseat to something far more personal: the discovery of his long-lost junior apprentice-brother, Mu Northson, reduced to a Grey ashen, life-drained husk of a man. This chapter is a masterclass in tonal shift, going from meticulous spy thriller to one of the most emotionally devastating reunions in the entire novel. Get your tissues ready, folks, because the story we’ve been building towards just got a hell of a lot more personal.

## Key Plot Points

- **Pawn Capture & Soul Search:** Ji Ning effortlessly captures two spineless Primal Daoists from the Eastwood Sect. He coerces them into a voluntary soul-search by threatening a violent alternative, learning the internal structure and secret rules of the corrupted sect.
- **The Spineless Sycophants:** The soul-search reveals that the Eastwood Sect was broken by a mysterious force decades ago. The ‘righteous’ were killed or went into hiding, while cowards like these two Primal Daoists became collaborators, who help oppress their own fellow disciples to win favor.
- **The Mysterious General & Forbidden Ground:** The occupying force is led by a figure known only as “the General,” apparently at the Celestial Immortal level. Their true base of operations is a heavily guarded ‘Forbidden Ground’ deep within the sect’s mountains.
- **Infiltration of the Forbidden Ground:** Ji Ning transforms into the captured Primal Daoist and re-enters the sect. He then assumes the form of a speck of dust stuck to a black-robed soldier’s shoe, successfully navigating a chain of eighteen (yes, eighteen!) formations to gain entry.
- **The Ancient Castle & Exhausted Immortals:** Inside the Forbidden Ground, Ji Ning discovers an ancient, shielded castle guarded by over a hundred Loose Immortals. He witnesses a procession of exhausted, drained ‘gray-robed’ Earth Immortals leaving the castle, their lifeforce dim and their spirits utterly broken.
- **The Horrifying Discovery:** Among the drained, gray-robed prisoners, Ji Ning spots a small figure and recognizes him with a shock that shakes him to his core: it’s his missing junior brother, **Mu Northson**.
- **A Broken Brother:** Mu Northson is nothing like the energetic youth Ji Ning remembers. He is thin, haggard, has streaks of grey hair, and emanates a profound sense of exhaustion and despair as if his very will to live has been extinguished.
- **The Heartbreaking Reunion:** Ji Ning follows his brother to his private room. After Mu Northson sets up a sound barrier, he is seen carving a wooden statue of his lost Dao-companion, his only solace. When Ji Ning reveals himself, Mu Northson finally breaks down. The chapter ends with Ji Ning making a cold-blooded vow: everyone responsible will die.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter relies heavily on classic Xianxia tropes cleverly inverted for maximum emotional impact. The **Soul Search (搜魂)** is a staple method for acquiring information. Its risk of turning the victim into an ‘idiot’ or killing them makes it a feared tool of “evil” cultivators. Ji Ning’s use of persuasion to get voluntary cooperation is a mark of his pragmatic, yet not purely sadistic, nature. The **Soul Search** is a staple method for acquiring information, a brutal but effective tool that risks permanent mental damage. Its risk of turning the victim into an ‘idiot’ or killing them makes it a feared tool of “evil” cultivators. Ji Ning’s use of persuasion to get voluntary cooperation shows his intelligence, but also a certain ruthless pragmatism.

The ‘**Spineless Sycophants**’ are a classic archetype found in any sect that has fallen from grace. Their existence provides a grim look at the survival instinct within the ruthless hierarchy of the Cultivation world.

And then there’s the ‘**Forbidden Ground**’ (禁地). In Xianxia, this term usually evokes ancient temples, dangerous ruins filled with treasures. Here, it’s been perverted into a secret prison and labor camp, a place of unspeakable exploitation. The image of Celestial Immortals and Loose Immortals being drained of their ‘mind power’ until they have grey hair and look like the walking dead is a terrifying subversion of the usual ‘Immortal’ fantasy.

Finally, the **Wood Carving**. This is a powerful symbol of Mu Northson’s Dao-heart. Unable to save his love, he has been ‘carving’ slowly and painfully out of grief, his Dao-heart not broken but bleeding. The fact he sets up a sound barrier before doing it shows the oppressors have taken even the privacy of his grief.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for the storm. This isn’t just a rescue mission anymore; it’s a declaration of war. Ji Ning’s primary goal was to find the key to his parents’ true bodies, but he has just discovered his own brother being used as a battery in a slave camp. The moment he sees Mu Northson’s condition, the entire calculus of this operation changes. The General and his mysterious power are now public enemy number one on Ji Ning’s personal list.

Notice the careful, forensic detail of Ji Ning’s investigation. Every formation, every level of security, the timing—he’s building the perfect blueprint for an escape and a massacre. And the final scene, the raw, silent sobbing of a once-bright youth, is a major character beat. This isn’t just a source of power for Ji Ning; it’s the rebirth of his fury. That final oath he swears is the quiet before a storm of absolute, righteous violence. The emotional groundwork has been laid. Now comes the payoff.
