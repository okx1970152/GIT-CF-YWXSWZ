## Chapter Overview

Old Man Xuanji is throwing a proper tantrum, and this chapter is all about the political chessboard being set. After confirming Ji Ning’s terrifying youth and talent, he marches straight to the Black-White College to demand the kid be handed over like a bad dish. But the college’s top brass doesn’t just roll over. Eight Loose Immortals, each worth ten ordinary ones, stand their ground in a delicious verbal cage match. Just when the tension peaks, a mythical figure from the Northmont Clan’s past—Celestial Immortal Tuoshan himself, thought long dead—shuffles in to draw a clear boundary: Anchan Commandery is Northmont turf, and Xuanji can’t push the sect. But he warns plainly: they won’t save Ji Ning outside either. The chapter closes with Ji Ning back in the Aquatic Manor, being calmly told to sit down, read some books, and *zen out* before explaining anything. It’s a brilliant counterpoint to all the political screaming—pure Dao-heart training.

## Key Plot Points

- Ancestor Xuanji acquires Ji Ning’s intelligence file from Heavenly Treasures Mountain. The key find: Ji Ning is preposterously young—barely a teenager—and has already awakened a Domain of the Sword. This cements Xuanji’s resolve to kill him before he grows into a monster.
- Xuanji visits the Black-White College Grand Hall. The confrontation is immediate: he demands they hand over Ji Ning. The Black-White College’s eight Loose Immortals, led by Diancai and Wufeng, refuse bluntly, with Diancai dropping a classic “a college that can’t protect its own disciples is no college.”
- The diplomatic stalemate intensifies. Xuanji threatens a clan-vs-sect war. The Loose Immortals hold firm, betting on the college’s top-tier combat power and Anchan City’s no-fighting law.
- In steps Celestial Immortal Tuoshan—a long-lost Northmont elder. He tells Xuanji the Black-White College is Northmont’s protected turf and that Anchan Commandery is not Shaoyan territory. Xuanji backs down diplomatically but swears Ji Ning *will* die.
- Back in the Aquatic Manor, the Yellow-haired Bear refuses to hear Ji Ning’s panicked report. Instead, he sends him to read the Starseizing Scrolls first to calm his heart. The reading session triggers an epiphanic rainfall—a sign of Dao resonance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The political landscape of a Grand Xia Commandery**. This chapter presents a gorgeous example of *how Xianxia politics work* beyond brute force. The Shaoyan Clan is indeed stronger than the Northmont Clan of Anchan, but not overwhelmingly so. Strength in Xianxia is a *matrix* of raw power, territorial jurisdiction, ancient alliances, and the number of Loose Immortals who can flee and become a guerrilla nightmare. The Black-White College’s eight Loose Immortals are each worth ten ordinary ones because the college only allows breakthroughs to Primal or Earth Immortal after mastering *multiple complete Daos*—a brutal academic standard that translates into terrifying combat efficiency.

**Celestial Immortal Tuoshan’s re-appearance** enriches a classic Xianxia trope: the “presumed dead old monster.” The Northmont Clan let the world think Tuoshan was gone, which gave them a hidden strategic card. His appearance is timed perfectly to remind Xuanji that even a stronger clan cannot cross into another vassal’s domain without consequence. The Grand Xia Dynasty enforces a strict “no fighting in cities” rule, so any large-scale war must happen outside—but in open terrain, top-tier Loose Immortals are a nightmare to pin down.

**The Starseizing Scroll reading sequence** is a masterstroke in showing Dao-heart tempering. Ji Ning is panicking, his mind swirling with thoughts of revenge, Nine Lotos, and the safety of his clan. The Yellow-haired Bear doesn’t explain or console—he just tells Ji Ning to *read*. And as he reads, the environment itself reacts: rain starts to fall. This is Dao resonance, a sign that his soul has synchronized with the ultimate truth contained in the scrolls. The rain is not a spell; it’s a phenomenon of understanding.

## Reading Guide

**Get ready for the classic “besieged sect refuses to hand over disciple” face-off**. If you’ve read enough Xianxia, you’ll recognize the beats—but the execution here is top-notch. The five Loose Immortals’ unified stance, Diancai’s cold fury, Wufeng’s deceptive cheerfulness, and the chain-wrapped giant’s rumbling threats create a *visual symphony of defiance*. The fact that the Black-White College doesn’t need a Celestial Immortal of its own because their Primals are trained to be Loose-Immortal killers is a flex worth noting.

**Pay attention to how Ji Ning is absent from all the politics**. He doesn’t know about the college’s defense, Tuoshan’s intervention, or the open-season declaration that awaits him outside. This creates a delicious dramatic irony—we, the readers, know the stakes are insanely high, but Ji Ning is currently having a spiritual epiphany under a starry sky, getting rained on by the Dao. The Aqua Manor’s timing is perfect: it takes him out of play precisely when the world is hunting him, giving him a chance to grow before the next crisis.

**Also, keep an eye on that final rainfall**. It’s not filler. When a cultivator’s reading triggers the environment to mirror their Dao comprehension, it means they’ve absorbed a truth at a soul-deep level. Ji Ning’s next power-up may come from this serene, rainy stillness—not from frantic sword-swinging.

**Tropes summarized**: Political stalemate at a sect | Presumed-dead elder returns to set boundaries | Hero isolated from politics, forcing solo growth | Dao-heart tempering through enforced stillness.

## Deep Dive: The Myth of Tuoshan from the Northmont Clan

Tuoshan is a fascinating case in this novel’s worldbuilding. The “hunched-backed elder leaning on a wooden staff” is a deliberate visual callback to classic Chinese depictions of *Dongfang Shuo* (东方朔) or the *Old Man of the Southern Pole* (南极仙翁)—figures of immense longevity who appear frail but carry the weight of centuries. In real Chinese folklore, such figures often emerge at critical moments to speak one line that shifts the entire power balance. Tuoshan is playing that exact role: a Celestial Immortal from the **Northmont Clan’s “golden generation”**, thought dead for so long that even the Shaoyan Clan had discounted him. His single speech act—*Anchan Commandery is mine*—redefines the battlefield line. Xuanji cannot cross that line without triggering a war with the Northmont Clan, and because the Grand Xia dynasty enforces territorial law, even the stronger Shaoyan Clan would face severe consequences if they initiated an unprovoked invasion of a fellow vassal’s heartland. Tuoshan is not just a fighter; he is a *political boundary device*.
