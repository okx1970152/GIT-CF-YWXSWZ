## Chapter Overview

So, our boy Ji Ning is all geared up to join the Rift Sword Sect — until a petty, scheming noble named Beishan Hu decides to throw a wrench in the works just to spite his cousin Beishan Baiwei. That’s Xianxia politics for you: a family grudge between two scions of the Beishan clan, and poor Ji Ning gets caught in the crossfire. This chapter isn’t about blazing sword-fights or power-ups; it’s about the *realpolitik* of the Grand Xia Dynasty. Baiwei and his new friend Hun Wuqi give Ning a crash course in the rules of the game: make powerful friends, use their influence, and *seize* opportunities before some rival noble can snatch them away. It’s a masterclass in political maneuvering, with a side of divine music and a bonus lesson on how wealth can be just as sharp as a sword.

## Key Plot Points

- Baiwei reveals he arranged for a young Zifu Disciple from the Rift Sword Sect, Zhou Li, to act as Ji Ning’s insider when he joins the sect. He outlines the brutal competition among tens of thousands of disciples and urges Ning to fight for the top spots.
- A powerful new character, Hun Wuqi of the Hun Clan, is introduced. The Hun Clan rose from nothing in just a thousand years and is now one of the top two mercantile powers in the Anchan Commandery. Baiwei explains the Hun Clan’s legendary founder, Hun Tianyou, a former street urchin who became so wealthy he could hire three Immortals to kill his enemies back when he was only a Wanxiang Adept.
- Beishan Hu arrives and publicly, triumphantly declares that Ji Ning will never enter the Rift Sword Sect, exposing the feud between the two branches of the Beishan clan. Baiwei is forced to admit his father Beishan Blacktiger and Hu’s father Beishan Yin are rivals for the title of the next Marquis of Anchan.
- Hun Wuqi smooths things over by pointing out the sheer number of sects in the Anchan Commandery, reminding Baiwei that one spiteful brat can’t block all the doors.
- The Zither Immortal, a master musician who has “entered the Dao” with her art, plays a transcendent song. Baiwei tries to cheer Ning up by inviting her to their chamber, but Beishan Hu once again barges in to steal her away, sparking more frustration.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Realpolitik of the Grand Xia Dynasty**

This chapter is a perfect window into the “Great Clan” politics that define so much of Xianxia society. It’s not all about individual cultivation; the world of *Desolate Era* is built on a brutal hierarchy of noble houses, each a law unto itself. The Beishan clan holds the title of Marquis of Anchan, giving them sovereign authority over a massive commandery. A position like “Marquis” isn’t simply inherited; it's a prize to be won. The struggle between Beishan Blacktiger and Beishan Yin for the next generation is a *succession war* fought through their children. Beishan Hu blocking Ji Ning isn’t about Ji Ning at all — it’s a calculated strike to make Beishan Baiwei lose face and appear weak. This is the “face culture” of East Asian politics turned up to eleven.

**The God of Wealth: The Hun Clan**

Hun Tianyou is a classic Xianxia trope: the scrappy nobody who overcomes a crippling lack of talent through sheer grit, ruthlessness, and business acumen. His story — taking the name “Hun” (from “hoodlum”) and “Tianyou” (Heaven’s blessing) — is a direct inversion of the usual cultivation narrative. You don’t need to be a peerless genius to win; you just need a *lot* of money. The fact that he could, as a mere Wanxiang Adept, hire three actual Immortals to do his dirty work shows how wealth can completely bypass the power ladder in this world. In a universe where might makes right, money can *rent* might.

**Entering the Dao Through Art**

The Zither Immortal’s performance is a beautiful display of a *Dao of Skill*. In Xianxia, an artist who dedicates their life to a craft (calligraphy, music, painting) can reach a state of Dao comprehension that rivals that of a warrior. Her music doesn’t just sound good — it has a physical effect on Ji Ning’s soul, washing away weariness. This is a great reminder that cultivation isn't just about punching people; it’s about understanding the nature of reality through *any* medium.

## Reading Guide

This is a “social currency” chapter — it teaches the reader how the game is played *outside* of the battlefield. Pay close attention to how Baiwei operates: he *invests* in friendships (like paying a high price for the Zither Immortal) and sees powerful connections as a form of power more reliable than raw force. The introduction of Hun Wuqi is a promise of future plot payoff — the Hun Clan’s legendary wealth is sure to become relevant when Ji Ning needs to buy rare treasures or hire an army. Also, keep an eye on the Zither Immortal. Her brief appearance feels like the seeding of a future character arc, or at the very least, a future ally with a unique skill set. This chapter quietly sets the stage for the broader world of the Anchan Commandery, proving that Ji Ning’s journey is about to get much more complicated than just swinging a sword.
