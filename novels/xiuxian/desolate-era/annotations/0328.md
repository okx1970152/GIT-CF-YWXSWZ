## Chapter Overview

Get ready, because the Immortal Fortunes Assembly’s opening round just went from a tactical scrimmage to a full-blown, earth-shattering brawl. Ji Ning and Yu Wei are ambushed by two of the most terrifying Wanxiang Adepts in the entire Grand Xia Dynasty: Cangwu Vulture and Xiamang Zishan. This isn't just a test of skill; it's a brutal, no-holds-barred cage match where a moment's distraction means a broken body. Ning’s brilliant Water-Fire Lotus holds against Cangwu Vulture’s secret art, but when the royal madman Xiamang Zishan decides to join the party, all bets are off. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia combat pressure, forcing our protagonist to deploy every trick in his arsenal—including the Starseizing Hand and Three Heads, Six Arms—just to survive. The real question isn't if he can win, but if he can save his senior apprentice-sister while doing it.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Trap is Sprung:** Cangwu Vulture, the second-ranked candidate, initiates the ambush with his signature Netherworld Poison Vulture Secret Art, a formation-based technique that houses his true body for a devastating close-range follow-up.
- **Yu Wei's Counter and Retreat:** Yu Wei unleashes her Rahu Godneedles to form Ice and Fire Phoenixes, but Cangwu Vulture shatters them in three exchanges. She is forced to retreat into the safety of Ji Ning's Water-Fire Lotus.
- **The Xiamang Prince Enters:** Xiamang Zishan, the number-one ranked contestant, enters the fray. His sheer, overwhelming power is on display as he effortlessly smashes through the Water-Fire Lotus with a single staff strike, revealing a massive power gap.
- **The First Clash:** Ji Ning, using the Starseizing Hand, barely matches Xiamang Zishan's third staff form. However, the prince’s newly-created fourth form, fusing four Daos into the Dao of Thunder, sends Ning flying into a collapsing mountain.
- **Three Heads, Six Arms Duel:** Both combatants unleash their full physical potential, growing two additional heads and four extra arms. Ning shifts to a defensive, agile style using Windwing Evasion to parry and redirect the royal prince’s relentless, crushing staff strikes.
- **The Critical Distraction:** With Yu Wei trapped, Ning makes a desperate gamble. While barely holding off Xiamang Zishan, he splits his concentration to activate the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation to harass Cangwu Vulture, buying a window for Yu Wei to escape. This distraction costs him dearly, as Xiamang Zishan lands a heavy blow.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Dao Fusion and Combat Arts:** This chapter provides a gorgeous look at high-level Xianxia combat. Simply having a powerful technique isn't enough—the true measure of a warrior is their *comprehension of the Dao*. Xiamang Zishan’s “fourth form” is terrifying not because it uses more energy, but because he has successfully fused four complete Daos into a single, overwhelming attack. This concept of "Dao Fusion" is a key pillar of progression fighting; it represents a qualitative leap in a cultivator's ability to impose their will upon reality. Ning, whose sword-art is more refined but whose Dao comprehension lags behind, is forced to rely on superior agility and legendary divine abilities to compensate.

**The Social Spectacle of the Imperial City Square:** The fight isn't just happening on a battlefield; it's being live-watched by the elite of the Grand Xia. The reactions of the various Loose Immortals, Earth Immortals, and scions of major clans in the Imperial City Square are a core part of the tension. This is a *tournament of fame*. Every exchange, every strategic retreat, every brutal hit is being judged, discussed, and analyzed by thousands. A fighter’s performance here shapes their reputation for centuries to come. Ji Ning’s display is shocking everyone because he, a cultivator of barely thirty years, is forcing a deadlock with the most famous prodigy of the royal clan.

**The Cost of Split Focus:** Xianxia literature often emphasizes the supreme importance of a focused "Dao-heart." In this chapter, Ning's attempt to multitask—defending himself against a powerhouse while attacking another—is immediately punished. This isn't just a gameplay limitation; it's a spiritual law. A divided heart is a weak sword. The novel uses this moment to heighten the stakes: Ji Ning isn't just fighting for victory; he's fighting for his senior apprentice-sister, and that very compassion is the weakness his enemy is trying to exploit.

### Cousin-brother (表弟)

In the Chinese family hierarchy, a 'cousin-brother' (表弟 / biǎodì) denotes a younger male cousin on the mother's side of the family, which is a different relationship than a paternal cousin. This chapter establishes this specific bond.

## Reading Guide

This is the "pressure cooker" chapter you’ve been waiting for! The author is cranking the tension dial to eleven by isolating our duo against the absolute cream of the crop. If you were worried Ning was getting too strong too fast, this chapter brings him crashing back down to earth. The genius of the fight lies in making the power gap feel real—Xiamang Zishan isn't a villain; he's just *better* right now. The point isn't for Ning to win this... not yet. The point is to see how far he can push himself under the kind of pressure that breaks most cultivators. Watch his strategy: he shifts from a bruiser using the Starseizing Hand to a slippery, technical fencer. He's learning, adapting, and even taking a massive hit just to create an opening for his ally. That's the mark of a true monster—not invincibility, but relentless, willful survival. Get ready for the cost of that gamble to become clear in the next chapters.

## Deep Dive: The Rahu Godneedles

In Chinese mythology and Daoist folklore, the Rahu Godneedle (罗睺神针, *Luóhóu Shénzhēn*) is a nod to the celestial demon Rahu. In Buddhist and Hindu cosmology, Rahu is a shadowy entity—the head of a demon who swallows the sun and moon, causing eclipses. Adapting this myth, Xianxia authors often grant the name “Rahu” to spiritual needles or flying swords, giving the weapon a sense of ancient, devouring power. Yu Wei’s set of 72 Rahu Godneedles isn't just a random number; it is a deliberate echo of the 72 Earthly Fiends (地煞七十二术), a concept from Daoist numerology and *Journey to the West*. Using a "72" pattern often suggests a technique that emphasizes flexibility, trapping, and coordinated formation attacks rather than brute force. This duality—a phoenix formation rising from these needles—perfectly captures Yu Wei’s own character: a delicate, graceful exterior sheathed in a weapon of immense, disciplined power.
