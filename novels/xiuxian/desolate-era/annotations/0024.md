## Chapter Overview

Our young protagonist wraps up his dinner conversation with a bold demand: he wants to leave the Western Prefecture city and roam the wilds. After surviving the Winged Serpent's ambush, Ji Ning realizes that the comfortable walls of home can no longer sharpen his sword. His father, Ji Yichuan, sets a seemingly easy condition—win a Golden Sword at the quadrennial Golden Sword Ceremony. But behind that condition lies deeper strategy: cutting off Ji Lie's ambitions and unlocking a second, secret reason. Ning throws himself into preparation, mapping out great demon territories and planning a brutal training journey that will culminate in a rematch with the thousand-year-old Winged Serpent. The chapter ends with Ji Lie's alarm—a ten-year-old prodigy just entered the arena, and the old viper doesn't like it one bit.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Family Debate**: Ji Ning argues with his parents about leaving the Western Prefecture. His mother worries about his age; his father tests him with sword strikes. Ning's footwork has reached Unity of Man and Heaven, shocking Ji Yichuan into reconsidering.
- **The Condition**: Ji Yichuan agrees to let Ning roam—but only if he wins a Golden Sword at the Golden Sword Ceremony. Ning, confident he could win with a tenth of his strength, accepts readily. The second reason for this condition remains a mystery.
- **Preparation and Route Planning**: Ning studies records of great demons and maps. He plans a route through deep mountain marshes, hunting early-stage Xiantian great demons, with the ultimate goal of slaying the late-stage Winged Serpent once he breaks through to the fourth stage of the Crimsonbright Diagram.
- **Qiuye's Joy**: The personal maidservant is thrilled to be chosen as one of Ning's two attendants (along with the experienced Mowu). Her excitement contrasts with her grim reality—she's been trained to sacrifice herself for her master if needed.
- **Golden Sword Ceremony Begins**: The quadrennial competition for all youths under sixteen opens across the Western Prefecture's territories. Countless tribe boys register, burning with ambition. The chapter ends with Ji Lie's outrage upon learning that the ten-year-old prodigy Ji Ning has entered.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Golden Sword Ceremony (金剑大典)**: This is no ordinary tournament. It's a life-or-death competition held once every four years for all youths under sixteen within the Ji Clan's territory. Winning a Golden Sword brings immense prestige and, for tribe youths, access to advanced cultivation techniques that can change their families' fate. For Ji Ning, it's also the formal path to becoming the next Prefectural Lord—a political tool to silence rivals like Ji Lie.
- **Unity of Man and Heaven (天人合一)**: A profound state of harmony with nature. When a cultivator achieves this in footwork, their movements become as fluid as wind, nearly impossible to track. Ji Ning reaching this at age ten is considered monstrous talent even by Xiantian standards.
- **Tacit Peace Between Humans and Demons**: The Ji Clan and the great demons of the Yan Mountains maintain a fragile, unspoken truce. Demons don't massacre tribes openly; cultivators don't wage full-scale extermination wars. The Winged Serpent's attack on Ji Ning was a major provocation, but the response is contained—a siege of the lake, not a war. This tension between order and chaos is a hallmark of xianxia worldbuilding.
- **The Crimsonbright Diagram's Scaling**: Ji Ning knows that at his current third stage of the Crimsonbright Diagram, his Fiendgod Body Refining power equals an early Xiantian Ki Refiner. The fourth stage matches a late Xiantian. He's planning to break through before facing the Winged Serpent—a tactical calculation that reveals his careful, analytical mind.

## Reading Guide

Get ready, because the Golden Sword Ceremony is about to bring the heat. This chapter sets up the classic "young master leaves the nest" arc, but with a twist—Ji Ning isn't running away; he's sprinting toward his first real bloodbaths. The map scene is pure catnip for progression fantasy fans: you can practically see him marking territories as "to be conquered." What makes this thrilling is the contrast between Ning's cool-headed planning and the raw ambition of the tribe boys who see this ceremony as their only shot at transcendence. Ji Lie's reaction at the end is a cherry on top—the old tiger has been threatened by an infant before, but now the infant is picking up a Golden Sword. Keep an eye on the mysterious "second reason" Ji Yichuan mentioned. And yes, we're all waiting for that rematch with the Winged Serpent. Slow burn, fellow Daoists. But it's coming.
