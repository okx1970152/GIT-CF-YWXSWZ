## Chapter Overview

What does it take to make Ji Ning cry? In this soul-piercing chapter, the third forbidden zone of the Fiendgod corpse hits him where it hurts most—not with blades or lightning, but with the warm, bittersweet memory of his parents. The toughest test of a cultivator’s Dao-heart isn’t pain or terror; it’s the thing they never want to wake up from. Ji Ning walks through illusion after illusion, each one a homecoming he knows he has to leave behind. When he finally emerges, he’s not just earned a legendary spirit bow—he’s faced the deepest longing in his soul and chosen to let go. Get ready for a chapter that’s equal parts soul-food and pure Xianxia hype.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning transforms into his Three Heads, Six Arms combat form and steps into the third forbidden zone.
- The forbidden zone is a layered illusory trial targeting a cultivator’s heart. The first illusion traps Ji Ning in his childhood home with his parents, Yuchi Snow and Ji Yichuan.
- Ji Ning relives the small, warm moments: eating flatbread by candlelight, learning sword and footwork, and hugging his parents goodnight. But he realizes his memories are fading and forces himself to wake, shattering the illusion with a roar.
- He passes the remaining illusions at an astonishing pace—each step taking less than an incense stick’s time.
- The black-robed youth (former Celestial Immortal) watches from afar, shocked by Ji Ning’s speed, and acknowledges him with a knowing smile before vanishing.
- Ji Ning reaches the Fiendgod corpse. He openly thanks his master (Patriarch Subhuti), saying the time spent in the illusion with his parents was the happiest since leaving the Yan Mountains.
- The artifact spirit of the black greatbow, Luohu Bow, emerges. It reveals itself as a top-grade innate spirit treasure forged from the corpse of the ancient terror Luohu.
- Ji Ning calmly refines the Luohu Bow with the spirit’s help, then collects the Fiendgod corpse, causing all restrictions in the Quya Mountains to dissipate.
- He teleports away, leaving the Golden-Furred Bear King and Wuyou Celestial Immortal to find the valley empty.
- Back at Mount Innerheart, Ji Ning prepares for the Houyi Archery trial, encountering his senior apprentice-brother the woodcutter, who wishes him luck.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Third Forbidden Zone & The Dao-Heart Trial (道心考验)** – This chapter is a textbook example of a Xianxia hallmark: the Dao-heart trial. Unlike physical or magical tests that can be brute-forced, a Dao-heart trial targets a cultivator’s innermost vulnerabilities. The sequence Ji Ning experiences—greed, attachment, obsession, inner demons—mirrors the classic Buddhist and Daoist concept of the “Four Mara” (demons of the mind). In Xianxia lore, a cultivator can have all the power in the world, but if their Dao-heart isn’t pure, they’ll crash and burn on the path to Immortality.

**The Luohu Bow & Innate Spirit Treasure (先天灵宝)** – The Luohu Bow is an innate spirit treasure, a tier above ordinary Pure Yang treasures. The key difference? It’s alive with its own spiritual nature. In Xianxia cosmology, an innate spirit treasure was not forged by mortal hands but birthed from the primordial forces of Heaven and Earth. That’s why it can choose its master. Even a mortal can wield it if the treasure *wills* it—a huge deal in a world where most weapons demand a certain cultivation base.

**Houyi (后羿) & Archery in Xianxia** – Houyi is the legendary archer who shot down nine of the ten suns that once scorched the earth. In *Desolate Era*, his archery art is one of the hardest trials in the Three Realms Palace. Archery in Xianxia is often a “Heartforce” path, relying on precision, intent, and Dao-comprehension rather than raw strength. This foreshadows Ji Ning’s expansion beyond pure swordsmanship into long-range combat.

### Heartforce

A rare and esoteric form of power in Xianxia, tied to the cultivator's will and emotions rather than physical energy. Masters of Heartforce can strike with near-unavoidable, causality-defying attacks.

## Reading Guide

This chapter might seem slower than the flashy battles, but it’s a pivotal character moment. Ji Ning’s breakthrough isn’t about leveling up—it’s about confronting his deepest emotional wound: the loss of his parents. Pay close attention to how he interacts with the illusion. He knows it’s fake, yet he chooses to enjoy the warmth before waking himself up. That self-awareness is the mark of a truly terrifyingly mature cultivator. Also, the Luohu Bow’s lore ties directly into the larger history of the Three Realms—keep an eye out for how this weapon changes Ji Ning’s combat style down the line.

## How Forbidden Zones Work in the Desolate Era Universe

Forbidden zones in *Desolate Era* aren’t just fancy walls. They’re autonomous metaphysical constructs left behind by Primordial-level beings like Patriarch Subhuti. Each zone is designed to filter out disciples based on specific virtues: raw power (first zone), comprehension of the Dao (second zone), and Dao-heart solidity (third zone). What makes Ji Ning special is that he didn’t just endure the third zone—he *walked faster* through it as he went. In Xianxia terms, that signals a Dao-heart so resilient that the illusions couldn’t even slow him down.
