## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, the siege of Mount Oxhorn has begun! In this chapter, the Snowdragon Mountain coalition—nineteen Zifu Disciples strong—finally arrives at the Ji Clan’s last stronghold. But they don’t just crash through the front gate like reckless barbarians. Their formation expert, Nong Zidao, calmly dismantles the outer illusion maze with a single whip strike (his spirit crane does the heavy lifting, showing off sect-level elegance). The counterplay is immediate: Ji Jiuhuo activates the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation, a five-node defensive array, and the clan makes their final stand preparation by entrusting the two Vassal Tokens to Ji Ning. The stage is set for a high-stakes formation duel, where every misstep means death. Get ready for some tactical Xianxia warfare—this isn’t just about who punches harder, but who out-thinks the other.

## Key Plot Points

- **Snowdragon Mountain scouts the mountain**: Dongzi Qi leads the vanguard, while Nong Zidao and Tongyu stay back as the heavy hitters. Nong Zidao’s crane easily extracts a formation flag from the outer maze without entering, proving that the Ji Clan’s trickery is child’s play to a trained formation master.
- **Ji Clan’s desperate gambit**: Ji Jiuhuo reveals the clan’s last-resort plan—the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation. He divides the five nodes (Dragon Head, Body, Claws, Pearl, Tail) among Ji Liuzhen, Granny Shadow, old servant A Xing, and Ji Ning himself, with Ji Jiuhuo commanding the core.
- **Vassal Tokens change hands**: In a move that underscores the gravity of the situation, Ji Jiuhuo gives both the captured He clan token and the Ji Clan’s own Ten Thousand Swords City token to Ji Ning. If the clan falls, Ji Ning is to flee with them and represent the Ji Clan before the Grand Xia.
- **The first probe**: Twelve Zifu Disciples enter the black fog, linked by Dongzi Qi’s Eight Trigrams Blood Dragon Formation. They advance cautiously, knowing that within the fog, a coordinated ambush awaits.
- **The silent preparation**: Every Ji Clan member takes their position, ready to burn their lives for the clan. The tension is palpable—this is a battle between sect discipline and a clan’s desperate will to survive.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Formation Warfare & Joint Formations**: This chapter is a textbook example of how Xianxia sects fight with numbers. A “joint formation” (合阵) allows multiple cultivators of the same cultivation base to merge their power into a single entity. This is why sects are so terrifying: six Zifu Disciples can form a stable combat formation, and several thousand can rival a Primal Daoist. The Snowdragon Mountain disciples are confident because they *share a common formation technique*. The Ji Clan, by contrast, is using a fixed defensive array (the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation) that requires specific node controllers—more like a siege weapon than a mobile battle formation.

**Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation (冥龙锁天阵)**: A classic five-part defensive formation. The Dragon Head leads the attack, the Dragon Body provides defense, the Dragon Claws strike from angles, the Dragon Pearl stabilizes the core, and the Dragon Tail is the most agile, capable of sweeping to any threatened sector. In Xianxia lore, this is a mid-tier array—scalable with the number of Zifu controllers but still vulnerable to a true formation master.

**Vassal Tokens (臣子令)**: In the Grand Xia Dynasty, owning a city’s Vassal Token grants the holder authority over that city and a binding contract with the imperial court. Tokens can be seized by killing the owner. They are both prizes and lifelines—losing one means losing a city. Ji Jiuhuo’s decision to give both tokens to Ji Ning is a final insurance policy: if the clan is annihilated, the tokens and the city rights pass to the next generation.

**Why the Crane Could Pull the Flag from Outside**: Nong Zidao’s spirit crane used a long whip to coil the formation flag without entering the maze. This is a classic trick for breaking “passive” illusion arrays: if the formation’s flags are physically exposed, a skilled practitioner can yank them out from a distance. More sophisticated formations (like the subsequent black mist array) hide their flags deeper, requiring direct entry to neutralize them.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *positioning*—both literal and tactical. Watch how Snowdragon Mountain’s first wave (twelve cultivators) enters the black fog, tightly packed inside a defensive joint formation. The Ji Clan, meanwhile, has placed their best fighters (including Ji Ning) in the four mobile nodes of the Blackflame Dragon Formation, with Ji Jiuhuo as the core director. The setup promises a clash where individual heroism must bow to collective coordination. Pay close attention to the bit about “if any of us dies… we must have a backup”—that’s foreshadowing that someone *will* fall. Also note that Nong Zidao and Tongyu stay out of the initial probe; they’re waiting to see the formation’s true patterns before committing. This is classic Xianxia pacing: first the pawns test the board, then the masters move.
