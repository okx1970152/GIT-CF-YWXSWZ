## Chapter Overview
This chapter slows down the cultivation grind to reveal the true scale of the Desolate Era’s power ladder. Ji Ning, fresh off repelling a Primal Daoist, gathers with his inner circle on Winged Serpent Lake, where a seemingly casual chat unveils the Immortal Gathering—a dynasty-wide megaevent that’s both a bloodbath of geniuses and a golden ticket to the Three Realms’ top-tier attention. The conversation shifts from polite congratulations to the cold reality that there are over a hundred thousand Wanxiang-level monsters out there who can match a Primal Daoist. The chapter is a masterclass in “the calm before the storm,” blending post-battle camaraderie with heavy foreshadowing about the true cost of advancement.

## Key Plot Points
- Celestial Immortal Cangjiang and Bloodshadow arrive on Brightheart Island to formally congratulate Ji Ning on his victory, revealing the ripple effect of his fame.
- Yu Wei’s talent is publicly acknowledged: she grasped the complete Grand Dao of Ice three years ago, and it’s implied her past life as a Loose Immortal is far more formidable than first thought.
- Beishan Baiwei introduces the Immortal Gathering—a quadrennial battle royale for cultivators below the Primal Daoist level, hosted by the Grand Xia Dynasty.
- The scale sinks in: over 3,600 commanderies means over 100,000 geniuses at the Flamesaint/Cangjiang level will be competing.
- The true prize is revealed: not just Immortal-ranked treasures or Primordial Liquid, but the chance to be noticed by beings who transcend Celestial Immortals—True Immortals, Pure Yang beings, and the hidden movers of the Three Realms.
- Over eighty percent of known Celestial Immortals first made their mark at this Gathering, setting up a long-term goal for Ji Ning to weigh: go now at age thirty, or hold back for three centuries and grow terrifyingly strong.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Immortal Gathering (仙缘大会) as a Xianxia Trope**  
This is a textbook implementation of the “Heavenly Assembly” trope—a recurring event in Chinese web novels where the protagonist is tested against the world’s best before the narrative scope explodes. It functions as a narrative filter: only those who survive are deemed worthy of the next tier of secrets and power. The twist here is the *waiting game*: geniuses deliberately stunting their own cultivation for three hundred years to compete in a lower bracket is a classic xianxia display of deferred gratification and strategic patience. Note how this mirrors Ji Ning’s own pragmatic Dao-heart—he doesn’t impulsively rush to sign up; he calculates.

**The Hierarchy: Dao → Grand Dao → Celestial Immortal → True Immortal**  
The chapter draws a crucial distinction: comprehending a complete Dao (even a Grand Dao like the Sword) is only the *prerequisite* for ascension. Actually *surviving* the tribulations to become a Celestial Immortal—let alone a Pure Yang True Immortal—is a different beast entirely. This layered progression is rooted in Daoist alchemical thought, where each breakthrough requires an internal “fusion” of yin and yang, earthly and celestial, before one can transcend. The term “Pure Yang Immortal” (纯阳真仙) specifically evokes the idea of a body and soul purified to absolute yang essence, leaving all karmic dregs behind.

**Reincarnated Immortals and Their Power Ceiling**  
Yu Wei’s background is gently expanded: not all reincarnated souls are created equal. A Loose Immortal who cultivated for hundreds of thousands of lifetimes has a very different ceiling than one who died young. This is a nod to the Buddhist concept of *karmic inheritance*—past-life cultivation carries forward as latent talent, but it’s not a free pass. Her mastery of both ice *and* fire is a clear sign she’s operating on a deeper philosophical level than most.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is prime **“worldbuilding through dialogue”**—the kind of scene that rewards attentive readers. Pay close attention to how each character reacts differently to the news: Beishan Baiwei delivers it with aristocratic weariness, Cangjiang with sober gravitas, Yu Wei with detached acceptance, and Mu Northson with wide-eyed shock. The way they talk about the Immortal Gathering tells you everything about their current place in the hierarchy. Also note Ji Ning’s silent reference to Daoist Threelives at the end—it’s the first hint that his secret inheritance might already put him ahead of the curve compared to these “True Immortals.” The chapter ends with a quiet, unspoken question hanging in the air: *does Ji Ning even need the Immortal Gathering?*
