## Chapter Overview
After surviving the ancient relic’s trials, Ji Ning doesn’t even pause to catch his breath—he charges straight back to Winged Serpent Lake to settle the score. The chapter opens with his parents’ relieved discovery that he’s alive, then pivots hard into action as Ji Ning boldly parts the lake and descends into the deep. The Winged Serpent, furious over his son Crimson’s death, accepts the challenge in his own lair. This is no random skirmish: the lake bottom is the Winged Serpent’s home turf, where a human’s combat power is drastically reduced. Yet Ji Ning, now a Xiantian Fiendgod Body Refiner with water control, strides in with cold confidence. The stage is set for a rematch that feels personal, raw, and deeply rooted in the blood feud that has driven the story since Chuncao’s death.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning emerges from the ancient relic and finds the token talisman marked with the Fiendgod script character “Right” (you). He realizes he must reach the Zifu stage to refine it.
- His parents, Ji Yichuan and Yuchi Snow, have been anxiously waiting for over a month. They sense his safe return via the bonded jade talisman and rush to the lake.
- Without wasting time, Ji Ning stands on the lake surface and shouts a challenge to the Winged Serpent. When the serpent doesn’t come out, Ji Ning uses his new water-control ability from the Crimsonbright Diagram to part the lake and walk directly to the bottom.
- The Winged Serpent, initially cautious of an ambush, is enraged that Ji Ning dares to enter his domain. He transforms into his true form and charges into the underwater passage.
- The two exchange verbal taunts—Ji Ning accuses the serpent of slaughtering thousands and killing Chuncao; the serpent vows to avenge his son Crimson—before engaging in direct combat. The Winged Serpent’s tail strike showcases overwhelming power, but Ji Ning’s Windwing Evasion proves even faster, allowing him to dodge and counterattack by leaping onto the serpent’s body and thrusting at its head.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Combat on home turf**: In Xianxia, fighting a water-type demon in its own lake is considered suicidal for a land-dwelling human. Water resistance, pressure, and the creature’s innate control over the element dramatically reduce the intruder’s effectiveness. Ji Ning’s ability to part water effortlessly is a mark of his Fiendgod Body Refinement—a path that grants dominion over elements early, bypassing the need for Ki-based spells. This is a classic trope where a cultivator turns the enemy’s advantage into a level playing field. Also notable is the **token talisman** with the character “Right.” Fiendgod script is born from Heaven and Earth, understood instinctively by readers—a wink to the Daoist concept that truth is self-evident. The fact that Ji Ning cannot refine it yet signals that greater powers await at the Zifu stage.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a pure payoff for everything built up since Chuncao’s death. Feel the tension: the Winged Serpent is not just any monster—he’s a perfected Xiantian great demon with centuries of instinct and rage. Ji Ning’s decision to fight in the water is audacious, but his calm analysis and superior speed (Windwing Evasion) suggest he has a plan beyond brute force. Watch for the moment when his water-control ability is first revealed to the antagonist—it’s a subtle flex that completely shifts the serpent’s expectations. The dialogue is stripped down, almost ritualistic: each line echoes the debt of blood. For readers new to Xianxia, note how the fight isn’t just about stats—it’s about territory, inheritance, and the unyielding will to protect what remains. How will Ji Ning overcome the raw power disadvantage in the deep? The answer lies in what he learned inside that relic.
