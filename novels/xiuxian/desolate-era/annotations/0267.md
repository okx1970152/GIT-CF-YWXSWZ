## Chapter Overview
The tension ratchets up as the team clears the Dao-Soldier Hall and stares down the final two branch halls—both locked behind impenetrable walls of beast Dao-soldiers. Shaoyan Nong’s confidence is starting to look like expensive armor over raw nerves. The real show-stopper, though, happens off-screen: the estate’s native beasts have done something they haven’t managed since the Wu Jiang Immortal’s fall. They’ve united. Duohe Immortal brokers a sweeping alliance, and for the first time, Yaksha and Wave Dao-soldiers are pointed at the same target—our heroes. The chapter closes with the team flying straight into a storm of twenty thousand coordinated killers, and the air is thick with the smell of a trap they can’t avoid.

## Key Plot Points
- Shaoyan Nong mentally notes his backup plan: after refining the estate, he’ll have his Shaoyan clan send an army to enslave all native beasts. A cold reminder of the power dynamics behind this “trial.”
- The Xiangliu Fiendgod Fang snags two top-grade Earth-ranked Dao-soldier armors before anyone else can react—a masterclass in practical dominance.
- Ji Ning, with Uncle White and Xiaoqing assisting, grabs three of the eight shares of Dao-soldier armor, causing some quiet envy. Xue Hongyi sneers inwardly, clinging to his Immortal-ranked treasure; Mu Northson grumbles to Xiaoqing and gets verbally dismantled as usual. The banter lightens the mood, but the underlying tension is palpable.
- The real bombshell: Duohe Immortal, having witnessed the team’s progress, convinces every beast faction in the estate world—including the proud Wu Jiang clan—to swear a Heavenly Dao oath of absolute unity, pooling over twenty thousand Dao-soldiers to guard the last two halls.
- The chapter ends with the warship flying straight into the teeth of the Wave Dao-soldier formation. The escape route is open (Shaoyan Nong has the key), but retreat means failure. The pressure is on.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Dao-Soldier Armor & Estate Armies (道兵甲铠 & 府兵体系)**  
The Wu Jiang Immortal’s legacy is clearest here: two breeds of Dao-soldier armor—the mass-produced Wave set (便宜货量产型) for quantity, and the far rarer Yaksha set for elite troops. Top-grade Earth-ranked armor sits below Immortal-ranked, but even so, it’s a haul that would make any Wanxiang Adept drool. The fact that the native beasts have never united until now highlights a classic Xianxia theme: **fragmentation under tyranny**. A single overlord (the Wu Jiang Immortal) kept everyone in line; after his fall, the factions squabbled for ages until an external crisis forced unity—a textbook Desolate Era political lesson, writ small.

**The Heavenly Dao Oath (天道誓言)**  
When Duohe Immortal says he can “swear a Heavenly Dao oath,” he’s not speaking figuratively. In this setting, the Heavenly Dao is a real, sentient cosmic law that punishes oath-breakers with metaphysical backlash (tribulations, karma, soul damage). An oath sworn on the Heavenly Dao is ironclad—no one who values their cultivation would dare break it. This is why the beast alliance is credible despite their old grudges.

**Spirit-Beast Economics (灵兽经济学)**  
The brief exchange about spirit-beasts reveals a law of Xianxia ecology: the strongest monsters are caught by the strongest clans. A Red-Scaled Flood Dragon (赤鳞虬龙) is a divine beast that could match a cultivator’s growth—but you’ll never find one in the wild; they’re already in the stables of superpowers like the Shaoyan clan. The Skygreen Serpent is a “poor man’s divine beast”—weaker in combat but invaluable for treasure-hunting. Ji Ning’s trio of helpers (Uncle White, Xiaoqing) puts him at a resource advantage that even reincarnated Immortals envy. It’s a quiet flex of his rise from a rural tribe to a player on the commandery stage.

## Reading Guide
*Allies of convenience, swords of desperation—welcome to the last stand, fellow Daoists.*

This is the calm before the storm—a chapter that does double duty as loot payout and dread setup. Five branch halls, and two are locked behind armies that have just sworn brotherly love over a shared goal. The question isn’t *if* the fight will break out, but how bloody it gets before Shaoyan Nong yanks the escape cord. Notice how even the banter has a nervous edge: Mu Northson’s goading of Xiaoqing feels like whistling past a graveyard. The real horror here is the unity of the enemy. Duohe Immortal pulled off a diplomatic miracle that the Wu Jiang Immortal never could—and he did it in hours. That’s the desperation of creatures who’ve seen the door to a wider world and will burn everything to reach it. Keep your eyes on Ji Ning’s quiet analysis in the next few chapters. When things go sideways (and they will), you’ll want to track who survives and who bought the farm.
