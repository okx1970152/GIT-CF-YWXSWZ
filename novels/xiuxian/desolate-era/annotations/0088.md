## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, the tension dial just got cranked up a notch! After our last chapter’s explosive battle, Ji Ning and his father Ji Yichuan track down the source of some massive energy fluctuations—and what they find isn’t a demon or a treasure, but something far more dangerous: a colossal, ultra-rich elemental essence stone vein buried right under the Ji Clan’s backyard. But instead of popping champagne, the clan elders look like they’ve just swallowed a fire pill. Why? Because in the cutthroat world of Xianxia, one man’s lucky break is another man’s death warrant. Get ready for some heavy political calculus and a looming shadow that’s about to fall over Yan Mountain.

## Key Plot Points

- **Father-Son Race**: Ji Ning is stunned to discover that his father Ji Yichuan can keep pace with him at top speed, hinting that Yichuan has secretly broken through to the Zifu level.
- **The Discovery**: After a painstaking underground search, Ning confirms the vein runs over three hundred li deep and spans over four thousand li in area—a truly major, high-grade deposit.
- **From Blessing to Catastrophe**: The clan elders, led by Ji Jiuhuo, realize that such a massive resource is not a blessing but a deadly threat. The Ji Clan is far too weak to defend this treasure.
- **The Real Danger**: Ji Jiuhuo explicitly fears Snowdragon Mountain. With a Primal Daoist at the head of its main sect, and its Yan Mountain branch Zifu Disciples already suspected to be nearby, the clock is ticking.
- **A Glimpse of Higher Powers**: The chapter reveals that the vein was formed over a hundred years by the cultivation formation of a Primal Daoist named Blazingfire, placing the scale of power far beyond any Xiantian or even most Zifu cultivators.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Elemental Essence Stones (Yuan Shi)** are the standard currency and fuel for the Xianxia economy. They come in low, medium, high, and top grades. A high-grade stone is a big deal for a Xiantian cultivator, but a *vein* of them—stretching for thousands of li—is the kind of wealth that can buy a dynasty. This is where the "we must be strong enough to keep what we have" principle kicks in. In Chinese cultivation fiction, finding a treasure you can't protect is worse than finding nothing at all; it paints a target on your back.

**The Blazingfire Daoist's Formation** explains *why* this vein was never discovered. A Primal-level cultivator's formation can mask a region for centuries. The vein’s existence is a byproduct of a failed or completed ascension attempt—a classic Xianxia trope where the hidden hand of a long-gone expert shapes the present. It also highlights the terrifying resource consumption of high-level cultivators: one man's breakthrough attempt created a treasure that could support an entire clan for generations.

**Delegated Awe** is on full display here. We’ve never met Blazingfire Daoist, nor have we seen a Primal cultivator fight. But the very mention of his existence—the scale of his formation, the fact that he could even *attempt* to become an Earth Immortal—sends a message to the reader about the terrifying heights of this world’s power ladder. The sect that hunts this vein (Snowdragon Mountain) also has a Primal Daoist, and that one fact is enough to freeze the entire Ji Clan leadership in their tracks.

### Elders of the Ji Clan

Ji Jiuhuo, Granny Shadow, and Ji Liuzhen represent the hidden Zifu-level power of the Ji Clan. Their shared terror at the vein’s discovery underscores the precarious position of the clan.

### Delegated Awe

A literary technique where the power of an off-screen character (like Blazingfire Daoist) is established through the awed reactions of on-screen characters.

## Reading Guide

This is a “quiet before the storm” chapter, but don’t let the lack of action fool you. The discovery of the vein is the single most important political event for the Ji Clan since… well, ever. Pay close attention to the clan elders' reactions. They aren’t greedy; they’re terrified. Their first instinct isn’t to mine—it’s to hide. This tells you everything you need to know about the world of Desolate Era: raw power trumps everything.

Notice the quiet reveal about Ji Yichuan. His ability to keep pace with a bullshit-level Fiendgod like Ji Ning at top speed is a major hint that there’s a lot more to Ning’s father than meets the eye. His reluctance to discuss his cultivation is also telling. We’re seeing the first cracks in the father’s stoic armor.

Finally, get ready for the storm. The name “Snowdragon Mountain” has been dropped. If you’ve been reading this far, you know that name only ever means trouble. The Zifu Disciples of their Yan Mountain branch are likely already on the move. The next chapter is bound to bring a confrontation.
