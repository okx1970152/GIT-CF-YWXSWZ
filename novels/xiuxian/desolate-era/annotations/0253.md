## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a shift in perspective, fellow Daoists! This chapter pulls the camera away from Ji Ning's secluded mountain lake and zooms all the way to the Crimson Dragon Mountains, where a high-stakes political game is unfolding. We are introduced to the arrival of **Shenyong Nong**, one of the Four Young Masters of the Grand Xia Imperial Capital, a figure from a clan so ancient and powerful it's practically a founding dynasty itself. Beishan Yin, a master of political scheming, and the local Yinglong Guard overseer scramble to offer their best resources—including a shocking gift of death warrior cultivators—to this young noble. The chapter masterfully builds suspense around his mysterious 'trial' before ending on a note that will make any Ji Ning fan's ears perk up: Shenyong Nong hears the name ‘Ji Ning’ mentioned as a genius of note. The chessboard is being set for a major confrontation.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Secret Arrival**: Beishan Hu is dragged to a classified meeting at the Yinglong Guard outpost by his father, Beishan Yin. The target is revealed to be Shenyong Nong, the heir apparent to the Shenyong clan.
- **The Shenyong Clan’s Status**: The narrative takes a moment to outline the Shenyong clan's terrifying lineage—almost as old as the Grand Xia imperial family itself, with a history stretching back to the primordial era. They are not just nobles; they are the foundation of the empire, holding three commanderies as fiefs.
- **The Gifts of Loyalty**: The East Stone Immortal offers two elite Second-Claw Yinglong Guards, but Beishan Yin goes a step further with a truly extravagant gift: ten Wanxiang Adepts trained as fanatical death warriors, a massive gesture of political investment.
- **The Trial Revealed**: Shenyong Nong explains that his visit is for a ‘minor trial’ set by his clan. He urgently needs manpower, which is why he accepted the gifts with haste.
- **The Cliffhanger**: As the blood-red warship flies over the Yan Mountains, Bu You mentions Ji Ning as a peerless genius of the Black-White College. Shenyong Nong’s interest is piqued, setting the stage for a future encounter.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Political Factions & ‘Supporting Pillars’**: The political structure here mirrors ancient Chinese dynasties. The Xiamang clan (Grand Xia rulers) conquered the world but relied on powerful allied tribes. The Shenyong clan is a ‘supporting pillar’ (*gǔ gōng zhī chén*), akin to the noble families of the Zhou dynasty. Their loyalty is bought with titles (Duke Yi), vast lands, and immense autonomy, creating a complex web of power beneath the emperor.
- **Death Warriors (死士, *sǐshì*)**: Training cultivators to be expendable assassins or soldiers is a classic Xianxia trope. It’s incredibly expensive and difficult because the mindset of a slave conflicts with the Dao of cultivation, which requires a strong, independent will. This makes Beishan Yin’s gift of ten Wanxiang Adept ‘death warriors’ an astonishingly valuable and rare token of allegiance. It’s the equivalent of giving away ten tanks driven by elite, brainwashed special forces.
- **The Yinglong Guard & Their Ranks**: The ‘Second-Claw’ rank system for the Grand Xia’s immortal army is a direct reflection of the meritocracy of power. More powerful members are given higher ‘claw’ statuses. A Second-Claw member being compared to a Primal Daoist shows the elite nature of this corps.

## Reading Guide
This chapter functions as a fantastic piece of geopolitical world-building and a major character introduction. It’s a classic Xianxia technique to show the vast and complex ecosystem the protagonist operates in before a major conflict. We’re not just fighting water serpents anymore; we’re stepping onto the stage of a dynasty-spanning power struggle. Pay close attention to the cold, calculating nature of Beishan Yin—he is the opposite of the beast-like Beishan Heihu we saw earlier. The gift of death warriors isn’t generosity; it’s a high-risk, high-reward future investment. The chapter also sets a subtle but powerful thematic contrast: Shenyong Nong, born with a silver spoon in a universe of resources, versus Ji Ning, who has clawed his way up from a backward tribal outpost. The moment his name is mentioned in such esteemed company is a quiet but potent validation of Ji Ning’s legend.
