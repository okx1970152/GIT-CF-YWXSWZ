## Chapter Overview

Drop the mic, folks—this is the chapter where Snowdragon Mountain gets absolutely folded. From Ji Ning and Yuchi Xiyue’s quiet banquet with Black-White College friends to the sky above Anchan Commandery turning golden with divine dragons of annihilation, this chapter is a stone-cold delivery of cosmic-scale vengeance. The nine squads of Gold-ranked Imperial Guards (each a Loose Immortal + 49 Primal Daoists) roll up to a sect that has never even seen an Earth Immortal and just… delete it from the map. Mountains become lakes. Peaks become plateaus. And the mastermind, Dong Qi, gets dragged back to the capital with his Zifu already shattered. No trial. No debate. Just a sovereign power flexing its muscles with surgical precision. It’s brutal, it’s beautiful, and it’s one of those rare chapters where the reader gets to lean back and enjoy the fruits of a revenge plan executed with imperial efficiency.

## Key Plot Points

- **Imperial muscle arrives**: Nine squads of Gold-ranked Imperial Guards, led by Loose Immortals, descend upon Snowdragon Mountain with the Anchan Northmont clan’s guide, Beishan Luoxiao.
- **Instant capture**: One captain teleports directly into the sect, seizes Dong Qi, and returns in a single breath. Dong Qi’s Zifu is already crushed before his grandfather even knows what hit him.
- **The reveal**: The three Primal Daoists—Snowfeather, Cold Sun, and Blackflood—only realize the soldiers are Gold-ranked Imperial Guards (not local troops) when it’s far too late.
- **Absolute annihilation**: Eight squads transform into ten-thousand-li golden divine dragons and shatter the mountain-guarding formations like paper. Snowfeather self-detonates to save his soul; Cold Sun and Blackflood are killed.
- **Geographical rewrite**: The entire Snowdragon Mountain range is flattened into lakes and plateaus. No peak remains standing.
- **Return to the capital**: The captain reports the capture to Yuchi Xiyue, who is hosting a banquet with Ji Ning and his fellow disciples. She and Ji Ning rise the moment they learn the news.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Gold-ranked Imperial Guards (金甲禁军)**: These are not your local sheriff’s deputies. Each squad consists of one Loose Immortal (散仙) leading forty-nine Primal Daoists (元神道人), all wearing Dao-soldier armor that allows them to merge their power into a single divine dragon. A single squad is worth ten ordinary Loose Immortals. Nine squads? Overkill. But *satisfying* overkill.
- **Self-detonation (自爆)**: Snowfeather Daoist chooses to blow himself up rather than be killed by the golden dragons. In Xianxia logic, this is actually a *mercy move*—self-detonation lets the cultivator’s soul survive and re-enter the Six Paths of Reincarnation, whereas being killed by the formation might obliterate the soul entirely. It’s the difference between a respawn and permadeath.
- **Mountain-guarding formations (守山大阵)**: A sect’s ultimate defensive trump card, built and reinforced by generations of ancestors. The Snowdragon Ascension Formation could hold off an ordinary Loose Immortal. Against ten Loose Immortals worth of concentrated force? It lasted about as long as a tissue in a hurricane.
- **The concept of sect karma**: Even Beishan Luoxiao notes that Snowdragon Mountain’s ruin was inevitable—lax discipline and rampaging disciples drain a sect’s “fortune” (气运). In the Xianxia cosmos, karma isn’t just spiritual; it’s institutional. Bad behavior catches up.

### Self-detonation (自爆)

A suicidal technique where a cultivator detonates their own energy core, releasing a devastating explosion but preserving the soul for reincarnation—a last-ditch act of mercy.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a textbook example of the **“power flex revenge”** trope at its most cathartic. If you’ve been following the Dong Qi storyline and the slow burn of Ji Ning and Yuchi Xiyue’s grief, this is your payoff. The chapter deliberately keeps the perspective tight—we don’t see the dragon strikes from ground level until the end; instead, we watch through the cold, professional eyes of the imperial captains and the dawning horror of the Snowdragon Daoists. The contrast between the quiet banquet at Prince Yan’s estate and the continent-shaping disaster in Anchan is what makes the reading experience sing. One last thing: Dong Qi is alive, captured, and helpless. The real trial hasn’t happened yet. *That’s* what we’re waiting for.
