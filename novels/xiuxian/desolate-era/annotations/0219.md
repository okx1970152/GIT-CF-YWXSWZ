## Chapter Overview

The quiet before the storm. Ji Ning, now a full member of the Yinglong Guard, finally allows the vengeance he has buried for years to surface. But he does not storm off blindly. Instead, he visits his ally Beishan Baiwei in Anchan City to discreetly procure intelligence on the three men who murdered his uncle and parents: Dong Qi, Shui Yi, and Yu Tong. What he finds is a carefully drawn map of obstacles—one enemy is coddled at Snowdragon Mountain’s very core, another hides behind a powerful master at a remote peak, and the third is nestled deep within an enemy clan. The chapter masterfully shifts from simmering hatred to cold, tactical preparation. The plan is set: first Yu Tong, then Shui Yi. The hunt begins.

## Key Plot Points

1. Ji Ning declares his intent to settle the blood debt from his past. His words are quiet but laced with an icy, soul-deep hatred that shocks even his close companions.
2. Mu Northson insists on joining, revealing that he too lost his mother and personally slew her murderer. The bond between the two brothers is sealed in shared trauma.
3. The group heads to Anchan City, where Ji Ning arranges a covert intelligence check through Beishan Baiwei, hiding his trail from the Heavenly Treasures Mountain.
4. The intelligence reveals the three targets: Dong Qi (spoiled grandson of a Primal Daoist, living inside Snowdragon Mountain), Yu Tong (a scheming Wanxiang Adept living at Crippled Moon Peak with his master), and Shui Yi (a former servant of the Dong Clan, now a Zifu Disciple in their territory).
5. Ji Ning analyzes each target’s viability. He decides to kill Yu Tong first, then Shui Yi. Dong Qi is unreachable for now—too deep within the sect.
6. The chapter ends with Ji Ning holding a lethal treasure and ordering a course south toward Crippled Moon Peak. The cold, patient planning gives way to the first step of execution.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Vengeance as a Dao-Heart Tribulation**: In the Xianxia tradition, revenge is not just an emotional release—it is a potential bottleneck or catalyst for a cultivator’s Dao-heart. A grudge left unresolved can fester and block spiritual progress. By finally acting on his hatred, Ji Ning is unblocking a passage he sealed years ago. The narrative treats this as a necessary step in his maturation, not mere melodrama.

**Discreet Intelligence in the Cultivation World**: The Heavenly Treasures Mountain (天宝山) is more than a market—it functions as a semi-public intelligence bureau. Everyone knows information can be bought, so the real skill is in hiding your inquiries. Ji Ning uses Beishan Baiwei as a proxy precisely to avoid tipping off Snowdragon Mountain. This cat-and-mouse over intelligence is a recurring trope in high-level Xianxia, where information is as deadly as any sword.

**The Stratification of Enemies**: The three targets represent a classic Xianxia spectrum of challenge. Dong Qi (inside Snowdragon Mountain’s core) is the unassailable final boss for now—protected by a Primal Daoist grandfather and a sect’s full array of formations. Yu Tong (isolated at a remote peak) is the practical first target, despite having a powerful master. Shui Yi (within a clan territory but under no Primal Daoist guard) is the medium-difficulty target. Ji Ning’s cold assessment of each threat level mirrors the hierarchical, risk-reward thinking drilled into every top cultivator.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a **strategic interlude** before the storm of actual combat. Savor it. The emotional core lies not in the action but in Ji Ning’s quiet confession of hatred to himself and Mu Northson’s raw loyalty. The most rewarding moment is the subtle bonding between the two sword-wielding brothers—both are orphans of vengeance, and their shared understanding bypasses words.

Also, notice how Ji Ning wields his authority as an Yinglong Guard. He doesn’t brute-force his revenge; he uses his new status to access intelligence while still acting outside official channels. This is the hallmark of a pragmatic cultivator who has learned from his early mistakes. The chapter sets up a classic three-tier revenge arc: one target now, one next, and one when he is strong enough to challenge a sect.

**Pro tip for cultivation fiction fans**: Pay close attention to the logistics here. The later payoff—how Ji Ning executes the attacks on Yu Tong and Shui Yi—feels far more satisfying because this chapter established exactly how difficult each target is. The author is setting the board. Enjoy the quiet tension before the swords are drawn.
