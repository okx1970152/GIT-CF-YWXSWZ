## Chapter Overview

Roll up, fellow Daoists! Our young beast-skin-clad sword immortal, Ji Ning, is still hoofing it to Anchan City when he stumbles upon a classic Xianxia roadside ambush: two Snowdragon Mountain thugs are about to finish off three struggling Zifu Disciples. But just when things look dire for the trio, a flash of unseen sword-light turns the hunters into the hunted. Ji Ning not only saves the day with a chillingly efficient kill, but he also gains some travel buddies who are absolutely *thrilled* to have a powerhouse like him as a free bodyguard. This chapter is a fantastic slice-of-life breather that mixes a tense rescue with the politics of clan life, setting the stage for the grand arrival at the legendary Anchan City.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter kicks off in media res with the Meng clan trio—Meng Yan (the honest, self-sacrificing 'Stone'), Meng Jun (the slick talker), and Meng Xin (the sharp-witted beauty)—being overwhelmed by two Snowdragon Mountain cultivators. We learn they are all early-stage Zifu Disciples who deliberately stopped cultivating to preserve their chances of joining a decent sect, a clever bit of setting that shows the ruthlessness of the cultivation world's gatekeeping. Meng Yan, in a desperate bid to buy time, burns a forbidden technique, damaging his foundation and likely ruining his sect prospects.

Just as all hope seems lost, Ji Ning’s hidden flying sword, the Annihilation Sword of Silence, strikes from the ground, instantly killing both assailants. This is a massive flex, showcasing his mastery of Divine Sense and his tactical cunning; he didn't even bother to show up physically. After the rescue, Ji Ning joins the trio for the journey, using the time to pump them for information about the outside world. Meng Jun’s shameless attempt to mooch some free treasures is a highlight, showcasing a perfect minor villain personality. The trio’s backstory is revealed: they are from the oppressed Eastern Bend branch of the powerful Meng clan, forced to flee their home to seek better opportunities. The chapter ends with the group finally laying eyes on the awe-inspiring walls of Anchan City, a moment of shared hope and anticipation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Politics of Clan Branches**: The Meng trio’s situation is a classic Xianxia trope—the 'oppressed side-branch.' In large cultivation clans, resources and techniques are tightly controlled by the main family and stronger branches. The ‘Eastern Bend’ branch is deliberately kept weak to prevent internal rivals. This is why the trio fled; the clan itself is a cage. This adds a layer of depth to the 'leaving the nest' narrative, showing that even elite clans can be toxic environments.

**Forbidden Techniques (禁术)**: Meng Yan’s "forbidden technique" isn't some dark, evil magic. In this context, a forbidden technique is typically a self-sacrificial art that burns one's vital essence or foundation for a temporary power boost. The consequence is a 'Severed Immortal Path' (仙路已断)—a permanent ceiling on growth. It's a brutal calculation: trade your entire future for a chance at present survival. This contrasts heavily with Ji Ning's Fiendgod path, where his body is naturally tougher and doesn't rely on such fragile self-destructive methods.

**The Road to the City**: Traveling on the ground via mount vs. flying via magic treasure is a huge strategic risk/reward decision in this world. Flying is faster but makes you a visible target for robbers, like Snowdragon Mountain. The trio’s fatal mistake was choosing speed over safety—a lesson many young cultivators learn the hard way.

### Severed Immortal Path

A permanent, irreversible ceiling on a cultivator's growth, often due to a crippled spiritual foundation.

### Delegated Awe

A literary device in Xianxia where the power of an off-screen character or location is conveyed through the awed reactions of on-screen characters who have witnessed it.

## Reading Guide

This is a classic 'calm before the storm' chapter, and it's a treat for a few reasons. First, the action scene is beautifully efficient; Ji Ning doesn't even need to be on-screen to dominate, which perfectly underlines his current power level compared to 'average' Zifu Disciples. Second, pay close attention to Meng Jun. He's a fantastic example of a 'small-time schemer'—loud, sycophantic, and greedy, but ultimately harmless and a bit pathetic. His failed attempt to scam Ji Ning is both funny and instructive. Finally, savour the description of Anchan City's distant pressure. The author does a great job of using 'delegated awe' (代位敬畏)—making the city feel legendary and powerful just through the characters' reactions to it. *Get ready, because the real adventure is about to begin once they pass through those gates!*
