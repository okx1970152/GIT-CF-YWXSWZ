## Chapter Overview

Our boy Ji Ning is officially stepping into his next big adventure—but not without a few serious pit-stops first. He's packing up, handing out precious Primordial Liquid to Uncle White and Xiaoqing like a generous dad giving his kids allowances before a business trip, and heading down to the vast, chaotic "Crescent Major World." This isn't a sightseeing tour; it's a trial set by Patriarch Subhuti himself—part recruitment drive, part assassination mission. Down on the Star Continent, we meet three desperate, fleeing royals from a fallen kingdom, who decide to risk their lives in the legendary Evil Dragon River. And guess who's casually boating there, looking for someone just like them? That's right—our man Ji Ning is on the hunt for both high-karma disciples and high-sin targets, and he just hit the jackpot on the first one.

## Key Plot Points

- **Setting Priorities**: Ji Ning decides he must go to the lower cave world alone, leaving his spirit-beasts behind to train on Mount Innerheart. He gives them 150,000 jin each of Primordial Liquid to jump from Primal perfection to the Void stage, showing his foresight and generosity.
- **The Trial’s Purpose**: This is a test set by Patriarch Subhuti: find a disciple and kill someone (the latter likely linked to karmic sin). Ji Ning chooses the chaotic Star Continent, where sin and merit both run rampant.
- **The Heavenly Tribulation Foreshadowing**: Ji Ning reflects on the apocalyptic nature of the Heavenly Tribulation—geniuses face greater trials, and he lists his prerequisites: Eight-Nine Arcane Art, Houyi Archery, Starseizing Hand, treasures, and an unbreakable Dao-heart.
- **The Fallen Royals**: A prince and his two sisters of the Qi Kingdom are being hunted by a demon king. They have no path left but to flee into the notorious Evil Dragon River—a stretch of water so cursed that even flight and escape techniques fail near it.
- **Ji Ning’s Luck**: Through divine sense, he spots a servant girl in their group radiating golden karmic merit for three hundred zhang—a huge find for his recruitment mission. He immediately rows over to intercept them.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Cursed Waterways of Xianxia: The Evil Dragon River**

The Evil Dragon River in this chapter is a perfect example of a recurring Xianxia trope: the "geographical constraint" that strips cultivators of their superhuman mobility. You see this in almost every classic—rivers, mountain ranges, or deserts cursed by ancient battles. Here, the river is said to have been formed from the corpse of an ancient evil dragon (hence the name). The rule is brutal: within 1,000 li of its banks, you can't fly, and you can't use elemental escape techniques. This is a massive equalizer—a Wanxiang Adept and a Houtian warrior are now on the same "ground" level. No cheap teleports, no soaring away. It forces the hunt to turn into a gritty, survival-style chase on foot or by boat, which is exactly what makes it the last hope for our fleeing royals.

**Karmic Merit vs. Sin: The Moral Currency of the Heavens**

The golden light Ji Ning sees on the servant girl isn’t just visual flair—it's a central mechanic in this universe. Karmic merit (功德) is tangible, spiritual gold. Cultivators who accumulate merit find doors opening for them: easier tribulations, better reincarnation, and protection from karmic hellfire. Those with heavy karmic sin (罪孽), on the other hand, face amplified tribulations and literal soul-burning. Ji Ning's mission is a brilliant application of this system. He is hunting "high-sin" demons because killing them is a service to the Heavenly Dao (and might earn him more merit). But he also needs a "high-merit" disciple—someone so pure that they will be easy to teach and blessed by fortune. A fallen noble family's servant with that much golden light is a once-in-a-lifetime find. The irony is delicious: in the chaos, the most valuable treasure is a humble, virtuous soul.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a shift in pacing. After the long, intensive training arc on Mount Innerheart, we’re finally leaving the cozy nest. This chapter is the *launch sequence*. Ji Ning is fully in "pragmatic planner" mode here. Watch how he allocates resources, prioritizes training for his team, and analyzes his own weaknesses (the Heavenly Tribulation won't spare him just because he's a monster). The introduction of the Evil Dragon River is a masterstroke—it immediately signals that the Star Continent operates under different rules than the relatively safe skies of Mount Innerheart or the Grand Xia. This isn't a place where overwhelming power alone solves problems. The terrain itself is a character. And the setup with the royal siblings? Classic Xianxia bait—don't get too attached to background characters because they're usually just stepping stones or tragic backstories, but keep an eye on that golden-light servant girl. She might become more than a side note.
