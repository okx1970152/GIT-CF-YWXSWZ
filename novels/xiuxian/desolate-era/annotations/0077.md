## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because we’ve officially entered the **big leagues**! Ji Ning’s hunting trip has taken a wild turn. After wiping out the grunt-level demonic cultivators in the mountain belly, he now faces their master: **Beisheng**, a genuine **Zifu Disciple** (and not a weak one at that). This isn’t a bully from a minor tribe—Beisheng commands a legion of Dao-soldiers, a brute-force-black-furred zombie, a plague-swarm of golden insects, and a forbidden, half-finished sin artifact. Our eleven-year-old sword-god has his work cut out for him. This chapter is a delicious clash of two very different cultivation philosophies: Yin-Yang poison & summoning versus raw, comprehended Dao intent. Get ready for Ning to be pushed to the absolute edge, learning by doing under lethal pressure.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Reveal of a Zifu Enemy**: Beisheng, upon seeing his base destroyed, immediately identifies Ji Ning as a Zifu Disciple from his ability to fly with magic treasures. He becomes wary but also more determined to eliminate this monstrous child.
- **Testing the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation**: Ji Ning uses the battle to practically test the **Fifth Layer** of the formation. He masterfully applies the principle of "leading" the formation, making it far more efficient to control than during his attempts in the Water Mansion.
- **The Essence-Soul Destroying Swarm**: Beisheng unleashes a terrifying golden insect swarm. Ning's sword beams only kill a few hundred per shot against a horde of hundreds of thousands. His Ki is too limited for an AoE solution.
- **The Defensive Power of the Water-Fire Lotus**: Trapped by the swarm, Ji Ning unveils his independent creation: a tri-layered **Water-Fire Lotus**. The rotating water and fire leaves, infused with a True Meaning of the Dao, grind down thousands of insects, stunning Beisheng with the rarity of such a technique.
- **A Deadlock and a Desperate Gamble**: The insects begin to pierce the final layer of the lotus. Ji Ning is about to abandon defense for a suicidal charge, while Beisheng, growing impatient at the loss of his insects, decides to use his unfinished and dangerous **blood-red cloth banner**—a sin artifact.
- **Climax of Desperation**: In a mirror-image moment, both combatants shout "Die!" Their auras erupt simultaneously. Beisheng shakes his banner while Ji Ning, pushed to the absolute limit of his soul and Ki, manifests **486 flying swords** in a frantic attempt to break into the **Sixth Layer** of the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation!

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The "True Meaning" (道之真意)** is a crucial Xianxia concept making its debut in this battle. It is a cyclic, self-reinforcing insight into a fundamental law of the Dao. Beisheng is shocked that Ji Ning has grasped the True Meaning of the Dripping Sutra (the essence of cyclical water flow) and incorporated it into a defensive technique. In the hierarchy of cultivation, comprehending a True Meaning is the first step beyond "Unity of Man and Heaven" and a prerequisite for forming a Grand Dao. It’s the difference between learning a spell and understanding *why* the spell works—it’s more fundamental and more powerful. Ning didn’t learn it from a book; he *understands* water because he lives with the Crimsonbright Diagram's Taiyin waters every day.

**Zifu Disciple Battlescape**: This chapter perfectly illustrates the "toolbox" nature of a Zifu-level fight. Beisheng doesn’t just have one attack; he uses a zombie (meat-shield/tank), a swarm (crowd control/DoT), and a sin artifact (big DPS button). A cultivator at this stage has had centuries to accumulate means. Fighting them isn't a duel; it's a war with multiple fronts. Ji Ning's advantage is his comprehension of the Dao (his soul's quality and insight) which often surpasses those who merely accumulated power.

**The Dao-Soldier Principle**: Ji Ning uses the "leading" (引领) principle from formation techniques. This is a standard Xianxia mechanic: a complex formation (like 405 swords) is too hard for one soul to control directly. The trick is to designate a few core swords as "leaders"; the practitioner only controls those, and the rest of the formation follows their flow through resonance. It drastically reduces calculational burden and is the mark of a true formation master.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the resource management**: Ji Ning is a Fiendgod who relies on physical recovery and liquid true Ki. He’s used to having a bottomless gas tank. Beisheng is his first real "blue bar" crisis. Notice how Ning constantly calculates his Ki consumption versus the insects’ kill rate. This is a classic "Glass Cannon vs. Swarm" scenario.
- **Don't underestimate the Water-Fire Lotus**: This isn't just a pretty shield. Ji Ning *created* this technique by combining his comprehension of the Crimsonbright Diagram’s dual essence (water & fire) with the True Meaning of Dripping. In the Xianxia world, creating your own protective skill at Xiantian level is a massive flex. It signals "future grandmaster."
- **Pay attention to the symmetrical structure**: Both Ji Ning and Beisheng are pushed to use their ultimate, unfinished, or desperate moves at the exact same moment. This "mirror" showdown tells you the author is setting up a collision where victory depends on who has the deeper Dao foundation, not just more brute force.
- **The 486 swords is the real cliffhanger**: The 5th layer (405 swords) wasn't enough. Ji Ning lunges for the 6th layer in desperation. We don't know if he will succeed, or if the sin banner will overwhelm him. This is the calm before the storm of a world-shaking explosion.
