## Chapter Overview
Welcome back to the cosmic deep freeze! Chapter 466 finds our favorite Sword Immortal, Ji Ning, drifting through the utterly desolate Realm of Annihilation—a featureless, storm-ravaged void that makes Siberia look like a tropical resort. After months of zero contact with any living being, he spots a massive piece of landmass, immediately identifies it as a potential part of the terrifying Ruined Ancient World (a place so dangerous even True Gods think twice before knocking), and, showing some *very* healthy survival instincts, promptly U-turns to get the heck away. But it’s too late! He’s already been spotted by an outpost full of horned, scaly warriors from the “Broken Horn World.” They don't know what he is, they don't know where he came from, but they want to capture him alive to find out. What follows is a classic Xianxia first-contact scenario: a scouting party is dispatched, some cheap tricks backfire, and after a brief, one-sided skirmish, the enemy commander makes a cold, calculated decision to withdraw. This chapter is a wonderful mix of cautious world-building, high-tension atmosphere, and a quick burst of action that showcases Ji Ning’s power *without* a Heaven-and-Earth shattering climax. It’s about the chilling reality of being an explorer in a truly hostile, alien universe.

## Key Plot Points
*   **The Wrong Turn:** Ji Ning spots a landmass in the distance, correctly deduces it’s a fragment of the Ruined Ancient World, and wisely decides to nope out of there.
*   **The Audience:** Unknown to Ji Ning, a hidden outpost of the “Broken Horn World” watches him through a magical mirror. The eighteen seated wardens are curious, cautious, and decide to probe his strength.
*   **The Welcome Party:** Commander Baro sends out ten of his winged, blood-eyed “children” on a void ship with orders to capture the foreigner.
*   **The First Skirmish:** The ten soldiers try to paralyze Ji Ning with a soul-based attack, which fails utterly due to his Fiendgod nature and Dao-heart. They then try to bind him with whips.
*   **The Clean Sweep:** Ji Ning, without even using his Starseizing Hand, effortlessly decapitates the first wave of five thousand-zhang warriors, realizing they aren’t true Fiendgods (who would be much harder to kill).
*   **The Power Burn:** The second wave of five smaller warriors activates a “burning” technique, boosting their strength. They manage to knock Ji Ning back, but he simply uses his superior speed and swordsmanship to decapitate them anyway.
*   **The Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Back on the ship, Baro coolly analyzes Ji Ning’s stats (Earth-class strength, Sky-class speed/technique) and concludes he’s an elite scout, not an unbeatable monster. Still, losing ten breeding stocks isn’t worth continuing the fight. He orders a retreat.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a stellar example of the **"First Contact" Trope** in cross-universe Xianxia, but with a uniquely pragmatic twist. Unlike Western sci-fi where humans and aliens try to communicate through universal translators, here the barrier is one of scale and biology. The Broken Horn World warriors are thousands of zhang tall, see Ji Ning as a tiny, pale “foreigner” (异族), and communicate through the ancient, universal Fiendgod language (神魔语). This instantly frames the encounter not as a meeting of equals, but as a territorial dispute between vastly different sovereign powers.

The enemy commander Baro’s analysis is pure Xianxia logic. He rates Ji Ning’s power using a “Heaven, Earth, Human” tier system (Sky-class 天级, Earth-class 地级), a common ranking method in many cultivation novels that goes beyond simple level numbers to judge specific combat attributes like strength, speed, and technique. He’s disappointed that his soldiers died, but he doesn’t get angry. Instead, he does a cold cost-benefit analysis: “This is an elite scout. He’s not a world-shaking lord, but the potential losses aren’t worth the potential gains.” This is a key hallmark of a sophisticated Xianxia antagonist. He’s not a mindless brute who charges in; he’s a manager of resources, treating his soldiers like “breeding stock” that can be replaced. His decision to retreat is a rational choice, not a cowardly one, which makes the Broken Horn World feel like a real, functioning empire, not just a collection of monster-of-the-week bad guys.

Also, the classic idiom “curiosity killed the cat” is perfectly matched here by Ji Ning’s internal motto. It’s a universal truth of Xianxia: in a world of cosmic horrors, survival favors the cautious.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in **atmospheric tension and power calibration**. The first half is slow, suspenseful horror—the feeling of being alone in the dark, watched by an unknown civilization. The second half is a surgical strike, a quick exhibition of Ji Ning's martial prowess. For the first time since entering the Realm of Annihilation, we see a benchmark for his cultivation. He effortlessly murders warriors that are likely on par with weak Void Fiendgods (返虚神魔). This tells us exactly where he stands relative to the “true elites” of this new cosmos: he can handle the patrol squad, but the “Warden Lord” back in the outpost is a force he wisely avoids.

Pay close attention to the detached, almost clinical way the Broken Horn World treats this encounter. They are not shocked by Ji Ning's strength; they are merely disappointed in their own data collection. This sets a fantastic precedent for the world-building. We are no longer in the Grand Xia Dynasty, where Ji Ning is a monster genius. We are in the vast, hostile wilderness of the cosmos, where he is just another curious variable to be observed and, if necessary, discarded. The chapter’s understated ending—where the enemy simply flies away after a logical cost-benefit analysis—is far more chilling than a full-blown battle. It signifies that they will be back, probably with a much more effective, personalized counter-strategy.
