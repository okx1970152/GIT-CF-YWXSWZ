## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists! After a brutal, triumph-soaked escape from the Yan Mountains, our boy Ji Ning is finally on his way to the big city. But instead of charging in blind, he's doing something shockingly mature: *thinking*. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia strategy. Ning rejects the foolish path of blind revenge, coolly analyzing the power gap between him and his father's murderers. He lays out a pragmatic, three-step plan for world domination: join a sect, gain power, then join the legendary Yinglong Guard. It's a quiet chapter of travel and introspection, but it’s packed with explosive world-building, a glimpse of the wider cultivation society, and a brutal reminder of the dog-eat-dog reality of this world. We also get a taste of the class snobbery that exists even among cultivators—and a cliffhanger that shows the ugly, predatory nature of the 'civilized' world.

## Key Plot Points

1. The Revenge Calculus: Ji Ning reviews the scroll from his father identifying his three targets: the instigator Dong Qi (whose grandfather is a terrifying Primal Daoist) and his two henchmen, Yu Tong and Shui Yi. He resolutely puts revenge on hold, recognizing he has neither the strength nor the intel.
2. The Strategic Roadmap: Ning plans to first enter a top-tier sect (not Snowdragon Mountain) to learn secret arts and a proper Ki Refining technique. Later, he aims to join the Yinglong Guard for even more profound techniques. A mature, layered plan!
3. A Change of Scenery: We experience Ning’s journey towards Anchan City. He maintains a disciplined schedule of flying and resting, showcasing his unshakeable Dao-heart even on the road. He witnesses a giant, opulent flying ship, a display of wealth and power that contrasts sharply with his simple leaf boat and elicits snide comments from the passengers.
4. The Artifact Spirit’s Advice: A brief reminder that even the ancient artifact spirit of the aquatic manor suggested Ning take multiple masters, validating his strategic planning.
5. The Cliffhanger Ambush: Just as they rest, Ning senses a battle. He finds three noble members of the mighty Meng Clan being cornered by two killers from Snowdragon Mountain. The killers discuss killing the weaker, low-status members, revealing the grim, class-based violence of the cultivation world.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a masterful showcase of Xianxia's internal logic. Let's break down Ning's strategic genius:

*   **The Power Hierarchy is the Law:** Ning doesn't just think Dong Qi's grandfather is "strong." He identifies him explicitly as a **Primal Daoist**. In this world, a single stage of cultivation can mean an unbridgeable gulf in power. A Primal Daoist’s **Primfire (元火)** is a fundamental, game-ending technique—it’s a soul-burning flame that a Wanxiang stage cultivator simply has no defense against. This isn't cowardice; it's physics.
*   **The Yinglong Guard as Endgame (应龙卫):** Ning’s goal to join the Yinglong Guard is a huge piece of world-building. The Yinglong Guard is the Grand Xia Dynasty's elite Immortal corps, a paramilitary force that only accepts Wanxiang Adepts (at minimum!). It’s like the CIA crossed with the Space Marines, and it holds far better secrets and techniques than any local sect or even a marquis's estate. It's the ultimate career path for an overachiever.
*   **The Reality of Sects:** Ki Refining techniques are jealously guarded. You can't just buy them; you must swear loyalty to a sect to learn them. Ning's decision to 'sell' his talent for access is the standard, necessary path for any lone genius.
*   **"Delegated Awe" (代位敬畏):** Notice how the power of a Primal Daoist is conveyed? We don't see one fight. We just hear that they have "Primfire." Ning's own fear and respect *for the concept* of that power is what sells the threat. This is a core Xianxia technique: showing power through the reaction of characters who understand the scale.
*   **The Class Divide:** The scene with the opulent ship is a stark reminder of the internal social hierarchy. The women's mockery of Ning's "barbaric" leather clothes is a classic sign of the arrogance of the urban elite, looking down on a talent from the backwoods. It's great dramatic tension.

### Delegated Awe (代位敬畏)

A classic Xianxia literary device where the overwhelming power of an off-screen or unseen character is conveyed through the awed reactions and fear of characters who are on-screen and understand the power scale.

## Reading Guide

This is a setup chapter, but it's the *good* kind of setup—the kind that makes you feel like you're building a war chest for a slaughter. Pay close attention to Ning's inner monologue. His decision to postpone revenge isn't a sign of weakness; it's the defining mark of a protagonist who understands the rules of the game. He's playing the long game, and that’s incredibly satisfying to watch.

Also, keep an eye on the "class snobbery" theme introduced with the boat. In Xianxia, a simple insult from a social superior is rarely just words; it's often the spark that lights a powder keg of conflict. How Ning navigates these social dynamics in the big city will be a fascinating test of his character.

Finally, the cliffhanger with the Meng Clan disciples being hunted by Snowdragon Mountain cultivators is a fantastic hook. It directly pits the 'villainous' sect of his origin story against a noble family of the wider world. This isn't just a random fight; it's a chess piece being moved on the board of the Commandery. Will Ji Ning intervene? The rules of Anchan's jurisdiction are unclear here. This is a perfect set-up for our first major conflict in the new arc.
