## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers the brutal, high-stakes payoff of the Shaoyin clan’s trap. Ji Ning, pushed far beyond his limits, finds himself locked in a desperate stalemate against four Celestial Immortals—each a master of a completely different combat style. The chapter masterfully showcases the terrifying power of a returning Void cultivator armed with the Eight-Nine Arcane Art, as he tanks attacks that would vaporize lesser immortals. But raw durability isn’t victory. The four Celestial Immortals pivot from direct damage to a tactical capture, testing Ji Ning’s resourcefulness and will to the breaking point. It’s a masterclass in Xianxia siege warfare, ending on a cliffhanger that makes you pray the cavalry arrives in time.

## Key Plot Points
– Ji Ning realizes his Grand Thousand Sword Formation (4th layer) cannot overpower the combined might of the Withered Tree and Profound Origin Ancestors. The power gap between a returning Void cultivator and a Celestial Immortal is starkly quantified.
– He crushes his messenger talisman, signaling for help from the Diancai Immortal and the Camel Mountain Celestial Immortal.
– The Golden Bell Ancestor enters the fray with the Shaoyin clan’s heirloom—a pure Yang-ranked top-grade bell that emits devastating golden shockwaves. Ji Ning’s Primaltwin and Mu Northson (in the Yixian puppet) are forced to retreat.
– The Heartseeker Ancestor joins, unleashing a frozen world domain and a guillotine-like ice blade that physically overwhelms Ji Ning, knocking him out of the sky. This attack is revealed to be his signature move from roaming the Three Realms.
– The four Celestial Immortals deduce Ji Ning must be using the Eight-Nine Arcane Art, switching their strategy from killing him to capturing him alive.
– In a desperate gambit, Ji Ning uses the Eight-Nine Arcane Art’s body-splitting ability, sacrificing one clone to recover power and evade the vines, but this consumes a massive 20-30% of his total divine power.
– The chapter ends with Ji Ning cornered, considering hiding in his mobile Immortal mansion, but realizing it’s too fragile to survive a combined assault from four Celestial Immortals.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Eight-Nine Arcane Art (八九玄功):** This chapter is a textbook demonstration of why this divine ability is legendary. It’s more than just passive damage reduction; it allows Ji Ning to physically split his body into two independent living forms, each containing half his soul and power. Sacrificing one clone is a brutal but effective recovery tactic—a core feature of the Art that separates it from mere armor or barriers. It’s a hallmark of Xianxia’s body-refining path: your body is your ultimate weapon and your ultimate escape mechanism.

**Pure Yang-ranked Treasure (纯阳极品法宝):** This is a massive power-up tier. The Shaoyin clan’s golden bell is an heirloom from the Primordial Pangu’s World era. In Xianxia, pure Yang-ranked treasures are often the pinnacle of what can be wielded by Celestial Immortals, marking a clear power boundary where a single artifact can dictate the outcome of a battle. Ji Ning’s ability to tank it is a testament to the Eight-Nine Arcane Art’s insane defensive scaling.

**The Dao of Siege Warfare:** This fight isn’t a brute force clash; it’s a tactical four-man siege. Withered Tree is the control mage (restrain/suppress), Golden Bell is the tank-buster (raw damage), and Heartseeker is the executioner (single-target nuke). The shift from “kill him” to “capture him” is a classic Xianxia tactical retreat, showing that even Celestial Immortals must adapt their strategies against an anomalous foe.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, because this is one of those chapters where the power scaling hits you like a frozen guillotine. Ji Ning has been on a tear recently, but here, the author carefully re-establishes the terrifying hierarchy of the Three Realms. Four Celestial Immortals is an overwhelming force, and watching Ji Ning’s toolbox shrink—his formation, his Primaltwin, his puppet—is a masterclass in tension. Pay close attention to the specific descriptions of each Celestial Immortal’s ability. They aren’t just energy blasts; each one represents a different school of immortal combat (control, suppression, domain, execution). Also, note Ji Ning’s internal calculation of his divine power reserves. In Xianxia, mana management is often more important than theoretical peak output. The climax is a brutal but brilliant tactical readjustment from both sides.

**Deep Dive: The Sacred Art of the Frantic Phone Call**
In Xianxia, the messenger talisman is the original panic button. Ji Ning crushed his early, showing immense situational awareness. A prideful cultivator might have delayed, thinking they could turn the tide. Ji Ning didn’t. He treated it like a fire alarm, not a request for backup. This small action reveals the core of his Dao-heart: he is ruthless with himself and prioritizes survival over ego.
