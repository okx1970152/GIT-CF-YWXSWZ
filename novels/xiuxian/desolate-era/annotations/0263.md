## Chapter Overview
Time to hold your breath, fellow Daoists. After the tension of breaking the immortal estate’s formation and the uneasy alliance with the Shaoyan clan, our heroes finally get a moment of pure, unfiltered Xianxia bliss: **loot**. Ji Ning, the pragmatic Sword Immortal we love, stumbles upon a treasure vault so massive it would make even a Loose Immortal weep with envy. This isn’t just a lucky find—it’s a complete paradigm shift for his cultivation journey. But as the chapter unfolds with clinical precision, we see that Ji Ning’s real superpower isn’t luck. It’s his ability to instantly calculate the strategic implications of a windfall while his rivals stew in their own jealousy. The payoff? A **hundred thousand** Human-ranked treasures, sixteen thousand Earth-ranked, and a stack of Heaven-ranked artifacts that would buy a small commandery. Get ready for a satisfying chapter about pure, earned power progression.

## Key Plot Points
- **Shaoyan Nong claims the Treasure Hall nexus.** After the formation is broken, Xiangliu Fang confirms the jade bed is safe, and Shaoyan Nong begins the process of refining the sub-palace’s core. He announces that his Fiendgod bodyguard won’t be competing for the loot, which is a massive relief for the Black-White College disciples.
- **The search begins.** Ji Ning, Mu Northson, Nine Lotos, Cangjiang the Immortal, and even the Whitewater Hound and Little Qing scatter to explore the vast Treasure Hall. Xue Hongyi trails behind, seething with jealousy.
- **Ji Ning finds the motherlode.** While checking secret chambers one by one, he stumbles into a hidden treasure vault that contains an unimaginable hoard of artifacts—a legacy left by Immortal Wu Jiang for his tribe. It’s a room full of stacked shelves, each holding piles of magic treasures sorted by grade.
- **The scale of the fortune is revealed.** After quickly refining the unclaimed treasures, Ji Ning counts over 180,000 Human-ranked artifacts, over 16,000 Earth-ranked artifacts, and over a hundred Heaven-ranked treasures that he cannot yet refine. His pragmatic Dao-heart immediately kicks in: he calculates the monetary value in Primordial Liquid and realizes he now rivals or surpasses the wealth of ordinary Loose Immortals.
- **Nothing is left behind.** True to his meticulous nature, Ji Ning even takes the shelves and tables—made from valuable Phantom-Sinking Fragrant Wood—leaving the treasure vault completely bare.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The “Windfall Chapter” Trope**: In progression fantasy/Xianxia, there is a special breed of chapter that every reader lives for—the treasure discovery scene. It’s the genre’s equivalent of a power-up sequence, but it’s more than just “character gets stronger.” It represents a **systematic reset** of the protagonist’s resource bottlenecks. In this case, Ji Ning’s immediate internal calculation (the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation needs ~700 Earth-ranked swords? Easy. The Starseizing Hand needs elemental treasures? Can trade.) is classic Xianxia writing: the loot isn’t just a pat on the back; it’s a **tactical upgrade menu**.

**Treasure Grading**: The Human → Earth → Heaven → Immortal → Pure Yang hierarchy is not arbitrary. In the Three Realms, a Primal Daoist (a high-tier cultivator) typically only possesses a few Heaven-ranked treasures. For a mere Wanxiang Adept like Ji Ning to suddenly own over a hundred is a plot-defining “quantum leap” in resource access. It signals that the author is setting the stage for a significant power surge.

**Phantom-Sinking Fragrant Wood (幽魂沉水木)**: This isn’t just an aesthetic detail. In the novel’s economy, 1 jin of this wood is worth 1 tael of Primordial Liquid. With a million jin in the room, Ji Ning essentially took the furniture and turned it into liquid wealth. It’s a classic Xianxia reminder that in the immortal world, even the display stands are worth more than a mortal kingdom.

## Reading Guide
- **Notice the contrast between Ji Ning and Xue Hongyi.** While Xue Hongyi is fuming with jealousy and seeing Ji Ning as lucky, Ji Ning himself is clinically analyzing the value and strategic application of his newfound wealth. This isn’t just a difference in temperament—it’s a difference in **Dao-heart maturity**. Xue Hongyi resents the outcome; Ji Ning calculates the next step.
- **Pay attention to the “clinical analysis” tone.** The way Ji Ning breaks down his loot into variables (Human-ranked, Earth-ranked, unrefinable Heaven-ranked) and immediately ties it to his technique requirements is a hallmark of his pragmatic personality. He doesn’t bask in glory—he **upgrades his combat loadout in his head**.
- **The theme of “fair reward.”** Compare this to the earlier treasure distribution. Ji Ning earned this find because his spirit-beast was key to breaking the grand formation. The narrative rewards merit, not just luck. This is a core Xianxia value: fortune favors the prepared, and it doubly favors the one who brings the right strategy.
- **A small but telling moment**: Ji Ning’s excited babbling (“A Celestial Immortal’s leg!”) is a rare crack in his usual composure. This is the only time in the chapter he lets his guard down, and it’s directed at the treasures. It’s a humanizing note that makes his overwhelming success feel earned and relatable.
