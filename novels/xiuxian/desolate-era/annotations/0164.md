## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to the *Dao-Repository Palace Shopping Spree*! Chapter 164 is a milestone for any Xianxia fan—the chapter where our protagonist finally gets access to the sect's premium loot table. Ji Ning has officially graduated from a starving tribal disciple to a Black-White College elite with six thousand Black-White Pearls burning a hole in his pocket. But as he quickly discovers, the gap between *being* a genius and *affording* the tools of a genius is depressingly vast. This chapter is a deep dive into the economics of cultivation: from Immortal-ranked techniques that cost more than a small tribe’s net worth, to the five Divine Abilities that will make any reader’s wallet (and heart) ache. It’s also a perfect showcase of classic Xianxia worldbuilding—the strict hierarchy of techniques, the brutal rarity of Divine Abilities, and the ever-present shadow of the Celestial Immortal bottleneck. Get ready for a chapter about lists, prices, and the crushing weight of the Heavenly Dao’s paywall.

## Key Plot Points

1. **Resource Distribution**: Ji Ning reports to the Dao-Repository Palace and receives his standard disciple allowance—6,000 Black-White Pearls and 100 catties of Primordial Liquid. The guardian casually explains that this is a *ten-year* quota for third-generation disciples, immediately establishing the wealth disparity between sect disciples and scattered cultivators.
2. **The Three-Floor Structure**: Ji Ning learns the organizational logic of the Dao-Repository Palace: Floor 1 = Techniques (cultivation methods), Floor 2 = Arts (Divine Abilities, sword-arts, spells, forbidden arts), Floor 3 = Miscellaneous (formations, alchemy, constructs).
3. **Selection of Ki Refining Technique**: After browsing, Ji Ning settles on the *Aquatic Source Annotation*, an Immortal-ranked water-aligned technique costing 1,500 Black-White Pearls for the first volume. He calculates that he needs a *second* copy for Uncle White, doubling the cost to 3,000.
4. **Crimsonbright Diagram, Volume Two**: Ji Ning finds the continuation of his Fiendgod Body Refining method for a paltry 100 Black-White Pearls—a stark reminder that the cheapest technique is also the hardest to master.
5. **The Five Divine Abilities**: Ji Ning reaches the treasure trove of the second floor and discovers the five Divine Abilities hoarded by the College over endless eras: *Heavenly Transformation*, *Divine Thunder Eye*, *Ten Thousand Poison Stingers*, *Three Heads, Six Arms*, and *Blazing Heart Eye*. All are prohibitively expensive; he can only afford *Heavenly Transformation* (500) and puts it aside.
6. **The Soul-Shattering Art**: Ji Ning’s search culminates in the discovery of an ultra-rare soul-based attack technique—the *Soul-Shattering Art*. Though its price is not stated, its placement alongside the Divine Abilities screams "astronomically expensive."

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Dao-Repository Palace as a Trope**
A “sect treasury” scene is a staple of the progression fantasy genre, serving two narrative purposes: it establishes the sect’s power by dangling legendary treasures, and it forces the protagonist to make agonizing economic choices. Ji Ning’s dilemma—which Divine Ability to sacrifice for a better spell, or whether to spend on a spirit-beast—perfectly mirrors a classic RPG character-building screen. Western readers familiar with loot systems will instantly grasp the tension.

**The Price of Techniques**
Immortal-ranked techniques (仙阶) are the sect’s crown jewels; they *must* be gated behind Black-White Pearls and blood oaths to prevent theft. The mention that "stealing" a technique triggers a Heavenly Dao Blood Oath punishment of *soul annihilation* is a classic Xianxia deterrent—in a world where gods exist, copyright law is enforced by the fundamental laws of reality itself. This also explains why genius cultivators rarely just steal techniques from ruins: the cost of breaking the oath outweighs the benefit.

**Three Heads, Six Arms (三头六臂) vs. Heavenly Transformation (法天象地)**
These two Divine Abilities represent opposite combat philosophies. *Three Heads, Six Arms* is the ultimate force-multiplier for close-quarters combat—it does not increase your strength, but it lets you *weaponize* it three times over. It is favored by sword-saints and tacticians. *Heavenly Transformation*, per our locked glossary rule, is a sacred emulation of heaven and earth, making your body a planet-sized vessel of raw mass. Ji Ning’s choice here (Heavenly Transformation) signals that his current bottleneck is *survivability*, not damage output. He needs to not die before he can hit harder.

**The Blockade of the Celestial Immortal**
The narrator casually mentions that Black-White College has produced only one Celestial Immortal in its history. Let that sink in. This is a sect with Immortal-ranked techniques, Divine Abilities, and Primordial Liquid—yet the breakthrough rate to true immortality is virtually nil. The path to immortality is not about resources; it’s about *Dao-heart comprehension*. Even Immortal Beixing, a legendary genius, failed. This is the invisible wall that separates ordinary cultivators from true legends.

### Dao-King Fu

A colloquial term for an extremely rare sword-art technique; the *Three-Foot Sword* is an Immortal Beixing legacy.

### Spiritbeast (灵兽)

An intelligent, cultivatable beast companion. Ji Ning’s Uncle White is a spiritbeast worthy of Immortal-level investment.

### Heartforce (心力)

A rare, will-based power system; the *Soul-Shattering Art* appears to be its forerunner or a closely related soul-attack technique.

### Seamless Gate (无间门)

The secret enemy faction that orchestrated Ji Ning’s mother’s poisoning; its Godking-level agents lurk in sects across the Grand Xia.

## Reading Guide

**Xianxia Economics 101**: Pay close attention to the prices. 6,000 Black-White Pearls sounds like a fortune until you realize a single Immortal-ranked technique costs 3,000 (for you and your beast), a single Divine Ability costs 500–5,000, and a Heaven-ranked magic treasure costs 10,000. Ji Ning is already *broke* after buying just one technique and one Divine Ability. This sets up a delicious narrative tension: he will *have* to earn more through missions, trials, or treasure-hunting. The *Soul-Shattering Art* being placed next to Divine Abilities all but confirms it will be the next major hurdle.

**The Uncle White Factor**: Ji Ning’s willingness to spend an *equal* amount for his spirit-beast tells you everything about his character. In a setting where most cultivators treat their spirit-beasts as disposable tools, Ji Ning considers Uncle White family. This is a huge red flag for future plot hooks: his generosity will either reward him or trap him.

**The Arsenal Build**: Ji Ning just locked in *Water Ki Refining*, *Crimsonbright Body Refining*, *Heavenly Transformation*, and a *Sword Art*. He’s building a fast, agile, regen-heavy playstyle. Keep an eye on the unclaimed Divine Abilities—*Three Heads, Six Arms* is practically screaming "future Ji Ning upgrade."

**The Three-Floor Mystery**: The fact that the Dao-Repository Palace has a dedicated “Miscellaneous” floor for formations and alchemy is a quiet reminder that Xianxia combat is not just about raw stats. The best cultivators often win via formations, poison, or artifacts, not just bigger swords. Ji Ning’s focus on purely combat arts at this stage suggests he’s relying on his natural talent rather than versatility—a weakness that may be exposed later.
