## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a brutal lore bomb and a gut-punch all in one. Ji Ning returns to the Western Prefecture not in triumph, but with a ticking clock hanging over his mother’s head. The cold, pragmatic realities of the Xianxia world come crashing down as Adept Mu Xiao drops a massive info dump about the terrifying Snowdragon Mountain, while simultaneously delivering a death sentence to Yuchi Snow. The chapter masterfully weaves worldbuilding with raw, visceral emotional stakes, revealing the hidden, painful sacrifices that made Ji Ning’s very existence possible. It’s a stark reminder that in this world, every ounce of power comes at a catastrophic price, and not all debts can be repaid with a sword.

## Key Plot Points

*   **Exposition on Snowdragon Mountain:** During the flight back, Adept Mu Xiao explains that Snowdragon Mountain is a top-tier power in the Anchan Commandery, with multiple Primal Daoists. He describes it as a chaotic, meritocratic melting pot of righteous, demonic, and evil techniques, which is why it's so powerful but also produces villains like Beisheng.
*   **Homecoming and Aftershocks:** The leaf arrives at the Western Prefecture, shocking Ji Yichuan and Ji Jiuhuo with the return of the missing Xiantian lifeforms, all of whom have crippled Dantians. The clan leader is horrified by the scale of the attack.
*   **The Mortal Sentence:** Adept Mu Xiao examines Yuchi Snow and delivers a chilling diagnosis: she is not sick, but her "life force is nearly exhausted." She has only three months to live.
*   **Unveiling the Past:** Ji Yichuan is forced to reveal the truth. Yuchi Snow was a Xiantian perfect powerhouse. She was gravely injured in a past catastrophe, her Dantian destroyed. She then burned her own lifespan with a secret art to save the unborn Ji Ning.
*   **The Impossible Cure:** Adept Mu Xiao explains that while pills to extend a cultivator’s lifespan are common, extending a mortal's life is exponentially harder. Only a "divine pill" from an Immortal can save her, leaving Ji Ning’s heart feeling as cold as ice.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Life-Burning Secret Arts (燃烧寿命的秘术):** This is a classic trope in Xianxia, highlighting the immense value of a lineage or a prodigy child. The "cost" of a powerful birth is almost always paid by the mother. Here, it’s not just a plot device; it's a fundamental law of the universe. You cannot defy the Heavenly Dao without paying a price. Yuchi Snow’s sacrifice perfectly illustrates the Xianxia concept of "karma" (因果) – her merit in creating a genius son is immense, but her sin of reversing life's natural order is paid for with her own vitality. This act, a mix of profound love and suicidal desperation, is a core part of her character.

**The Yinglong Guard vs. The Sects (应龙卫 vs. 宗门):** This chapter perfectly illustrates the stratified power structure of the Grand Xia. A mighty sect like Snowdragon Mountain, with its several Primal Daoists, is treated as a local gang. The Yinglong Guard, the Dynasty's own super-agency, is portrayed as a force so overwhelmingly powerful that a single member (like Adept Mu Xiao) can casually threaten to obliterate the entire sect with a "Loose Immortal." This isn't arrogance; it's a statement of fact about how the world works. The Guards are the ultimate law, and the sects are just elements within that law.

**The Tragic Weight of a Shattered Dantian (丹田被毁):** In the world of cultivation, a shattered Dantian is a fate worse than death for a cultivator. It’s the physical manifestation of being cut off from the Dao. For Yuchi Snow, a Xiantian perfect expert, having her Dantian destroyed and being reduced to a mortal wasn't just a physical injury; it was a spiritual and psychological death. The fact that she survived and managed to raise a child—let alone a legendary genius—speaks to an indomitable will that the novel only now begins to hint at.

### Life-Burning Secret Arts

Forbidden techniques in cultivation that exchange a cultivator's lifespan or vitality for a powerful effect; used here by Yuchi Snow to save her unborn child at the cost of her own health.

### Shattered Dantian

The destruction of a cultivator's primary energy center in their abdomen; an injury that cuts them off from their Ki Refining path and is nearly impossible to heal, effectively crippling their cultivation.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the turning point where Ji Ning’s personal journey expands from revenge against a demon to a desperate quest against the universe itself. Pay close attention to the new "range" of problems. He can kill a Xiantian demon with his own sword, but that sword is useless against the ravages of time and life force. The solution—a divine pill from an Immortal—is a goal so impossibly high it makes the might of Snowdragon Mountain seem like a trivial annoyance. This sets the stage for Ji Ning’s core motivation for the next major arc: he must grow strong enough to enter the wider world and find a way to save his mother. He can no longer just be a talented boy in the Yan Mountains; he must become a force capable of challenging Heaven itself.

## The Great Powers Chessboard

Let's take a moment to admire the subtle geopolitics dropped in this chapter. The Anchan Commandery isn't just a region; it's a chessboard. The Ji Clan is a local feudal lord, Snowdragon Mountain is a powerful, semi-independent guild, and the Yinglong Guard is the Emperor’s secret police. The fact that the Ji Clan could be nearly crippled by a Zifu Disciple from Snowdragon Mountain (Beisheng) without the sect facing immediate reprisal shows that the Yinglong Guard operates on a "response, not prevention" model. They are the nuclear deterrent, not the local police force. This explains why the Ji Clan needs to grow its own power, just as Snowdragon Mountain grows its network of branches. It’s a system built on the threat of overwhelming force, where safety is never guaranteed, only earned.
