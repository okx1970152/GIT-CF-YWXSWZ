## Chapter Overview

Ji Ning has fully taken control of the World Prison, and now he faces a strategic problem: how to upgrade his golden core from the lowest grade to the second. His newly soul-searched prisoner, Liangqiu, lays out the cold, ruthless arithmetic of survival inside these eternal barriers. The weakest prisoners are Celestial Immortals—too poor in knowledge and treasures. The strongest—Elder Gods who draw power from chaos itself—are too dangerous. The sweet spot? True Immortals and True Gods. They have the wealth, the techniques, and the desperation. But Ji Ning must first test his theory against one very proud, very angry True Immortal named Dongjun. The result is a brutal lesson in Xianxia siege warfare: raw power means nothing when the attacker controls the supply lines.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning questions Liangqiu about methods to upgrade his golden core. She explains that the best targets are True Immortals and True Gods—they possess the knowledge and the chaos treasures, but they cannot draw power from the chaos to sustain prolonged combat.
- Liangqiu proposes a brutally pragmatic strategy: attack from outside the formation barriers, drain the prisoners' power through forced combat, and either force their submission or kill them for their treasures.
- Ji Ning reflects that he would never dare fight a True God (Daofather-level) outside this prison, but the World Prison’s unique geometry makes it possible.
- He search-sweeps six more Celestial Immortals and Empyrean Gods; two go mad and die trying to kill him (failing against his Arcane Art defense), while four welcome submission.
- Ji Ning confronts True Immortal Dongjun, a prideful being used to commanding billions. Dongjun scorns Ji Ning as a "lowly worm" and the weakest grade of True Immortal.
- Ji Ning unleashes the ninth-level Grand Qian Sword Formation with 729 flying swords. Dongjun effortlessly shatters it with a single golden beam from his eyes—demonstrating the chasm between their raw power.
- However, Ji Ning realizes that every block costs Dongjun Immortal energy. The True Immortal is forced to turtle inside a bowl-shaped treasure to conserve power.
- Ji Ning switches tactics, deploying the Eight-Fires Cosmos—a treasure that burns the enemy with minimal energy cost to himself. He has found the perfect siege method.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### The World Prison: A Prisoner’s Economy of Energy

This chapter illustrates one of the most fascinating and brutal mechanics in high-level Xianxia: the **energy economy of imprisonment**. On the surface, Ji Ning is vastly outclassed—Dongjun can shatter his strongest attack with a glance. Yet Ji Ning holds all the cards because he controls the *supply lines*.

Outside the World Prison, cultivators regenerate power by absorbing ambient cosmic energy. Inside the prison, that flow is cut off. Prisoners survive on stored divine pills (Pure Yang pills), which are a finite resource. Every spell cast, every treasure activated, every breath of defensive energy drains that finite reserve. Ji Ning understands that Xianxia combat isn't just about who hits harder—it's about who runs out of fuel first. This is a pure **war of attrition**, and Ji Ning, sitting pretty outside the formation barriers, can afford to wait eons. Dongjun cannot.

This energy-economy logic is a recurring theme in cultivation epics. A cultivator's "stamina" or "mana pool" is often treated as a tactical resource, and battles between equals frequently devolve into who can sustain their output longer. The World Prison simply magnifies this principle to an absurd, life-or-death extreme.

### True Immortal vs. True God: The Hierarchy Within the Hierarchy

The chapter clarifies a critical bit of power scaling. In the World Prison's taxonomy:
- **Celestial Immortals / Empyrean Gods**: Bottom tier. Low status, few treasures, bound by life oaths that prevent them from sharing high-level knowledge.
- **True Immortals / True Gods**: The "sweet spot." Powerful enough to possess valuable techniques and chaos treasures, but still reliant on finite divine pills for survival. Most cannot yet draw power directly from the chaos.
- **Ancestral Immortals / Elder Gods**: Apex predators. They *can* draw power from the chaos, making the attrition strategy useless. Ji Ning must avoid them entirely.

This maps neatly to the Three Realms hierarchy—a True Immortal (Pure Yang) is roughly the equivalent of a Daofather-level presence. Dongjun's casual dismissal of Ji Ning's 729-sword Grand Qian Formation is a visceral reminder of the gap between a newly-Pure-Yang cultivator and a veteran True Immortal.

### Heavenly Lock

The unbreakable chains of the World Prison; bound to the truesoul, not the physical body, making escape impossible even for Elder Gods.

### Indestructible Body

A six-level body-protection divine ability that surpasses the Eight-Nine Arcane Art; the first three levels match the Arcane Art's peak.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow Daoists, strap in—because this chapter is a masterclass in *strategy over strength*. Ji Ning just got handed the single most useful piece of information for his prison-break arc: he doesn't need to be stronger than his enemies. He just needs to be *more patient*.

Watch how Ji Ning shifts gears mid-fight. He starts with raw firepower (the Grand Qian Formation), realizes it's too energy-inefficient, and immediately swaps to the Eight-Fires Cosmos—a "set it and forget it" method of attrition. That's the mark of a true cultivator: not just having a big stick, but knowing when to put it down and pick up a slow-burn flame instead.

Also, keep an eye on Dongjun's character. He's arrogant, sure, but he's no fool. He realizes the trap within three exchanges. The bowl treasure is a smart response—turtling to conserve energy. But against the Eight-Fires Cosmos, turtling just means slow cooking. The real question is: how long can his pride hold out before he begs for mercy? Next chapter should answer that.

One more thing: note the tragic fate of the two Empyrean Gods who went mad and attacked Ji Ning. Their deaths are a Xianxia reminder: adapting to a new reality is a survival skill just as important as divine power. The four who submitted? They live. The two who couldn't accept the fall of their nation? They're gone.

## Deeper Dive: The World Prison's Place in Xianxia Cosmology

The World Prison (世界牢狱) is more than just a plot device—it's a microcosm of the entire Xianxia power structure. In many cultivation epics, imprisonment of transcendent beings is a recurring trope, but the logistics are rarely explored. Here, the author I Eat Tomatoes (IET) goes deep into the *physics of confinement*.

A cultivator above the True Immortal level is essentially a self-sustaining cosmic engine. They can draw power from chaos, regenerate from a single drop of blood, and live for tens of thousands of Chaos Cycles. Ordinary prisons are meaningless. The only way to hold them is to sever their connection to the cosmic energy supply—and that's exactly what the World Prison does. It's a metaphysical quarantine, a bubble of isolation where the fundamental laws of energy exchange are broken.

This makes the Prison a kind of inverted xianxia paradise for the protagonist. Normally, Ji Ning would be prey to anyone above his level. Here, he's the apex predator—because he controls the environment. It's a brilliant inversion of the standard "power-level stack" narrative, and it gives Ji Ning a chance to punch *way* above his weight class through pure cunning.
