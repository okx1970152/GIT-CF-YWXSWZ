## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists—because the training wheels just came off. In this chapter, Ji Ning gets the grand tour of the aquatic palace’s four inner halls: the Divine Ability Hall, the War God Hall, the Star Hall, and the treasure-filled Treasure Hall. The yellow bear artifact spirit drops chilling warnings about danger and opportunity, while the black ox gives us the real lowdown on the rules of the game. The big revelation? Magic treasures aren’t just shiny sticks—they have a five-tier ranking system (Human, Earth, Heaven, Immortal, Pure Yang), and getting your hands on them requires either leveling up your Fiendgod Body Refining or braving the ten-floor death gauntlet known as the War God Hall. And spoiler alert: Ji Ning’s current chances of clearing floor one? Less than ten percent. The kid’s got grit, but the road ahead just got a whole lot steeper.

## Key Plot Points

- The yellow bear explains that Ji Ning must reach Zifu in Fiendgod Body Refining within ten years to prove his potential and qualify for a single attempt at the Divine Ability Hall. Failure means losing the divine ability forever.
- The War God Hall is introduced as the most dangerous yet most rewarding place in the palace, its bronze door radiating blood-red killing intent.
- The Star Hall is revealed to be the key to Celestial Immortal Juhua’s millions of years of life—a place where complete Daos are laid out for comprehension.
- The artifact spirit vanishes, leaving the black ox as Ji Ning’s primary guide.
- In the Treasure Hall, Ji Ning reads the rules inscribed in Fiendgod script: treasures are granted either by advancing one’s Fiendgod Body Refining realm or by clearing the War God Hall’s floors.
- The five treasure grades are detailed, along with the fact that Zifu disciples in backwater regions like the Yan Mountains often only possess low- or medium-grade Human treasures.
- The black ox explains that even Celestial Immortal Juhua couldn’t obtain an Immortal-ranked treasure until he cleared the seventh floor of the War God Hall as a Loose Immortal.
- The black ox reveals his own identity: an Immortal-ranked flying sword forged from the materials of a defeated enemy, the Thousand Sword Immortal.
- Ji Ning is told he has two chances per realm to challenge the War God Hall (ten total before becoming an Empyrean God), but his current odds of clearing floor one are under ten percent.
- The chapter ends with the black ox asking Ji Ning whether he wants to try the War God Hall or head to the Star Hall to cultivate enlightenment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Five Treasure Grades**: This chapter explicitly introduces the standard Xianxia loot hierarchy. In most cultivation novels, items are ranked by a system like Mortal → Human → Earth → Heaven → Immortal → Pure Yang. The key takeaway: a treasure’s tier determines who can use it. A Human-ranked treasure requires a Zifu Disciple’s soul to activate; an Immortal-ranked treasure can only be wielded by a Void-level cultivator (or a Loose Immortal who has honed their power over centuries). This creates a natural power ceiling—a young genius can’t just pick up a god-killing sword and one-shot the villain. The system enforces the rule that *realm must match equipment*.

**Fiendgod Body Refining vs. Ki Refining in Inheritance Design**: Notice that the palace’s reward system only cares about Fiendgod Body Refining. This is a deliberate authorial choice reflecting a common Xianxia trope: ancient Fiendgod inheritances prioritize raw, physical supremacy over fragile Ki Refining. It ties back to the primordial era when Fiendgods shattered stars with their bare hands and didn’t care about fancy magic. For our protagonist Ji Ning, who practices both paths, this bias adds extra weight to his Fiendgod progression.

**The War God Hall’s Ten Floors**: Ten-layer towers are a classic Xianxia testing structure (think Dragon Ball’s Korin Tower or countless sect trials). Each floor is exponentially harder than the last. Even the first floor is designed to filter out 90% of Xiantian-stage challengers, as the black ox warns. This sets a brutal baseline—Ji Ning’s swordplay may be impressive against local demons, but the palace was built by beings who saw planets as mere pebbles.

## Reading Guide

This is a textbook “system introduction” chapter—the kind that hardcore cultivation fans live for. If you’re new to the genre, don’t glaze over when the black ox starts listing treasure grades. This info is *gold*. It tells you exactly how the author plans to gate Ji Ning’s power progression. He can’t just luck into an OP artifact; he has to earn it with blood, sweat, and probably a few broken bones. The ten-percent odds are also a classic Xianxia motivator—it sets up a clear “underdog must train harder” arc. Watch for how Ji Ning reacts: he doesn’t complain, he just absorbs the information and starts calculating. That’s the mark of a true Dao heart. Also, keep an eye on the Star Hall—places with “complete Daos” are basically cheat codes for enlightenment. Ji Ning’s next major power-up might come from sitting under a waterfall in a thatched hut rather than swinging a sword.

Listen to him carefully—his advice is probably the best thing Ji Ning has going for him right now.
