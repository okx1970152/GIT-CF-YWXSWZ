## Chapter Overview
The celebration banquet turns sour as Shao Yanong’s mask of humility slips completely. Fresh from claiming the Immortal Estate, he wastes no time testing boundaries—first asking Nine Lotos to dance, then physically pulling her close for a toast. Ji Ning’s reaction is immediate and explosive: he shatters his table, confronts the young master to his face, and ignites a powder-keg conflict over status, respect, and the core question of what a Dao-companion should—and should not—endure. Buckle up, because the tension that’s been simmering in the Wu Jiang Immortal Estate finally boils over.

## Key Plot Points
- Xue Hongyi desperately tries to ingratiate himself with Shao Yanong, still scheming to escape with his life and Immortal-ranked treasure.
- The group fakes drinking the wine, using ki and Earthfire to destroy it without ingesting anything.
- Shao Yanong orders two seductive female Zifu Disciples to dance, setting a frivolous, objectifying tone for the feast.
- He then asks Nine Lotos to dance; she initially demurs, but Shao Yanong pressures her by invoking his “face,” and she eventually agrees.
- Uncle White warns Ji Ning that Shao Yanong’s complete disregard for him during the request is a deliberate provocation.
- Nine Lotos reveals hidden talents as an exquisite dancer, surprising Ji Ning by how perfectly she performs.
- When Nine Lotos passes near him, Shao Yanong physically grabs her arm, pulling her closer for a private toast—the trigger for Ji Ning’s outburst.
- Ji Ning smashes his treasure-table, the shockwave shredding dishes and even cutting a nearby Zifu Disciple’s body.
- Ji Ning openly rebukes Shao Yanong for hypocrisy, contrasting the pre-Estate humility with post-Estate arrogance.
- Nine Lotos frantically tries to mediate, sending mental messages telling Ji Ning he is being “childish” and that such social games are normal.
- Shao Yanong internally dismisses Ji Ning as an easy mark, deciding not to kill him now for fear of political backlash from the Dongyan clan.
- After the feast, Nine Lotos apologizes and concedes she won’t do it again, but Ji Ning’s Dao-heart feels a deep crack—their worldviews are fundamentally incompatible.
- Shao Yanong’s gaze shifts to Yu Wei, revealing he harbors an even stronger, more predatory desire for her.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Face (Face / Mianzi)**  
The entire banquet scene is a choreographed dance of “face” (面子). Shao Yanong has absolute face as the conqueror of the Immortal Estate; he tests how much he can demand without resistance. When he asks Nine Lotos to dance “for his face,” he is leveraging social protocol to force submission. Ji Ning’s table-shattering is an extreme loss of face for Shao Yanong—but Ji Ning doesn’t care, because his personal Dao-heart code values Nine Lotos’s dignity over political etiquette.

**The Dao-Companion Bond (Dao-Companion / 道侣)**  
A Dao-companion in Xianxia is more than a spouse; they are a shared foundation for the Dao. For Ji Ning, this bond is sacred and exclusive—allowing another man to physically handle his Dao-companion is a violation of the Dao itself. Nine Lotos treats it as a political trifle; Ji Ning treats it as a spiritual insult. This is not just a lovers’ quarrel; it is a clash of fundamental values: Pragmatic statesmanship vs. absolute personal loyalty.

**The Young Master Archetype (Young Master / 公子)**  
Shao Yanong perfectly embodies the classic Xianxia “Young Master”: confident, arrogant, and accustomed to having every whim indulged. He tests boundaries not because he’s irrational, but because testing is how his world works. He calculates risk (killing Ji Ning = problem with Dongyan) vs. reward (humiliating a genius = pleasure). This cold, political calculation makes him far more dangerous than a simple hothead.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in tension-building without a single fight scene. The explosive violence is compressed into a single table-shattering, but the real damage is psychological: the fracture between Ji Ning and Nine Lotos opens wide, and readers should pay close attention to *why* each character acts the way they do. Notice how Nine Lotos’s concession at the end doesn’t fix the problem—it just paper over it. Also, keep an eye on Shao Yanong’s final glance at Yu Wei. That’s not a throwaway line; it’s a storm warning. The political game in the Immortal Estate is far from over, and Ji Ning’s patience is wearing thin.
