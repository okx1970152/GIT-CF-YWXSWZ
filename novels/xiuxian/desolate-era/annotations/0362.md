## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow cultivators, let’s take a deep breath and ascend the Divine Palace of Immortals! Our boy Ji Ning just discovered that learning from the Patriarch isn’t just a matter of sitting at the master’s feet and nodding sagely. Oh no. The Divine Palace is a nine-layer cake of pure power, and each slice requires a bizarre, seemingly arbitrary chore: sweeping a path, wiping tables, pulling weeds for a month… planting fruit trees for half a year! It feels like a cosmic maintenance gig at first glance, but Ji Ning, ever the pragmatist, immediately senses the catch. This chapter is a masterclass in worldbuilding through constraint: revealing that the Divine Palace’s true test isn’t for the patient drudge, but for the warrior who can *prove* their Dao.

## Key Plot Points
Silvermoon Brother unveils the Divine Palace’s dual-track trial system. The first track is pure, unadulterated labor and servitude—the “Trials of Service”—that scales exponentially from half a day to a thousand years at the eighth floor. The second track, however, is for the sword-happy genius: the “Trials of Combat.” Ji Ning learns of the nine puppets in the courtyard, each representing a floor. Beat a first-level puppet once, get a first-floor art. The catch, explicitly stated by Silvermoon, is brutal and absolute: *no divine abilities, no formations, no forbidden arts.* It’s a test of **Dao comprehension** alone, a pure sword clash unfiltered by raw strength. Ji Ning, ignoring the lower floors’ peasant work, boldly picks the **sixth-level puppet** as his starting point, a leap of logic that earns Silvermoon’s approving smile.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The philosophy of “Dao cannot be lightly taught” (*道不可轻传*) is laid bare here. In traditional Xianxia, a master does not just hand out techniques like candy to lazy students. The act of sweeping paths and weeding for months is a profound cultivation ritual in disguise—a test of *sincerity* and *endurance*. It separates the entitled from the dedicated. However, Ji Ning, as a reincarnated soul with a warrior’s heart, bypasses the peasant trials for the true Xianxia trial: **combat**. This is a core trope. The sword is the measure of the man. The puppets scaling their power to match the cultivator’s level is a classic “mirror match” device, forcing the fighter to confront their own Dao, not their energy level. The hierarchy of techniques—from mere “Pure Yang” to the legendary “Grand Dao” level—also hints at the mind-boggling gulf between mortal Immortals and the True Gods of the Three Realms.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for one of the most satisfying payoffs in the early Mount Innerheart arc. This chapter sets all the rules on the table, and then Ji Ning, with his characteristically quiet confidence, just picks a fight with the sixth-level puppet right away. The tension isn’t in *if* he wins, but *how*—and what that victory says about his current understanding of the Dao. For readers, this is the moment the story pivots from “training montage” to “real combat.” If you were wondering why the Patriarch was so impressed with Ji Ning’s sword-heart earlier, this upcoming battle is your answer. Buckle up; a sword is about to speak volumes.
