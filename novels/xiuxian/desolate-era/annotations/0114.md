## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers exactly what its title promises: a crushing, overwhelming beatdown. Ji Ning, fresh from his breakthrough in the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation, turns his attention to the pocket of six surviving Zifu Disciples holed up inside an elaborate defensive formation. After a brief information relay from Patriarch Ji Jiuhuo, Ning calmly walks up to them and, with a combination of rain-based Dao attacks, the Water-Fire Lotus, and seven hundred flying swords, dismantles their five-layer defense piece by piece. The chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia “face-smashing”—the young genius proving that comprehension of the Dao trumps cheap tricks and numbers.

## Key Plot Points
- Patriarch Ji Jiuhuo transmits the composition of the six defenders: Dongzi Qi (Eight Trigrams Blood Dragon Formation), Musi (Green Vines), an eagle-beaked cultivator (formation flags), a black-robed woman (poisonous insects), a gray-robed elder (beast soul banner), and a sixth who remains hidden.
- Ji Ning confirms his strategy to finish them quickly, fearing that Nong Zidao (an enemy Wanxiang Adept) might find a way to break the prison formation.
- The six set up a layered defense: an outer ring of poisonous insects, a white light tower formation, green vines wrapped around the tower, roaming blood dragons, and beast soul specters.
- Ning’s rain attack begins—every strand of rain is charged with the Dao, instantly slaughtering the poisonous insects.
- The cultivators debate whether this is a “Dao Domain,” finding it unthinkable for a sixteen-year-old.
- Ji Ning approaches openly, surrounded by his full power. Musi’s strengthened vines cannot even reach him; the Water-Fire Lotus shreds them.
- The dragon tail smashes the white light tower formation; the rain sweeps away the formation flags.
- The gray-robed elder unleashes beast souls, which are purified instantly by the Nuwa statue in Ning’s sea of consciousness.
- Finally, Ji Ning unleashes the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation in the form of raindrops, killing all six—Musi’s head is the first to fly.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Dao Domain (道之域境):** A major milestone in a cultivator’s understanding of the Dao. Those who reach this level can overlay their comprehension onto the environment, causing natural phenomena to obey their will. The fact that Ji Ning, a mere Zifu Disciple, has achieved a Dao Domain shocks even Wanxiang Adepts, let alone other Zifu cultivators. In standard Xianxia power scaling, this qualifies as “protagonist-level abnormal.” The rain here is not a spell—it’s a spontaneous manifestation of Ning’s connection to the Dao of Water, turned into a weapon.

**The Nuwa Soul Purification:** The beast souls the gray-robed elder unleashes are not trapped human souls but powerful monster spirits. They can ignore physical defenses entirely—a classic Xianxia attack type. Ji Ning’s counter, the Nuwa visualization technique gifted by Judge Cui early in the story, is a supreme soul-protection method. It doesn’t destroy the souls; it frees them into reincarnation, which also demonstrates the karmic merit of a righteous cultivator.

**Face and Momentum:** In Xianxia culture, a young warrior walking openly toward a prepared enemy formation and dismantling it while smiling is the ultimate “face-slap.” It reinforces the hierarchy—comprehension of the Dao literally makes raw numbers and layered defenses irrelevant. This scene also illustrates the typical “cannon fodder reaction”: disbelieving, awed, then dead.

## Reading Guide
Strap in for one of those rare satisfying moments where the protagonist flexes his new power in style. If you’ve been waiting for Ning to show off what “Dao Domain” really means, this chapter is pure dopamine. The back-and-forth with the six defenders—especially their slow realization that they’re facing something beyond normal Zifu logic—is textbook “I am the danger” energy. Pay attention to how IET uses the rain as both atmosphere and weapon; it’s a beautiful example of “setting as offensive tool.” Also note the subtle tactical thread: Ning is racing the clock against Nong Zidao’s intellect, so he doesn’t waste a single moment. This is Ji Ning at his most efficient and ruthless.

**Bonus Fun Fact:** The concept of a cultivator’s ambient presence making the environment behave like a domain is common in Xianxia but rarely described this vividly. It’s a direct predecessor to the “territory” or “reality marble” effects you see in later cultivation novels.
