## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a classic training arc with a twist—Ji Ning, at just over three years old, already knows exactly what he wants. Rather than passively accepting his parents’ guidance, he declares his intention to learn archery, dual swords, and a movement technique. The real kicker? He reveals his ability to use a **divided mind** (一心二用), shocking his parents and unlocking the path of dual-wielding swords. The chapter closes with the start of his archery training under the Western Prefecture’s top archer, Meng Yu, who demonstrates just how terrifying a Xiantian-level bowman can be. Get ready, fellow Daoists—baby Ji Ning is about to level up in style.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning chooses three disciplines: archery, dual swords, and a movement technique. His parents initially push for a single sword, but Ji Ning drops the bomb that he can think two thoughts at once without interference.
- Ji Yichuan and Yuchi Snow are thunderstruck. A divided mind is a rare gift—Ji Yichuan himself only achieved it after years of cultivation. This means Ji Ning is naturally suited for dual swords, a style the entire Ji Clan has never fully mastered.
- Ji Yichuan decides to personally teach his son swordsmanship starting the next day.
- Morning training begins with Meng Yu, who demonstrates the power of Xiantian archery by turning four heavy mill wheels into dust midair.
- Ji Ning learns the two stages of archery: the foundation stage (five basic postures, absolute accuracy) and the heart-arrow stage (shoot without looking, by instinct alone). The chapter ends with him enduring the harsh discipline of posture training.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Divided Mind (一心二用)**: In Chinese cultivation lore, the ability to split one’s consciousness is considered a sign of an exceptionally strong soul. It allows a cultivator to perform multiple actions simultaneously—in this case, wielding two swords as if two separate fighters were cooperating, dramatically amplifying combat power. This is different from mere multitasking (e.g., rubbing your belly while patting your head); it requires two independent streams of thought with zero interference.
- **Fiendgod Body Refining vs. Ki Refining**: Ji Ning’s focus on Fiendgod Body Refining makes him naturally stronger, faster, and more resilient, but raw power must be paired with technique. Archery leverages his divine vision and brute force; swordsmanship leverages his divided mind. The chapter reinforces the Xianxia principle that talent alone is not enough—skill and training are essential.
- **Heart-Arrow Stage (心箭)**: A common concept in Chinese martial arts fiction where a master archer shoots without conscious aiming, relying on intent and instinct. It echoes the Daoist idea of “the heart moves, the arrow follows.” This is a prerequisite for truly lethal archery in the heat of battle.

## Reading Guide
- This chapter sets up Ji Ning’s core combat style for the next several arcs. Pay attention to the **divided mind** moment—it’s the foundation for his dual-sword mastery, which will become his signature.
- Meng Yu’s archery demonstration is not just for show; it foreshadows Ji Ning’s later usage of ranged attacks. The Thunder Dragon Bow is also a named treasure—remember it for future references.
- The training montage at the end feels like classic “hard work pays off” setup. Ji Ning gets yelled at, but he absorbs it like a sponge. His attitude of “a tall building rises from the ground” shows his mature, pragmatic approach to growth.
- For new readers: don’t underestimate the importance of these early lessons. The skills he learns here—precision, instinct, dual-wielding coordination—will carry him through life-and-death battles for centuries to come.
