## Chapter Overview

It’s always a beautiful thing when a cultivator sits in the rain and has a complete spiritual meltdown—in the best way possible. This chapter is the calm, quiet breath between two storms: Ji Ning emerges from his Starseizing Hall breakthrough, masters the Dao of Rainwater in a single, clean stroke, and then sits down with his two artifact-spirit uncles for a brutally honest heart-to-heart about what comes next. The result is equal parts catharsis and cold calculation—a mental and philosophical debrief that sets the stage for the next major leap. From “I’ve just comprehended a complete Dao” to “so, about that Celestial Immortal–level enemy clan I’ve made,” the pacing is masterful. It feels like the book is taking a breath—and so should the reader.

## Key Plot Points

1. **Rainwater Epiphany**: While standing in heavy rain, Ji Ning’s accumulated insights finally crystallize. He completely masters the Dao of Rainwater in one smooth burst, shocking both the yellow-furred bear and the black ox. They agree that something profound must have happened to him—something that “baptized” his spirit.
2. **Sword Dao Consolidation**: Ji Ning doesn’t just stop at rainwater. His time in the Starseizing Hall has also helped stabilize his Domain of the Grand Dao in the Sword Dao. He tests his newfound power with a sword finger strike—a move so potent it rivals top Loose Immortals in sheer profundity.
3. **A Major Question**: Ji Ning asks the two spirits directly: should he break through to the Primal Daoist stage now? He has the Primordial Liquid, he has the Dao foundation, but he wants guidance. He then lays out the full story of his conflict with the Shaoyan clan, his harvest from killing Shao Yanong, and Yu Wei’s advice about the Immortal Feast.
4. **Xianxia Politics 101**: The black ox delivers a sharp lesson on the Grand Xia Dynasty’s “open scheme” system—how the imperial clan uses cities, the Yinglong Guard, and inter-clan rivalry to keep the feudal lords in check while staying legally untouchable. The key takeaway: stay inside a city, and even a Celestial Immortal from the Shaoyan clan can’t touch you.
5. **The True God Threshold**: The yellow-furred bear explains the terrifying difficulty of becoming a True God—ten to a hundred times harder than becoming a Celestial Immortal—but also the overwhelming power that comes with it. He reaffirms that the Immortal Feast is precisely the right crucible for Ji Ning’s growth, comparing it to raising poisonous insects in a jar.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of **learning by analogy (触类旁通)** is a beautiful piece of Daoist cultivation philosophy in play here. It’s not just a skill issue; it’s a metaphysical reality in the Desolate Era universe. Once you fully master one complete Dao—like the Dao of Rainwater—you automatically gain insight into related branches (water-type Daos, here). Master one river, and you start to understand all rivers. This mirrors a very real principle in classical Chinese thought: deep mastery in one domain unlocks a natural intuitive grasp of adjacent domains. The “ten thousand books vs. ten thousand miles” maxim is also a staple: book-learning only takes you so far; real-world pressure and death-defying experience is what forges a true cultivator.

The **poisonous insect jar (炼蛊)** metaphor used by the yellow-furred bear is a classic Xianxia trope with roots in ancient Chinese folk magic. The Gu (蛊) arts involve sealing venomous creatures in a container and letting them devour each other; the survivor becomes a super-potent poison. The Immortal Feast is the same: throwing all the geniuses of a major world into a life-or-death competition to see who emerges strongest. It’s brutal, it’s deliberate, and it’s the fastest way to produce a monster.

### immortal-rising feast (仙缘大会)

A major life-or-death competition that gathers geniuses from across an entire major world. Referred to here as the 'Immortal Feast,' it functions as a crucible that forges the strongest survivors into monsters.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is one of those rare “breather” chapters that feels satisfying because it earns its quiet. There are no flashy fights, no last-minute escapes—just Ji Ning sitting in the rain and having a philosophical breakthrough, then having a straight talk with his seniors about the cold reality of Xianxia power politics. Take the time to absorb the **feeling** of this chapter. Ji Ning isn’t just getting stronger; he’s building the mental architecture for the next massive leap. The conversation about the Grand Xia imperial clan’s “open scheme” system is gold for understanding how the world’s power structure works—it’s a rare moment of clear, concrete lore delivery that will pay off later. Also, keep an ear out for the word “True God.” That’s a new ceiling, and it just got firmly planted in your mind.

## On Dao Mastery and the Path Ahead

One thing that’s easy to gloss over in this chapter is Ji Ning’s “Sword Dao as a Grand Dao” insight. He’s not just a talented sword-user; he’s walking a Grand Dao—one of the most exalted paths in the entire Xianxia cosmology. Mastering a complete Dao (like Rainwater) already puts him in the general ballpark of Loose Immortals for depth. Layering that on top of a Domain of the Grand Dao in the Sword Dao? That’s a dual-axis proficiency that most Primal Daoists would envy. It means his baseline combat power is not just “above his grade”—it’s structurally monstrous. The chapter is deliberately low-key about it, but this is a huge power spike dressed in quiet rain.
