## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter delivers one of the most explosive, game-changing moments in Ji Ning's journey so far. The black ring—a seemingly innocuous trinket that our boy has been carrying since his aquatic palace days—finally shows its true, terrifying colors. What follows is not a battle. It's an execution. Shao Yanong, the smug, untouchable heir of the Shaoyan clan, and Xiangliu Fang, an ancient Primal-level Fiendgod, are erased from existence in the span of a few heartbeats. The atmosphere shifts from tense standoff to absolute chaos as our hero, spitting blood and still reeling from the fight, watches his ultimate trump card annihilate enemies that should have been impossible for him to kill. But victory comes at a staggering price. Ji Ning has just painted a target on his back so big that even the heavens can see it. The chapter's final moments are a raw, emotional crossroads where his companions realize the scale of the catastrophe he's unleashed—and Ji Ning, true to form, immediately switches from shock to cold, pragmatic survival mode.

## Key Plot Points

- The mysterious black ring activates, passing through Xiangliu Fang's Fiendgod-body palm without causing any physical damage, demonstrating its intangible, soul-targeting nature.
- Both Shao Yanong and Xiangliu Fang are completely baffled; neither recognizes the treasure, despite their vast experience, which underscores the profound, otherworldly origin of Daoist Threelives' creation.
- The ring begins to glow with a hazy light, producing an invisible suction force that targets the soul *directly*, bypassing all physical defenses and protective treasures.
- Shao Yanong's soul is forcibly ripped from his body; his silent psychic shrieks of terror and desperate pleas for mercy are witnessed by everyone in the hall.
- Xiangliu Fang, a Primal-level Fiendgod whose soul was completely fused with his flesh, is also torn apart—a feat that seems to defy the very laws of Fiendgod biology.
- The black ring shatters like ice after completing its task, a one-use treasure that served its singular purpose perfectly.
- Ji Ning, after a moment of stunned realization, immediately takes decisive action: he burns Xue Hongyi's corpse, collects the fleeing senior apprentice-brother's Immortal-grade treasure, and incinerates Shao Yanong's body while storing Xiangliu Fang's corpse and all other spoils.
- He kills the surviving Zifu Disciple retainers to prevent witnesses from exposing the truth.
- Nine Lotos breaks down in tears, warning him that the Shaoyan clan will hunt him relentlessly; Ji Ning coldly counters that he had no choice—it was kill or be killed, and he will not apologize for surviving.
- Yu Wei delivers the final, chilling warning: Celestial Immortals can reverse time itself to replay past events, rendering all his efforts at secrecy potentially useless.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a clinic on the Xianxia concept of **karmic debt versus overwhelming cosmic power**. Shao Yanong isn't just any noble; he's the *heir* of the Shaoyan clan and one of the "Four Young Masters of the Grand Xia's capital." In Chinese web novel culture, these titles aren't just for show—they signal a character backed by enough ancestral wealth, hidden experts, and political clout to crush nearly anyone who dares touch him. Ji Ning's act of killing him is therefore not just murder; it's a declaration of war against an entire ancient clan's pride.  

The black ring's behavior is a textbook example of a **"soul-targeting treasure" (神魂类宝物)**. Unlike physical or energy-based attacks, soul attacks bypass the body entirely and strike the immortal spirit directly. This explains why Xiangliu Fang's regenerating Fiendgod body was useless and why Shao Yanong's golden protective talisman (which defended against physical and elemental harm) couldn't stop it. The description of Xiangliu Fang's *flesh-and-soul fusion* being forcibly torn apart is a critical lore detail—it reinforces that Primal-level Fiendgods are so spiritually integrated with their physical forms that killing them via soul extraction is nearly impossible without a treasure of this caliber.

Finally, Yu Wei's warning about **time-reversal divination (时光回溯)** pulls from a classic Xianxia trope: the idea that supreme Celestial Immortals can act as omniscient cosmic detectives. In Daoist cosmology, the "record" of all events is etched into the fabric of spacetime itself; an Immortal powerful enough to "read" this record needs no mortal witnesses to solve a crime. This is Ji Ning's first real brush with the terrifying fact that, in the higher leagues, there are *no secrets* from the truly powerful.

### Soul-targeting treasure

A rare type of magic artifact that bypasses physical defenses and directly attacks or extracts the target's soul or primordial spirit.

### Time-reversal divination

A legendary ability of supreme Celestial Immortals to view past events by reversing the flow of time in a specific location.

### Self-detonation

The act of a cultivator forcibly detonating their own internal energy source (e.g., Zifu lake, Primal soul) as a suicidal final attack.

## Reading Guide

This is one of those *Desolate Era* chapters where the emotional whiplash is real. Ji Ning goes from being flung aside like a ragdoll to holding the ultimate winning card in his hand—the black ring. But notice how his reaction isn't triumphant. It's cold, clinical, and tinged with a kind of grim fatalism. He doesn't gloat. He doesn't taunt. He immediately pivots to *damage control*. This is the mark of a cultivator who truly possesses a Dao-heart forged in the crucible of constant danger. He knows that a moment of celebration could be the moment an Immortal's gaze falls upon him.  

Pay close attention to Nine Lotos's breakdown. Throughout this arc, she's been the composed, powerful noblewoman. Seeing her reduced to tears, begging him to understand the scale of the mess, humanizes her and raises the stakes. And Yu Wei's final line is a masterstroke of tension—just when Ji Ning thinks he has a plan, she drops a bombshell that fundamentally changes the rules of the game. The question is no longer "Can he escape the Shaoyan clan?" but "Can he escape the *past itself*?"
