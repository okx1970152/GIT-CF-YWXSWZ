## Chapter Overview

Hold onto your seats, fellow Daoists! We take a break from bloody battles and desperate breakthroughs to tag along with Beishan Bawei on a sightseeing tour of Anchan City, the beating heart of the Anchan Commandery. And this isn't just any tour guide spouting facts from a dusty scroll. Bawei, a spoiled but charming Beishan clan noble, gives Ji Ning the VIP treatment, including a ride in his father's ridiculous, overpowered Nine Astral Immortal Chariot—the kind of vehicle that can incinerate a Primal Daoist! Our boy Ning is about to get a serious lesson in “how the other half lives” as Bawei casually explains the city's lavish layout, its hidden neutral zones for Immortals, and the bureaucratic carve-up between the Yinglong Guard and the Beishan clan's private armies. It's a prime slice of Xianxia worldbuilding: the raw display of a dynasty's overwhelming power channeled through bureaucracy and luxury. And just as we think it's just a tour, we roll up to the legendary Carefree Grotto—a pleasure den owned by three Celestial Fox Immortals—for a glimpse of top-tier high society. The chapter ends on a delicious note of dramatic irony when a trio of familiar faces—Meng Jun, Meng Yan, and Meng Xin—spy Ji Ning being treated like royalty. Cue the awkward silence and dropped jaws!

## Key Plot Points

- **The Nine Astral Immortal Chariot**: Bawei introduces Ji Ning to his father's golem vehicle, the Nine Astral Immortal Chariot, which possesses the Nine Astral Heavenly Fire capable of killing even a Primal Daoist. The chariot is intelligent and can respond to commands independently.
- **Anchan City's Hierarchy**: Bawei unveils the city's unwritten rules: the West City is a chaotic "melting pot of dragons and snakes," the North City is dominated by the Yinglong Guard, the South City by the Beishan clan's private armies, and the East City is the center for merchants and rogue cultivators. The booklets given to new arrivals are deliberately kept simple.
- **Secret Estates & Immortal Residences**: Bawei explains that the city holds countless estates belonging to Loose Immortals, Earth Immortals, and powerful figures from outside the commandery, many bearing no clan name. These are the city's most dangerous unknowns.
- **The Carefree Grotto**: Ji Ning is brought to the Carefree Grotto, a unique pleasure palace in Anchan City run by three Celestial Fox Immortals with Beishan backing. It serves as a high-end alternative to the emperor-backed Heavenly Treasure Mountain chain.
- **Unexpected Encounter**: While inside, Bawei's arrival with a personal Wanxiang-level greeter shocks the hall, and the Meng siblings (Jun, Yan, Xin) recognize Ji Ning as the mysterious "Young Master Ji" being treated like an equal to a Beishan noble.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Golem Treasures & Immortal Chariots**: The Nine Astral Immortal Chariot perfectly encapsulates the Xianxia trope of "transcendent wealth." The chariot is not just a vehicle; it's a fully intelligent, combat-ready entity that can burn even a Primal Daoist to death. This shows the hierarchy of resources—a big shot's spare ride is a weapon of mass destruction. The detail that it is a "golem" (a complex animated construct) ties into ancient Chinese legends of puppetry arts and magical automata, here scaled to absurd, planet-level proportions.

**The Power of the State (Anchan City):** Beishan Bawei’s description reveals a layered, paranoid bureaucracy. The city is a single Immortal-tier formation from the Primordial Fiendgod Era. The Marquis of Anchan, backed by overwhelming immortal force, governs an absolute hierarchy: safe zones (East, North, South) and a lawless zone (West City) where only killing in the streets is forbidden. The fact that the Marquis gifts estates to visiting Immortals as a courtesy, and that the Yinglong Guard (the dynasty's overseers) occupy half the North City, shows how state power and individual cultivation power are in a constant, tense negotiation. It’s a subtle blend of feudal administration and "might makes right."

**The Heavenly Treasure Mountain & the Carefree Grotto**: This isn't just "going to a casino." The Heavenly Treasure Mountain chain is a Grand Xia Dynasty-owned enterprise profiting from slaves, gambling, and exotic spirit beasts across every commandery city. It is a state monopoly. The Carefree Grotto, backed by the local Beishan clan, is a private competitor. This reflects the classic Chinese trope of the imperial court vs. local aristocratic power, but here it's all framed as high-level offerings for Immortals. The location is a Fox-shaped palace—a wink at the classic myth of fox spirits (huli jing) as beautiful, seductive, and duplicitous beings.

### Beast-Head Gold

A standard unit of gold currency in tribal society, each piece weighing exactly ten jin (approximately 5 kg), stamped with a beast's head design.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a massive shift in tone! This chapter is all about worldbuilding, status, and power displays. The story slows down to let you soak in the sheer scale of Anchan City and the lifestyle of its ruling elite. Don't skim the descriptions of the chariot or the different zones—Bawei is handing Ji Ning (and us) a cheat sheet for navigating the political minefield of the city. Also, pay close attention to the character of Beishan Bawei: he's generous and friendly, but he's also showing off. He's exactly the kind of rich, entitled young master Ji Ning met in the prefecture, but amped up to commandery-level wealth. This contrast is everything. Finally, savor the dramatic irony of the final scene. The Meng clan member who was most eager to suck up to a "big shot" just got a front-row seat to that exact scenario—with the boy he may have looked down on as the star of the show. The *face-slapping* is already loaded, albeit silently.
