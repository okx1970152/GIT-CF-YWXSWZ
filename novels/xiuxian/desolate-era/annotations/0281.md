## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a dual-focus breather and setup chapter that masterfully shifts the chessboard. Ji Ning, fresh from the Wujiang Immortal Estate’s carnage, is not resting on his laurels. He’s on the move, locking down his home base with monstrous firepower and sending his people to safety with ruthless efficiency. Meanwhile, the camera pans to the Shaoyan clan, where the fallout of Shaoyan Nong’s death has just hit the fan. We get our first real look at the terrifying machinery of a top-tier ancient clan—a world of Loose Immortals, Void-level Fiendgods, and hidden Celestial Immortal patriarchs. The stakes have officially left the mortal realm. Get ready—the game is entering its apocalyptic phase.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning arrives at the Ji clan’s inner hall and drops a bombshell: he introduces two Loose Immortals, Duohe and Wusui, who will command a garrison of 30,000 transformed Dao-soldiers to protect Ten Thousand Swords City.
- He assures the clan elders that this force can withstand dozens of Loose Immortals and that the city will be safe for a thousand years, barring an attack by a Celestial Immortal.
- Ji Ning leaves behind Uncle White and Xiaoqing with strict orders: only come to Winged Serpent Lake if the given talisman shatters, signaling his need for extraction.
- He personally forces Qiuye to use a Lesser Teleportation Dao Talisman and flee to Ten Thousand Swords City immediately, prioritizing her safety.
- Ji Ning retreats to his meditation room and uses the aquatic palace’s phantom bear-head to enter the independent space of the water manor, effectively vanishing from the mortal world.
- The scene shifts to the Grand Xia Imperial Capital, where the Shaoyan clan’s Patriarch, Marquis Yushen, receives the shocking news of Shaoyan Nong’s death from a shattered life tablet.
- Marquis Yushen travels to a dormant volcano to consult an ancient, lava-dwelling Fiendgod named Ba Huo, who serves as a gatekeeper to the clan’s hidden Celestial Immortal patriarchs.
- Ba Huo reveals that one patriarch left the world, another went to the Heavenly Realm, and only Patriarch Xuanji remains in seclusion. He agrees to summon him.
- Patriarch Xuanji arrives in a spectacle of starlight, an old man whose mere presence warps reality, marking him as a terrifyingly powerful Celestial Immortal. The Shaoyan clan’s hunt has officially begun.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Panopticon of a Xianxia Clan: Life Tablets and Ancestral Halls**
In Chinese mythology and cultivation fiction, the life tablet (命简) or soul lamp is not a mere trinket. It is a soul-linked artifact kept in the clan’s ancestral hall. When a member dies, the tablet shatters, providing instantaneous, undeniable confirmation of death across any distance. This bypasses all the mundane delays of news delivery and is a crucial trope for instantly triggering revenge arcs. The ancestral hall itself is the spiritual and political nerve center of a clan, a sacred space where the past and present connect.

**The Pyramid of Power: From Mortal Steward to Celestial Immortal Patriarch**
This chapter gives Western readers a crystal-clear view of the Xianxia power hierarchy within a major clan. Marquis Yushen, the Patriarch, is the public face and operational manager of the Shaoyan clan. Yet, he must defer to the Fiendgod Ba Huo, a mere *gatekeeper*. And Ba Huo serves the *real* powers: the Celestial Immortal patriarchs. These are beings who have lived for billions of years and are the clan’s true foundation. Their numbers, locations, and statuses are state secrets. Even the Patriarch doesn’t know them all. This highlights the immense gap between worldly authority and immortal might.

**The Fiendgod Sentinel: Ba Huo and the Legacy of the Primordial Era**
Ba Huo, the red-haired giant bathing in a volcano, is a perfect example of a residual powerhouse from a bygone age. He is a Void-level Fiendgod, but his combat prowess is comparable to a Celestial Immortal. He was defeated and bound by a legendary Shaoyan ancestor, but that ancestor is long dead. Now, Ba Huo serves as a sentinel and messenger, living in a volcano and guarding access to the clan's hidden Celestial Immortals. His presence underscores the profound debt and danger of ancient pacts.

## Reading Guide
Alright, fellow Daoists, we've officially left the tutorial zone. This chapter is a masterclass in "consequences." Ji Ning is playing 4D chess, fortifying his home base with an army that would make most cultivators weep, while simultaneously vanishing himself from the board. That’s the mark of a true strategist. But the real treat here is the Shaoyan clan’s reaction. For the first time, we see the terrifying machinery behind a "young master" with a Celestial Immortal backer. Marquis Yushen is a puppet king, running to a lava-dwelling giant to beg for an audience with a Celestial Immortal. The hierarchy is brutal and absolute. Pay close attention to the entrance of Patriarch Xuanji. The description of starlight bending to his will is a classic trope to establish "heavenly might." This is the first time we’ve seen the engine of a major clan's revenge power up. It’s ominous, it’s awe-inspiring, and it sets the stage for a conflict where the Ji clan is nothing more than an anthill in the path of a tsunami. Buckle up.
