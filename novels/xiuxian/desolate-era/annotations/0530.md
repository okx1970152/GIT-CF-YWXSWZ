## Chapter Overview
War! The opening clash between the Grand Xia and the Seamless Gate is a meat grinder of epic proportions, and the casualties are staggering. Ji Ning and his fellow Xingtian commanders get their first real taste of a prolonged, high-stakes campaign. The chapter isn't just about the brutal fight with the Venomous Empyrean God and his White-Faced Flood Dragons; it's about the sobering reality of a war where legendary figures are suddenly just soldiers in a vast, anonymous army. The emotional center is the post-battle reckoning, where the sheer scale of loss (hundreds of Celestial Immortals, tens of thousands of Loose Immortals) sinks in, forcing everyone—from the Xia Emperor down to the newest recruit—to confront the cold arithmetic of a war of attrition with no retreat.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Scrap:** The Venomous Empyrean God, commanding the White-Faced Flood Dragons and nine Empyrean God puppets, ambushes the Xi River Legion. Ji Ning, commanding his Xingtian divine spirit, struggles against the Empyrean God but holds his ground, showing the gap in pure power but proving his durability. The other Xingtian spirits arrive to assist, and the Seamless Gate force withdraws.
2. **The Casualty Report:** The aftermath is devastating. The Grand Xia lost 436 Celestial Immortals and over 51,000 Loose Immortals in a single engagement. This is a moment of stark horror, as these numbers represent the entire immortal might of dozens of top-tier clans.
3. **The Morale Shift:** The Xia Emperor immediately turns the loss into fuel. He announces that Ji Ning's unit killed one White-Faced Flood Dragon, over 600 Celestial Immortals, and nearly 70,000 Loose Immortals—proving they can and are killing more than they lose. He whips the army into a fervor, shouting "Kill them all!"
4. **Tactical Debrief:** In the main hall, the commanders analyze the battle. They confirm the Venomous Empyrean God (a True God level fighter) is a threat no one can face alone. The Empyrean God puppets are identified as tough but untalented—hard to destroy, but vulnerable to binding or suppression.
5. **The Trade:** Ji Ning produces the Empyrean God puppet he acquired from a past encounter and trades it to the Xia Emperor for a bottle of pills to fuel his Eight-Nine Arcane Art. This is a classic Xianxia resource exchange, trading personal utility for war needs.
6. **The Long Game:** The Xia Emperor outlines the grim strategy for the coming years—daily skirmishes, attrition warfare, and the hope of whittling down the Seamless Gate's high-end forces one by one.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**War Economy: The Terrible Arithmetic of Tribulation Wars**
This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia war economy. The scale of loss—436 Celestial Immortals—is deliberately framed against the clan strength readers know (e.g., the Shaoyin clan has 9 Celestial Immortals). This instantly translates "huge number" into "unfathomable tragedy." It's a world where the value of a single Celestial Immortal is immense, but the war treats them as cannon fodder. This is the "scale of war mobilization" trope taken to its logical extreme: seeing the mightiest beings of a generation reduced to anonymous, replaceable soldiers in a machine of slaughter.

**The Emperor's Speech & Morale Mechanic**
The Xia Emperor's speech isn't just propaganda; it's a strategic necessity. In a Xianxia world, morale is a tangible force that can affect the power of formations and the resolve of Dao-hearts. By framing the battle as "we are winning by killing more than they do," he provides a psychological anchor. The collective roar of "Kill them all!" is a classic xianxia moment where shared intent amplifies the army's collective power, a faint echo of the Dao of War.

**The Empyrean God Puppet & The Loot Economy**
Ji Ning's trade of an Empyrean God puppet for pills is a perfect example of the Xianxia loot economy. An object is valued not by its grade, but by its *tactical utility* in the current crisis. Ji Ning can't use the puppet (requires multiple Celestial Immortals to control), but the Xia Emperor can. In exchange, Ji Ning gets what he needs most: cultivation resources for his Eight-Nine Arcane Art. This is "economic specialization" in a high-fantasy war setting.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, because the real grind has just begun. This chapter sets the brutal, grinding tone for the entire war arc. The flashy, heroic duels are over; now it's about survival, attrition, and the terrifying scale of a conflict where the world itself is the prize. The emotional gut-punch of the casualty report is the key moment to feel. It transforms the war from a distant threat into a present, bleeding reality. Pay attention to how Ji Ning's role is evolving—he's not just the breakthrough genius anymore; he's now a key tactical commander and emotional anchor for the army. The stage is set for a long, punishing campaign where every small victory comes at a terrible cost.
