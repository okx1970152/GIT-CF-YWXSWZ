## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow Daoists, the moment we’ve been waiting for has finally arrived! Ji Ning’s desperate gambit in the third trial of the aquatic palace pays off. Barely alive, his meridians shredded and his energy spent, he watches as the black-furred monster collapses—wounded! It’s a raw, emotional victory, and the old monster’s howl of pain and relief after aeons of isolation hits harder than any blade. This is a classic Xianxia beat: the protagonist cheats death through a clever combination of unique traits (Divine Sense + Fiendgod regeneration), and the ancient guardian gets the bittersweet release it craved. Ji Ning is now the heir apparent, and the estate itself opens up to reveal a mind-bogglingly vast hall—a chamber built for giants. His real audience with the legacy just begins when a mysterious talking black ox strolls out, identifying itself as the artifact spirit of Celestial Immortal Juhua’s personal treasure. Get ready for some world-building, lore drops, and a hint of just how rare Ji Ning’s talent truly is.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning’s soul, having reached the level of Divine Sense (a power normally reserved for Primal Daoists), allows him to control thousands of weak flying treasures simultaneously and aim them at a single point.
- His Fiendgod Body Refining cultivation gives him the monstrous regeneration needed to survive the backlash of expelling all his true energy at once without dying from burst meridians.
- The black-furred monster deliberately takes the hit after aeons of solitary suffering, crying out in relief at being finally wounded. It thanks Ji Ning as its new master and vanishes.
- Ji Ning spends a day recovering, confirms his survival, and explores deeper into the estate. He emerges into a colossal main hall with prayer mats a hundred zhang in diameter—clearly not built for ordinary humans.
- The exits are sealed by an invisible barrier. Just as Ji Ning ponders his next move, a black ox emerges from a thousand-zhang-high corridor.
- The black ox introduces itself as the artifact spirit of an ancient magic treasure owned by the deceased Celestial Immortal Juhua. It reveals that no other entrant in countless ages has ever passed the third trial.
- The ox analyzes Ji Ning’s feat, praising his rare combination of a powerful soul, the Crimsonbright Diagram, and a True Meaning of the Dao. It notes that if he were any older or had reached the sixth level of the Diagram, the Grand Xia Dynasty would have never let him risk becoming Juhua’s disciple.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is packed with foundational Xianxia lore. First up: **Divine Sense**. Most cultivators have to reach the Primal Daoist stage to unlock this telekinetic soul-power. Ji Ning’s ability to wield it at Xiantian is a testament to the immense strength provided by the Nuwa Diagram—a top-tier visualization technique that strengthens the soul from infancy. This is a classic example of the “hidden foundation” trope: a resource obtained early in the story pays off in a completely unexpected way later. The second key concept is the **Artifact Spirit**. In Xianxia, extremely powerful treasures, after being nurtured for endless ages by Immortal energy, can give birth to a sentient spirit. The black ox is just such an entity—it’s not a monster or a demon, but the waking consciousness of the magical weapon itself. The chapter also reinforces the value of the **Crimsonbright Diagram of the Nine Heavens**. The ox immediately identifies it as the strongest Fiendgod Body Refining technique from the Primordial Era simply by noticing the Taiyin and Sunstar divine patterns on Ji Ning’s body. For context, praising a technique for being “Fiendgod Era” is like saying a weapon was forged by Hephaestus in Greek myth—it’s ancient, legendary, and virtually unmatched.

## Reading Guide
This chapter marks the end of the first major “trial arc” of the aquatic palace, but the real prize—knowledge and the legacy—is just about to unfold. Pay close attention to the black ox’s assessment. It confirms that Ji Ning’s feat is an anomaly, impossible for anyone who didn’t have his specific combination of traits. This reinforces the idea that his journey is unique. The introduction of the artifact spirit also signals a shift: Ji Ning is no longer a desperate test-taker; he is now in a dialogue with a knowledgeable ancient being. This sets the stage for the acquisition of techniques, the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation, and the Fiendgod Blood-Refining Art. Also, take note of the ox’s casual mention of the “Grand Xia Dynasty.” This is the first major expansion of the world’s political scope. The story is moving beyond the tribal politics of the Yan Mountains and hinting at a vast empire with its own immortal armies and strict rules. Strap in, because the lore dump is about to get heavy, and the cultivation road is about to get a whole lot wider.
