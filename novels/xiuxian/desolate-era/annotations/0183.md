## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a beautifully paced breather that lets the dust settle after Ji Ning’s explosive entry into the Black-White College. We pivot from combat spectacle to two quieter, but equally significant, forms of progression: the forging of loyalty and the deepening of a master-disciple bond. First, Ji Ning extends his hand to his old friend Meng Yan, offering him a retainer position that literally changes the course of his life—a classic Xianxia payoff that rewards the bonds forged in earlier hardship. Then, we shift to a private audience with the Diancai Immortal, where the chapter’s philosophical heart beats strongest. The master doesn’t teach a new sword move; he imparts a *doctrine*, defining the Dao of the Sword not as raw power, but as the supreme art of application—a path requiring a heart of absolute sincerity. Get ready, fellow Daoists, for a chapter that proves sometimes a single conversation can refine one’s Dao-heart more than a thousand battles.

## Key Plot Points

*   **A Fateful Invitation:** Yunzhou, one of Ji Ning’s retainers, finds Meng Yan and the Meng brothers in a tavern. He delivers Ji Ning’s personal invitation, shocking Meng Yan, who has been crippled and rejected by every sect.
*   **From Cripple to Retainer:** Meng Yan is brought to Ji Ning’s Northmont Peak manor. He quickly realizes the immense gap between the friend he once knew and the monstrous genius he is now. Ji Ning offers him a retainer position, which Meng Yan accepts with overwhelming gratitude and a vow of loyalty.
*   **A Debt of the Heart:** Ji Ning treats Meng Yan with the warmth of an old brother, downplaying the retainer status and instructing Yun Lu to prepare a welcoming feast. This reinforces Ji Ning’s character: he never forgets those who were kind to him, even as he ascends.
*   **The Master’s Guidance:** The next morning, Ji Ning visits the Diancai Immortal. The master begins an even deeper level of instruction, delving into the philosophical nature of the Dao of the Sword itself.
*   **The Dao of Application:** The Diancai Immortal reveals a crucial distinction: while some Daos govern the mysteries of Heaven and Earth (like Rainwater or Ice), the Dao of the Sword is a Grand Dao of *application*. It is the ultimate technique for unleashing the power of other insights.
*   **The Sincere Heart:** The master imparts his core doctrine—*utmost sincerity leads to illumination* (至诚, 通明). A true Sword Immortal must constantly polish their Sword Heart, keeping it pure and unwavering.
*   **The Foundation for the Future:** The Diancai Immortal assigns Ji Ning thirty-six Earth and Heaven-grade sword technique manuals to study. This is not just homework; it’s the strategic building of a foundation for the intense, life-and-death battles that await him outside the College’s protective walls.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**诚于剑，诚于心 (Sincere to the Sword, Sincere to the Heart):** This is one of the most philosophically dense passages in the early *Desolate Era* cultivation journey. The Diancai Immortal’s teaching—that a Sword Immortal must possess a heart of utmost sincerity—is a direct reflection of a real concept in Chinese Confucian and Daoist self-cultivation. The term *Cheng* (诚 / Sincerity) is a vital virtue, signifying the perfect alignment of one’s inner truth with one’s outer actions. In the Xianxia context, this is weaponized: if your Sword Heart is honest and your will is unified, your sword strikes will be clear, decisive, and unstoppable. The phrase ‘polishing the Sword Heart’ (擦拭剑心) is a metaphor for constant self-reflection and will-forging. A dull heart leads to a dull sword. This isn’t just a character moment; it’s a foundational piece of Ji Ning’s future metaphysical power, setting him apart from those who train only the body and not the spirit.

**The Dao of Application vs. The Dao of Mystery:** The Diancai Immortal makes a critical distinction here that explains the power hierarchy in *Desolate Era*’s cultivation system. A cultivator might comprehend the ‘Dao of Rainwater’—this is a ‘Dao of Mystery,’ an insight into a natural phenomenon. But how do you **use** that insight to kill an enemy? You need a ‘Dao of Application.’ The Dao of the Sword is the ultimate expression of this concept. It is the *technique* of using your sword to channel all other insights (rainwater, fire, wind, etc.) into a devastating attack. This is why Sword Immortals are feared—they are not just powerhouses; they are the ultimate *conductors* of power. Other Daos of Application include the Dao of the Taiji (Supreme Ultimate), which harmonizes opposing forces.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in what makes *Desolate Era* a top-tier progression fantasy. It’s not just about getting stronger; it’s about the **quality** of that growth. Pay close attention to the structure: the first half delivers the emotional payoff for Meng Yan’s arc (a character we’ve followed since the early tribulations), while the second half lays the intellectual and philosophical groundwork for Ji Ning’s future power. This is a classic IET move—balance pathos with info-drops to keep you hooked.

Think of the Diancai Immortal’s ‘homework assignment’ of 36 manuals not as a grind, but as a strategic investment. The master is essentially building Ji Ning’s **comprehension engine** from the ground up. He’s not handing him the final answer; he’s equipping him with the complete dictionary he needs to read the language of the Dao. The true genius isn’t the one who learns a single powerful sword stance, but the one who understands why all stances work. Also, enjoy the quiet moment of Ji Ning showing his human side. After the relentless glory and slaughter of the trials, seeing him treat a crippled friend as a brother and provide a path forward is a powerful reminder of the Dao-heart the Diancai Immortal is asking him to polish.
