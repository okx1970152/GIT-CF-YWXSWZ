## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up. This chapter is the ultimate "all or nothing" gamble. Ji Ning has been pushed to his absolute limit by the Snowdragon Mountain siege, and after seeing his father's desperate plan, he makes a run for it—not to flee, but to trigger a nuclear option. He teleports straight into the ancient aquatic palace beneath Winged Serpent Lake, hoping to claim a divine ability powerful enough to one-shot a Wanxiang Adept. But the palace’s ancient spirit, the Yellow Bear, drops a horrifying truth bomb: Ji Ning is only five years old, and he can only ever enter the Divine Abilities Hall once. The Yellow Bear practically begs him to wait five years to secure a truly unstoppable power, but Ji Ning’s father is burning his lifespan as we speak. The chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia tension—a direct clash between long-term cosmic potential and the urgent, bloody present. The stakes are sky-high, and the emotional gut-punch at the end, as Ji Ning steps into the void alone, is pure gold.

## Key Plot Points
- **Immortal Xu Li's Bluff Exposed**: We get inside the Wanxiang Adept's head. He openly admits he has no idea where Ji Ning is and that his earlier threat to track him was a complete fabrication to keep the Ji Clan elders from sending their best weapon back into the fray. It’s a rare peek into the villain's pragmatic, tactical mind.
- **The Aquatic Palace Rescue**: Using the Ten-Thousand-Mile Trace-Away Talisman, Ji Ning teleports directly into the deep water of Winged Serpent Lake. The Yellow Bear, the aquatic palace’s gruff artifact spirit, immediately senses Ji Ning’s overwhelming desperation and rage.
- **The Divine Abilities Hall is a One-Shot Deal**: The chapter’s core lore dump. The hall contains seven divine abilities: one from the First Master (the aquatic palace's owner) and six from his disciples. You can only enter once in a lifetime, and the tougher the trial you overcome, the better the ability you get.
- **Ji Ning Refuses to Wait**: The Yellow Bear, showing surprising care, urges Ji Ning to wait until he’s ten to maximize his chance at the First Master’s legendary divine ability. Ji Ning refuses point-blank. His father is dying *right now*; a hypothetical future is worthless if his family is dead.
- **The Odds**: The Yellow Bear calculates Ji Ning’s current chance of getting the top divine ability at less than one in ten thousand. But he also confirms that the *third* divine ability is more than enough to kill the mid-stage Wanxiang Adept. The stage is set for a desperate gamble.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Weight of a "One-Shot" Opportunity**: This chapter perfectly illustrates a core Xianxia trope—the "one-time inheritance chamber." In Western fantasy, a hero might find a magic sword that anyone can use. In Eastern cultivation fiction, legendary inheritances are often guarded by strict *opportunity costs*. You get one shot, and if you take it too early, you get a "good" result instead of a "godly" one. The Yellow Bear's plea to Ji Ning to wait is a classic test of a cultivator's Dao-heart: can you delay gratification for absolute maximum power, or do you sacrifice potential for what you love right now? This is the central philosophical conflict of the entire progression fantasy genre.
**Delegated Awe of the First Master**: We finally learn the scale of the aquatic palace’s origins. The Yellow Bear mentions that the First Master left this place in the era *before* the Three Realms were even fully formed—before the War of the Primordial Fiendgods. This is a massive flex. The Yellow Bear also notes that none of the First Master’s *six disciples* were worthy of his ultimate technique. This is a perfect example of "Delegated Awe"—we don’t see the First Master fight, but we see the impact of his legacy, and the fact that even his "failures" are now the source of legend-tier abilities.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a pure upgrade-porn chapter next time. Ji Ning is about to face a brutal trial that tests his physical strength, mental fortitude, and comprehension of the Dao. We know the third-place prize is enough to win *this* fight, but the question is whether Ji Ning’s insane talent and current rage can push him to the top. This is the kind of payoff you read Xianxia for—a character putting everything on the line for a power-up that feels genuinely earned because of the emotional cost. Also, keep an eye on the relationship between Ji Ning and the artifact spirits; the Black Ox is too scared to even watch, while the Yellow Bear is clearly rooting for him despite the bad odds. This chapter sets a brutal, emotional stage for what promises to be a legendary action sequence.

## Deeper Dive: The Yellow Bear's Character Shift
The Yellow Bear is one of the most interesting characters in this arc. Initially, he was cold, distant, and treated Ji Ning like just another test subject. But after witnessing Ji Ning’s comprehension of the Water-Fire Sword Domain—a true sign of Dao talent—his attitude shifts. In this chapter, he practically begs Ji Ning not to waste his potential. This isn't just a machine giving instructions; this is an ancient being who has watched genius after genius fail to meet his master's standard, and now he sees a spark of the real thing. His desperation to have Ji Ning succeed mirrors Ji Ning's desperation to save his family, creating a deep, tragic parallel between the two.
