## Chapter Overview

Get ready, because this chapter is a genuine tear-jerker and a monumental flex of cosmic power, all rolled into one. We finally get the full, heartbreaking closure for the yellow-furred great bear spirit of the aquatic estate, as Patriarch Subhuti confirms the ancient truth: Daoist Threelives perished in a cataclysm eons ago. But the emotional gut-punch is just the preamble. Subhuti, the mysterious master of spacetime, officially takes Ji Ning as a nominal disciple and immediately delivers the kind of cheat-code training session that defines a legendary Xianxia inheritance: the “Dream of a Thousand Years.” Ji Ning is walked through the entire history and future of the Dao of the Sword in what feels like millennia but is only a single incense stick of real time. This is not just a power-up; it’s the metaphysical equivalent of installing the universe’s operating system into a new computer. The chapter ends with Ji Ning receiving the legendary *Crimsonbright Diagram* and the *Beiming Sutra*, solidifying his foundations for the terrifying road ahead—with a strict ‘no telling anyone I’m your master’ clause, of course.

## Key Plot Points

- The old master confirms the yellow-furred bear’s deepest fear: Daoist Threelives died in a primordial cataclysm, leaving the bear spirit trapped and waiting for a trillion years. The bear’s anguished howl of “Master!” is a raw, painful moment that gives tragic depth to the aquatic estate’s legacy.
- Patriarch Subhuti officially accepts Ji Ning as a nominal disciple, laying down his two and only two rules: no disobedience, and absolute secrecy about their master-disciple relationship.
- The “Dream of a Thousand Years” begins. Subhuti touches Ji Ning’s forehead, and the young cultivator experiences an entire lifetime within a dream corridor, walking through the complete spectrum of the Dao of the Sword—from beginner techniques to levels far beyond the *Three-Foot Sword*.
- Ji Ning emerges after only a single incense stick of real-time, his soul etched with the profound mysteries of the sword. Though his immediate combat power hasn’t skyrocketed, his foundational understanding and future path are now crystal clear.
- Subhuti gifts Ji Ning two supreme cultivation manuals: the *Crimsonbright Diagram of the Nine Heavens* for Fiendgod Body Refining and the *Beiming Sutra* for Ki Refining, setting him up for a dual-path cultivation all the way to the Daofather and True God levels.
- Subhuti mentions Mount Innerheart’s two Dao palaces, hinting at a vast treasury of divine abilities and secret arts waiting for Ji Ning to earn. He also cryptically promises “two great benefits” when Ji Ning is finally strong enough to leave the mountain.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The “Dream of a Thousand Years” (一梦千年) is one of the most powerful and classic teaching tools in Xianxia. It directly taps into a foundational Daoist concept: that time is relative and that a master can “transmit” enlightenment directly into a disciple’s soul, bypassing years of slow, physical training. This isn’t a simple download, though. The corridor Ji Ning walks is a representation of the *complete theoretical framework of the Dao of the Sword*, a path laid out for him to explore experientially. The technique doesn’t grant him power; it grants him a roadmap. This aligns with the principle of “learning by analogy” (触类旁通)—now that he knows where the path goes, he can travel it far more efficiently than someone groping in the dark.

Also noteworthy is the name of Subhuti’s home: the **Tristar Crescent Abode** (斜月三星洞). This is a famous riddle from *Journey to the West*. The Chinese characters for “slanted moon” (斜月) and “three stars” (三星) are a visual riddle that forms the character 心 (xīn), meaning “heart.” The lesson is that true cultivation and the path to mastery are found within one’s own heart. This deepens the chapter’s theme of internal discovery versus external power.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as the official end of Ji Ning’s “tutorial” phase in the world of the Three Realms and the beginning of his true apprenticeship under one of the setting’s ultimate powers. Pay close attention to the dual inheritance: Subhuti is setting Ji Ning on a path to master both body and soul, preparing him for the immense tribulations ahead. The emotional beat with the yellow-furred bear is not just nostalgic filler; it establishes the weight of the legacy Ji Ning has inherited from Daoist Threelives and raises the stakes for what it means to be the inheritor of such a power. The “Dream of a Thousand Years” is a massive lore drop disguised as a training montage—it establishes the sheer scale and depth of the Dao of the Sword as a metaphysical force. And finally, the cryptic promise of “two great benefits” and the insistence on secrecy are classic Xianxia hooks, setting up the long, slow-burn arc of Ji Ning’s hidden power and the eventual revelation of his true master.
