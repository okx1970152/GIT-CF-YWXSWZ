## Chapter Overview

Well, well, well. If it isn’t the reckoning. Last chapter, we saw Source Ancestor step in to save Ji Ning from the Seamless Gate’s assassination squad. Now, the old man makes his true intentions clear: he wants Ji Ning as his disciple! But before Ji Ning can even process this incredible offer, a far greater problem presents itself. He's already a disciple of Patriarch Subhuti, and taking a new master without his permission could create a terrible conflict of loyalty. While Ji Ning wrestles with this dilemma, the Seamless Gate leadership is left utterly demoralized, having lost a batch of treasures and any face they had left. And just when Source Ancestor demands Ji Ning spill the name of his master… who should appear but the man himself. Get ready for an epic showdown of Daofather-level negotiations!

## Key Plot Points

*   The Seamless Gate and their allies watch helplessly via a water mirror as Source Ancestor casually obliterates their avatars, an act of supreme dominance that leaves them powerless and furious.
*   The leaders of the assassination squad bitterly lick their wounds, with Celestial Immortal Wu Xiao suffering the greatest individual loss—an Innate spirit-treasure—and fuming with rage at his failure to kill Ji Ning.
*   Source Ancestor offers to take Ji Ning as his disciple, but Ji Ning, loyal to his master Subhuti, hesitates. He won't accept without his master's explicit blessing.
*   Source Ancestor, in a bid to win Ji Ning over, summons the Diancai Immortal—a man who still considers the old man his master despite having only received a single sword technique.
*   Ji Ning’s internal struggle is revealed: he is honored by the offer but fears that Source Ancestor might be an enemy of his current master, which would put him in an impossible position.
*   Just as Source Ancestor demands to know Ji Ning's master's name and begins to apply pressure, Patriarch Subhuti himself makes a dramatic and timely entrance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Weight of a Master.** The concept of a *master* (师傅, shīfu) in Xianxia is far more sacred than a simple teacher. The text even uses the term *shifu*, which is more like "Master-Father." A master doesn't just teach techniques; they grant you a new life by opening the door to the Dao. Betraying or disrespecting a master is one of the most heinous sins a cultivator can commit, directly poisoning one's Dao-heart. Ji Ning’s hesitation isn't just polite defiance; it's the foundational logic of his cultivation. He literally cannot take a new master without the blessing of the old one, as it would create a spiritual conflict that could shatter his path. The Diancai Immortal, kneeling in gratitude for a single, casually-gifted sword technique, perfectly illustrates this absolute reverence.

**Daofathers and the Great Catastrophe.** Source Ancestor's comment that "when the great catastrophe truly erupts… no one can escape" highlights a core Xianxia trope. These are not just local wars; they are cosmic tribulations that transcend realms and force even the most reclusive "hidden bosses" like Source Ancestor to pick a side. The Seamless Gate's fear of offending Source Ancestor right now perfectly illustrates the precarious pre-war political landscape. They want him as an ally, not an enemy, and so they must swallow the humiliation of their avatars being destroyed. Higher powers play a waiting game, not a constant war.

## Reading Guide

Alright, brace yourselves. We've entered a major political landmark in the story. This isn't a chapter about combat power, though Source Ancestor waving away avatars with a pair of scissors is a glorious flex. This is a chapter about the power of alliances and the importance of loyalty. Watch how the Seamless Gate's leadership reacts. It’s a masterclass in how to handle public failure: blame the other guy, grit your teeth, and wait for orders from on high.

Also, pay very close attention to Ji Ning's reasoning. He thinks this through step by step, weighing the grace of Subhuti against the immense potential of a new master. This isn't indecisiveness; it's the calculated caution of a cultivator who knows that a wrong choice here could destroy his Dao Heart. And then, that final moment. The way Subhuti enters is *chef's kiss*. It tells us he's been watching, he knows what's happening, and he's stepping in before a confrontation can even begin. The next few chapters are where the true high-stakes politics of the Three Realms are decided.
