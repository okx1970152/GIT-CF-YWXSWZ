## Chapter Overview

Chapter 22 is where Desolate Era shifts gears from physical cultivation to *dao-heart cultivation* and it does so with absolute style. Ji Ning, fresh off his Black-White Diagram enlightenment, turns to the wall of Immortal inscriptions. This is the classic Xianxia trope of *legacy resonance* the ancient words of past powerhouses aren’t just motivational quotes. They’re active, spiritual batteries left behind to temper the will of later generations. Ning walks through them like a sword through water, absorbing the hardened Dao-hearts of countless predecessors who walked the razor’s edge of Immortal ascension. And when he hits the line from Immortal Beixing: “In hand, a three-foot sword; slay all that is unjust” the chapter erupts into a full-blown sword-intent resonance event that catches the attention of a mysterious, wine-guzzling elder in the sky. Who is he? What does his reaction mean? Welcome to the real test.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter is structured into three parallel arcs: Ji Ning’s Dao-heart baptism, Mu Northson’s puppetry breakthrough, and the outsider observer who judges them both from above.

First, Ji Ning moves from the Black-White Diagram to the wall of Immortal inscriptions. The text here is crucial each line carries the weight of a lifetime’s worth of piled-up will. Ning experiences everything from ruthless ambition (“The path of Immortal cultivation is the path of great struggle”) to carefree abandon (“A single day of joy is better than a hundred years of misery”). His own Dao-heart about seeking great freedom and taking control of his own fate is hardened and purified through this exposure.

Second, Mu Northson remains locked in on the Black-White Diagram. His breakthrough is visible: components of beasts constantly assemble and reassemble around him in a storm of creativity. His “extraordinary insight from accumulated experience” is exploding, showing that his talent in the puppet/construct Dao is genuinely monstrous.

Third, the chapter introduces a hidden observer: a short old man in tattered robes, casually flying on a cloud and chugging Immortal wine. He just overcame the Nine Tribulations, buying himself another 900 years of life. He assesses both disciples: he approves of Northson’s talent, but when he sees Ji Ning resonating with the sword-intent of Immortal Beixing (a million-year Loose Immortal who rivaled Celestial Immortals), his shock is palpable. This resonance isn’t just impressive it’s a legendary sign. The cliffhanger is all about what this elder will do next.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

A quick breakdown of key concepts that hit hard in this chapter:

**Dao-Heart (道心) Tempering Through Inscriptions**: This is a classic Xianxia technique where a sect preserves the final will of its predecessors. Each Immortal who died left behind a sentence that carries their accumulated Dao-heart and sword-intent. For a junior to read them is to undergo a spiritual forging: the will of the dead directly polishes the spirit of the living. It’s part legacy, part test, and part spiritual weaponry.

**Loose Immortal vs. Celestial Immortal**: Immortal Beixing is called a “million-year Loose Immortal.” In Desolate Era’s cosmology, a Loose Immortal is a cultivator whose physical body was destroyed during the tribulation, forcing them to survive as a soul-bound existence. They suffer the Three Calamities and Nine Tribulations every few centuries, each wave deadlier than the last. To survive a million years is insane. That’s why Beixing’s sword-intent is treated as the real deal: he matched a Celestial Immortal in power despite having a weaker energy foundation. That’s the mark of a Dao genius who had to work ten times harder.

**Sword-Intent Resonance (剑意共鸣)**: When a cultivator’s heart is so aligned with a predecessor’s Dao that the dead Immortal’s residual will awakens, that’s resonance. It’s a sign that the living cultivator is *worthy* of carrying that legacy. In this chapter, Ji Ning’s resonance causes the inscription to *glow*, and the elder watching from above is visibly taken aback. This foreshadows that Ning is about to receive a major inheritance from Senior Beixing.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for the next chapter because it’s going to hit big. We’re about to see a **full sword-art inheritance** dropped into Ji Ning’s lap, and from the sound of it, Beixing’s “Three-Foot Sword” is anything but gentle. The old man’s song “Kill, kill, kill. Slay all that is unjust. Slay all who deserve death!” tells you everything you need to know about his philosophy: it’s an aggressive, pragmatic, no-bullshit approach to swordplay. Ji Ning’s Dao-heart already leans toward decisive action and ruthless compassion. This inheritance is going to fit him like a glove.

Also, keep an eye on Mu Northson. His puppetry breakthrough is being observed too, and the elder in the sky approves. But the real drama is whether Ji Ning’s resonance with Beixing draws the elder down to personally intervene. You don’t just ignore a disciple who awakens a million-year Loose Immortal’s legacy right in the middle of the Black-White training ground. Expect either a test, a recruitment, or a teaching moment dropping in the next few sections. Fellow Daoist, your sword is about to level up.
