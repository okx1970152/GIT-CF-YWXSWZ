## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow Daoists, because we've just hit the motherlode! Chapter 271 is the ultimate "loot pinata" chapter for Ji Ning. While everyone else is frantically searching through the Daoist Scripture Hall, our boy's spirit-beast, Qingqing, accidentally stumbles upon the secret vault that holds the real treasure: Celestial-ranked Ki Refining techniques, divine abilities, and a divine sense secret art. We're talking about the kind of stuff that can define a cultivator's entire combat style. The tension is palpable as Xue Hongyi almost catches Ji Ning red-handed, but luck holds. More importantly, Ji Ning finally gets his hands on two divine abilities he's been desperately craving: 'Three Heads, Six Arms' to combo with his 'Starseizing Hand', and the lightning-based 'Five Thunder Adamantine Body', a simplified version of the legendary 'Eight-Foot Adamantine Body'. This is a massive power spike on the horizon for our protagonist.

- **The Power-Up Blueprint**: Internally, Ji Ning analyzes his new gains. 'Three Heads, Six Arms' is the key. Combined with his 'Starseizing Hand', he can have six hands all using an ultimate weapon technique. This is not just an upgrade; it's a complete combat revolution.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Importance of Divine Abilities**: In Xianxia, a genius cultivator's power isn't just about their cultivation level or technique. When two geniuses are evenly matched, victory is decided by their **divine abilities (神通)**. These are powerful, supplementary techniques that offer a unique tactical advantage, like growing extra heads and arms for multi-directional attacks. The novel is explicit: "When geniuses of comparable strength clashed, victory often depended on who possessed the superior divine ability." This is a core tenet of this genre—raw power is just the foundation; true strength comes from the special "moves" you have.

**The Eight-Foot Adamantine Body vs. The Five Thunder Adamantine Body**: The 'Five Thunder Adamantine Body' is described as a simplified version of the legendary 'Eight-Foot Adamantine Body'. In Chinese mythology and folklore (especially in *Journey to the Wild* and stories of Sun Wukong), the **Eight-Foot Adamantine Body (丈六金身)** refers to a Buddha's dharma body, an invulnerable, golden-skinned form that is impervious to nearly all harm. It is a top-tier divine ability in the Three Realms, on par with Ji Ning's own 'Starseizing Hand'. The 'Five Thunder' version is a deliberate downgrade, making it more accessible but requiring five successive lightning tribulations to master, showing that even simplified versions of legendary skills can be game-changers.

**The Scarcity of Immortal Treasures**: The reaction of Xue Hongyi and Cangjiang Realperson highlights just how rare Celestial Immortal legacies are. Snowdragon Mountain, a major power in its own right, has never produced a single Loose Immortal or Earth Immortal in its history. A complete inheritance from a Celestial Immortal like 'Wujiang' is enough to make a Wanxiang Adept like Xue Hongyi believe it could give him the edge to transcend his Heavenly Tribulation. This underscores the immense value and rarity of what Ji Ning has just obtained.

**Divine Sense as an Independent Path**: The book 'One Thought, An Army' is a **divine sense secret art**. This is a subcategory of magic separate from Ki techniques or divine abilities. It weaponizes a cultivator's spiritual perception (their 'divine sense') directly, allowing for mental attacks and defenses. These are incredibly rare, as they rely on an exceptionally powerful soul foundation, which Ji Ning possesses thanks to his early cultivation of the 'Nuwa Painting'. Finding one is a huge boon.

## Deep Dive: The 'Five Thunder Adamantine Body' and Chinese Lightning Worship
The 'Five Thunder (五雷)' element is a powerful one in Daoist magic and Chinese martial folklore. In traditional Chinese culture, 'Five Thunder' is not just about lightning; it's a primal, celestial force that represents the ultimate punishment against evil spirits and chaotic forces. Daoist exorcists and priests often use 'Five Thunder Talismans' (五雷符) to ward off demons and break monstrous curses. The fact that this simplification of a Buddha-level divine ability uses five types of lightning to forge an indestructible body is a brilliant fusion of Daoist and Buddhist tropes. It implies that the cultivator must be "purified" by this celestial, punitive force to achieve a golden, Buddha-like state of invulnerability, mixing the harsh, external judgment of the Dao with the internal, resilient perfection of Buddhist cultivation.
