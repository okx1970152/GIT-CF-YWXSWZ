## Chapter Overview
Our boy Ji Ning has just looted the most incredible treasure chest of his young immortal life, and he doesn't even know it yet. This chapter is a pure, unadulterated *info-dump* of the best kind—the kind where a mysterious, soul-shaking artifact is examined, tested, and partially cracked open, revealing a legacy that promises to redefine the power ceiling. Forget the revenge plot for a second; Ning is now a student faced with a cosmic textbook. The chapter methodically walks us through his inspection, his shock at the stele's properties, and his initial, thrilling success in reading its ancient techniques. It’s a masterclass in how to build anticipation for a power-up, using the classic Xianxia device of the ancient inheritance. Get ready for some serious system-breaking techniques!

## Key Plot Points
*   Ji Ning scrutinizes the massive, mysterious black stele. He notices its faint but sublime pressure and the strange, unfamiliar script covering its surface.
*   He attempts to probe the stele with his divine sense, but it reveals nothing—an utter void. This is a major flag for the stele being extraordinary.
*   Switching to heartforce, a far more powerful and esoteric form of perception, Ji Ning is shocked to find *even heartforce* cannot detect the stele’s existence. This confirms it’s likely not a native object of the Three Realms.
*   He realizes this must be a ‘Chaos curio,’ possibly even a ‘Chaos treasure’—an artifact born from or forged in the primordial Chaos beyond the Three Realms.
*   Ji Ning attempts to refine the stele with his divine power. It fails to refine like a normal treasure, but the divine power does trigger a transmission of information from the characters.
*   He learns that the two large characters on the front read ‘seventeen,’ suggesting this stele is part of a set.
*   By carefully channeling his divine power, he begins to read the millions of tadpole-like characters, successfully obtaining the first of seven techniques recorded on the stele.
*   The first technique is the ‘Eighteen Fiendgods of Taowu,’ a heaven-defying technique allowing one to split into eighteen identical bodies, far surpassing any known doppelganger arts in the Three Realms.
*   He reads the second technique, ‘Nine Interpretations of Insect Beasts,’ but decides it’s too time-consuming and unsuitable for him.
*   Unable to read deeper with divine power, he switches to Pure Yang ki and finds it much more effective, setting the stage for him to potentially unlock all seven techniques and learn the stele’s ultimate origin.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a godsend for explaining the cosmology of Xianxia! Let’s break down a few key concepts. First, the **Chaos (混沌)**. This isn't just disorder; it's the primordial, undifferentiated state of the universe from which everything was born. It's the space *between and beyond* reality as we know it (the Three Realms). Things that come from the Chaos are by definition ancient, powerful, and follow different rules. Second, the idea of **Divine Sense (神念)** and **Heartforce (心力)**. Divine sense is the standard psychic perception of a cultivator. Heartforce is a far more rarefied, metaphysical power tied directly to the cultivator's will and Dao-heart. For the stele to block both means it operates on a level of reality that defies even the most profound spiritual detection methods within the Three Realms. Finally, the concept of techniques having multiple ‘layers’ or ‘stages.’ This is a core Xianxia trope. A technique isn't a spell you just learn; it’s a path of mastery. The ‘Eighteen Fiendgods of Taowu’ is a perfect example: Layer 1 gives you weak clones, Layer 2 gives you perfect clones, and Layer 3 allows for a fusion that boosts the main body. It’s a progressive system for the ultimate power-up.

## Reading Guide
Alright, fellow Daoist, settle in. This isn't a chapter of action; it's a chapter of *revelation*. The author, IET, is playing the long game here. He's giving us, through Ji Ning's eyes, a peek behind the curtain of the multiverse. Pay close attention to the *concept* of the stele. It’s not just a power-up; it’s a piece of cosmic lore. The number ‘Seventeen’ is a huge hint—there’s a bigger mystery here. Think of it like finding a single file in a massive, encrypted database from a forgotten civilization. Also, note Ji Ning's reaction. He's not just excited; he's *analyzing*. He tests with divine sense, then heartforce, then tries different energies to refine it. This is the hallmark of a true Xianxia protagonist—a pragmatist with a scientist's curiosity. So grab your popcorn and enjoy the lore dump. This is the beginning of Ji Ning's journey from a genius of the Three Realms to a player on a much, much bigger board. The power-up is real, and it's spectacular.

## Deep Lore: The Seven Techniques of the Stele
This black stele isn't just a random artifact; it's a library of seven supremely advanced techniques, each one a game-changer. While we only get details on two, the existence of seven is a massive promise of power yet to come. The first, the ‘Eighteen Fiendgods of Taowu,’ is a body-splitting technique that makes even the great ones of the Three Realms look humble. Daoist Three Purities can only split into three. The Buddha can only manage five. This technique promises *eighteen* perfect copies? That’s not just a power multiplier; it’s a complete redefinition of how a cultivator can wage war, explore, and even survive. The second technique, ‘Nine Interpretations of Insect Beasts,’ explains Shaoyan Chou's creepy pets. It’s a fascinating peek into an alternate path of cultivation—one focused on raising and controlling bio-weapons. Ji Ning rightly judges it as too costly in time for his direct combat style. But the fact that the stele holds five *more* techniques, each one likely as profound as the first two, is what truly sets the stage for an epic power curve. The mystery of the stele's origin, the identity of the ‘seventeen,’ and the nature of the other five arts are now the most tantalizing questions in the story.
