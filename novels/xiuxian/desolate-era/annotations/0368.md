## Chapter Overview

Welcome to one of the most lore-dense chapters in the early Mount Innerheart arc! Ji Ning has spent a year mastering *Plow Heaven* and refining his sword-heart, and now he finally steps into the legendary Three Realms Palace—the secret vault of Patriarch Subhuti that only a handful of disciples ever get to see. And who’s guarding the door? None other than the second senior brother, Jidian, a figure whose reputation shakes the Three Realms yet spends his days snoring in the sun. What follows is a glorious tour of nineteen divine abilities, including the mind-boggling *Candle Dragon’s Eye*, the top-ten-ranked *Houyi Archery Art*, and a tantalizing glimpse of the *Nine Heavens Mysterious Art*. Get ready, fellow Daoists—the power-ups are about to get legendary.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning defeats the ninth-level puppet three times over the course of a year, earning three Daofather-level sword-art manuals, including *Plow Heaven*, from which he comprehends thirteen sword moves.
- Realizing his progress has plateaued, Ji Ning sets out for the Three Realms Palace, the most mysterious location on Mount Innerheart.
- He meets the second senior brother, Jidian, who has been sleeping for decades. Ji Ning learns about Jidian’s incredible background: he inherited the supreme divine ability *Dream of the Three Realms*, roams every minor world as an incarnation, and has accumulated boundless karmic merit, making him coveted by both Buddhist and Daoist sects.
- Jidian grants Ji Ning permission to enter the palace, noting that each manual requires a trial.
- Inside, Ji Ning first picks up *Candle Dragon’s Eye* (complete edition) and is shocked to find its trial is as simple as sweeping a mountain path. Jidian explains that the ability is both difficult to master and widely known among Daofathers.
- Ji Ning then discovers *Houyi Archery Art*, a top-ten divine ability comparable to the *Starseizing Hand*. He compares the two: the *Starseizing Hand* is raw power through refinement, while Houyi’s art is a technical school requiring deep insight.
- Jidian warns that no one has ever reached Houyi’s level with this art, and suggests Ji Ning look at other manuals first. Finally, Ji Ning spots the *Nine Heavens Mysterious Art*, leaving the reader hanging with anticipation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a treasure trove of Chinese mythological references and cultivation logic. Let’s break down the big ones:

- **Jidian (济癫)**: The second senior brother is none other than the legendary “Mad Monk Ji Gong” of Chinese folklore—a Buddhist monk who feigns madness while performing miracles, helping the downtrodden with a tattered fan and a flask of wine. In Chinese pop culture, Ji Gong is beloved as a folk hero who “roams the world doing good deeds.” The novel reimagines him as a disciple of Patriarch Subhuti, master of the *Dream of the Three Realms*—a fitting power for a character whose legends are spread across countless minor worlds.

- **Three Realms Palace (三界宫)**: This is the hidden library of the absolute top-tier techniques. In Xianxia traditions, a master often keeps his best inheritance hidden until the disciple proves worthy. Here, Subhuti’s collection includes divine abilities that *he himself* could not create—like Houyi’s archery. That’s a massive flex: it means Subhuti’s network and power are so vast he owns techniques from mythical figures who predate him.

- **Candle Dragon (烛龙)**: In Chinese mythology, Candle Dragon (Zhulong) is a mountain-sized deity with a human face and a serpent body. Its breath creates wind and clouds; when it opens its eyes, it is day; when it closes them, it is night. The *Candle Dragon’s Eye* divine ability is a direct nod to this being. The trial is easy because the technique is both hard to master (only the original Zhulong perfected it) and not unique (multiple Daofathers know it).

- **Houyi (后羿)**: The archer who shot down nine suns in the classic myth of Houyi and Chang’e. Here, he’s elevated to a “True God” who created a top-ten divine ability. The distinction between the *Starseizing Hand* (brute force, refinement-based) and *Houyi’s Archery Art* (technical, insight-based) mirrors the classic Xianxia dichotomy of two paths to power: the savage body-refiner vs. the elegant technique-master.

- **“The Dao cannot be passed down lightly”**: This is a recurring Xianxia theme: even in a sect where treasures are freely available, each technique has a “trial” to ensure the disciple is worthy. The trials can be absurdly simple (sweeping paths) or impossibly hard (solving a cosmic riddle), but they always tie into the technique’s nature.

### Pangu’s top-ten list

A reference to the legendary ranking of divine abilities since the primordial god Pangu split heaven and earth, indicating the absolute pinnacle of power in the Xianxia hierarchy.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a perfect example of what makes Desolate Era’s cultivation system so addictive: the constant promise of new, broken abilities. Ji Ning has already claimed the *Starseizing Hand* — a top-ten divine ability — but now he learns there are *multiple* top-ten techniques, each with a different flavor. The *Houyi Archery Art* is especially tantalizing because it’s a “technical” school, meaning it will test Ji Ning’s soul and comprehension rather than his raw cultivation level. Keep an eye on that *Nine Heavens Mysterious Art* at the end — those four characters have enormous weight in Chinese mythology (often linked to the highest Daoist cultivation). Will Ji Ning pick it next? And what will its trial be?

Also, savor the character moment with Jidian. He’s the first senior brother Ji Ning has truly interacted with on equal footing, and his offhand remark about “Master’s hidden reasons” hints that Subhuti sees something special in Ji Ning — possibly his reincarnated soul or his prior-life karmic merit. This chapter plants seeds that will bloom into major revelations later.
