## Chapter Overview

We have a brand-new, terrifying variable on the battlefield, fellow Daoists! The Ji Clan's desperate gambit—relying on the mighty Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation to trap their enemies—just hit a major snag. Nong Zidao, the formation expert who escaped last chapter, is back! And he has brought a Wanxiang Adept with him: True Immortal Xu Li. This chapter is all about the calculus of a siege. It opens with Nong Zidao calmly dismantling the Black Mist Illusion Formation from the outside like he was taking out the trash, exposing the Ji Clan to the full view of their predators. The conversation between the invaders is a masterclass in tactical arrogance—a Zifu Disciple explaining the rules of engagement to a Wanxiang powerhouse. The Ji Clan, now trapped within a sealing formation that prevents any escape, must confront their mortality. They formally structure a last stand, funneling all their hope onto Ji Ning's shoulders. The stage is set for a desperate, no-holds-barred clash between a clan's fragile hopes and the overwhelming, cold-blooded machinery of the Snowdragon Mountain sect.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Strategy is Leaked:** Nong Zidao, having survived the formation, now serves as the key to defeating it. He explains the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation to the Wanxiang Adept, Xu Li, revealing its five sub-sections (the Dragon Head, Dragon Tail, Dragon Body, Dragon Claws, and Pearl) and its weakness: the netherworld energy (the illusionary component) can be severed, leaving only the attacking Blackflame Dragon.
- **The Sealing of Oxhorn Mountain:** Acting on Nong Zidao's advice, Xu Li instantly sets up a massive sealing formation around the entire mountain. This is the death knell for the Ji Clan's retreat tactics. The sealing barrier covers the sky, ground, and earth, trapping them inside. The narrator coldly notes that breaking this seal would require strength equivalent to Xu Li's own.
- **The Clan's Last Will and Testament:** Inside the dome of the sealing formation, the Ji Clan elders make their peace. Ji Jiuhuo, Granny Shadow, Ji Liuzhen, and Ji Yichuan each say their goodbyes to Ji Ning, explicitly charging him with the legacy of the clan. The atmosphere is heavy with the weight of a final sacrifice.
- **The Wanxiang Adept's New Fear:** Down on the ground, Nong Zidao warns Xu Li about the clan’s hidden weapon: the sixteen-year-old Ji Ning. He relays that Ji Ning has comprehended a “Domain of the Dao” in the sword (Rainwater). This revelation shocks Xu Li, who hasn't even reached that level himself, instantly elevating the “native tribe genius” to a high-priority threat in his mind.
- **The Price of a Guide:** Nong Zidao has a final, crucial demand. He refuses to re-enter the formation without a Minor Teleportation Dao Talisman—a life-saving escape artifact—because he fears Ji Ning specifically. Xu Li, after a brief hesitation (these talismans are precious), agrees to lend him one. Armed with his insurance policy, Nong Zidao confidently leads the charge back into the formation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chess game played in this chapter is a classic Xianxia tactical trope: **The Duel of the Formation Master.** If you only have raw power (Xu Li is a Wanxiang Adept with a powerful seal), you can be beaten by a clever formation. Raw strength means nothing if you can’t find your enemy. This is why cultivators of wisdom and insight are so highly valued. The real battle here isn’t Xu Li vs. the Ji Clan; it’s Nong Zidao vs. the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation. His ability to “lead” (引领) the process—decoupling the illusion (the netherworld energy) from the attack (the dragon) with the brute force of the Wanxiang Adept—is a textbook example of how a Zifu-level formation expert can neutralize a formation that should be out of his weight class.

Secondly, we have the **Sealing Formation**. This is a classic Xianxia “cage match” device. It forces the weaker side (the Ji Clan) to fight to the death, burning every last resource because there is no escape. It also creates a **pressure-cooker environment** for the protagonist. When the elders all turn to Ji Ning and say, “You are our hope,” it’s not just a gut-wrenching family moment; it’s an accelerator for his Dao Heart. The higher the pressure, the greater the potential for a breakthrough—or a collapse. Notice how Ji Ning's response to this pressure is pure, lethal calculation. He shifts his entire mental register to “how to kill the biggest threat first,” which is a perfect example of a protagonist with a true **Dao Heart**—he doesn’t panic, he reallocates resources.

Finally, note the value system at play. The **Minor Teleportation Dao Talisman** is treated as a priceless treasure. Xu Li hesitates to give one away, even though it's a tactical necessity. This highlights that in this world, a reliable, instant-travel artifact is one of the most precious resources you can have—often worth more than offensive weapons because it represents a definitive "get out of jail free" card against most threats.

### Scaled-Vein (元脉)

The underground channels of Earth energy that formations draw upon; severing these veins can disrupt a formation's power supply.

## Reading Guide

Buckle up for the storm that's about to break. This chapter is the long, tense inhale before a nuclear explosion. The power balance is set: Xu Li is a tank, Nong Zidao is the scout who can strip away your defenses, and the other Zifu Disciples are the cleanup crew. The Ji Clan, on the other hand, has everything riding on a hybrid fighter who can't even take a punch from a Wanxiang Adept's prime artifact without his defensive magic robe helping.

Pay close attention to the rules of engagement Nong Zidao lays out. He tells Xu Li that when rain starts falling, that's Ji Ning's cue to attack. This is not just a cool image; it's the cultivator version of a "tell." It tells us that Ji Ning's Domain of the Dao is not a passive or continuous state—he has to activate it, and the enemy has now seen the summoning cue. How will Ji Ning adapt now that his primary "enter battle" signal has been identified and broadcast to the enemy commander?

Also, watch the **Lonely Wolf vs. The Vicious Pack** dynamic. Ji Ning has killed top Zifu experts (like Tongyu), but that was after a protracted battle of attrition. Now he faces a Wanxiang Adept who is fresh, has better gear, and has a tactical analyst whispering weaknesses into his ear. The narrative is setting up a classic underdog ascension—where the protagonist must find a way to defy the logic of the power ladder using a trump card his enemies haven't fully priced in. What will it be? The raw power of the Starseizing Hand? A new trick from the Aquatic Palace? Or something else entirely?
