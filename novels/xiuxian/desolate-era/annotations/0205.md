## Chapter Overview

Well, well, well—our boy Ji Ning just picked up a second spirit-beast, and not just any spirit-beast, but the very Skygreen Serpent who once gave him a run for his money back in the Eastmarsh Swamp days. Talk about a full-circle moment! While she was originally hoping to cling to him as a traveling companion and maybe settle down, Ji Ning (ever the pragmatic Sword Immortal) had other plans: a proper spirit-beast pact, complete with the promise of freedom whenever she wants it. And yes, she immediately takes his surname—because in this world, that's a power move and a half. The chapter then transitions into a cozy lore dump about two legendary natural resources: cold-sha veins and earthfire veins. Our green-scaled queen casually reveals she found *both* deep in Mount Yujue Wasteland. Meanwhile, in a shadowy parallel scene, the Wanxiang disciple Dongyi, who survived the earlier ambush, stumbles upon a golden-tinged earthfire vein of his own. The stage is set for a race to harvest these elemental treasures—one that's bound to spark some serious friction.

## Key Plot Points

- **Reunion with the Skygreen Serpent**: The green-robed girl, now calling herself Ji Qingqing, formally becomes Ji Ning’s spirit-beast after he saves her from the Dragon Whale King. She adopts the surname Ji, much to Mu Northson’s amusement.
- **Explaining the Absence of Uncle White**: Ji Ning clarifies that his spirit-beast Uncle White could not join due to the Yinglong Guard trial’s restrictions, establishing a respectful bond between equals rather than master-servant.
- **Qingqing’s Backstory**: She recounts her journey from fleeing Tie Muzhan at the Eastmarsh Swamp, advancing to Zifu through roaming, joining the White Dragon Immortal’s tutelage, and reaching mid-Wanxiang after three years in Mount Yujue Wasteland.
- **Revelation of Two Treasure Veins**: Qingqing casually drops that she discovered both a cold-sha vein and an earthfire vein in the wasteland—natural resources essential for elemental nurturing—and offers them to Ji Ning and Northson.
- **The Species and Grades of Earthfire and Water**: The chapter pauses to explain the Xianxia classification system for elemental forces (human, earth, sky, true levels) and the nine-grade earthfire hierarchy, along with the process of ‘planting’ these elements within one’s body.
- **Mu Northson’s Choice**: As a cultivator inclined toward water-wood, Northson chooses the cold-sha vein. Ji Ning, whose Fiendgod body was born from both Taiyin True Water and Sunstar True Fire, declares he will extract from *both* veins.
- **Dongyi’s Survival and Discovery**: The surviving enemy from the earlier six-man squad recovers in a hidden cave and accidentally discovers his own earthfire vein, laying the groundwork for future conflict.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### The Hierarchy of Fire and Water in Xianxia Lore
This chapter introduces a wonderfully nerdy Xianxia staple: the classification of elemental forces into a multi-tiered hierarchy. Fire isn’t just fire—it ranges from *human fire* (the weak stuff we cook with) to *earthfire*, *skyfire*, and finally *true fire*, which is so terrifying even Immortals avoid it. Earthfire alone has nine grades, with first-grade being the peak. The same goes for water, where ‘cold-sha’ serves as the equivalent of earthfire. Absorbing these isn’t about eating a pill; you need to locate a natural vein, ‘plant’ a seed in your Violet Palace, and nurture it over time—turning your body into a living elemental furnace. It's a brilliant way for authors to give cultivators a customizable, upgradeable power source that evolves alongside them.

### The Taming of a Great Demon
When a Wanxiang-level great demon willingly becomes a spirit-beast to a Zifu Disciple, it’s a huge deal in the Xianxia world. The Skygreen Serpent is a proud godbeast, but Ji Ning’s meteoric growth (from a Xiantian kid who barely survived their fight to someone who can one-shot her pursuers) earns her genuine respect. The spirit-beast talisman isn’t just a collar—it’s a contract. Ji Ning’s promise of eventual freedom echoes his father’s philosophy: spirit-beasts are siblings, not tools. That’s the mark of a true Dao Heart: power that comes without cruelty.

### White Dragon Mountain: A Demon Academy
The White Dragon Immortal, described as a female dragon who teaches without discrimination, is a classic example of the ‘benevolent demon sect’ trope. Unlike the human sects that often hoard techniques, she accepts almost anyone, creating a melting pot of demon talents. This reflects the Xianxia theme that strength comes in many forms, and even a ‘monster’ can be a mentor.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow cultivators, put on your mineral-mining hats because we’re deep in resource-gathering mode this chapter! The reunion with the Skygreen Serpent—now proudly named Ji Qingqing—is a treat for long-time readers who remember the Eastmarsh Swamp arc. It’s also a classic Xianxia power fantasy moment: the one who used to beat you up is now begging to join your team. But the real juice here is the lore about earthfire and cold-sha. If you’ve ever wondered why cultivators spend chapters just sitting around absorbing strange glowing substances, this is the chapter that explains the *why*. Also, keep an eye on the Dongyi subplot—he’s discovered an earthfire vein of his own, and in a wasteland where resources are scarce and grudges are deadly, two people wanting the same flame is a recipe for an explosive showdown. Get ready for some elemental smackdown in the chapters to come.
