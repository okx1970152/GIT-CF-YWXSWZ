## Chapter Overview
Welcome, fellow Daoists, to the moment Ji Ning officially steps onto the grand stage of the Grand Xia Dynasty's military apparatus! This chapter is a brilliant piece of world-building and tension-setting. We leave the comforts of Swallow Mountain far behind as Ji Ning and his new friend Mu Northson board the jaw-dropping, eight-hundred-zhang-tall teleportation array of the Yinglong Guard. The chapter masterfully builds the mystique of the Mount Yujue Wasteland—a legendary, shattered battlefield from the Fiendgod Era where Immortals once fought primordials. We get the crisp, brutal assessment rules (survive three months, bring back a Wanxiang demon's head), a chilling reminder that other cultivators are just as dangerous as the demons, and a deliciously ominous side-plot about the Yinglong Guard's ruthless political purges. The calm before the storm has never felt so electrifying.

## Key Plot Points
*   **Arrival at the Teleportation Array:** The group of one hundred cultivators arrives at the peak where a massive, eight-hundred-zhang-tall pagoda-shaped teleportation array stands, maintained by ten elderly attendants.
*   **The Scale of the Array:** The silver-haired elder explains that this top-tier formation can instantly reach any commandery in the Grand Xia Dynasty, highlighting the political and military power of the state.
*   **The Legend of Mount Yujue:** Fellow candidate Donghe Feiyun shares the secret history of Mount Yujue—an ancient prison-treasure that suppressed Fiendgods after the Pangu Era. One Fiendgod eventually broke free, leading to a cataclysmic war that shattered the land into the dangerous Mount Yujue Wasteland of today.
*   **The Silent Political Purge:** The silver-haired elder briefly disappears to order a Yinglong Guard dog-spirit to investigate and summarily execute any of the Marquis of Anchan's clan members who are obstructing the Guard's affairs, showing the cold power of the central state.
*   **The Assessment Rules Announced:** The elder returns and officially lays down the ground rules for becoming a Yinglong Guard: survive three months in the wasteland and bring back the severed head of a Wanxiang-level great demon. Failure means death or rejection.
*   **The Shadow of Treachery:** Mu Northson privately warns Ji Ning that other cultivators now see them as "rich targets" from the Black-White College, setting up the classic Xianxia dynamic where the environment, the beasts, and fellow humans are all potential enemies.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Yinglong Guard (应龙卫):** The name "Yinglong" refers to a winged rain dragon from ancient Chinese mythology, often associated with flood control and military might. Naming the Grand Xia's elite Immortal corps after this mythic beast instantly conveys their power, authority, and connection to the state's sovereign will.

**The Legend of Mount Yujue & Fiendgod Suppression:** This legend is a powerful example of Xianxia world-building. The idea of powerful Immortals choosing *not* to kill their enemies (because it's too costly) and instead using a magic treasure to "suppress" and starve them out over hundreds of thousands of years is a classic trope. It showcases the pragmatism, cruelty, and long-term strategic thinking of high-level cultivators.

**The Political Background Note:** The brief scene where the silver-haired elder coldly orders the execution of Beishan clan members "if they obstruct" is a critical reminder of the Xianxia political ecosystem. The Grand Xia Emperor rules as a supreme overlord, and the local Marquis (like Anchan Hou) are hereditary vassals who must constantly prove their loyalty. The Yinglong Guard acts as the Emperor's secret police and army, a force that can purge a noble's entire family on suspicion alone.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, because this chapter is the firing of the starting pistol. The safe, tutorial-like zones of Swallow Mountain are over. From here on out, it's the wild west of the Xianxia world. Pay close attention to the *physically broken* nature of the Mount Yujue Wasteland—this isn't just a forest with monsters; it's a dimensionally unstable war relic from a time of gods. The rules about surviving three months and killing a Wanxiang demon seem simple enough on the surface, but the real danger is the utter lawlessness. Ji Ning isn't just hunting demons; he's also a walking treasure trove for any rogue cultivator who recognizes his Black-White College identity. The true test here isn't just martial strength, but ruthless survival instincts and knowing who to trust. Mu Northson is shaping up to be a solid ally, but can he be relied upon in a life-or-death scramble? The stage is set for a brutal, no-holds-barred trial.
