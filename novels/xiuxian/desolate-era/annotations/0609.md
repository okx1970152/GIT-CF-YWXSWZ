## Chapter Overview

Chapter 609 finds Ji Ning elbow-deep in the most stubborn negotiation of his immortal career. He knows the World Prison is a treasure trove of ancient secrets—specifically, methods to upgrade his third-rate Golden Core—but the imprisoned Elder Gods and Ancestral Immortals are veterans of eons. They won’t give up a single chaos oddity without seeing the “World-level” existence at his back. Ji Ning tries every angle: pleading, serving delicacies, offering servants. The answer is always the same: bring Mother Nuwa or a comparable World God. And if they can’t escape those Heavenly Locks, they have no reason to trust a weak True Immortal. The chapter closes with a sharp pivot: Ji Ning approaches a female prisoner and drops a bombshell—the Ninefaced Chaosworld Kingdom has fallen. It’s a classic Xianxia gambit of asymmetric information.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning learns that his third-tier Golden Core can, in theory, be elevated to the second tier using chaos oddities and a rare technique. The wild dog Elder God admits he knows one such method but refuses to share it without guarantees.
- The grimy Ancestral Immortal confirms the same limitation: no technique, no treasure, no help unless a World God arrives to break the Heavenly Locks.
- Ji Ning realizes that even if he gathers all the Daofathers of the Three Realms, they can’t break the manacles. The World Prison is a fortress that only a Pangu-level existence can crack.
- He decides to wait—perhaps until the war with the Seamless Gate grows desperate—before revealing the prison to his allies. It’s a calculated, pragmatic decision that shows his growing maturity as a leader.
- The final scene is a masterstroke of psychological warfare: a weak-looking True Immortal walks up to a powerful prisoner and declares, “The Ninefaced Chaosworld Kingdom has already fallen. Submit or die.” The asymmetry of power turns into a test of nerve and belief.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**No Rabbit, No Hawk (不见兔子不撒鹰)**  
This is a classic Chinese idiom meaning “one does not release the hawk unless the rabbit is seen”. It describes a person who refuses to act or invest without a clear payoff. In Xianxia, it’s the universal principle of business among immortal cultivators: promises mean nothing without leverage. Both the Elder God and the Ancestral Immortal apply this ruthlessly to Ji Ning.

**Heavenly Locks (天锁)**  
These are not physical chains but truesoul-bound bindings. Even an Elder God who can regenerate his body from a drop of blood cannot escape because the lock is attached to the very core of his being. Only a qualitative breakthrough to the World God level (like Pangu or Nuwa) can shatter them. This is a classic “hard ceiling” trope in progression fantasy: no amount of cleverness can bypass a fundamental power threshold.

**Chaos Nectar (混沌灵液)**  
A precious fluid condensed from primal chaos energy. The World Prison’s grand formation extracts it from the prisoners themselves—a parasitic system that keeps them weak but alive. Ancestral Immortals can still siphon a tiny amount to maintain their dimensional treasures, but the nectar is the prison’s primary “crop”. This ties into the broader Chinese mythological idea that chaos is both origin and resource, the raw material of creation itself.

## Reading Guide

If you’ve been itching for Ji Ning to finally catch a break, this chapter is a masterclass in pacing frustration. The author I Eat Tomatoes knows you want him to level up quickly, but instead we get a brutal lesson in immortal economics: no one gives away a Golden Core upgrade for free. The wild dog and the grimy old man are magnificent in their stillness—they’ve been waiting for eons, and they won’t be swayed by a kid with a wooden tray. But Ji Ning’s final pivot—walking up to that female prisoner and dropping the “your kingdom is dead” bomb—is pure genius. He can’t force them, but he can manipulate their perception of reality. The chapter ends on a delicious cliffhanger: will she believe him? And if she does, what price will she demand? Fellow Daoists, the negotiation arc is just warming up.
