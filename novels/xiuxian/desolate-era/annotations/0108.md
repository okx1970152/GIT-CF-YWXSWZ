## Chapter Overview

Get ready, Dao companions. This is the chapter where the Ji Clan stops running and starts digging in. After weeks of desperate maneuvering, the final battlefield is chosen—Oxhorn Mountain, a remote, twin-peaked slab of rock in the middle of nowhere. The elders lay out their plan: stall Snowdragon Mountain for three days until the Grand Xia Dynasty’s envoy arrives. It’s a gritty, realistic siege strategy built on six painstakingly prepared formations. But then, Ji Ning drops a bombshell. He reveals the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation, a treasure so legendary it makes the clan patriarch weep with joy. The mood shifts from grim resignation to wild, crackling hope as the clan realizes they might actually have a fighting chance. This isn’t a battle chapter—it’s the quiet, electric moment before the storm, where every chess piece is placed and every heart steels itself for what's coming.

## Key Plot Points

- The clan selects Oxhorn Mountain as their battlefield, a location chosen for its commanding view and lack of cover, forcing a fair fight.
- Ji Jiuhuo reveals his six painstakingly prepared formations as the primary delaying strategy; the goal is to buy three days until the Grand Xia envoy arrives.
- The elders discuss the political blowback of offending the garrison commander of Yan Mountain City and pragmatically decide survival is worth the cost.
- Ji Ning suddenly produces the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation, shocking everyone including his own father, who had no idea his son possessed such a treasure.
- Ji Jiuhuo refines the dragon pearl and scales on the spot; his emotional reaction transitions from stunned disbelief to ecstatic triumph as he realizes its power.
- The formation’s mechanics are explained: five Zifu Disciples can share Yuan power and defense like Dao-soldiers, and the dragon soul can attack independently.
- The chapter ends with the clan training through the night, familiarizing themselves with the new formation, their morale completely transformed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Delegated Awe: How Xianxia Shows Power Without Showing It**
This chapter is a masterclass in a technique I call *Delegated Awe*. We don’t see the Grand Xia envoy fight. We don’t see Snowdragon Mountain’s army. Yet their power is felt entirely through the reactions of the on-screen characters. The elders *fear* the garrison commander, they *dread* the Snowdragon Mountain Zifu Disciples, and they place all their *hope* in the envoy. The raw awe they feel for these off-screen powers does all the storytelling work. When a Xianxia character says “A Zifu master’s casual word can terrify an entire generation,” they aren’t exaggerating. The power scales are so vast that reputation itself becomes a weapon, and a single name can shift the outcome of a war before a single punch is thrown.

**The Life-Burning Strategy**
Xianxia clans facing extinction have a classic playbook: burn everything. The treasure stockpile, the clan’s dignity in front of officials, and in more tragic cases, the cultivators’ own life spans. While this chapter doesn’t show a Life-Burning Secret Art (a forbidden technique that trades vitality for power), it foreshadows the logic. The clan is willing to offend the garrison commander, to spend their heirloom treasures, and to stand on a hill waiting for death. In a genre where longevity is the ultimate currency, every resource spent here is a kind of sacrifice—an investment in a future that might not come.

### Delegated Awe (代位敬畏)

A literary device in Xianxia where the power of an off-screen character is conveyed through the awed reactions of on-screen characters who have witnessed their legacy, without direct demonstration.

### Life-Burning Secret Arts (燃烧寿命的秘术)

Forbidden cultivation techniques that trade a cultivator's own lifespan or vitality for a powerful effect, often used as a desperate last resort or a sacrifice to protect another.

## Reading Guide

Tension and release. That's the emotional architecture of this chapter. The first half is all tightening screws—the political cost, the desperation, the gamble of stalling for three days against a vastly superior enemy. You can feel the weight pressing down on the characters. Then comes the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation, and it’s like a pressure valve bursting. Watch Ji Jiuhuo’s reaction carefully. A clan patriarch, centuries old, weeping with joy over a treasure. That’s not just a power-up moment; it’s a character beat that tells you how hopeless things truly were. He wasn’t just afraid of losing—he was facing the complete annihilation of his bloodline. Soak in that relief. It won’t last forever (this is Xianxia, after all), but for now, let the clan have their hope. They've earned it.
