## Chapter Overview
The chapter opens with Ji Ning, Yu Wei, and Cangjiang debating the terrifying difficulty of the Immortal Destiny Gathering. Yu Wei’s resolve to participate is unshakable—she sees the battle as the crucible she needs. As the conversation deepens, the three Wanxiang-tier powerhouses agree to spar at Serpentwing Lake, with Mu Northson crashing the party in indignant protest. Then, after a poignant moment where Yu Wei reveals the childhood tragedy that shaped her, a new figure descends from the skies: Nine Lotos. In a scene charged with quiet romantic tension, she and Ji Ning confirm their bond as Dao-companions, marking a major emotional milestone for the protagonist—and a subtle, heartrending moment for the observer left standing on the sidelines.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning and Yu Wei discuss the brutal standards of the Immortal Destiny Gathering: mastering one Dao is the baseline, two is rare, and three virtually guarantees a top-ten finish. Yu Wei admits she can’t reach the top ten but insists she can survive.
- Cangjiang and Yu Wei agree to stay at Serpentwing Lake to spar with Ji Ning. Northson, furious that golem-users are banned from the Gathering, forces his way into the “training camp” anyway.
- The conversation takes a somber turn as Yu Wei reveals her parents were killed when she was only three years old, triggering her past-life soul eruption. Ji Ning, whose own parents are dead, feels a deep resonance with her story.
- Nine Lotos arrives from Shangshui Commandery, having been trapped by her own clan in the Ten Thousand Lotus Caverns for ten years. She announces that the Dongyan clan has withdrawn its opposition.
- Ji Ning and Nine Lotos publicly reveal their Dao-companion bond in front of Yu Wei, Cangjiang, and Northson. While the others celebrate, Yu Wei watches in silence, a faint smile hiding whatever emotion she feels beneath the surface.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Reincarnated Immortal Trope (转世仙人)**  
Yu Wei is a classic example of the *reincarnated Immortal* archetype—a cultivator who failed their Heavenly Tribulation in a past life but managed to reincarnate with their soul intact. Such characters often possess fragments of past-life memories, giving them access to secret arts or Dao insights beyond their current cultivation level. However, there’s a catch: the stronger the past life, the harder it is to unlock those memories. A Celestial Immortal-level figure who reincarnates might not even remember their past until they’re already an Earth Immortal again. Yu Wei’s “partial awakening” places her in the middle tier—stronger than a normal genius, but not yet able to casually tap into her former power.

**The Dao-Hierarchy at the Gathering**  
The novel reinforces a brutal truth of Xianxia combat: a cultivator who has mastered one complete Dao is a threat, but someone who has mastered three is a monster. Understanding a full Dao means you can draw on that specific law of reality directly—fire bends to your will, wind obeys, rainwater becomes your domain. Adding a second or third Dao means layering complementary laws on top of each other, creating a synergistic explosion of combat power. This is why three-Dao cultivators are virtually guaranteed top-ten slots at the Gathering: they’re stacking entire metaphysical systems on top of each other while everyone else is still operating on a single foundation.

**Dao-Companion (道侣) vs. Mere Spouse**  
In modern usage, *Dao-companion* can simply mean “spouse,” but Xianxia fiction imbues it with deeper weight. A Dao-companion is someone who walks the same path of cultivation, sharing insights and tempering one another’s Dao-heart. Ji Ning and Nine Lotos aren’t just romantically involved—they are making a public spiritual pact. This is why the chapter’s final scene carries such layered emotional gravity: Yu Wei’s silent smile isn’t just jealousy; it’s the recognition that two people she cares about have found a bond that transcends the mortal concept of marriage.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a quieter chapter packed with heavy lore drops and some serious emotional gut-punches. If you’ve been waiting for the romantic subplot to kick into gear, this is your moment—Ji Ning and Nine Lotos go official, and it’s as heartwarming as it is bittersweet for everyone watching. But don’t sleep on the technical talk: the conversation about the Immortal Destiny Gathering’s power tiers is pure gold for understanding just how high the ceiling really is. Plus, Yu Wei’s backstory hits like a freight train. A Xianxia orphan origin story is always tragic, but her “three years old, soul eruption” flashback is delivered with such raw economy that it lands harder than ten pages of melodrama. There’s also a subtle, painful parallel threaded through the chapter: both Yu Wei and Ji Ning lost their parents violently—but while Ji Ning is moving forward with a new partner by his side, Yu Wei is once again left standing alone, watching. That final silent smile of hers? That’s the kind of character work that separates great cultivation fiction from the forgettable stuff.

## Deep Dive: The Dao of Golems and Why It’s a Dead End
Mu Northson’s complaint might sound like a throwaway gag, but it actually hints at a core structural rule of the Xianxia universe. The reason golem-users are banned from the Immortal Destiny Gathering isn’t arbitrary—it’s because there are no *Daofathers* or *Celestial Immortals* who have fully comprehended the Dao of Golems. Why? Because the Dao of Golems involves understanding the physical properties of *everything* in heaven and earth to such a degree that you can replicate any natural process mechanically. The sheer breadth of knowledge required is so vast that no one in recorded history has ever fully mastered it. It’s a dao with no ceiling and no endgame—which makes it philosophically beautiful for those who walk it, but disqualifying for a tournament designed by major powers looking for disciples who can reach immortality. Northson’s path is a lonely one, and this chapter reminds us of that sacrifice with masterful subtlety.
