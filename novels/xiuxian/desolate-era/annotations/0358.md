## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow Daoists, because Ji Ning is about to step into the absolute heart of mystery. After the chaos of the Immortal Fortunes Assembly, the white-robed Immortal Jun keeps his promise and escorts Ji Ning to the most secluded, most guarded location in all the Three Realms: the Tristar Crescent Abode, the hidden sanctuary of the legendary Patriarch Subhuti. This chapter is less about explosive combat and more about *immersion*—a slow, awe-filled journey into a world within a world. We trade the frantic competition of the Grand Xia for the serene, almost mundane beauty of a cave-fortress that contains its own entire universe. But don’t let the peacefulness fool you. The deeper we go, the more we realize that the rules here are absolute, and the beings who dwell here are far more terrifying than any arena champion. Ji Ning is about to meet the family.

## Key Plot Points
- The chapter opens with Ji Ning, Uncle White, and Qingqing traveling through a bizarre, ever-changing void channel. The journey is unusually long, far longer than a standard spatial tear, because they are being guided by Patriarch Subhuti’s own power to reach his hidden pocket dimension.
- Immortal Jun explains that the Tristar Crescent Abode is not merely a mountain, but an entire “cave-fortress world” created by the Patriarch. It’s a self-contained reality with its own reincarnation cycle, completely free from the control of the Three Realms’ Heavenly Daos—a true sanctuary.
- Upon arrival, they are taken off the main path to meet a mysterious woodcutter. True to the Daoist theme of “hiding brilliance,” Ji Ning cannot sense even a trace of cultivation or pressure from this humble figure. He looks, walks, and sounds like a completely ordinary mortal.
- Immortal Jun shocks Ji Ning by revealing that this woodcutter is the *senior apprentice-brother*—the very first disciple of Subhuti. According to Jun, he is the undisputed number one in terms of combat power among all of Subhuti’s pupils, but has not left the cave-world since the Primordial Era shattered.
- They finally approach the mountain gate, marked by the iconic “Tristar Crescent Abode” boulder. The serene atmosphere is explained by an ironclad rule: any fighting on Mount Innerheart is punishable by instant soul-annihilation by the Patriarch himself.
- The chapter ends with them at the door of a simple Daoist temple, where Subhuti awaits them. Ji Ning is granted direct entry.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Riddle of the Heart (心)** The name “Tristar Crescent Abode” (斜月三星洞) is a classic piece of Chinese literary and Daoist wordplay. The character for “heart” (心) is visually constructed from three dots (the “three stars”) and a hook (the “crescent moon”). Patriarch Subhuti’s cave is quite literally a pictographic riddle for the word “heart.” This isn’t just a clever name; it reinforces the central Daoist tenet of seeking truth through internal cultivation. The journey to the Abode is not a physical one, but a journey to find one’s own heart and mind. By placing Ji Ning here, the author signals that his true growth will now be spiritual, not just a matter of stacking cultivation levels.

**The “Hidden Immortal” Trope** The senior apprentice-brother “Woodcutter” is a perfect archetype of the “Hidden Immortal.” In countless Xianxia stories, the most terrifying power doesn't come from ostentatious displays of golden light and roaring auras. It comes from the unassuming woodcutter, the old beggar, or the village idiot. True mastery of the Dao leads to a return to simplicity. The fact that Ji Ning cannot sense any power from him at all is the greatest possible testament to his strength. He has perfected the art of being ordinary, a state that is infinitely harder to achieve than simply being powerful.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a palate cleanser and a world-expander all in one. Pay very close attention to the absolute hierarchy being established. The Immortal Fortunes Assembly was for Wanxiang Adepts; the Daofathers and Empyrean Gods of the top ranks were distant observers. Here, Ji Ning is walking among the children of those observers. The senior apprentice-brother is so far beyond mere Celestial Immortals that his name is unknown to the outside world. This is a reminder that even within the power structure of the Three Realms, there are hidden realms of strength that the public never sees. Also, take note of Subhuti’s “iron law” against fighting. This creates a perfect pressure-free environment for Ji Ning to learn. For the first time in a long while, he won’t have to look over his shoulder. The focus now shifts entirely from “how do I survive” to “how do I understand the Dao.”

## The Mystery of the Senior Apprentice-Brother (Woodcutter)
Let’s talk about the elephant—or rather, the woodcutter—in the room. Who *is* this guy? While the novel doesn’t confirm his identity right away, the clues are massive. He is the first disciple of the mysterious Patriarch Subhuti and has lived since before the Three Realms were formed. He is so unfathomably powerful that he could easily be a figure from ancient Chinese myth reimagined. Some readers speculate he is a master of a specific Heavenly Dao, or perhaps even a manifestation of a certain concept. The key takeaway is that he represents the ultimate goal of cultivation: to be so in tune with the Dao that you are indistinguishable from a natural part of the world. He doesn’t need to look impressive because he *is* the Dao. His presence here is a silent lesson to Ji Ning about the true value of humility and inner power.
