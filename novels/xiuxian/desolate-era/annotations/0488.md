## Chapter Overview

Brace yourselves, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a brutal emotional rollercoaster that hits Ji Ning exactly where his Dao-heart is most vulnerable. After a long and agonizing wait—interrupted by his banishment to the Realm of Annihilation—Lord Yan finally delivers the news Ji Ning has been praying for: the reincarnation records of his parents, Ji Yichuan and Yuchi Snow. What starts as a burst of pure, heartbreaking hope quickly curdles into disbelief, fury, and a desperate, soul-shaking need for the truth. We watch the peerless Sword Immortal, the man who can face down armies, shatter as he stares at a book containing the aftermath of his parents' second life. This isn’t a battle of divine abilities; it’s a test of the rawest, most buried chambers of his heart. The chapter masterfully sets the stage for a journey into the very mechanisms of fate as Ji Ning, with the King of Hell of the Small Cycle of Reincarnation at his side, prepares to tear open the fabric of time itself.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Long-Awaited Report:** Lord Yan, King of Hell of the Small Cycle of Reincarnation, presents Ji Ning with two bamboo tomes containing the reincarnation records of his parents. The information confirms they were both reborn as mortals in the same commandery of the faraway Wuqiong World, seemingly fulfilling a karmic bond.
*   **A Second Life, A Second Death:** The initial relief is obliterated when Lord Yan reveals he investigated their second lives. He produces a book detailing their fates: Ji Yichuan, reborn as Liuhe Chuan, and Yuchi Snow, reborn as Dongliu Xue, both died in that world. Their story ended in tragedy a second time.
*   **A Dao-Heart Shatters:** Ji Ning’s composure completely breaks down. He is consumed by denial, fury, and a crushing sense of injustice. He cannot accept that his parents, who endured so much in their first life and found each other again, were given a second chance only to be cut down as "minor figures" on the path of cultivation.
*   **Demanding the Truth from Time Itself:** Refusing to trust mere written records, Ji Ning makes a decisive choice. He asks Lord Yan to take him to the heart of the Small Cycle of Reincarnation and use the Time Rewind spell to show him the actual judgment of his mother, Yuchi Snow. He insists on witnessing the truth with his own eyes.
*   **Mother, In Phantom:** The chapter ends on a deeply poignant note as the Time Rewind spell works, and Ji Ning sees a ghostly phantom of his mother being brought before the Judge. He is overwhelmed by emotion, wanting to reach out and touch her but knowing it is impossible, as the scene from the past plays out before him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter offers a fascinating look into the administrative machinery of the Netherworld in a post-cosmic-war Xianxia setting. We delve into the **Small Cycle of Reincarnation**, a localized version of the Six Paths established by Daofather Crimsonbright after the original system was shattered. This isn't just a back-alley operation; it’s a massive bureaucracy managing dozens of major worlds with its own Kings of Hell, Judges, Ox-Head and Horse-Faced enforcers.

The concept of **Karmic Merit** (功德) comes into play here. Judge Cui, the legendary official Ji Ning met in his past life, could alter a judgment, but it consumed his own merit. In this bureaucracy, a standard judgment follows the Heavenly Dao's natural flow—a karmic calculation for the soul’s next life. The fact that a King of Hell is accompanying Ji Ning showcases his immense "face" (面子), a currency of reputation and power that can bypass standard protocol.

Finally, the **Time Rewind** (时光逆转) spell itself is a powerful tool of the Immortal realm. It's a visualization spell that projects the "shadow" of past events, allowing an observer to witness history without interacting with it. For a cultivator's Dao-heart, this can be more torturous than any physical attack, as it shows you the past without granting the power to change it.

## Reading Guide

Get ready to put on your emotional armor, because this is a heavy chapter. The pacing is deliberately slower here, focusing entirely on Ji Ning's internal world. Don't rush through it. Feel his initial, giddy hope when he reads the good news about his parents reincarnating together. Let that gut-punch of Lord Yan’s follow-up hit you just as hard. The author doesn't let Ji Ning (or us) dwell in happy discovery; he immediately shoves us into the abyss of a second tragedy.

Pay close attention to Ji Ning’s language. He's usually a master of control, but here, his sentences become fragmented and desperate: “How could this be?” “I don’t believe it!” This is a rare moment of raw, unfiltered humanity from him. His demand to go to the Small Cycle of Reincarnation isn't just a request; it's a power move, a desperate assertion of agency in a universe that seems to have conspired against his family. This chapter is the quiet before the storm, setting up a crucial quest for truth and, perhaps, a chance to rewrite a terrible script.
