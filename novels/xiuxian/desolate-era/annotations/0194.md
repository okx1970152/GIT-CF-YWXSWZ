## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, strap in, because this chapter is a double-feature of raw emotion and cold, hard Xianxia logistics. We open with Ji Ning and Mu Northson stumbling upon a horror show: a demon mountain where humans are bred like livestock for the table. The raw, visceral anger from our protagonists is palpable, and their swift, merciless justice against the lesser demons is a stark reminder of the brutal power dynamics in this world. But Ji Ning's head is cooler than the molten core of a chaosworld. He immediately shifts from righteous fury to pragmatic problem-solving, planning to relocate the traumatized survivors to the only safe haven in the wasteland—the Yinglong Guard camp. This isn't just a good deed; it's a litmus test for the protagonist's character, showing his Dao-heart is as much about protecting the weak as it is about personal power.

Then, the camera cuts to a far more familiar face—the little cyan serpent from the Yan Mountains! The journey of this Skygreen Serpent demon, whom Ji Ning once named “Little Snake-Snake,” has been a solo adventure of incredible fortune and growth. We see it luxuriating in a treasure trove of Ten-Thousand-Year Ice, its power swelling from Zifu to Wanxiang over three short years. Its internal monologue is a perfect mix of scheming pride, simmering vengeance, and a surprising flicker of warmth toward the human boy who respected it. But the chapter ends on a note of impending violence as a pair of cultivators, hunting for Wanxiang corpses to qualify for the Yinglong Guard, set their sights on the very same cave. This is a masterclass in parallel storytelling, weaving together the paths of two old acquaintances destined to cross again.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Human Pens:** Ji Ning and Mu Northson discover a pit of humans, generations deep, being kept as food by the elephant demon. The scene is deliberately horrifying, showcasing a casual evil that is normalized in the wasteland.
*   **Swift Justice and Pragmatic Rescue:** Mu Northson annihilates the demon guards in a fit of rage. Ji Ning, however, takes command, using his divine sense to calm the traumatized slaves and loading them onto their dragon-headed ship. He decides to take them to the Yinglong Guard's camp, acknowledging they can't protect the group in the wild.
*   **A New Alliance Falters:** The Peacock Demoness, leader of the surviving Wanxiang monsters, investigates the scene. She correctly deduces their allies were killed by a powerful cultivator and wisely chooses not to pursue, prioritizing her surviving members' safety over revenge—a sign of a cunning leader.
*   **A Familiar Serpent's Fortune:** The narrative switches to the Skygreen Serpent from Book 1. We learn it has been in the wasteland for three years, growing from Zifu to Wanxiang mid-stage by exploiting a rare Ten-Thousand-Year Ice pool. It plots revenge against Tie Muzhan and muses fondly about the "beast-hide youth."
*   **Hunters Become the Hunted:** Two human cultivators, Wanxiang Adepts themselves, have received word of a lone Skygreen Serpent in the area. They explicitly need its corpse to complete their own quota for the Yinglong Guard trial, setting up an imminent deadly ambush.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter digs into several pervasive Xianxia tropes that color the world's harsh reality. First, the **"Human Pens" (人圈, rén juān)**. This is a dark, recurring motif in Xianxia depicting mortals as a renewable resource for demonic cultivators or great demons. It's a literary device to underscore the existential stakes of the mortal realm and the absolute importance of power for survival. The 'slave mentality' (奴性) that Ji Ning observes is a sobering consequence, a complete loss of self that comes from generations of captivity.

Next, we have the **Alliance of Demon Kings (妖王联盟)**. The Peacock Demoness's calculated retreat is a perfect example of a Xianxia villain's decision tree. Revenge is not an emotional impulse; it's a risk/reward calculation. Her words—"half of us would die"—show a cold pragmatism that separates the leaders from the beasts. This is common in settings where resources and territory are everything; a smart leader knows when to cut their losses.

Finally, the chapter highlights a classic Cradle of Cultivation trope: the **Treasure Den with a Time Limit (机缘之地)**. The Ten-Thousand-Year Ice pool is a "lucky encounter" (奇遇) for the serpent, a spot of concentrated natural energy that fuels its rapid growth. But it's also a vulnerability. In Xianxia, a cultivator's secret cultivation spot is never truly safe; the very energy it emits can be detected by other, more predatory cultivators, turning a safe haven into a death trap.

### Dharmic powers (法相)

The manifestation of a cultivator's internal energy as an external, often giant, form. Mu Northson uses his dharmic powers to conjure the massive golden hand that crushes the demons and breaks the prison chains.

## Reading Guide

*   **Closing in on Fate:** This chapter is a masterful convergence. Ji Ning is heading to the Yinglong Guard camp with a boatload of refugees. The Skygreen Serpent is about to be attacked by the very same type of candidate. The wasteland is shrinking around these characters, and their reunion—if it happens—promises to be explosive.
*   **The Debt of "Little Snake-Snake":** Don't forget the history. Ji Ning spared this serpent, danced with it, and helped it comprehend 'Void Shuttle.' The serpent remembers this debt. In Xianxia, personal gratitude (人情) is a powerful binding force. A saved life, especially given freely by a mortal to a demon, carries immense narrative weight.
*   **Morality in the Wasteland:** How Ji Ning handles the refugees is a key character benchmark for his journey. He's not just a killing machine; he's a lord-in-the-making. His decision to use his limited resources (time, mana, safety) to help helpless strangers is a direct test of his Dao-heart, proving his 'protectiveness' isn't just lip service.
