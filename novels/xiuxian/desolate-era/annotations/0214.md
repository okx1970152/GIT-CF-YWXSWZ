## Chapter Overview
A brand-new volume kicks off with a sharp pivot from survival horror to bureaucratic induction! After the blood-soaked trials of Mount Yujue, Ji Ning and the forty-four other survivors arrive at the Crimson Dragon Mountains and officially become One-Claw Yinglong Guards. But before they can strut around the Grand Xia with their shiny new badges, they have to sit through a mesmerizing—and I mean *literally mesmerizing*—orientation session led by the dangerously charming Daoist Yulan. The chapter serves up a generous platter of world-building, detailing the Guard’s privilege ladder, its merit-based reward system, and the critical fork in the road: stay a freewheeling guest elder, or swear total loyalty as an Inner Guard. Oh, and we also get a wholesome reunion with Uncle White and a delightfully shameless new tag-team partner who declares them the “Three Heroes of Yan Mountain.” Get ready, fellow Daoists—the bureaucracy of immortals is surprisingly spicy.

## Key Plot Points
- The fifty-two survivors teleport into the Crimson Dragon Mountains and are instructed to wait for an official Yinglong Guard greeter.
- A massive flying ship descends, carrying the stunningly seductive Primal Daoist, Yulan, along with a retinue of beauties and a flock of spirit-beasts.
- Ji Ning is joyfully reunited with Uncle White (the Whitewater Hound) and reveals his new contract with the Skygreen Serpent, Qingqing, who enthusiastically dubs their trio the “Three Heroes of Yan Mountain.”
- Daoist Yulan inspects the heads of the Wanxiang great demons to confirm eligibility; seven unlucky souls who failed to secure a head are separated from the main group.
- The remaining forty-five board the ship, where Yulan’s cultivation-based charm technique proves so potent that even hardened Wanxiang Adepts struggle to maintain their composure.
- Yulan distributes guidebooks outlining the Yinglong Guard’s privileges (basically lawless impunity across the entire Grand Xia) and responsibilities (a laughably light mission quota every ten years).
- The real hook is revealed: high-tier divine abilities and secret arts are locked behind rank, requiring either slow promotion or pledging absolute loyalty as an Inner Guard of the Grand Xia.
- The chapter closes by formally announcing the start of Volume Nine, *Winged Serpent Lake Ji Ning*, signaling a shift in focus toward Ji Ning’s independent cultivation and adventures.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Yinglong Guard’s Dual-Track System**  
The setup here is a brilliant piece of Xianxia worldbuilding that mirrors real-world historical institutions. The Outer Guard system (guest elders with light duties) was a strategic move to attract free-spirited rogue cultivators who loathe being tied down—a core tenet of Daoist philosophy that prizes wandering freedom (*xian you*). The Inner Guard, in contrast, is modeled on the imperial bodyguards of ancient China, demanding absolute loyalty in exchange for state-backed resources. This genius “both-and” structure is why the Yinglong Guard became the Grand Xia’s supreme martial force, reminiscent of how the Ming Dynasty’s Jinyiwei combined imperial police power with elite espionage.

**Charm Arts and the Dao of Seduction**  
Yulan’s techniques aren’t mere flirting; they’re a formalized cultivation path that weaponizes allure. Unlike innate beauty (like Yun Lu’s), Yulan’s charm is a *secret art*—a honed Dao of enticement that can override even a Wanxiang Adept’s iron will. This traces back to ancient Daoist alchemical traditions where sexual energy (*jing*) was considered a potent force, but in the novel it’s pushed to extremes: a Primal Daoist’s charm art is practically a psychological domain, bypassing reason and targeting the instinctual soul. The dramatic effect on the cultivators (blushing, closing eyes, sweating) is a masterful display of soft power that no amount of brute force can counter.

**The Merit Economy**  
Karmic Merit isn’t just a spiritual score—it’s the Yinglong Guard’s internal currency. This mirrors the historical “merit system” used by Daoist monasteries and imperial courts, where good deeds and services were tallied for promotion. In the novel’s context, it creates a fascinating tension: the most powerful techniques are locked behind a grindwall of contribution, incentivizing Inner Guard loyalty while letting Outer Guards coast on basic duties. It’s a clean narrative mechanism that explains why powerful cultivators would bother with bureaucracy instead of just hoarding power in a cave.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a classic “system introduction” breather after the intense survival arc of Mount Yujue, but don’t skip it—the Yinglong Guard’s structure becomes the backbone of Ji Ning’s resource acquisition for volumes to come. Pay close attention to the merit rank requirements: they foreshadow the enormous power gap Ji Ning will need to bridge before he can access top-tier techniques. The introduction of the Inner Guard choice is also a subtle character test—will Ji Ning ever bend the knee to a dynasty, or will he forever walk his own sword-path? The reunion with Uncle White and the addition of Qingqing to the party inject warmth and humor, while Yulan’s barely-suppressed charm attack reminds us that in this world, even paperwork comes with peril. Prepare for a volume that pivots from survival to strategic cultivation, with Winged Serpent Lake as the new stage.
