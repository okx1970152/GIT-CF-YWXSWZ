## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists! The dust has barely settled on the mountain belly massacre, and Ji Ning has just taken a monumental leap forward. In this chapter, we shift from raw combat to the quiet, strategic moments that decide the fate of a clan. Ning emerges from the elemental ore vein as a fully-fledged Zifu Disciple, but the Primordial Liquid that fueled his breakthrough is gone. Now he faces a brutal calculus: burn the rest to chase a fleeting power boost, or save it and keep his future path clear? As the Ji Clan huddles to count their spoils and prepare for the inevitable counterattack, a storm is brewing in Snowdragon City. The surviving enemy Zifu Disciples are panicking—and sending out a desperate call for reinforcements. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia pacing; it’s the quiet before the apocalypse, where every decision feels like it’s written in stone.

## Key Plot Points

*   **Ning’s Breakthrough is Complete:** Ji Ning has successfully opened his Zifu using the Primordial Liquid, and even consolidated his early stage. He realizes he could push to mid-stage, but wisely chooses not to, fearing it would ruin his future prospects with the low-grade <Water Element Art>. He stores the remaining Liquid for later.
*   **The Water-Fire Lotus vs. The Earth:** Ning uses his Water-Fire Lotus to blast a path out of the deep underground ore vein. He emerges to a tense reunion with the clan elders.
*   **The Loot Pool:** The clan discusses the distribution of treasures from the slain Snowdragon cultivators. Ji Ning makes his request clear: he needs graded flying swords for his sword formation, the more the better. The clan can only offer five, far short of his ideal of eighty-one.
*   **Final Arrangements:** The elders scatter to make their own preparations, treating this as a farewell. Ji Yichuan says he must go see Uncle White (the Whitewater Hound) at Winged Serpent Lake one last time, a testament to their bond.
*   **The Enemy’s Panic:** Back in Snowdragon City, Beizi Shan and the other surviving Zifu Disciples are in a panic. They learn of the colossal ore vein and realize the Ji Clan is rich beyond measure. They decide to call in dozens of fellow disciples from across the region for an overwhelming assault.
*   **The Final Step:** Alone in his quiet room, Ji Ning uses his token to enter the mysterious aquatic palace, the ancient ruin left by Celestial Immortal Juhua, seeking the power that will let him survive the coming storm.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Primordial Liquid (元液): The Ultimate Foundation Pill**
Let’s break down why Ji Ning is so cautious with this stuff. Primordial Liquid is described as the ultimate essence of natural energy, so pure it can be absorbed instantly without taxing the body. This makes it a legendary resource, comparable to a Nine Revolutions Golden Pill—an alchemical miracle. In normal cultivation, a Xiantian expert has to spend ages refining elemental stones, slowly compressing the energy, removing impurities, and then converting it into usable power for the Zifu. Primordial Liquid skips *all* of that. It’s the cheat code to a perfect foundation. Ning’s decision to stop is a classic Xianxia trope: knowing when to be greedy for *power* and when to be greedy for *potential*. Burning it for a minor realm boost now would be a one-way ticket to a capped growth ceiling.

**The Zifu (紫府): A Solo Pocket Dimension**
When a cultivator opens their Zifu, they aren’t just leveling up—they are literally changing their anatomy. The term ‘Violet Palace’ comes from Daoist internal alchemy, where it represents a chamber in the brain. In *Desolate Era*, it’s a physical, dimensional space created inside the body. This is the new reservoir for their Yuan power, a place so deep and vast that it replaces the old Dantian. This is why a Zifu Disciple crushes a Xiantian cultvator: they aren’t just stronger; they are fighting with an entirely different energy infrastructure. It’s the difference between running a car on AA batteries versus tapping into a dedicated nuclear reactor.

**The ‘Family Tax’ for a Genius**
The scene where the Ji Clan leaders admit they can only scrape together five flying swords is a stark reminder of the hierarchy in these worlds. A top-tier clan can equip their geniuses fully. A small clan like the Ji Clan has to sacrifice and bargain to even give their prodigy a fighting chance. This is the gritty economic reality of tribal Xianxia—a single genius can be the clan’s salvation, but supporting that genius can also bankrupt them. The ‘family pact’ is an emotional weight most Western fantasy rarely touches on without direct magic.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a **structuring chapter**. Don't skim it for action; read it for the *setup*. Pay close attention to the geometry of the conflict:
1.  **Ji Ning’s Internal Scaling:** He’s a Zifu Disciple now, but he’s still worried about facing ‘a batch of experts.’ The gap between him and a small army of veteran Zifu Disciples is terrifyingly real.
2.  **The Enemy’s Calculus:** Beizi Shan is not a bumbling idiot. He’s a cold, pragmatic killer. When he loses three members of his team, he immediately decides to call for forty more. This is the textbook ‘overwhelming force’ strategy. He is terrified of Ning’s talent and is choosing to drown it in numbers.
3.  **The Drama of the Loot:** Ning needs *eighty-one* graded swords. The clan can give him *five*. This mismatch creates a deep tension. How will he fight the coming war with such limited resources? The answer is implied to be the aquatic palace.
4.  **The Tragic Interlude:** Ji Yichuan going to see his beast-companion Whitewater Hound for a final talk is a heavy moment. It’s a modern-day ‘arming for battle’ trope, but in Xianxia, it means ‘I might not come back from this one.’ The bond between man and beast, built on shared battles, is powerful.
