## Chapter Overview
This is it—the final stretch. Our crew enters the main hall of the Sorcerer River immortal estate, where Shao Yanong is one sub-hall nexus away from total ownership. But as expected, the big boss hall doesn't surrender without a fight. An illusory formation snaps shut on them, and it's *not* just the usual pushover phantom maze—this one has a brain behind it. Xiangliu Fang, the resident powerhouse Fiendgod, does what Fiendgods do best: brute-force the entire thing into submission. But the real tension is the ticking clock. While Shao Yanong smugly calls for a celebration feast mid-refinement, a pack of Qiongqi golems led by a sentient, scheming general is already plotting the perfect assassination strike. The stage is set for a final, desperate gamble between the attackers and the defenders.

## Key Plot Points
- The party reaches the main hall of the Sorcerer River estate; Shao Yanong identifies the bronze lamp as the last sub-hall nexus and sends a Zifu death warrior to refine it.
- The hall reacts instantly: a powerful illusory formation activates, trapping everyone in a peach blossom garden.
- Xiangliu Fang assesses the formation; it generates Azurewood Divine Thunder and is stronger than the one in the Treasure Hall.
- The formation unexpectedly attacks Shao Yanong directly, revealing it is being *controlled* rather than just running on autopilot.
- Xiangliu Fang deduces the truth: a powerful golem with a living soul sealed inside is directing the formation, but lacking elemental Ki, it can only use 20-30% of its power.
- With a single, monstrous technique—turning his fingers into giant serpents that 'strangle' the formation's energy—Xiangliu Fang tears the illusion apart.
- The black-armored leader of the eighteen Qiongqi golems strategizes: they have one hour before the estate is lost, and their only hope is to assassinate Shao Yanong in a single all-out strike.
- Back in the hall, Shao Yanong, overconfident, orders a celebration feast set up despite the lingering danger, ordering his wary companions to sit down and drink.
- Xue Hongyi internally counts his luck: survived the golem attack that killed Buyou, found an Immortal-ranked treasure, and escaped the thunder—convinced fortune is on his side.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Qiongqi Golems (穷奇傀儡):** Qiongqi is one of the Four Perils of Chinese mythology—a winged tiger-like monster that devours good people and rewards the wicked. Naming these golems after it immediately flags them as dangerous, cunning, and morally inverted opponents, not just brute-force guards.
- **Golems vs. Living Beings in Xianxia:** A golem (傀儡) is a construct animated by a core mechanism, usually a spirit-seal. A golem *with a soul* is a much rarer, higher-tier creation—it can think, adapt, and even execute complex tactics (like controlling a formation), making it far deadlier than a simple automaton. Xiangliu Fang's insight that the leader has a "living soul inside" is the Xianxia equivalent of realizing the security AI is actually a ghost in the machine.
- **'Breaking' a Formation by Hand:** Xiangliu Fang's method—physically grasping the "node" of the illusion with giant serpent-fingers and crushing it—is a signature Fiendgod combat style. In Xianxia logic, formations have tangible energy structures (nodes, meridians, cores). A cultivator strong enough in body-refining can bypass the need to 'solve' the formation like a puzzle and just *sunder* its physical manifestation. It's the difference between picking a lock and kicking the whole doorframe off its hinges.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch Shao Yanong's mask slip:** Notice how he switches from "we're all in this together" to barking orders ("I told you to sit!") the moment his guests show caution. His hospitality is a leash, not a gift. His confidence is entirely borrowed from Xiangliu Fang's fists.
- **Xue Hongyi's "luck" is a trap:** Pay close attention to how Xue Hongyi interprets his survival streak. In Xianxia, believing you are "destined for fortune" is the fastest way to get sloppy. His survival so far has been pure circumstance and being *near* the strong, not any actual merit of his own. That kind of karmic arrogance usually gets you devoured right before the finale.
- **The golem leader's intelligence:** The black-armored general isn't just a guard; he's a brilliant tactician. He correctly identified that killing Shao Yanong is the only win condition, he knows exactly how much time he has, and he's waiting for the perfect moment. This isn't a final boss—it's a last-ditch ambush predator, and it changes the fight from a dungeon crawl to a hunting party.
