## Chapter Overview
Hold onto your scrolls, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a whirlwind gold rush of epic proportions. The crew has breached the fourth branch hall of Celestial Immortal Wu Jiang’s estate, and Shaoyin Nong is fuming over the insane cost three treasures down, and he’s still got a whole fifth branch hall to crack. But the real treasure? It’s the Daoist Scripture Hall. Ji Ning, Xue Hongyi, Mu Northson, and the rest descend into a free-for-all scramble for ancient technique manuals, snatching up hundreds of thousands of books in a chaotic, no-holds-barred race. Shaoyin Nong, meanwhile, plays the long game, settling down to refine the hall’s central nexus, promising himself total control. It’s a chapter of greed, calculation, and the cold reality that in a world where power is knowledge, the ones who grab first and think later often win the day.

## Key Plot Points
- Shaoyin Nong’s burning frustration over the cost of forcing the fourth branch hall—three top-tier treasures gone, and the fifth one promises to be even more painful.
- Xue Hongyi suggests negotiating with the demons to enter the fifth hall for free. Shaoyin Nong utterly dismisses him as a fool, revealing the real value of demon Dao-soldier slaves as a massive financial asset.
- The discovery of the Daoist Scripture Hall triggers a mad scramble as everyone, including Nine Lotos and Uncle White, begins frantically grabbing books, with the Skygreen Serpent leading the charge.
- Everyone quickly discovers most books are abridged—but that doesn’t stop them from cleaning out the entire hall, bookshelves and all.
- Ji Ning and the others deduce there must be a hidden chamber for complete editions, sparking a second gold-rush.
- Whitewater Hound discovers the secret chamber of complete editions, calling Ji Ning over. They engage in a fierce three-way snatch with Cangjiang and Xue Hongyi.
- Outcome: thousands of complete Human and Earth-ranked techniques. Nothing top-tier like Heaven-rank or above, but a huge haul for the Ji Clan’s legacy.
- Shaoyin Nong returns to the central nexus of the hall, beginning a slow, methodical refinement that will take an hour, aiming to discover all hidden treasures.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The Daoist Scripture Hall (道藏殿)** is a staple of any Xianxia sect or major power—it’s the library of techniques, secret arts, and divine abilities that forms the core of the clan’s heritage. Having a strong collection means you can train generations of disciples; losing it means your bloodline’s power declines over time.
- **Abridged vs Complete Editions**: In a sect’s scripture hall, the most valuable techniques are locked away as abridged versions (showing only the first few pages) to prevent theft, while full copies are hidden behind additional restrictions or secret chambers. Finding a full copy of a rare, Heaven-ranked or above technique is considered a legendary find—and a massive Contribution Point score if you donate it to the sect.
- **Dao-soldiers and the Wealth Calculus**: Shaoyin Nong’s insistence on enslaving the demons rather than letting them go is pure Xianxia economics. Demons that can wear Dao-soldier armor are a reusable military asset; owning tens of thousands of them, especially Yaksha-grade, compensates for nearly any treasure spent to capture them. Selling them to your clan is seen as a colossal profit, not a loss.

## Reading Guide
If you’ve ever wanted to feel the adrenaline of throwing open a vault of ancient scrolls with your buddies while a rival noble fumes in the corner, this is your chapter. The rush of the scramble, the sinking feeling when you realize half the books are junk, and the thrill of discovering a hidden chamber—it’s all here. But pay close attention to the *other* dynamic: Shaoyin Nong isn’t enjoying the chaos; he’s methodically setting up to control *everything* by refining the hall’s core. That’s classic Xianxia villainy—patience, long-term planning, and the assumption that brute wealth will eventually win out. Ji Ning, meanwhile, does what Ji Ning does best—stay pragmatic, calculate the value of each prize, and secure what he can for his clan’s future. The competition isn’t over, but this chapter makes one thing clear: the estate’s biggest secrets haven’t been uncovered yet.

## Deep Dive: The Economics of Immortal Plunder
Let’s break the math down, Xianxia-style. Shaoyin Nong burned through three top-tier treasures to breach the fourth branch hall. That’s a painful sum. But if he captures the tens of thousands of demon Dao-soldiers inside, he can sell them back to his clan as martial assets. The Oceanwave and Yaksha armor sets alone are worth a fortune—each worn by a disciplined soldier-demon that can operate formations. The net profit? Enormous. In the world of Immortal cultivation, war is expensive, but slavery and plunder are the fastest ways to refill your coffers. It’s not cruelty for cruelty’s sake; it’s brutally efficient economics. Xue Hongyi’s suggestion to let them go free was naive—he was thinking like a mortal, not a clan heir. And Shaoyin Nong? He’s thinking like a lord of the Grand Xia: everything is a transaction.
