## Chapter Overview

In this explosive chapter, IET delivers on a promise that’s been building for dozens of chapters: the full, unleashed might of Ji Ning’s Primaltwin. What starts as a cleanup operation—a surgical, almost contemptuous execution of the seven Dharma Kings—escalates into a full-blown crisis when a new, horrifyingly powerful faction emerges from thin air. The chapter is a masterclass in power scale demonstration, showing off Ji Ning’s new heights while simultaneously slamming the readers with the cold reality that the real game hasn’t even started. Get ready for a satisfying slaughter, a strategic mind-game, and a cliffhanger that will leave you shouting at your screen.

## Key Plot Points

* **The Slaughter of the Seven Dharma Kings**: Ji Ning’s Primaltwin, wielding the nine top-grade Immortal-ranked Li Tian swords, executes the Blood God Church’s most feared team in a single, overwhelming sequence. The text makes a point of showing the gap in treasure quality and sword-art tier.
* **Blooddrop’s Misjudgment**: The Church Lord initially scoffs at Ji Ning’s Primaltwin deployment, believing his seven generals can handle it. His shock is palpable as his forces are annihilated in seconds, forcing him into a defensive, revenge-driven maneuver.
* **The Fortress Ship and a False Flag**: Blooddrop retreats into his turtle-shell treasure ship, swearing to kill the Diancai Immortal. This creates a deliberate misdirection, making it seem like he’s the final, desperate threat, which serves to lower the reader’s guard.
* **The Curtain Rises: The Bashe Formation**: The real threat arrives without warning via a Grand Teleportation Dao-seal—an enormous expenditure that signals the attacker’s wealth and determination. Twelve white-robed specialists instantly manifest a Primordial Godbeast Bashe, a legendary entity from the age of chaos, and swallow Ji Ning whole.
* **Eyes on the Prize**: Throughout his fight with Blooddrop, Ji Ning is secretly wary of an unknown “threat,” holding back his full power. This instinctual caution is validated by the Bashe attack, proving his Dao-heart and instincts are top-tier, even if they weren’t enough to prevent the ambush.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Power of Legacy Treasures (The Qianqiu Sword)**: Ji Ning’s sword isn’t just a random weapon. It’s described as a “nearly Purely Yang” treasure, an artificial artifact that, unlike a true innate Purely Yang treasure, has massive Ki consumption requirements. This is a classic Xianxia trope: you can wield a super-weapon, but not at its full capacity until you level up. Ji Ning only now, at the late Void stage, unlocks its true power. It’s a “level-locked” rarity that rewards patience and cultivation over raw chance.

**The Primaltwin Instincts**: Separating a Primaltwin from the true body in combat is considered a suicidal risk in Xianxia. You’re halving your own defensive and reactive capacity, inviting a “defeat in detail.” Ji Ning does this because his analytical mind has calculated that the Primaltwin, armed with the Li Tian swords, is a force multiplier against the weaker seven, while his true body can hold a line against Blooddrop. It’s a high-risk, high-reward tactical decision that shows his confidence in his own power and his mastery of simultaneous parallel operations.

**The Bashe (巴蛇)**: The Bashe is not just a “big snake.” In ancient Chinese mythology (specifically the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*), it’s a legendary serpent so massive it could swallow an elephant and digest it for three years. In the novel’s cosmology, it is raised to the level of a Primordial Godbeast—an entity born from chaos itself. The formation technique to manifest it is considered a supreme cooperative art, far more advanced than the simple “cooperative blood dragon” the seven Dharma Kings used. Its appearance signals that the enemy is playing in the big leagues now.

## Reading Guide

**Why the Seven Dharma Kings Were Destroyed So Easily**: Don’t be fooled into thinking they were weak. The text explicitly states they were “virtually undefeatable below the Celestial Immortal level.” Ji Ning’s Primaltwin destroyed them because he is a multi-layered powerhouse: a top-tier sword-art (Daofather-created + perfected), a top-tier sword formation (Li Tian), and **nine** top-tier swords. In a Xianxia 1v1, every upgrade in treasure quality multiplies your power. Ji Ning having nine peak-tier treasures for a 7v1 is a qualitative leap, not a quantitative one.

**The Architect of the Ambush**: The chapter brilliantly sets up an apparent final boss (Blooddrop) only to show he is nothing but a pawn. The real enemy, the white-robed leader, is using a Grand Teleportation Dao-seal—an item so rare it would make a lesser sect bankrupt—just as a *transportation fee*. This tells the reader that the hidden faction has resources and determination far beyond the Blood God Church. The mystery of who sent them is one of the most potent hooks in the current arc.

**Face vs. Overwhelming Force**: When his forces are shattered, Blooddrop doesn’t flee in panic. He instead enacts revenge and looks for a new reward. This is a key trait of a Xianxia villain; maintaining “face” and a cool, strategic demeanor in the face of disaster is common, but his underlying madness is the real danger.
