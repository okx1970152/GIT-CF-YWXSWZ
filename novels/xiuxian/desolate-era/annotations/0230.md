## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists—the War God Hall’s third level is no joke. After a breathtaking half-month of Dao comprehension inside the Stellar Hall, Ji Ning demonstrates a terrifying leap in his sword art by executing the third stance of the Three-Foot Sword, *Sword Light Flashes*, leaving both Yellow Bear and Black Ox genuinely impressed. But when he asks about his odds against the third level, he gets a sobering “thirty percent.” That’s it. Thirty percent. And yet, Ji Ning doesn’t flinch. Why? Because his Crimsonbright Diagram is about to pop into the Wanxiang stage, and he refuses to let a perfectly good attempt go to waste. So he marches in—and what does he find? A golden-armored general who speaks of “a sliver of my divine power” and smiles like Ji Ning is a warm-up snack. The chapter ends with Ji Ning realizing the Water Mansion holds a secret far bigger than he ever imagined. Cue the ominous music.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning emerges from his Stellar Hall epiphany, having glimpsed the complete Grand Dao of the Sword. His sword-qi has become more condensed and profound.
- He demonstrates the third stance of the Three-Foot Sword, “Sword Light Flashes,” leaving a visible trail in the air—proof that his comprehension has skyrocketed.
- Yellow Bear praises the creator of the Three-Foot Sword (Immortal Beixing) as more talented than Celestial Immortal Juhua, triggering a bickering match with Black Ox, who fiercely defends his master.
- Ji Ning insists on challenging the War God Hall’s third level despite only 30% odds, reasoning that his upcoming breakthrough to Wanxiang will grant new attempts anyway—wasting the current one is unacceptable.
- Inside the third level, the trial manifests not as a beast or formation but as a golden-armored general wielding twin hammers, who identifies himself as a clone formed from a sliver of divine power.
- The general exudes a threat level higher than ordinary Primal Daoists, shocking Ji Ning and hinting at the Water Mansion’s hidden secrets.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The War God Hall’s attempt economy** is a classic Xianxia resource-management trope: every major cultivation breakthrough grants two fresh attempts, but unused attempts disappear. Ji Ning’s logic here is pure min-maxing—he’d rather risk dying at 30% now than lose the slot entirely. This “use it or lose it” mechanic is common in Chinese cultivation novels, forcing protagonists to constantly make high-stakes decisions about timing.

**Divine power clones** introduce a power-level clue. In the Fiendgod Body Refining path, Zifu stage gives you Blood-Drop Rebirth, Wanxiang lets you reshape your face, and Primal stage lets you split off a clone—but that clone can’t act independently. A clone that acts on its own, formed from a mere *sliver* of divine power, indicates the original body is at *least* a Void God (Void-level Fiendgod). That’s a being on par with the ancient Fiendgods who roamed before the Three Realms stabilized. The fact that such a clone guards the third level means the Water Mansion’s real inheritance is locked behind far greater trials.

**Immortal Beixing vs. Celestial Immortal Juhua**: The contrast between “Loose Immortal who lived over a million years” and “Celestial Immortal who became a Loose Immortal” is subtle but important. Juhua was a Celestial Immortal who fell to Loose Immortal status during his tribulations? Actually, the text says he was an Earth Immortal who couldn’t pass War God Hall’s seventh level and ended up a Loose Immortal. Beixing, on the other hand, was a Loose Immortal who lived over a million years purely by surviving his tribulations—a sign of either incredible luck or profound Dao insight. Yellow Bear’s judgment that Beixing’s talent surpasses Juhua’s is therefore not just dogmatic.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in pacing: it starts with a serene, almost meditative training montage, builds to a boast-and-bicker comedy section between two artifact spirits, then pivots to an ominous cliffhanger. Pay close attention to the golden-armored general’s words: “a sliver of my divine power.” That’s the smoking gun. Any cultivator who can project an independent clone from a fragment of power is operating on an entirely different scale—think continental destroyer vs. city wrecker. For readers new to Xianxia, this is your first hint that the Water Mansion’s trials are not merely tests of strength but gateways to a legacy that predates the current order of the Three Realms.

Also, relish the banter between Yellow Bear and Black Ox. These two ancient artifact spirits have been locked in a sibling-style rivalry for eons, and their petty squabbling over whose master was more gifted adds a warmth that balances the cold-blooded power scaling. That’s classic Xianxia texture: epic scope with moments of levity.
