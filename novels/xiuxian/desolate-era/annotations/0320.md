## Chapter Overview

The first trial of the Immortal Fortunes Assembly is officially declared, and the scale is absolutely horrifying. More than one hundred thousand of the most talented Wanxiang Adepts from across the Grand Xia Dynasty and beyond are unceremoniously dumped into the Grand Xia Emperor's Pure Yang treasure, the Brightmoon Landscape Painting, with a single, brutal directive: kill or be outlasted. The rules are simple and merciless—only 80 to 96 survivors will make the cut. It's a raw-numbers game that even the most confident genius has to respect. Ji Ning, Yu Wei, and the Slovenly Adept share a moment of quiet dread before the chaos begins. And while the slaughter hasn't yet started, the tension is palpable as countless spectators, including Nine Lotos and Yuchi Xiyue, lock their gazes onto the painting, praying for their champions to survive.

## Key Plot Points

* The Grand Xia Emperor dispatches Prince Qi to announce the rules: over 109,000 Wanxiang Adepts will enter the Brightmoon Landscape Painting for a one-year free-for-all.
* Survivors are determined by the number of talisman-tokens collected; the top 80 token holders by year's end pass, or if only 80 remain earlier, the trial ends.
* If the year expires with more than 80 alive, a final purge reduces the excess to 16 more survivors, capping the pass list at 96.
* Use of Dao talismans, puppets, or strange artifacts is absolutely forbidden—violators are summarily executed.
* The massive painting is displayed for all to watch, turning the slaughter into a public spectacle for Celestial Immortals and common observers alike.
* The Grand Xia Emperor muses privately on the difference between karmic merit and luck, hinting at deeper forces at play behind the assembly.
* Nearby Celestial Immortals openly discuss the collapse of the Six Paths of Reincarnation decades ago, suggesting a hidden connection to the selection's sudden brutality.
* Ji Ning drops into a mountain valley, instantly draws his swords, and avoids using divine sense to stay undetected.
* He receives his own talisman and braces for the coming storm.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Brightmoon Landscape Painting & Pure Yang Treasures**  
The trial takes place inside a "Brightmoon Landscape Painting" (明月山水图). This isn't just a tourist-class artifact—it's a Pure Yang (纯阳) magic treasure, the absolute top tier in the treasure hierarchy. Pure Yang treasures are sentient, world-bearing vessels that can house entire pocket realities. The Grand Xia Emperor casually using one as a battle royale arena shows both his wealth and the absurd stakes of this assembly. For the Celestial Immortals watching, seeing this treasure is a flex as intimidating as any threat.

**Karmic Merit vs. Luck (B站级拆解)**  
New Daoists, listen up! The Emperor draws a subtle but crucial distinction here: Karmic Merit (功德) and Luck (气运) are *not* the same thing. Karmic Merit is a visible, cosmic bank account—you save up good deeds (feeding the poor, saving lives) and you literally glow with golden light. Luck, on the other hand, is an invisible cheat code. It comes from being the disciple of a major power, having monstrous parents, or stumbling into a legendary inheritance. You can have zero merit and still be blessed by the heavens just because your grandpa was a Celestial Immortal. The Emperor can count karmic merit like coins, but luck? That's the wild card that makes even *he* curious.

**The Collapse of the Six Paths of Reincarnation**  
The Celestial Immortals whisper about an event decades past—the sudden attack on the Netherworld Kingdom that shattered the Six Paths of Reincarnation. This is not casual gossip. In Xianxia cosmology, the Six Paths are the fundamental cycle of life, death, and rebirth for all souls in the Three Realms. If that machine breaks, souls get stuck, reincarnation stalls, and the entire cosmic order wobbles. The fact that the Grand Xia Emperor might be using this assembly to scout for talents to deal with the fallout of that crisis is a major hint that the world is heading for some truly dark times.

## Reading Guide

Gear up, fellow Daoists—the real fight is finally here, and it's a bloodbath waiting to happen. This chapter is essentially the starting gun firing for a battle royale of 109,000 top-tier cultivators, and the rules are dirty, honest, and terrifying. The Emperor isn't looking for gentlemen or debate champions—he wants survivors. The ban on talismans and puppets forces everyone to rely on their own comprehension of the Dao, their own sword-arm, their own Dao-heart. That's Xianxia at its most raw. Also, pay close attention to the whispers about the Six Paths collapse—this is one of those 'read-between-the-lines' moments that will pay off massively later. For now, just enjoy the spectacle of more than one hundred thousand egos getting dropped into a painting and told to sort it out. Ji Ning is in the valley, swords out, and the chaos is just beginning.
