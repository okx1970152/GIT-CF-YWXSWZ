## Chapter Overview

Brace yourselves, fellow Daoists—the celebratory banquet just turned into a full-blown tribunal! Shao Yanong, who was all smiles and gratitude moments ago, has suddenly dropped the mask. After recognizing Ji Ning’s Windwing Evasion as a technique unique to the long-annihilated Yuchi clan, he demands answers with cold, absolute authority. Ji Ning is cornered: deny and live, or admit and face a death sentence from one of the most powerful clans in the commandery. But when Xue Hongyi coldly exposes Ji Ning’s mother as Yuchi Snow, the mask shatters entirely. A blood feud spanning generations erupts into the open, and the only question left is whether anyone can survive the fallout.

## Key Plot Points

- Shao Yanong’s demeanor shifts from casual host to icy interrogator after witnessing Ji Ning’s Windwing Evasion in battle.
- Xiangliu Fang demonstrates the Shao Yan version of the technique, confirming it was stolen from the destroyed Yuchi clan via soul-scouring atrocities.
- Shao Yanong reveals the backstory: the Yuchi clan was wiped out by the Shao Yan clan generations ago, and the Windwing Evasion was reconstructed from the tortured memories of countless Yuchi victims.
- Ji Ning is given an ultimatum: lock himself with the Soulchain of Imprisonment and await judgment, or be branded an enemy on the spot.
- Nine Lotos, Yu Wei, Mu Northson, and Cangjiang Zhenren all try to defuse the situation politically, offering negotiation and oaths as a way out.
- Xue Hongyi delivers the killing blow: he publicly states that Ji Ning’s mother is Yuchi Snow, forcing Shao Yanong to escalate to an immediate kill order.
- The chapter ends with Shao Yanong declaring that anyone who blocks the Shao Yan clan’s vengeance will be slaughtered without mercy.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Blood Feuds and Clan Extermination (灭族)**: In the Xianxia ecosystem, wiping out an entire clan is a grim but plausible endgame for long-simmering rivalries. The Yuchi clan’s annihilation wasn’t random—it was a calculated move to monopolize the Windwing Evasion technique. This is a recurring trope: rare techniques are treated as strategic assets, and clans will commit genocide to secure them. The irony? The Shao Yan clan still didn’t find the original manual and had to use soul-scouring (搜魂) on survivors—a fate worse than death, as the victim’s soul is usually shattered in the process.

**Soul-Scouring (搜魂)**: A terrifying interrogation technique in Xianxia where a powerful cultivator forcibly rips memories from a victim’s soul. The result is almost always permanent soul damage or outright oblivion—no reincarnation, no afterlife. It’s considered one of the most heinous acts a cultivator can commit, reserved for the most ruthless or desperate.

**The Soulchain of Imprisonment (锁神链)**: A magic treasure designed to suppress all divine power and physical strength, rendering even a Fiendgod as helpless as a mortal. It’s not a weapon but a tool of absolute control. Shao Yanong offering this “way out” is a transparent power play—he wants Ji Ning completely defenseless before deciding his fate.

**The Windwing Evasion as Clan Legacy**: Divine abilities are the lifeblood of a clan’s military power. The Windwing Evasion is a movement-type technique that grants terrifying speed and spatial flexibility. Its theft or inheritance outside the clan is treated as high treason. Shao Yanong’s reaction isn’t just personal rage—it’s a clan leader performing his duty to protect their most precious asset.

### Xianxia Clan Politics

Powerful clans treat unique divine abilities as strategic assets. Learning a clan’s secret technique without permission is a capital offense, as the technique is considered part of the clan’s sovereign inheritance.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia political tension. Watch how the power dynamics shift in real time: Shao Yanong transitions from patronizing host to cold-blooded executor within a single conversation, and Ji Ning’s allies all try different angles of diplomatic intervention—Nine Lotos uses her clan status, Yu Wei appeals to reason, Mu Northson plays the loyalty card, and Cangjiang Zhenren suggests a negotiated settlement. But in the end, it’s the cold, factual dagger thrown by Xue Hongyi—the simple truth of Ji Ning’s mother’s name—that shatters every escape route.

Also, pay attention to how Shao Yanong frames his actions. He doesn’t just say “I want to kill you.” He repeatedly invokes “my Shao Yan clan” and positions himself as the enforcer of clan law. This is a classic Xianxia move: as long as he’s acting on behalf of the collective, he can justify any brutality. Ji Ning’s internal fury, meanwhile, is volcanic but perfectly restrained—until the end, he doesn’t even raise his voice. That cold, buried rage is the hallmark of a Dao-heart that’s been forged in real tragedy.

Get ready, because the cage has just slammed shut.
