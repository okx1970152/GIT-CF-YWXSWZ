## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists! This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia tactical deception—a full-blown reversal that had us fist-pumping. After the ambush kill on the Firewing Demon King in the previous chapter, Ji Ning finds himself besieged by the remaining eleven demon kings. They’re furious, they’re coordinated, and they’ve got a simple but effective Yuan-sharing formation that turns each attack into a pile-driver. But Ji Ning has been playing the long game: he lets them think they’re winning, lets them get comfortable pounding his main body, and then—BAM—drops his second true body with the devastating Plow the Heavens Sword Formation. The battlefield flips from a defensive struggle into a one-sided slaughter. Get ready for some glorious sword-intent fury!

## Key Plot Points

*   **Pressure Test:** The eleven kings, using a basic joint Yuan-power formation, lay into Ji Ning. The Golden-Furred Bear’s ninety-nine spherical treasures and the Mountain-Bearing King’s mountain artifact are especially punishing, forcing Ji Ning onto the defensive. His divine body is battered but regenerates quickly.
*   **The Setup:** Ji Ning deliberately holds back the power of his Thousandbull Sword, making all six of his swords seem equal. He also takes a beating to lull the kings into overconfidence—they believe sustained pressure will eventually kill him.
*   **The Drop:** Right when the kings think victory is close, Ji Ning summons his second true body—a late-Void Earth Immortal carrying a full set of nine top-grade Immortal flying swords. This is the exact moment the “weaker” prey turns predator.
*   **Plow the Heavens Unleashed:** The second true body activates the Daofather-created sword-art, “Plow the Heavens,” tearing black gashes across the sky. Three demon kings are killed instantly; they don’t even have time to use escape talismans.
*   **Full Explosion:** With the element of surprise lost, Ji Ning’s main body stops hiding its power. The Thousandbull Sword strikes with Pure Yang-level might, heavily wounding and then finishing off the Jade Toad Demon King. The remaining kings are thrown into panic and the Golden-Furred Bear orders retreat, but it’s already too late for many of them.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Second True Body (第二元神):** This is one of the most powerful trump cards a cultivator can have. The “second true body” or “Primaltwin” is an independent clone cultivated from a cultivator’s Primal soul. It can train separately, possess its own treasures, and even fight simultaneously with the main body. In the Grand Xia setting, most cultivators can only manage one; Ji Ning’s second true body is a full late-Void Earth Immortal, making the pair effectively two peak-level combatants in one fight. This is why ancient Xianxia texts say: “a cultivator with a second true body is never truly alone.”

**Yuan-Sharing Formation (元力共用):** Not every formation needs to be a flashy beast transformation. Sometimes the simplest trick—linking everyone’s Yuan power together—is the most effective. Here, the eleven kings share their energy reserves, boosting each attack by an entire level. This is the same principle as the “Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation” used by the Ji Clan earlier. It’s a testament to the depth of Xianxia war tactics: even a crude formation can make a group of weaker demons a genuine threat to a top-tier genius.

**Plow the Heavens (犁天):** This sword-art was created by a Daofather, the highest beings in the Three Realms. The imagery is beautiful and brutal: the sword-light is a plow, and the sky is a field. Each stroke carves a deep black gash in reality itself—a wound in the fabric of Heaven. Ji Ning’s set of nine “Plow the Heavens” flying swords are top-grade Immortal-ranked and fully matched to the technique, making this combination his single strongest offensive tool, even outclassing the Thousandbull Sword’s individual might.

## Reading Guide

*   **Watch for the Ritualized Calm:** Ji Ning’s behavior in this chapter is a perfect example of the “ritualized calm” of a veteran cultivator. He doesn’t panic under the beating; he’s methodically gathering intel on each king’s treasure and fighting style. The quiet satisfaction when he thinks “Just about right” is the mark of a plan fulfilled after decades of training.
*   **Power Level Note:** The demon kings aren’t pushovers. Each one is a veteran loose immortal, most with Immortal-ranked treasures. The Golden-Furred Bear matches a Celestial Immortal in raw power. Ji Ning’s main body, even with the Thousandbull Sword, was at a clear disadvantage against the combined assault. That makes the second true body’s clean-up all the more impressive—it’s a reminder that in Xianxia, the gap between a “genius” and a “true monster” is the preparation you never see coming.
*   **The Trope of the Hidden Ace:** This chapter beautifully executes a classic Xianxia staple: the protagonist lets the enemy believe they are in control, feeds them a false read of their power, then flips the script with an ace they never knew existed. Ji Ning’s restraint in not using the Thousandbull Sword earlier was a deliberate misdirection, and it paid off with multiple kills in a single exchange.

## Deeper Dive: The Strategy of Deception in Xianxia Warfare

This chapter is a textbook example of the “lead the enemy to overconfidence” strategy. In Xianxia, raw power alone is often insufficient against a numerically superior, veteran opponent. The true art of war lies in controlling what the enemy *thinks* they know. Ji Ning allowed himself to be beaten, coughed blood, and appeared to weaken—all to make the eleven kings commit fully to a sustained assault. He then unleashed his second true body only when the kings had exhausted their initial burst and were settled into a predictable rhythm. This mirrors ancient Chinese military philosophy: “make your enemy believe you are weak, and they will become careless. Then strike with overwhelming force where they least expect it.” Ji Ning isn’t just a sword genius; he’s a battlefield psychologist.
