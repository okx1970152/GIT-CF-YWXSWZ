## Chapter Overview

Get ready for an emotional gut-punch, fellow Daoists. This chapter pivots hard from the brutal action of the mine formation to the quiet, domestic terror in the Western Prefecture. Ji Yichuan, the ice-cold commander, is stripped of his composure as his beloved wife Yuchi Snow collapses without warning. We're served a masterclass in Xianxia pathos: the quiet tragedy of a family built on sacrifice. This is the chapter where the novel reminds you that even the strongest cultivators can be utterly powerless in the face of a loved one's failing body. It's not all quiet tears, though—the camera swings back to Ji Ning, trapped in a hellish cavern where a cult is harvesting Xiantian lifeforms like cattle, with a creepy master lurking just beneath the floorboards. The stakes are rising on two fronts.

## Key Plot Points

- Yuchi Snow collapses without any external cause, sending Ji Yichuan into a panic. The maidservant's frantic report foreshadows something far worse than a simple fainting spell.
- Sorcerer Cao, the Western Prefecture's best healer, is summoned but confesses his limits and advises summoning the Clan Leader, Ji Jiuhuo, a Zifu-level elder.
- Ji Honghua (Hua Gu) is dispatched to Ten Thousand Swords City on a Greenflame Bird to bring the Clan Leader back. Yichuan's uncharacteristic desperation is palpable.
- Yuchi Snow wakes and reveals the root of her frailty: severe injuries from the North Sea calamity a decade ago, which she partially healed by sacrificing her own lifespan to safely bear Ji Ning.
- The backstory of Yuchi Antong (her elder brother) is revealed. He sacrificed himself during that calamity, shouting for Yichuan to save his sister, forging an unbreakable bond of blood and debt.
- Yuchi Snow expresses no regret. She views the past ten years of happiness with her husband and son as a worthy trade. She instructs Yichuan to hide her condition from Ning unless she is truly on the verge of death.
- The scene shifts to the mountain cavern where Ji Ning is trapped. The cultists within discuss their bottleneck: they have captured 100 Xiantian lifeforms but need more. They are terrified to enter the formation after their spirit beast "Black Needle" was killed.
- A hidden underground entrance opens with a chilling green light. Their "master" has arrived, and the six disciples bow in respect.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Injuries in Xianxia**: Unlike Western fantasy where a hero's past injury just causes them pain, in Xianxia, a severe wound can permanently damage one's cultivation foundation. Yuchi Snow's "hidden injury" (暗伤) is a classic trope. A cultivator can suppress it for years with effort, but it remains a ticking time bomb that can collapse under the strain of childbearing or life events.
- **The Role of the Clan Leader**: Ji Jiuhuo, the Zifu Disciple clan leader, is the ultimate authority. In tribal society, such a figure is not just a ruler but a living treasure—the one person who holds the ancient knowledge to diagnose a soul-origin injury or a poison that lesser sorcerers cannot touch. The fact that Yichuan is confident he will come speaks volumes about Yichuan's standing within the clan.
- **Cultivator Ethics**: Yuchi Snow's choice to bear Ning'er even knowing it would shorten her life is a powerful illustration of the Xianxia value system. It's not about martyrdom; it's about legacy and bloodline. A cultivator's child is an extension of their Dao, their power, and their hope for transcendence. Sacrificing decades for that legacy is a rational, noble choice.
- **The Sect Beneath the Mountain**: The six disciples in the cavern, dressed in finery while torturing slaves, are a classic "evil cult" (邪教) setup. Recruiting Xiantian lifeforms and buying slaves on a massive scale points to a forbidden technique or a sacrifice ritual. The hidden master's arrival signals that Ji Ning is about to face something far more sophisticated than a mere trap formation.

### Hidden Injury (暗伤)

A long-term, latent wound or internal damage to a cultivator's foundation, often sustained in battle, that can suddenly worsen under physical or spiritual strain.

### Twelve Great Demons (十二大妖)

The twelve most powerful Xiantian-level demon beasts ruling the Eastmarsh Swamp, each controlling a territory and a hierarchy of subordinate beasts.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *contrasting tension*. You have the quiet, heartbreaking intimacy of the Snow-Yichuan bedroom scene, followed by the horrific, industrial-scale torture chamber in the mountain. Pay close attention to the dialogue between Yuchi Snow and Yichuan—particularly the flashback to the *North Sea Calamity*. This isn't just emotional filler; the "North Sea" and the Yuchi Clan's connection to Celestial Immortal Su Huan are lore seeds that will bear major fruit later. Also, note the cult's complaint about needing *more* Xiantian lifeforms. They aren't just killing; they are *collecting*. That kind of systematic harvesting always points to a higher purpose—a ritual, a forbidden spell, or an evil world-shattering technique. Brace yourselves, because Ji Ning's escape is about to get a lot more complicated.
