## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a brutal, emotional gut-punch. Ji Ning, fresh from a poignant conversation with King Yan about searching for his reincarnated parents, rushes to visit his junior brother Mu Northson—only to walk directly into a perfectly laid trap by the Shaoyin clan. Four Celestial Immortals strike simultaneously, and the result is catastrophic: the fragile soul of Mu Northson’s beloved Dao-companion, Yu Xia, is instantly annihilated by a soul attack. The chapter ends not with a tactical retreat, but with the raw, earth-shattering roar of a broken man unleashing his fury through an Immortal-Winged Puppet. Get ready; the carnage is about to begin.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Investigation into Ji Ning’s Parents**: King Yan agrees to search through the archives of the minor cycle of reincarnation to find Ji Yichuan and Yuchi Snow’s current lives. He explains the limitations of the new system—no Book of Life and Death, only records going back a century, and a focus solely on karmic merit.
2. **The Shaoyin Clan’s Ambush**: Four Celestial Immortals (Chixin, Xuanji, Goldenbell, and Kumu) have been lying in wait near Mu Northson’s cabin. Their plan is to ambush Ji Ning when he arrives, catching him completely off guard and without allies.
3. **The Soul Attack**: As Ji Ning calls out to his brother, Ancestor Chixin launches a devastating soul-based attack. Ji Ning’s powerful soul resists it, but Yu Xia’s soul, trapped in the jade sphere, is too weak and is instantly obliterated.
4. **Mu Northson’s Breaking Point**: Witnessing his beloved’s soul vanish sends Mu Northson into a frenzy of grief and rage. He merges with his own Immortal-Winged Puppet, his killing intent soaring as he prepares to destroy the four Celestial Immortals who took everything from him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a textbook example of a Xianxia “trap” and a character’s ‘Dao-heart collapse’. The ambush relies on a cheap, classic tactic: attack the hero’s weakest bonds. The soul attack is not a spell of fire and lightning; it’s a subtle assault on the spirit, bypassing physical defenses. It vividly illustrates how a cultivator’s Dao-heart can be broken—not by sword or spell, but by emotional devastation. When Mu Northson screams, it’s his very soul crying out; this is the sound of a Dao-heart shattering, the prelude to either a demonic fall or a terrifying, tragedy-fueled power-up. His silent, obsessive grief (carving statues, talking to a jade sphere) was already a symptom. This is the final, violent collapse.

## Reading Guide
This is a story that hits you right in the chest. The chapter contrasts two powerful, bittersweet moments: Ji Ning’s quiet hope about his parents’ reincarnation versus the immediate, brutal reality of what’s happening to his ‘little brother’ right now. Pay close attention to the cold, pragmatic malice of the Shaoyin clan—they aren't just attacking Ji Ning; they are surgically destroying his support network. Watch for Ji Ning’s reaction. He always controls his emotions, but hold on to your seats. This personal tragedy is the catalyst for an inferno of vengeance. The Shaoyin clan just made the biggest mistake of their immortal lives.
