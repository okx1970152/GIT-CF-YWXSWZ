## Chapter Overview
Fresh off his victory over Qinghe, Ji Ning stands atop his stone pillar and throws down the gauntlet to every senior disciple in the Dao Debate Palace. The silence is telling—until Xueying, a white-haired Two-Claw Yinglong Guard, steps up. Though Xueying is a Fiendgod Body Refiner who normally excels at close combat, he is still a cut above Qinghe in puppeteering. Ji Ning pushes himself to the breaking point, unleashing five simultaneous New Three Talents Lotus Swords—a feat that leaves the entire hall stunned. The victory is decisive, but Ji Ning knows it came at the cost of all his reserves.

## Key Plot Points
- Qinghe concedes defeat gracefully, depositing his wager and leaving the hall.
- Ji Ning issues an open challenge; no one responds until Xueying volunteers.
- Xueying is revealed to be a Two-Claw Yinglong Guard with a fearsome reputation.
- The duel begins: Xueying’s nine-sword puppet conjures a blood mosquito while Ji Ning counters with lotus sword-lights.
- Ji Ning escalates from one to three, then to five simultaneous lotus swords, overwhelming Xueying’s defense.
- The Diancai Immortal and a short old man observe, noting that Ji Ning’s divine sense-level soul is the key to his multi-sword control.
- Xueying admits defeat with grace, and the chapter ends with Ji Ning’s renewed invitation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Yinglong Guard (应龙卫)** – The elite immortal corps of the Grand Xia Dynasty, named after the winged dragon Yinglong, a rain deity in Chinese mythology. Members are ranked by claw marks (One-Claw, Two-Claw, Three-Claw), with higher claws signifying greater combat power. Even the lowest rank requires at least Wanxiang Adept strength, and rewards from the Black-White College for joining are substantial (1,000 Black-White Pearls for One-Claw). Xueying being a Two-Claw Guard means his real fighting ability is on par with a Primal Daoist—a monster even among geniuses.

**Multiple Sword Techniques Simultaneously** – In Xianxia, controlling more than one advanced technique at a time is an indicator of either a vastly superior Dao realm or an extremely powerful soul. Ji Ning’s ability to command five Three Talents Lotus Swords simultaneously at the Zifu stage marks him as an anomaly. The Diancai Immortal’s comment confirms that his soul has reached the divine sense level—normally a Primal Daoist’s privilege.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a pure dopamine hit for progression-lovers. Ji Ning doesn’t just win—he *dominates* in a way that makes the entire hall stop and take notice. The author smartly uses the puppet format to level the playing field, letting Ji Ning’s sword-system comprehension and soul strength shine without the brute-force gap of raw cultivation. Watch how the senior disciples’ murmurs shift from condescending to awed. And that callback about the Fiendgod Blood-Refining Art? Foreshadowing for Ji Ning’s own future inheritance.

## What Makes Xueying a Threat Even in Defeat
Don’t let the loss fool you—Xueying is an absolute beast. His Fiendgod Body Refining and divine abilities are his true weapons; the puppet duel is just a technicality. The fact that Ji Ning beat him at all is a testament to Ji Ning’s freakish mental capacity (divine sense). But if this were a real fight, Xueying would turn Ji Ning into paste in a heartbeat. That gap is what keeps the power fantasy grounded: Ji Ning is a genius among geniuses, but he’s not invincible yet.
