## Chapter Overview
Two heads are better than one, and in this chapter, Ji Ning and Mu Northson prove it with a textbook demonstration of coordinated Xianxia monster-hunting. Having selected an unremarkable demon mountain to avoid overreaching, the pair infiltrates the lair of a Wanxiang great demon (the black-faced man) and his Zifu wife. What follows is a brutal, high-tempo beatdown—Mu Northson takes the lead in a fierce one-on-one duel, while Ji Ning, the tactical anchor, identifies incoming reinforcements (two more Wanxiang great demons) with his divine sense. The chapter closes with the promise of a 3v2 brawl that will let Northson stretch his legs even further.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning uses his divine sense to fully map the demon mountain, detecting one Wanxiang, a dozen Zifu, and a crowd of Xiantian monsters before they make a move.
- The pair bypasses most of the mountain's defensive formations, relying on Ji Ning’s formation knowledge (honed through discussions with Uncle White) to sneak directly into the core lair.
- Mu Northson, furious at discovering a human child kept as livestock for the demon’s meal, engages the Wanxiang great demon alone. His Sun-Moon Dharma Idol overpowers the enemy’s starlight manifestation, revealing his deep foundation.
- The seductive Zifu fox-wife attempts to flee and is summarily crushed into paste by a casual tail-whip from Northson’s puppet technique—a ruthless display of the power gap between Zifu and Wanxiang.
- The black-faced great demon, realizing he is outmatched, stalls for time, having secretly summoned two fellow Wanxiang demon-lords. Ji Ning detects them with his divine sense and calmly announces the arrival of reinforcements.
- Mu Northson, far from being intimidated, greets the news with excitement, and Ji Ning prepares to engage the newcomers, setting up a chaotic 3v2 melee.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Divine Sense vs. Formations (神识与阵法):** This chapter showcases a classic Xianxia counter-strategy: a cultivator with powerful soul-based perception (divine sense) can neutralize most defensive formations by simply mapping them out and walking around them. Formations that aren't actively maintained in a "triggered" state are mere lines in the sand to someone like Ji Ning, who possesses Primal-level divine sense at the Zifu stage. This is why "spiritual perception" and "array mastery" are often paired in cultivation literature—they form a deadly reconnaissance toolkit.

**The Human-Animal Conflict (人妖之争):** The conversation between the black-faced demon and his wife reveals a brutal symmetry. Humans hunt monsters for their inner cores and flesh; monsters eat humans as delicacies. The demon's bitter complaint about the lack of a "Human-Slave Seal" (a mirror to the "Beast-Spirit Seal" used by cultivators to tame monsters) is a fascinating piece of worldbuilding, showing that the conflict is not one-sided and that the Grand Xia Dynasty's human-centric laws create a power imbalance even the monsters resent. This is a rare moment where the novel allows the "villain" a moment of political grievance, adding depth to the setting.

**Wanxiang Dharma Idols (万象法相):** The clash of "stars and moon" versus "sun and moon" is a visual representation of cultivation quality. A standard Wanxiang Adept manifests a starlit sky; a truly elite genius manifests both sun and moon, symbolizing a perfect balance of Yin and Yang power and a deeper foundation. Mu Northson’s Sun-Moon Dharma Idol immediately marks him as a monster talent, explaining why he crushes a seasoned Wanxiang demon with raw force.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a palate cleanser of pure, unadulterated Xianxia action. After the heavy worldbuilding of the Black-White College and the philosophical depth of the Sword Heart Illumination, we return to what the genre does best: talented youths cutting loose on arrogant monsters. Watch how the power dynamics shift—the Wanxiang demon who thought he was an apex predator becomes the bait in a trap of his own making. Also, pay attention to Mu Northson's character: his rage at the child's suffering and his glee at the prospect of more enemies reveals a young cultivator with a strong moral core and an absolute love for combat. Ji Ning, as always, plays the calm strategist, letting his junior brother have his fun while keeping an eye on the bigger picture.

## The Human-Monster Conflict
This chapter is a brilliant microcosm of the entire Xianxia "human vs. demon" trope. The black-faced demon is not a mindless beast; he has a wife, a cultivation schedule, political opinions, and a gourmet’s appreciation for human flesh. Yet, in the grand calculus of the Grand Xia Dynasty, his entire existence is classified as "pest control" for cultivators like Ji Ning. This moral ambiguity—where both sides are equally predatory, yet only one side has the backing of a celestial empire—is a constant undercurrent in the genre. The fact that the demon is monstrous (literally eating children) makes Ji Ning and Northson’s violence cleanly righteous, but the worldbuilding hints at a much darker, more complex system of oppression.
