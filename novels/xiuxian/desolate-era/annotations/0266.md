## Chapter Overview

The chapter dives straight into the tactical nightmare that awaits our crew: the Immortal manor’s native demon tribes have closed in like a vice. Xiangliu Fang drops the bad news—two elite forces, the Wavefiend Demon-Soldiers and the Yaksha Dao-Soldiers, each over ten thousand strong, led by Loose Immortals. Our boy Shaoyan Nong has a clever trick up his sleeve (teleporting via the refined hall to appear thousands of li away), but he knows it’s a one-shot gambit. Once the demons figure out the manor’s secret, they’ll just set up camp at the unrefined halls and wait. It’s a classic Xianxia stalemate: the demons are playing the ultimate “waiting by the tree” game, and our heroes have no choice but to walk right into the trap. The crew manages to slip into a second hall—the Herb Hall—but the loot is a mixed blessing. Xiangliu Fang, in true Fiendgod fashion, scoops up the eighteen best Immortal herbs before anyone can blink, leaving the rest for the scrambling juniors. Pressure is building.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiangliu Fang briefs the group on the demon forces: two Loose Immortal-led armies, each over ten thousand strong, that even he can’t beat.
- Shaoyan Nong reveals his refined hall’s teleportation trick: he can appear anywhere within ten thousand li, avoiding the immediate blockade.
- The demons instantly adapt: Duohe and Wu Sui swear a Heavenly Dao oath to cooperate and split up to guard the unrefined halls.
- The crew enters the Herb Hall—a massive space filled with an overgrown Immortal medicine garden.
- Xiangliu Fang’s giant hand plucks the eighteen top-tier Immortal herbs effortlessly, ignoring the golem guards.
- The rest of the team scavenges the leftover herbs, grumbling but obedient.
- Shaoyan Nong finishes refining the second hall in one hour, setting up the next push.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Waiting by the Tree, Watching for the Rabbit (守株待兔)**  
This is one of the most famous fables in Chinese literature, originating from the Daoist philosopher Han Feizi. The original story mocks a farmer who, after seeing a rabbit accidentally kill itself by running into a tree stump, abandons his fields to wait for more rabbits to do the same—only to starve. In Xianxia strategy, the idiom is flipped: “waiting by the tree” becomes a brilliant, high-pressure tactic. The demons know exactly where Shaoyan Nong *must* go (the unrefined halls), so they don’t need to chase. They just sit and wait, making the hunter become the hunted. It’s a reminder that in cultivation wars, raw power isn’t everything—sometimes the most devastating move is simply being patient.

**Wu Jiang Clan and Yaksha Dao-Soldiers**  
The name “Wu Jiang” (巫江) suggests a lineage tied to ancient shamanic or sorcerous traditions, a staple in Xianxia where clans are defined by bloodline techniques and inherited treasures. The Yaksha Dao-Soldiers (夜叉道兵) are a step up from standard Dao-soldier armor. In Buddhist and Chinese folklore, Yakshas are nature spirits, often depicted as powerful, flesh-eating demons with a fierce appearance. Translating that into a military unit: imagine a phalanx of soldiers whose synchronized armor manifests a giant, translucent specter of a hunchbacked demon wielding an iron fork. That’s the kind of visual doom that says “elite” in this setting.

**Primordial Fiendgod Physiology**  
Xiangliu Fang’s instantaneous transformation into a nine-headed, serpent-tailed giant is a classic display of a true Primordial Fiendgod’s battle form. Unlike humans, who rely on technique and treasures, Fiendgods are walking catastrophes—their bodies are weapons, their size is a statement. The thousand-zhang height (over three kilometers) is not for show; it gives them the reach and mass to swat armies. His casual grab-and-toss of three Primal-level golems reinforces the hierarchy: even a “low-realm” Fiendgod can bully those beneath his ancient lineage.

## Reading Guide

This is a setup chapter, but don’t sleep on it. The tension isn’t in big explosions—it’s in the quiet dread of knowing your next move is predicted. The demons aren't stupid; they adapt instantly. That’s what makes this arc compelling: the antagonists have brains, and they’ve been desperate for escape for millennia. Every move Shaoyan Nong makes is one step closer to a brutal, unavoidable confrontation. Pay attention to how the loot is distributed—Xiangliu Fang’s greed hints at his independent agenda, and the juniors (Ji Ning, Northson, Xue Hongyi) are clearly playing second fiddle in the treasure race. Also, note that the crew is now effectively trapped: three halls down, two to go, and those two are fortified. The chapter’s quiet ending, with Shaoyan Nong calmly declaring “On to the next hall,” is the calm before the storm.
