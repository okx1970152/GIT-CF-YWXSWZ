## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because the pressure valve just got cranked to max. Our crew of Wanxiang Adepts isn’t just outnumbered—they’re facing a literal tide of demonic Dao-soldiers so dense it’s like a tsunami made of murder. Shaoyan Nong pulls out the Golden Light Divine Orb as a panic room, and just when you think the escape is the highlight, we get a **primal spectacle**: his servant Xiangliu Fang reveals his true form as a thousand-zhang-tall, nine-headed serpent-tailed ancient Fiendgod. The clash between a real, blood-and-scale Fiendgod and the coordinated might of the estate’s Dao-soldier army is the kind of raw, mythological flex that defines this novel’s cosmology. But the world just keeps getting bigger—the Yaksha Dao-soldiers show up, and our crew barely slips through the gates of the main palace. A chapter about scaling threats, hidden power, and the sheer audacity of the immortal world.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Golden Light Bubble**: Shaoyan Nong deploys the Golden Light Divine Orb, a top-tier portable fortress that can tank multiple Loose Immortals. This is the tactical equivalent of a rich kid pulling out a million-dollar shield—and it works, allowing the group to retreat safely while Xiangliu Fang goes loud.
- **Xiangliu Fang’s True Form**: The swarthy servant everyone underestimated? Turns out he’s a genuine Primal-level Fiendgod of the Xiangliu clan—nine serpent heads, a thousand-zhang body, and a fighting style that combines raw power with serpentine agility. He single-handedly holds off the entire Dao-soldier tide, showcasing the “fight-above-your-level” trait of true Fiendgods.
- **The Five Pillars Revealed**: The group spots five golden pillars of heaven at the edges of the world, confirming the estate is divided into five sub-palaces. This spatial revelation drives their escape route and hints at deeper inheritance layers ahead.
- **The Wild Card—Yaksha Dao-soldiers**: Just when Xiangliu Fang has the tide under control, the Wu Jiang clan’s own elite Yaksha Dao-soldiers manifest as a giant specter, raising the stakes even for a Primal Fiendgod. The sheer depth of this estate’s military power is staggering.
- **Escape Through the Gate**: Shaoyan Nong uses the estate key to open the main palace gate, pulling everyone inside just as the Yaksha tide closes in. The team survives, but the threat hierarchy has been brutally redefined.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Fiendgods vs. Fiendgod Body Refiners**: This chapter draws a critical line in the sand. True Fiendgods (like the Xiangliu clan) are born from primordial chaos—they’re natural, ancient beings with innate physical superiority. A Primal-level true Fiendgod can match or even overwhelm Loose Immortals and Earth Immortals, while a Void-level one can rival Celestial Immortals. In contrast, human cultivators who practice Fiendgod Body Refining are *imitating* that nature through brutal self-modification. They’re close, but never identical—smaller bodies, weaker innate talents. This isn’t a flaw; it’s the entire philosophical tension between a human striving to transcend their default form and a being born already at the apex.

**The Xiangliu Clan**: In Chinese mythology, Xiangliu is a deadly ancient Fiendgod associated with floods and venom, famously defeated by the god Yu the Great during his taming of the world’s waters. The novel borrows this name to anchor Xiangliu Fang in a lineage of primordial ferocity—nine heads, serpent body, control over frost and poison. This isn’t just a random monster; it’s a mythic callback to one of China’s most legendary water-related monsters.

**Dao-Soldier Armor & Formation Power**: The demonic Dao-soldiers in this chapter are a terrifying demonstration of Xianxia militarization. Each soldier wears runic armor that allows a group to fight as one—sharing soul protection, merging attacks, and amplifying each other’s power to an extent that even a true Fiendgod is on the back foot. This is the martial equivalent of squad-level formation tactics, and the Yaksha variant pushes it even further.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in **escalation by revelation**. The Golden Light Divine Orb establishes a safety ceiling—but immediately, Xiangliu Fang’s true form *resets* that ceiling by showing the team that true power isn’t just about wealth. It’s about bloodline. And then the Yaksha Dao-soldiers *reset it again*. IET is teaching us, slowly, that every layer of this immortal estate holds a military force that can crush the team if they’re not smart about it. Also, notice how Ji Ning stays clinically analytical—he catalogs the orb’s power, the Fiendgod’s capabilities, and his own relative standing, all without panic. That’s his Dao-heart hardening post-Crimson Dragon Mountains.

If you’re new to Xianxia, this is a great chapter to study the **true Fiendgod concept**. These aren’t demon cultivators or monsters; they’re primal entities that predate the human race and the fixed laws of the Three Realms. They’re living fossils, and seeing one in action is always a treat.
