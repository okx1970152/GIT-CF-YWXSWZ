## Chapter Overview
Alright grab your popcorn, xianxia fans—this chapter delivers the first true taste of mortal terror in this world. Ji Ning is getting cocky, using a Fiendgod-blooded serpent as a living punching bag to polish his *Dripping Sutra* moves. Classic young master flexing, right? Wrong. He didn’t count on one tiny detail: that serpent has a *dad*. And Dad is a millennium-old, scale-flapping, city-destroying nightmare named the Winged Serpent. What starts as a routine Cage Battle turns into a desperate survival scramble, with Ji Ning learning a brutal lesson about the food chain in this primordial world. By the end of the chapter, his arm is gone, his confidence is shattered, and he’s facing the kind of threat that makes you wonder if the main character just got oneshot for real. If you’re here for the soft power fantasy, buckle up—this is where the grit begins.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning faces off against Crimson (赤芒), a vermilion serpent with claws and Fiendgod blood, as part of the secret Cage Battle training. He dominates the fight purely to practice and refine *Dripping Sutra* moves.
- The nine moves of *Dripping Sutra* are fully listed with English names, showing the breadth of Ji Ning’s sword foundation.
- Crimson, realizing it’s being toyed with, lets out a bitter, maddened roar before being killed by Ji Ning with a lethal combo.
- High above, the Winged Serpent (Crimson’s father) hears the death cry and dives straight toward the Cage, hidden in clouds.
- Ji Ning tries to cut through the iron chain ceiling with his Northsea Swords, but the chains are too dense—he needs at least a breath to escape.
- The Winged Serpent smashes the cage open effortlessly, its bone wing tearing through Ji Ning’s defensive sword technique and snapping his wrists.
- Ji Ning’s left arm is severed, but he guts it out, using his *Windshadow Steps* to flee the Cage.
- The Winged Serpent pursues with a supersonic tail whip, and Ji Ning realizes he can’t dodge—he screams internally that it’s over.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a crash course in the stakes of cultivation-world parenting. In xianxia, you harm the cub, you face the *lao yao* (old demon). The Winged Serpent is a classic “vengeful parent” archetype—an ancient beast whose patience for human antics ended the second its offspring died. This mirrors a broader pattern in *Desolate Era*: demons aren’t just monster-of-the-week farming loot. They have bloodlines, pride, and a terrifying memory for grudges. The chapter also explores the foundational “divided mind” (一心二用) concept in dual-wielding combat, where each hand executes an independent sword technique simultaneously, multiplying effective power tenfold. Ji Ning has that advantage, but against a being with a thousand-year cultivation base, raw numbers don’t matter—*one force defeats a hundred techniques* (一力降十会). The flight described here is a classic survival reflex: in a world where seniority beats talent, the smartest move is knowing when to *run*.

## Reading Guide
This is the kind of chapter that separates the casual readers from the invested ones. If you just breezed through thinking *“he’ll be fine, he’s the main character”*, you’re missing the point. IET is building Ji Ning’s character not through victory speeches, but through the *fear* of annihilation. Watch how Ji Ning’s internal monologue shifts—he starts clinical, analytical, cataloging sword effects like a training log. But the moment the Winged Serpent smashes through, his thoughts collapse into pure, raw survival: *“Not good! Run!” / “Too late.” / “It’s over.”* That’s the sound of a protagonist who hasn’t grown into a god yet. And honestly? That’s refreshing. Also, keep an eye on that shout—did Ji Yichuan hear it? Because if Dad doesn’t show up with that silver great bow in the next beat, things are about to get very, very messy.
