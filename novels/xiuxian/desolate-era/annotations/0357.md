## Chapter Overview

Ji Ning’s destiny takes a sharp, secret turn. After being handed a token to summon Celestial Immortal Evergreen—a Pure Yang True Immortal whom the Grand Xia Emperor has selected as his teacher—Ji Ning instead meets with his cousin Yuchi Xiyue, says his farewells, and follows the mysterious Whiteclad Immortal Jun (a disciple of Patriarch Subhuti) into a torn void passage, leaving the Grand Xia World behind forever. The chapter closes with a comic coda: twelve days later, Celestial Immortal Evergreen shows up at the palace, only to learn that Ji Ning never crushed the token and has long vanished. The arrogant Evergreen shrugs and leaves. Ji Ning has officially slipped the net of imperial arrangements and stepped into the unknown.

## Key Plot Points

- King Yan and King Qi deliver Celestial Immortal Evergreen’s token to Ji Ning, with King Yan whispering that the immortal is “a bit of a recluse” and that Ji Ning must be respectful.
- Ji Ning’s internal calculation: he already knows he will follow Patriarch Subhuti, so he cannot accept Evergreen as a master. The Whiteclad Immortal Jun reminds him that revealing the truth would risk Daofather Crimsonbright’s interference.
- Yuchi Xiyue visits for a heartfelt farewell. Ji Ning cannot tell her the truth—only warns her not to let her hatred for the Shaoyin clan consume her, promising that once he becomes a Celestial Immortal, revenge will be easy.
- The Whiteclad Immortal Jun takes the token from Ji Ning and explains that after ten days it will dissolve naturally; the ruse is safe.
- Ji Ning, Little Qing, and Uncle White are swept up by clouds. The Whiteclad Immortal Jun tears open the void, and they enter the passage, leaving the Grand Xia World.
- Epilogue: the Grand Xia Emperor learns that Ji Ning never used the token. Celestial Immortal Evergreen, indifferent, closes the matter. Ji Ning is now untraceable.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Tristar Crescent Abode” — A Riddle of the Heart.**  
The name literally means “a slanted crescent moon and three stars” (*xie yue san xing*). As the Whiteclad Immortal Jun points out, this is a visual riddle: a hooked stroke (the crescent) plus three dots (the stars) forms the Chinese character **心 (xīn)**, meaning “heart.” This is a direct reference to *Journey to the West*, where Sun Wukong’s master, Patriarch Subhuti, dwells in the “Crescent-Moon Three-Star Cave.” In *Desolate Era*, the author I Eat Tomatoes reinterprets this as the ultimate hidden sanctuary—a place that can only be found through the heart. It reinforces the Daoist idea that the truest mysteries are internal, not geographical.

**Why Ji Ning Can’t Tell Anyone.**  
The Grand Xia World is under the domain of Daofather Crimsonbright. If the Daofather learns that a disciple of his prized Immortal Fortunes Assembly champion has been stolen away by an even greater master (Patriarch Subhuti), it could cause a political incident. This is classic Xianxia court intrigue: even the most powerful beings must tread carefully around the egos of immortals higher up the ladder. Subhuti’s secrecy is both a protection and a test.

**Celestial Immortal Evergreen — A Missed Connection.**  
This Pure Yang True Immortal is a genuine powerhouse: he has fully comprehended the Dao of the Sword, a Grand Dao. Under normal circumstances, being taken as his disciple would have been a phenomenal honor. But Ji Ning, guided by an even more mysterious force, cannot accept. The scene where Evergreen shrugs and walks away perfectly captures the *indifference of high-level cultivators*—they do not beg. A disciple must be willing.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a **quiet, emotional turning point** rather than an action set piece. Savor the farewell between Ji Ning and Yuchi Xiyue—it’s laced with unsaid truths, and the weight of decades of separation. The Whiteclad Immortal Jun’s casual tone when he tosses the token away is a reminder that for beings at his level, the schemes of the Grand Xia court are almost laughably petty. The final beat, with Celestial Immortal Evergreen learning that his would-be disciple has vanished, is a dry punchline that closes the volume on a note of clever escape. Get ready: the next volume promises the mysteries of the Tristar Crescent Abode and a new kind of training under the most secretive master in the Three Realms.
