## Chapter Overview

Ji Ning does something so brazen it borders on insane—he walks straight into a Shaoyan clan ambush with zero disguise, rides their own teleportation array to the Grand Xia Imperial Capital, and then leisurely sightsees as if he owns the place. This chapter is a masterclass in using sheer audacity as a power move. No combat, no bloodshed—just pure psychological domination and some world-class city porn as we get our first jaw-dropping look at the heart of the Grand Xia Dynasty. The stakes are quietly recalibrated: Ji Ning isn't running; he's *arriving*.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning returns to the Anchan Commandery's Yinglong Guard branch **without any disguise**, his iconic animal-hide outfit, green serpent, and white hound all on full display. The Shaoyan clan’s lookout—a white-robed elder who has been squatting there for over a year—is initially too stunned to believe it’s really him.
- The ten servants confirm his identity, and Ji Ning calmly announces his destination: the Grand Xia Imperial Capital. The elder tries to board the same array, delivering a threat; Ji Ning dismisses him with open mockery.
- They arrive at the halfway point of the **Yinglong Divine Mountain**—a ten-thousand-li-tall magic treasure that stands taller than even the Imperial Palace. The elder signals the Shaoyan clan's permanent guards, but Ji Ning simply strolls to the cliff edge to take in the view.
- From this vantage point, Ji Ning beholds the **Heaven-Mang Hall**, the Grand Xia Emperor's residence: nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine li tall, radiating multicolored light, with nine layers of clouds starting at six thousand li. The scale is breathtaking—a structure that rivals the legendary Cloud Palace of the Heavenly Court.
- Ji Ning, Little Qing, and Uncle White descend to the cloud-paved streets of the capital, noting the complete absence of mortals and the overwhelming presence of immortal cultivators everywhere. Ji Ning reflects on the power balance between mortal-world emperors and the Celestial Thearch—a subtle clue that the Grand Xia is no ordinary kingdom.
- They approach the Imperial City gate, guarded by **four ten-thousand-zhang Fiendgods**—one fire, one water, one metal, one darkness—each radiating an aura comparable to the terrifying guardian from the Mount Yujue Wasteland trial. The chapter ends on this haunting image of absolute, overwhelming power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Nine-Layer Heaven-Mang Hall**: The Heaven-Mang Hall’s nine layers of clouds (red, plus eight more above) are a direct nod to the *Nine Heavens* (九天) of Daoist cosmology—the layered celestial realms that form the abode of the highest deities. In Chinese mythology, the Jade Emperor resides in the 36th Heaven; here, the Grand Xia Emperor’s palace mirrors that celestial hierarchy on a mortal-world scale, reinforcing the Xianxia trope that a major-world emperor is effectively a terrestrial god-king.

**Yinglong Divine Mountain vs. Heaven-Mang Hall**: The fact that the Yinglong Guard’s headquarters is deliberately built *taller* than the Emperor’s own palace is a massive political statement. In traditional Chinese city planning and feng shui, building height was a strict symbol of hierarchy—no one could build higher than the ruler. The Yinglong Divine Mountain breaking this rule hints at the extraordinary, perhaps quasi-divine, authority of the Yinglong Guard within the Grand Xia system. It’s a subtle worldbuilding detail that Ji Ning (and the observant reader) immediately picks up on.

**Four Guardian Fiendgods**: The four elemental Fiendgods guarding the Imperial City gate—Fire, Water, Metal, and Darkness—echo the Four Symbols (四象) of Chinese cosmology (Azure Dragon, Vermilion Bird, White Tiger, Black Tortoise), but reimagined as raw, primordial divine beings. Their ten-thousand-zhang stature and oppressive auras signal that the Grand Xia Imperial Capital is a place where even the gatekeepers are apocalyptic-level threats, setting the stage for the scale of power Ji Ning will need to navigate.

## Reading Guide

This is a **breather chapter** disguised as a flex. Don't expect action—the tension here is entirely psychological and architectural. Watch how Ji Ning’s behavior has shifted: he’s no longer a hunted fugitive dodging shadows; he’s walking into the lion’s den with a smirk, daring the Shaoyan clan to make a move in the heart of the dynasty’s power. The real treat is the worldbuilding. The descriptions of the Heaven-Mang Hall and the four Fiendgods are meant to awe you—take the time to visualize them. This is your first real look at the Grand Xia’s true scale, and it’s meant to humble both Ji Ning and the reader. Also, pay attention to Ji Ning’s quiet observation about the Celestial Thearch not being treated with absolute reverence in ancient texts. That’s not just trivia—it’s a hint at the novel’s broader cosmology, where mortal-world empires can genuinely rival Heaven itself. The seed of that idea is planted right here.
