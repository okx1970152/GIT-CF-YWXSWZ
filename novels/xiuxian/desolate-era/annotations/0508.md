## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia political negotiation disguised as a failed apprenticeship. Old Man Yuan, a mysterious and wealthy ancient power, desperately wants to take Ji Ning as his disciple, offering mountains of divine abilities and treasure. But Patriarch Subhuti, Ji Ning’s current master, blocks the deal with a single, immovable condition: choose a side in the coming war. What follows is an explosive, hour-long argument between two primordial brothers that cracks open the secret behind the Three Realms’ brewing storm. Ji Ning doesn’t get a second master, but he walks away with a priceless consolation prize—a chaotic but brilliant pile of Heartforce application techniques that promise to fix his biggest tactical weakness. The chapter shifts from tense debate to cerebral treasure-hunt as Ji Ning processes his new inheritance.

## Key Plot Points

- Bodhi blocks the apprenticeship deal with an absolute condition: Old Man Yuan must pledge allegiance to Bodhi’s faction in the upcoming war.
- Old Man Yuan frantically tries to negotiate around the demand, offering treasures and pleading precedent (Little Peacock’s dual-master status), but Bodhi remains unmoved, citing the war’s changed stakes.
- The two Daofathers argue for over an hour, revealing deep ideological rifts: Bodhi frames the war as a fight for survival, while Old Man Yuan argues it’s better to bow to the ‘old undying one’ ruling the Three Realms rather than bleed out.
- Ji Ning overhears hints of a terrifying ‘old undying one’ who wants to unify the Three Realms, identified as the Seamless Gate’s leader—a major power whose identity is kept obscure for now.
- The argument ends in a stalemate; Old Man Yuan angrily leaves but gives Ji Ning an unsystematic collection of Heartforce techniques called *The Heart Sutra* before departing.
- Ji Ning realizes the techniques are fractured but profound, especially one that uses Heartforce to dramatically boost the number of flying swords a soul can control—a potential game-changer for his Ki Refining combat style.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Dao-Companionship vs. Dual Mastery**
Old Man Yuan argues that Ji Ning can call both him and Bodhi ‘master,’ citing ‘Little Peacock’ as precedent. This touches on a classic Xianxia trope: the cross-institutional disciple. In many novels, a protagonist has multiple teachers, but here, the war makes that impossible. The stakes aren’t academic—they’re about which Daofather gets to write Ji Ning’s death warrant if their sides go to war.

**The ‘Old Undying One’ and the Seamless Gate**
The chapter explicitly names a mysterious supreme being unifying the Three Realms. This is the first clear hint at the true scope of the conflict. In Xianxia, the ‘old monster’ archetype is a ruler who has lived so long that they view all new powers as upstart insects. The Seamless Gate’s leader fits this perfectly—and Bodhi and his allies are the insurgents trying to keep their world from being subjugated.

**Heartforce as a Daoist Concept**
Heartforce (心力) is distinct from Ki or Divine Power. It’s drawn from a cultivator’s will, sincerity, and Dao-heart. In Daoist practice, the ‘heart’ is the seat of intention and spiritual awareness. The novel weaponizes this: Old Man Yuan’s *Heart Sutra* is a collection of ‘heart’ applications—using willpower to command the soul, body, and treasures—making it a supreme art of mental coordination rather than raw energy.

## Reading Guide

Buckle up for a chapter that’s 90% two old gods arguing in a possessed craftsman’s body—and it’s absolutely riveting. The payoff here is twofold. First, you get a masterclass in faction politics: Bodhi’s refusal is cold, principled, and terrifyingly rational. He’s not just being stubborn; he’s protecting Ji Ning from a future execution scenario. Second, the *Heart Sutra* is a genius writer’s tool for upgrading the protagonist without making him instantly overpowered. Ji Ning gets a toolbox, not a finished weapon—and because the techniques are unsystematic, *he* has to do the work to turn them into coherent power. That’s the kind of progression that keeps a Xianxia satisfying for another thousand chapters.

## The Principle of the ‘Limited Way’
In Daoist philosophy, a *Dao* that has a name is already a limited Dao. Old Man Yuan’s scattered techniques mirror this: they are brilliant flashes of insight, each perfect for one specific problem, but they don’t form a complete ‘way.’ Ji Ning’s genius is that he can see the underlying logic and synthesize a whole path from the fragments. This is a core Xianxia skill—not just learning techniques, but understanding the Dao behind them.
