## Chapter Overview

This chapter is where the reunion between Ji Ning and Mu Northson reaches its emotional and narrative peak. After years of silence and suffering, Northson finally breaks down—and breaks open his story. What emerges is a harrowing account of captivity, grief, and a mysterious power so vast it rivals the Grand Xia Dynasty itself. Ji Ning, hardened yet tender, must first earn his broken brother’s trust with displays of god-tier wealth (a thousand supreme-grade Immortal-ranked flying swords) and reality-bending transformation techniques before the truth comes spilling out. The forbidden zone isn’t just a prison—it’s a puppet factory for an unseen empire of terrifying ambition. Buckle up, fellow Daoists… this is where the quiet storm turns into a typhoon.

## Key Plot Points

- After crying out years of repressed anguish, Mu Northson regains composure and apologizes for his emotional outburst. Ji Ning immediately presses him to explain what happened.
- Northson initially refuses to speak, fearing that revealing the truth will only drag Ji Ning into danger. His guilt and despair are palpable—he sees himself as already ruined.
- Ji Ning convinces him by revealing his own transformation: after the Immortal Fortunes Assembly, he became the personal disciple of a Daofather. To prove it, he produces a thousand supreme-grade Immortal-ranked flying swords—a display of wealth and power that stuns even a seasoned Earth Immortal.
- As final proof, Ji Ning demonstrates a shape-shifting technique, transforming into Northson himself, Wufeng Immortal, Xiangliu Fang, and others, perfectly replicating their aura and karma.
- Convinced, Northson tells his story: he and his Dao-companion Yu Xia were attacked by black-robed Loose Immortals. Yu Xia was killed, and her soul was captured.
- Northson was brought to this forbidden zone, a puppet-manufacturing stronghold of a mysterious power that spans over a hundred commanderies. He and hundreds of other puppet masters are forced to build increasingly powerful war puppets.
- The pinnacle of their work: a Divine Fiend Puppet that, in the hands of a single Celestial Immortal, can overwhelm ten or even a hundred Celestial Immortals.
- Northson doesn’t dare rebel or commit suicide—Yu Xia’s soul is held hostage, and the general issued a Heavenly Dao oath promising freedom after a thousand years of faithful service.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Soul Imprisonment (神魂被囚)** — In Xianxia, the death of the body is only the first death. The soul (or spirit, 神魂) can continue to exist and even reincarnate through the Six Paths. But capturing a soul before it can enter Samsara? That is a fate worse than death. The victim is rendered conscious, helpless, and completely at the mercy of the captor, with no hope of escape. It turns a “final” loss into an eternal leash around a loved one’s neck.

**Heavenly Dao Oath (天道誓言)** — A self-enforcing vow sworn on the laws of the universe itself. Breaking it invites instant karmic annihilation, not just an inconvenience. When a Celestial Immortal general issues such an oath, it carries lethal weight. It’s the one thing that can persuade captives to trust their captors, no matter how much they hate them. You can lie to men, but you cannot lie to Heaven.

**Puppet Grandmaster vs Puppet Artisan** — In this world, making puppets isn’t blacksmithing with strings. Each puppet is an intricate metaphysical device inscribed with Dao matrices (阵图), each containing fragments of deeper cosmic truths. A grandmaster like Northson doesn’t just assemble parts; he *comprehends* the Dao through the act of creation. It’s why his cultivation advanced so fast—the suffering was real, but so was the forced enlightenment.

### Samsara (轮回)

The endless cycle of death and rebirth through the Six Paths. Capturing a soul prevents it from entering Samsara, a fate most cultivators consider a graver horror than death itself.

## Reading Guide

Prepare yourself, because this chapter hits hard. The reunion we’ve been waiting for comes with a gut-punch of tragedy. Watch how Ji Ning handles his brother’s trauma: not with grand speeches or hasty revenge, but with raw presence, patience, and overwhelming proof of his own power. The shift from controlled calm to cold fury is subtle but bone-chilling. Northson’s description of the Divine Fiend Puppet is a huge red flag—if one stronghold can produce a weapon that makes Celestial Immortals look like children, what does the *full* might of this mystery power look like? This revelation sets the stage for a conflict far bigger than Snowdragon Mountain or any disciple rivalry. And through it all, the carrot-and-stick of Yu Xia’s soul—and the general’s thousand-year oath—reminds us that even enemies can be scarily rational and effective.
