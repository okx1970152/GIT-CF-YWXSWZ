## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the high society of the Grand Xia capital, fellow Daoists! This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia political theater. Ji Ning, now publicly backed by Prince Yan and a retinue of show-stopping Golden Armored Guards, steps into the Watercloud Pavilion for a banquet hosted by none other than Zhuanshui Xiaolou, one of the capital's legendary Four Young Masters. The tension is delicious: we get a closer look at the complex web of alliances and rivalries among the top-tier young geniuses of the realm. Ji Ning, the rising star from the backwater Swallow Mountain, is now sitting at the same table as the true elite—Tiannong Quge, Bo Yan, and the sour, jealous Xiao Lang. It’s a scene brimming with subtext, barely concealed contempt, and a stark class divide that Ji Ning navigates with the cool, unflappable demeanor of a seasoned immortal. Get ready for some power-broking with monsters!

## Key Plot Points

- **Arrival in Style**: Ji Ning arrives at the magnificent Watercloud Pavilion, a creation of the mighty Zhuanshui clan. His entry with a full squad of Golden Armored Guards immediately establishes his newfound political capital and triggers the servant’s respect and the nobles’ curiosity.
- **The Table is Set**: Hosted by the barefoot and frivolous Zhuanshui Xiaolou, Ji Ning is formally introduced to Tiannong Quge, the low-key powerhouse of the Tiannong clan; the beautiful and famous Bo Yan; and the petulant, jealous Xiao Lang. The purpose of the gathering is blatant networking and information exchange ahead of the Immortal Fortunes Assembly.
- **The Jealousy of Xiao Lang**: Xiao Lang, a cultivator full of self-importance, openly mocks Ji Ning in his thoughts and to his companions, dismissing his strength and attributing his rise solely to luck and his connection with the Xiyue Commandery Princess. This establishes him as a likely antagonist or petty rival.
- **Networking with the Elite**: Ji Ning, Tiannong Quge, and Zhuanshui Xiaolou engage in a friendly, respectful conversation, highlighting the bond between the truly promising talents. They represent the highest potential for the upcoming tournament.
- **A Beastly Entertainment**: To close the scene, Zhuanshui Xiaolou brings out two captured alien creatures from a minor world to fight each other as entertainment for his guests, setting a classic Xianxia backdrop for further dialogue and power displays.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Power of 'Face' and the 'Four Young Masters':** This chapter perfectly illustrates the high-society politics within the Xianxia aristocracy. The status of 'Young Master' isn't just a title; it’s a measure of a clan’s power and the individual's potential. Shaoyan Nong was one of the Four Young Masters, and his death at Ji Ning’s hands was the catalyst for this entire political drama. When Zhuanshui Xiaolou states that he is a cut above Shaoyan Nong, he is essentially confirming the power hierarchy. The Golden Armored Guards serve as a ‘face’ symbol—an undeniable mark of high favor from Prince Yan, instantly elevating Ji Ning's status and forcing everyone in the room to recalibrate their expectations of him. The subtle way Xiao Lang tries to tear Ji Ning down is a classic 'losing face' defense mechanism from a lesser noble who feels threatened.

**The Golden Armored Guards as Political Currency:** Ji Ning’s gift from Prince Yan is a major plot device. In the Grand Xia Dynasty, these guards aren't just soldiers; they are a direct extension of the ruler's will. High officials and valued clansmen receive them as a sign of trust. By having them, Ji Ning is publicly showing that he is under the powerful protection of an Imperial Prince, making any direct attack on him a challenge to Prince Yan himself. This is a nuclear deterrent in the social arena.

**Training World Cuisine: Alien Fights as Entertainment:** The finale with the alien cage fight is a classic trope in the ‘super rich and powerful’ Xianxia scene. These aren’t just frightful beasts; they are rare specimens from other minor worlds, showcasing the host's wealth and connection to cosmic resources. It’s a form of macabre entertainment akin to the gladiatorial contests of Ancient Rome, but with a distinctly Xianxia flavor—it’s a spectacle to ‘liven the mood’ and a subtle flex of power.

### Cousin-brother (表弟/表姐)

In Chinese family hierarchy, this term denotes a maternal relationship, distinct from a paternal cousin. This chapter solidifies this specific bond between Ji Ning and Yuchi Xiyue.

## Reading Guide

- **Watch the Dynamics**: Pay close attention to how Ji Ning’s status changes the moment the servants see his Golden Armored Guards. This isn’t just about his fighting ability; it’s about his political affiliations. The dialogue is a dance of veiled threats and strategic flattery.
- **Xiao Lang is a Setup**: Don’t just dismiss Xiao Lang as a background bully. His jealousy and scorn are a loud signal that he will play a significant role, likely as an early-stage antagonist for Ji Ning to crush in the Immortal Fortunes Assembly. His character profile—handsome, proud, but not genuinely powerful—is a staple archetype for a swift and satisfying beatdown.
- **The First Encounter with Future Titans**: This is our first proper look at Tiannong Quge and the elite tier of the tournament participants. How Ji Ning builds or avoids alliances with these figures will shape his entire experience in the capital. Notice how naturally he interacts with them, a sign of his own burgeoning status.
- **The Gladiatorial Subtext**: The alien cage fight is a perfect background mirror for the Immortal Fortunes Assembly they are all preparing for. Everyone present, including Ji Ning, is just a gladiator in a grander arena, fighting for the favor of the ultimate powers.
