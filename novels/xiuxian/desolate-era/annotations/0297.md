## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists! This chapter is pure, concentrated Xianxia *social combat* at its finest. Ji Ning has officially arrived in the Grand Xia imperial capital, and he doesn't just tiptoe in—he kicks the door down. After a major prison break and a daring rescue, you’d think he’d keep a low profile, right? Nope. He heads straight to the most exclusive auction house in the city and picks a very public fight with his mortal enemies, the Shaoyan clan. This is a masterclass in soft power warfare: the goal isn't just to buy a formation diagram, but to shout his name from the rooftops of the Imperial City. The chapter culminates in a jaw-dropping reveal of a true sacred beast treasure, setting the stage for a bidding war that could define his next power spike. Get ready for a spectacle of political maneuvering, face-slapping, and astronomical bids.

## Key Plot Points
*   **The Bidding War as a Statement**: The chapter opens with the Shaoyan clan bitterly bidding on a formation diagram. Ji Ning, with surgical precision, begins to counter-bid, forcing the price up. The core tension here isn't the diagram itself, but the reputation on the line. Ji Ning's goal is transparent—he *wants* to be recognized.
*   **The Shaoyan Clan’s Dilemma**: Shaoyan Xiushui, the clan elder present, quickly diagnoses the problem. He realizes that engaging with Ji Ning only gives him more fame. However, once public, they can’t back down without losing face. Their internal debate highlights a classic Xianxia dynamic: the conflict between cold pragmatism and hot-blooded pride.
*   **The Ultimate Troll Move**: Ji Ning doesn't just win the bid (overpaying slightly at 128,000 jin). He delivers a crushing verbal blow, publicly stating that Shaoyan Nong gave him *millions* of jin worth of treasures. This is a direct, vicious insult that reminds everyone of the clan's greatest recent humiliation, making it the highlight of the social duel.
*   **The Treasure Reveal**: The narrative shifts from social combat to material desire. The auction house unveils its crown jewel: five tail feathers from a mythical, pure-blooded Peacock sacred beast. These feathers, attuned to the Five Elements, are a once-in-a-lifetime material for forging top-tier treasures.
*   **Ji Ning’s Personal Interest**: Unlike the others who see a forging component, Ji Ning recognizes the feathers as the perfect fuel for his *Starseizing Hand* cultivation. He knows that the pure elemental quintessence within them directly translates to a massive boost in his personal combat power, making this more than just a fancy artifact.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Art of Face and Soft Power Warfare**: This chapter is a perfect primer on the Xianxia concept of "Face" (面子 / miànzi). The Shaoyan clan isn't just upset about the money; they are enraged that Ji Ning is *publicly* undermining their social status. In a world where power defines reputation, a loss of face can translate to a loss of influence, alliances, and even safety. Ji Ning’s strategy is brilliant: he uses their own pride against them. By forcing them into a public bidding war, he dictates the terms of engagement, turning their desire to save face into a tool for his own fame. This is soft power applied with a sledgehammer.

**The Sacred Peacock and the Five Elements**: The sacred peacock (孔雀 / kǒngquè) in Chinese mythology and Xianxia is strongly tied to the Buddhist philosophy. A famous legend tells of the Peacock Mahamayuri who could devour people, and even once swallowed the Buddha, who had to tear himself out from its back. In Daoist/Xianxia contexts, they are seen as children of the Five Elements (五行 / Wǔxíng). The five elements—Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth—are not just materials but the fundamental building blocks of all existence. A pure-blooded Peacock can manifest these elements perfectly in its tail, representing a harmonious convergence of the very forces of creation.

**The Immortal Fortunes Assembly as a Setup**: The mention of the recruitment tournament in the Shaoyan elder's head-calculations is a classic Xianxia trope. These massive tournaments are the ultimate career fair for cultivators. Ji Ning knows that by making himself a famous, controversial figure, he becomes a more attractive prospect for major powers. An established, famous cultivator is a better investment than an unknown genius from the sticks. He is intentionally creating an entry point for his next major power-up.

## Reading Guide
*   **Watch the Character Tells**: The side characters in the auction house are fantastic. Watch how the Shaoyan elder speaks calmly while his son panics. Their internal struggle between strategic withdrawal and emotional pride is a masterclass in Xianxia nobility behavior. Also, see how Ji Ning's analytical mind is always working, even while being flashy.
*   **Savor the Worldbuilding Through Dialogue**: The auction is a brilliant vehicle for exposition. We learn about the Grand Xia's royal clan, the existence of the Heaven Realm, the power levels of Celestial Immortals, and the value of rare materials, all through natural, competitive dialogue. This is how proper Xianxia deepens its world without an info-dump.
*   **Appreciate the "Magic System" Resource Loop**: This chapter is beautiful in showing what makes a cultivator tick. It’s not just about meditation and insights; it’s about resources. Ji Ning’s *Starseizing Hand* requires specific elements. He just got a massive payday from his last victory. Now, he’s on the hunt for specific items to convert that wealth into **power**. Watching him calculate this conversion is a core joy of the Progression Fantasy genre.
*   **Get Ready for a Bidding War**: The end of the chapter sets up an epic auction. The treasure is rare, the pool of bidders is deep, and Ji Ning has a personal interest. This isn't just about who has the most money; it's about who wants it more and who can afford the political cost of backing down. This is going to be good.
