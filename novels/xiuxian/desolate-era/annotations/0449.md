## Chapter Overview

This is a masterclass in psychological warfare from Ji Ning. Having just detonated the head of the Shaoyin clan in a single, devastating strike, he now stands in the ruins of their base, *waiting for five Celestial Immortals to come to him*! But this isn't mere bravado. While he taunts them like a mad god, his true ace in the hole is being set up: seventy-one layers of formations laid down by Uncle White. Ji Ning has transformed the enemy's heartland into his own killing ground, baiting the trap with his own body. The chapter crackles with the tension of a stalemate—the attacker cannot bind him, and the defenders are refusing to take the bait just yet. It’s a rare, delicious moment of pure tactical taunting that we, the readers, get to enjoy before the storm breaks.

## Key Plot Points

- The Seamless Gate observes Ji Ning’s attack from afar; Celestial Immortal Blackcloud believes the Shaoyin clan can handle him if they use their ultimate trump cards.
- Five Shaoyin Celestial Immortals—Huoba, Chixin, Witheredwood, Goldenbell, and Jingyu—gather and plan to use the ‘Lesser Yin Demon-Subduing Formation’ to capture Ji Ning.
- The Vicious Old Fiend’s second true body returns, realizing his true body was destroyed by the disguised Ji Ning; he seethes with vengeful rage but remains hidden among the cowering Loose Immortals.
- Ji Ning, while destroying the terrain with his Ten-Thousand-Mile Flow River, accidentally unearths a colossal, mysterious fiery-red copper pillar buried deep underground, covered in ancient runes.
- Uncle White confirms that 71 layers of hidden formations are ready; Ji Ning returns to his original form, drops all pretense, and begins goading the five Celestial Immortals.
- His insult-laden challenge echoes throughout the mortal cities, galvanizing commoners who have never heard a Celestial Immortal spoken of with such contempt.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“I Might Just Kill All Five of You” – The Psychology of the Xianxia Provocation**  
This chapter is a textbook example of the *Public Challenge* or *Profession of War Tinder* trope. In Xianxia, openly insulting a Celestial Immortal—especially on their own turf—is a calculated act of supreme confidence. By broadcasting his challenge to mortals and cultivators alike, Ji Ning is doing two things: (1) forcing the Shaoyin Celestial Immortals to respond publicly (backing down would cost them face, or *mianzi*, which destroys a clan’s authority) and (2) rigging the psychological battlefield. The mortals hearing his words aren’t just cannon fodder; they are the instrument of a “Face Trap.” If the Celestial Immortals fail to kill the blasphemer publicly, the mystique of their power cracks.

**The Formation Domain Tactic**  
The classic Xianxia maneuver of “retreating into my own domain” is inverted here. Ji Ning is not retreating to a fortress; he is creating a fortress *inside* the enemy’s palace. With Uncle White’s seventy-one formation layers, Ji Ning has effectively carved out a pocket dimension of safety in hostile territory, making it so the “fortress” moves with him. This is the mark of a high-level strategist who understands that geography is fungible when you have the tools to rewrite the local laws of combat.
