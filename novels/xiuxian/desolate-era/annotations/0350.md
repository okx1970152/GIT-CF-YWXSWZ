## Chapter Overview

The dust settles on the Immortal Fortunes Assembly—but the tension only thickens. Ji Ning, Mu Northson, and Blackstone Realperson have clinched the top three spots, yet the true prize remains undecided: whose name will Daofather Crimsonbright call? While the Grand Xia Emperor’s Primaltwin rushes to deliver the battle records to the Daofather, the three geniuses find themselves frozen in agonized anticipation, their hearts a drumroll of hope and dread. Meanwhile, in a hidden world within the void, a mysterious old black-haired Patriarch dispatches his messenger, the fearsome Floodcover Sage, to retrieve his hundred-year-old secret disciple. A new cosmic player is about to step onto the board, and the ripple in the Three Realms’ delicate power balance is just beginning.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Patriarch’s summons:** An ancient Daoist master, identified by the Taiji Diagram over his head (hinting at his supreme rank), sends his golden-robed boy to summon Floodcover Sage—a terrifying Divine God known across the Three Realms. The Patriarch reveals that he accepted a secret disciple a hundred years ago in the Grand Xia World: Zhang Qi, also known as the Slovenly True Immortal.
- **Floodcover’s mission:** Floodcover Sage is ordered to travel to the Grand Xia imperial capital and bring Zhang Qi back without causing any trouble. The Patriarch emphasizes discretion and restraint, suggesting the gravity of the selection process.
- **Waiting game at the Skycrown Palace:** The top three—Ji Ning, Mu Northson, and Blackstone—stand nervously before the Grand Xia Emperor and nearly a thousand Celestial Immortals, awaiting the Daofather’s decision. The Emperor sends his Primaltwin to deliver the battle recordings.
- **Divine Ability reward:** While waiting, the three are granted the right to each choose a Divine Ability from the Grand Xia treasury, but their minds are elsewhere—consumed by the question of who the Daofather will select.
- **The emperor’s promise:** The Xia Emperor confirms that at least one of the three will be accepted by Daofather Crimsonbright, and the other two by Pure Yang True Immortals—ensuring all three will ascend in status, but the stakes for being the Daofather’s personal disciple remain sky-high.
- **Floodcover’s dramatic entrance:** As the trio departs, a colossal aura descends upon the hall. Floodcover Sage strides in, his presence chilling the Celestial Immortals. He loudly demands, “Which one is Zhang Qi? Which one is the Slovenly True Immortal?”—sending the slovenly youth into sheer panic.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Patriarch and the Taiji Diagram:** The old black-haired man meditating beneath a massive Taiji Diagram is a classic symbol in Daoist iconography. In Xianxia, this combination is almost synonymous with a figure of supreme cosmic rank—likely a Daofather. The Taiji (Supreme Ultimate) represents the interplay of Yin and Yang, the origin of all things in the universe. For a character to have a Taiji Diagram hovering above them is a visual shorthand for “I am the Dao made manifest.” This reverential imagery immediately distinguishes him from lesser gods and immortals.

**Floodcover Sage and Divine God Ranking:** Floodcover is a Divine God (天神), the pinnacle of Fiendgod Body Refining, which makes him absolute top-tier in the Three Realms’ hierarchy. His entrance causes Celestial Immortals to tremble, showing the gap between even the highest Ki Refiner and a true Chaos True God. This chapter reinforces the power pyramid: Daofathers and Chaos True Gods are the true rulers; Celestial Thearchs like the Grand Xia Emperor are powerful but still subordinates.

**Pure Yang True Immortal vs Daofather:** The chapter clarifies the reward structure: the top three are guaranteed at least a Pure Yang True Immortal master, but only one can be Daofather Crimsonbright’s personal disciple. A Pure Yang True Immortal is a True Immortal at Perfection—supremely powerful, but still infinitely below a Daofather. This gap underlines why the top three’s hearts are pounding.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is pure emotional throttle after a slugfest of combat. If you’ve been reading for the epic battles, don’t worry—this breather packs a psychological gut punch. The contrast between the top three’s silent, desperate hope and the boisterous, overwhelming entrance of Floodcover Sage is a masterfully built tension bomb. Pay close attention to the Slovenly True Immortal’s panic. That panic isn’t just comic relief; it’s a signal that a major plot twist is unfolding. The slow reveal of the Patriarch’s secret apprentice is classic Xianxia world-expansion—someone we thought was a background side character may just be a sleeper legend. Get ready for a power shift that will echo through the next arc.

## Behind the Door: The ‘Secret Apprentice’ Trope

In Xianxia, the *bai nian qian mi shou tu* (hundred-year-old secret disciple) is a hallmark of the genre’s “incremental reveal” narrative structure. A cultivation superpower plants a hidden seedling in a remote world, waits for centuries, then plucks them at the moment of greatest political consequence. The Slovenly True Immortal was always presented as a comedic, lazy figure—yet now we learn he has been a Daofather’s chosen for a century. This subverts the reader’s expectations and demonstrates that in the Three Realms, power hides in the most unassuming faces. Keep an eye on the slovenly one.

## The Floodcover Mystery

Why does Floodcover Sage, a Divine God of near-cosmic power, need to be ordered to “not cause trouble”? This tells us that Floodcover is a known *troublemaker*—his mere presence at the imperial capital is a destabilizing event. The Patriarch’s warning *not* to cause trouble is itself a mark of how much trouble Floodcover is capable of. His casual address to the Xia Emperor (“Xiamang”) and the Xia Emperor’s cordial, slightly deferential response reveals the hierarchy: even the ruler of a major world is subordinate to a Divine God acting on a Daofather’s orders.
