## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to another heart-pounding installment of the Desolate Era chronicles! And oh, what a chapter this is. Our boy Ji Ning has officially crashed the party of the legendary Dongliu Twelve Demon Kings, and he did it with the most delicious bait-and-switch in recent memory. Chapter 380 is a masterclass in Xianxia warfare—psychological deception, calculated power scaling, and a brutal, surgical strike that announces Ji Ning isn't just a promising talent anymore; he's a genuine threat. The tension is thick enough to cut with a Northsea sword, as the imperial Qi clan watches their greatest nightmare roll up in full force, only to have their new protector pull off a shocking assassination right under the noses of eleven other Demon Kings. Get ready for a tactical takedown that redefines the word "cheeky."

## Key Plot Points

- The Dongliu Twelve Demon Kings arrive in full force, and the imperial Qi clan is paralyzed with fear. Prince Qi Rufeng and the black-robed girl recognize each Demon King from their imperial records and immediately assume Ji Ning is doomed. They realize a Greater Teleportation Dao Talisman won't save them—at the Demon Kings' level, they can attack before the talisman activates.
- Qingya Xiaoyu's emotional awakening: having just found a new master after severing ties with the imperial Qi clan, she is terrified of losing Ji Ning, revealing her profound loneliness and desperate hope.
- Ji Ning uses his divine sense to scan the enemy and discovers a shocking anomaly: among the twelve, the infamous Goldfur Bear Demon King, their leader, has the *least* karmic sin. Eleven are drenched in blood-light and vengeful spirits, while the leader bears only black turbid energy. Ji Ning files this crucial intel away.
- The Flamewing Demon King underestimates Ji Ning, attempting to crush him with a mere manipulation of heaven and earth. Ji Ning deliberately fights at a reduced power level—matching a top-tier Loose Immortal—to lull the Demon Kings into complacency.
- Ji Ning taunts them with boasts about his "master," keeping the other Demon Kings on edge and distracted, preventing them from joining the fight immediately.
- When the Flamewing Demon King closes in for a real kill using his Immortal-ranked treasures, Ji Ning flips the switch. He swaps out his Heaven-ranked swords for the Qianniu sword and Immortal-ranked flying swords, unleashes his true sword-art, and transforms his six sword-lights into devastating black divine dragons.
- In a single, devastating blitz, Ji Ning overwhelms and annihilates the Flamewing Demon King before any of the other eleven can intervene.
- The chapter ends with Ji Ning's calm, almost playful confession to the remaining Demon Kings: he had to trick them because he knew they had a joint formation, and he needed to kill one before they could use it.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*The Art of the Feint and the Value of Intelligence*: Ji Ning's strategy here is a textbook Xianxia application of "know thy enemy and know thyself." He doesn't just charge in blindly. First, he uses divine sense for reconnaissance, learning that the Goldfur Bear Demon King, the leader, has astonishingly low karmic sin—a red flag that this "demon" is far more complex than he appears. Second, he confirms his suspicion of a joint formation, a classic trope where a group amplifies their power exponentially through synchronized technique. By psychologically baiting them into letting one fight alone and then executing a speed kill, he neutralizes their greatest combined asset before the battle truly begins. This is the difference between a brute and a cultivator with a tempered Dao-heart.

*The Weight of Karma in Decision-Making*: The chapter also beautifully uses the karmic merit/sin system as a tactical tool. Ji Ning's divine sense reveals the blood-light of sin on eleven Demon Kings, confirming their brutality and justifying their slaughter. But the anomaly of the leader's clean karmic record plants a seed of mystery and caution. In Xianxia, a high-karma enemy is often either a hidden figure protected by fate or a survivor who has survived through intelligence, not just murder. Ji Ning's wariness of the Goldfur Bear is a sign of his growth as a strategist.

*Trial by Combat and the Mirror Match*: Ji Ning's initial restrained fight is a form of "Trial by Combat" where he deliberately restricts himself to pure sword technique and Dao comprehension, hiding his divine abilities (like the Starseizing Hand, which he notably does NOT use) and his true combat foundation. This is a classic Xianxia trope—a cultivator tests the waters with a mirror match of foundations before revealing their full, devastating hand.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up—because Chapter 380 is one of those rare gems where the protagonist plays 5D chess while everyone else is still on checkers. Ji Ning has officially graduated from "survivor" to "hunter," and the way he dissects the Dongliu Twelve is *chef's kiss*. Notice how he doesn't just rely on raw power; he uses his divine sense like a forensic analyst, spots the leader's anomalous karma, and correctly deduces a joint formation threat. Then, he performs a flawless act, hiding his full strength and striking with surgical precision. The moment where he swaps his swords and the black dragon sword-qi erupts is pure catharsis. Keep an eye on that Goldfur Bear Demon King, though—his low karmic sin is a massive red flag suggesting he's either a hidden master with a code, or he's simply smart enough not to leave evidence. Either way, the real war is just beginning.
