## Chapter Overview

This chapter hits hard — both in stakes and in heart. After the wave of emotion (and carnage) from killing Shao Yanong, the dust settles, and Ji Ning is forced to stare down the terrifying political and military machine that is the Shao clan. His friends rally around him with advice, offers, even promises to join him on the run. But one person’s hesitation cuts deeper than any enemy blade. Ji Ning asks Nine Lotos the question he’s been building toward across dozens of chapters: *Will you walk this perilous road with me?* Her answer — or rather, her inability to answer — sends their relationship spiraling toward an irrevocable break. It’s a chapter of hard choices, fast travel, and a young cultivator learning that the hardest tribulations aren’t always fought with a sword.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning learns that the Shao clan possesses a “Retrocognition” secret art, meaning they can scry the past and identify him as the killer in moments — drastically shortening his window of safety.
- Yu Wei takes command of the crisis, laying out two broad strategic paths: flee to a faraway world (or the Yinglong Guard headquarters) or counter-attack by entering the Grand Xia’s imperial capital and joining a rival power via the Immortal Fate Convention.
- She also astutely advises him that killing his clan in a Grand Xia city is almost impossible due to the dynasty’s laws, effectively limiting any retaliation to assassins.
- Yu Wei’s deep familiarity with the top-tier political landscape of the Grand Xia hints at her true background far more overtly than before — a quiet but significant reveal for attentive readers.
- Nine Lotos proposes to take Ji Ning to her Ancestor for teleportation to another world, but this plan is superseded by the more critical personal choice.
- Mu Northson volunteers to go on the run with Ji Ning, but Ji Ning brutally — and lovingly — rejects him by flatly calling him “too weak.”
- Ji Ning, rallying his courage, directly asks Nine Lotos to become his Dao-companion and walk the chaotic road ahead together.
- Nine Lotos hesitates; she weighs her destiny as the heir of the Dongyan clan against her feelings for Ji Ning, and ultimately cannot cast off her obligations. She admits, “I cannot do it.”
- The farewell is devastating: Nine Lotos leaves in tears, Mu Northson makes an emotional vow of vengeance if Ji Ning dies, and Ji Ning stands alone in the hall.
- The chapter closes on a poignant note of resolve: Ji Ning declares he will walk the immortal path alone, but finds comfort in his two beast companions — Little Qing and Uncle White.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Weight of a Dao-Companion (道侣)**: In Xianxia culture, a Dao-companion isn’t merely a romantic partner or a spouse. The term describes a fellow cultivator whose Dao-heart and life-path are so intertwined that they would sacrifice everything — even immortality — for the other. Nine Lotos perfectly articulates the ideal when she quotes her Ancestor: “A Dao-companion is someone you would die for… someone you would slay Immortals and Buddhas to reclaim.” She then confesses she *cannot* live up to that ideal. This isn’t a petty breakup; it’s a philosophical admission of incompatible Dao-hearts. Nine Lotos’s identity as the *next leader of the Dongyan clan* is not just an excuse — it’s a root-level conflict of life paths. In the Xianxia framework, this is a legitimate and deeply tragic reason for parting, not a shallow plot device.

**The Shadow of the Grand Xia**: Yu Wei’s political briefing in this chapter reveals a critical layer of the Desolate Era setting: the Grand Xia Dynasty isn’t a monolithic state. It’s a turbulent ecosystem of ancient, blood-feuding clans (like the Shao clan and the Piao River clan) that have been slaughtering each other’s Celestial Immortals and leaders since the Primordial Fiendgod Era. The royal family enforces a fragile law within its capital cities, but outside of them? It’s a warzone of factions. This is the Xianxia logic of “state vs. sect” at its finest — the dynasty provides a safety net (city laws, the Yinglong Guard), but real survival depends on who you can ally with.

**The Immortal Fate Convention (仙缘大会)**: This is a classic Xianxia trope — a dynasty-wide tournament or gathering where young prodigies showcase their talent. For a cultivator like Ji Ning, who has already comprehended a “Domain of the Dao” (an ability usually only seen in Primal Daoists), the Convention is a golden opportunity to be headhunted by a top-tier faction. It’s the Xianxia equivalent of a career fair for the ultra-wealthy and superpowered.

## Reading Guide

Get ready to feel things, folks. This is the chapter where Ji Ning’s first great romance — such as it was — shatters. Nine Lotos’s hesitation and her tearful “I cannot do it” are not signs of a weak or shallow character. She is a woman carrying the weight of an entire clan on her shoulders, and her refusal is actually a sign of maturity and self-awareness. She knows she can’t be the Dao-companion Ji Ning needs. Meanwhile, Mu Northson’s raw, brotherly loyalty is the emotional counterbalance: the disciple who *does* have nothing to lose and is willing to throw it all away for his senior apprentice-brother. Notice how Ji Ning’s goodbye to Mu Northson is deliberately cruel (“You are too weak”) — it’s the tough love of a man protecting the people he cares about from his own doomed path. The final image of Ji Ning standing alone, then turning to his two beasts with a bittersweet smile, is a classic Xianxia hero beat: stripped of human support, he finds his anchor in blood-bound, loyal companions. The chapter sets up the next major arc beautifully — Ji Ning is no longer a disciple in a school; he’s a fugitive who must become a faction unto himself.
