## Chapter Overview
Nine years have passed, and Ji Ning’s power has grown beyond measure—yet the pace of his daily life masks a brewing storm. As the Ji Clan expands to fill the entire Yan Mountains, a single stubborn nail remains: the Snowdragon Mountain branch, holed up in Snowdragon City and refusing to budge. While Ji Jiuhuo and the elders deliberate nervously, Ji Ning responds the only way he knows how—with a single, uncompromising letter. This chapter is a classic Xianxia “show of force” pivot: the quiet before the decisive blade falls, reminding us that in cultivation, time is the ultimate multiplier of power.

## Key Plot Points
- The chapter opens with a serene scene of Ji Ning practicing calligraphy, which doubles as sword-training. Autumn Leaf observes that his sword-intent has undergone a qualitative transformation over the years.
- We learn that Ji Ning has been performing Yinglong Guard missions, the most notable being the slaying of the Realperson Venomous Dove—a dangerous evil cultivator capable of fleeing from Primal Daoists, killed by Ji Ning without issue.
- The Ji Clan’s expansion has forced nearly all tribes to relocate from the Yan Mountains. Only the Snowdragon Mountain branch remains entrenched, calculating that the Ji Clan dares not fight them due to Snowdragon Mountain’s backing.
- The elders visit Ji Ning for counsel. Ji Ning immediately writes a letter giving the Snowdragon Mountain branch ten days to leave the Yan Mountains, or else face his sword.
- That night, the letter is delivered to Xu Fang, the Xu clan overseer of Snowdragon City. The sheer sword-intent in Ji Ning’s calligraphy terrifies the room. Xu Fang rages and orders a report to the sect just as the letter self-destructs.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Calligraphy as Cultivation**: In Chinese tradition, calligraphy is a high art that trains both mind and spirit. Within Xianxia, it often serves as a deliberate Dao-tempering method—every stroke reflects the cultivator’s mastery and intent. Here, Ji Ning’s calligraphy is so sword-intent-laden that even seasoned Zifu cultivators feel their legs go weak. This isn’t just decoration; it’s a visible measure of his progress over the nine-year time skip.
- **The “Deliver an Ultimatum” Trope**: This is a classic Xianxia narrative beat—the protagonist, after years of growth, issues a deadline to an entrenched foe. The self-destructing letter adds a layer of theatrical intimidation; the message burns itself after being read, signaling that the warning is absolute and non-negotiable. The recipients’ roar to “report to the sect” mirrors the archetypal reaction of a minor antagonist who realizes they are outmatched.
- **Nine-Year Time Skip**: The condensed, montage-style narration emphasizes how seasonal cycles pass while Ji Ning’s inner drive remains static and focused. Years glide by in a paragraph, underscoring that for high-level cultivators, time becomes a resource—and even a decade can yield exponential power gains.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the sword-first subtleties**: The calligraphy scene isn’t filler. Autumn Leaf’s internal commentary—“even I feel my heart tremble” from a Xiantian-perfection sword-user—tells us Ji Ning’s edge hasn’t dulled; it’s sharpened to a terrifying degree. This quiet scene sets the stakes before the aggressive letter.
- **Notice the faction dynamics**: The Xu clan’s fear is palpable. They left only a token Zifu overseer after their Wanxiang Adept fled back to the sect. That one fear-driven calculation contrasts with Ji Ning’s absolute confidence—he isn’t bluffing, and the Xu clan knows it.
- **The unsaid backer**: Ji Ning’s confidence doesn’t come from raw power alone. He reminds us that behind him stand the Diancai Immortal, Northmont of Anchan, and his Black-White College credentials—a stark contrast to the Snowdragon branch’s fragile reliance on a distant mountain sect.
- **Cold fury dressed in quiet**: Ji Ning doesn’t rage or threaten dramatically. He just writes a few lines and burns the evidence. That’s the hallmark of a cultivator whose Dao-heart has matured—action without noise, severity without posturing. Buckle up for the next chapter’s confrontation.
