## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists! The banquet is over, and the hangover is real—not from the wine, but from the *drama*. This chapter is a pressure cooker of simmering interpersonal conflicts, broken wide open by a violent puppet raid. While Ji Ning and Nine Lotos are stewing in their first major relationship spat, a heist attempt shakes the Wujiang Immortal Estate to its core. And just when you think the action is the main course, the final whistle drops a lore bomb that changes everything: Shaoyan Nong’s sharp eyes catch Ji Ning using a movement technique that should be the exclusive property of a long-dead clan. Get your popcorn ready; the political game just got personal.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Aftermath of the Banquet**: The chapter opens with the Black-White College disciples trying to console a visibly frustrated Ji Ning. Cangjiang advises patience, while Yu Wei delivers a crucial perspective: true power comes from oneself, not from clan titles. A Celestial Immortal commands respect even from ancient dynasties.
- **The Ji Ning–Nine Lotos Rift**: The emotional core of the chapter. Both characters are nursing their grievances. Ji Ning feels Nine Lotos is being too accommodating to a hated rival; Nine Lotos feels Ji Ning is being unreasonable about what she sees as trivial social obligations. Their relationship hits a real snag for the first time.
- **The Puppet Raid**: A coordinated attack of Qiongqi puppets and a black-armored leader crashes the party. Their goal isn’t to kill the guests, but to occupy them while the main force tries to steal the critical ‘bronze lamp’ from Shaoyan Nong.
- **Xiangliu Fang’s Clone Ability**: The Primal Fiendgod bodyguard reveals a terrifying ability—he can separate his limbs into independent clones, allowing him to fight and pursue simultaneously. He effortlessly recaptures the bronze lamp, foiling the assassination-theft plot.
- **The Lore Bomb**: In the aftermath, Shaoyan Nong notices Ji Ning’s ‘Windwing Evasion’ movement technique, a divine ability that belonged to the Yuchi clan—a clan that was nearly exterminated. Xiangliu Fang confirms his suspicion: Ji Ning must be connected to the remnants.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The Weight of a ‘Title vs. True Power’**: Yu Wei’s advice is a classic Xianxia philosophy: titles like ‘Feathersouth God’ are temporary and political, but the power of a ‘Celestial Immortal’ is absolute and universal. In this genre, your cultivation realm is your true rank in the world; everything else is just a mask.
- **The Politics of a ‘Dao-companion’**: The rift between Ji Ning and Nine Lotos isn’t just a lovers’ quarrel; it’s a clash of worldviews. In Xianxia, finding a Dao-companion is like finding a partner for a lifelong cosmic journey. Compatibility isn’t just about love; it’s about whether your Dao-hearts and fundamental values can withstand the endless tribulations together. This is a big deal.
- **The Yuchi Clan & Windwing Evasion**: The ‘Windwing Evasion’ isn’t just a cool move—it’s a legacy. It was a signature divine ability of the Yuchi clan, a powerful force that was supposedly wiped out. For Shaoyan Nong to spot it on Ji Ning means Ji Ning carries a secret that could overturn the political landscape of the Grand Xia. In Xianxia, a lost clan’s technique is a red flag that screams “hidden inheritance” or “secret bloodline.”
- **Xiangliu Fang’s Clone**: The ability to separate a limb into a full-functioning clone is a classic display of a high-level Fiendgod Body Refiner’s mastery over his own flesh. It showcases the terrifying versatility of the Fiendgod path, where the body is not just a vessel but a weapon that can defy conventional physics.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in tension and worldbuilding. Watch how the author uses a simple fight scene to expose multiple layers of conflict: the surface-level class struggle (Ji Ning vs Shaoyan Nong), the intimate relationship drama (Ji Ning vs Nine Lotos), and the ancient, buried history (Ji Ning vs the Yuchi clan’s past). The key is that Ji Ning is completely unaware of the bomb that just dropped on him. The reader gets the pleasure of the dramatic irony, knowing that Shaoyan Nong now holds a dangerous piece of leverage. Buckle up; the Xianxia rulebook says that when a rival knows your secret, things are about to get *very* interesting.
