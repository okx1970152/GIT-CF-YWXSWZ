## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, buckle up! The calm before the storm is over, and the Shaoyin clan's deepest, darkest secret is about to be ripped out—literally. Ji Ning, with all the subtlety of a wrecking ball, has found an ancient copper pillar buried deep underground and is dead set on pulling it out, consequences be damned. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia siege logic: raw, unstoppable power versus the frantic, failed desperation of an entire clan's Celestial Immortal backbone. The real kicker? The massive, horned figure in the chapter's opening reveal is now confirmed as the Shaoyin clan's trump card—and he is not happy.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with a jaw-dropping power display: a single-horned humanoid being who casually commands a ten-thousand-zhang insectoid beast as a pet, and who is revered by two Celestial Immortals as their "Ancestor." This confirms the Shaoyin clan has an ultimate trump card backing them, which explains their refusal to bow even to the Zhuanshui clan.

Back in the fray, Ji Ning's relentless excavation of the fiery red copper pillar triggers a panic button across the entire Shaoyin clan. The five Celestial Immortals watching his battle instantly recognize the threat: this pillar is the seal for the mysterious "Endless Cupping Furnace," an artifact so vital it's considered a second pillar of the clan alongside their Celestial Immortals.

The Immortals abandon their siege on the Whitewater Hound and use a Greater Teleportation to directly intercept Ji Ning. A desperate tug-of-war ensues. Ji Ning, using his fourth-cycle Starseizing Hand and the raw power of his fifth-cycle Arcane Art, is simply too strong. He ignores their spells, their bindings, and their treasure attacks like a mountain ignoring a drizzle, focused only on pulling the pillar out. His sheer physical might overwhelms all five of them combined.

The chapter climaxes with a tangible sign of success: the fiery rock beneath Ji Ning's feet cracks with runic light, signaling the suppression formation is failing. Just as the Immortals are in utter despair, they sense another spatial fluctuation—their Ancestor has arrived.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The True Power of a Fiendgod Body Refiner**: This chapter is a textbook example of why the Proud Fiendgods are so terrifying. Ji Ning isn't using clever sword formations or complex spells here. He's using raw, brute strength. In the logic of Xianxia, a peak body refiner is an immovable object and an unstoppable force rolled into one. He lets five Celestial Immortals wail on him, and it's nothing but an itch. This "I can take everything you dish out and keep doing what I want" is the ultimate flex of a pure body refiner, far removed from a squishy mage.

**The "What Do I Fear?" Gambit**: Ji Ning's logic in this chapter is pure sword-heart pragmatism. "They are panicking and trying to stop me, so clearly I should do this thing even more." This is a staple of Xianxia strategy—reverse psychology applied to the Dao. Your enemy's desperate attempts to stop you are the best confirmation that you've found their weakness.

**Ancestors and Clout**: The single-horned being is the Shaoyin clan's "Ancestor." In Chinese clan society, "Ancestor" isn't just a title for an old guy; it's a rank of supreme, inherent authority. This being is the living foundation of the clan's power and prestige. His arrival is not just a power shift; it's the clan's highest authority figure personally stepping onto the battlefield, signaling the absolute highest state of emergency.
