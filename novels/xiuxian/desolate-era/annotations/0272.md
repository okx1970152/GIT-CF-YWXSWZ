## Chapter Overview

The final push to the last sub-palace of Wu Jiang’s immortal estate is underway—and Shao Yanong, who has been playing the gracious noble until now, finally drops the mask. After a tense standoff with the foreign Dao-soldiers, he unveils a jaw-dropping treasure: the Star-Swap Disk, a spatial artifact that swaps the positions of his entire party with a chunk of the enemy formation. One slick move and—bam—they’re inside. The foreign races can only scream in despair. Once inside the magnificent Wu Jiang Hall, Shao Yanong’s cold, calculating side fully surfaces; he starts eyeing Xue Hongyi’s Immortal-grade treasure and sends not-so-subtle warnings that the Zifu Disciples are expendable. The delicate balance of power among the group is about to shatter.

## Key Plot Points

- The group thoroughly searches the Daoist Scripture Hall but finds nothing. Shao Yanong suspects someone may have pocketed a hidden technique, but he keeps his cards close.
- The party flies toward the final golden pillar of light, where over twenty thousand foreign Dao-soldiers (Wave and Yaksha) are massed. The foreign leaders, Daoist Duohe and Daoist Wusui, propose a negotiation: let them go to the Grand Xia world in exchange for passage.
- Shao Yanong counters with an outrageous demand—all their Dao-soldier armor in exchange for freedom. Negotiations break down amid fury.
- Just as the Wave Dao-soldiers attack, Shao Yanong activates his **Star-Swap Disk**, a black-and-white disc that instantaneously swaps the positions of his shuttle with a portion of the Yaksha formation, opening a clear path into the sub-palace gate. The disc shatters after use.
- The foreign races are completely crushed; their last hope is gone. Duohe roars in rage; Wusui weeps silently.
- Inside the magnificent Wu Jiang Hall, Shao Yanong reveals he lost two of his three strongest treasures (Fixation Scroll and Star-Swap Disk), but considers the estate fair compensation. His thoughts turn to scheming—targeting Xue Hongyi’s treasure and the beautiful Immortal Yu Wei.
- Xiangliu Fang tests the hall wall, revealing three characters: “Wu Jiang Hall.” It’s confirmed as the main hall.
- Shao Yanong releases his stored Zifu Disciples to scout and warns everyone to stay close for safety.
- Cangjiang Realperson mentally notes to the others that Shao Yanong’s earlier courtesy was all a facade; his true domineering nature is now on full display.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Star-Swap Art (移星换斗)** is a classic high-level spatial technique in Chinese Xianxia lore, often described as “moving stars and swapping constellations.” In this chapter, its token form—the Star-Swap Disk—performs a targeted positional swap at close range. Note that the name evokes the legendary **Stellar Transposition** magic, typically associated with Celestial Immortals or greater beings who manipulate space-time itself. Shao Yanong’s disk can only swap two roughly equivalent volumes once before self-destructing; it’s a one-shot consumable treasure rather than a sustainable divine ability, underscoring its extreme rarity and the immense wealth of a clan like Shao.

**“Foreign race Dao-soldiers” (妖族道兵)** are not common demons; these are specifically **spirit-soldiers** formed from Dao-soldier armor sets, each set fusing the wearers’ power into a single monstrous entity (the Wave phantom or the Yaksha specter). The scale here—over twenty thousand—means they can form a giant that even a peak Xiantian Fiendgod like Xiangliu Fang cannot overcome alone. This highlights the military-industrial scale of a Celestial Immortal’s legacy.

## Reading Guide

If you’ve been watching Shao Yanong’s smooth diplomacy this whole time and thinking “this guy is too nice to be a real Xianxia noble,” congratulations—your spidey-sense was spot-on. As soon as the last gate opens, the mask comes off. His casual reference to losing his “two best treasures” is the kind of flex that makes the rest of the party (and readers) realize just how deep his pockets run. The moment he mentally notes Xue Hongyi’s treasure and Yu Wei’s beauty, the tension ratchets up—this estate is no longer just a dungeon crawl; it’s a powder keg.

Also, keep an eye on the Zifu Disciples. Shao Yanong calls them “explorers,” but we all know they’re sacrificial pawns. Ji Ning and his friends are quickly learning that in the world of immortals, a noble’s smile can be deadlier than a demon’s fangs.
