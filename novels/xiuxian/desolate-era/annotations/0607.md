## Chapter Overview

Hold onto your Dao-hearts, fellow travelers, because this chapter is a masterclass in heartbreaking Xianxia tragedy. Our boy Ji Ning makes his first real contact inside the World Prison, and it's not a gentle interrogation—it's a soul-crushing demolition of a being who has held onto a single hope for over three Chaos Cycles. Forget talking sense into him; Ji Ning accidentally breaks his world, and the fallout is spectacularly devastating.

Witness the sheer power disparity too: a lone Celestial Immortal, starved and broken, hits hard enough to force Ji Ning into using full fourth-stage Heartforce just to pin him down. If that doesn't scream "something is deeply wrong with this prison universe," nothing will. Brace for lore bombs, a spectacular mental breakdown, and a very juicy loot drop.

## Key Plot Points

Ji Ning confronts the young Celestial Immortal prisoner and delivers the cold truth: the Ninefang Chaosworld has fallen. This single sentence shatters a man who held on for three Chaos Cycles, his only anchors being his master and his princess. The result is an immediate, suicidal mental break. The young man, Liangqiu, unleashes a devastating emerald sword-aura composed of three hundred sixty Pure Yang supreme-grade flying swords—an attack that staggers Ji Ning's Starseizing Hand. Ji Ning barely suppresses him with fourth-stage Heartforce but cannot get any answers; Liangqiu refuses to explain his impossible power and commits suicide via self-detonation, his soul utterly scattered.

After the suicide, Ji Ning collects Liangqiu's possessions. The sheer wealth is staggering: two complete sets of three hundred sixty Pure Yang supreme-grade flying swords (emerald and fiery red), plus assorted other Pure Yang treasures. However, there are no cultivation manuals or records of the Ninefang Chaosworld—standard policy forbids carrying such things. Ji Ning realizes asking a higher-status prisoner is the only way forward, so he sets course for the wild-dog Elder God he sensed earlier.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Lore Bomb: The Celestial Immortal Anomaly**

For new readers: a Celestial Immortal should, by Three Realms standards, be like a deer fighting a lion against Ji Ning's current power. Yet this starved prisoner hits harder than most Pure Yang True Immortals. This is our first concrete hint that the Ninefang Chaosworld or the World Prison itself operates on a fundamentally different rulebook—perhaps the techniques or energy density there are simply on a different tier. It's a classic Xianxia "power curve spike" that signals an expansion of the world's scope.

The suicide as an act of defiance is deeply resonant in Xianxia culture. A cultivator's final, self-willed act—choosing to scatter their own soul—is both a tragic release and a final "screw you" to their captor. This is not a weak man dying; it's a defiant spirit refusing to be interrogated, denying even the shred of hope Ji Ning offered.

## Reading Guide

This is a worldbuilding-heavy, action-light chapter that tastes bittersweet. Beware the whiplash: we go from a brutal, one-sided emotional massacre to Ji Ning happily counting his new flying swords like a kid on Christmas morning. That's the Xianxia way—respect the dead, loot the corpse, move on to the next boss.

Keep an eye on the sheer scale of the haul. Two sets of three hundred sixty Pure Yang flying swords is a "generational wealth" level breakthrough for any cultivator, especially one who can command formations. Also note Ji Ning's immediate pivot: he correctly identifies that prisoners of higher status (the Elder God and Daofather) are his next target. His strategic coldness is on full display—mourning a dead man takes a backseat to getting answers.

Get ready for the wild-dog Elder God.  
That's not going to be a normal conversation.
