## Chapter Overview

This chapter shifts perspective from Ji Ning’s desperate survival in the void to the ripples his “death” sends through the outside world. The Xia Emperor, who watched the entire Karmic Hellfire incident through his Perfect Mirror, sends his Celestial Fox retainer to the Black-White College seeking confirmation. There, the Diancai Immortal, Uncle White, and the Fox piece together the full tragedy: Ji Ning was tricked into slaying billions of mortals, triggering the Hellfire, and then teleported Gods-know-where by the Shaoyin clan’s Realm-Suppressing Tower. Uncle White confirms Ji Ning survived the Hellfire but cannot return—his Greater Teleportation Dao Talisman is useless wherever he’s been sent. The Diancai Immortal seals the Starfire Hall containing Ji Ning’s Life Tablet, a silent testament to a vigil that stretches into months. Meanwhile, in the Realm of Annihilation, Ji Ning endures a monotonous half-year of drifting, his only solace the endless practice of archery until, finally, a colossal continent shimmers into view on the horizon.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Xia Emperor’s non-intervention and request for intelligence:** The Emperor watched the battle through the Perfect Mirror but remained neutral, torn between his ties to the Shaoyin clan and his goodwill toward Ji Ning.
- **The Celestial Fox delivers the news to the Diancai Immortal:** The Fox clarifies that the Emperor only learned of Ji Ning’s involvement *after* the fight, implying he could not have intervened in time.
- **Uncle White reveals the full scope of the tragedy:** Ji Ning was tricked into slaughtering billions, triggered Karmic Hellfire, survived, but has been missing for seven days—his Dao Talisman unusable.
- **The Realm-Suppressing Tower is named as the culprit:** The innate spirit treasure of the Shaoyin clan is confirmed to have teleported Ji Ning beyond the known universe.
- **The Diancai Immortal seals the Starfire Hall:** Guarding Ji Ning’s unbroken Life Tablet, he endures a silent, powerless vigil that stretches to three months.
- **The Shaoyin clan’s paranoia:** Having lost two Celestial Immortals to Ji Ning, they desperately try to confirm his death, while the college remains a sealed fortress.
- **Ji Ning’s archery training in the void:** Cut off from all Dao comprehension, he refines his *Houyi Archery Art*, pouring his Heartforce into endless practice.
- **Discovery of a mysterious continent:** After half a year of hopeless drifting, Ji Ning’s Candle Dragon Eye spots a vast landmass in the distance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Life Tablet (命简):** A soul-linked jade slip found in nearly every major clan and sect. It shatters the moment its owner dies, providing instantaneous confirmation. The Diancai Immortal’s vigil over Ji Ning’s intact tablet is a powerful narrative device—it assures the reader (and the college) that Ning is alive, even when he is beyond all communication.
- **Candle Dragon Eye (烛龙之眼 / Candle Dragon Light):** Based on the primordial deity Zhulong (the Torch Dragon) from the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*—a god whose closed eyes are night and open eyes are day. In Desolate Era, this divine ability allows the user to gather all ambient light into their pupils, granting vision across millions of li. It is an essential survival tool in the primordial darkness of the void.
- **The Role of a Celestial Immortal (天仙) in faction politics:** The Diancai Immortal’s authority to seal the Starfire Hall without objection demonstrates the immense power a Celestial Immortal wields within a Loose Immortal-run sect. He is the sole decision-maker, and his word is law.

## Reading Guide

This chapter acts as a beautiful *breather arc*—a moment to check in on the characters left behind while our protagonist drifts in nothingness. The parallel narratives serve two purposes: they ground the stakes in the real world (the Shaoyin clan plotting, the college honoring his memory) and reinforce Ji Ning’s isolation. The slow passage of time (from seven days to three months to half a year) is a masterful pacing choice, letting us *feel* the weight of his wait. And that final beat—the appearance of a continent—is a classic “light at the end of the tunnel” hook. Get ready, fellow Daoists… Ji Ning’s next trial is about to begin.

This is also a key chapter for *character voice*: the Diancai Immortal’s paternal rage (“like a child to me”), Uncle White’s grim pragmatism, and the Celestial Fox’s diplomatic neutrality all shine through.
