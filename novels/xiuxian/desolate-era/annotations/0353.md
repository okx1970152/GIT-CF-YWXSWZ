## Chapter Overview
If you've been riding the high of Ji Ning's Immortal Fortunes Assembly victory, this chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia humility. The top-ranked genius doesn't always get the Daofather's nod. Here, Daofather Crimsonbright, a cosmic-level being who could reshape reality, flips the script by choosing Mu Chuan Realperson—the cultivator who placed second and spent three centuries on his journey—over the flashy young monster Ji Ning and the formation prodigy Blackstone. It's a brutal, humbling lesson in what truly matters in the long, uncaring grind toward immortality: not talent, but the unwavering Dao-heart. The chapter isn't just about the selection; it's a political and philosophical chess match among True Immortals, with Lu Dongbin making a bold, frustrated bid for Ji Ning and settling for Yu Wei. Get ready for some serious cosmic power politics.

## Key Plot Points
- The chapter opens with Ji Ning, Blackstone, and Mu Chuan learning of Crimsonbright's shocking decision. The Daofather chose the oldest and least flashy of the top three.
- Through a flashback, we witness Daofather Crimsonbright, a being so powerful his presence stabilizes the void, reviewing the battle recordings. He praises Ji Ning's talent and Blackstone's formation skill, but his heart is captured by Mu Chuan's unshakeable, grounded Dao-heart.
- The Xia Emperor explains the Daofather's philosophy: ignore momentary brilliance; look at the vast future of a cultivator. Patience and a solid temperament are worth more than raw speed.
- Back in the Celestial Heaven Hall, the political fallout begins. Lu Dongbin, backed by other True Immortals, pressures the Xia Emperor to let him take Ji Ning as his disciple, leveraging his immense reputation and connections.
- The Xia Emperor firmly refuses, citing the Daofather's command to keep the promising young swordsman within the Crimsonbright lineage.
- Lu Dongbin, disappointed but pragmatic, shifts his target to Yu Wei, Ji Ning's Dao-companion, asking to take her as his disciple instead. The Xia Emperor, happy to smooth things over, agrees.
- The chapter ends with Lu Dongbin calling Yu Wei over and offering her a place as his disciple, leaving her stunned and the hall buzzing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Daofather's Choice: A Lesson in Xianxia Meritocracy**
This chapter is a brilliant illustration of the Xianxia adage: "The path of immortality is long, and momentary brilliance means nothing." Daofather Crimsonbright isn't looking for a fast sprinter; he's looking for a marathon runner. In real-world Daoist philosophy, the core of cultivation is 'Xiu' (修), meaning to cultivate, refine, and *slowly* perfect oneself. A cultivator who rushes is often building a castle on sand. Lu Dongbin, for all his sharp eyes, represents the allure of raw talent, while the Daofather embodies the patience of ancient, timeless power. This tension—between flash and substance—is a recurring theme in the genre. Mu Chuan's victory isn't a snub of Ji Ning's talent; it's a validation of the Dao-heart as the ultimate weapon.

**The Power of 'Face' (面子) and Factions**
The negotiation between Lu Dongbin and the Xia Emperor is a perfect Xianxia politics scene. Lu Dongbin doesn't just ask; he marshals his allies (the Northern Earth Immortal, the Fuhai God) to create social pressure—a classic 'give me face' (给个面子) maneuver. The Xia Emperor's refusal is equally political: he must obey his Daofather, but he also knows Lu Dongbin's favor is valuable. The final compromise—letting Lu Dongbin take Yu Wei—is a masterstroke of 'saving face' for everyone. It's a gentle reminder that in the Three Realms, power isn't just about how hard you can punch, but about the alliances you forge and the debts you collect.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a palate cleanser and a reality check. After the action-packed peak of the Immortal Fortunes Assembly, the story slows down to let the consequences breathe. Pay close attention to how each character reacts to the reward distribution: Ji Ning's quiet envy, Mu Chuan's humble calm, and Blackstone's pragmatic acceptance. This is the emotional core. Also, watch for the subtle power dynamics in the Celestial Heaven Hall. The way the True Immortals and Empyrean Gods talk among themselves—making requests, calling in favors, and ultimately submitting to the Daofather's whim—paints a vivid picture of the cosmic hierarchy. Ji Ning may be a monster genius, but he's still a small fish in an ocean of ancient, monstrous power. The chapter ends on a bittersweet but hopeful note for Ji Ning and Yu Wei, setting the stage for their parallel growth under different masters.

## Deeper Dive: The Dao of Patience vs. The Speed of Genius
Why did Crimsonbright choose the 'slow' cultivator? In Xianxia, a cultivator's foundation is everything. Mu Chuan's three hundred years of cultivation aren't a waste; they represent layer upon layer of condensed, purified, and perfected ki. He's like a slow-growing oak with deep roots, while Ji Ning is a fast-growing bamboo. In a storm, the bamboo might bend but survive, but the oak stands firm. The Daofather is playing the long game. He sees in Mu Chuan a cultivator who won't be shaken by external events, who won't be seduced by shortcuts, and who—when facing the ultimate tribulations—will have the unshakeable Dao-heart to endure. This is a classic debate in the genre: is it better to be a peerless genius who must constantly conquer your own arrogance, or a plodding, steady cultivator who is nearly impossible to derail? Crimsonbright placed his bet on the latter.
