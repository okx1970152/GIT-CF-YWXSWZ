## Chapter Overview
This chapter is the quintessential Xianxia "indestructible cockroach" moment—but with a divine cheat code. Ji Ning, protected by the endlessly flowing Su River, turns himself into the ultimate siege breaker: an indestructible, golden-light-wreathed, six-armed juggernaut that everyone can hit but nobody can hurt. Every faction throws their best shot: the Ba Serpent wraps him up, magic treasures rain down like a cosmic hailstorm, and a Three-Tailed Thunder God Puppet tries to flee and tattle. But Ji Ning has reached that glorious realm where "mortal siege" becomes little more than a nuisance. The real story, however, shifts from the battlefield to the war room, where a silver-armored Celestial Immortal general—with the calculating patience of a veteran who survived his Tribulations—decides whether to burn his trump cards or cut his losses. The chapter ends with a mountain-rattling, rune-glowing killing formation powering up, setting the stage for a literal trial by fire.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Great Siege of Ning Ji**: The D-9 squad of the Bloodcloud Hall, along with the Eastwood Forest Sect's grand formation, attack Ji Ning with everything they've got—including the massive Ba Serpent and a volley of Immortal-ranked treasures. They fail. Miserably.
2. **The Puppet’s Flight**: The Three-Tailed Thunder God Puppet, piloted by Han Da, tries to escape to inform his master. Ji Ning deploys the "Nine Heavens Annihilating Wind" divine ability—a sign of his growing versatility—and cuts the puppet down mid-flight.
3. **The Han Da Report**: Han Da escapes the mist and reports to his master, the silver-armored Celestial Immortal general. His message is blunt: "Ning Ji is like a rotten bone. Unbreakable."
4. **The General’s Calculus**: We get a masterclass in Xianxia survival philosophy. The general refuses to personally attack Ning Ji, arguing that a Celestial Immortal lives long by knowing *when not to fight*. He views the assassination mission as a low-reward, high-risk job—not worth the possibility of angering a Daofather-level patron behind Ning Ji.
5. **The Nine Heavens Extinguishing Poisonfire Furnace**: As a “favor” to the D-9 squad, the general orders nine Devil Guards to activate the region's most powerful killing formation. Nine runic mountain peaks rise into the sky, radiating a pressure that terrifies even the general's own disciple.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The "Immortal's Hedonism" vs "Immortal's Ambition"**: The silver-armored general's speech about not wanting to die unjustly is a brilliant example of a recurring Xianxia archetype—the *wizened survivor*. Unlike the hot-headed young masters who charge into battle for glory, veteran Celestial Immortals like him have survived their Three Calamities and Nine Tribulations by learning one critical lesson: *always calculate your risk-to-reward ratio*. He explicitly states he came to the Grand Xia world to "enjoy himself," not to die for a pittance of three Immortal-ranked treasures. This is a classic reframing of immortality: not as a quest for ultimate power, but as the ultimate hedonistic retirement plan. The best way to live forever is to never fight a war you don't have to.

**The "Gear Check" Trope**: Ji Ning's golden light defense rendering every attack useless is a textbook example of the "gear check" in Progression Fantasy. The narrative isn't saying Ji Ning is invincible; it's saying that his treasure tier is so astronomically higher than his attackers' that their "math" simply doesn't compute. His attackers are throwing Wanxiang-level and Earth Immortal-level attacks, but Ji Ning's defense has been calibrated to withstand attacks from True Immortals. This is less a fight and more of a "your weapon is obsolete" notification.

**The Immortal Fortunes Assembly Legacy**: It's explicitly noted that Ji Ning won first place in the Immortal Fortunes Assembly over thirty years ago. This is a key Xianxia social landmark: winning such a tournament doesn't just give you a trophy; it makes you a "marked man" whose reputation forces higher-tier powers to calculate their moves around you. The general's hesitation to attack is a direct consequence of the fame (and implied connections) Ji Ning earned there.

## Reading Guide
The chapter is split into two distinct flavors: the noisy, chaotic siege (where Ning is basically a raid boss) and the cold, quiet calculus of the general's war room. The siege scene is pure power fantasy—watch Ning effortlessly swat away a panicking puppet while the enemy squad frantically admits they can't even scratch him. The real meat, however, is in the general's dialogue. His refusal to personally intervene is a masterful piece of worldbuilding: it shows that Xianxia has a "food chain," and that intelligence and risk assessment are just as important as raw combat power. The final shot of the nine runic mountain peaks rising is a classic Xianxia "tension ramp"—it's the calm before the storm. The general is sending a message to the D-9 squad: "I've done my part. If this doesn't kill him, you're on your own." How will Ji Ning counter an attack that's designed to kill top-tier Celestial Immortals? The answer likely lies in the "jade water" of the Su River.
