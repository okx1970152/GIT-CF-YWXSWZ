## Chapter Overview

Hold onto your beast-head cups, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a masterclass in political maneuvering wrapped in a father’s cold, fierce love. Fresh off his one-versus-seven Golden Sword massacre, ten-year-old Ji Ning isn’t just a prodigy anymore—he’s *the* designated heir of the Ji Clan’s Western Prefecture. But this chapter doesn’t rest on the laurels of that victory. Instead, it tightens the screws and loads up the parting gifts. We see the grizzled old tiger Ji Lie rage, submit, and storm out in a huff, his pride carefully dissected. Then, under the cover of feast and ritual, Ji Ning’s parents play their final, calculated moves: securing the clan’s most precious heirloom, the one-shot teleportation talisman, and handing down a secret family wind art. The adventure is about to begin, and the safety net is being woven from steel and sacrifice.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Aftermath of Conquest**: The chapter opens with Ji Lie’s raw, unfiltered reaction. He’s convinced by Ji Ning’s overwhelming talent but remains bitterly defiant toward his brother, the Prefectural Lord Ji Youyang. This establishes the internal power dynamic: respect for strength, resentment for perceived weakness. Ji Ning’s victory has effectively cemented his succession without a single political battle.
- **The Feast and the Ten Stewards**: The scene shifts to a grand banquet in the Drifting Snow Hall. This isn't just celebration; it’s a display of the Western Prefecture’s power structure. The thirty barefoot dancers, the musicians, the beast wine—all of it is the backdrop for a critical succession timeline. Ji Youyang sets the date: Ji Ning will become Prefectural Lord at age twenty.
- **The Heirloom Gambit**: Ji Yichuan drops the bomb. He requests the clan’s one remaining "Ten-Thousand-Mile Trace-Away Talisman" for his son. The hall goes silent. This is the ultimate safety net—instant teleportation within ten thousand li. This single request elevates the stakes from a local training trip to a full-blown expedition that requires a nuclear option for survival.
- **The Father’s Warning and the Jade Sword**: In the quiet walk home, Ji Yichuan explains the true purpose of winning the Golden Sword: it gave him the political capital to claim the talisman. He then hand Ji Ning a blood-bonded jade sword. If Ji Ning breaks it, no matter the distance, Ji Yichuan will teleport to him instantly. He also lays down a ruthless command: stray beyond ten thousand li, and it’s a three-year dungeon sentence. This is tribal-family discipline with an iron fist.
- **The Mother’s Tears and the Windwing Evasion Manual**: On the morning of departure, Yuchi Snow layers Ji Ning with homemade clothes and, more importantly, a golden book—the "Windwing Evasion." This is a secret immortal-demon movement technique from her own clan, forbidden from being taught to anyone in the Ji Clan. It is her ultimate personal gift, a piece of her heritage for his survival.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a textbook example of how power and emotion intersect in a xianxia clan setting. The power structure here is crucial to understand. The **Ten Stewards (十大执事)** aren't just advisors; they’re the executives of the Western Prefecture, each holding a vote on vital matters. When Ji Ning takes over, the weakest among them will be forcibly removed, showing how talent and bloodline directly reshuffle political power in this feudal system. This is why Ji Yichuan gambled on the Golden Sword—without that title, Ji Ning would have no claim to the heirloom treasure.

Now, let's talk about the **Ten-Thousand-Mile Trace-Away Talisman (万里无踪符)**. This isn't a common teleportation spell. In the xianxia hierarchy, this is a **Dao talisman (道符)**, a high-tier magical artifact inscribed with the profound mysteries of the Heavenly Dao. It is a consumable, one-use item so rare that the clan considers it a miracle to have stumbled upon three centuries ago. Its extreme power comes with extreme limitations—like being capped at ten thousand li, which might seem vast for mortals but is a small bubble for Xiantian-level threats.

Finally, note the **Windwing Evasion (风翼遁法)**. This is a top-tier movement skill from Yuchi Snow’s bloodline. In cultivation fiction, "forbidden arts" passed down through maternal lines are treasures because they represent a unique inheritance outside the clan’s core library. The fact that Yuchi Snow is giving this to Ji Ning as a "read after Xiantian" manual shows this will be a game-changer when he needs to outrun or outmaneuver creatures that hunt across huge distances.

## Reading Guide

Get your spicy popcorn ready, because the formula for the next arc is now loaded and primed. Think of this chapter as the calm before the storm. The "nuclear button" has been installed, the inheritance has been secured, and our young protagonist is about to step out of the city walls for the first time as a true cultivator. The emotional weight is real—especially the mother's anxious farewell and the father’s gruff, prison-threat version of "I love you." The story has just handed Ji Ning all the tools a young hero could ask for: the raw power of the Crimsonbright Diagram, the tactical sharpness of a divided mind, and now, a divine escape plan. But xianxia is cruel. The question isn't *if* Ji Ning will have to snap that jade sword, but *when*. The shadow of the Winged Serpent and the politics of Snowdragon Mountain still loom in the background. This is the quiet before the bloodshed. Enjoy the warmth, because the Yan Mountains are calling, and they’re hungry.
