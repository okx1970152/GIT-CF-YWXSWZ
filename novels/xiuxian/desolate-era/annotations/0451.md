## Chapter Overview
The five Celestial Immortals of the Shaoyin clan are stuck. Ji Ning, having pushed through to the fifth stage of the *Profound Arcane Art and the fourth revolution of the Starseizing Hand, has turned the tables completely. The very formation designed to trap him is now a cage for them. They can’t break out, and they can’t admit defeat. As the Shaoyin clan stews in humiliation, Ji Ning calmly decides to switch strategies—if he can’t shatter their turtle shell in one go, he’ll outlast them. And while the enemy is paralyzed, he takes a moment to investigate a peculiar red copper pillar he sensed deep underground… and what he finds is *way* bigger than he expected.

## Key Plot Points
1. Celestial Immortals report a crisis via spirit-beast.
   - Fathomhearted contacts Ancestor Xuanji, as the five CIs realize they can’t suppress Ji Ning anymore.
2. Ji Ning assesses the stalemate aloud.
   - He admits killing a Celestial Immortal is extremely hard even with his power boost.
   - The Shaoyin clan’s pride prevents retreat; they must risk a gamble or let him stay forever.
3. A strategic pivot.
   - Ji Ning resolves to use a sustained harassment tactic—keeping the CIs on edge for months—until the Shaoyin clan makes a mistake.
4. Exploration of the underground copper pillar.
   - The pillar is 99 zhang thick and over 3000 li long, embedded in a mysterious crimson rock wall.
   - Neither divine sense nor elemental energy can refine it.
   - Ji Ning’s full-strength stomp *and* a Starseizing Hand palm strike fail to damage the rock.
5. The copper pillar *moves*.
   - Using heavenly transformation + fourth-revolution Starseizing Hand, Ji Ning pries it upward a bit.
   - The groaning sound reaches another world, awakening a terrifying being.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The "Endless Cupping Furnace" setup.** The author introduces an ancient artifact buried deep under the Shaoyin ancestral domain. In Xianxia, such objects are often fragments of a primordial battle or part of an even larger hidden mechanism. The fact it is called an “endless furnace” and that its disturbance woke a distant, scale-covered figure with cyan flames suggests this is a **cross-world inheritance or prison**. The copper pillar may be the furnace’s core or a seal spike—and Ji Ning just yanked on it.

**Celestial Immortal siege mechanics.** A group of five Celestial Immortals working in perfect defensive coordination is nearly impossible for a single cultivator to crack head-on, even with the upper hand. This demonstrates the genre convention of **strategic attrition**: forcing the opponent to overextend or starve rather than trying to one-shot the formation. Ji Ning’s “outlast them” plan is a classic Xianxia counter when raw power isn’t enough.

**Friction between power levels.** The battle here shows a practical truth of the cultivation ladder: raw offensive power may exceed an opponent’s, but finishing kills at higher tiers is hard because everyone has escapes, formations, and layered defenses. The chapter nicely captures the difference between “winning a clash” and “winning a war.”

## Reading Guide
Fellow Daoists, get ready—the war of attrition has begun, and the *real* hornet’s nest is about to be kicked! This chapter slows down the action just enough for you to feel the tension tightening around the Shaoyin clan. Ji Ning is playing the long game, and that’s a terrifying prospect for any ancient clan. But the buried treasure hook is what steals the spotlight. A copper pillar deeper than a mountain range, stone that laughs at Pure Yang-grade palms, and a distant, enraged creature feeling the vibration… It’s the classic Xianxia “you just poked the slumbering dragon” trope, and you’ll *love* watching the fallout land.

## What the Hell Is the Crimson Copper Cavern?
The moment Ji Ning discovers the fiery red cliff-face, the author nods to an old fantasy staple: the **forbidden sealed relic**. In many Eastern myths, ancient artifacts or defeated immortals are bound deep beneath the earth using pillars and chains. The “crimson copper” here resists all known forms of probing—divine sense and elemental energy—marking it as something beyond the Three Realms’ normal Dao. The mysterious figure who awakens at the end is likely either the artifact’s owner, its guardian, or an imprisoned monster. Either way, Ji Ning just pulled the trigger on a subplot that will echo for volumes to come.
