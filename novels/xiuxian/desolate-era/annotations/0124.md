## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, let’s get ready for the true final phase of the Battle of Oxhorn Mountain! Last chapter, Ji Ning pulled off a miracle by wiping out Tongyu and his squad of Zifu Disciples. But the celebration is cut short as a *True* Immortal, Xuli of Snowdragon Mountain, arrives with a new team and a nasty formation-breaking specialist named Nong Zidao.

This chapter is a brilliant study in pressure. It’s not about raw brawling (yet). It’s a tense, tactical duel between the Ji Clan’s last hope—Ji Ning—and an enemy methodically peeling away their only defense. Watch as Nong Zidao uses pure Formation Dao knowledge to dismantle the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation piece by piece, forcing the Ji Clan from the shadows. More importantly, witness the psychological war: Nong Zidao’s clinical precision versus Ji Ning’s growing, desperate, and utterly cold resolve. The stage is set for a clash that will define the clan’s survival. Buckle up!

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Nong Zidao, the formation expert from the Whitewater Sect, taking charge. He and True Immortal Xuli, along with seven other Zifu Disciples, have entered the Blackflame Dragon Prison Formation and immediately formed a simplified ‘Jiaolong Formation’ (a protective phantom flood dragon). Nong Zidao proves his worth instantly, using a geometric pattern of craters to block earth veins and dispel the obscuring mist of the Dragon Claw sub-formation in mere minutes. He confidently estimates each of the remaining four sub-formations will take only half an hour.

This sends a wave of panic through the Ji Clan’s leaders. Their entire defensive strategy was built on the formation’s mist, giving them the ability to ambush and retreat from anywhere. Without it, they are cornered. Patriarch Ji Jiuhuo makes the painful but obvious call: only Ji Ning can stop them. They cannot wait.

In a moment of quiet preparation, Ji Ning tries to push his sword formation to its limits. With his new influx of graded flying swords from the slain enemies, he finds he cannot control the ninth layer of the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation. He settles on a very strained eighth layer, which is still more powerful than the ninth layer he used before. He accepts this limitation and prepares for the kill.

The chapter’s climax is the perfect set-piece. Ji Ning activates his Rainwater Sword Domain, the tell-tale sign of his imminent attack. Xuli counteracts with a more refined technique, using a manifestation of a moon and constellations to force back the rain, revealing how he is no mere brute but a powerful cultivator with his own Dao insights. Unfazed, Ji Ning abandons stealth and launches a full-on assault, using the Windwing Evasion Divine Ability to accelerate to a shocking speed, manifesting as a fiery Roc. He blitzes past the protective flood dragon’s tail strike, appearing right before a stunned True Immortal Xuli. The final line, “A streak of sword-light blazed!” signals the start of the decisive, high-speed duel.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Formation Hierarchy and Naming:** This chapter showcases a classic Xianxia concept of technique lineage. Snowdragon Mountain has a supreme art, the ‘Snowdragon Soaring Heaven Formation’, which is a total-war-level tactic deployable by an army of cultivators. Only *that* pinnacle formation earns the prestigious ‘Snowdragon’ name. Its simplified, smaller-scale versions are all demoted to ‘Jiaolong’ (Flood Dragon). This naming convention isn’t just decoration; it tells the reader the power scale immediately. A ‘Jiaolong Formation’ is dangerous, but it implies that facing the true ‘Snowdragon’ is a death sentence reserved for Immortals.

**Dual Cultivation Systems in Tandem:** Notice Ji Ning’s attack. He is a Fiendgod Body Refiner (physical), but he supplements his assault with the Water-Fire Lotus (a technique born from his Dao comprehension) and the Windwing Evasion (a Divine Ability). This perfectly illustrates the *Desolate Era* power system. A pure Ki Refiner might rely purely on their magic treasures and spells. A pure Fiendgod relies on brute strength and regeneration. Ji Ning, however, leverages *all* his skills. His raw physical power grants him speed and toughness, while his Dao insights and Divine Ability provide the devastating finishing move. This hybrid approach is what makes him a monstrous talent.

**The Meaning of ‘Dao Domain’:** Xuli references a concept called ‘Dao Domain’ (道之域境). This is a step beyond the ‘True Meaning of the Dao’ that Ji Ning achieved with the Dripping Sutra. Imagine the ‘True Meaning’ is understanding the physics of water; the ‘Dao Domain’ is the ability to impose that physics upon reality. When Xuli’s Moon-and-Constellation technique pushes back Ji Ning’s Rain, it shows they are playing a game of metaphysical territory. One Dao is trying to overwrite the other, and in this first exchange, Xuli—with his higher cultivation base—wins the zone control. It sets up a classic underdog dynamic: Ji Ning has to break through Xuli’s absolute defense using overwhelming, focused physical speed rather than a domain war.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is all about the **poker game** before the all-in. Pay close attention to the pacing. The author spends a good portion of the chapter on Nong Zidao calmly, patiently dismantling the formation. This builds a sense of dread in the reader that mirrors the Ji Clan’s own panic. The enemy isn’t just strong; they are *organized* and *methodical*. This is not a monster to be fought; it’s an army to be routed.

The key character beat here is **Ji Ning’s evolution into a leader**. He is no longer a child genius proving himself in a cage match. He looks at the desperate faces of his elders—his father, the Patriarch, Granny Shadow—and understands the weight of being the clan’s only weapon. His final, silent thought, “All of you must die!” is not angry bluster. It is a cold, clear, calculated decision born from the pressure of responsibility. He knows exactly what will happen if he fails, and that fear is transformed into pure, single-minded murder.

Watch for the contrast in combat philosophy. Xuli relies on a defensive, group-based formation (the Jiaolong) and an overwhelming realm advantage (his Moon Domain). Ji Ning abandons all that and bets everything on a single, explosive, high-speed assassination. It’s the classic David vs. Goliath setup, but with a Xianxia twist: David has spent his whole life sharpening a single, specialized God-killing tool.
