## Chapter Overview
Welcome to Mount Yujue Wasteland, fellow Daoists—the final obstacle on the path to becoming an elite Yinglong Guard! If you thought the Black-White College trials were tough, this chapter cranks the survival difficulty to eleven. Our boys Ji Ning and Mu Northson arrive via teleportation array, get a brutally pragmatic orientation from two old-timers (who've clearly seen countless promising youngsters die out there), and immediately start strategizing. The wasteland is a massive, monster-infested death trap where fractured space can literally eject you from the universe, and your sense of direction is useless. But Ji Ning, being Ji Ning, has a secret weapon up his sleeve—one that makes the entire trial almost laughably easy. Get ready for a masterclass in Xianxia-style problem-solving: when your divine sense is OP, everything else is just details.

## Key Plot Points
- The cultivators are transported two million li in an instant to a teleportation array within Mount Yujue Wasteland, the trial grounds for the Yinglong Guard.
- The plump gray-haired elder lays down **four life-or-death rules**: no returning to the safe zone for three months, never stay in one place too long (monsters will gang up on you), watch for spatial fractures that can kill you instantly, and use the sun/stars for navigation—not your instincts.
- After the briefing, the hundred candidates scatter in all directions. Ji Ning and Northson choose a northern route via their dragon-headed warship, keeping it small to avoid attracting attention.
- From the clouds, they spot a massive monster mountain occupied by an army-like horde. Northson identifies the classic dilemma: guessing the ruler's power from a distance is impossible, and a Primal-level monster would be a party-wipe.
- Northson proposes three strategies: tail other cultivators as bait, target weak-looking monster mountains, or set a counter-ambush for cultivator-bandits. Ji Ning listens patiently, then unveils his ace.
- **Ji Ning reveals his divine sense**—the soul-level perception ability normally locked behind the Primal Daoist stage. Northson is shocked and immediately realizes the implications: with a divine-sense scout, they can safely identify Wanxiang-level targets from range and bail the instant they sense a Primal monster.
- Northson, riding the hype, openly wonders if Ji Ning is a reincarnated Immortal. Ji Ning dodges the question with a smile—his past life is complicated, but not in the way Northson thinks.
- The chapter closes with Ji Ning sweeping his divine sense over a promising-looking monster mountain, ready to kick off the hunt.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Yinglong Guard (应龙卫)**: This isn't just any elite corps—it's the Grand Xia Dynasty's immortal military arm. Named after Yinglong, a winged dragon deity from ancient Chinese mythology who served as a rain god and assisted the Yellow Emperor in battle, the Guard represents the pinnacle of state-sanctioned martial power. Becoming a member means access to massive Dao repositories and treasures—essentially a VIP pass to accelerated cultivation. The trial is brutal by design: only the best deserve that kind of backing.

**Mount Yujue Wasteland (狱山大荒泽)**: Notice the character "狱" (prison/jail) in the name. This is the Xianxia trope of a "testing ground as a natural prison"—a lawless, monster-filled expanse where the environment itself is an adversary. The space fractures are a callback to the "fragile world" trope common in Chinese cultivation fiction: higher-level combatants and unstable realms literally break the fabric of reality, and mundane travelers can stumble into voids that lead to instant death.

**Divine Sense (神念)**: In most cultivation novels, the ability to project one's spiritual perception externally—like a radar or omniscient eye—is locked behind the Primal Daoist stage. Ji Ning achieving this at the Zifu level is an absurd anomaly. In Xianxia lore, this kind of "impossible early mastery" is a classic sign of a reincarnated Immortal or a monstrous talent. The other characters' shock is fully justified: it's like a middle schooler solving a quantum physics equation that stumps PhDs.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the power dynamic shift**: When Northson learns about Ji Ning's divine sense, his reaction is pure gold. It's the moment the junior brother realizes just how ridiculously far behind he's lagging—and exactly whose coattails he should be riding for this trial. The "reincarnated Immortal" tease is a great little hook for readers.
- **The four rules are survival gold**: Don't skip the orientation scene. Those four rules aren't just flavor—they're a perfect summary of how Mount Yujue Wasteland works. Every monster-hunting strategy Ji Ning and Northson discuss is built on these constraints. The "never stay in one place" rule is particularly clever, forcing mobile hit-and-run tactics rather than siege warfare.
- **Ji Ning's pragmatism on full display**: He doesn't show off his divine sense until it's tactically useful. Notice how he lets Northson brainstorm first, praising his ideas, before revealing his own much simpler solution. It's not about ego—it's about training his junior brother to think independently, then dropping the hammer when the timing is right.
- **Self-awareness check**: Ji Ning's internal reflection—"My past life was definitely not an Immortal"—is a quiet, humanizing moment. Even with all his monstrous talent, he doesn't delude himself. That groundedness is what makes his Dao-heart so terrifyingly stable.
