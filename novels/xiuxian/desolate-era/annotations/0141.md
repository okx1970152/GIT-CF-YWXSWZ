## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, buckle up—our young sword-wielding protagonist Ji Ning has finally made it to the big city! After months of delay, he arrives at the legendary Anchan City, the bustling, ancient heart of the Anchan Commandery. This chapter serves as a vibrant tour of a Xianxia metropolis, where the rules of the road are as strict as they are deadly, and a single token can open doors to unimaginable power and luxury. Ji Ning explores the city's districts, visits the chaotic Treasure Exchange Square, and finally reunites with his influential friend, Northmont Baiwei, at the awe-inspiring Northmont Blacktiger Estate. It's a world-building feast that sets the stage for the next major arc of his journey.

## Key Plot Points
- The party lands outside Anchan City, noting its absolute flight ban (unless you're a Primal Daoist, a loophole Meng Jun embarrassingly discovers).
- Ji Ning pays the 2-jin Thunder Gold entry fee for himself and Whitewater Hound, receiving the official city guidebook.
- The gang reads up: Anchan City is 9,321 li by 8,910 li, split into East, South, North, and West districts, with the Marquis of Anchan's estate as the central forbidden zone.
- East, South, and North Cities are safe havens where killing is banned and cultivators and merchants live in peace.
- West City is the lawless, bustling zone where only streets are safe—estates and sect branches are open for any kind of mayhem.
- The trio and Ji Ning visit the Treasure Exchange Square, a massive, chaotic bazaar of ten thousand cultivators where murder for treasures is a real (if rare) risk.
- They bump into the trio's "Third Uncle" Ming, a bald, well-connected local who offers to show the Meng siblings around, but Ji Ning politely declines to go along.
- Meng Jun's shallow, dismissive attitude toward Ji Ning becomes obvious, cementing his role as a minor, grating character.
- Ji Ning wanders the city alone until sunset, then heads straight to the Northmont Blacktiger Estate.
- The estate's sheer scale—ten li square with a dominating black tiger statue—is as intimidating as its reputation.
- Ji Ning flashes his Northmont Blacktiger token (a gift from Northmont Baiwei), and the guards immediately become deferential.
- Northmont Baiwei himself bursts out, greets Ji Ning like a long-lost brother, and pulls him into a flaming carriage, ready to play the perfect host.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The City as a Living Formation**: The description of Anchan City as a single, "Immortal-tier formation" is a staple of Xianxia world-building. In Chinese mythology and Daoist cosmology, giant defensive arrays that protect entire cities are a common trope—a supernatural blend of architecture, geomancy (Feng Shui), and mystical rune-craft. The 'oppressive aura' Ji Ning feels isn't just flavor; it's the palpable presence of the formation's power, a constant reminder that the Marquis of Anchan's authority is backed by absolute, overwhelming force. Think of it as a nuke-shaped deterrent that's always humming beneath your feet.

**Thunder Gold (雷金) as a Universal Currency**: Thunder Gold isn't just pretty metal; it's a high-density, energy-rich substance that serves as the standard currency for cultivators across the Grand Xia Dynasty. Its name hints at its origin—likely mined from regions ravaged by lightning tribulations or infused with violent heavenly power. For mortals, it's a fortune; for Xiantian lifeforms, it's pocket change. This system elegantly separates the immortal and mortal economies, ensuring that powerful cultivators always have a practical medium of exchange.

**The Lawless West City vs. the Safe Zones**: This division mirrors a very real tension in ancient Chinese urban planning and social control. The East, South, and North Cities act like inner sanctums of imperial law, a place where the state's monopoly on violence is absolute. The West City, by contrast, is akin to a tolerated "wild west" within the walls—a place for the powerful to settle scores, do business with plausible deniability, and flaunt their influence. The rule "only streets are safe" is a brilliant Xianxia twist; it makes public order a fragile, stage-managed illusion, while the true struggle for power happens behind closed doors (or, in this case, estate walls).

**The "Face" and Status of the Northmont Blacktiger Estate**: The sheer size of the Northmont Blacktiger Estate (ten li square) in a city where land is worth its weight in Thunder Gold is a direct display of **'face' (面子, mianzi)** —a core concept in Chinese social dynamics. This isn't mere wealth; it's a public statement of power, prestige, and the favor of the ruling Marquis of Anchan. The giant, dominant black tiger statue isn't just decoration; it's a totem of intimidation, a visual curse telling every passerby exactly who is in charge on this block.

## Reading Guide
Welcome to the urban jungle, Xianxia-style! This chapter is a masterclass in efficient world-building. The way I Eat Tomatoes (IET) drops the city's rulebook into our hands alongside Ji Ning is a treat for lore lovers. Get ready to see Anchan City not just as a setting, but as a character in its own right—a breathing, dangerous, and magnificent entity. Pay close attention to the contrast between the orderly, peaceful Outer Districts and the vibrant, cutthroat West City. This distinction sets the stage for all the political intrigue, resource-gathering, and future conflicts that Ji Ning will navigate.

Also, watch for the subtle character beats. Meng Jun's immediate shift from fawning to cold dismissal the moment he feels Ji Ning has no more immediate value is a sharp, classic Xianxia villain-in-the-making moment. He's a walking, talking *courting death* flag. In contrast, Northmont Baiwei's enthusiastic, brotherly welcome—"you have come to your own home"—is a breath of fresh air. His power is genuine, his influence is absolute within these walls, and his friendship with Ji Ning feels real and generous. This chapter is where Ji Ning transitions from a wandering wilderness warrior to a player in a much grander political game. Enjoy the ride, fellow Daoists—the real adventure is just beginning!
