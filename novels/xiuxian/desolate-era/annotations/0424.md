## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, it's time for a classic Xianxia segment: gear shopping! But this isn't just any shopping trip—this is Ji Ning making the most high-stakes, strategic loot selection of his life (so far). After the epic haul from the War God Hall, Ning is faced with the deliciously painful choice of what supreme-grade Immortal-ranked flying swords to build his main combat loadout around. He's gunning for a formation that uses *hundreds* of swords, which, as any cultivator knows, is both a flex and a necessity when you're planning to face down the Heavenly Tribulation and a looming war. The chapter delivers that wonderful mix of calculated assessment, power fantasy, and the quiet, intense prep work that makes a protagonist's rise feel earned. Plus, we get a nice pivot back to the investigative plotline as Ning begins his stakeout of the mysterious East Forest Sect.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning browses the aquatic palace’s scripture, searching for a set of flying swords that can support his Grand Thousand Sword Formation, which requires up to 729 swords.
- He considers that his Ki Refining (via his Primaltwin) should undergo the Heavenly Tribulation first, before his Fiendgod true body, following advice from senior fellow disciples at Mount Innerheart.
- Ning settles on the **Yin-Yang Twin Polarity Thousand Slaughter Formation**, a set of 1,000 supreme-grade Immortal-ranked flying swords (500 Yin ‘One Qi Frost Ice Swords’ and 500 Yang ‘Qiangang Blazing Fire Swords’).
- He discovers the formation was originally designed for a pair of Celestial Immortal Dao-Companions, and that controlling all 1,000 swords simultaneously is exceptionally rare even among Immortals.
- Back at the Hall of Stars, Ning spends three solid days imprinting the complex Grand Thousand Sigil onto 729 of the flying swords, with the remaining 200+ set aside as spares.
- Upon testing, his true body and Primaltwin can each only control 182 swords (the Second Layer), though this already grants his Ki Refining power that surpasses his close-combat Fiendgod strength.
- Consolidating his strength, Ning leaves the Yan Mountains and heads to the Firefan Commandery, where he begins to surveil the East Forest Sect.
- Using the Candle Dragon’s Gaze, he spots two Primal Daoists leaving the sect and decides to use them as his way in.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Art of the Loot Drop – Xianxia Style**
There's a beautiful, almost ritualistic process to upgrading your gear in a cultivation novel, and this chapter is a masterclass. It's not just about picking the shiniest thing; it's about *synergy*. Ning has a specific technique (Grand Thousand Sword Formation) that requires a ridiculous number of swords. A single super-sword is useless; he needs a *swarm*. This turns the treasure selection into a mathematical puzzle. The twist here is that the best formation, the Yin-Yang Twin Polarity set, is actually a couple's item – a reminder that this path can be brutally lonely for those without a Dao-Companion. Ning muses that using these swords as mere 'formation bases' is a waste of their potential as swords, immediately claiming he will make them sing – a small but powerful statement of his identity as a Sword Immortal.

**Primaltwin vs. True Body Tribulation**
The discussion about who goes first in the Heavenly Tribulation is a crucial bit of Xianxia strategy. A Ki Refiner's tribulation is generally considered less intense than a Fiendgod Body Refiner's, because the latter involves 'shedding the mortal body for a divine one'. Ning's plan is brilliantly pragmatic: let the immortal soul (Primaltwin) go first, attain the Celestial Immortal realm, and use that boosted comprehension to better prepare his physical body for the *real* storm. It's a classic min-max strategy that shows Ning’s respect for the severity of the Heavenly Dao’s trials.

## Reading Guide

This is a 'set-up' chapter, but it's the *best* kind of set-up. If you love seeing the mechanical guts of a cultivation system work—the logistics of building a formation, the costs of refining artifacts, the strategic calculus behind breakthroughs—then you'll eat this up. It’s the calm before the storm. Ning is literally forging his main weapon for the coming chaos. Pay close attention to the limits he discovers (182 swords max). This is the ceiling he’s going to have to break through. Also, the shift to the stakeout is a great tonal gear change. He's gone from a meticulous craftsman to a patient predator. The Candle Dragon's Gaze is back, and he's ready to find his prey.
