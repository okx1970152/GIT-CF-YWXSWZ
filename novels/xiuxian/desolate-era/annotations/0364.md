## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow cultivators, buckle up. This chapter is a pure cultivation montage—and one of the most satisfying ones yet. We skip the “training arc” filler and land right in the thick of Ji Ning’s grinding on Mount Innerheart, with a clear target: beat a ninth-tier puppet to unlock the Three Realms Palace. The pressure is real. He’s got a reunion with Yu Wei on the line, and the clock is ticking. But it’s not just Ji Ning leveling up—Uncle White and Xiaoqing get their own glow-up, and Xiaoqing’s breakthrough is a genuine *moment*. This is the kind of chapter that makes you feel the weight of time, the cost of ambition, and the sheer joy of watching side characters bloom.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning enters the fourth floor of the Immortal Palace and realizes the sheer depth of sword techniques available—every manual is on par with the complete *Three-Foot Sword*.
- He commits to a laser-focused strategy: abandon everything except sword arts. No distractions, no side techniques—pure blade work until he can beat a ninth-tier puppet.
- The timeline is set: Ji Ning has been cultivating for just over thirty years total.
- Uncle White and Xiaoqing struggle at first. They can’t beat even low-tier puppets, so they resort to doing odd jobs (weeding, manual labor) to earn access to better techniques.
- Their side-arc pays off big. After 26 years of grinding, Xiaoqing bursts into the rain, screaming that she’s “invincible.” She’s reached the Domain of the Grand Dao of Space, unlocking **Greater Teleportation**—the ability to cross worlds in an instant.
- The chapter fast-forwards through Ji Ning’s own progress: he defeats puppets from tier 4 all the way to tier 7, studying hundreds of manuals, but always keeping the *Three-Foot Sword* as his core, refining it into something uniquely his own.
- Thirty years on Mount Innerheart have passed. Ji Ning is still climbing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The core mechanic here is **puppet-based trial progression**, a classic Xianxia structure. The puppet fights are not just combat tests—they are **Dao mirrors**. They force Ji Ning to fight purely with technique and comprehension, because divine abilities and formations are banned. This is the purest distillation of Xianxia training philosophy: raw understanding of the Dao beats raw power every time.

We also see the **odd job system** on Mount Innerheart—a charmingly grounded detail. Even godbeasts have to pull weeds if they want access to Immortal-level techniques. It reinforces the Daoist principle of humility and effort before reward. No shortcuts.

And then there’s **Greater Teleportation**—a major milestone. Lesser Teleportation is local; it can be blocked by spatial seals. Greater Teleportation punches through dimensions, crossing from the Netherworld to the mortal world in a single step. Xiaoqing’s achievement is actually a big deal: mastering the Domain of a Grand Dao at her level speaks to her innate talent as a Skygreen Serpent.

Finally, Ji Ning’s decision to **specialize entirely in the sword** is a classic “Dao purity” move. Spreading out is tempting, but depth is what unlocks the next tier. He’s not just training—he’s forging his Dao-heart.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a breather, but don’t skim it. The emotional payoff comes from the side characters: Xiaoqing’s rain-soaked victory dance is earned, and Uncle White’s quiet excitement over a formation manual shows how far they’ve come from the early days in the Ji Clan. Ji Ning’s focus is admirable, but notice the tension underneath—thirty years is a long time for the people he left behind. The clock is his real enemy.

Also, pay attention to the pattern: every tier of puppet gets harder not just in stats, but in tactical variety. The puppets evolve, switching weapons and adding formations. This isn’t a brute-force ladder—it’s a test of adaptability. Ji Ning passing tier after tier is proof that his foundation is solid.

And if you’re a fan of the *Three-Foot Sword*, this is where it transforms from a legacy technique into Ji Ning’s own. The grafting of outside insights onto his core style is a subtle but important craft—it’s how a cultivator goes from student to master.
