## Chapter Overview

Picture this: six young geniuses from the big-shot Ancestral Manor, who have been waiting all afternoon on a quiet lake island, fuming that the legendary Ji Ning hasn't rolled out the red carpet. They're muttering that at sixteen, how strong could he *really* be? By the time this chapter is over, they're kneeling. One chapter. That's all it takes for I Eat Tomatoes to deliver one of the purest, most satisfying doses of "the master humbles the arrogant youth with a single lesson." If this doesn't get you hyped for what Ji Ning has become, nothing will.

## Key Plot Points

- Qiuye, the head steward of Brightheart Island, receives the six-man delegation from the Ancestral Manor. They sulk and complain about being kept waiting, their arrogance clearly visible.
- As sunset falls, Ji Ning returns from his daily boat-drift meditation on Winged Serpent Lake. His presence is so attuned to nature that he seems like part of a painting—a visual clue to his immense sword realm progress.
- Ji Ning summons them to the Sword Practice Ground and decides to target the strongest one first for maximum impact: Ji Mo, the genius who has just reached Grand Completion of the clan secret art *Myriad Sword Melody*.
- Ji Mo unleashes his ultimate killing move—but Ji Ning effortlessly flicks away his flying sword with a single finger, stunning the entire group.
- Ji Ning then gives a live demonstration: he uses the same *Myriad Sword Melody* technique, but this time he infuses it with a "soul"—first joyful, then murderous—showing what the art truly means.
- Ji Mo is completely conquered; he kneels and begs Ji Ning to accept him as a disciple.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Meaning of “Melody” in Sword Arts (剑曲之魂)**
In Eastern cultivation fiction, a special technique isn't just about the power of individual moves—it's about the *spirit* or *soul* that binds them together. Ji Ning’s lesson here is pure gold for understanding the difference between copying the moves and truly mastering the art. The *Myriad Sword Melody* has three named stances, but the true secret is in the word “melody” (曲). Think of each sword-shadow as a note. If you simply jam all the notes together, you get noise. But if you weave them together—with a coherent emotional intent, a *soul*—then every note resonates with the others, and a roaring, unified symphony of death is born. This concept of a “soul” or “spirit” within a technique (剑魂) is a cornerstone of high-level xianxia combat.

**The “Grand Completion” Trap**
Ji Mo proudly declares his *Myriad Sword Melody* has reached “Grand Completion” (大成). In the xianxia world, this generally means he has mastered the physical execution of the moves flawlessly. But Ji Ning instantly exposes the difference between technical perfection and true enlightenment. Grand Completion is a milestone, not the finish line. The real leap happens when the technique becomes an extension of the cultivator’s own Dao—when it has a soul that reflects the cultivator’s heart. This is a classic trope: the genius who thinks he’s reached the peak, only to be humbled by someone who has surpassed the peak itself.

## Reading Guide

Get ready, because this is one of those feel-good chapters where Ji Ning isn't fighting for his life or losing someone he loves—he’s just being awesome. It’s a victory lap chapter, and you should enjoy every second of it. If you’ve been following his journey from a sickly child to a sword prodigy, seeing him completely outclass the best the Ji Clan Ancestral Manor has to offer is a fantastic payoff. Watch how he doesn’t even need to use his full strength; a single fingertip and a demonstration are all it takes. This chapter perfectly sets up Ji Ning’s growing reputation and his role as a mentor figure, not just a lone warrior. Also, keep your eyes open for that state of “spirit merging with heaven and earth” (神融天地)—it’s a sign of his insane progress outside of the main combat arcs.
