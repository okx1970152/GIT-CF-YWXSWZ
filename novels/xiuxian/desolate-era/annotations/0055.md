## Chapter Overview

Welcome back to the agony of the Aquatic Palace trials. Ji Ning has passed the seed-born giants and the sword formation only to run headfirst into the third and most soul-crushing obstacle: a guardian from the Primordial Fiendgod Era whose skin is so tough that even Ji Ning’s best sword strike merely bounces off like a toy. The rules are deceptively simple—wound him and he’ll willingly fall. But after endless centuries of isolation, no one has managed to draw a single drop of blood except one lucky youth with a legendary Dao talisman. Ji Ning learns what true despair feels like when his Dripping Water Pierces Stone, his Water-Fire Lotus, and even Moth to the Flame all fail to leave a scratch. And then the monster starts moving. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia tension: the hero, battered and cornered, must scrape the bottom of his ingenuity to survive. Get ready for a final, insane gambit that will leave you breathless.

## Key Plot Points

- **Impregnable Defense**: Ji Ning launches a full-speed dive attack using Windwing Evasion, Lightness Talisman, Agility Talisman, and the True Meaning of Dripping—only to have his blade stop dead on the monster’s cheek. The creature’s jade-green eyes fill with disappointment; it has seen too many similarly talented failures.
- **Failed Follow-up**: Undeterred, Ji Ning chains together Silk Rain Forms Threads and Moth to the Flame, striking chest and face. Neither scratches the monster. He is forced to retreat and recalibrate.
- **The Second Blow**: The monster appears beside him as if teleported. Ji Ning’s Water-Fire Lotus petals barely ripple around it. The creature slaps him, shattering his magic armor and punching a hole through his chest. Ji Ning crashes to the ground, nearly severed in two, unable to move.
- **The Clock Ticks**: With half a minute needed to regenerate and the monster approaching in seconds, Ji Ning’s mind races. His only remaining tool is his divine sense—the telekinetic power that allows him to control objects with his mind. But divine sense, even at its peak, only matches a late Xiantian Fiendgod Body Refiner’s strength: far too weak to wound this ancient horror.
- **The Gambit**: In a final, desperate inspiration, Ji Ning recalls the thousands of discarded magic treasures he collected from the corridor’s dead cultivators. He releases them all, thousands of swords, sabers, and spears hovering in the air, each controlled by his divine sense. With a savage howl of pure life-craving, he pours every shred of his Xiantian true ki into each weapon, tearing his own meridians apart, and unleashes a simultaneous barrage of blinding light upon the monster.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Divine Sense (神念)**: In standard Xianxia progression, divine sense is a soul-based power that only becomes available at the Primal Daoist stage—well above Xiantian. It grants omni-directional perception, telekinetic control, and the ability to release soul attacks. Ji Ning gained this anomalously early due to his unusually strong soul (bolstered by the Nuwa Diagram and his Fiendgod body refinement). This chapter demonstrates the raw creative potential of divine sense when combined with an arsenal: you don’t need physical arms to wield a thousand weapons if your mind can hold them all. However, splitting one’s energy across so many objects drastically weakens each individual attack—a gamble that relies on overwhelming quantity rather than quality.

**The True Meaning of Dripping (滴水真意)**: This is the fundamental insight Ji Ning gained from mastering the Dripping Sutra. It goes beyond the realm of Unity of Man and Heaven, representing a cyclical, self-reinforcing comprehension of the Dao of Water as expressed in a single drop. While immensely powerful for a Xiantian cultivator, the black-furred monster’s skin has survived attacks from cultivators who had grasped entire True Meanings—this only highlights the yawning power gap between Ji Ning’s current level and the Primordial Era’s standards.

**Artifact Spirit vs. Undying Guardian**: The black-furred monster is not a living cultivator but a guardian left by Celestial Immortal Juhua, bound to the trial. Its skin is described as virtually impenetrable except by Dao talismans, which are rare consumables from the Celestial Immortal level. This reflects a common Xianxia trope: ancient trials are calibrated for beings far above the current protagonist’s league, forcing cleverness and outside-the-box solutions.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a turning point in the Aquatic Palace arc. Ji Ning has relied on speed, precision, and raw power so far, but now he faces an enemy that simply ignores all of that. Pay close attention to how Ji Ning’s desperation slowly transforms into creative problem-solving—this is the mark of a true cultivator: not giving up when brute force fails. The cliffhanger ending (the barrage of thousands of weapons hitting the monster at once) sets up the next chapter’s resolution. Will the sheer mass of diluted energy be enough to break the monster’s skin? Or will Ji Ning have to find another way? Also, keep an eye on the monster’s tragic backstory—its longing for death is both pitiable and a crucial narrative clue about the weight of endless loneliness in a Xianxia world. If you enjoy clever tactical solutions over pure power-level wins, you’ll love what comes next.
