## Chapter Overview

Ji Ning takes his first cautious steps into the World Prison. Having cultivated the Eighteen Fiendgod Bodies of Tao Wu to the second layer with Chaos Nectar, he sends one of his bodies through the spatial door to scout the vast, desolate prison world. His heartforce sweeps across an area the size of several Greater Worlds, only to brush against the consciousness of terrifyingly powerful beings—one a skeletal old man with masterful heartforce, the other an emaciated wild dog that devours psychic probes. Realizing the power gap, Ji Ning decides to start small, approaching what should be the weakest prisoner: a Celestial Immortal. But in a single, devastating exchange, he blindsides the prisoner—not through violence, but through a single misstep of words.

## Key Plot Points

1. **Chaos Nectar Investment Pays Off**: Ji Ning successfully perfects the second layer of the Tao Wu technique, noting how many drops of the rare Chaos Nectar he saved compared to previous guardians. The resource cost scales with raw power, confirming his strength is still just below the True God threshold.
2. **First Glimpse of the Prison Landscape**: The World Prison is a boundless realm veiled in eternal twilight, with every prisoner confined to a thousand-kilometer zone by ancient formations. Teleportation is impossible; slow flight is the only travel option.
3. **Contact with the Elders**: Ji Ning’s heartforce brushing against a raggedy old man triggers a masterful counter-illusion, and a wild-dog Elder God simply "eats" a portion of his force, forcing Ji Ning to sever his own power to escape. These are beings leagues beyond his ability to challenge.
4. **Choosing the Weakest Target**: Ji Ning logically selects a Celestial Immortal as his first interrogation subject, hoping to extract information safely.
5. **Instant Exposure**: The emaciated prisoner immediately recognizes Ji Ning as an outsider—not a true member of the "Ninefang Chaos Kingdom"—when his casual response to the "order talisman" question reveals he fundamentally misunderstands the prison’s release mechanism.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The "Heavenly Lock" as Soul-Binding Torture**: The black shackles in the World Prison are not mere physical restraints—they are bound to the truesoul, making escape impossible even if the body is destroyed. This mirrors a common Xianxia trope of "soul-prison treasures," devices that bypass physical defenses entirely. Note the grim detail: the prisoners grow increasingly emaciated not from starvation in the mortal sense, but from having no ambient *ki* or energy to sustain their bodies. They are forced to burn their own stored power, slowly dying from the inside out. The crumbling magic robes are a direct visual cue—these are beings who have lost hope so completely that they no longer bother to maintain even their own appearance.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia worldbuilding by *contrast*. Ji Ning has grown so powerful that a swath of space larger than several Grand Xia Worlds is covered by his heartforce in an instant—yet the moment he touches the fringes of a real ancient power, he is forced to retreat. The tonal shift is immediate and humbling: from "I am the strongest here," to "I am a cautious scout among sleeping monsters." The ending twist—a single sentence exposing his entire masquerade—is a perfect showcase of how a cultivator's intelligence is as important as his raw combat power. Notice how the prisoner Liangqiu tests Ji Ning not with force, but with a single, casual question about the talisman, revealing a millennia of desperate cunning.
