## Chapter Overview

Holy. Crap. If you thought the last chapter was intense, strap in—this is the kind of Xianxia warfare chapter that makes you put the book down and pace. The opening is a brutal, surgical slaughter: the four Bloodcloud golems tear through a thousand Celestial Immortals like paper, including the Xingtian divine spirit Zhanghong, who is obliterated before anyone can blink. The emotional beat hits hard—Ji Ning watches his ‘Brother Six’ fall and feels the crushing helplessness of being pinned down by Real Immortal Chibing’s black chains. Meanwhile, the battlefield escalates into a cosmic chess match: Real Immortal Baiji reveals himself as the Seamless Gate’s true ace, a Pure Yang True Immortal who can duel a Pangu war formation to a standstill, and the Xia Emperor finally unleashes a forbidden imperial secret art. But the real bomb drops at the very end, when the white-robed Xia Emperor activates the Grand Xia World-Suppressing Grand Formation—a continent-spanning array that rewrites the rules of engagement. This is the turning point where victory becomes a matter of resources and resolve, not just brute force.

## Key Plot Points

- **Massacre of the Thousand Immortals**: The four Bloodcloud golems execute a lightning-fast massacre of the Seamless Gate’s deployed Celestial Immortals and Loose Immortals. The Xingtian divine spirit Zhanghong is killed in seconds. The sheer speed of the slaughter leaves even allies like Zhuanshui in shock—there is no time to rescue, no time to react.
- **Ji Ning’s Struggle and Restraint**: While his mind burns with fury, Ji Ning’s Heartforce at sovereign perfection keeps him cold and calculating. He is pinned by Real Immortal Chibing’s black chains and cannot intervene. Crucially, he considers but rejects summoning the seven Empyrean Gods—this is not the right moment to burn that once-in-a-lifetime favor. The chapter shows his growth: impulsive rage is replaced by strategic patience, even when watching a comrade die.
- **Real Immortal Baiji’s Revelation**: The coldest fighter on the Seamless Gate side unveils his true power. He manifests a ‘frozen sun’ of thirty-six white immortal swords and engages Empyrean God Hanzheng’s Pangu war formation in a stalemate. The Xia Emperor’s internal monologue confirms Baiji as the most dangerous Seamless Gate combatant present, shifting the audience’s threat assessment.
- **The Xia Emperor’s Secret Art**: The black-robed Xia Emperor unleashes the forbidden ‘Ten Thousand Thralls Come to Pay Court’ art, summoning nine black dragons from collapsed space. These dragons are comparable to Empyrean God golems in power, forcing the Qinghu Sect Leader to overextend by revealing her nine-tailed form to counter them. The balance of power is visibly tilting.
- **The Grand Xia World-Suppressing Grand Formation**: The climax of the chapter is not a punch, but a strategic masterstroke. The white-robed Xia Emperor activates the formation originally forged by Daofather Yinglong, using Tianmang Hall as the core and all 3,600 commandery cities as nodes. The entire realm becomes a weapon. This is a declaration that the war has moved beyond skirmishes into total existential conflict.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The ‘Ten Thousand Thralls Come to Pay Court’ as Imperial Authority**: This secret art isn’t just a cool attack name—it is a direct reflection of the ancient imperial clan’s cosmological status in the Three Realms. The ability to summon dragons from collapsed space isn’t simply combat magic; it’s a symbolic assertion of the emperor’s mandate to command all lesser beings (the ‘thralls’ or ‘vassals’ of the world). The art’s name literally describes a scene where ten thousand vassal states come to pay homage at the emperor’s court, which is a core ritual concept in ancient Chinese political philosophy. The Xia Emperor can only summon nine dragons, which is both a sign of his relative weakness and a terrifying hint of what full mastery looks like—ten thousand dragons that could eclipse armies.
- **The Nine-Tailed Fox and Bloodline Hierarchy**: The Qinghu Sect Leader’s transformation is a direct reference to the legendary nine-tailed fox (九尾狐), a major figure in Chinese mythology from the *Classic of Mountains and Seas* (Shanhaijing) onward. In the text, she is not a true nine-tailed fox by bloodline—her temporary nine-tail state is forced and weaker. This matters in Xianxia cosmology because bloodline purity often directly limits the ceiling of innate abilities. A true nine-tailed fox at the Empyrean God level could likely match the Xia Emperor’s secret art; this gap is what allows the Grand Xia side to retain the upper hand. It’s a subtle reminder that in Xianxia, who you are born as matters as much as how you train.
- **The Symbolism of the Artificial Sun**: Real Immortal Baiji’s technique manifests as a frozen sun made of thirty-six swords. In Daoist alchemy, the sun (Golden Crow) represents pure Yang, fire, and life force. Creating an ‘icy sun’ is a deliberate inversion of that symbolism—it’s the suppression of life, an omen of death. Thirty-six is also a significant number in Daoist numerology, corresponding to the Heavenly Fiends (天罡), reinforcing the sense that Baiji’s art is not random but built on precise cosmic patterns.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *chef’s kiss* example of how Xianxia escalates warfare from personal duels to strategic, multi-layered conflict. The key insight for readers is the shift in tools: Ji Ning is an overpowered individual, but this chapter is not about him. He is *stuck*. The real drivers are Real Immortal Baiji (individual peak power matching a formation), the Xia Emperor (strategic deployment of a forbidden imperial art), and the grand formation (continental infrastructure as a weapon). Notice how the chapter ends not on a cliffhanger punch, but on a description of a formation activating. This is Xianxia at its most ‘grand strategy’—the fighter’s battlefield is giving way to the realm’s battlefield.

Also, pay close attention to Ji Ning’s internal refusal to summon the Empyrean Gods. A lesser Xianxia protagonist would have screamed for backup the moment a friend died. Ji Ning’s restraint—the cold, clinical awareness that a single-use trump card is not to be burned on a setback—is the mark of a true Dao-heart at work. It’s frustrating to watch, but it signals that he’s thinking two moves ahead. When he finally does deploy them, it will matter.

And can we talk about the white-robed Xia Emperor? The black-robed version is the frontline fighter, but the white-robed one is the silent architect. The fact that he can activate a realm-wide formation from the capital, in the middle of a distant battle, is a flex of absolute authority. He is not just a cultivator; he is a geopolitical asset. That distinction is rare in Xianxia and makes the Grand Xia side feel much more ‘real’ as a state.

## Armies of Legend

This chapter delivers an unforgettable moment of “legendary figures reduced to anonymous soldiers.” We have seen Xingtian divine spirits, Fiendgod armies, and Celestial Immortals operate as named characters with histories. Now, the four Bloodcloud golems demonstrate the other side of Xianxia warfare: the sheer, terrifying disposability of even high-tier cultivators when the machinery of war is deployed at full scale. A thousand Celestial Immortals—cultivators who have survived Heavenly Tribulation and are considered immortal in any normal context—are torn apart in a sentence. The shock Empyrean God Zhuanshui and the others feel is the reader’s own shock. This is the genre reminding you that power is relative, and that the battlefield is the great equalizer.
