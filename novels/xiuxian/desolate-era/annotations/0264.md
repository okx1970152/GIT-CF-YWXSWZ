## Chapter Overview

The treasure hunt inside Immortal Wu Jiang’s estate hits its climactic peak—and a raw, greedy underbelly surfaces. Xue Hongyi, through sheer aggression and a desperate forbidden technique, bags an Immortal-ranked treasure right under the noses of Ji Ning and Yu Wei. But the real shadow doesn’t come from the Snowdragon Mountain prodigy. It comes from Shao Yanong. Behind closed doors, the Young Master of the Shao Yan clan reveals his true colors: his eyes aren’t set on the estate’s remaining loot, but on Yu Wei and Nine Lotos themselves. The chapter expertly shifts from the high of treasure-finding to the creeping dread of aristocratic entitlement, setting up a powder keg that the rest of this arc is about to ignite.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning reflects on his haul from the Water Mansion treasure room, mentally balancing his gains against the need for better weapons and resources, showing his pragmatic, cold-eyed self-assessment.
- A sudden, overwhelming Immortal-ranked treasure fluctuation rips through the entire treasure palace, drawing every cultivator like moths to a flame.
- Xue Hongyi reaches the lamp first, but his attempts to seize it are repeatedly thwarted by the artifact spirit’s obstinate resistance.
- Forced to act fast as others close in, Xue Hongyi burns a forbidden technique and forcibly suppresses the lamp, securing the treasure with ruthless efficiency.
- The artifact identifies itself as the Azure-Silk Divine Fire Lamp; Yu Wei calculates its value at a staggering three million catties of Primordial Liquid—a sum that dwarfs everything found so far.
- In the main hall, Shao Yanong dismisses Xiangliu Fang’s suggestion to seize the treasure, but immediately transitions to discussing Yu Wei and Nine Lotos as spoils to be enjoyed later, revealing that his true appetite is for the women, not the loot.
- The gap between the cultivators’ camaraderie and the Young Master’s predatory calculation sets the stage for a coming confrontation that is political, personal, and deeply unsettling.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Battle of Fame (成名之战)** is a recurring Xianxia trope where a rising cultivator clashes with a major power and fights to a near-draw or forces a tactical retreat, functionally announcing their arrival on the big stage. The outcome of the fight is less important than the statement it makes: “I belong at this level.” Ji Ning is not there yet—he is still analyzing his own growth variables like a mechanic reading a diagnostic report—but every time he walks out of a fight alive against a stronger foe, he pumps more reputation into the engine. The narrative is preparing him for a Battle of Fame whether he seeks one or not.

**Immortal-ranked treasures (仙阶法宝)** are a watershed tier in the Xianxia power curve. Below them, Heaven-ranked treasures are already game-changing. But an Immortal-ranked artifact is a force-of-nature anchor—it can speak, resist its captor, and requires a certain level of cultivation just to subdue. Only a Celestial Immortal can truly wield one without strain. When a Wanxiang Adept like Xue Hongyi acquires one, he becomes a target for every Loose Immortal and sect elder in the vicinity. It is, as the artifact spirit itself warns, “a curse, not a blessing” for the weak-handed.

**Primordial Liquid (元液)** is the universal high-value currency of the Grand Xia cultivation economy. Three million catties is an eye-watering sum—enough to outfit a small army of Wanxiang Adepts with peak-grade treasures. In human terms, it’s a fortune that could buy a mid-sized clan’s entire annual output. Ji Ning’s quiet internal calculation of the numbers (versus his current resource bottlenecks) reinforces the constant tension in Xianxia between rapid growth potential and hard scarcity.

**The Young Master’s Entitlement Mentality:** Shao Yanong’s casual discussion of Yu Wei and Nine Lotos as targets for “a few pleasurable nights” is not mere villainy—it’s a window into the social Darwinism of the top-tier Xianxia aristocracy. A future Duke of Plumes and Feathers, born into a clan that has ruled a commandery for eons, views the daughters of weaker clans as currency. The fact that he speaks of it in private, with Xiangliu Fang as a knowing accomplice, shows that this is normalized predation, not impulsive cruelty. Even Nine Lotos’s distress and submission to her own Ancestor’s authority earlier in the arc is part of this same system—clan hierarchy is a cage that even strong wills struggle to escape.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a master class in tonal shift. The first half is pure, celebratory treasure-hunting dopamine—Ji Ning grinning as he catalogs his loot, Mu Northson’s charmingly greedy “one more corpse!” mantra, the shared race toward the Immortal-ranked fluctuation. Then Xue Hongyi’s desperate forbidden technique and the tension of the lamp’s resistance give the scene a frantic, edge-of-the-seat energy. And then Shao Yanong and Xiangliu Fang enter. The temperature drops. The language becomes clinical, hungry, possessive. When the Young Master licks his lips and says “this Yu Wei is different… she surpasses any woman I’ve enjoyed in the capital,” the reader feels the narrative’s moral center shift. This is no longer about who gets the treasure—it is about who gets to *take*.

Pay attention to the contrast between Ji Ning’s reaction (clinical analysis of his own combat variables, resource planning) and Shao Yanong’s reaction (casual objectification). The author is drawing a line between the “rising genius” archetype and the “entitled scion” archetype—one grows through self-critique and hunger for power; the other grows through access and predation. The payoff will not be pleasant, but it will be deeply satisfying when these two arcs collide.

Also, the Azure-Silk Divine Fire Lamp is not just loot—it is a Chekhov’s Lamp. When a treasure that can burn through Loose Immortals falls into Xue Hongyi’s hands (a man who would trade his grandmother for a promotion), it is only a matter of time before it gets pointed at the wrong person. Watch this space.
