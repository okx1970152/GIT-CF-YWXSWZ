## Chapter Overview
Alright, buckle up, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a masterclass in "how to spot a snake in human skin." After a long build-up of investigation and preparation, Ji Ning finally arrives at Crippled Moon Peak, the lair of the man who orchestrated the tragedy at Serpentwing Lake. But before he draws his swords, Ji Ning does what the best cultivators do: he pauses, unleashes his divine sense, and *watches*. What he discovers is a chillingly layered portrait of his target. Yu Tong is no simple, glory-hunting bully. He’s a meticulous, two-faced psychopath, playing the role of the faithful disciple for his master during the day, and indulging in cruel debauchery and plotting murder by night. This chapter peels back the mask with surgical precision, showing us the depth of Yu Tong’s depravity just in time for the storm to break.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with a cold, calculated look at Crippled Moon Realperson’s household, establishing his twilight stagnation and his fondness for his outwardly perfect disciple, Yu Tong. We witness the gifting of the rare ‘Tri-Immortal Brew’—a masterstroke of sycophantic appeasement. The true face of Yu Tong emerges the moment the manor doors close. He commands his master’s own daughter, Yue Wei, to kneel and serve him like a dog, revealing a twisted sexual and psychological domination that is a far cry from the respectful disciple act. In private, Yu Tong’s impatience surfaces; he has been a Wanxiang Adept for years, yet his master refuses to teach him the legendary Crippled Moon Saber Formation. He gives his puppet a deadline: three more years, or he will kill the old man. The chapter then reveals the nature of the beast—a ruthless social climber who has used murder, seduction, and cunning to climb from a mortal to a Wanxiang Adept, viewing everyone, including his master and the Snowdragon Mountain genius Dong Qi, as tools. Finally, Ji Ning, having observed this whole sordid play through his divine sense, makes his move, flying his warship directly over Yu Tong’s manor and announcing his presence as an ‘old acquaintance’.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Secret Art of Enticement (魅惑秘术)**: This chapter provides a classic example of how seduction is weaponized in Xianxia. Yue Wei’s charm is not merely natural beauty; it is a cultivated technique, a form of mental suppression that can bend the will of others. In the Desolate Era, this is a legitimate, if often looked-down-upon, branch of cultivation. The master, Crippled Moon Realperson, himself falls under this spell, seeing his daughter’s relationship with the ugly Yu Tong as a natural match, when in truth, she is a victim of a powerful, cultivated charm that challenges even firm Dao-hearts.

**The Rotten Core of a Sect**: This chapter gives us a peek into the systemic rot that can fester inside a power structure. Yu Tong is a perfect parasite. He identifies his master’s weakness (waning lifespan, desire for comfort and legacy), serves it perfectly, and prepares to seize the inheritance (the Crippled Moon Saber Formation) by force or by fraud. This is a dark mirror to a proper master-disciple relationship, where the disciple should uphold the Dao and legacy out of gratitude and respect. Yu Tong is the ultimate bad disciple—a cautionary tale for any cultivator who trusts without verifying a follower’s *Dao-heart*.

## Reading Guide
This is a setup chapter, and it’s a deliciously dark one. For readers who love a good villain reveal, this is prime material. The contrast between Yu Tong’s public humility and his private sadism is the sort of character writing that makes you root for the protagonist’s sword to land with extra force. Pay close attention to the details: Yu Tong’s calculated impatience, his use of his father-figure’s daughter, and his cold analysis of the ‘old bastard’s’ value. Ji Ning’s divine sense is the perfect narrative tool here—it bypasses the mask and shows us the raw ugliness, justifying the coming retribution completely. This chapter installs a new type of antagonist: not just a brute, but a cunning, patient spider who has been weaving his web for decades. The fly is about to walk right into the center.

## Deep Dive: Yu Tong's Social Climbing — A Xianxia Archetype
Yu Tong is a textbook example of the ‘scheming climber’ archetype. From a mortal background, he clawed his way up through amoral pragmatism. In a world where raw power often trumps all, characters like Yu Tong highlight the terrifying effectiveness of psychological manipulation. He seduced his master’s daughter, performed flawless sycophancy, and even murdered Snowdragon Mountain geniuses without ever being caught. This makes him a funhouse mirror to Ji Ning. Both are resolute and patient, but Yi Ning’s patience serves his Dao and his bonds, while Yu Tong’s patience serves only his own greed and ambition. This contrast sets the thematic stage for a brutal clash of Dao-hearts.
