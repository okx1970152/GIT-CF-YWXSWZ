## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists. The Chenyu Mountains are about to get a serious makeover. After slaying Fengmo the Fiend, Ji Ning isn't just kicking back and counting loot—he's gone full force with the River Su, systematically destroying formation foundations across a hundred-thousand-li swath of Shaoyin territory. But here's the thing: we see the carnage through the eyes of three young Xiantian hopefuls from Giant Ape City. They're at the bottom of Shaoyin's brutal hierarchy, dreaming of making it big, only to witness a disaster that could end their world. This chapter masterfully contrasts the raw, world-shattering power of a reborn monster with the fragile humanity trapped in the crossfire. Ji Ning protects the mortals (he has standards), but Mu Northson? The grief and rage have pushed him into a frenzy that threatens to cross the ultimate line. It's a sobering look at the cost of revenge, the weight of karma, and the harsh reality that even a righteous rampage leaves scars—not just on the land, but on the soul.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Mortal Perspective**: Three young Shaoyin Xiantian cultivators (an axe-wielder, an archer, a black-robed girl) are caught in the tidal wave. They are ordinary dreamers hoping to earn clan support. The chapter puts us in their shoes—showing that Shaoyin isn't a faceless empire but a collection of individuals with their own ambitions and grudges. Ji Ning spares them by wrapping them in a bubble of River Su.
2. **Ji Ning's Targeted Annihilation**: Ji Ning is methodical. He destroys all formation foundations in Fengmo's former territory to cripple Shaoyin's defenses. Mortals are carefully shielded by bubbles of river water. Cultivators, on the other hand, are ruthlessly swept away. Ji Ning consciously limits the karmic sin he accrues by not killing mortals, watching his golden karmic aura shrink and turn into a mere clear aura.
3. **Mu Northson's Blood Frenzy**: Junior apprentice-brother has snapped. He pilots his Yixian puppet, unleashing Seven Cycles Wind and Thunder, murdering every Shaoyin cultivator in sight. His killing spree is raw, emotional, and dangerously close to losing control. Ji Ning's divine sense notes the dark karmic sin cloud rising around Northson.
4. **The Forbidden Act**: Mu Northson, blinded by rage, spots a city of mortals and prepares to slaughter them. Ji Ning roars him back to reason—killing mortals will trigger karmic hellfire and kill Northson instantly. Ji Ning's firm intervention stops a catastrophic mistake.
5. **Shaoyin's Waiting Game**: The Loose Immortals and Earth Immortals of Chenyu Mountains have activated defensive formations. They obey the ancestor's command: do not engage Ji Ning. Instead, they have sent a message talisman to summon Celestial Immortal ancestors. The calm in their reaction is chilling—they are buying time for the bigger guns to arrive.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
### The Shaoyin Internal Dynamics
This chapter is a masterclass in how a Xianxia clan's internal politics work at ground level. Shaoyin is a behemoth of a tribe, so large that it's essentially a nation-within-a-nation. The *high-level* cultivators (Loose Immortals, Earth Immortals, Celestial Immortals) present a united front to outsiders. But among the *million-strong lower ranks*, there's bitter rivalry, grudges, and unresolved blood feuds. The system works because the clan's ancestors actively support 'monster geniuses' (like Ji Ning would be, if he were on their side) even when they kill other Shaoyin members. This breeds loyalty in the geniuses, while the ordinary members are kept pacified by the clan's basic welfare policies. It's a brutal but effective social machine.

### Karmic Sin and Merit in Mass Slaughter
Ji Ning carefully avoids killing mortals, because in Xianxia cosmology, slaying large numbers of mortals incurs tremendous karmic sin. Mortals are deemed 'pure' in the cosmic ledger; their deaths weigh heavily on a cultivator's soul. Killing cultivators, on the other hand, generates far less sin, because they are considered participants in the martial world. Ji Ning's prior golden karma (from helping people on Earth) has already shrunk to a faint clear aura. He's burning goodwill to achieve his revenge. The system is brutally simple: a cultivator's karmic status directly impacts the difficulty of their Heavenly Tribulation.

## Reading Guide
Brace your Dao-hearts, because the real pressure is only beginning. Ji Ning has crippled a major Shaoyin fortress, but he hasn't touched the Celestial Immortals yet. The fact that the enemy's Loose Immortals are *not* attacking—they're just waiting—tells you that the most dangerous phase is yet to come. They have called for their ancestors. That means the next confrontation could bring down a Celestial Immortal or two. Meanwhile, Mu Northson's mental state is a critical concern. Ji Ning can't afford to have a loose cannon at his side when the real fight begins. And then there's the matter of the **Soul-Prison Jade**—if the Beishan clan official has been captured in it (as hinted earlier), Ji Ning's next move will have to be even more desperate. The calm before the storm is over. The storm is here, and it has a name: **Sword Immortal Darknorth**.

A quick note for web novel aficionados: we're hitting that zone around the million-word mark where many serialized stories hit a 'creative wall'. IET (the author) is using this breather chapter to recalibrate pacing, giving us a grisly but strategic slaughter scene that advances the plot while also examining the emotional fallout. Classic Xianxia pacing—after the high-octane kill, we look at the wreckage.
