## Chapter Overview
Holy. Crap. Remember how I kept hyping up Ji Ning's explosive growth? This chapter is the *proof of concept*. After enduring a brutal siege that severely wounded eight combined Celestial Immortals defending against nine hundred-plus Void Fiendgods, Ji Ning simply *waltzes* in and, in one charge, shatters their formation and annihilates two of them instantly. The chapter is a masterclass in tactical arrogance and its immediate, violent punishment. The Shaoyin clan's Ancestor, brimming with confidence and a 'plan,' watches his trump card get dismantled in seconds, while Ji Ning unleashes his custom-made sword technique for the first time. This isn't just a fight scene; it's a declaration that Ji Ning has officially stepped into a league where 'top-tier Celestial Immortal' is just a speed bump.

## Key Plot Points
- *The Misjudgment:* The eight surviving Celestial Immortals of the Shaoyin clan, all heavily wounded from the Fiendgod siege, choose to face Ji Ning. They mistakenly believe that because he was once blocked by a five-immortal formation, their eight-immortal 'Eight Trigrams Wandering Dragon Formation' will be sufficient, especially with their Ancestor leading the main attack. This is a fatal error born of underestimating Ji Ning's recent paradigm-breaking growth, not just his raw power.
- *The Speed Factor:* Ji Ning unleashes the 'Nine Heavens Oblivion Wind', a teleportation divine ability created specifically for him by Patriarch Subhuti. He effortlessly evades the Ancestor's three massive, venomous strange beasts, proving that raw, monsterous power is useless if it cannot land a hit. His speed is the key that forces the battle onto his terms.
- *The Annihilation Assault:* Ji Ning deploys a new, self-created sword technique, manifesting six massive black divine swords from his Starseizing Hand-empowered sword-fingers. The golden wandering dragon of the formation is shattered with a single blow. The formation itself is completely obliterated. The sheer conceptual weight of his attack power is so overwhelming that the defensive formation might as well be wet paper.
- *The Devastating Payoff:* In the chaos, Ji Ning doesn't just break the formation; he kills two Celestial Immortals instantly: the Golden Bell Celestial Immortal (whose divine bell is captured) and the Fire Mackerel Celestial Immortal. The Ancestor can only watch in helpless fury as his carefully laid plan collapses in a single, brilliant stroke.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Void Fiendgods vs. Celestial Immortals:** The chapter provides a fascinating power-scale calibration. It explains that while Fiendgods are powerful in raw force, their Dao comprehension is notoriously poor, often hitting permanent bottlenecks. A single high-tier Celestial Immortal like the Infatuated Heart is worth over a dozen Void Fiendgods in strategic value. This reinforces the Xianxia theme that brute strength is secondary to spiritual enlightenment.
- **The Power of Self-Creation:** Ji Ning's new technique is explicitly stated to be *self-created*. In Xianxia, a technique forged from one's own comprehension of the Dao is infinitely more powerful and fluid than any external inheritance. It is the sign of a true master beginning to forge their own path, rather than merely walking the road of another. The chapter narratively rewards this with a decisive, overwhelming victory.
- **Starseizing Hand Scaling:** The 'Starseizing Hand' is recognized as a peerless divine ability from the primordial era, far surpassing the innate abilities of the local Fiendgods. Its fourth cycle has already made Ji Ning's hands comparable to Pure Yang ultimate treasures. His offensive power is no longer just about his sword-art; his very *fingers* are now weapons of mass destruction.

## Reading Guide
This is a **MASSIVE** hype chapter, and you should savor every second of it. Watch how the narrative frames the Shaoyin Ancestor's confidence as a tragic flaw. He is playing chess with pieces he fundamentally misunderstands. Notice the poignant contrast between the exhausted, injured Immortals and Ji Ning's fresh, explosively charged power. This is the payoff for all those chapters of training and Dao comprehension. If you've ever felt Ji Ning was getting knocked around by groups, this is the resounding answer. Also, keep an eye on the captured Golden Bell. In Xianxia, trophies like that aren't just loot; they're seeds for future power-ups or tactical leverage. The chapter title should be "One Charge, Two Kills."

## Why This Fight Feels Different
A key detail often missed: the author explicitly states that Ji Ning's earlier sword technique was only 'equivalent' to the Golden Bell Ancestor's. But now, his new technique is on a *higher level entirely*. This isn't just an incremental upgrade; it's a qualitative leap in his understanding of the Sword Dao itself. The eight Celestial Immortals were fighting the Ji Ning of a few days ago. They were utterly unprepared for the Ji Ning of *today*. This 'temporal mismatch' between the enemy's intel and the protagonist's explosive growth is a classic engine for the most satisfying 'face-slapping' moments in the Xianxia genre.
