## Chapter Overview
It’s loot time, and I do mean *loot time*. Ji Ning has finally refined the aquatic palace with his newly-hatched Primaltwin, unlocking its core nexus and a mountain of treasure that would make any cultivator weep with joy. But there’s a twist—two of Celestial Immortal Juhua’s three legendary Immortal-ranked artifacts are gone, and the only one left is… talking. Buckle up, because this chapter is equal parts cultivation milestone, artifact lore dump, and a setup for a world-class power-up.

## Key Plot Points
- **Primaltwin cultivation complete**: Ji Ning’s second incarnation uses up a whopping 100,000 catties of Primordial Liquid to reach the late Primal stage. He tests the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation but can only reach the eighth layer—a humbling reminder that raw ki isn’t everything.
- **Refining the aquatic palace**: With his Primaltwin’s soul, Ji Ning processes to refine the estate. The yellow-furred bear casually disassembles the Qiongqi puppets and the black-armored man puppet, hinting at its reality-warping authority within the palace.
- **The meditation chamber reveal**: Ji Ning is led to the core nexus—a chamber equipped with a Buddhist “Bodhi Mind-Quieting Formation” and a “Netherwater Jade” bed worth a Pure Yang treasure. Sitting on it calms his Dao-heart and deepens his self-reflection.
- **Six days of refinement**: It takes six full days to complete even the most basic binding of the palace. Ji Ning can now sense most areas, but the Divine Abilities Hall has mysteriously vanished from his perception.
- **Treasure mountain**: The bear reveals Juhua’s hoard—three top-tier Immortal-ranked treasures. But only one remains: the Thousand Ox Sword, which has taken the form of… the black ox.
- **The missing two**: The other legendary artifacts—the Su River Scroll (a river-dimension trap) and the Nine Realms Seal (a brute-force smasher)—are unaccounted for. Cliffhanger: where did they go?

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Primaltwin and refinement mechanics**: In Xianxia cultivation, having a second soul-body (Primaltwin) opens a whole new dimension of capability. Here, Ji Ning uses his Primaltwin’s superior power to refine the aquatic palace—a standard trope where a deeper cultivation base unlocks higher-tier treasures. The six-day duration is a classic callback to the idea that true inheritance requires patience, not instant gratification.

**Bodhi and the Dao-heart**: The “Bodhi” in the Buddhist meditation formation refers to the *bodhi tree*, under which the historical Buddha attained enlightenment. In a Xianxia context, formations named after sacred Buddhist concepts produce a parallel effect—calming the mind to allow the cultivator to introspect without distraction. The Netherwater Jade bed amplifies this by suppressing mental noise, coldly but gently.

**Self-Created World and the Dao**: The “Su River Scroll” and the “Nine Realms Seal” represent two poles of treasure design—one traps the enemy in a pocket dimension of anti-guest rules, the other just smashes them with brute material weight. Both are classic Xianxia fantasy tropes: domain control vs. overwhelming force. The Thousand Ox Sword, as a top-tier flying sword, represents the soul-bound weapon of a Sword Immortal who has comprehended a Grand Dao.

**Why only one treasure remains**: This is a masterful setup by the author. Juhua’s three treasures were legendary even among Celestial Immortals. Having two go missing—and only the sentient sword remain—hints at a larger plot: perhaps the treasures were taken by a stronger being, or perhaps they are part of a hidden inheritance. The black ox’s smugness (“Having me with you is your great fortune”) suggests the exchange may be more than fair.

## Reading Guide
If you came here for non-stop action, this chapter trades punches for profound progress and artifact lore. Let the bear’s playful teasing wash over you; he’s the ultimate hype man and plot engine. Pay close attention to the description of each treasure—the Su River Scroll and the Nine Realms Seal are classic “domain boss” items, and their disappearance is a hundred-percent deliberate Chekhov’s Gun. The real treat is Ji Ning’s quiet Dao-heart tempering on the meditation bed. Watch how his six days of stillness mirror his earlier, frantic battles—this is the cultivation version of “peace before the storm.”
