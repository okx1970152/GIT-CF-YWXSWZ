## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, strap in. The reunion we’ve been waiting for has arrived, but it’s not the warm hug of a homecoming. The rescue of Mu Northson has devolved into a full-scale siege, with Ji Ning’s hands full of flying swords and a silver-armored General who refuses to negotiate. This chapter is a masterclass in escalating Xianxia tension: from a tactical skirmish against Loose Immortals to a brutal slugfest against divine-beast formations, all leading to an emotional gut-punch when Ji Ning and his traumatized junior brother finally lock eyes. The action is spectacular, the stakes are lethal, and the emotional core is as hard as forged Immortal steel.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning’s opening assault with the Greater Thousand Swords Formation is brutally effective, shredding through black clouds and slaying over twenty Loose Immortals before the enemy can mount a proper defense.
- The silver-armored General deploys 36 Bronze Demon Guards, who merge into four Pixiu divine beasts via a combined formation, unleashing a coordinated assault that turns the castle grounds into a battlefield.
- Ji Ning, having trained the Crimsonbright Diagram to the 16th stage, counters with his Fiendgod body, using the Starseizing Hand to batter the Pixiu formations into submission with pure, overwhelming might.
- The General deduces Ji Ning’s identity from his use of the Seventy-Two Transformations and the Eight-Nine Arcane Art, revealing the terrifying intellectual reach of this mysterious organization.
- Ji Ning proposes a trade: the half-forged divine golem and a Heavenly Oath of secrecy in exchange for Mu Northson’s freedom. The General refuses, revealing that even a Heavenly Oath wouldn’t save him from his superiors’ wrath.
- Cornered, the General deploys a binding-type Immortal treasure (a green serpent rope), Ji Ning’s kryptonite as a practitioner of the Eight-Nine Arcane Art. In response, Ji Ning brings out his Primaltwin, unleashing the fourth level of the Greater Thousand Swords Formation for a finale that shatters the power ceiling.
- **The emotional core**: The scene cuts to Mu Northson, who is not the same vibrant junior we remember. He is described as silent, exhausted, a shell carving statues as his only solace. When Ji Ning finds him, his breakdown is wordless and raw, revealing the profound trauma of his captivity.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Pixiu: This chapter prominently features the Pixiu (狴犴), one of the nine legendary sons of the Ancestor Dragon (祖龙) from primordial Chinese mythology. In folklore, the Pixiu is a justice-loving beast often depicted guarding courthouses and prisons. The novel weaponizes this myth, turning the celestial guard dog into a terrifying war-form created by a formation of elite soldiers. It’s yet another example of how *Desolate Era* recontextualizes ancient myths into a brutal, combat-focused cosmology.

The Eight-Nine Arcane Art’s Vulnerability: Ji Ning’s reliance on the Eight-Nine Arcane Art gives him a body harder than an Immortal-ranked treasure. But it has a glaring weakness: binding-type treasures. This is a classic Xianxia trope where the ultimate offense is tempered by a specific, exploitable vulnerability. It creates tension not by diminishing the hero’s power, but by giving the enemy a specific counter, forcing Ji Ning to adapt or die.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a double-edged sword of action and emotion. On one side, you get a gorgeous display of tactical Ki Refining combat (the Greater Thousand Swords Formation) versus raw Fiendgod power (the Starseizing Hand). The visual of three hundred-plus flying swords merging into a single golden beam is pure peak Xianxia. On the other side, we finally see the cost of Northson’s capture. He is not fine. His ritual carving of statues is a heartbreaking detail, a form of silent, self-preserving madness. Ji Ning’s response—that cold, silent internal vow—is the real climax. Watch as he transitions from a calculating warrior into an absolute force of protective vengeance.
