## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow cultivators, strap in—this chapter is a pressure cooker that goes from bad to worse to “holy crap, everyone might die.” With Daoist Buyou dead, the remaining team is fracturing under the sheer weight of the Qiongqi puppet onslaught. Xue Hongyi, the most pragmatic of the bunch, pulls a tactical retreat that leaves Shaoyan Gongzi screaming bloody murder. But here’s the thing: the puppets don’t care about noble titles or personal drama. They just kill, and they kill efficiently. The ten Wanxiang Adept death warriors—our expendable cannon fodder—get absolutely *wrecked* in one brutal sequence, and suddenly the main squad is next on the menu. Just when you think it’s curtains for Ji Ning and the gang, the Whitewater Hound (Uncle White) drops a bombshell: he can break the formation. But can he pull it off before the puppets tear everyone apart?

## Key Plot Points

- **Xue Hongyi’s escape**: After watching Buyou die, Xue Hongyi makes a cold calculation and flees the center of the fight, leaving Shaoyan Gongzi to face eight Qiongqi puppets solo. He frames it as “self-preservation,” but Shaoyan knows it’s pure cowardice—and files it away.
- **Shaoyan Gongzi unleashes top-tier firepower**: Until now, Shaoyan has been holding back. But when the puppets turn on his death warriors, he pulls out a black whip wreathed in first-grade Earthfire (approaching Heavenly Fire in power) and starts *launching* Qiongqi puppets across the battlefield with single strikes. His combat strength is revealed to be on par with Ji Ning, Yu Wei, or even a Primal-level cultivator—a reminder of why the Shaoyan clan’s heir is no pushover.
- **Massacre of the ten death warriors**: Despite Shaoyan’s intervention, eleven puppets overwhelm the ten-man squad in a single, horrifying breath. One by one, they are ripped apart, their formation shattered. This loss drastically reduces the team’s numerical advantage.
- **Reconsolidation under pressure**: With the death warriors gone, the puppets turn their full fury on Ji Ning’s group of five. Shaoyan is forced to retreat into their formation with Xue Hongyi and the Crimson Scaled Flood Dragon, creating a makeshift standoff.
- **The Whitewater Hound’s big reveal**: In a moment of desperate hope, Ji Ning announces that Uncle White has spent the entire battle analyzing the formation and has found its fatal weakness. A formation without a controller, no matter how powerful, is rigid and predictable. With a new Earth-ranked top-grade Fuxi Staff Formation set from Shaoyan Gongzi, Whitewater Hound is ready to attempt the impossible: breaking a Celestial Immortal’s array.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Formations Without a Controller: A Deadly Weakness**: One of the deepest concepts in Xianxia formation theory gets a spotlight here. A formation that’s pre-set and running on its own is like a trap that cannot adapt. It’s powerful—terrifyingly so—but it lacks *intelligence*. Uncle White’s insight is classic Daoist logic: the more complex a system, the more points of failure it has. If you can “jam” just one layer of its energy transformation cascade, the entire thing stops dead. A living formation master could constantly shift the energy flow to counter such an attack, but a dead, automated one? Easy prey for a skilled formation expert. This is why immortal inheritances are so prized; they don’t just give you raw power, they give you the intelligence to wield it.

**The Fuxi Staff Formation**: Named after the mythical Fuxi, one of the Three Sovereigns of ancient Chinese legend, this formation is a portable set of sixty-four staffs that can be deployed to calculate and counter enemy arrays. It’s not a combat formation; it’s a *counter-formation* tool—essentially a programmable calculator for untangling spiritual energy patterns. Receiving an Earth-ranked top-grade version from Shaoyan Gongzi is a massive power-up for Whitewater Hound’s efforts.

### Vanguard

In this context, a disposable force sent ahead to absorb the enemy’s initial attacks, preserving the core team’s strength for the main confrontation.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in tension escalation. The team is breaking down under pressure: Xue Hongyi’s selfishness, Shaoyan Gongzi’s rage, and the death warriors’ expendability are all laid bare. But the real highlight is the shift from brute force to strategy. Ji Ning and Uncle White aren’t going to out-muscle a Celestial Immortal’s array; they’re going to out-think it. This is a classic Xianxia trope—the moment where *knowledge* becomes the ultimate weapon. Pay attention to Shaoyan Gongzi’s character here; he’s a noble with genuine power and a willingness to use it, but his arrogance nearly got everyone killed. His grudging acceptance of Ji Ning’s plan shows a sliver of wisdom beneath the entitlement.

Also, can we take a moment to appreciate the Crimson Scaled Flood Dragon? That divine beast is an absolute beast—Wanxiang level, holding off eight Qiongqi puppets and regenerating like it’s nothing. It’s the silent MVP of this chapter, and it deserves respect.
