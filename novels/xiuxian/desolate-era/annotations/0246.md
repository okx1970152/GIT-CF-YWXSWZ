## Chapter Overview
Time to lock horns! Ji Ning’s ten-day ultimatum to Snowdragon Mountain’s Yan Mountain branch doesn’t just get a shrug—it gets the personal attention of one of the sect’s three founding patriarchs, the Primal-level **Snowplume Daoist**. What follows is less a negotiation and more a cold-blooded size-up: both sides test soul-power, trade verbal jabs, and lay down their absolute positions. Snowplume Daoist pegs Ji Ning as a reincarnated Immortal (and warns that such status is a double-edged sword), while Ji Ning stands his ground with the icy, polite finality of a man who has already made up his mind. By the end, the talk is over, and the two cultivators prepare to settle it the old-fashioned way—by crossing hands. Get ready, because the first major clash between an ascending monster genius and an ancient sect’s heavy-hitter is about to begin.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Letter’s Weight**: Xu Fang and Co. report to Snowplume Daoist that Ji Ning’s handwritten ultimatum contained a terrifying sword-intent that made even Zifu Disciples tremble. The letter self-destructed after being read, confirming its high-level seal.
- **The Patriarch Arrives**: Snowplume Daoist, a Primal Daoist at perfection, personally flies to Winged Serpent Lake on an ancient war chariot. His presence carries the weight of a sect patriarch, and his tone mixes curiosity with cold contempt.
- **Divine Sense Duel**: Snowplume Daoist’s divine sense sweeps the island. Ji Ning not only deflects it but counterattacks with the **Soul-Shattering Art**, forcing Snowplume to acknowledge that Ji Ning’s soul-power rivals—or even slightly exceeds—his own.
- **Reincarnated Immortal Revealed**: Snowplume Daoist deduces that Ji Ning must be a reincarnated Immortal. However, he quickly reminds his disciples that this status makes the Heavenly Tribulation far more dangerous, lowering the odds of a successful Celestial Ascension.
- **Face-to-Face Showdown**: Ji Ning refuses to budge on his expulsion order. Snowplume Daoist mocks his arrogance. Both parties escalate from words to preparation for combat, and the chapter ends with them agreeing to fight—a classic Xianxia ‘let’s see if your fists match your mouth’ moment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Reincarnated Immortal (转世仙人)**: In Desolate Era, when a cultivator’s soul is strong enough, they can reincarnate into a new body while retaining fragments of past-life memories and talents. This gives them a meteoric cultivation speed in childhood—hence Ji Ning’s freakish growth. But the flip side is brutal: “knowing too much” makes the Heartfiend Tribulation far worse. Reincarnated Immortals statistically fail the Heavenly Tribulation *more often* than ordinary cultivators, because their accumulated karmic and soul-depth attracts a stronger cleansing from Heaven’s laws.

**Divine Sense (神念) / Soul-Shattering Art (撼神术)**: Typically, only Primal Daoists can use divine sense offensively. Ji Ning achieving this at the Wanxiang stage is the sort of anomaly that immediately brands a cultivator as either a monster or a reincarnated being. The Soul-Shattering Art is a rare weaponization technique that converts raw soul strength into a mental shockwave, bypassing physical defenses entirely. It’s a flex of pure spiritual dominance.

**Primal Daoist Perfection vs. Wanxiang Adept**: Snowplume Daoist is at the peak of the Primal stage—a thousand-year-old cultivator with a Primordial Fire, Celestial Water, and high-grade Heaven-ranked treasures. In normal logic, he should be able to crush any Wanxiang Adept without breaking a sweat. But Xianxia lives on exceptions: Ji Ning’s soul power, sword-intent, and potential divine abilities make this fight genuinely uncertain.

## Reading Guide
*Why does this chapter feel so satisfying?*

Because it’s a textbook Xianxia standoff: the **arrogant old patriarch** vs. the **unshakeable young monster**. Snowplume Daoist does *everything* right by normal sect logic—he sends a show of force, makes a veiled threat, and even offers a face-saving out. But Ji Ning isn’t playing by normal rules. He’s a Black-White College disciple, a suspected reincarnated Immortal, and a man who has already proven he’ll kill for his principles. When Snowplume says “speak as loudly as your strength allows,” Ji Ning doesn’t flinch—he *welcomes* the test.

Notice how the power balance is set up: Snowplume owns the Ki-and-treasure category, with a millennium of accumulated resources. Ji Ning owns the soul-and-Dao category, with a sword-intent that terrifies elders and a divine sense that matches a Primal. The coming fight is a classic Xianxia equation where raw age meets quality of insight. Who wins will tell us whether genius can truly overturn hierarchy.

Also keep an eye on the **reincarnated Immortal** subplot—it gives Snowplume Daoist a reason to both *fear* and *dismiss* Ji Ning. In his mind, the kid will likely burn out before the tribulation anyway. But Ji Ning’s past-life memories might hold secrets that defy that grim statistic.

*One sentence takeaway: When words run out, swords begin—and this is the quiet before the storm of a heavyweight clash.*
