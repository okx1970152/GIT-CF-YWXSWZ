## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up—Ji Ning has officially entered a *proper* danger zone. For the first time in the Tristar Crescent Continent, the kid who treats this world like his own backyard feels genuine, bone-deep *dread*. We get to watch him put his money where his mouth is, ditching the Primaltwin back-up plan and charging headfirst into the Quya Mountains with only his true body. What follows is a brutal gauntlet of invisible, mountain-crushing pressure and literal walls of lightning that would make any Wanxiang Adept cry. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia tension: a rare place where raw power and treasures don’t solve everything, and you need to rely on pure grit, Fiendgod endurance, and a little bit of shameless "call-master-for-help" strategy. The stage is set for a true trial by combat against the mountain itself—and the chapter ends with a mysterious, taunting voice that promises things are about to get *real*.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning experiences genuine trepidation for the first time in the Tristar Crescent Continent, a feeling he analyzes and trusts. He mentally notes his emergency plan: hide in the aquatic palace and call Master Subhuti if things go south, a safety net he won't have once he graduates.
- He sends Qingya Xiaoyu into his Immortal estate for safety, then enters the Quya Mountains alone.
- Ji Ning identifies the peculiar, heavy soil and lingering killing intent of the mountains, correctly deducing it is likely a fragment of the ancient Primordial Pangu's World.
- He navigates a long, exhausting passage through a zone of formless, crushing repulsive force. He uses the 'Three Heads, Six Arms' and 'Starseizing Hand' divine abilities intermittently, burning through his divine power multiple times to push forward.
- Ji Ning encounters the Golden-Furred Bear King, the last survivor of the Twelve Eastern Stream Demon Kings, who is also attempting the treasure hunt. The Bear King, a Loose Immortal, is forced to stop and recover, his frail Ki Refiner's body unable to handle the pressure. Ji Ning surpasses him with his superior Fiendgod physique.
- After finally breaking through the pressure zone, Ji Ning faces a new, more lethal challenge: a bombardment of devastating lightning bolts. The power escalates rapidly, stopping him cold after just a hundred zhang.
- Defeated for the moment and strategizing a possible retreat, Ji Ning is startled by a sharp, taunting voice from the mist, warning him to leave or die.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a beautiful illustration of the "Trial by Combat" trope, but on a macro scale. The Quya Mountains aren't just a location; they are a *trial*. The ban on flight and the suppression of divine sense are classic Xianxia mechanics, forcing the protagonist to rely on his most fundamental strengths. For Ji Ning, that strength is his Fiendgod Body Refining—the brute force endurance that allows him to tank what his sword can't fully block. The pressure and lightning are a pure, relentless test of his Dao comprehension and his physical cultivation, stripping away all his fancy trump cards and leaving only his core abilities. It's a mirror match against the ancient formation itself, a battle of endurance and will.

The "heavy soil" and "lingering killing intent" are a fantastic touch of worldbuilding. In many traditional Chinese stories, ancient battlefields and places where Primordial Gods fell retain an echo of that violence. This "residual will" can influence the living, a concept rooted in the belief that strong emotions, especially from death, can stain a place. The fact that the ground feels "heavy" and the air "denser" suggests this isn't just a memory; it's a fragment of a completely different physical reality—a piece of Pangu's World, with different cosmic laws, intruding into the Tristar Crescent Continent.

Also, note Ji Ning's cheeky internal monologue about calling for Master Subhuti's help. In Xianxia, a master-disciple relationship is sacred. While you are a disciple *within* the sect, you have the sect's backing. Once you "graduate" and leave the mountain, you are on your own to face the world's tribulations. Ji Ning is exploiting the last loopholes of his training period, and he knows it's a bit shameless. It's a great character moment that highlights his pragmatic Dao-heart: he'll use every tool available, including his master's grace, before the training wheels come off.

### : Quya Mountains (渠牙山脉)

A dangerous mountain range in the Tristar Crescent Continent, believed to be a fragment of the Primordial Pangu's World, filled with ancient traps and lingering killing intent.

### : Tristar Crescent Continent (斜月大世界)

The world containing Mount Innerheart's sect, described as a safe haven for Ji Ning where even True Immortals would call him junior master-uncle.

### : Three Heads, Six Arms (三头六臂)

A Fiendgod divine ability that allows the cultivator to manifest two additional heads and four extra arms, multiplying their close-combat effectiveness and ability to wield multiple weapons.

### : Starseizing Hand (摘星手)

A top-ten divine ability since Pangu, created by Daoist Threelives; allows the cultivator to use their hands as ultimate weapons, greatly amplifying attack power at a high cost of divine power.

### : Thousandbull Sword (千牛剑)

An Immortal-ranked flying sword used by Ji Ning, noted for producing sword-light in the form of a black divine dragon.

### : Plow Heaven Swords (犁天剑)

Top-grade Immortal-ranked flying swords used by Ji Ning's true body in this chapter, described as faintly ethereal and black.

### : Loose Immortal (散仙)

A cultivator who failed the Heavenly Tribulation, surviving by transcending physical form. They are physically frail Ki Refiners with immense magical power but must constantly endure periodic tribulations.

### : Golden-Furred Bear King (金毛罴妖王)

The last surviving member of the Twelve Eastern Stream Demon Kings, a Loose Immortal who also seeks the treasure of the Quya Mountains.

### : Qingya Xiaoyu (青崖小雨)

Ji Ning's disciple, stored within his Immortal estate for safety before the exploration of the Quya Mountains.

### : Primordial Pangu's World (上古盘古世界)

The original creation world brought into being by the god Pangu, whose shattered fragments now form the basis for many legendary and dangerous realms in the Desolate Era cosmos.

## Reading Guide

*Oh, you thought the pressure was bad? Here, have a lightning storm.* Ji Ning is stuck, and it's a fantastic spot to be in as a reader. The introduction of the mysterious voice is the perfect cliffhanger—is it a guardian spirit of the treasure? A rival cultivator? Something ancient and hungry? The chapter masterfully builds a sense of scale. That pressure zone? It took a team of twelve Demon Kings to even think about tackling it. The Qi Kingdom, a whole nation, gave up on it after generations of trying. And now, Ji Ning has solo'd the first part, but the second part has him completely stonewalled. It's a humbling moment that reinforces the vast power gap between what he can do and what the truly ancient powers of the world could do. The "kill or be killed" stakes are set. Can he find a way through the lightning? Or will he have to face whatever is making that snide comment without first winning the right to enter? Get ready for a classic Xianxia challenge where raw power meets a clever solution—or a very bad day.
