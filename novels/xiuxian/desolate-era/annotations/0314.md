## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a masterclass in how a single scroll can shake the very foundations of a Xianxia world. Ji Ning and his cousin-sister, Yuchi Xiyue, are still fuming over the Shaoyan clan's audacious assassination attempt when they crack open the Floating Cloud Immortal's storage treasure. What they find isn't gold or pills—it's a golden scroll with a single line of writing, signed by a name that makes the heavens themselves tremble. In a narrative that switches between the imperial capital's simmering tension and two far-off prodigies' silent breakthroughs, this chapter sets the stage for the upcoming Immortal Fortunes Assembly in a way that feels both intimate and cosmically vast. Get ready for ancient emperors, primal divine abilities, and the quiet hum of destiny.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning and Yuchi Xiyue examine the loot from the slain Floating Cloud Immortal. Amidst the expected treasures, Ji Ning discovers a mysterious golden scroll bearing a single line of text and a signature: "Xiamang Sun."
- The scroll's pressure is so immense that even Ji Ning, who has faced an ancient Fiendgod, feels his soul tremble.
- Yuchi Xiyue rushes to consult her grandfather, the Celestial Immortal ancestor of the Yuchi clan, and returns with staggering news: the signature belongs to none other than the Grand Xia Emperor himself—the founder and eternal ruler of this vast dynasty.
- The scroll is identified as a *Reincarnation Writ*: a cosmic get-out-of-death-free card that guarantees the bearer's soul will be reincarnated back into their original clan and world after death.
- Ji Ning senses a pair of invisible eyes watching him from the void the moment he learns the Emperor's true name, confirming that speaking the name of such a supreme being is no trivial act.
- The scene shifts to the Black-White College, where Lata Realperson—the slovenly number-one disciple—achieves a breakthrough in his 'Black Tortoise Grand Divine Ability', manifesting a colossal Turtle-Snake phantom. He receives a mysterious premonition that the Immortal Fortunes Assembly is his great opportunity.
- In the North Sea, the fearsome Suncrow Immortal is lecturing his disciples when one of them asks to attend the Assembly. His dismissive refusal is abruptly cut short as his own expression shifts, hinting at a mysterious change.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Power of True Names in Xianxia**: In Daoist cosmology and Chinese folklore, a true name carries immense power. Speaking the name of a god, a demon, or a supremely powerful cultivator is not a casual act—it creates a karmic link, a resonance that allows that being to perceive you. This is exactly what happens to Ji Ning: the moment he learns the Grand Xia Emperor's true name, Xiamang Sun, he senses a pair of eyes watching him from the void. This isn't paranoia; it's a metaphysical rule. The Emperor, having ruled for billions of years since the Fiendgod Era, has likely long since transcended normal physical constraints, and his awareness can pierce through space and time the moment his name is invoked. It's a powerful reminder that in the world of *Desolate Era*, knowledge is not just power—it's a direct connection to the source of that power.

**Turtle-Snake (Xuanwu / Black Tortoise)**: Lata Realperson's 'Black Tortoise Grand Divine Ability' draws from one of the most revered figures in Chinese mythology—the Xuanwu, or Black Tortoise. In Daoist tradition, the Xuanwu is one of the Four Symbols (Si Xiang), the guardian spirit of the North. It is a hybrid creature: a snake coiled around a tortoise. The tortoise represents endurance, stability, and the solid foundation of the Earth; the snake represents hidden, Yang-like activity, sharpness, and transformation. When a cultivator manifests a Turtle-Snake phantom, it signifies an unparalleled balance of Yin and Yang within their foundation, giving them both immense defensive power (the shell) and deadly, piercing offensive potential (the snake). Lata's premonition—being drawn to the Assembly by this very power—suggests his destiny is now intertwined with the great events to come.

**The Reincarnation Writ**: This is a phenomenal example of how the "connections" (guanxi) system operates even in the afterlife. In Chinese folk religion, the Ten Kings of Hell and Lord Cui are the ultimate bureaucrats of the underworld. They judge souls and assign them to the Six Paths of Reincarnation based on their karmic merit. A Reincarnation Writ, personally penned by a reigning Emperor who rules an entire major world, is like a diplomatic passport or a royal decree. The writ doesn't bypass karma; it simply ensures that, regardless of how a cultivator dies (even in battle), their soul has a first-class ticket back to their original family. It's a deeply pragmatic tool—a "continue from last save point" item for a powerful immortal's reincarnation—that highlights the sheer, unimaginable authority of the Grand Xia Emperor.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a critical gear-shift from the raw action of the assassination attempt to the deep, strategic setup for the *Immortal Fortunes Assembly*. Pay close attention to the **dramatic irony** at play: Ji Ning now holds a cosmic-level failsafe in the form of a Reincarnation Writ, but he's already one of the most protected geniuses in the realm. The writ isn't a sign of his weakness—it's a symbol of the terrifying peak he's climbing towards and the enemies he's making along the way. Also, keep an eye on the two parallel threads: Lata Realperson, the "lazy genius" of the Black-White College, and the Suncrow Immortal in the North Sea. They are not random background noise. Their personalized, almost spiritual call to the Assembly suggests that the competition will be far from a simple tournament of brawlers. It's shaping up to be a convergence of the most uniquely talented beings in the entire major world, each carrying their own incomplete destiny. This is the calm before the storm, and the storm is going to be a beauty.
