## Chapter Overview
Woof. Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because the ripple effects from Ji Ning’s midnight slaughter of a Loose Immortal are about to crash through the entire Grand Xia like a tidal wave. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia politics and emotional fallout — no combat, just pure, delicious consequences. We watch the Shaoyan clan’s leader squirm as he tries to spin this disaster, see the Black-White College erupt in utter disbelief and joy, and then… we visit Nine Lotos. It’s a quiet, heartbreaking gut-punch that shows how far apart their paths have truly drifted. Get ready for a chapter that proves sometimes the loudest battles are the ones fought with whispers, scrolls, and shattered pride.

## Key Plot Points
- **Shaoyan Clan Damage Control** : Yushen Gong learns Floating Cloud Immortal’s assassination failed and that Ji Ning’s Primaltwin killed him. To save face, he publicly expels the dead Loose Immortal from the clan, framing the assassination as an unsanctioned rogue action.
- **Reassessment of Ji Ning** : The intelligence spreads among the Imperial Guard barracks and then to all the major clans of the capital. They are forced to shed their previous condescension and recognize Ji Ning as a genuine powerhouse, not just a lucky upstart.
- **Black-White College Jubilation** : College Lord Bihai Daoist reads the report to a stunned assembly of Immortals and Primal Daoists. The news provokes shock, disbelief, and then wild celebration, with Crazy Immortal Wufeng demanding it be spread to every disciple.
- **Nine Lotos’s Quiet Vigil** : On a cold mountain peak, Nine Lotos sits in solitude, haunted by guilt over her past with Ji Ning. Her mother tries to belittle Ji Ning as a “little native,” but Nine Lotos firmly defends him as a “true genius” and her former Dao-companion.
- **A Heartfelt Farewell** : After reading the very same battle report, Nine Lotos offers a silent, selfless blessing for Ji Ning to live well. The chapter closes with the poignant realization that their paths, once intertwined, are now irrevocably separate.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The Life Tablet (命简)** : A staple of Xianxia worldbuilding. Yushen Gong watches a jade tablet shatter, signaling Floating Cloud Immortal’s death. These soul-linked artifacts provide instantaneous confirmation of death across vast distances, a brutal and efficient tool for any clan leader tracking their assets (or liabilities).
- **Loose Immortals as Clan Assets** : Floating Cloud Immortal was a *Loose Immortal* — a cultivator who failed the Heavenly Tribulation but survived by abandoning his physical body. In the Grand Xia’s political structure, these powerful beings often serve as enforcers or assassins for noble clans. Yushen Gong’s cold expedient expulsion of Floating Cloud after death is a classic Xianxia survival tactic: the clan is eternal, but the individual is disposable.
- **The Post-Battle Reputation Bomb** : In Xianxia, a public feat of strength instantly recalibrates political threat assessment. Ji Ning’s kill wasn’t just a victory; it was an advertisement that he possessed both a high-level Primaltwin and a powerful sword formation. The author uses this as a vehicle to show how the gossip mill of the Imperial Guard and the Heavenly Treasures Mountain intelligence network reshapes the entire political landscape overnight.
- **The Weight of “Dao-companion” (道侣)** : Nine Lotos’s internal conflict is framed by this term. A Dao-companion in Xianxia is more profound than a romantic partner; it signifies a shared path on the Dao, a lifelong commitment to mutual cultivation. Her statement that Ji Ning was her “former Dao-companion” carries immense weight, showing that their bond was a spiritual and philosophical pillar that has now been broken.

## Reading Guide
This is a *consequence* chapter, and it’s a masterclass in Xianxia pacing. Watch how carefully the author contrasts the two reactions. On one side, we have the cold, political calculus of the Shaoyan clan leader — his first instinct isn’t grief for a fallen clansman, but brand management and saving face. On the other, we have the raw, unadulterated joy of the Black-White College. It’s a wonderful dynamic that shows Ji Ning’s roots and his enemies in sharp relief.

But the real treasure of this chapter is the final scene with Nine Lotos. Pay close attention to her dialogue with her mother. Madame Lian’s accusations that Ji Ning is a “little native” are meant to wound, and Nine Lotos’s defense of him as a “true genius” and her “former Dao-companion” shows she still carries the weight of that relationship. The final, silent blessing is not a sign of weakness, but of a profound, hard-won maturity. She has chosen her clan’s burden, and she is letting him go so that she can shoulder it. It’s a beautiful, bittersweet farewell that doesn’t need a single sword stroke to be devastatingly effective.

## A Note on the Role of the Primaltwin
This chapter elevates the concept of the “Primaltwin” (第二元神) from a theoretical advantage to a game-changing military asset. Ji Ning’s true body is at the Wanxiang Adept stage, but his Primaltwin has reached Primal Daoist perfection. The Primaltwin is essentially a second, independent life—a perfect clone created from a unique treasure. It can cultivate separately and even survive the destruction of the original body. In the merciless politics of the Grand Xia, this means that killing Ji Ning’s true body is no longer a guaranteed solution; his Primaltwin could still grow into a catastrophic threat for the Shaoyan clan, keeping them permanently on edge.
