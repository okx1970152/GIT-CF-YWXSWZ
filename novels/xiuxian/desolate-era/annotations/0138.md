## Chapter Overview

This is it—the farewell. After the brutal battle at Oxhorn Mountain, Ji Yichuan’s body has reached its limit. The forbidden arts he burned through have collapsed his Zifu, leaving him with only a month to live. Ji Ning must face the one thing every cultivator dreads most: watching a loved one choose a glorious death over a crippled existence. Ji Yichuan passes peacefully, surrounded by his son and his lifelong brother, Whitewater Hound. But before he goes, he hands Ji Ning a beast-hide scroll—the names of the enemies who destroyed his maternal uncle and ruined his mother’s life. Then, after a winter of reflection and creation, Ji Ning writes a new sword manual for his clan, says goodbye to Qiuye, and finally leaves the Yan Mountains with Uncle White, stepping into the boundless world beyond. This chapter closes the Yan Mountains arc with raw emotion, a funeral pyre, and a promise carved into the wind.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Final Collapse**: Ji Yichuan reveals that his Zifu has completely disintegrated due to the forbidden arts he used. He has only one month to live. Ji Ning is devastated, but his father insists that a brilliant death in battle is far better than lingering on as a cripple. He speaks of rejoining his wife, Yuichi Snow.
- **Uncle White’s New Duty**: Ji Yichuan asks Whitewater Hound to follow Ji Ning from now on—not as a servant, but as an elder and guardian. The great hound, weeping, accepts.
- **The Revenge List**: Ji Yichuan hands Ji Ning a beast-hide scroll with the names and information of the enemies who killed Yuichi Snow’s brother, the event that shattered their family. Ji Ning swears to kill them all.
- **A Father’s Final Words**: Ji Yichuan asks to be cremated and his ashes scattered over Winged Serpent Lake, to be with his wife. His last words to his son: “Live well, live splendidly, live happily, live freely, live at ease.” He dies with a smile.
- **The New Sword Art**: Over the next six months, Ji Ning distills his comprehension of the Dao of Rainwater into a new manual called *Rainwater Sutra*, which he donates to the clan as a heirloom treasure, alongside other manuals and a Loose Immortal’s formation scroll.
- **The Quiet Departure**: In the dead of a snowy night, Ji Ning kneels by the lake to pay his respects to his parents, then takes a boat into the sky with Whitewater Hound. Qiuye watches from the shadows, crying, but Ji Ning does not look back. He is gone.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Forbidden Arts (禁术)**: In cultivation fiction, forbidden arts are techniques that deliberately damage the cultivator’s body, spirit, or lifespan in exchange for a massive boost in combat power. Ji Yichuan’s multiple uses of these arts accelerated his Zifu’s collapse, turning what might have been a crippled life into a guaranteed, short-term death. This is a classic theme: the warrior’s pride in choosing a fiery end over slow decay. The cost is always explicit—here, the complete disintegration of the Zifu dimensional space.

**Cremation and Ashes Over Water**: Ji Yichuan’s request to be cremated and his ashes scattered over Winged Serpent Lake is deeply resonant in Chinese culture. Fire purifies and returns the body to the elements, while scattering ashes over a body of water (especially one tied to family memories) symbolizes eternal union with the land and with loved ones. It’s a common trope in Xianxia—a warrior’s final, poetic return to nature.

**Writing a Sword Manual as Cultivation**: Ji Ning spends six months writing the *Rainwater Sutra*. This isn’t just a literary exercise; in Xianxia, the act of formally writing down one’s insights into the Dao is itself a form of cultivation. By organizing and recording the hundreds of layers of True Meaning of Rainwater, Ji Ning deepens his own understanding. The process is both a legacy for his clan and a self-training technique. The resulting manual is so potent that even the clan elders feel oppressed by the sword-intent emanating from its cover.

**Leaving the Nest**: This chapter ends the “Beginning Arc” or “Yan Mountains Arc” typical of progression fantasy. The protagonist has outgrown his home, settled his debts, said his goodbyes, and now steps into a wider world. The snow, the night departure, and the single boat heading into the unknown are iconic imagery—a young hero venturing into the vast unknown, carrying the weight of a father’s dream and a mother’s memory.

## Reading Guide

Get your tissues ready, fellow Daoists. This chapter is a gut punch. Watching Ji Yichuan die—not in battle, but peacefully, with his son holding his hand—is one of the most emotionally mature moments in the series so far. The author doesn’t drag it out with melodrama; the death is quiet, intimate, and devastating. Notice how Ji Ning doesn’t rage or break down; he howls once, then composes himself. That’s the mark of a Dao heart forged in tragedy. The true emotional weight comes from the small details: the trembling hand, the fallen hand, the hound leaning against the chair.

Take note of the vengeance list. These names will drive Ji Ning’s next major narrative arc. The *Rainwater Sutra* is also a huge power-up for the entire Ji Clan—a treasure that will shape future generations. And Qiuye… her silent wait, her promise, her tears—this is a bond that will echo later in the story.

If you’ve loved the slow-burn worldbuilding and family drama of the Yan Mountains arc, this is a satisfying, bittersweet sendoff. From here, Ji Ning enters the wider world of the Grand Xia Dynasty. The training wheels are off. Buckle up.
