## Chapter Overview
Well, fellow Daoists, the moment of truth has arrived. Crimsonbright Daofather has spoken, and the name he chose wasn’t Ji Ning. Ouch. That one stings. After all that build-up—the perfect score, the unstoppable sword-intent, the overwhelming victories—our protagonist gets passed over for the quiet, humble boy with the strange vitality. This chapter is a masterclass in how the Xianxia world’s most brutal lottery works. Talent and achievement matter, sure, but the whims of a being who has lived for countless Chaos Cycles matter more. The Xia Emperor masterfully teases the reveal, and when it lands, the emotional ripple effect across the hall is palpable. Ji Ning doesn’t crumble, though. He takes the hit, recovers in the span of a breath, and immediately congratulates the winner. That’s the Dao-heart of a monster, right there. And just when you think the bad news is over, we learn that even the slovenly man—the Black-White College’s own eccentric—has been secretly claimed by Xuanwu the Grand Emperor. The game board of the Three Realms keeps getting more interesting.

## Key Plot Points
* The Xia Emperor receives word from his Primaltwin: Crimsonbright Daofather has made his choice. The hall holds its breath.
* After a deliciously cruel pause from the Emperor, the name is announced: Mu Northson. The hall erupts in a mix of shock, envy, and celebration.
* Mu Northson is ceremoniously welcomed as a Daofather’s disciple. His fate is permanently and irrevocably altered. He is now a junior apprentice-brother to the Xia Emperor himself.
* Ji Ning experiences a brief, visceral moment of blank shock, but his Dao-heart holds firm. He immediately and sincerely congratulates Woodpass, showcasing his emotional maturity and resilience.
* The Xia Emperor reassures Ji Ning and Blackstone, promising them both as disciples to Pure Yang True Immortals within three days, with himself as a backup option.
* Yuchi Xiyue and Yu Wei reveal a second bombshell: the slovenly man (the Black-White College master) has always been a secret disciple of Xuanwu the Grand Emperor. He is being taken away immediately.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Brutal Arbitrariness of Fate (The Daofather’s Choice)**: In many cultivation stories, hard work and raw talent are supposed to be the ultimate deciders. But this chapter introduces a harsher reality: the Dao of being selected by a supreme being. A Daofather’s choice isn’t a job application you can ace with a good resume. It’s a karmic resonance, a hidden match of energy and philosophy that we, the mortals, can’t always perceive. Ji Ning perfectly performed the Sword Dao, but Mu Northson’s comprehension of the Grand Dao of Life and Death apparently struck a deeper chord with Crimsonbright. This is a classic Xianxia trope—the ‘school of fate’ where chance encounters and intangible preferences of the powerful can sideline the purest genius.

**The Weight of a Title: ‘Daofather’s Disciple’**: It cannot be overstated how monumental this event is. A new Daofather disciple isn’t just a strong student. They become a member of an exclusive club that includes the Emperor of the Grand Xia. Their very presence alters the political ecosystem of entire worlds. Clans that would have bullied them yesterday will now come bearing gifts today. This is why Ancestor Xuanji was terrified—a Daofather’s disciple with a grudge against the Shaoyin clan is a nuclear launch code waiting to be activated.

**The Dao-Heart Under Pressure**: Ji Ning’s reaction to losing the spot is a perfect, real-time demonstration of what a Dao-heart actually is. It’s not about being emotionless. He felt the shock, the anger, the bitterness, and the helpless “what if” of hiding the Starseizing Hand. A lesser cultivator might have wallowed, questioned their path, or even broken down into Qi Deviation. True strength is the ability to feel all of that, acknowledge it, and then, within the same breath, turn to the winner and offer genuine congratulations. That’s forging your will into steel.

## Reading Guide
This is the chapter where the story reminds us that Ji Ning is still climbing, not already at the top. If you expected a clean, easy path to absolute power, this is a healthy dose of reality. But this is also a crucial character-defining moment. Watch how Ji Ning handles this loss. His composure, his quick recovery, and his immediate shift to practical planning (asking about the slovenly man’s identity) shows a maturity beyond his cultivation years. The setup for the next few chapters is also clear: Ji Ning needs a new master. With the promise of a Pure Yang True Immortal as a teacher, his growth isn’t stunted—it’s just taking a different, arguably more interesting, path. The dual revelations of this chapter (Woodpass and the slovenly man) set the stage for the Black-White College to become a powerhouse nursery, producing geniuses for the upper echelons of the Three Realms. Buckle up.

## Deep Dive: The Politics of the Immortal Fortunes Assembly
This chapter is a brilliant example of how a tournament arc in Xianxia isn’t just about who punches hardest. The real prize was never the Primordial Liquid or the treasures. It was a *network effect*. Winning puts you in a room with the Xia Emperor, True Immortals, and the potential gaze of a Daofather. The value of that exposure is incalculable. Even “losing” the top prize, Ji Ning and Blackstone are now on a shortlist for the most powerful Pure Yang masters in the realm. They’ve been upgraded from provincial geniuses to assets of the Grand Xia Dynasty itself. The assembly is a recruitment fair for the gods, and every performance is a resume.
