## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, get ready for the ultimate gear shopping spree! Chapter 106 is a pure dose of Xianxia satisfaction: Ji Ning passes his War God Hall test and steps into the *real* treasure vault. Gone are the chump-change Mortal-ranked relics from the Yan Mountains—the first master’s stash is a showcase of high-grade and top-grade gear that makes everything we've seen so far look like rusty farm tools. Our boy Ning finally gets a peek at the inventory that can actually level him up, and the chapter masterfully balances the tension of a failed attempt with the pure dopamine hit of browsing a legendary loot table. If you've ever wanted to see a cultivator's eyes glaze over with greed and strategic calculation at the same time, this is your chapter.

## Key Plot Points

- **First Level Passed**: Ji Ning uses the **Lesser Thousand Sword Formation** (core technique) combined with the **Rainwater Sword Domain** to cut down the wooden post within the time limit, officially passing the first War God Hall trial. The sword-light from his formation, boosted by Zifu-level Yuan power, is described as comparable to a Wanxiang Adept's attack.
- **How It Worked**: The sword-light, invisible when merged into the rain domain, repeatedly sliced the post. The final blow severed it with less than an incense stick's time left.
- **Second Level Disaster**: Urged by the bear spirit not to waste his "Xiantian lifeform" attempts, Ji Ning tries the second level. He lasts *10 seconds* against dozens of black leopard beasts (each as strong as Beizi Shan!). He's forced to teleport out or be shredded.
- **The Loot Table Opens**: Arriving at the Treasure Hall, Ji Ning receives a golden tome listing all available Mortal-ranked treasures. The disparity is brutal—these are all high-grade or top-grade, making his previous hauls look like garbage.
- **Standout Items**: The chapter teases several game-changers: the **Nine Yang Sword Formation** (nine flying swords sharing the same source, perfect as a core for his formation), the **Netherfire Bottle**, the **Starlight Divine Needles**, and the game-breaking **Netherdragon Lockheaven Formation**—a defensive/offensive formation that could protect the entire Ji Clan.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### Formations as Clan Foundations
One of the most overlooked elements in Xianxia is the strategic value of **formations**. In this chapter, the *Netherdragon Lockheaven Formation* is explicitly described as a "clan guarding" formation. This isn't just a flashy attack—it's a permanent defensive infrastructure. In a world where a single Zifu enemy can massacre an entire tribe, formations provide a force multiplier that allows *multiple* weaker cultivators to pool their power. Ji Ning's decision to prioritize this over personal gear underscores his growing maturity: *repelling an invasion is more important than winning a single duel.*

### The "Moocher" Mentality of Ancient Loot
The first master's Treasure Hall is a classic Xianxia trope: the "Ancestor's Vault." The rules are strict (must pass trials), but the loot is absurdly generous. It's a deliberate system designed to create a **power spike** for the protagonist. What's fun is Ji Ning's realization that Celestial Immortal Juhua—who could see this loot but couldn't take it—must have been *tormented*. It's the ultimate "you can look, but you can't touch" curse, inverted for our hero who finally *can* touch.

### The Logic of a "Perfect Core"
Ji Ning's sword formation was previously a chaotic mix of random flying swords. The **Nine Yang Sword Formation** is special because all nine swords are "from the same source." In Xianxia metaphysics, homogeneous energy (same origin, same resonance) creates exponentially greater efficiency than a hodgepodge of different treasures. Think of it as upgrading from a general-purpose toolbox to a matched set of precision instruments.

### Delegated Awe

A Xianxia literary device where the power of an off-screen character is conveyed through the quality of the treasures they left behind.

## Reading Guide

### The Dao of a Tier List
This chapter is basically a Xianxia version of a game's endgame item showcase. Pay attention to the **specific mechanics** of each treasure. The *Nine Yang Sword Formation* boosts his core technique; the *Starlight Divine Needles* leverage his soul strength; the *Netherdragon Lockheaven Formation* protects his clan. It's not just a list—it's a character-defining menu of strategic choices. Ji Ning's need for the formation shows he's thinking about the *long war* ahead.

### Beware the "Dazzled" Trap
The bear spirit's warning—"Don't let your eyes get dazzled"—is a subtle lesson in **cultivation economy**. Ji Ning (and the reader) could easily pick the flashiest, most destructive item. But the text rewards the *smart* player who prioritizes synergy (the Nine Yang swords for his formation) and the *clan* asset (the Lockheaven formation). This is where the gap between a brute and a strategist widens.

### A Promise of Escalation
Ji Ning failed the second level in ten seconds. That's a *massive* hint at the power curve. If the first level required a Wanxiang-level attack, the second level throws *dozens* of equal-tier enemies at you. For Western fans used to RPGs: think of it as a level-sync gate. Ji Ning's current build (even with his formation) isn't ready. But the loot in this chapter is exactly what he needs to *break through* that gate. The gears of progression are grinding, and they sound delicious.

## Deep Dive: The Art of "Delegated Awe" in the Treasure Hall

Desolate Era loves to build hype not by showing a character fight, but by showing them *react* to treasure they can't use yet. Ji Ning staring wide-eyed at a treasure list, realizing that his peak-Zifu enemies use "dog shit" gear compared to this ancient vault, is a textbook deployment of **Delegated Awe**. We don't see the first master fight—we see the *bounty* he left behind. The quality of the loot is directly proportional to the implied power of the original owner. This is how Xianxia authors create a sense of a "higher realm" without a single flashback scene.
