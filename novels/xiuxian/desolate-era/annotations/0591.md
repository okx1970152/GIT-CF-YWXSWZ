## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a classic Xianxia breather arc with a sharp moral punch. Ji Ning wraps up his tour of the Twenty-Seven Worlds of Fuju, but not before dealing with a piece of unfinished business: the arrogant Prince Dongji, who has been stewing over his earlier humiliation. What starts as a potential clash of pride turns into a masterclass in Ji Ning’s particular brand of cold, paternal judgment. He doesn’t crush the prince out of spite—he diagnoses him. By the time the Wuyou True Immortal shows up, the real drama is not about who wins, but about who can be saved. The chapter closes with Ji Ning finally arriving at the legendary Five Treasure Peaks, home to the complete Five Treasures Sword Art, and the sheer oppressive weight of that place is enough to unsettle even a Heavenly God.

## Key Plot Points

- Prince Dongji, still nursing his wounded pride, brings his master the Three-Scorpion Celestial Immortal to confront Ji Ning, expecting his sect backing to intimidate the white-robed cultivator.
- The intimidation backfires catastrophically. Nine-Fang Heavenly God reaches through space and drags the Celestial Immortal to the scene, instantly revealing the vast power gap.
- The Wuyou True Immortal arrives, bows deeply to Ji Ning, and bluntly offers to execute his own disciple for the offense, revealing Ji Ning’s status as an invincible veteran of the Realm War—a terrifying reputation that means nothing to the small-world cultivators until they hear it.
- Ji Ning spares the prince, sentencing him to serve three hundred years as a mortal tavern waiter—a punishment designed to grind away his arrogance.
- The Three-Scorpion Celestial Immortal, disgusted with his disciple, effectively washes his hands of Dongji’s future cultivation, showing the cold pragmatism of demonic masters.
- Ji Ning finishes his year-long tour of the twenty-six fragmented worlds and arrives at the final destination: the Sword Immortal World, where the five towering Five Treasure Peaks contain the complete, legendary Five Treasures Sword Art.
- The sheer spiritual pressure of the peaks forces even the experienced Nine-Fang Heavenly God to pale, underscoring the scale of the inheritance awaiting Ji Ning.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Mortal Waiter” Punishment (凡间小厮三百年):** This is a classic “tempering through adversity” trope in Xianxia. Forcing a proud genius to serve in the lowest, most humiliating job removes the protection of status entirely. The rule “do not fight back against blows or insults” (打不还手、骂不还口) is a sharp implement that carves away arrogance. It is a more surgical punishment than imprisonment or death, meant to reform the soul, not just to hurt. The fact that Dongji later becomes a Celestial Immortal and a war hero proves Ji Ning’s judgment was spot-on.

**The Wuyou True Immortal’s “Shamelessness” (勿道真仙的不羁放荡):** In Chinese fiction, a high-level cultivator who is “不羁放荡” (uninhibited and wild) is a stock character. Their lack of outward dignity is often a mask for immense confidence. It takes a truly powerful being to be this casually shameless in front of juniors. The Wuyou True Immortal’s offer to personally kill his own disciple is not cruelty; it is a blunt, pragmatic acknowledgment of Ji Ning’s seniority. Any hesitation would have been a greater loss of face.

**The Weight of the Five Treasure Peaks (伍宝峰的威压):** The spiritual pressure of a supreme inheritance is a recurring Xianxia theme. It is not just a test of cultivation base, but of Dao-heart. A veteran Heavenly God like Nine-Fang pales, not because he is weak, but because the Dao of its creator (Daofather Fuju) is so overwhelming. Future success in such a place requires not just strength, but the humility to bend without breaking under that pressure.

### Tempering through adversity (磨砺心性)

A Xianxia trope where a cultivator is subjected to degrading or painful experiences to carve away spiritual flaws and forge a stronger, purer Dao-heart.

## Reading Guide

This is a **breather episode** with a hidden payoff. Don’t skim the Dongji scene—it is a rare moment of Ji Ning acting as a *teacher* of character rather than a destroyer of enemies. His willingness to see the redeemable spark in a brat like Prince Dongji shows a maturity that comes from living thousands of lives in the Heartforge Tribulation. Also, pay close attention to the description of the Five Treasure Peaks. The visual of five mountains, rising in a perfect arithmetic sequence from one thousand to five thousand li, is a deliberate image of escalating profundity. Ji Ning’s real trial is about to begin, and the fact that even his Heavenly God bodyguard struggles to breathe under the pressure is a very clear warning: this is no ordinary sword training.

## The Art of the Moral Diagnosis

Ji Ning's verdict on Dongji is a cold, surgical form of justice. He does not care about the prince's feelings. He looks past the tantrums and the "lack of murderous intent" to see a flawed but salvageable piece of ore. The three-hundred-year sentence is not a punishment for the crime—it is a prescription for the soul. This mirrors the Daoist ideal of the true teacher, who does not just impart power, but path-fixes the disciple's character. Ji Ning has evolved from a simple avenger into a cultivator who can measure a man's future worth with a single glance.
