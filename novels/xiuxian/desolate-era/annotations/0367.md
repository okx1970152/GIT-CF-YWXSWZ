## Chapter Overview

Thirty years of quiet cultivation on Mount Innerheart have passed since Ji Ning was accepted as Patriarch Subhuti’s personal disciple. Now, the young Sword Immortal finally steps onto the stage for his most significant challenge yet: a ninth-stage puppet—a living, intelligent creation on par with a Pure Yang treasure, capable of matching even a Celestial Immortal in combat. In this high-stakes ‘mirror match of the Dao’, where divine abilities and formations are banned, only pure sword technique, comprehension, and will decide who strikes first and who falls. And Ji Ning, with his refined Sword Roaming the Three Realms technique, doesn’t just survive the bout—he wins on his very first try.

But the chapter is far from a hollow victory lap. It is an initiation, a quiet announcement to every disciple on the mountain that Ji Ning is not merely a genius—he is a phenomenon. And the echoes of his success ripple into a deeper conversation about the nature of talent, patience, and the legacy of the Patriarch himself. Prepare for a tournament of swords, secrets, and immortal pride.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Challenge and Its Context:** Ji Ning enters the arena against a ninth-stage puppet, a creation so powerful it would be the envy of many Celestial Immortals. The crowd is electric—this is not just a fight, it is a measure of the Patriarch’s judgment in accepting this new disciple only thirty years ago.
- **The Battle’s Flow:** The fight begins with an even exchange, the puppet’s ferocious, heavy attacks meeting Ji Ning’s resilient, flowing sword defense. The puppet reveals its second sword, unleashing a devastating sea-vortex technique that suppresses Ji Ning.
- **The Turning Point:** Under pressure, Ji Ning activates his perfected **Sword Roaming the Three Realms**, channeling his sword-light into two black swimming dragons. In three lightning-fast exchanges, Ji Ning lands the decisive strike—a single hair’s breadth faster than the puppet.
- **The Aftermath and Revelation:** The puppet concedes with respect, and Silvermoon reveals that only personal disciples of the Patriarch can normally accomplish such a feat. Ji Ning learns that among the Patriarch’s weaker disciples, the time to accomplish this feat ranges from a single year to over thirteen thousand years.
- **An Unexpected Lore Drop:** Silvermoon shares the stories of the Patriarch’s most gifted—the Ninth Apprentice, an ordinary painting child who comprehended an entire Dao overnight; the Third Apprentice, a Golden Crow born from the Sunstar; and the Sixth Apprentice, a spirit monkey born from Nuwa’s leftover divine stone. Ji Ning is shocked at the sheer scale of talent in the Patriarch’s lineage.
- **The New Threshold:** As Ji Ning steps into the ninth floor of the Divine Immortal Palace, he learns that the true prize lies beyond: the **Three Realms Palace**, a vault holding divine abilities that can make a cultivator an immortal legend across the Three Realms.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Ninth-Stage Puppet as a Mirror Match for the Dao:** In classic Xianxia, a ‘trial by combat’ against a non-human opponent (golem, puppet, construct) serves as a pure test of comprehension and technique, stripped of external advantages. By banning divine abilities, formations, and forbidden techniques, Patriarch Subhuti ensures that only enlightenment—not brute force—determines one’s rank. It is a very Daoist concept: the puppet is not a monster to slay, but a riddle to solve with one’s own cultivation.
- **The Nine-Stage Puppet’s ‘Intelligence’:** The puppet speaks, assesses Ji Ning’s power, and even shows personality (the cold laugh, the grudging admission of defeat). This reflects the Xianxia concept of a ‘spirit-born artifact’—a high-grade treasure that develops its own will after millennia of absorbing spiritual energy. It is no longer a mere tool but a life form bound by its master’s rules.
- **The Three Realms Palace and Pure Yang Techniques:** The Three Realms Palace is the Patriarch’s ultimate treasury, holding divine abilities that can, as Silvermoon says, allow a cultivator to “roam the Three Realms unchallenged.” The term **Pure Yang** (纯阳) in Daoist internal alchemy represents the ultimate state of masculine, purified qi—a state of immortality and perfection. Techniques marked as Pure Yang are considered among the highest treasures in any sect’s vault.
- **Nuwa’s Divine Stone and the Spiritual Monkey:** Silvermoon’s reference to the “sixth apprentice, a spiritual monkey born from a leftover of the stone Nuwa used to patch the heavens” is a direct callback to one of the most famous figures in Chinese mythology—Sun Wukong, the Monkey King. While the novel adapts the lore significantly, the echo is unmistakable: a being born from creation’s remnants, destined for greatness but bound by a mischievous, rebellious spirit.
- **The Price of Talent and Time:** Ji Ning’s shock at the range (one year vs. thirteen thousand years) illustrates a core Xianxia tension: talent is not evenly distributed, and even the Patriarch’s personal disciples are not immune to the grinding passage of cultivation time. The monster disciples, with their longer lifespans, compensate for poor comprehension with sheer patience, while humans rely on bursts of insight. This mirrors the real-world Daoist understanding that the path to immortality is uniquely personal—no two cultivators walk the same road.

### Trial by Combat

A classic Xianxia trope where a cultivator faces a non-human opponent (puppet, golem, construct) in a contest that tests pure technique, Dao comprehension, and will, stripping away all external advantages like divine abilities or formations.

## Reading Guide

This is a quiet but masterful chapter—a perfect example of how Xianxia pacing works. Ji Ning doesn’t just win; he wins elegantly, and then immediately the conversation pivots to ‘why does this matter?’ The lore about the Patriarch’s other disciples is a world-building gem. It sets the stage for future appearances: the three-year genius, the thousand-year monkey, the thousand-year lazybear. These aren’t just numbers—they are characterization for characters we haven’t even met yet.

Also, notice the contrast between Ji Ning’s casualness (“It’s passable”) and the crowd’s awe. This isn’t false modesty—it’s the mindset of a cultivator who knows that a ninth-stage puppet is only the first gate. The Three Realms Palace, with its mythical techniques, is where the real journey begins.

**Fellow Daoists, keep your blades sharp.** The next floors of the Divine Immortal Palace may not be as welcoming as this one.
