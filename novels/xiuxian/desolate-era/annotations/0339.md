## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because the Immortal Fortunes Assembly is about to get *real*. We have reached the top twenty-four, and the Xia Emperor kicks off the third round with an absolute banger of a match: Ji Ning versus the Blade Servant Truly. This isn’t just a contest of strength; it’s a philosophical clash between the refined, explosive art of the sword and the raw, relentless speed of the blade. For the first time in the tournament, Ji Ning faces an opponent who can push him to his limits, leading to a savage, high-speed duel that showcases both cultivators at their absolute peak. Get ready for a chapter that is pure, uncut Xianxia combat porn where every slash and parry matters.

## Key Plot Points
- The chapter opens with the top twenty-four discussing their potential opponents. Everyone is a monster, and no one can be underestimated. Yuchi Xiyue and Yu Wei are especially nervous for Ji Ning.
- The Xia Emperor announces the first match of the third round: Ji Ning (Black-White College) vs. Blade Servant Truly (Loose Immortal). The tension is palpable among the crowd and in the imperial city.
- Both combatants enter the sealed formation, transforming instantly into towering giants via Heavenly Transformation and deploying Three Heads, Six Arms.
- The initial clash is a head-on power struggle. Even with the blade’s natural advantage in chopping, Blade Servant is physically overwhelmed by Ji Ning’s overwhelming strength, fueled by the Crimsonbright Diagram and the Starseizing Hand.
- Blade Servant switches tactics, using his incredible speed and bizarre, phantom-like blade techniques. The battle becomes a blur of movement, with both cultivators trading blows across the entire arena.
- Ji Ning finally decides to stop holding back, shifting from pure defense to a dazzling, all-out offensive. The fight becomes a brutal exchange of wounds, with both men taking heavy damage.
- In the end, Ji Ning severs one of Blade Servant’s hands, sending a chopping blade flying. Bleeding from multiple grievous wounds and outmatched, Blade Servant Truly concedes the match.
- The chapter ends with the other cultivators realizing that even a terrifying monster like Blade Servant was defeated, leaving the even more fearsome Ji Ning as the rising threat.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Blade vs. The Sword in Xianxia:** This chapter beautifully illustrates a classic martial trope. The blade (刀, dāo) is often associated with fierceness, brutality, and overwhelming speed—think of a barbarian or a lone killer. Its single edge makes it devastating for chopping. The sword (剑, jiàn), however, is the "gentleman of weapons." It represents elegance, finesse, and versatility. A Sword Immortal is often seen as more refined, while a Blade Fiend is a terrifying warrior. Their clash here isn't just physical; it's a clash of two very different Dao philosophies.

**The Starseizing Hand and Divine Abilities:** Ji Ning’s Starseizing Hand is one of the "top ten divine abilities since Pangu created the world." In the Xianxia hierarchy, most divine abilities are simplified, mortal-made versions of divine techniques. A top-ten ability is a fragment of a primordial *law*, making it astronomically more powerful. Ji Ning’s strength advantage is largely due to this—it’s not just his body refining, but the *quality* of his divine ability that gives him such an edge over a genius like Blade Servant.

**Heavenly Transformation & Three Heads, Six Arms:** These two divine abilities are the bread and butter of any high-level Fiendgod Body Refiner. They aren't just cosmetic changes. Heavenly Transformation massively increases strength and durability to match the colossal size, while Three Heads, Six Arms allows for perfect offense and defense from all angles, multiplying a combatant's tactical options. In the wider universe of the Three Realms, these are standard tools for any major battle.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch for the shift in Ji Ning's demeanor.** Early on, he is cautious, even a little playful (using the fight to "temper himself"). It is only when faced with Blade Servant's desperate, fanatical speed that he switches to a cold, calculative pragmatism. This is the Ji Ning we know and love: ruthless efficiency when the game is on the line.
- **Pay attention to the reactions of the other characters.** Yuchi Xiyue and Yu Wei's dread, the Xia Emperor's silent observation, and the quiet awe of the other twenty-four contestants—these all serve to build the stakes. We *see* how terrifying Blade Servant is through the fear he generates, making Ji Ning's victory all the more impressive.
- **Consider Blade Servant’s final line.** "Now with only five blades... I fear you would have severed my entire body." This isn't just an excuse. At this level, a loss of a single weapon or limb creates a massive imbalance in a multi-limbed combat style. He saw the inevitable end and chose to preserve his life and dignity. He’s a smart fighter.
- **This fight is a statement.** The Black-White College is no longer just a dark horse; Ji Ning has just publicly demolished a top-four contender. The narrative has officially shifted its attention from the chaos of the tournament to the singular figure of Ji Ning as the central protagonist to watch.
