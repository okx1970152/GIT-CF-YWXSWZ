## Chapter Overview
Our young Sword Immortal is finally leaving the nest! After three years of study and battle inside the Black-White College, Ji Ning is stepping out into the wider world—and he’s taking his best friend Mu Northson and his godbeast uncle Whitewater Hound along for the ride. But before they go hunting for adventure, fortune, and glory, there’s one essential pit stop: the Heavenly Treasures Mountain! This chapter is a classic Xianxia “gearing up” montage, as Ji Ning liquidates his hoard of looted treasures and converts them into an absolutely *terrifying* arsenal of seven hundred twenty flying swords, plus the legendary Fuxi Staff Formation. Witness the birth of a long-range combat monster. Get ready, fellow Daoists—Ji Ning’s training arc is over, and the real journey begins.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning assigns Meng Yan as the caretaker of Northcloud Peak before his departure, showing trust in the ex-crippled retainer whose loyalty was bought with a second chance at life.
- Yun Lu, the scheming retainer, attempts to attach herself to Ji Ning’s expedition and is politely but firmly shut down by the pragmatic protagonist, who wants no dead weight on a dangerous journey.
- Mu Northson and Ji Ning discuss the Black-White College’s economic ecosystem: new techniques brought back from outside earn Black-White Pearls and Primordial Liquid. Both are eager to start earning.
- Ji Ning processes his conflicting loyalties over the Windwing Evasion and Lesser Thousand Sword Formation—family secrets and clan inheritance weigh against sect contribution.
- The chapter’s centerpiece: a massive shopping trip to the Heavenly Treasures Mountain where Ji Ning sells his accumulated loot (nearly 900 catties of Primordial Liquid!) and buys a staggering 720 top-grade Human-ranked flying swords, split evenly between water and fire elements.
- Ji Ning also acquires the Fuxi Staff Formation, a portable formation-artifact set beloved by formation masters, as a gift for Uncle White.
- The two friends decide their first major goal: joining the Yinglong Guard, the Grand Xia Dynasty’s elite immortal corps, which will give them official status, mission access, and a measure of protection from petty harassment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Treasures Mountain Economy.** The Heavenly Treasures Mountain (天宝山) is a staple institution in Xianxia novels—a cross between a government-backed auction house, pawn shop, and department store for cultivators. It buys low (40% off list) and sells high (30% off for favored clientele like Black-White disciples), making a razor-thin margin on raw materials but crushing profits on finished treasures. For a cultivator, managing one’s “loot-to-resource pipeline” is as important as combat skill. Ji Ning has just executed a perfectly efficient liquidation cycle: kill enemies → loot their treasures → sell to Heavenly Treasures → buy better, unified equipment → become stronger → kill harder enemies.

**The Fuxi Staff Formation (伏羲棍阵).** This is a portable formation set named after the legendary Fuxi, a primordial cultural hero in Chinese mythology credited with inventing fishing, trapping, and the Eight Trigrams (八卦). In the Desolate Era universe, Fuxi is a real, unfathomably powerful ancient figure. The 64-staff set allows a formation master to rapidly deploy a wide variety of battlefield arrays, from simple traps to cataclysmic, realm-shaking formations. It is the go-to tool for any wandering formation expert who needs to bring their artillery with them.

**Yinglong Guard (应龙卫).** Named after the Yinglong, a winged rain dragon from Chinese mythology that helped the Yellow Emperor vanquish Chi You, the Yinglong Guard is the Grand Xia Dynasty’s elite immortal corps. Think of it as an inter-realm CIA, FBI, and special forces rolled into one—its members are roaming enforcers of the dynasty’s will. A minimum of Wanxiang Adept level is required to join, making it a perfect destination for monster talents like Ji Ning who have already surpassed their peers.

## Reading Guide
**Ji Ning’s Operational Tempo.** Notice the calm, businesslike precision with which Ji Ning handles his affairs: delegating peak management, evaluating his inventory, calculating his budget, and prioritizing purchases. This is the *Pragmatic Sword Immortal* in his element. He is not wistful about leaving, nor anxious about danger. He treats preparation as a solvable equation. This is a man who trusts his Dao Heart and his calculations.

**Northson’s Eagerness.** Mu Northson’s youthful excitement is a deliberate foil to Ji Ning’s calm. He wants recognition, treasure, and fame. Ji Ning wants growth, strength, and preparation. Their dynamic is that of a seasoned veteran (despite having no field experience) and an eager rookie—but both are geniuses whose combined strength will be terrifying.

**The Formation Core.** Ji Ning’s choice of 360 water + 360 fire flying swords is not random. Water and fire, in Daoist cosmology, are mutually overcoming yet mutually generating. In the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation, this dual-element tension creates a cycle of destruction that can crush enemies caught in the crossfire. His close-combat monster (Fiendgod body) is now backed by a long-range artillery platform. He is becoming a complete combat machine.

**The Yinglong Guard as Narrative Anchor.** Deciding to join the Yinglong Guard isn’t just about power—it’s about giving Ji Ning and Mu Northson a purpose and a framework for their adventures. Missions provide direction. The badge provides immunity from petty harassment. And the rank allows the author to introduce the broader Grand Xia political landscape through the missions they’ll be assigned.
