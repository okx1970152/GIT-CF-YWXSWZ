## Chapter Overview
Welcome back to the most brutal entrance exam in the Grand Xia! The first round of the Black-White College trials is a doozy: a torrential lightning gauntlet manned by 810 dragon-headed puppets. Our boy Ji Ning breezes through with his water-fire lotus and twin swords, but the real treat this chapter is meeting a fellow monster genius—Mu Northson, a fourteen-year-old puppet master who's so talented he accidentally splashes lightning at Ji Ning and *actually apologizes* about it. We also get our first glimpse of divine sense used as an offensive weapon, which immediately becomes Ji Ning's new obsession. But the cruelest twist? Even after this nightmare run, the examiners drop a bombshell: this was just the *screening*. There's a final test still waiting. Get ready, fellow Daoists—the heat is only turning up.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Lightning Gauntlet**: 810 dragon-headed constructs unleash a saturation-level barrage of lightning at the candidates. Most are blasted down; only a handful survive.
- **Introducing Mu Northson**: A fourteen-year-old prodigy who controls a mighty black dragon puppet. He accidentally sends some lightning Ji Ning's way and immediately apologizes, winning Ning's goodwill. The two bond instantly as fellow young geniuses.
- **Ji Ning's Defense**: His water-fire lotus and "Not a Single Leak" swordstyle prove more than sufficient, letting him pass through the saturation zone in one smooth rush.
- **A Sneaky Free Rider**: A black-clothed man, a fellow Crimsonbright Diagram cultivator (7th stage), barely makes it through by hiding under Northson's dragon puppet, taking injuries in the process. The white-robed examiner later reveals he's her nephew, which adds a layer of familial tension to his desperation.
- **The Eight Survivors**: Only eight candidates reach the cave, including the icily arrogant purple-robed girl and several others. The examiners reveal one more *final screening* awaits.
- **Offensive Divine Sense**: The black-haired examiner uses divine sense as a psychic attack to blast the failed candidates out of the sky. Ji Ning is electrified—this is the first time he's seen the soul used as a weapon, and he vows to learn the technique.
- **Rules Enforced**: A disciple from the mighty Donghe clan is publicly disqualified for using a Dao talisman, proving the college's ruthless impartiality.
- **The Nephew's Desperation**: The black-clothed man's aunt (a college examiner) warns him he won't pass the final test at his current level. But he's stubborn and insists on trying anyway, showing the immense pressure young cultivators face.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Black-White College & Prestige**: The Black-White College isn't just any sect—it's the top-tier institution for immortal cultivation in the Anchan Commandery, comparable to an Ivy League for cultivators in the Grand Xia Dynasty. Getting in means access to techniques (like divine sense attacks) that most warriors can only dream of. The exam's brutality is intentional: they don't just want *strong* disciples; they want *unrivaled* monsters. A 14-year-old puppet master and a 16-year-old Fiendgod body refiner like Ji Ning are exactly the kind of freaks they're looking for.

**Divine Sense vs. Soul Attacks**: Divine sense is the spiritual perception of a high-level cultivator—think of it as combined telepathy, radar, and clairvoyance. Normally, it's only used for detection and telekinesis. But some ancient techniques allow you to weaponize your soul power directly, attacking a foe's mind without any physical vector. For Ji Ning, whose soul is already freakishly powerful thanks to the Nuwa Diagram, this is the missing piece to turn his passive defense into an invisible, lethal weapon. In Xianxia lore, a cultivator who masters soul attacks is terrifying because they can kill without lifting a finger.

**The Immortal Puppet Tradition**: Mu Northson's black dragon construct falls into the niche Xianxia field of puppet mastery (*jixie* or *kui lei*). Unlike Fiendgod body refiners who rely on their own flesh, or Ki refiners who throw spells, puppet masters pour their Yuan power and mental focus into mechanical constructs. A high-quality puppet is not just a weapon—it's a platform that can execute complex dragon-form techniques, effectively multiplying the user's combat power. Northson's ability to control one so expertly at age fourteen marks him as a once-in-a-generation talent.

## Reading Guide
**Ride the Lightning, Find a Friend**: This chapter nails the "band of young prodigies" feeling that makes Xianxia so addictive. Ji Ning meets Mu Northson, and their instant camaraderie—bonding over mutual talent and a quick apology—is pure serotonin. Watch how Northson's innocent excitement contrasts with the grim desperation of older cultivators. He's having *fun*.

**The Information Diet**: Pay close attention to the power display here. Ji Ning's water-fire lotus easily shrugging off a full saturation attack is the chapter's quiet flex. Meanwhile, the divine sense attack from the examiner is a major foreshadowing—this is the first hint that the Black-White College teaches techniques far beyond what Ji Ning has access to in the Ji Clan. Ning's obsession with learning it is a character signal: he's a practitioner who craves *understanding*, not just raw power.

**The Final Test Tease**: The examiners' subtle comments—especially the aunt's warning to her nephew—create a palpable sense of dread. If the lightning gauntlet was the warm-up, what horror is the "final screening"? The anticipation is deliberately painful. And the Donghe clan disciple's public elimination for cheating is a classic Xianxia favor: a reminder that in this world, pedigree means nothing if you can't back it up with your own hands.
