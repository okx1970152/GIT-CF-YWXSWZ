## Chapter Overview

The *Immortal Fortunes Assembly* isn't just a tournament—it's a **cosmic homing beacon**. In this chapter, we skip across the vastness of the Grand Xia Dynasty to witness a phenomenon that defines Xianxia at its most sublime: the Dao itself reaching out to nudge its chosen children. From a seafaring demon of the North Sea to a grimy, nine-times-reincarnated drunkard in a tavern, powerful cultivators who previously had zero interest in the Assembly suddenly feel an **irresistible, instinctive pull** towards the imperial capital. The chapter masterfully shifts POVs to showcase three such wayward geniuses—the Golden Crow Immortal and his elusive true body, a mysterious black-robed wanderer, and the despairing, wine-soaked girl—before reuniting us with Ji Ning, who finally reunites with his Black-White College family as they all converge on the same destiny. Get ready, fellow readers—the board is being set for a war of monsters.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Golden Crow Immortal’s True Body Speaks:** We learn the Golden Crow Immortal (a famous North Sea power) is actually the *second true body* of a man named Bu Yi. His true body has remained hidden, a Wanxiang Adept waiting for his moment. Now, that moment has arrived via a divine premonition tied to the Assembly.
- **Bu Yi’s Origin Story:** A classic legacy trope unfolds: Bu Yi, a fisherman’s son, survived a shipwreck as a child and stumbled into an underwater immortal’s cave, gaining a massive inheritance including a Golden Crow Egg. He split his soul and possessed it, but the incompatibility makes a Celestial Immortal breakthrough for his second body impossible. His *true body*, however, has never been tested.
- **The Wayward Wanderer Senses the Call:** A black-robed man, already a disciple of a powerful master and traveling the worlds, suddenly feels a premonition that he *must* attend this Assembly or lose something vital. He immediately warps through space toward the capital.
- **The Drunken Girl’s Ninth Chance:** A grimy, penniless girl in a tavern who resorts to scams for wine receives the same call. We learn she is a nine-times reincarnated cultivator—this is her absolute last chance to break through before her soul is spent. She vanishes, her purpose finally clear.
- **Reunion at the Yan Estate:** Returning to Ji Ning’s perspective, he is delighted to find Mu Northson, Cangjiang, Yu Wei, and the Slackerly Realperson waiting for him. Yu Wei and the Slackerly Realperson have both decided to participate in the Assembly, unexpectedly forming a three-person Black-White College team.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Tyranny of Compatibility (契合度):** Bu Yi’s core problem is a perfect textbook example of a brutal Xianxia rule: **soul-body compatibility**. You can’t just shove any soul into any body and call it a day. When Bu Yi’s soul (human) possessed the Golden Crow egg (divine beast Yao), he created a powerful hybrid, but the dissonance is permanent. For a pure-blooded Golden Crow, a Celestial Immortal Tribulation is already insanely dangerous. With a 70-80% mismatch, the Tribulation senses the “flaw” and becomes near-impossible to survive. This is why cultivators prize natural-born talent and fear crippling their foundations with shortcuts.

**Reincarnation & The Nine-Life Limit (九世轮回):** The grimy girl mentions she has reincarnated nine times and that this is her final hope. In many Xianxia systems, reincarnation isn’t free—it degrades the soul’s “original spark” (真灵, Truesoul). Each life chips away at one's potential. Nine times is often the absolute limit; after that, the soul is too weak to form a new life or even be reborn. This girl is running on her very last tank of gas, giving her a tragic, desperate edge that makes her sudden divine purpose all the more powerful.

**“A Feeling from the Void” (冥冥中的感应):** This is one of the most beloved tropes in Xianxia—the “Heavenly Dao Intuition.” It’s not a logical deduction; it’s the universe itself transmitting a message directly to a cultivator’s soul. The higher your cultivation and the stronger your Dao-heart, the clearer these signals become. When a character feels this pull, they **always** follow it, because in this setting, the Dao is never wrong. It’s the author’s way of saying, “This character is fated to be here, and this is the most important thing they’ll ever do.”

### Cousin-brother (表弟/表姐)

In Chinese family hierarchy, a 'cousin-brother' or 'cousin-sister' denotes a maternal relationship, distinct from a paternal cousin.

## Reading Guide

This is a **setup chapter**, and it’s glorious. If you’ve been reading *Desolate Era* for a while, you know a tournament arc is coming, but IET (the author) knows better than to just jump into the fights. Instead, he takes a whole chapter to build *hype*. He introduces three new, incredibly powerful competitors by giving us just enough of their backstories to make them feel real. The subtext here is delicious: Ji Ning is the most monstrous genius in his prefecture, but he’s about to step onto a stage where guys like a hidden master’s disciple and a nine-life veteran are the *entry-level* competition. Pay close attention to Bu Yi and the drunken girl—both have very specific, very desperate motivations, which makes them far more dangerous than a simple glory-seeker. Also, take note of how Yu Wei and the Slackerly Realperson both independently felt the call. The Dao is gathering its pieces, and that means the storm is about to break.
