## Chapter Overview
The pressure is suffocating. The Immortal Fortunes Assembly has reached its final, most brutal crucible: six Wanxiang Adepts, one hour of enforced stillness, and a clash that will decide who earns the right to even *try* for Daofather Crimsonbright’s personal tutelage. The Grand Xia Emperor, that master of psychological warfare, doesn’t just throw them into the arena—he makes them sit with their own ambition and fear for sixty agonizing minutes. And when the matches are finally announced, the pairings are so perfectly matched they feel like destiny: raw power vs. raw power, the unconventional against the eccentric, and two deceptively simple men who must now break each other. Get ready, cultivators—this is where legends are made and shattered.

## Key Plot Points
*   The Grand Xia Emperor announces the final six: Ji Ning, Xiamang Zishan, Cangwu Jiu, Slacker Daoist, Mu Chuan, and Blackstone Recluse. The stakes are absolute—one will become Daofather Crimsonbright’s disciple.
*   The nine Pure Yang True Immortals watch from above, discussing which candidate carries the most potential. They all have their favorites, but the only consensus is that the Slacker Daoist, already a disciple of the Black Tortoise Grand Emperor, is off the table for the Daofather.
*   The Emperor orders a one-hour meditation period, letting the invisible pressure of the coming battle grind against the cultivators’ Dao-hearts as a final tempering.
*   The matchups are announced: Slacker Daoist vs. Mu Chuan, Blackstone Recluse vs. Cangwu Jiu, and the explosive finale of Ji Ning vs. Xiamang Zishan. The pairings pit style against direct counter-style.
*   The first match begins. Mu Chuan, the seemingly simple giant with hammers, reveals a shocking hidden card—he has been cultivating **two** Grand Daos simultaneously, the Grand Dao of Verdant Wood *and* the Grand Dao of Source Water. His defensive world instantly becomes more fluid and lethal, stunning even the watching Immortals.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Divine Farmer & Renhuang:** When the Immortals speculate about Blackstone Recluse’s mysterious origins, they mention ‘Divine Farmer’ (神农氏, Shennong), also known as Yan Emperor. In Chinese myth, he is a legendary culture hero and deity who taught humanity agriculture and herbal medicine. In the Desolate Era cosmology, he is elevated to a primordial major power from the age of Pangu, a supreme Daofather with terrifying heritage.

**Fuxi & the Staff Formation:** Blackstone Recluse’s signature technique is tied to ‘Fuxi’ (伏羲), the serpent-bodied first of the Three Sovereigns in Chinese mythology. Fuxi is credited with inventing fishing, trapping, and the Eight Trigrams (八卦). His name attached to a formation technique implies a link to ancient, primal knowledge, making Blackstone a potential inheritor of a truly legendary bloodline.

**Walking Two Grand Daos:** In standard Xianxia cultivation, a single Grand Dao is already the lifelong pursuit of most geniuses. Mastering two simultaneously—especially complementary ones like Wood and Water—is considered freakishly talented and strategically devastating. Wood needs Water to grow; Water needs Wood to channel force. Mu Chuan’s reveal is the equivalent of a hidden powerhouse dropping a second ace from his sleeve.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in **pre-combat tension**. The one-hour wait isn’t filler—it’s a psychological weapon. If you’ve ever wondered how the best Xianxia writers make a tournament arc sing, this is it: give your fighters time to think, and their nerves will do half the work for you.

Keep your eyes on the matchups, because the author is doing something beautiful here. He’s not just randomizing seeds; he’s pairing thematic opposites. The Slacker Daoist (defensive turtle) vs. Mu Chuan (defensive tree). Ji Ning (surgical precision) vs. Xiamang Zishan (overwhelming force). The clash of *philosophies* is as important as the clash of fists.

And don’t sleep on Mu Chuan. The humble, simple cultivator who’s been hiding a dual-Dao mastery for *three hundred years* is a classic Xianxia trope—the unassuming monster. His reveal sets the bar astronomically high for the fights to come. If he’s this strong, what else are the others hiding?
