## Chapter Overview

Holy crap, folks! This is one of those chapters that makes the entire journey worth it. Ji Ning hasn't even officially unpacked his bags at the Black-White College, and he's already stumbled into a legendary inheritance that has the college's top Immortals scrambling like kids at a dessert table. The short old man (the crazed Wufeng, fresh off surviving his nine-hundred-year tribulation) spots a beast-pelt-clad youth—our boy Ning—resonating with the stone-carved words of a figure who makes even Celestial Immortals nervous: Immortal Beixing, the original owner of the legendary "Three-Foot Sword." What follows is a cross-generational transmission of the complete sword art, triggering a reunion of five top immortals, including the famously picky Diancai (who's never taken a disciple), the majestic tall-crowned elder, the icy child-like ancient, and the chain-wrapped giant. And the best part? Ji Ning's backstory gets summarized in a single awe-struck monologue by College Lord Bihai, confirming that yes, this kid really is that monstrous. The chapter ends with Diancai claiming Ning as his sole disciple—a huge power-up moment for our hero.

## Key Plot Points

- The short old man Wufeng, having just survived the nine-hundred-year tribulation, spots Ji Ning resonating with Beixing's sword-intent on the stone wall. He immediately summons his junior brother Diancai.
- Diancai arrives and reveals he himself received Beixing's inheritance back in his youth—and that he has never taken a disciple because his condition is that only someone who can inherit Beixing's sword-intent is worthy.
- Five top immortals of the Black-White College (Wufeng, Diancai, the tall-crowned elder, the child-like ancient, the chain-bound giant) plus College Lord Bihai gather to witness the transmission.
- The stone wall's characters explode into glowing sword-shadow characters that fly into Ji Ning's eyes, transmitting the complete Three-Foot Sword technique. This marks a historic moment—the last complete transmission was thirty million years ago.
- The immortals discuss the significance: the Three-Foot Sword's final three stances cannot be recorded in text; they require pure sword-intent inheritance. Ji Ning has a "sword-immortal heart," pure Dao-heart, and strong soul—essential conditions.
- Diancai asks Bihai for Ji Ning's background. Bihai delivers a concise summary: Ji Ning from Yan Mountains, killed Beizi Shan at 11, caused the Snowdragon Mountain massacre, reached the Domain of the Dao, etc.
- Diancai formally claims Ji Ning as his disciple. The other immortals yield, acknowledging that Diancai's deep understanding of Beixing's techniques makes him the best teacher.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**"Cross-Generational Inheritance" (隔代传授):** In Chinese xianxia, this refers to a master passing down their legacy to a disciple across time—often centuries or millennia after the master's death. The technique is sealed in an artifact, inscription, or stone wall, waiting for the right soul to resonate with it. It's a trope that instantly upgrades a protagonist's power ceiling without a "gifted-from-above" feel; the disciple earns it through spiritual compatibility. Here, Immortal Beixing's sword-intent is so profound that his final three stances can only be transmitted through pure spiritual resonance—not through books or diagrams.

**"Sword-Immortal Heart" (剑仙之心):** This isn't just being good with a sword. In Desolate Era's metaphysics, a sword-immortal heart is a spiritual awakening—a fundamental recognition of one's path as a swordsman. It combines conviction (道心, Dao-heart), talent (天赋), and a soul quality that aligns with the sharp, decisive nature of the Sword Dao. It's the prerequisite for eventually comprehending the "Grand Dao of the Sword" (剑道), one of the supreme metaphysical laws.

**"Three-Foot Sword" (三尺剑):** The name itself is a classical Chinese reference—a "three-foot sword" is the standard length of a traditional Chinese jian (double-edged straight sword). In martial and literary tradition, it symbolizes the scholar-swordsman ideal: a weapon of justice, not brute force. Beixing's philosophy—"slaying all that is unjust"—embodies this righteous killer archetype. The final three stances being untransmittable by text reflects the Daoist idea that the highest truths must be "passed from heart to heart" (以心传心).

**Immortal Beixing as a Subversion:** Note that Beixing's legend surpasses even the college's only Celestial Immortal. This establishes that raw cultivation realm isn't everything—a cultivator with supreme technique and Dao-heart can exceed their nominal tier. This is a recurring theme in xianxia, where "monster geniuses" break the rules.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a classic "hidden inheritance" payoff, but with a twist. Ji Ning didn't seek it—he happened to stand in the right place at the right time after surviving the college entrance trial. That randomness feels earned because his earlier actions (Domain of the Dao, massacring Snowdragon Mountain) already proved he's worthy. The real treat is watching the immortals geek out. Wufeng's "get your ass over here" transmission, Diancai's stone-cold-facade cracking into joy, the five seniors bickering over who gets the kid—it's all delicious payoff for readers who've followed Ning's grind. Also, Bihai's dossiér-style recap (killing Beizi Shan at 11, the Primal Stone Vein incident, Domain of the Dao reveal) serves as a wonderful mid-series power-resume that makes you feel just how far Ning has come. And the conclusion—Diancai's monopoly—is a perfect "teacher unlocked" beat that sets up a whole new arc of sword training and Immortal-level backing.
