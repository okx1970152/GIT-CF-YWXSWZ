## Chapter Overview
This chapter serves as the emotional payoff for Ji Ning's harrowing adventures in the Eastmarsh Swamp and the ancient water palace. Returning to his parents, he unveils the gruesome corpse of the Winged Serpent—an enemy that had plagued the Ji Clan for years. But that's just the beginning: in rapid succession, he reveals his breakthrough to the Xiantian realm, his comprehension of a True Meaning of the Dao, the slaying of the Ironwood Clan's Tie Muzhan, and a trove of treasures including the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation and a priceless visualization technique. The chapter masterfully balances familial warmth—Ji Ning's guilt over worrying his parents, his mother's tender touch—with the jaw-dropping display of a child prodigy whose abilities have already surpassed those of veterans.

## Key Plot Points
1. Ji Ning presents the Winged Serpent corpse; his parents are stunned.
2. He recounts the encounter with Tie Muzhan and the Skygreen Serpent King's escape via its innate Void Shuttle ability, showing Ning's grudging respect for the serpent.
3. Ji Ning describes his night of Dao comprehension and subsequent breakthrough to Xiantian, during which he killed both the Huan Beast and Tie Muzhan.
4. He demonstrates the True Meaning of Dripping through his swordplay; Ji Yichuan confirms that even most Zifu Disciples cannot reach this level.
5. Ji Ning explains that he attained the True Meaning within a secret realm (the aquatic palace), and his parents realize why he had disappeared for so long.
6. He presents the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation manual; both parents immediately recognize its superiority over any Ji Clan technique, comparable to the Yuchi Clan's Windwing Evasion.
7. Ji Ning hands over thousands of ungraded magic treasures; the sheer quantity stuns his parents and hints at the wealth of the ancient palace.
8. Finally, he reveals the Inner View of the Sun-Moon Buddha of Light—a visualization technique. Yuchi Snow recalls her own clan's lost scroll and praises her son's fortune.
9. Ji Yichuan makes strategic decisions: the visualization technique will be shared clan-wide immediately, the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation kept secret for now, and the treasures handled discreetly.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **True Meaning of the Dao (道之真意):** This is the cultivation level above Unity of Man and Heaven. It represents a deep, intuitive grasp of a specific aspect of the Dao—here, the 'dripping' essence of water. Most cultivators at the Zifu realm have not reached this stage; a child achieving it at age eleven is extraordinary. Even Ji Yichuan, who possesses the same True Meaning, could not kill the Winged Serpent without the right tools, underscoring that insight alone isn't enough—power manifestations and treasures matter.
- **Lesser Thousand Sword Formation (小千剑阵):** A high-level sword array that coordinates multiple flying swords into an overwhelming offense. Its requirements—hundreds of quality swords and a powerful soul—make it impractical for most. This is the first major combat inheritance Ji Ning receives, foreshadowing his signature style of sword-formation dominance.
- **Visualization Technique (观想之法):** In Xianxia, the soul is the root of all being. Strengthening it through mental imagery (e.g., a Buddha radiating sun-moon light) guards against mental attacks and tribulations. Techniques like the Nuwa Diagram or the Inner View of the Sun-Moon Buddha can be transferred directly into the soul by immortals, making them more powerful than physical scrolls. The Yuchi Clan's loss of such a scroll in war hints at the brutal cost of clan conflicts.
- **Crimsonbright Diagram of the Nine Heavens (赤明九天图):** The premier Fiendgod Body Refining method in the Three Realms. Reaching Xiantian via this method involves a cosmic spectacle of Sunstar True Fire and Taiyin True Water descending to forge the cultivator's body. Ji Ning's success here, on top of his Dao comprehension, marks him as a once-in-an-era prodigy.

## Reading Guide
This is a quintessential "return and report" chapter that lets both the characters and the reader savor the fruits of past trials. Notice how Ji Ning downplays his accomplishments—he presents the Winged Serpent corpse almost as an afterthought, then builds up to the real treasures. His parents' reactions range from disbelief to pride to strategic calculation. The chapter also deepens character bonds: Ji Ning's guilt over worrying his parents shows his emotional maturity, while Ji Yichuan's cold exterior cracks as he praises his son. Decision points here—what to share with the clan, what to keep secret—set the stage for future political tensions. The sheer magnitude of the gains (thousands of treasures, a soul-tempering technique) signals that Ji Ning's trajectory is now far beyond the Yan Mountains. Keep an eye on how the clan will react when they learn of the visualization technique—this could shake the internal power balance.
