## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, gather round. The pot has finally boiled over. After chapters of mounting pressure, the mask of civility has been torn away. In this chapter, Snowdragon Mountain’s Zifu Disciples drop all pretense of negotiation and openly threaten the Ji Clan with genocide. But our young Ji Ning isn't just a spectator anymore—he's the very target they fear. When a Zifu sorcerer’s ultimate technique is shattered by a sixteen-year-old, you know the power dynamics are shifting. The only problem? Power isn't about who *can* win a fight—it's about who can survive the political fallout. Get ready for a masterclass in Xianxia realpolitik as the Ji Clan makes a decision that will set the stage for everything to come.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Dongzi Qi reeling from the shock of Ji Ning’s feat. He had come expecting to cow a backward tribe with his superior force, but Ji Ning’s raw display of power has thrown his calculations into chaos. The Zifu Disciple Musi, arrogant just moments ago, is left in stunned silence after his blood sorcery was annihilated by Ji Ning’s sublime technique.

Inside the Ji Clan camp, the mood is a volatile mix of pride and terror. Ji Yichuan checks on his son with a rare display of visible relief, while Granny Shadow launches a blistering verbal attack on Snowdragon Mountain’s treachery. Dongzi Qi, however, masterfully twists the narrative, claiming it was merely a “friendly spar,” daring the Ji Clan to retaliate against his overwhelming force.

The tension reaches a boiling point when Ji Liuzhen and several hot-headed warriors cry out for a desperate, suicidal charge. But Ji Jiuhuo, the ancient chieftain, slams down his authority. Through private transmissions, he lays out the brutal reality: a direct fight would probably kill half of Snowdragon Mountain’s team, but it would destroy the Ji Clan in the process. The plan to leverage a Grand Xia contract is shot down as a pipe dream—it takes too long and only delays the inevitable.

In the end, Ji Jiuhuo delivers the crushing verdict. The Ji Clan will surrender their Vassal Token, abandon their ancestral home, and beg for a new plot of land. The humiliation is absolute. But as the chapter closes, all eyes turn to Ji Ning. He is no longer just a prodigy; he is the sole hope for the clan’s redemption. The weight of an entire tribe now rests on his shoulders.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### Delegated Awe and the Power of Reputation
This chapter is a textbook example of a classic Xianxia trope: **Delegated Awe** (代位敬畏). Notice how we don’t see a single flashy sword technique from the highest Ji Clan elders. We don’t need to. All the *narrative* power is generated through the reactions of the Zifu Disciples themselves. When a cold, calculating killer like Dongzi Qi stops thinking about crushing an ant and starts calculating how many comrades he might lose, the author has built Ji Ning’s threat level without a single punch. This is how Xianxia conveys overwhelming power—not just by showing it, but by showing the fear it inspires in the powerful.

### The Politics of the Yuan Stone Mine
The “Yuan Stone mine” is the McGuffin that drives this entire conflict. In the Grand Xia Dynasty, natural treasures don't just make you rich—they make you a target. The Xianxia rule of thumb is simple: if you can’t protect it, you don’t deserve it. The Ji Clan’s decision to surrender everything is a pragmatic, if soul-crushing, choice. Attempting to contract with the Grand Xia would be like calling the police on a mafia hitman—it might create a truce, but it won’t stop the vendetta. This is the harsh reality of cultivator politics: mercy and law only extend as far as your own sword.

### The Burden of the Genius
The closing scene of the chapter is a core pillar of the “Progression Fantasy” genre. The clan’s hope isn’t placed in any of the current Zifu elders; it’s placed entirely in the child, Ji Ning. This isn’t just emotional—it’s a structural statement. The old generation has failed. The system has failed. The only way forward is for the young protagonist to *break* the ceiling and rewrite the rules through sheer, overwhelming power.

### Delegated Awe

A literary device in Xianxia where the power of a character is conveyed through the awed reactions of their enemies, establishing threat level without direct demonstration.

## Reading Guide

Alright, brace yourselves, because this is the chapter where the saga officially leaves the sandbox. The “tribal politics” game is over. Now it’s time for the big leagues. The Ji Clan’s decision to give up their home isn’t a loss—it’s a narrative crucible. Ji Ning has just been handed an anvil’s worth of motivation. The humiliation he feels here? That’s going to be the fuel for his cultivation fire for a long, long time.

Pay close attention to Dongzi Qi. He’s not a blundering brute; he’s a cynical, pragmatic administrator of terror. He knows exactly how far to push before the ants bite back. His inability to kill Ji Ning *right now* is the clearest sign yet that our protagonist is already a threat to be reckoned with.

Also, keep an eye on the term “Vassal Token.” It’s more than just a deed to a city. It’s the Ji Clan’s identity, their protection from the Grand Xia, and the very ground they stand on. Losing it is worse than losing a battle—it’s losing the map.
