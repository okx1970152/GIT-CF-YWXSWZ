## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists! After over thirty years of silence, Ji Ning has finally resurfaced at the Black-White College, and the ripples are felt across the Grand Xia. But this isn’t a triumphant homecoming. Our protagonist is immediately hit with a gut-wrenching blow: his sworn brother, Mu Northson, has vanished without a trace for over two decades. Worse, Northson’s Dao-companion is dead. As Ji Ning processes this, he also catches up with his old retainers—learning that time has been merciless, scattering his six followers to death, return, and quiet desperation. The chapter masterfully blends personal grief with political tension, reminding us that in the Xianxia world, even a genius’s return can be overshadowed by unresolved tragedy.

## Key Plot Points
- The Diancai Immortal reveals that Mu Northson took a Dao-companion (Realperson Yu Xia of the Thousand Rivers Sect) and later vanished with her over 20 years ago; her life tablet has shattered, but Northson’s remains intact.
- Despite investigations by the Black-White College, the Beishan clan, and Heavenly Treasures Mountain, no trace of Northson’s location has ever been found.
- Ji Ning is shaken, but his master reassures him: Northson is likely trapped somewhere, not dead. Still, the news leaves Ji Ning with a burning need to investigate.
- The Diancai Immortal announces he will soon undergo his tribulation, fueled by the three sword manuals Ji Ning gifted him—he feels the pressure of an unseen threat and cannot afford to wait.
- Ji Ning returns to Northmi Peak, where his six retainers (now much changed) await. Wang Wei (killed in training), Wei Fang (killed by his lover’s senior), and Ming Yang (gone home) are all absent. Only Meng Yan, Yun Lu, and Yun Zhou remain.
- Meng Yan has risen to become a formal Black-White College disciple under the Wufeng Immortal, while Yun Lu has endured a bitter Dao-companion marriage and now lives alone with her child.
- The news of Ji Ning’s return spreads like wildfire—over a thousand cultivators rush to report it, signaling how many powers across the Grand Xia are watching him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Life Tablet (命简):** A common trope in Xianxia, a life tablet is a soul-linked artifact kept in a clan or sect’s ancestral hall. It shatters automatically when its owner dies, providing instantaneous proof of death. That Northson’s tablet remains intact after 20+ years is a huge relief, but his Dao-companion’s shattered tablet confirms she encountered fatal danger. This is a classic Xianxia “cliffhanger hook”—the protagonist’s ally is alive but trapped, driving a future rescue arc.

**The Retainer System (从属/仆从):** In Xianxia, powerful cultivators often take on retainers—weaker cultivators who serve them in exchange for resources, protection, and access to better training. Ji Ning’s six retainers from his early days (Meng Yan, Yun Lu, Yun Zhou, Wei Fang, Wang Wei, Ming Yang) were exactly this. The fate of such retainers is often a mirror of the protagonist’s own luck. Here, Meng Yan’s rise to formal disciple shows Ji Ning’s indirect influence, while Yun Lu’s tragic Dao-companion story highlights the harsh realities of sect politics and social climbing.

**Dao-companion (道侣):** More than just a spouse, a Dao-companion is a cultivation partner bound by a shared Dao-heart and mutual commitment. Their bond is spiritual as much as romantic. The tragic end of Northson’s Dao-companion (killed immediately) while he survives trapped is a classic Xianxia set-up: it plants the seed of a future revenge or rescue mission, and it shows that even in hidden realms, death is never far from romance.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in “tranquil dread.” Ji Ning’s return isn’t a victory lap—it’s a cold shower of bad news. The author uses a quiet, almost domestic setting (the feast with his retainers, the walk with his master) to deliver devastating emotional blows. Readers should pay close attention to the Diancai Immortal’s mention of a “vague threatening pressure”—this is a classic Xianxia sign that a major calamity is brewing in the background. Meanwhile, the rapid spread of Ji Ning’s return across the Grand Xia isn’t just a political note; it’s a ticking clock. His enemies know he’s back. The stage is set for a storm.
