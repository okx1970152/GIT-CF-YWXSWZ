## Chapter Overview
Get ready, because the pressure is stacking up fast! This chapter is a masterclass in escalating stakes. We’ve moved from a few harassing Celestial Immortals to a full-blown, nine-Immortal deployment, all because Ji Ning has his hands wrapped around a pillar that the Shaoyin clan absolutely cannot afford to lose. The deep lore drops about the Shaoyin clan’s founder are worth the price of admission alone. Ji Ning is facing down ancient monsters, a mysterious horned overlord, and an entire clan’s panic—and he’s doing it with a grin.

## Key Plot Points
The horned, golden-robed figure reveals himself as the Shaoyin clan’s ultimate trump card. He’s flanked by the Heavenly Mystery Celestial Immortal and two other unnamed Immortals, instantly upping the enemy count to an intimidating eight.
The boss unleashes his ultimate weapon—not a spell, but three monstrous, ten-thousand-zhong creatures from his mouth: a blade-legged insect, a winged rhinoceros, and a crown-patterned snake. Each is a top-tier Celestial Immortal combatant.
Despite the overwhelming force, Ji Ning continues to pull the copper pillar upwards. A mysterious, ancient force from beneath the earth is now helping him, almost matching his strength.
The chapter cuts to a Seamless Gate outpost, where the Blackwind Celestial Immortal and his companion watch the spectacle. This provides crucial exposition on the Shaoyin clan’s founder, Shaoyin Clown (Poison-Clown Celestial Immortal), revealing him as a tricky, nearly unkillable being with dozens of clones and a gift for raising terrifying poison insects.
The chapter ends on a tense ultimatum: the horned figure directly threatens Ji Ning, showing the clan’s desperation now that their most sacred treasure is at risk.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a fantastic example of the ‘Hidden Master’ trope in Xianxia. The person you thought was the boss (the Zixuan Patriarch) is just a servant for the *real* boss. This horned man, the Shaoyin clan founder, is a masterclass in this. He’s a Celestial Immortal who commands the respect of other Celestial Immortals due to his unconventional power—poison insects and multiple clones. This challenges the rigid hierarchy, showing that ‘genius’ and ‘talent’ aren’t the only paths to becoming a force to be reckoned with. In Xianxia, a cultivator with a unique, unorthodox niche (like the Blackwind Celestial Immortal or Poison-Clown) can be more dangerous than a generic powerhouse. Also, the practice of keeping multiple clones, as mentioned here, is a classic survival technique in Xianxia. Killing a single body is meaningless; you have to find and destroy them all to truly end the threat. It’s the ultimate insurance policy against a world where assassination is a common solution.

## Reading Guide
Keep your eyes peeled for the giant, monstrous bugs. The fact that the founder can spit them out and they grow to 30,000 zhang is a very Chinese Xianxia way of showing how powerful someone is—not with a huge explosion of magical energy, but with the sheer, grotesque scale of their minions. The Seamless Gate scene isn’t just an info-dump; it’s the story telling you that the founder is a key player in a much bigger game, someone even the Seamless Gate wants to recruit. Pay close attention to the phrase ‘touching my furnace.’ The fact that an entire clan’s leadership is mobilized for a *furnace* is a huge clue that this isn’t just a relic; it’s the foundation of their power. This chapter sets up a classic Xianxia confrontation: a lone hero against an ancient, multi-faceted power, with a mysterious artifact as the centerpiece.
