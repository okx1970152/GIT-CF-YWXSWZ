## Chapter Overview
This chapter is the cathartic, long-awaited payoff for one of the deepest emotional wounds in Ji Ning’s life. Yu Tong, the architect of the ambush that killed his father and mother, is cornered. What follows isn’t a quick execution—it’s a methodical, psychological dismantling. Ji Ning doesn’t just want Yu Tong dead; he wants him to *understand* why he’s dying. The chapter masterfully contrasts Yu Tong’s frantic self-preservation with Ji Ning’s cold, premeditated vengeance, culminating in a chilling use of the Heart-Scorching Art. This is Xianxia revenge served ice-cold, with a side of savage satisfaction.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning reveals his identity to Yu Tong as the child of the pregnant woman from that long-ago ambush, shattering Yu Tong’s desperate pleas of mistaken identity.
- Yu Tong, realizing he can’t win in a fight or talk his way out, orders his brainwashed junior apprentice-sister Yue Wei to martyr herself via self-detonation to buy him an escape window.
- Yue Wei obeys without hesitation, detonating her Zifu Lake while tearfully professing love for Yu Tong—a chilling showcase of his long-term mental enslavement.
- Yu Tong uses the chaos of the explosion and his poisonous insect swarm to flee, but Ji Ning annihilates the bugs with his Water-Fire Lotus and pursues him through a maze formation, which his divine sense renders useless.
- Cornered and outmatched at every turn, Yu Tong realizes Ji Ning has locked the spatial void, disabled his escape talismans, and shattered his last line of defense—his master, Crippled Moon Realperson, is already dead.
- Ji Ning destroys Yu Tong’s Zifu and dantian not to kill him, but to prevent him from committing suicide, then delivers the ultimate psychological blow: he has Uncle White transform into his original beast form to trigger Yu Tong’s memory of the original massacre.
- The chapter ends with Ji Ning finally unleashing the Heart-Scorching Art on a helpless Yu Tong, confirming their identities and revealing the depth of the familial debt being repaid.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Heart-Scorching Art (焚心术)** — This technique is a perfect example of a classic Xianxia trope: the torture method that is almost useless in combat but devastatingly effective when you have a helpless, defeated enemy. It inflicts purely physical torment by twisting and burning the victim from the inside out, but it doesn’t disrupt cultivation or provide any tactical advantage. The fact that it was stored in the College’s freely accessible library, rather than behind a paywall of contribution points, says a lot about how little value the Black-White College placed on it. To Ji Ning, however, it was a treasure—a tool of poetic justice he had been saving for this very moment.

**Tamed Loyalty vs. True Love (驯服 vs. 真爱)** — Yue Wei’s self-sacrifice is a stark illustration of a darker Xianxia concept: the complete domination of a victim’s heart and mind. Yu Tong didn’t just order her to die; he had spent years molding her psyche so thoroughly that she *believed* her loyalty was love. This is a recurring dark undercurrent in the genre—the idea that a cultivator’s methods for creating absolute loyalty can be more horrifying than any physical weapon. Ji Ning’s reaction is telling: he doesn’t mourn her, but he recognizes the brutality of her enslavement.

## Reading Guide
This is a *satisfying* chapter, and you should savor every moment of it. Pay close attention to how Ji Ning methodically counters every single escape option Yu Tong has: formations, insects, teleportation, suicide, and even Yu Tong’s numerical advantage via his master. This is the mark of a cultivator who has been planning this for years, not acting on rage alone.

Also, watch Uncle White’s role. He doesn’t fight. He waits. When Ji Ning finally asks him to transform, it’s the single most devastating blow of the entire chapter—a final, irrefutable piece of evidence that breaks Yu Tong’s spirit completely. This chapter proves that sometimes the most powerful weapon in a cultivator’s arsenal is memory.

And finally, reflect on Yue Wei. Her fate is a grim reminder that in the world of Xianxia, not every death is a heroic sacrifice—some are merely the final, tragic consequence of having your will utterly broken.
