## Chapter Overview
The final month of the Brightmoon Scenery erupts into savage, desperate warfare. When Yu Wei is struck down by a deadly poison from the imperial genius Xiamang Qi, Ji Ning’s Dao Heart shatters under the weight of fear and rage. His unleashed fury triggers a breakthrough in the Three-Foot Sword—the seventh stance, transforming his sword-light into silken strands of pure annihilation. In a decisive surge of power, he carves through Xiamang Qi and Feixue Realperson in a single breath, scattering the remaining competitors like startled birds. The chapter ends not in further bloodshed, but in a raw, long-awaited embrace between Ji Ning and Yu Wei, as he finally sheds his hesitation and admits the fear of losing her.

## Key Plot Points
1. Yu Wei is gravely wounded by Xiamang Qi’s Ten Thousand Venoms Stinger—a grievous, poisonous strike that leaves her depleted and coughing black blood.
2. Ji Ning, witnessing her collapse, undergoes a violent emotional and spiritual ignition: his killing intent floods his entire soul, triggering a threshold breakthrough in the Three-Foot Sword.
3. He executes the seventh stance—the Word-Sword Chop—transforming his six sword beams into razor-thin silk strands capable of cutting through Ninefate Recluse’s indestructible flesh.
4. Xiamang Qi tries to intercept but is overwhelmed: Ji Ning shatters his weapon and chops him to pieces, with a Water-Fire Lotus grinding his soul to oblivion.
5. Feixue Realperson attempts to flee, hurling her talismans, but Ji Ning catches and kills her before she can escape.
6. Zhuanshui Qi, Danzhu Realperson, Blackstone Recluse, and Ninefate Recluse all choose tactical retreat—recognizing that Ji Ning’s new power makes a direct confrontation too risky.
7. After the battle, Ji Ning collects spoils, hurries to Yu Wei’s side, and finally confesses his fear and love, holding her tightly in a cathartic embrace.
8. In the Tianshun Hall, Lu Dongbin laughs and approves of the long-overdue romance, breaking the tension with his characteristic revelry.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
“Sword-light silken strands” (剑光成丝) is a hallmark of profound mastery in Xianxia sword Dao. It represents compressing the vast, chaotic force of a giant sword-beam into an infinitely fine line—concentrating every ounce of killing intent into a microscopically thin edge. Like a candle flame focused through a lens, the thinner the strand, the more absolute its cutting power. Reaching this level is considered the threshold of a true Sword Immortal’s maturity; in the hierarchy of the Three Realms, only those who achieve this can be taken seriously by True Immortals and Daofathers.

The Ten Thousand Venoms Stinger (万毒蛰) is a divine ability cultivated by refining thousands of rare poisons directly into the user’s palm, turning any physical touch into a lethal injection. Its extreme toxicity makes it a perfect tool for Ki Refiners to bypass Fiendgod-level defenses—but it also poisons the user’s own body over time, a classic Xianxia trade-off.

The Three-Foot Sword (三尺剑) inheritance is unique: the first six stances are progressive teachings, designed to guide a student step-by-step to the Domain of the Dao. But the final three stances—impossible to write in any manual—represent exponential leaps in power. Each stance requires a breakthrough in insight and heart-force, not just technique practice.

## Reading Guide
This is the chapter where the Loose Immortal disciples and Celestial Immortals watching from the Tianshun Hall stop treating Ji Ning like a promising rookie and start talking about him as a potential First Place contender—even comparing him to Xiamang Zishan. That’s a massive power gap leap on the social ladder. For context: this is the same Assembly where the capital’s proudest geniuses draw their swords in arrogance, and now the kid from the Western Prefecture is making the Ninefate Recluse—known for her freakishly tough body—retreat in genuine fear. Read the moment Ji Ning howls in rage as his Sword Heart cracking open. That’s not just anger; it’s the sound of a cultivator shedding their last layer of restraint and finally admitting what they truly want.

Also, keep an eye on Lu Dongbin’s reaction at the end. His slap on the table and his laughter aren’t just comedic relief; they’re the reaction of an elder who has watched young geniuses waste their lives in hesitation. His approval carries weight—like a True Immortal applauding two warriors for finally taking a leap that matters more than any technique.
