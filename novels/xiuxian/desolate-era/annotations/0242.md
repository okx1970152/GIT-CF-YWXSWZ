## Chapter Overview
This chapter takes a sharp, emotional turn from the all-out action of the previous chapters. It is a quiet, heavy, and philosophical interlude focusing entirely on Nine Lotos. Upon being brought back to the ancestral home of the Dongyan clan, she is brought before the clan Ancestor—a figure of immense power and wisdom who raised her. Expecting a confrontation over her chosen Dao-companion, Ji Ning, Nine Lotos finds herself not punished, but dismantled instead. The Ancestor does not forbid her relationship outright; he challenges the very foundation of her understanding of what it means to have a Dao-companion. It’s a masterclass in Xianxia emotional stakes, forcing both the character and the reader to question the depth of a young love that, until now, felt like a fairytale. The chapter ends with a haunting, timeless sorrow from the Ancestor, revealing a heart that has known legendary love and devastating loss. Get ready, Daoists, for a knife to the heart that isn’t made of steel.

## Key Plot Points
*   **Homecoming and Judgment:** Nine Lotos returns to the Dongyan Mountains with Immortal Northom, fully aware that she is on trial for her choice to bind herself to Ji Ning.
*   **The Ancestor’s Challenge:** The clan Ancestor doesn’t rage or forbid. Instead, he asks a simple, crushing question: “Do you understand what a Dao-companion truly is?” He picks apart Nine Lotos’ idealized teenage concept of love.
*   **The Definition of ‘Dao-companion’:** The Ancestor lays out the brutal, sacred definition: a Dao-companion is someone you would die for, someone you would fight gods for, and someone whose absence would leave your very soul incomplete. He sees hesitation in Nine Lotos’ eyes.
*   **The Trial of Time:** Instead of a direct ban, the Ancestor imposes patience. He grounds Nine Lotos in the Ten Thousand Lotus Cave, a trial of the heart, and sets her freedom condition: she can only leave when she becomes a Primal Daoist. If her feelings for Ji Ning survive that long journey, he will support her.
*   **A Ghost of the Past:** After Nine Lotos leaves, the Ancestor’s composed facade cracks. He mutters the name “Ruyin,” revealing that Nine Lotos’ appearance reminds him of a Dao-companion he lost in a cataclysmic war long ago, explaining the weight of his words.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Sacred Weight of a ‘Dao-companion’ (道侣):**
Western fantasy often uses terms like “lover,” “spouse,” or “significant other.” In Xianxia, *Dao-companion* is a concept of entirely different gravitas. It is not a mere relationship; it is a spiritual pact forged on the path of defying heaven itself. The Ancestor’s lecture here is not just tough love; it is a core philosophical statement of the genre. A Dao-companion is a shared cultivator of the Dao. They are meant to weather the Three Calamities and Nine Tribulations together. The Ancestor’s three-pronged test—the willingness to die, to fight, and to remain whole—is a perfect encapsulation of the extreme, life-and-death loyalty expected in this world. A half-hearted partnership is worse than none, as a wavering heart can become a fatal flaw, a heart-demon ready to be exploited during a tribulation.

**The Silent Tyranny of Clan Hierarchy:**
Even the most talented, strong-willed heir of a major clan is ultimately bound by its Ancestor. This chapter shows the immense social gravity of a clan. Nine Lotos, a prodigy and the chosen future leader, is powerless before the Ancestor’s calm, fatherly authority. This is a classic Xianxia dynamic: the clan nurtures, protects, and empowers, but it also commands absolute obedience. The Ancestor doesn’t need to chain Nine Lotos; his word, backed by millennia of power and wisdom, is a law she instinctively bends to. This tension between individual will and clan duty is a driving force in many of the greatest Xianxia stories.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is less about action and more about emotional and philosophical excavation. Pay close attention to the Ancestor’s tone. He is not angry; he is weary and wise. His sadness is a product of his own history, which is heavily foreshadowed at the very end. The name “Ruyin” is a huge lore-drop. It ties the current, peaceful scene to a cataclysmic past war—the ‘Celestial Light Major World’ campaign—suggesting the Ancestor has already lived through a story of loss that Ji Ning and Nine Lotos are only beginning to write. This chapter lays the foundation for a major long-term character development arc for Nine Lotos. Her cultivation is about to take a backseat to the real work of hardening her soul. For Ji Ning, this means his path may become lonelier, or it may create a stronger, more resilient partner in the future. The straight hook the Ancestor fishes with is a classic Daoist symbol of purpose without attachment—he is not fishing for fish, just as he is not clinging to the outcome of this young love.
