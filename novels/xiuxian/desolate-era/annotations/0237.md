## Chapter Overview

Ah, the classic love rival has appeared! And not just any rival—this is Chen Jin, the childhood friend who followed Nine Lotos all the way from the Upper River Commandery to Anchan City, abandoning his own tribal position just to be near her. The moment he spots Ji Ning and Nine Lotos in their quiet, intimate repose on Serpentwing Lake, the fuse is lit. What follows is a masterclass in Xianxia romantic conflict: no subtle dinner table conversations, no misunderstood glances—just Chen Jin tearing into the sky with a hundred and eight golden plates of power, reshaping the clouds into divine beasts, and throwing down an earth-shaking challenge. Ji Ning, ever the no-nonsense pragmatist, simply tells Nine Lotos, “Leave this to me,” and accepts. Forget the gossip; this is a fight where Dao-hearts and pride are on the line, and the wind over Serpentwing Lake is about to get very, very sharp.

## Key Plot Points

Chen Jin arrives at Serpentwing Lake with Flamesaint, the top disciple of the Black-White College, having learned of Ji Ning’s location through Qingqing. Seeing Ji Ning and Nine Lotos together, he loses his composure and tries to charge down, but Flamesaint restrains him, advising calm. On the boat, Nine Lotos immediately senses trouble and warns Ji Ning to be wary of Chen Jin, though Ji Ning is initially confused about the hostility.

Chen Jin confronts Nine Lotos directly, demanding an answer. Nine Lotos, with a sigh, tells him to give up. The rejection shatters Chen Jin’s last hope, and his jealousy turns to pure rage. He accuses Nine Lotos of changing her heart and points at Ji Ning, declaring he will prove to her how “pitifully weak” her new choice is.

Chen Jin unleashes his technique: a hundred and eight golden plates fly into the sky, drawing in massive clouds that transform into a hundred and eight distinct cloud-beasts—lions, chariots, divine dragons—surrounding the entire lake. The display is a clear declaration of war. On Brightheart Island, the Ji Clan elders and Qiuye watch in alarm; the Whitewater Hound identifies both Flamesaint and Chen Jin, noting that the latter is a true elite of the Black-White College.

Ji Ning steps into the air, tells Nine Lotos to stand back, and draws his Northsea swords. His reply to Chen Jin’s challenge is simple and cold: “Come.” The stage is set for a battle that will determine more than just victory—it is a public test of whether a small-tribe genius can stand against the established power of a sect aristocrat.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Cloud-Based Combat Techniques**: Chen Jin’s hundred-and-eight golden plates are a classic Xianxia expression of ‘brandishing power through domain control’. In Daoist numerology, 108 is a sacred number representing the 36 Heavenly Fiends and 72 Earthly Fiends—a complete cycle of celestial and terrestrial energy. By shaping clouds into lions, chariots, and divine dragons, Chen Jin isn’t just showing off; he’s displaying a technique that can control the battlefield’s very landscape. It’s not magic in the Western sense—it’s a manifestation of his Dao comprehension made visible through raw energy.

**The ‘Childhood Friend’ Trope in Xianxia**: Chen Jin’s argument—“We grew up together, I sacrificed my status for you”—is a classic emotional trap in Chinese cultivation novels. The underlying assumption is that emotional investment over time should guarantee a return. Nine Lotos’s rejection of this premise is significant: in Xianxia, a Dao-companion bond requires mutual resonance of Dao-hearts, not a ledger of sacrifices. Chen Jin’s failure lies in confusing debt with destiny.

**Flamesaint’s Role**: The presence of Flamesaint—a figure who has defeated a Primal Daoist—adds an interesting layer. He doesn’t intervene to stop the fight; he only instructs Chen Jin not to commit fratricide. This reflects the unwritten code of many sects: personal grudges can be settled via open challenges, but killing a fellow disciple is a line that must not be crossed. Flamesaint is there as a witness and a leash, not a peacemaker.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the classic “rival appears and throws down a gauntlet” moment, but it’s executed with the weight it deserves. Pay attention to Nine Lotos’s reaction: she warns Ji Ning and tries to step in, but ultimately lets him handle it. This isn’t passivity—it’s trust. The real tension isn’t whether Ji Ning will win or lose, but how the fight will affect the fragile political balance between the Black-White College’s factions.

Notice how Chen Jin frames his anger: he feels betrayed not by Nine Lotos’s rejection, but by the fact that she chose someone “beneath” him. His pride is wounded more than his heart. This distinction makes him a more complex antagonist—not a simpleminded bully, but a man whose worldview (that investment and social rank should dictate love) is being violently overturned. Also keep an eye on Flamesaint. His quiet observation suggests he might be there to evaluate Ji Ning as much as to supervise Chen Jin. The stage is set, the swords are drawn, and the lake is about to witness a clash that will echo far beyond Swallow Mountain.
