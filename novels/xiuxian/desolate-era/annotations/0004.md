## Chapter Overview

Ji Yichuan and Yuchi Snow argue about their newborn son's future. Yichuan insists that Ji Ning must win the Golden Sword to earn the Prefectural Lord's position, rather than having it handed to him through political maneuvering. This reflects his philosophy that a leader must be strong enough to hold the seat. Meanwhile, the ambitious elder Ji Lie begins scheming to prevent Ji Ning's success, planning to secretly cultivate talented tribal youths to defeat him in the future. The chapter ends with Ji Ning, still a baby, deciding to begin his cultivation of the Nuwa Diagram visualization technique, setting the foundation for his journey on the path of immortality.

## Key Plot Points

1. Yuchi Snow is furious that Ji Yichuan committed their infant son to winning the Golden Sword, an exceedingly difficult competition held every four years for youths under sixteen. She believes it would be easier to bribe one more Steward to secure the position politically.

2. Ji Yichuan explains his reasoning: if their son is talented, he will win the Golden Sword and earn the position with no objections; if he is not, a comfortable life without the burden of leadership is better. He names the child 'Ning,' wishing for him to maintain a peaceful heart.

3. Ji Lie, the second-strongest expert of the Western Prefecture, reveals his long-held resentment over losing the Prefectural Lord's seat to his elder brother decades ago. He plots to select talented tribal youths and provide them with resources so they can defeat Ji Ning in future Golden Sword Ceremonies.

4. The infant Ji Ning struggles with babyhood but resolves to begin his cultivation journey. He recalls Lord Cui's advice and starts practicing the Nuwa Diagram, a supreme visualization technique that can absorb natural energy to strengthen his soul.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Visualization Techniques (观想法):** In xianxia lore, visualization techniques are used to train the soul and mind by forming mental images of divine beings or cosmic principles. The Nuwa Diagram here is exceptionally rare because it can actually draw in natural energy, not just strengthen willpower. This marks the beginning of Ji Ning's cultivation even as a newborn.

**The Book of Life and Death (生死簿):** A legendary artifact in Chinese folklore, said to record the lifespan and fate of every mortal. Judge Cui is the underworld official who controls this book. Ji Ning's previous meeting with him is a pivotal plot point—having one's name rewritten in the book to allow favorable reincarnation is a major boon.

**Golden Sword Ceremony (金剑大典):** A life-or-death competition for young members of the Ji Clan and the surrounding tribes. Winning it is an extraordinary achievement that proves the victor's martial prowess and qualifies them to claim the Prefectural Lord's position. The ceremony occurs only once every four years.

### Judge Cui of the Netherworld

**Judge Cui (崔府君):** In Chinese folk religion, Judge Cui is one of the four great judges of the underworld, holding the Book of Life and Death and a brush that determines fates. In Desolate Era, Lord Cui Jue is the First Judge of the Netherworld Kingdom. He personally oversaw Ji Ning's reincarnation, allowing him to retain his memories and granting him the Nuwa Diagram technique—making him a key benefactor in Ji Ning's life.

## Reading Guide

This chapter establishes the central conflict of Ji Ning's childhood: his father's high expectations and the political scheming of Ji Lie. Readers should note that winning the Golden Sword is portrayed as nearly impossible, setting up Ji Ning's future struggle to prove himself. The introduction of the Nuwa Diagram is crucial—it hints that Ji Ning possesses extraordinary advantages from his past life, including a transcendent visualization technique that even heavenly soldiers would envy. Pay attention to the contrast between Yichuan's cold exterior (short, decisive sentences) and his deep love for his son, revealed through his actions and his naming choice. Ji Lie's bitter monologue about waiting eighty years gives depth to his antagonistic role; he is not a simple villain but a man nursing a long grievance.

## The Nuwa Diagram {#nuwa-diagram}

**Nuwa Diagram (女娲图):** Named after the goddess Nuwa, this visualization technique allows the practitioner to mentally construct the form of the creator goddess. In Chinese mythology, Nuwa molded humans from yellow clay and repaired the sky. Within Desolate Era, this technique is a supreme method for strengthening the soul, and it is so rare that even heavenly soldiers would benefit from it. The fact that it can absorb natural energy marks it as an anomaly—ordinary visualization techniques cannot interact with the physical world's energy.
