## Chapter Overview

This is a classic Xianxia breather-and-buildup chapter that serves two vital functions. First, Ji Ning wraps up a very loose end—the Qi Kingdom royal siblings—with a brutal lesson in princely failure. Second, he lays the groundwork for his next big expedition: a cursed treasure ground called the Quya Mountains, hidden deep in the Crescent Major World. There's method in every move he makes, from treasure analysis to karmic sin hunting, all leading to a quiet, hungry stare at a mist-shrouded mountain range.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Truth Behind the Twelve Demon Kings:** Ji Ning cross-references the identical treasure maps found on the eight slain demon kings and realizes the entire Twelve Eastern Flow alliance had gathered *before* his massacre, not in reaction to it. Their purpose was a shared expedition to a secret treasure ground.
- **The Qi Kingdom's Stolen Legacy:** Ji Ning finds an ancient journal in the Firewing Demon King’s storage, written by the Qi Kingdom’s royal clan. It records generations of attempts to plunder the Quya Mountains, a place so dangerous it terrified even Loose Immortals. The founding emperor built his kingdom on a single fragment of its wealth.
- **The Prince's Fatal Blunder:** When Ji Ning asks about the treasure, Prince Qi Rufeng hesitates, unwilling to share his clan’s greatest secret. His sister knows nothing, but his silence is a test—and he fails it spectacularly. Ji Ning teleports them out of the mansion without a second word, leaving the prince to wail in regret.
- **Karmic Cleanse and Power Upgrade:** Ji Ning systematically tracks down the three surviving sin-laden demon kings (finding only the Venomsting Demon King) and then hunts down a six-hundred-thousand-year-old evil Loose Immortal patriarch. The battle is fierce, but Ji Ning wins via main body + clone tactics, and the killing intent from these kills pushes his three Northsea swords to the equivalent of high-grade Immortal-ranked flying swords.
- **Setting the Stage:** With his apprentice secured and his sin quota met, Ji Ning finally arrives at the misty, fang-shaped Quya Mountains, the site of his next great adventure.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Crescent Major World as a Sandbox:** This chapter casually drops a huge lore bomb. The Crescent Major World—home to Patriarch Subhuti’s Mount Innerheart—is not just a planet. It’s a creation of the Patriarch himself, into which he deliberately transplanted fragments of the shattered primordial Pangu’s World. Think of it as a cosmic sandbox where the Patriarch conducted experiments and sealed leftover battlefields. For the Patriarch’s personal disciples, this world is one giant, open-world treasure hunt.
- **The Gluttony Rule of Karmic Merit and Sin:** Ji Ning is operating under a classic Xianxia moral-economy principle. He needs to take on a high-karma disciple (Qingya Xiaoyu, a pure soul) and kill ten “great sinners” to maintain his cultivation balance. This isn’t just about being a good guy; it’s about eschewing karmic hellfire. Notice that the old patriarch he killed was the strongest foe yet, but also the most valuable source of killing intent *and* karmic merit.
- **Treasure Grading and the Cultivator’s Economy:** Ji Ning’s joy over his Northsea swords matching “high-grade Immortal-ranked” is a big deal. It means he can now go toe-to-toe with major powers on equipment alone, without relying on his Starseizing Hand or divine abilities. In the Xianxia economy, weapon upgrades are a direct, measurable path to power—more reliable than luck. The fact that he estimates ten more evil patriarchs will push him to “top-grade Immortal-ranked” shows his clinical, goal-oriented approach.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in pacing. It gives you the satisfaction of a completed checklist—apprentice secured, sinners hunted, power leveled up—before dangling the next shiny object: the Quya Mountains. Pay close attention to Ji Ning’s analysis of the treasure maps. The fact that all twelve demon kings gathered for the same treasure *before* he showed up means there is something *very* big and *very* scary in those mountains. The royal journal’s note that even Loose Immortals felt “terror” from the deep fluctuations is a direct promise that the next arc will not be a walk in the park. Also, note the prince’s mistake. In the cultivation world, hesitation is death, and Ji Ning is a man who makes decisions instantly. The Prince’s fumbled test is a perfect example of how Xianxia logic punishes indecisiveness in the face of overwhelming power.
