## Chapter Overview
So here it is—the moment our boys officially become card-carrying, territory-holding members of the Empire’s beat squad. But the Grand Xia Dynasty isn’t exactly running a charity. Yes, they dish out an absurdly generous perk (ten thousand years of protected territory even after death for Inner Guards), but they also dangle a very clear, very tantalizing paywall. Those techniques in the library? Locked behind Karmic Merit. The freedom to roam? Comes with a ten-year timer. Ji Ning, as ever, reads the fine print with the cold clarity of a man who’s been burned before. And then there’s the little reunion with Qingqing laoking—let’s just say the Ten-Thousand-Year Frost Ice transaction turns into a hilarious hostage negotiation.

## Key Plot Points
- The new Yinglong Guards are given the opportunity to designate a ten thousand li territory for their tribe, protected by the Guard for life—and for an additional thousand years after death. Inner Guards get even more: a ten-thousand-year shield.
- Ji Ning calmly marks the map, centering his territory on Ten Thousand Swords City, barely including the four prefectural cities.
- Daoist Yulan dismisses the group with a reminder: complete one Karmic Merit mission every decade, or face forced conscription. No excuses, no exceptions.
- Ji Ning and Mu Northson pick two ten-Merit missions targeting Karmic sin-heavy cultivators, deciding that a hundred years of freedom beats chasing prestige.
- Back in Anchan City, Ji Ning sells off the Mount Yujue spoils at the Heavenly Treasures Mountain, netting over ten thousand jin of Primordial Liquid.
- A chaotic negotiation with Qingqing ends with her reluctantly parting with half her Ten-Thousand-Year Frost Ice for Primordial Liquid.
- Ji Ning quietly gives eight thousand jin of his Primordial Liquid to the Whitewater Hound (Uncle White), arguing that his own bottleneck is comprehension, not fuel.
- The pair decides to return to the Black-White College for a long cultivation session.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Protection of territory as dynasty strategy**: The Grand Xia Dynasty’s offer of ten-thousand-year territorial protection for Inner Guards is a masterstroke of soft power. In the Xianxia world, where tribes rise and fall in a single generation, a thousand or ten thousand years of guaranteed peace is practically an invitation to breed a super-clan. But there’s a leash—the requirement to stay alive and stay compliant. The Dynasty isn’t just buying loyalty; it’s systematically fostering new power centers that owe their very existence to the central state. It’s the carrot that makes the stick seem optional.

**The Karmic Merit currency**: The Guard’s internal economy runs on Karmic Merit (功德), a concept that in authentic Chinese Buddhism and Daoism refers to the spiritual credit earned through good deeds. In this fictional system, it’s literalized into a resource that can be spent on techniques and treasures. Killing Karmic-sin-laden criminals increases the Dynasty’s destiny (气运), which is a classic Xianxia justification for what is essentially a bounty-hunting bureaucracy.

**The paywall trope**: Ji Ning’s reaction to the restricted library—intense hunger, immediate frustration—is a hallmark of the Xianxia genre. The world is full of power, but access is always gated by contribution, status, or sacrifice. This “visible-but-unreachable” dynamic is one of the genre’s most effective engines for generating reader desire and narrative drive. It’s the same mechanism that makes a shopping chapter feel like a boss fight.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a classic “after the storm, count the loot” breather. Don’t skip it—there’s a lot of subtle worldbuilding baked into what looks like bureaucratic paperwork. The ten-thousand-year protection offer is a massive long-term plot seed, and Ji Ning’s quiet decision to invest his Primordial Liquid in Uncle White rather than himself says volumes about his character growth. He’s no longer the kid who hoards every resource for himself; he’s thinking like a clan leader. Also, keep an eye on the mission targets—both are described as having killed innocents in horrific ways. That sets the tone for the kind of enemies Ji Ning will be hunting as a state-sanctioned executioner. The next arc is going to be a lot darker than shopping in Anchan City.
