## Chapter Overview

Ji Ning walks into a nightmare. The maze-like formation he’s trapped in isn’t just a crude barrier—it’s a sophisticated prison maintained by a Zifu Disciple-level cultivator, complete with spectral screams and the feeling of countless tortured souls. After a brief skirmish with a strange beast that tests the limits of his new Water-Fire Lotus technique, Ning realizes the scale of the operation he’s stumbled upon. Meanwhile, back at the Ji Clan’s Western Prefecture, the leadership is in crisis mode: over twenty Xiantian cultivators have disappeared across the clan’s territories, including members of Ji Lie’s own family. As tempers flare and accusations fly, Ji Yichuan steps up with a cold-headed analysis that points toward a hidden power manipulating events behind the scenes. The chapter masterfully contrasts one young cultivator’s life-or-death struggle in a fog of war with the political panic of the clan that spawned him, setting the stage for a major confrontation.

## Key Plot Points

- **Formation revealed as Zifu-grade**: Ji Ning observes the stability of the formation and deduces it cannot be operated by a Xiantian lifeform—only a Zifu Disciple (or higher) could control such a sophisticated, graded formation-flag treasure.
- **First test of the Water-Fire Lotus**: The protective spell formed from Ning’s comprehension of Taiyin and Sunstar forces shows its worth, blocking both poison needles and a beast’s claw while Ning counters with a swift kill.
- **Beast fight and tactical assessment**: Ning faces a strange hybrid beast (approximating a Huan Beast with modifications) and dispatches it with a single ‘Dripping Water Pierces Stone’ strike, confirming his swordsmanship can handle late-stage Xiantian demons efficiently.
- **The true scale of the threat**: Ning hears the beast’s handler mention a master, and another cold voice casually dismisses non-Zifu foes as trivial, implying an organized force with at least one Zifu, possibly more.
- **Clan-wide crisis at Drifting Snow Hall**: Over twenty Xiantian lifeforms have vanished across the five prefectures of the Ji Clan, including high-profile commanders and even a son of Ji Lie.
- **Ji Yichuan’s strategic assessment**: Drawing on his experience traveling the outside world, Ji Yichuan diagnoses two possible scenarios: a wandering tribe seeking a new city, or an evil cultivator collecting souls for forbidden magic.
- **Call for restraint before retaliation**: Despite the emotional outcry, Ji Yichuan insists on gathering intelligence on the enemy’s strength before any attack—a rare moment of cool-headed leadership cutting through the clan’s righteous fury.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Graded Treasures vs Mortal Tools**: The formation-flag treasure supporting this black mist array is described as an ‘entry-level graded treasure’ (入阶法宝). In Xiantian-level conflicts, ordinary treasures suffice, but graded treasures require a Zifu-level soul to bind and activate, serving as a power-level marker.
- **Formation Volumes and Dedication**: Ji Ning’s ability to study the *Nine Volumes of Formations* while on the move is a classic sign of his exceptional mental discipline. In Xianxia, formation mastery requires both rote memorization of complex diagrams and intuitive reasoning—Ning’s ‘divided mind’ technique is put to a new kind of test here.
- **Demonic Cultivator Tropes**: The idea of a rogue cultivator capturing Xiantian lifeforms for their souls to fuel evil spells is a recurring xianxia danger. The screams Ning hears echo traditional descriptions of ‘blood refinement’ or ‘soul-nourishing’ practices, signaling that the evil present here is not casual cruelty but deliberate, systematic harvesting.
- **Political dynamics of the Ji Clan**: The emergency council in Drifting Snow Hall reveals deep internal tensions—Ji Lie’s personal grief over his son’s disappearance fuels his rage, while Ji Yichuan’s measured response reinforces his reputation as the clan’s most worldly and strategic fighter.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a shift in gears. This chapter moves from the intimate, personal scale of Ji Ning’s hunt to the political and military machinations of the Ji Clan as a whole. That black mist formation isn’t just a cool set piece—it’s a ticket into a much larger conspiracy that’s been brewing in Mount Yan. Notice how Ning’s deductions about the Zifu Disciple align with Ji Yichuan’s warnings back at the hall: this isn’t random banditry. Someone is engineering chaos, and they’ve got the capital to hire Zifu-level muscle.

The fight with the hybrid beast is also a nice benchmark for Ning’s growth. He’s no longer the kid squirming through cage battles. He’s now casually one-shotting late-stage Xiantian demons while multi-tasking formation analysis. The writing here emphasizes his clinical, almost surgical approach—kill, assess, plan, repeat.

Also, keep an eye on Ji Yichuan’s role. He’s the one voice of restraint in a room full of angry tigers. This isn’t cowardice; it’s the wisdom of someone who’s traveled far enough to know that monsters don’t always have fangs—sometimes they wear robes and carry formation flags. The foundation for the next major arc is being laid right here, one tense council meeting at a time.

Finally, don't skip the sensory details: the screams within the formation, the feeling of endless trapped souls, the eerie stability of a Zifu-controlled maze. The author is building a very specific atmosphere of dread. This is not a clean battlefield—it’s a slaughterhouse run by a methodical butcher. And Ji Ning is walking right into the middle of it.
