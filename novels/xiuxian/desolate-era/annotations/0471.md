## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to another high-octane beat in the Broken Horn World arc. In this chapter, Ji Ning is put through the wringer—not by some ancient Daofather or hidden World God, but by an alien war machine that treats Celestial-Immortal-level fighters as disposable grunts. The chapter is a masterclass in “pressure cooker” pacing: Ji Ning tears through over ten thousand soul-attacking cannon-fodder monsters without breaking a sweat, only to realize that each one of those *was a snack* for the real enemy. The true threat—the black-cloaked, four-armed curved-horn warriors—casually regenerate wounds and wield strength that forces Ji Ning to flash his Heavenly Transformation just to hold the line. And behind them? A purple-cloaked commander whose speed outclasses Ji Ning’s best movement ability. Our boy is cornered, outgunned, and running out of divine power—but the hardest choice is yet to come: does he burn his trump card now, or play dead a little longer?

## Key Plot Points

1. **The shift in enemy types.** Ji Ning immediately registers the difference between the earlier scale-winged blood-eyed warriors (expendable cannon fodder) and the new black-cloaked, four-armed golden-eyed warriors (elite soldiers). The visual contrast—from “kittens” to “tigers”—is hammered home with cold, clinical observation.

2. **The purple-cloaked commander’s entrance.** A single warrior in a purple cloak steps out of the ship’s hatch. His speed, even without any technique visible, outclasses Ji Ning’s Nine Heavens Oblivion Wind ability. Ji Ning immediately classifies him as “more powerful than any Celestial Immortal I’ve fought.”

3. **Fodder economy revealed.** The black-cloaked warriors literally vomit out ten thousand blood-eyed warriors from their mouths. These are *spawned* cannon fodder—mass-produced, disposable, and incapable of regeneration. Ji Ning kills them all using pure sword-art while tanking hits with his Eight-Nine Arcane Art, consuming minimal divine power.

4. **Elite engagement with regeneration.** A single black-cloaked warrior, Qu You, is dispatched. His speed is roughly comparable to Ji Ning’s, but his four arms and brute strength push Ji Ning to manifest Three Heads, Six Arms. Ji Ning wounds him, but the wound heals instantly—confirming these elites have undying bodies.

5. **Escalation to squad level.** The commander orders Qu You’s entire team of ten to swarm Ji Ning. Surrounded, Ji Ning activates Heavenly Transformation to match their thousand-zhang scale, buying time but draining his precious divine power reserves.

6. **The strategic trap.** The enemy’s grand formation locks down tens of millions of kilometers. Ji Ning’s internal calculation is bleak: using Starseizing Hand now would burn through his remaining fuel too fast, but he doesn’t see a way out. The chapter ends on a note of tactical desperation, not surrender.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter showcases a defining structural element of Xianxia warfare: the **fodder economy**. The curved-horn warriors’ ability to “hatch” disposable troops from their mouths is a literalization of the classic Daoist battlefield technique “sprinkle beans to form soldiers” (*sa dou cheng bing*). The original technique, derived from Daoist ritual magic, allowed a cultivator to turn a handful of beans into an army of armed warriors. Here, the Broken Horn species weaponizes this principle at an industrial scale—each elite soldier can carry a pocket army inside their own body, ready to vomit out a miniature horde. The key difference? True “bean soldiers” in Xianxia lore are often indestructible constructs that reform when shattered; these blood-eyed warriors are cheap, mortal-realm spawn with no self-recovery. They exist to exhaust the enemy’s energy. Ji Ning’s brutal farming of ten thousand of them while standing still and tanking hits is a tactical choice: conserve divine power by any means, even if it means letting himself be knocked around like a pinball.

Also worth noting is Ji Ning’s **Iceheart Realm** cultivation. His soul successfully withstood a coordinated soul-assault from over five thousand blood-eyed warriors. In Xianxia, a perfect soul-hardening technique like the Iceheart Realm is the difference between an ordinary immortal and a monster who can wade through armies of mind-attack specialists without a scratch. This is Ji Ning’s silent flex in the chapter: the real battle isn’t just physical; he’s *soul-tanking* an entire army.

## Reading Guide

This is a **tactical pressure chapter**—one that forces Ji Ning (and the reader) to confront the sheer scale of the Broken Horn World’s war machine. If you’re used to “Celestial Immortal-level = big deal,” prepare to recalibrate. Here, Celestial-Immortal-tier enemies are being used as *grunts* in a vanguard force. The chapter deliberately starves the reader of any easy power fantasy: Ji Ning is stronger than each individual elite, but he’s fighting an attrition war he cannot win without burning his ultimate move. The question isn’t whether he can kill the squad—it’s whether he can afford the *cost* of doing so.

Pay attention to Ji Ning’s internal monologue. He’s calculating, adapting, and deliberately holding back—even when wounded and surrounded. This is not a reckless brawler; it’s a survival game of resource management. The chapter also hints at a larger structure: the three “powerful wills” that observed the battle from a distance. They are interested in Ji Ning’s *body cultivation*, not his swordplay. That’s a chilling detail: the enemies respect his destructive power, but they *covet* his Eight-Nine Arcane Art. That alone should make readers very nervous about what happens if he’s captured.

Get ready for one of the most tense “fight smart, not hard” sequences in the Broken Horn World arc.
