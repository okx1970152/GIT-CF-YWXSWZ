## Chapter Overview
Get ready for one of the most satisfying inflection points in early *Desolate Era*: Ji Ning’s transformation from a prodigy on the rise into an undeniable powerhouse. Fresh off his Dao enlightenment and Fiendgod breakthrough, our young sword-wielder demonstrates exactly what a Xiantian body-refining cultivator is capable of. But his opponent, Tie Muzhan, isn’t just some random thug—he’s a cunning, century-old Xiantian hunter with Dao talismans and a bag of dirty tricks. The chapter delivers a brutal, tactical clash where raw talent meets ruthless experience, and where Ji Ning’s most startling new ability—Divine Sense—makes its dramatic debut. By the chapter’s end, Ji Ning isn’t just alive; he’s ascended twice over. Both his Ki Refining follows suit to Xiantian, and he finally opens the forbidden manual left by his mother—the *Windwing Evasion*—opening the door to a new level of mobility and power.

## Key Plot Points
1. **Tie Muzhan’s Desperate Escape**: Cornered and outmatched, Tie Muzhan triggers his Agility and Lightness Talismans, becoming a near-invisible blur. The tables seem to turn—until Ji Ning discovers that sheer panic can unlock exactly the power he needs.
2. **Divine Sense Awakens**: Mid-chase, Ji Ning’s soul-level breakthrough manifests in the most dramatic way possible. The trees, rocks, and earth around him become his weapons. He’s not even consciously controlling it at first—his raw will takes form, blocking Tie Muzhan’s path and proving that Ji Ning’s soul cultivation has leaped far beyond his visible stage.
3. **Water-Fire Lotus Execution**: The newly-comprehended signature technique of Ji Ning’s Dao understanding makes its lethal combat debut. The twin lotus leaves of fire and water grind down Tie Muzhan’s golden guardian talisman, leaving him exposed and charred.
4. **The Clean Kill**: A single drop of rain—the final move of the Dripping Sutra’s essence—pierces Tie Muzhan’s forehead. The century-old Xiantian fighter dies in disbelief, cursing an imagined hidden master he thinks must be helping Ji Ning. The irony is delicious; the “hidden master” is Ji Ning himself.
5. **The Blazing Aftermath**: Ji Ning claims his spoils—a bow, a storage armguard, eight Dao talismans—and burns all evidence. That same night, his Ki Refining follows his body to Xiantian as naturally as rivers flow to the sea, healing the old damage from his birth.
6. **The Forbidden Manual Opened**: With the condition finally met, Ji Ning unseals his mother’s gift: *Windwing Evasion*, a movement divine ability from the Yuchi Clan, passed down from Celestial Immortal Su Huan. A door to an entirely new dimension of speed opens.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is *packed* with classic Xianxia structural payoffs and tropes that will thrill any genre veteran. First: **Divine Sense (神念)**. In standard cultivation lore, this soul-perception ability is locked behind the Wanxiang (or even Primal) stage. Ji Ning accessing it here, as a Xiantian, is a sign that his soul strength is monstrously ahead of his physical cultivation—a direct result of his *Nuwa Diagram* training, his Dao enlightenment, and the soul-nourishing effect of his Crimsonbright-enhanced body. Second: **The Water-Fire Lotus**. This is not a “spell.” It’s a pure manifestation of Ji Ning’s comprehension of the True Meaning of Water and Fire, birthed from his epiphany. In Xianxia, techniques born from Dao understanding are always more potent and flexible than pre-learned spells. The lotus isn’t a fixed attack—it’s a natural law that Ji Ning has learned to make his own. Third: **The Late-Night Ki Refining Breakthrough**. This one is all about inner consistency. The *Crimsonbright Diagram* cleansed and rebuilt Ji Ning’s entire physical body from the cellular level, removing the birth defect that blocked his meridians. So his Xiantian breakthrough in Ki Refining becomes a *fait accompli*—the water finds its channel. Fourth: **Treasure Economy**. Tie Muzhan, after a century of life as a Xiantian hunter, only has eight Dao talismans, a single bow, and a storage armguard to show for it. This underscores how lean and dangerous the life of a wandering cultivator is in the Desolate Era world.

## Reading Guide
If you’re reading this and thinking “Man, Ji Ning is just steamrolling everyone now,” hold that thought—partially. This chapter is a *qualified* victory. Yes, Ji Ning won, but he won by exploiting an ability (Divine Sense) that shouldn’t logically exist at his cultivation level. Tie Muzhan’s death was caused as much by his ignorance as by Ji Ning’s skill; he literally couldn’t understand what was happening to him, and assumed a senior was helping Ji Ning. That’s a sign of how non-linear Xianxia power progression can be. Ji Ning isn’t just a stronger Xiantian—he’s a *broken* Xiantian, with a soul far beyond his tier. Also, pay close attention to the description of the *Windwing Evasion* manual in the upcoming chapters. This isn’t just a speed boost; it’s a legacy technique from Celestial Immortal Su Huan, a figure who will have massive implications for the power scaling of the story later on. Finally, understand the emotional weight here: Ji Ning has been carrying the trauma of his past life and the pressure of his clan’s expectations. This double breakthrough is him screaming to the universe that he will not be toyed with. The chapter isn’t just about getting stronger—it’s about Ji Ning declaring to fate itself: “This is only the beginning.”
