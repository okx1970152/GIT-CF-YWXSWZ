## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers the long-awaited puppet duel between Ji Ning (Beiming) and the senior disciple Yinxue Wenjian—a confrontation that breaks the pattern of past defeats and marks a turning point in the newcomer’s status. While the senior disciples expect another easy trampling of fresh meat, Ji Ning demonstrates that his sword-art comprehension runs deeper than anyone anticipated. The chapter is a masterclass in escalating tension: from a probing single-sword exchange to a full-scale formation clash, all within the sealed arena. And when Ji Ning’s Flowing Water Sword effortlessly neutralizes Wenjian’s killing intent, you know this is no ordinary rookie.

## Key Plot Points

- Mu Northson and the senior disciples watch from outside the sealing formation; Nine Lotus unexpectedly defends Ji Ning’s chances, hinting she sees something others don’t.
- The white-haired elder explains the rules: the puppet’s outer protective layer represents the cultivator’s life—once broken, the match ends.
- Both puppets retreat to gain distance, respecting the lethal nature of the duel.
- Yinxue Wenjian attacks first with “Alkaid Breaks the Army,” a bloodthirsty stance meant to overwhelm with sheer killing momentum.
- Ji Ning counters with a single Flowing Water Sword from the *Lesser Five Elements Sword Manual*, showing that water can wrap and exhaust even the fiercest steel—an elegant philosophical refutation of brute force.
- Bu Ying recognizes the technique and is visibly shocked, while other disciples begin to reevaluate Ji Ning.
- Wenjian, humiliated, unleashes his ultimate move: “Big Dipper – Greedy Wolf Ripping Heaven,” a seven-sword formation that manifests a wolf phantom.
- Ji Ning reveals his own formation mastery: Nine Palaces Sword Formation, turning his nine swords into over a hundred rain-drops that shatter the Greedy Wolf and slice open Wenjian’s puppet armor.
- The white-haired elder declares Ji Ning the winner, leaving Wenjian with a ghost-pale face.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The *Lesser Five Elements Sword Manual* referenced in this chapter is a classic example of how Xianxia treats elemental philosophy as a direct combat system. The Five Elements (Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, Earth) are not just natural forces—they are archetypal, principle-bound Daos. Ji Ning’s Flowing Water Sword embodies the nature of water: yielding yet inexorable, able to wear down the hardest substance over time. This is a direct application of the Daoist concept “weakness overcomes strength” (以柔克刚), famously illustrated by water wearing through rock. In Xianxia physics, comprehending a single elemental principle can yield devastatingly effective techniques.

The Nine Palaces Sword Formation draws from the ancient Chinese cosmological Nine Palaces (九宫)—a grid derived from the Luo River Writing (洛书) used in Feng Shui and military tactics. In cultivation, formations based on these patterns allow a small number of flying swords to multiply their killing potential. Ji Ning’s ability to wield such a formation at the Zifu Disciple level shows his extraordinary foundation in both sword and formation Dao.

## Reading Guide

**Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this is the chapter where Beiming stops being a target and starts being a monster.** The fight is a pure clash of comprehensions: Wenjian relies on sheer murderous aura and the Big Dipper’s ferocity, while Ji Ning calmly lets the Dao do the talking. The Flowing Water vs. Greedy Wolf match-up is *chef’s kiss*—it’s the classic soft-versus-hard, and the soft wins because it *understands* the hard.

Also, note Nine Lotus’s quiet confidence. She’s been watching this whole time, and she *knew*. That’s the mark of a true senior—she sees potential before it proves itself. And poor Wenjian? He just got schooled in front of everyone, including the girl he was trying to impress. Ouch.

For cultivation nerds: pay attention to how Ji Ning’s Nine Palaces Sword Formation works. It’s not just a random swarm—it’s geometry meets Dao. He’s using a formation he picked up from the aquatic palace, which is already a hint that his inheritance is far beyond what the college teaches freshmen. This is a major flex disguised as a duel.
