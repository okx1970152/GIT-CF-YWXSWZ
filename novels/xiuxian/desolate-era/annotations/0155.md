## Chapter Overview
Okay, fellow Daoists, buckle up! Chapter 17 slams us right into the heart of the Black-White College entrance exam—and let me tell you, this isn’t your average pop quiz. We’re talking a full-on puppet formation with eight hundred and ten dragon heads that spit lightning bolts like there’s no tomorrow. Nine hundred-plus hopefuls cram into a valley, given one incense stick’s worth of time to fly through a screaming hailstorm of azure thunder and reach a cave. Sounds simple? It’s brutal. The weak get weeded out in seconds, and the strong start to reveal themselves. But the real fireworks? That’s when our boy Ji Ning finally stops observing and makes his move. And just when you think it’s all about raw power… the Primal Daoist judges drop a bombshell that changes how everyone sees him.

## Key Plot Points
- The second round of the Black-White College exam is revealed: a puppet formation (八百一十个龙头傀儡) unleashes a ten-thousand-lightning assault. Candidates must endure the barrage and fly into a mountain cave within one incense stick’s time.
- The strategy is clear and brutal: there's no shortcut. You have to charge straight through the thickest concentration of dragon heads, which means taking the full force of all eight hundred and ten puppets at close range.
- Several hopefuls try and fail spectacularly—including a promising black-clad girl who gets swatted down by half the formation, and the black-clothed Crimsonbright Diagram cultivator who nearly makes it but is hammered back by the group attack.
- Ji Ning hangs back to observe the pattern, confirms his analysis, and then launches himself with a six-layered Water-Fire Lotus and a flying sword.
- Up above, the six Primal Daoist examiners are casually discussing the candidates. They spot a white-robed youth with astonishing puppet-control talent, but mostly write off the rest. Then the black-haired examiner drops a massive reveal: the fur-clad kid with the Water-Fire Lotus is almost certainly Ji Ning—the Wuyou Caverns’ monster genius who comprehended a Domain of the Dao at the early Zifu stage.
- The other five Primal Daoists are visibly shocked, and the chapter ends with them all staring down at Ji Ning with newfound intensity.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Domain of the Dao (道之域境)** — This is a rare and terrifyingly advanced ability. Normally, projecting one's understanding of a Dao as a persistent, tangible field of influence is something only Primal Daoists (元神道人) can achieve. For a Zifu Disciple (紫府前期) to have a Domain of the Dao is so freakish that the Black-White College examiners instantly label him a "monster genius" or suspect he’s a reincarnated exalted figure. Think of it as having a “force field” of your sword intent—everything within that field is subject to your Dao-based rules. In standard Xianxia logic, this is the ultimate shortcut to crushing opponents who only rely on raw power.

**Inherited Escape Talisman (传承遁术)** — The reason Ji Ning isn't terrified of being ganged up on or assassinated later. In the Desolate Era universe, top-tier sects like the Black-White College often have life-saving escape talismans built into their recruitment process or personal treasures. It’s the Xianxia equivalent of a nuclear self-destruct button that says, “If you kill me, you and your entire clan get vaporized.” It’s a staple trope that lets young prodigies act boldly in dangerous cities like Anchan.

**The Wuyou Caverns (无忧洞)** — Foreshadowed as Ji Ning's public tag. The Wuyou Caverns are Anchan City’s premier blood-soaked gambling arena, where cultivators fight to the death for noble entertainment. Ji Ning’s reputation there—"Monster Genius of the Wuyou Caverns"—is an instantly recognizable badge of honor among the city’s elite, a name that signals terrifying combat prowess far beyond his cultivation level.

## Reading Guide
**This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia social-calibration.** Watch how the energy shifts the instant the Primal Daoists learn Ji Ning is *that kid* from the Wuyou Caverns. Their lazy dismissal turns into genuine shock. This isn’t just about raw power; it’s about reputation and the politics of monster talent. The author is setting up a delicious dynamic: Ji Ning is the dark horse nobody expected, but now *everyone* important is watching. The white-robed puppet genius is played up as the obvious front-runner, but the real headline is the kid in furs who might have already surpassed them all in Dao comprehension. Anchan City is about to get a lot more interesting.

*Also, keep your eyes on the black-clothed Crimsonbright Diagram youth hitting his limit under the full formation assault—this creates a perfect benchmark to measure just how insane Ji Ning’s potential really is. The stage is set. The lightning is falling. And our boy is about to step into the spotlight.*
