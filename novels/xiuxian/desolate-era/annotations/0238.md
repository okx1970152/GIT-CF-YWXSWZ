## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the payoff chapter you’ve been waiting for. After chapters of simmering tension, jealous taunts, and Chen Jin’s pathetic attempts to tear Ji Ning down in the name of unrequited love, the two Wanxiang Adepts finally throw down in the skies above the Black-White College. And let’s just say… Chen Jin *really* should have kept that mouth shut. When he crosses the line one too many times by bringing up Ji Ning’s dead parents, the young Sword Immortal stops holding back, and what follows is a brutal, surgical beatdown that shows the true gap between a genius and a mere “top ten” disciple. By the end of this chapter, you won’t just be satisfied—you’ll be grinning.

## Key Plot Points

- Chen Jin, driven mad by jealousy and rejection, challenges Ji Ning to an aerial duel right in front of Firemoon and Nine Lotos, hoping to publicly humiliate his rival.
- Despite Nine Lotos’s pleas, Firemoon refuses to intervene, reasoning that Chen Jin needs to vent his pent-up fury or risk a worse inner demon tribulation later.
- Chen Jin opens with vitriolic telepathic taunts, comparing family backgrounds and cultivation levels, and crosses the ultimate line by sneering at Ji Ning's dead parents.
- Ji Ning, visibly angered, fires back with scalding psychological precision, ruthlessly highlighting Chen Jin's failure to win Nine Lotos after years of pursuit.
- The fight begins: Chen Jin unleashes his Windcloud Grand Formation, summoning a massive azure wind-bird, but Ji Ning’s ninth-level Lesser Thousand Sword Formation cleanly cleaves it apart.
- Chen Jin escalates with a combined wind-bird and golden divine dragon, only for Ji Ning to reveal his mastery of the *Three-Foot Sword*, third stance: Sword Light Flashes, cutting through both summoned beasts effortlessly.
- Firemoon and Nine Lotos are both stunned—recognizing the technique instantly—and realize Ji Ning’s sword realm rivals the top disciples.
- In the final sequence, Ji Ning activates his Windwing Evasion, shatters the Windcloud chains with raw Fiendgod power and his Starseizing Hand, and physically grabs Chen Jin by the throat, demanding an answer to the question: “Who is a piece of shit?”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Sword Light Flashes (剑芒乍现)** is the third stance of the legendary *Three-Foot Sword* (三尺剑), a sword art created by the mad genius Immortal Beixing. In the Black-White College, the first six stances are openly passed down to disciples, but reaching the third stance this quickly is considered extraordinary. It's a technique that leaves a visible, painterly trail of sword-light in the air—a signature of artistic lethality. The truly terrifying part? Ji Ning hasn't even used his full combat power yet. He's holding back his Fiendgod body’s raw physicality and his most devastating divine ability: the Starseizing Hand. In Xianxia terms, this is the classic “I beat you with one hand tied behind my back” flex—and it makes Chen Jin’s humiliation so much more delicious.

**The Three-Foot Sword** is based on the Daoist philosophy of “utter sincerity leading to illumination” (至诚通明). It’s not just a sword style; it’s a reflection of the cultivator’s Dao-heart. A user who wields it well must have a clear, unyielding will. Ji Ning’s rapid mastery of it signals that his heart is as sharp and unshakeable as his sword-edge.

**The Windcloud Grand Formation** that Chen Jin uses is a formation-based combat method, powered by 108 Earth-ranked top-grade treasures personally gathered by his Primal Daoist father. In Xianxia, a top-tier formation grants the user strength that rivals a Divine Ability. That’s what makes Ji Ning crushing it with raw sword-light so astonishing—he's overpowering a collection of specialized treasures through sheer technical superiority and brute force.

### Dao-heart

The innermost will and conviction of a cultivator, the bedrock of their spiritual power. A cracked or unstable Dao-heart can invite inner demons and lead to cultivation failure.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is pure emotional catharsis and power-showcase combined. If you’ve been bristling at Chen Jin’s entitled, sour-grape attitude for the past few chapters, strap in—this is the part where the villain gets crushed and learns a painful lesson about poking a Sword Immortal where it hurts. Pay close attention to the way Firemoon handles the situation: he’s not being cruel by refusing to step in; he’s being smart, letting Chen Jin’s rage burn itself out in a controlled way. It’s a subtle piece of senior-brother wisdom. Also, don’t miss the moment where Ji Ning uses Chen Jin’s own favorite insult against him while holding him by the throat—it’s the kind of cold, poetic justice that makes Xianxia revenge so satisfying. Get ready to cheer, fellow Daoists. This one’s for all the underdogs.

## Deep Dive: The Mentality of a Rejected Cultivator

In the Xianxia cosmos, emotional turmoil isn’t just sad—it’s spiritually dangerous. A cultivator’s Dao-heart is the bedrock of their power, and when that heart is shattered by unrequited love and public humiliation, the result isn't just a bad mood; it's a crack in the foundation that inner demons (心魔) can exploit during tribulations. Chen Jin’s rage, jealousy, and loss of face are so intense that Firemoon *allows* the duel to happen because bottling it up would be worse. This is a classic Xianxia trope: emotional provocation is a legitimate combat tactic, and a cultivator who loses their cool is a cultivator who loses. Ji Ning, by staying cold and surgical while Chen Jin burns hot and messy, is already showing the superior Dao-heart.
