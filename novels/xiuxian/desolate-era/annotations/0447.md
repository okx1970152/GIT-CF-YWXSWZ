## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia planning and surgical escalation. Ji Ning, Uncle White, Mu Northson, and Qingqing finalize their strategy to break into the Shaoyin clan’s heavily fortified mountain lair. The core tactical insight? You can’t destroy a million-li formation, but you can assassinate its human operators one by one. The gang sets their sights on the first target: Fomenter Fulong, a slimy and ancient Loose Immortal who’s lived a million years through sheer, disgusting persistence. Ji Ning, using his Seventy-Two Transformations to impersonate Fulong’s own disciple, pulls off a surgical strike that is cold, efficient, and absolutely devastating. The assassination itself is a blur of golden Starseizing Hands and pulverized bodies, and the aftermath is a Ten-Thousand-Li River turning a mountain range into a demolition site. It’s a perfect blend of tactical genius and raw, cathartic violence.

## Key Plot Points
*   **The Formation Weakness:** Uncle White dissects the Shaoyin clan’s defense of the Chenyu Mountains. The mountain range is a million li long, but Loose Immortals can only maintain formations over a hundred thousand li range. This means the region is broken into zones, each controlled by a different Immortal. Kill the controller, and you open a window of chaos in that zone.
*   **The Plan:** The team decides to assassinate these zone-controllers one by one. The first target is Fomenter Fulong, the most senior and powerful of the stationed Loose Immortals. The key is speed—they must wreak havoc before a Celestial Immortal can arrive to take over command of the entire mountain’s formation.
*   **The Disguise:** Ji Ning uses his Seventy-Two Transformations to mimic the form of ‘Daoist Xuxiu’, Fulong’s own despicable disciple. The disguise is flawless, allowing him to walk right into the heavily guarded palace complex without triggering any alarms.
*   **The Kill:** Inside the opulent Spring Sun Hall, Ji Ning drops the act. His arms stretch out dozens of meters, and with two massive, golden Starseizing Hands, he swats aside Fulong’s shield and annihilates the ancient Loose Immortal in a single, brutal blow. The assassination is clinical and over in the span of a breath.
*   **The Rampage:** With Fulong dead, his zone is momentarily defenseless. Ji Ning unleashes the Ten-Thousand-Li River, flooding the region and churning it like a cosmic blender. He systematically destroys the mountains, the earth, and—most importantly—the hidden formation bases buried deep within the land, opening a gaping wound in the Shaoyin clan’s iron defenses.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Logistics of Xianxia Fortresses:** This chapter beautifully illustrates a core logistical reality of Xianxia world-building. A million-li mountain range can be protected by a single ultimate formation, but *powering* that formation is a different beast. Ordinary mortals can’t fuel it; even Loose Immortals have a limited range of control. This forces high-level sects into a distributed defense model—dozens of ‘node’ controllers scattered across the territory. The classic Xianxia solution isn’t to brute-force the formation itself (impossible), but to find and surgically remove those human nodes. This is exactly what Ji Ning does.

**The Seventy-Two Transformations:** This is a quintessential Daoist divine ability, famously mastered by Sun Wukong, the Monkey King. In Chinese mythology, it represents not just changing shape, but changing one’s aura, scent, and even soul frequency to perfectly imitate a target. It is the ultimate infiltration tool, making it a staple trope in Xianxia for bypassing even the most paranoid security systems.

**The ‘Fomenter’ Archetype:** Fomenter Fulong is a perfect example of a specific Xianxia villain archetype: the ancient, hedonistic, but powerful elder. He has lived for over a million years, not through supreme cultivation talent, but by occupying a useful niche (fortress defense) and indulging in earthly desires (lust). The opulent, almost decadent description of his Spring Sun Hall—a literal “kingdom of women”—serves to highlight his moral decay, making his sudden, brutal annihilation at the hands of a disguised hero all the more satisfying.

## Reading Guide
Fellow Daoists, get ready for a chapter that proves brains are just as good as brawn! This is a classic ‘planning episode’ executed to perfection. You get the tactical thrill of watching our heroes find the chink in the enemy’s impenetrable armor—a classic Xianxia puzzle that relies on understanding the physical limits of their power system.

But don’t worry, it’s not all board games and finger-pointing. The payoff is immediate and brutal. The assassination of Fomenter Fulong is a lightning-fast sequence that spares no gory detail. The contrast between the serene, luxury-filled palace and the sudden eruption of golden, world-crushing hands is *chef’s kiss*.

Also, pay attention to Mu Northson’s quiet, burning rage. The chapter frames his trauma powerfully, and his desire for revenge forms the emotional core driving this entire assault. Finally, the scene is a fantastic showcase of Uncle White’s strategic mind. He is not just a loyal pet; he is a master tactician. This entire operation—from the analysis to the target selection—is his brainchild, proving the Whitewater Hound is a force multiplier in every sense.
