## Chapter Overview
The Immortal estate’s first real death trap snaps shut around our heroes, and the tension ratchets up from “can we hold out?” to “people are dying, right now.” We thought the endless frost arrows were the big threat—turns out, they were just the opening act. Once the eighteen Qiongqi puppets roll in with Primal-level combat power, the situation flips from a grinding attrition war to a sudden, brutal slaughter. And in the chaos, the weakest link snaps: Buyou the Realperson becomes the first casualty. This chapter hits hard because it forces everyone to realize that raw talent and numbers aren’t enough—you need a formation master, and the only one who fits that bill is locked outside.

## Key Plot Points
- The group identifies the frost arrow storm as a posthumous relic of the Wu Jiang Immortal’s array, capable of killing even Primal Daoists through sheer attrition; they realize the formation draws on endless natural energy and cannot be brute-forced.
- A frantic discussion reveals that the party’s only true formation master, the Fiendgod Xiangliu Fang, was left behind in the outer hall to delay Dao-soldiers—a tactical miscalculation that now looks fatal.
- The frost arrow assault forces Xue Hongyi and Buyou the Realperson, the weakest defensive nodes, to retreat into the core, compressing the formation and increasing the strain on Ji Ning’s five-man core group.
- Before anyone can formulate a new plan, eighteen Qiongqi puppets emerge from the formation mist, each possessing Primal Daoist-level combat power and magic-treasure-hardened bodies.
- Ten of the puppets occupy and lock down the main defensive group; the remaining eight swarm directly at Shaoyan Nong, overwhelming Xue Hongyi and Buyou the Realperson.
- Buyou the Realperson is torn apart by a single claw strike—the first death among the Wanxiang Adepts, serving as a brutal reminder that in a Celestial Immortal’s trap, the margin for error is zero.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Qiongqi (穷奇) Puppets**: In ancient Chinese mythology, the Qiongqi is one of the Four Perils (四凶)—legendary creatures embodying chaos and evil, often described as a winged tiger ox hybrid that devours humans. Deploying them as puppets here isn’t accidental; the author is drawing on their mythological reputation as unstoppable, malicious beasts to signal that these are no ordinary guardians. They’re the kind of creature that would eat you and then savor the memory.

**Formation Crisis as a Classic Xianxia Trope**: This entire chapter is a textbook example of the “formation trap” sub-trope. In Xianxia, whenever a party enters an ancient Immortal’s legacy site, someone must be the formation master—the designated “key” who can navigate the arrays. If that person is separated from the group? Someone dies in the next ten minutes. It’s a narrative device that forces characters—and readers—to confront the consequences of strategic miscalculations, even for geniuses. Here, Shaoyan Nong’s decision to leave Xiangliu Fang behind is already costing lives.

**Wanxiang Suicide Soldiers (死士)**: The presence of ten Wanxiang Adepts deployed as disposable shields is a chilling glimpse into how major clans operate. A Wanxiang Adept is already a rare and powerful figure in most local sects, but a top-tier clan like Shaoyan Nong’s treats them as expendable resources. It’s a brutal economic statement: when you’re the heir of a Primordial Fiendgod clan, “consumable talent” is a line item in your expedition budget.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is one of those “oh no” inflection points that experienced Xianxia readers can smell from a mile away. The frost arrow barrage was already setting off alarm bells, but the reveal of the eighteen Qiongqi puppets is the true kicker—it tells you that the Immortal estate is not testing these cultivators; it’s actively trying to kill them. There’s a grim satisfaction in seeing the group’s tactical hierarchy stress-test under real pressure: Ji Ning’s analytical calm, Mu Northson’s panic, Shaoyan Nong’s desperate regret. Pay attention to how the chapter frames the difference between “strong in a duel” (full-power sword strikes only stagger a puppet) and “surviving a war of attrition” (the formation has unlimited energy, you don’t). That contrast is going to be the central tension going forward.
