## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, the mountain that is the Black-White College entrance exam is finally being climbed, and the summit is *right there*! This chapter throws us headfirst into the final, nail-biting round. It’s a gauntlet, a bottleneck, a one-on-one cage match of talent versus seniority. Our boy Ji Ning walks into a tunnel with a smug senior cultivator waiting at the end, and what follows is a masterclass in why raw power isn't everything—until you have a sword formation big enough to make up the difference. Get ready for the clash of the truly gifted.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Final Stage Announced:** The eight candidates are told they must each pass through a tunnel guarded by a single formal Black-White College disciple. The guard is limited to one technique, but the challenger can go all out. The goal is simply to reach the other end.
*   **The Cultivator Class Divide:** The Primal Daoist overseers discuss the likely pass rates. They are unanimous: Ji Ning is the only guaranteed success. They pity Hua Chong, the nephew of Fairy Hua Yun, believing he has no real chance, having relied on trickery to pass the earlier trial.
*   **The Arrogant Guardian:** Ji Ning chooses a tunnel guarded by Yinxue Wenjian, a prideful sword-freak from a major tribe. Yinxue Wenjian is openly arrogant, dismissing Ji Ning by saying, "We won't be meeting again," implying his immediate and total defeat.
*   **The First Exchange:** Wenjian opens with a powerful lightning-element technique, “Descent of the Thunder Swamp.” Ji Ning easily deflects it using his Water-Fire Lotus technique, but the force is enough to show the difference in their raw Yuan power.
*   **Overwhelming the Guardian:** When Wenjian unleashes his ultimate single-sword technique, “Prison of the Thunder God,” Ji Ning is forced to reveal his true hand: the full Lesser Thousand Sword Formation with 729 flying swords. The raw power of the formation shatters Wenjian’s attack, forcing him to break the rules and use his full arsenal (his Big Dipper Sword Formation) just to survive.
*   **The Unspoken Victory:** By using his other treasures, Wenjian admits defeat according to the rules of the trial. He turns and flees. Ji Ning calmly walks out of the tunnel, officially becoming a member of the Black-White College as he soars over the mountain peaks.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **The "Inner Disciple" Hubris:** In Xianxia, the gap between an outer disciple or a junior from a remote tribe and an inner disciple of a major sect is portrayed as an unbridgeable chasm. Yinxue Wenjian embodies this perfectly. His confidence isn’t just ego; it comes from the institutional power of the Black-White College. To a sect’s eyes, a "monster genius" from the wilds like Ji Ning is still a bumpkin until he proves he can master their high-grade arts. The narrative here brilliantly subverts this by showing a formal disciple being forced to cheat to keep his position.
*   **One Technique vs. All-Out:** The restriction of "one technique" is a classic trial trope. It’s designed to test a cultivator’s fundamental comprehension of a single Dao, rather than their ability to just brute-force through with a bag of tricks. Ji Ning, having comprehended two True Meanings and the Domain of the Dao, bypasses this by making his own comprehension (the sword formation) so potent that it forces the guardian to break the rule.
*   **The Meaning of a Sword Formation:** The Lesser Thousand Sword Formation isn't just a lot of swords. In Xianxia lore, a formation is a way to link the users’ understanding of the Dao (converted through the swords) into a coherent whole. The 81 core Human-ranked swords act as the directors, guiding the 600+ unranked swords like a choir of magic. This allows even a "simple" cultivator to punch way above their weight class—a perfect illustration of the Xianxia principle that intelligence and comprehension can overcome raw power.

### Domain of the Dao

The ability to project one's comprehension of the Dao as a persistent field of influence; a mark of a monster genius.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a clinic in layered tension. The setup—eight tunnels, all equal—is a quiet nod to the cruel lottery of fate. Watch how the counselors casually write off the other candidates; it sets a grim mood that highlights just how high the stakes are. Then, the payoff with Ji Ning vs. Yinxue Wenjian is perfect. The fight isn't just about flashy moves; it's about *domination*. Ji Ning doesn't win by sneaking past; he wins by being so overwhelming that he forces the other genius to admit he broke the rules just to survive. It’s a statement. He isn’t just at the level of these formal disciples; his *potential* terrifies them. Enjoy the sweet, sweet sound of Ji Ning’s peerless talent forcing the established hierarchy to bend.

### Deep Lore: The Dao of "Saving Face"
The entire final duel is a masterclass in the Xianxia concept of "face" (面子, *miànzi*). Yinxue Wenjian is obsessed with *his* face, refusing to allow any junior to embarrass him. He opens with arrogance, a form of "attacking face." When Ji Ning counters, Wenjian loses a bit of face but can still claim it was a warm-up. The moment he summons his Big Dipper formation, he has *lost* all face—he has broken the rules, admitting he couldn't beat a newbie with the allowed handicap. His silent, humiliated retreat is the ultimate consequence; his reputation within the college is now tarnished. For the reader, watching a proud senior lose face is a deeply satisfying payoff, a fundamental pleasure of the genre.
