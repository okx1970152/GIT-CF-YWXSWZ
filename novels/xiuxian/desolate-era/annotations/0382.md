## Chapter Overview

Holy smokes, folks. Our boy Darknorth just went full reaper-mode on the Dongliu Monster Kings, and he made it look *easy*. After the tactical setup in previous chapters, we finally get to see Ji Ning's full, terrifying combat effectiveness—from his second truebody raining immortal-level Sword Immortal devastation from a distance, to his true body wading into the fray like an unstoppable Fiendgod. The chapter isn't just about the slaughter, though. It's about the cold calculation behind it. He let them see his "weakness," baited them into overconfidence, then flipped the switch. But the real meat? The philosophically rich dialogue with the Golden-Haired Bear King. It's a masterclass in showing that not all monsters are mindless villains, and that Ji Ning's path is a pragmatic one, not a blind crusade of extermination. Get ready for a deep dive into the art of the Xianxia snatch-and-grab, the weight of karmic sin, and a cliffhanger that leaves us wanting that final, glorious technique.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Painless Rout:** Ji Ning, with his second truebody and true body working in tandem, systematically dismantles the Dongliu Monster Kings. The second truebody’s Plowshares Sword Formation (a complete, top-grade Immortal flying sword set) dishes out Celestial Immortal-level damage from afar, while his Fiendgod true body bullies the scattered Immortal Kings in close quarters. The Flying Bear Formation was their only hope; without it, they're just eleven Loose Immortals.
*   **Eight Out of Twelve Down:** Despite his overwhelming advantage, Ji Ning laments only killing eight. The remaining four, including the crafty Zhu Yan and the invulnerable Golden-Haired Bear, use superior escape arts to flee. This isn't just a tally; it's a record of failure against the *Eight-Nine Arcane Art* trial requirement.
*   **The Golden-Haired Bear's Confession:** The lone surviving leader doesn't flee in blind rage. Instead, he confronts Ji Ning, demanding to understand the reason for the massacre. This leads to a pivotal revelation: the Golden-Haired Bear is fundamentally different from his subordinates. He deliberately cultivates with restraint, avoiding karmic sin to survive his Tribulations, while the others fell into despair and madness.
*   **A Dao-Heart Revealed:** Ji Ning explains his trial from Patriarch Subhuti: a test of killing ten beings of great sin. This isn't about personal grievance or even just accumulating merit; it's a targeted, sacred mission to train for the ultimate divine ability. He even engages the Golden-Haired Bear for a genuine power assessment.
*   **The Witnesses:** Back at the cliff, the stunned Qi Kingdom prince and princess witness the aftermath. Ji Ning casually displays the eight Immortal-ranked treasures as proof of the massacre, fulfilling his promise to them regarding the Firewing Monster King with a chilling nonchalance. The chapter ends with him walking away, needing two more sinners to complete his trial.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Eight-Nine Arcane Art & The Trial of Sin:** The *Eight-Nine Arcane Art* is the supreme defensive divine ability beneath the True God level. It's passively always active, requiring no divine power to maintain, which makes it a god-tier investment. But the training is brutal and absurdly expensive. The trial itself—killing ten beings of great sin—is a classic Xianxia motif. It validates the concept of "Karmic Merit vs. Karmic Sin" as a measurable, tangible force. A demon who has killed thousands radiates "bloody light," which isn't just a colorful description; it's a sign of a soul so tainted it's practically a beacon. Killing them grants you Karmic Merit, which literally helps you survive your Heavenly Tribulation. It's cosmic brownie points for the soul.

**The Scattered Immortal Power Scale:** This chapter gives us an incredible, authorial ranking of power. It isn't just "levels." It's a timeline.
*   **Hundred-thousand-year Loose Immortals:** The baseline.
*   **Fifty-thousand-year Loose Immortals:** The typical "Dongliu" Monster King.
*   **Million-year Loose Immortals:** The monster tier. Beating these guys puts you on par with the likes of Senior Beixing and the Golden-Haired Bear.
*   **Multi-Million-year Loose Immortals:** Think of old-timers like Celestial Immortal Juhua. They've had eons to refine their art.
The Golden-Haired Bear’s statement that he's fought a dozen Celestial Immortals to a standstill is jaw-dropping. It means Ji Ning, with his *second truebody's* "average Celestial Immortal" level power, is now a force that can contend with immortals who are millennia old! This is the exponential growth of a true genius.

**Monster King Morality:** This isn't a world of purely evil monsters. The Golden-Haired Bear is a masterclass in a "pragmatic" villain. He's ruled, he's fought, but he has a Dao-heart. He avoids sin to survive. He is more powerful than his subordinates, not just in combat, but in wisdom. He doesn't hate Ji Ning for killing his "brothers"; he respects the trial. This nuanced character is a hallmark of great Xianxia—it shows that the path of the Dao is universal, and even a monster king can tread a road of relative righteousness.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow Daoists, we just witnessed a surgical strike on a scale that redefines "power level" in this arc. But this chapter is a masterclass in the *aftermath*. It’s not just about the loot (though, *eight* Immortal-ranked treasures? Yes, please). It's about the psychological chess game Ji Ning is playing. By letting the Golden-Haired Bear live, he made a strategic ally and a venerable source of intel. By fulfilling his promise to the Qi prince, he closed a karmic loop without unnecessary fuss. And by lamenting his missing two kills, we get a perfect narrative hook: he’s going to find another nest of righteous sinners somewhere in this vast continent. The quiet moment with Qingya Xiaoyu and the stunned royal siblings perfectly contrasts the brutal violence of the battle with the quiet dignity of a cultivator who has *already moved on to the next step.* Get ready for Ji Ning to become a hunter, scouring the land not for treasure, but for karma itself. The Eight-Nine Arcane Art is getting closer.
