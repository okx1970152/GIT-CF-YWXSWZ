## Chapter Overview
This chapter serves up a colossal, delicious slice of *schadenfreude* as Ji Ning returns to his island home only to find a small army of Seamless Gate elites waiting to assassinate him... and they promptly run into the biggest, baddest iron wall in the entire Three Realms. The mysterious old gardener sheds his disguise to reveal himself as none other than **Elder Yuan**, a top-tier Daofather who has been chilling on the island for months. What follows is a masterclass in the power hierarchy of Xianxia: the arrogant hunters become the helpless prey, frozen mid-air like insects in amber, before being casually snipped out of existence by a pair of ordinary-looking gardening shears. It’s a brutal, hilarious, and immensely satisfying reminder that in the world of immortals, there is *always* a bigger fish.

## Key Plot Points
- The Seamless Gate assassination squad, led by True Immortal Flowing Hua, successfully teleports directly to Brightmoon Island, thanks to their mastery of the Grand Dao of Space.
- Just as they prepare to strike Ji Ning, an overwhelming force freezes all fifty-five of them in place, completely paralyzing their divine power and Pure Yang ki.
- The old gardener, whom Ji Ning and Yu Wei had ignored as a mere mortal servant, reveals himself to be a supreme Daofather: **Elder Yuan**.
- Elder Yuan toys with the terrified Seamless Gate cultivators, eventually asking Ji Ning to be the one to decide their fate—a test of the young Sword Immortal's Dao-heart.
- After Ji Ning decisively answers "they deserve death," Elder Yuan casually uses his iron shears to snip every single one of them out of existence, including True Immortals and True Gods.
- The sheer value of the loot is staggering: three supreme-grade Pure Yang treasures and one Innate spirit-treasure, which Elder Yuan dismissively gives to Ji Ning.
- Elder Yuan reveals he has been observing Ji Ning's sword practice and, more importantly, his Heartforce. He bluntly tells Ji Ning he is "dense" for not extending his Heartforce mastery beyond his sword-fingers to his entire body for evasion.
- The chapter ends with Elder Yuan puffing out his chest and telling Ji Ning to **bow and become his disciple**, setting the stage for a major new mentorship.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Elder Yuan (源老人) and the 'Four Ancestors of the River Yuan' (源河四祖)**
Elder Yuan is a *Chaos True God*—a True God born directly from the primordial chaos, not cultivated from mortality. This places him in a different league from newly ascended True Gods. His faction represents a classic Xianxia power structure: the neutral "wild card" superpower. The Four Ancestors are four top-tier Daofathers bound by unbreakable brotherhood, a trope known as *sworn brotherhood* or *blood brothers* (结拜兄弟). This kind of faction is so powerful that both sides of a cosmic war actively court it, giving its members a terrifying degree of impunity. As the text notes, Elder Yuan could kill Lü Dongbin (the beloved disciple of the Daoist leader himself) without fear of retribution, but a hostile Daofather like Patriarch Subhuti could not.

**The Iron Shears and Divine Power**
The fact that Elder Yuan kills supreme Immortals and True Gods with a pair of mundane *gardening shears* is a classic demonstration of a supreme being's power. It’s not about the quality of the weapon; it’s about the user's comprehension of the Dao. At this level, the user's will and understanding of reality are so absolute that a blade of grass can become a universe-destroying weapon. This is a core Xianxia aesthetic: the greatest masters often look like the most ordinary people, using the most ordinary tools, to perform the most extraordinary feats.

**Heartforce (心力) as a Training System**
Elder Yuan's critique of Ji Ning is a fantastic look at how the secondary system of Heartforce works. Unlike Ki or Divine Power, which are about energy quantity and quality, Heartforce is about **will and projection**. It is incredibly difficult to master, requiring years of meditative insight just to apply it to a single body part (the sword-finger). A proper teacher, however, can skip over mountains of trial and error, providing the correct "formula" for applying it to the whole body, which would revolutionize the disciple's speed and evasion capabilities. This opens a massive new power vector for Ji Ning.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, fellow Daoists, because Ji Ning is about to get his hands on the ultimate cheat code: a mentor who is actually a *specialist* in his weakest area. For all of Ji Ning's insane talent with a sword, his defensive and escape capabilities have always lagged behind his offense. Elder Yuan isn't just a powerful patron; he is the key to unlocking an entirely new dimension of Ji Ning's power. This chapter is a turning point that transitions Ji Ning from a regional genius with a sword into a terrifyingly well-rounded combatant who will soon be untouchable. Watch how the tone shifts from the cold, clinical fear of the Seamless Gate assassins to the warm, playful, and deeply rewarding dynamic between a doting (and eccentric) grandmaster and his new, brilliant student.

## The Politics of Killing
A key nuance to appreciate is *why* Elder Yuan can kill these people when Patriarch Subhuti could not. It's not just about raw power. The Seamless Gate and the Three Realms are locked in a cold war. Daofather Subhuti is the commander of an opposing army. If he kills their soldiers, the other side will respond in kind. But Elder Yuan is the leader of a *neutral* faction that both sides are trying to win over. If they attack him for killing their people, they lose any chance of his alliance. He can commit acts of violence with complete political impunity because the cost of retaliation is far higher than the cost of swallowing the loss. This is a very realpolitik look at the "soft power" dynamics within a Xianxia universe's upper echelons.
