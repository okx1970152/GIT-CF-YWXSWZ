## Chapter Overview

This chapter serves a delicious double shot of Xianxia awesomeness. First, we reunite with Ji Ning and Yu Wei, whose teamwork chemistry is off the charts—ice phoenix meets fire lotus, and the token talismans start rolling in. But just when you think the chapter is about power-couple synergy, we get yanked into a cave where a greasy, bone-chewing slob we’ve barely noticed casually obliterates a squad of elite sect disciples and reveals an honest-to-gods top-100 divine ability. The Xia Emperor and his Pure Yang True Immortal retinue are shook. We, the readers, are shook. This is the kind of narrative whiplash that makes the Immortal Fortunes Assembly an unforgettable arc.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Ning rescues Yu Wei from a three-on-one ambush, quickly collecting the token talismans and fleeing before more contestants converge. The duo demonstrates lethal efficiency—Ji Ning’s Water-Fire Lotus and Yu Wei’s dual-phoenix secret art complement each other perfectly.
- In a quiet cave, Yu Wei admits she was moments from using a desperation Dao talisman, and they have a brief, heartwarming exchange about sharing token talismans—Ji Ning tries to give her the spoils of the kill, but she counters with the reminder that he saved her life. Their relationship matures with mutual respect and subtle affection.
- Time-skip montage: over a month, the two dominate together. Wufeng Immortal boasts to rival sects about his disciples, confirming that Ji Ning and Yu Wei are both on track for the top 96.
- Meanwhile, the Messy Daoist (Sanpang) is chilling in a cave, deliberately lying low for the first three months to let the weak get weeded out. He even has the audacity to gnaw on a giant bone mid-competition.
- Fate intervenes—eight disciples from the Ten Thousand Beasts Sect barge into his cave. Sanpang tries to play nice, but they force his hand. He unleashes the Black Tortoise Divine Ability, transforming into a turtle-snake hybrid monster and effortlessly killing six of them while two flee in terror.
- The Xia Emperor and the observing Pure Yang True Immortals immediately recognize the divine ability and discuss its terrifying pedigree—it’s one of the top hundred divine abilities since Pangu, belonging to none other than the Black Tortoise Grand Emperor of the Three Realms.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Black Tortoise Divine Ability (玄武大神通)** — In Chinese mythology, the Black Tortoise (Xuanwu) is one of the Four Symbols, representing the north and winter. It is often depicted as a tortoise intertwined with a serpent. In Desolate Era, divine abilities that rank in the top hundred since Pangu are *legendary*. The fact that Sanpang, a seemingly lazy and overlooked contender, wields such a power instantly rewrites his threat level. This is a classic Xianxia trope: the hidden expert who deliberately flies under the radar (or in his case, sleeps and eats bones) and then casually one-shots a team of elite geniuses. The Xia Emperor’s reaction underscores how rare and valuable such an inheritance is—the Black Tortoise Grand Emperor is a real heavyweight in the pantheon.

**Ten Thousand Beasts Sect (万兽门)** — A major superpower sect from the Eastern Seas, far stronger than the Black-White College. Their disciples specialize in beast-taming and beast-related secret arts. The leader’s “Crane Soaring Nine Heavens” secret art is noted to be comparable to the Lesser Thousand Sword Formation in raw potential, showing that even among top-tier sects, individual talent still determines outcomes.

**Pure Yang True Immortals (纯阳真仙)** — These are beings who have undergone the Nine Tribulations, shed their mortal bodies, and achieved a state of pure, immortal yang energy. They can oversee the entire Brightmoon Landscape World without leaving the Tianmang Hall. Their sudden shift from casual banter to solemn acknowledgment when Sanpang reveals his trump card shows just how seriously they take Black Tortoise-level inheritances.

## Reading Guide

If you came for epic power-couple dynamics, this chapter delivers—but the real feast is the Messy Daoist’s mic-drop moment. Pay close attention to how the narrative builds him up as a background character (the “third-generation senior apprentice-brother” everyone dismisses) before shattering expectations. This is a masterclass in Xianxia pacing: the first three months are the “predator culling” phase, and Sanpang is playing it smart by waiting for the loot to consolidate. His nonchalant attitude (gnawing a bone while eight killers surround him) is peak “I’m too lazy to even pretend to be scared.” Also, note the contrast between Yu Wei’s vulnerable confession and her authoritative combat presence—her character layers are thickening beautifully. Get ready for some serious power re-evaluation in the coming rounds.
