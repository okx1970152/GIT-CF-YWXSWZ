## Chapter Overview

Take a deep breath, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a pure adrenaline shot straight into the heart of Xianxia: **the gambling duel**. Ji Ning steps onto the stage of Anchan City for the very first time, and he does not come to play. When his friend Beishan Baiwei is publicly humiliated by the arrogant Beishan Hu, Ji Ning volunteers to fight a monstrous rhinoceros great demon that has already learned divine abilities. Everyone expects him to die—he’s only a late-stage Zifu Disciple in Fiendgod Body Refining and barely early-stage Zifu in Ki Refining. But Ji Ning has been hiding his true power for too long. This is the moment he decides to stop lying low and **declare himself a monstrous genius**. The result? One of the most satisfying beatdowns you’ll ever read, complete with spectacular Water-Fire Lotuses and a decapitation that silences an entire hall of nobles. Get ready to cheer.

## Key Plot Points

- Beishan Baiwei is taunted by Beishan Hu, who has a rhinoceros great demon trained in Heavenly Transformation and other divine abilities. Baiwei is hesitant to risk Ji Ning, but Ji Ning insists: the prize is Primordial Liquid, and he’s confident.
- Ji Ning makes an internal decision: no more hiding. Anchan City is where he will shine as a true “monstrous genius” to attract the attention of major sects and grand powers.
- At the Wuyou Caverns, Ji Ning’s strength is tested by two crystals. The results—late Zifu Fiendgod, early Zifu Ki—draw widespread mockery. Everyone writes him off.
- The duel begins. The rhinoceros uses Heavenly Transformation to become over twenty zhang tall. Ji Ning’s speed and sword art are on an entirely different level: he casually dodges the demon’s charges and slices off one of its hooves.
- The rhinoceros refuses to surrender, believing his Fiendgod regeneration makes him unkillable. Ji Ning ends the debate by decapitating the beast with a single precise stroke, then summoning twelve massive Water-Fire Lotuses to grind the demon’s body to pulp.
- Ji Ning looks up at a stunned Beishan Hu and coolly repeats his earlier warning: “I told you to prepare your second subordinate.” The win is absolute.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Gambling Battles (赌战) at Wuyou Caverns**: This is a classic Xianxia “face-slapping” scenario. Nobles stake huge sums of Primordial Liquid on life-or-death fights between their retainers or pet demons. The brutality is part of the entertainment—the spectators treat the fighters as mere assets, cheering for blood. For the cultivators themselves, these duels are a fast track to reputation and resource, but also a grave risk. Ji Ning’s decision to step up is a calculated gamble: he knows he can win, but in doing so he publicly declares his power level and invites scrutiny.

**Heavenly Transformation (法天象地)**: This divine ability is a staple of Chinese mythology and cultivation fiction. It does not simply make the user bigger—it amplifies their mass, density, and power proportionally, turning a human-sized fighter into a living mountain. That Ji Ning, while much smaller, can physically challenge such a foe, highlights his superiority in technique and Dao comprehension.

**Water-Fire Lotus (水火莲花)**: This technique is born from Ji Ning’s cultivation of the Crimsonbright Diagram, which merges the essence of the Sunstar and the Taiyin Star. The lotus symbolizes the harmonious yet destructive interplay of water and fire. By manifesting twelve lotuses simultaneously, Ji Ning demonstrates an extraordinary depth of Dao insight, far beyond what his cultivation level would suggest. In Xianxia, such natural-formed manifestations are a hallmark of peerless talent.

## Reading Guide

Alright, let’s talk about what makes this chapter *so* cathartic. First, the setup: Beishan Hu is the classic arrogant young master—loud, boastful, and backed by his father’s shadow. Beishan Baiwei is the good noble, trapped by his own pride and desperate for a win. Then Ji Ning shows up, looking like a harmless kid in animal skins, and proceeds to dismantle a monster that could crush city walls. The joy here is in the *contrast*. Every scoff, every laugh from the audience gets turned back at them when Ji Ning’s swords start moving.

Pay close attention to Ji Ning’s inner monologue. This is a turning point. He’s done being cautious. In the Yan Mountains, he had to hide his strength because of local threats. But Anchan City is a bigger pond, and the only way to attract the best mentors and resources is to **blaze**. This mindset—actively courting the label of “monstrous genius”—is a classic progression fantasy beat. It signals that the protagonist is ready to climb the ladder aggressively.

Also, note the detail of the strength-testing crystals. Many Xianxia stories use such measurements to establish stakes: the audience sees the numbers and underestimates the hero until the fight proves otherwise. It’s a satisfying narrative trick that makes the eventual victory taste sweeter.

Finally, the Water-Fire Lotus is breathtakingly described. Twelve blooms. Each ten zhang across. Spinning and grinding a Xiantian-level Fiendgod into dust. That image is *pure Xianxia poetry*—violent, beautiful, and unmistakably the work of a future legend.

So sit back, enjoy the carnage, and remember: never bet against a man with a Dao heart as steady as Ji Ning’s.
