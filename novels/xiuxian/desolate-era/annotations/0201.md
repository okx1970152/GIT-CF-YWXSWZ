## Chapter Overview
Holy smokes, fellow Daoists—it’s a *reunion episode*! When Ji Ning and Mu Northson dive into a Wanxiang-level demon encirclement, they aren’t just looking for any random beast. The trapped Skygreen Serpent turns out to be none other than Ji Ning’s old sparring partner from his Eastmarsh Swamp days: the feisty, poison-breathed serpent who once called him a “stubborn brat.” What starts as a high-stakes rescue mission instantly flips into a walk down memory lane. But the real thrill? Seeing how far Ji Ning has come. Back then, he was a mere Houtian squirt yelling “Little Snakey Snake” from the mud. Now? He’s a Wanxiang-stage Sword Immortal who just one-shot a tortoise-shelled Wanxiang great demon like it was nothing. The Dragon Whale King—the apex predator of Yaolian Mountain—is not amused.

## Key Plot Points
1. **Divine Sense Confirmations**: Ji Ning, using his anomalously powerful divine sense, visually identifies the Skygreen Serpent among the chaos, noting the formation flags that create a space-locking barrier to prevent escape.
2. **Formation Rush**: Ji Ning and Mu Northson exploit a timing window to charge their dragon-headed warship *inside* the sealing formation before it fully activates, executing a classic Xianxia risk-reward play.
3. **One-Shot Supremacy**: The tortoise-shelled Wanxiang demon “Qushan” intercepts them. Ji Ning effortlessly bisects his human form with a single strike from the Northriver Sword Formation, forcing the demon to revert to its true form and flee. This establishes an instant power asymmetry.
4. **The Reunion**: The injured Skygreen Serpent recognizes Ji Ning’s face and voice. The dialogue shifts from deadly serious to warm and bantering, revealing their history in the Dongshan Swamp as rivals who grew beyond their old battleground.
5. **Intimidation Exchange**: The Dragon Whale King offers a condescending ultimatum. The Skygreen Serpent privately warns Ji Ning of the King’s true strength—Fiendgod Body Refining, Wanxiang perfection, an innate divine ability. Ji Ning, utterly unimpressed, mirrors the ultimatum back: “I’ll give you two choices. One: I take the snake. You live. Two: I kill all of you, *then* I take the snake.”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Power of a Reunion in Xianxia**: In Chinese cultivation novels, chance encounters between old friends are almost sacred. They transform a generic fight scene into a high-emotional-stakes event. Ji Ning doesn’t owe this serpent a thing—they were never close friends; they were rivals. But in the harsh code of the Xianxia wilds, *a rival you’ve bled against is closer than a stranger*. This bond from shared struggle is the bedrock of countless Dao-companion and brotherhood narratives.

**“Courting Death” as a Narrative Signal**: Ji Ning’s muttered “Courting death” before cutting Qushan in half is not just a badass line—it’s a genre-signal. In Xianxia, when the protagonist utters these words, it signifies the activation of “Overwhelming Power Mode.” It tells the reader: the gap is so vast that the opponent’s resistance is comically irrelevant. The author uses this short phrase to compress an entire scene of tension into a single, satisfying release.

**The Dragon Whale – Apex of Aquatic Demons**: The Dragon Whale (龙鲸, *Lóng Jīng*) is a mythical hybrid that combines the serpentine mystique of a dragon (*龙*) with the brute oceanic mass of a whale (*鲸*). In the novel’s cultivation world, this makes it an apex species that naturally masters both water-type energy manipulation and devastating physical body-crushing techniques, especially when paired with Fiendgod Body Refining. The Skygreen Serpent’s terrified warning (“He alone is stronger than the other fifteen combined”) is a classic foreshadowing tool—the protagonist must overcome a qualitative leap in enemy scale.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a masterclass in “Don’t Blink.” This is one of those short-but-lean chapters that Xianxia does best: it delivers a satisfying power flex (Ji Ning is now *very* scary), a heartwarming character moment (the “Little Snakey Snake” callback is pure gold), and sets up a major clash cliffhanger. Pay attention to the sheer self-confidence radiating from Ji Ning. He doesn’t just banter—he gives the Dragon Whale King a face-saving exit, and when the King takes the bait of arrogance, Ji Ning’s counter-ultimatum is essentially a declaration of war. The subtext here is that our boy has leveled up so dramatically that even a Wanxiang perfection-stage demon king is just another obstacle to be trampled. Also, keep an eye on Mu Northson’s silent, smiling ease in the background—it tells you everything about the trust these two already share.
