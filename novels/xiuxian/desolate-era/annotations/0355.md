## Chapter Overview
The dust settles on the Immortal Fortunes Assembly, and the aftermath is a bittersweet symphony of farewells and new beginnings. The True Immortals and Empyrean Gods depart like fading stars, each taking their newly claimed disciples to the infinite cosmos beyond the Grand Xia. For Ji Ning, the number one spot is a triumph tinged with melancholy. He watches helplessly as his Dao-companion, Yu Wei, vanishes into the clouds with her new master, the legendary Lu Dongbin. The raw, aching separation between them forms the emotional core of this chapter, a poignant reminder that even the mightiest cultivators are bound by the threads of the heart. Amidst the celebrations and congratulations from his Anchan Commandery allies, Ji Ning quietly awaits his own fate, unaware that an enigmatic, unfathomably powerful visitor has already slipped past all defenses to find him.

## Key Plot Points
- The cosmic hierarchy of the Three Realms is subtly reinforced as Daofathers and True Immortals take center stage, with the Celestial Thearch and his mortal emperor serving merely as stewards of their will.
- Yu Wei is formally taken as a disciple by Pure Yang True Immortal Lu Dongbin, and the couple's deep, emotional farewell underscores the heavy price of pursuing the Dao—the inevitable separation from loved ones.
- Mu Zishuo sees Ji Ning off at the King Yan's estate, their brotherly bond a stark contrast to the celestial coldness of the immortal world.
- Ji Ning returns to his estate, only to find his formidable formation-based defenses entirely bypassed by a mysterious white-robed youth whose power rivals that of the Grand Xia Emperor himself.
- The mysterious senior casually jokes with Ji Ning, hinting at a grand purpose for his visit, leaving both Ji Ning and the readers on the edge of their seats.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Weight of the Dao Heart and Farewell**
This chapter masterfully explores the quintessential Xianxia theme: the eternal conflict between the quest for immortality and human attachment. Yu Wei and Ji Ning's farewell is not a melodramatic sob-fest but a quiet, stoic exchange of trust. This reflects the core belief that a true cultivator's Dao-heart must be as firm as steel, yet it is precisely these deep attachments that often provide the strongest motivation for growth. Ji Ning's vow is not a promise of happiness, but a promise to *wait*—a subtle but powerful commitment in a world where time spans eons.
**The Unseen Guardians of the Cosmos**
The chapter reveals the true power structure of the Three Realms. The Celestial Thearch and even the Grand Xia Emperor are essentially regional managers; the real authority lies with the Daofathers and Pure Yang True Immortals who can snatch disciples from any corner of the world. This reinforces the 'Iceberg Theory' of Xianxia world-building—the power we see on the surface is just a fraction of the terrifying depth beneath.
**The Silent Gate-Crasher**
The mysterious white-robed youth effortlessly bypassing King Yan's estate formations is a classic 'hidden expert' trope. His casual demeanor and the fact that Ji Ning's artifact spirit, Yellow Bear, admits it cannot even *probe* him, establishes a chasm in power that is both humbling and exciting for the reader. In Xianxia, when an expert of this level takes the initiative to visit a junior, it always signals a monumental opportunity or a world-shaking mission.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, fellow Daoists, because the real game is about to begin! The tournament arc is over, and our boy Ji Ning has earned his reputation. But as we all know, the title of 'First Place' is just a fancy business card in the cosmic job market. The true prizes are the masters you attract. Yu Wei's departure is a gut-punch, but it's also a necessary pruning of attachments for her growth. Pay close attention to the quiet intimacy between Ji Ning and Yu Wei—it's a far cry from the political tension with Nine Lotos. The real highlight of this chapter, however, is the cliffhanger. Who is this white-robed visitor? Is he a new master? An agent of a hidden power? Or perhaps... a problem? The way he slipped past King Yan's defenses is a terrifying display of power, setting the stage for a development that will propel Ji Ning far beyond the boundaries of the Grand Xia. Don't blink!

## Deeper Dive: The Myth Behind Lu Dongbin
The appearance of Lu Dongbin as Yu Wei's master is a brilliant nod to Chinese folklore. Lu Dongbin is one of the legendary **Eight Immortals (八仙)**, a group of Daoist deities who achieved immortality through various trials and spiritual practices. He is typically depicted as a scholarly, wise, and slightly mischievous figure, often carrying a magical sword that slays evil spirits. By making him a Pure Yang True Immortal in Desolate Era, the author directly ties his mythological power of sword-wielding and demon-slaying to the novel's cultivation system, instantly giving Yu Wei a prestigious and powerful lineage. Her path for the foreseeable future is tied to the highest echelons of the immortal world.
