## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, prepare your spiritual teas! This chapter marks a classic turning point in our favorite Sword Immortal’s journey. After drifting aimlessly down the menacing Evil Dragon River like a true wandering cultivator, Ji Ning stumbles upon a literal beacon of light—a maidservant wreathed in a jaw-dropping, three-hundred-zhang halo of golden karmic merit. But in Xianxia, treasure is never unguarded, and this particular treasure comes with a tail of demonic hunters. As our man Northmont casually invites a fallen royal family onto his ship, the stage is set for a collision between mercy and the relentless killing machine of the Firewing Legion. Get ready for the first real lead in Ji Ning’s disciple hunt, and the bloody storm that’s about to follow.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Evil Dragon River:** Ji Ning has been drifting on this legendary, flight-suppressing river for nearly half a month, unsuccessfully searching for beings of great sin or merit. The setting itself is a classic Xianxia “forbidden zone,” stripping cultivators of their aerial mobility and forcing a ground-level game of survival.
- **The Encounter:** After a bit of idle bamboo-leaf playing (a flex of his carefree Dao-heart), Ji Ning spots a group of nine approaching the river. He jumps ashore with deliberate clumsiness, leading the paranoid Qi Kingdom remnants to mistake him for a mortal—a classic misdirect.
- **Aura Face-Off:** The Wanxiang prince tries to intimidate Ji Ning with his aura, only for our Primal-level protagonist to release a wave of pressure that completely dwarfs theirs. The prince quickly shifts to diplomacy, using a water-drop technique to discreetly analyze Ji Ning’s cultivation level.
- **The Disciple Candidate:** Ji Ning’s gaze locks onto Qi Xiaoyu, a maidservant with an absurdly vast aura of golden karmic merit (300 zhang). He invites the whole group aboard his boat, revealing his intent to investigate her background for potential discipleship.
- **The Hunters’ Arrival:** The chapter cuts to a parallel scene: a black greatship flying the banner of the ‘Firewing Legion,’ the personal elite guard of the ‘Firewing Demon King.’ All twelve members are at least Wanxiang level, and they are closing in on the very same imperial remnants Ji Ning is questioning.
- **The Looming Battle:** The Firewing Legion’s captain detects the Qi Kingdom remnants within 10,000 li. Their greedy chatter about the Qi Kingdom’s legendary treasure hoard and their casual dismissal of a lone Wanxiang Adept sets up the inevitable clash—a pack of seasoned killers about to bite off more than they can chew by targeting Ji Ning’s ship.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Evil Dragon River as a ‘Geographical Equalizer’:** This is a staple trope in high-level xianxia: a location that suppresses flight and certain elemental escapes, forcing all cultivators, regardless of rank, to engage on the same terrestrial plane. It creates a tense, survival-horror atmosphere where ambushes and riverine tactics dominate, and a cultivator’s mobility advantage is nullified.
- **Analyzing Aura with Water:** The prince’s act of smearing his eyes with water is not just a nervous gesture; it is a specific, low-grade divination technique that allows him to perceive the ripples of a cultivator’s energy on the surface of water. It’s a clever, resourceful trick from a prince who has survived decades of pursuit, using whatever method he can to gauge an unknown powerful foe.
- **The Firewing Legion as a Demon King’s Elite:** The Firewing Legion represents a classic demon power structure. A Demon King, at or above the Primal level (likely a Loose Immortal or Earth Immortal), builds a nation of demon subjects and uses an elite force to hunt down any opposition. Their casual mention of killing a Primal Daoist last time highlights the gap between a roaming solo cultivator and a well-funded, coordinated military unit. Their greed for the Qi Kingdom’s “treasure hoard” is a direct nod to the classic xianxia motivation for empire-building—a legendary legacy left by a powerful predecessor.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a perfect example of the “implosion setup” technique common in progression fantasy. The narrative deliberately builds two parallel threads: a peaceful, almost domestic chat about the merits of a potential disciple on a boat, juxtaposed with the cold, hungry approach of a demonic war party. As a reader, you know the exact moment these two timelines will collide, and the tension is masterfully drawn out. Watch how Ji Ning’s recruitment of Qi Xiaoyu isn’t just about finding a talented student; he is also inadvertently creating a protective shield over a group of marked fugitives. The Firewing Legion believes they are hunting crippled, helpless prey, unaware that the target they are about to surround is under the protection of a Primal-level Sword Immortal with a Starseizing Hand. The coming fight is less a battle and more a lesson in what happens when predators forget that the sheep they are eyeing is standing next to a dragon. Fellow Daoists, prepare for a satisfying dose of karmic justice.

## The Weight of Karmic Merit
In the world of *Desolate Era*, a cultivator’s golden halo of karmic merit is not just a nice glow—it is a tangible, cosmic credit score tracked by the Heavenly Dao itself. Qi Xiaoyu’s halo of 300 zhang (over 900 meters!) is staggeringly vast. To put it in perspective, if a mortal’s life-saving deed gives off a glow the size of a candle, her aura is a lighthouse. This level of merit is almost impossible to gain in a single lifetime through mere good deeds; it is more likely a reward from a past life, or she is a reincarnation of a being who accumulated immense virtue. This makes her an incredibly rare and valuable find for Ji Ning, as taking her as a disciple would bring a cascade of good karma to his own path. However, such a being is also a target. Her very presence attracts the attention of powerful entities—both good (like Ji Ning) and evil (like the Firewing Legion, who would see value in capturing such a potent karmic vessel).
