## Chapter Overview
Get your popcorn ready, fellow Daoists, because it’s time for a pure, unadulterated curb-stomp battle! Ji Ning has finally had enough of being the hunted and is now the hunter. This chapter is a masterclass in the Desolate Era power fantasy, where our boy unleashes the full might of his Lesser Thousand Sword Formation for the first time on a grand scale. The result? Three Wanxiang-level great demons, who thought they were the top predators of a deadly wasteland, get turned into loot pinatas in a matter of seconds. It is a glorious, one-sided massacre that reminds us exactly why Ji Ning is considered a 'monster genius' of the Black-White College. We also get a touching side-plot with a terrified child, reminding us that in this brutal world of demon kings and immortal cultivators, there is still a place for compassion. Read on as Ji Ning’s sword-intent slices through demonic arrogance and straight into pure satisfaction.

## Key Plot Points
- The chapter opens by establishing the 'mutual protection pact' of eighteen Wanxiang great demons in the Mount Yujue Wasteland, a detail that immediately raises the stakes.
- Two demon kings, alerted by a signal talisman, rush to aid their "big brother," confident in their combined strength.
- Ji Ning calmly deploys his full Lesser Thousand Sword Formation (729 swords), its water-and-fire harmony creating a devastating 'Water-Fire Grinding Disc Sword' technique.
- In a breathtaking display of speed and power, Ji Ning one-shots the golden eagle demon king, its legendary lifeblood claws proving utterly useless against his sword-light.
- The second demon king, a four-hooved beast, panics and attempts to flee but is also instantly cleaved in two by the pursuing sword light.
- Mu Northson finishes off the original black-faced demon (the Blacktusk Elephant) with his own techniques, securing three massive demon corpses for their Yinglong Guard trial.
- Amidst the carnage, they find a traumatized young child who points towards a distant location, setting up a new narrative thread and a reminder of the human cost of this demon-infested wasteland.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a stunning showcase of the **Sword Immortal (剑仙)** archetype, a pinnacle trope in Xianxia. Unlike brute-force warriors, a Sword Immortal's lethality comes from an almost metaphysical level of precision and speed. Ji Ning's sword art isn't just powerful; it is *perfect* and *inescapable*. The great demons' lifeblood treasures—their claws and horns—are helpless because Ji Ning has transcended the level of "clashing with treasures." He has reached a state where his attack is a 'Dao manifestation,' a piece of cosmic truth that his enemies simply cannot grasp.

The concept of a demon's **Lifeblood Treasure (本命法宝)** is also crucial here. In Xianxia, a powerful demon can refine a part of its own body (like the eagle's claws) into a magical artifact. This treasure is bonded to their very soul, making it incredibly powerful and intuitive to use. The fact that Ji Ning's flying sword shatters this advantage with contemptuous ease is the ultimate power-level flex. It tells the reader: "This human's comprehension of the Dao has rendered your biological evolution obsolete."

Finally, the **Lesser Thousand Sword Formation** reaches its terrifying apex. The 'Sword Condensation' described in this chapter is the formation's ultimate expression. It takes the power of hundreds of swords and compresses it into a single, impossibly sharp blade of pure sword-intent. This isn't just a swarm of swords; it is a single, focused thought of annihilation.

## Reading Guide
Welcome to the part of the story where the protagonist starts fulfilling his potential. If the Black-White College trials were about proving he was a genius, the Mount Yujue Wasteland is about showing the *scale* of that genius. The core emotion here is catharsis. After all the setup, technique acquisition, and training, we finally see Ji Ning operate at the level of a true 'monster.' Let the heads of Wanxiang great demons roll.

Pay close attention to the contrast between the demons' arrogance and their despair. These weren't weaklings; they had a sophisticated alliance and battle tactics. Ji Ning doesn't just beat them; he breaks their spirit before he breaks their bodies. The single line, "He had never heard of such a sword art," is the perfect reaction from a broken opponent.

Also, don't overlook the child. This isn't a random encounter. In Xianxia, a seemingly insignificant NPC in a dangerous area is usually a plot hook. The child’s tearful pointing is an invitation for our heroes—and the reader—into the next adventure. It’s the classic trope of "rescuing the weak leads to a new trial," ensuring that their quest for the Yinglong Guard token is far more complicated than a simple three-month hunt.
