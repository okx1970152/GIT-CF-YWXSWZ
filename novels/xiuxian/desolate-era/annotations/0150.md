## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow Daoists, pin your ears back—this is the chapter where Ji Ning goes from "interesting newcomer" to "absolute must-recruit" in the eyes of the Anchan Commandery's movers and shakers. Our boy throws down a Rainwater Sword Domain at the early Zifu stage, and the whole Wuyou Caverns just freezes. Beishan Hu? He does a 180 faster than you can say "courting death," swallowing his pride and trying to play nice. Meanwhile, the elite scions of every major sect, tribe, and cult come flocking like moths to a flame. It's a masterclass in how raw talent reshapes the political chessboard overnight—and a reminder that in this world, your backing matters a lot, but your own Dao matters even more.

## Key Plot Points
- The White Witch prepares to burn her life force with forbidden sect techniques, forcing Ji Ning to ready the Water-Fire Lotus for a decisive kill.
- Beishan Hu suddenly concedes the battle mid-swing, shocking the White Witch and everyone present, and does a full face-turn from mortal enemy to gracious admirer.
- Ziyi the fox-attendant publicly re-labels Ji Ning from "Beishan Baiwei's cultivator" to "Young Master Ji Ning," signaling the official arrival of a new monster talent in Anchan City.
- The onlookers—all Wanxiang Adepts and scions of great powers—immediately understand what a Zifu-level Domain of the Dao means: a guaranteed Primal Daoist, with an excellent shot at Immortality.
- Beishan Hu, despite his earlier bloodlust, pulls back with politician-level precision, claiming all enmity was aimed at Beishan Baiwei and even trying to poison the well between the two friends.
- A parade of recruiters descends: Hu Shui of the Qianchuan Sect, a black-robed man from another faction, and representatives from the Meng clan and beyond all try to lock down Ji Ning.
- Beishan Baiwei privately warns Ji Ning not to commit to any sect on the spot, and Ji Ning wisely stalls while absorbing the full roster of Anchan Commandery powers.
- The Fire Cauldron Youth (Huo Qian) leaves brooding, suspicious that Ji Ning might be a reincarnated exalted one—a thread that will clearly matter later.
- Beishan Hu, back on his chariot, analyzes the political fallout with cold pragmatism, realizing he may have just helped his rival Beishan Baiwei gain an asset that could swing the succession for the Marquisate.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The "Domain of the Dao" (道之域境) Threshold**: In standard cultivation progression, a Domain of the Dao (the ability to project your understanding of an aspect of the Dao as a persistent field of influence) is normally only achievable at the Primal Daoist stage—the tier where your soul has undergone qualitative transformation. For a Zifu Disciple to wield one is the Xianxia equivalent of a middle-schooler publishing a breakthrough physics paper. It signals either monstrous innate talent, or the recovered memory of a reincarnated powerhouse. Either way, it marks Ji Ning as a guaranteed high-tier cultivator and a "must-invest" asset for any major faction.

**The Politics of Recruitment**: The scene where every noble scion rushes to claim Ji Ning is a pure distillation of Xianxia sociology. In a world where one Immortal can tip the balance of a commandery's power, early recruitment of a "sure-thing" prodigy is a multi-generational strategic investment. Ziyi’s shift from calling him "the cultivator sent by..." to "Young Master Ji Ning" is a subtle but critical power meter—she's basically updating his tag from "hireling" to "independent heavy-hitter."

**Beishan Hu's Arrested Redemption Arc?**: Beishan Hu’s instant concession is a masterclass in the Xianxia art of "changing face." In a setting where pride and reputation are everything, the ability to immediately and credibly retreat from a position of hostility when you’ve miscalculated is a survival skill. It doesn't make him a good guy—his private grumbling on the chariot makes that clear—but it shows he's not a foolish bully. He's a calculating predator who knows when to fold.

## Reading Guide
Pay close attention to the Fire Cauldron Youth's internal monologue at the end. He's a reincarnated Immortal himself, and his suspicion that Ji Ning might be another one is a huge piece of lore glue that anticipates the later revelation of Ji Ning’s past life memories. Also, soak in the sheer variety of the Anchan faction-state: pure cultivation sects, noble clans, cults, and the Marquisate itself all competing for talent—it sets the stage for the multi-polar political chaos Ji Ning will have to navigate. And finally, give a nod to Beishan Baiwei’s unexpected emotional intelligence—he doesn't try to recruit Ji Ning himself, he just offers him shelter and intelligence. That's the move of a man who understands that binding a monster to a contract is worse than earning his genuine friendship.

## Deeper Dive: The Wuyou Caverns Political Arena
The Wuyou Caverns (无忧洞) is not just a gambling den—it's the unofficial diplomatic salon of the Anchan noble youth. The "gambling battles" where cultivators fight on behalf of young masters are a way to measure rival factions' power without triggering open warfare. When Beishan Hu's move fails, it's not just a monetary loss; the fact that he conceded *after* the Domain was revealed, rather than allowing the White Witch to die fighting, shows he's playing the long game politically, not just the short game of pride.
