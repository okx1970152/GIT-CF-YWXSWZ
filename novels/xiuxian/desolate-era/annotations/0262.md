## Chapter Overview
Time to eat some humble pie, demon tribes! After a harrowing slog through the freezing nightmare of the Nine Palaces, Ice Door Formation, our squad finally catches a break—courtesy of Uncle White’s formation-cracking genius and the brute-force arrival of Xiangliu Fang. The chapter pivots from desperate survival to triumphant cleanup: first, a tactical shift opens space for the Whitewater Hound to do his thing; then Xiangliu Fang strolls in, one-punches a Primal-level puppet, and bag-n-bags the entire Qiongqi squad like he’s collecting stray groceries. The cherry on top? They discover they’ve been in the **Treasure Hall** all along. You can practically hear the loot-goblin screams from across the cosmos.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Formation Breaks**: Under ice-arrow pressure, the team expands their defensive formation to give Uncle White a 10-zhang buffer. Using the Fuxi Staff Formation, he harmonizes with–and then “jams”–the grand array, collapsing it completely.
- **Xiangliu Fang Arrives**: Shaoyan Nong’s Fiendgod bodyguard, who’s been playing ring-around-the-rosy with the estate’s demon armies, finally gets the summons. He catches up mid-Qiongqi swarm.
- **Puppet Purge**: Xiangliu Fang demonstrates raw dominance—one punch sends a Primal-level Qiongqi puppet flying; then he pulls out a massive bag and shoves all eighteen puppets inside in a matter of seconds.
- **Treasure Hall Revealed**: After disabling the formation’s residual nodes, Xiangliu Fang casually blasts the words “Treasure Hall” onto the wall, confirming this branch hall holds the plunder everybody’s here for.
- **Outside Threat Lingers**: The demon tribes have given up chasing Xiangliu Fang and are now lying in wait outside the hall, ready to ambush the humans for the estate keys.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Division of Loot in Immortal Estates**: A hallmark of Xianxia exploration arcs is the “treasure hunt contract.” Here, Shaoyan Nong’s promise—“any treasure you find is yours”—isn’t just generosity; it’s a standard pact in trials where a powerful heir needs specialists. This echoes real-world expedition protocols: the patron covers logistics, the experts keep what they can carry, and nobody gets stabbed in the back until after the final boss.

**Fuxi Staff Formation (伏羲棍阵)**: Named after Fuxi, the legendary first of the Three Sovereigns in Chinese mythology who taught humanity fishing, hunting, and the trigrams of the I Ching. Naming a formation after him implies deep, primordial knowledge of patterns and divination—fitting for Uncle White’s analytical, almost mathematical approach to breaking arrays.

**Qiongqi (穷奇)**: In the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*, Qiongqi is one of the Four Perils—a winged tiger that eats humans from the head down. In Xianxia, it’s recycled as a popular puppet template: dangerous, aggressive, and hard to kill. The fact that these are “Primal-level” means they’d be a nightmare for Wanxiang cultivators under normal circumstances.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch Uncle White’s Moment**: This chapter is a rare chance to see the Whitewater Hound step out of “loyal companion” mode and into “tactical genius” territory. His formation-breaking sequence is deliberately mechanical—staffs flying, light-lines connecting—emphasizing that he treats formations like puzzles, not battlegrounds. It’s a subtle but powerful character note.
- **Xiangliu Fang’s Efficiency**: Notice how he doesn’t brag or posture. He just… solves problems. The puppet bagging is almost comically efficient. This is classic Xianxia “power gap” demonstration—showing the audience exactly how much higher a true Fiendgod bodyguard ranks compared to the wandering cultivators.
- **Treasure Hall Hype**: The reveal is a classic “hooray” beat that sets up the next phase. Don’t get too comfortable—the demon tribes are still circling outside. The real test might not be the loot, but getting out alive with it.
