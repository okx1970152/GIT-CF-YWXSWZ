## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in the Xianxia genre’s favorite narrative trick: the hidden senior watching the whole time. As Ji Ning catches his breath after his grueling victory over Beizi Shan, a mysterious green-robed Wanxiang Adept descends and reveals he’s been observing the entire battle. And he’s not just any cultivator—he’s a member of the **Yinglong Guard**, the Grand Xia Dynasty’s elite Immortal corps. What follows is a recruitment pitch that opens a dazzling new horizon for our protagonist. The Yinglong Guard offers prestige, protection for his tribe, and a path to power beyond anything the Yan Mountains can provide. But Ji Ning, ever the pragmatist, weighs the offer against his own secrets, especially the aquatic palace he inherited from Celestial Immortal Juhua. The chapter ends with a tantalizing cliffhanger: Ji Ning has a favor to ask, and the Real Person is all ears.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Hidden Senior Revealed:** Real Person Mu Xiao, a Wanxiang Adept, was hiding nearby the entire time, watching Ji Ning dismantle the grand formation and kill Beizi Shan. He reveals he was the one hunting Beizi Shan for the Yinglong Guard.
- **The Talented Outsider:** Mu Xiao immediately identifies Ji Ning’s talent: not yet a Zifu Disciple, but already wielding a True Meaning of the Dao and possessing divine sense—an anomaly for a Xiantian lifeform. He confirms Ji Ning practices the Crimsonbright Diagram, the #1 Fiendgod Body Refining method in the Three Realms.
- **The Yinglong Guard Pitch:** Mu Xiao offers Ji Ning entry into the Yinglong Guard’s reserve corps. He outlines the benefits: unparalleled cultivation resources, divine abilities, and a guarantee that a 10,000-li territory of Ji Ning’s tribe will be protected for a thousand years, even if he dies.
- **The Price of Power:** The reserve corps is not a free ride. Members are confined to a mountain headquarters until they reach the Zifu Disciple level. Even after that, they can only leave when ordered. True freedom comes only with full membership as a Wanxiang-level Yinglong Guard.
- **Ji Ning’s Calculated Refusal:** Ji Ning politely declines, citing his mother’s critical illness. His real reason is his inheritance—the aquatic palace. He knows that once he becomes a Zifu Disciple, he can explore that legacy, a resource far more promising than a locked-down sect.
- **A Token and a Favor:** Mu Xiao gives Ji Ning an Yinglong Guard token for future protection and offers to fulfill a favor. Ji Ning, sensing an opportunity, immediately asks for one, leaving the chapter on a suspenseful note.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Power of an “Outsider’s Perspective” in Xianxia:** A classic trick in this genre is to let a more powerful character witness a protagonist’s feats from a hidden position. This serves a dual purpose. First, it validates the protagonist’s achievement before an objective expert (“The bystander sees clearly,” as Mu Xiao says). Second, it creates a powerful external link—the hidden senior becomes an ally, a mentor, or a recruiter. Here, Mu Xiao is the perfect gatekeeper to the wider world of the Grand Xia, making his presence a narrative device to introduce the full might of the Yinglong Guard without a jarring info-dump.

**The Yinglong Guard and Its Mythological Roots:** The name “Yinglong” (应龙) is deeply significant. In ancient Chinese mythology, the Yinglong (or “Responsive Dragon”) was a winged dragon and a powerful rain deity who served the Yellow Emperor, the legendary ancestor of the Chinese people. It was a creature of immense authority and martial prowess. By naming their elite corps after this being, the Grand Xia Dynasty is making a clear statement: this is not just a group of soldiers, but a force with divine backing and absolute imperial authority. Their status as the most feared organization in the land is a direct reflection of that mythological weight.

## Reading Guide

**For the Progression Fantasy Purist:** This chapter is pure gold. We get a clear power scale from an external source (Mu Xiao), an understanding of what’s coming next (the Zifu threshold), and a taste of the vast political structure beyond the Yan Mountains. Pay close attention to the terms of the reserve corps—it’s a classic “power for freedom” trade that every progression fantasy reader will recognize. Ji Ning’s refusal is the smart play, and it demonstrates his maturity as a protagonist.

**For the Worldbuilding Enthusiast:** The Yinglong Guard is a massive lore drop. This chapter tells you exactly how the Grand Xia Dynasty polices its Immortal cultivators, how it absorbs talent, and how it projects power through a monopoly on force—but also through benevolence and protection. The 10,000-li protected territory is a brilliant mechanic, showing that a cultivator’s strength directly benefits their family, creating a powerful incentive system. Note how “protection after death” is the real selling point.

**For the Clock-Watcher:** The chapter ends on a perfect cliffhanger. Ji Ning is about to ask for a favor… but what? Given his mother’s illness, and given that Mu Xiao is a powerful Wanxiang Adept bound by a debt of gratitude, the scope of possible favors is immense. Did he want Mu Xiao to heal his mother? To escort him somewhere? To send a message? The suspense makes the next chapter a must-read.
