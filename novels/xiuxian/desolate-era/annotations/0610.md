## Chapter Overview
This chapter throws us into a tense, high-stakes negotiation inside the World Prison. Ji Ning confronts an imprisoned Celestial Immortal from the fallen Ninefang Chaos Kingdom—a woman whose desperation is matched only by her cunning. Instead of brute force, Ji Ning deploys cold pragmatism: a soul-scouring technique is his price for her life. The payoff is immense. We get a sweeping lore-dump on the Ninefang Kingdom’s structure, its terrifying king, and the shocking origin of the legendary Five Treasures Sword Art. It’s a chapter that reframes everything we thought we knew about power ceilings in this universe.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning meets Qianrou, an emaciated female prisoner who rapidly restores her beauty and attempts to use a Charm Art on him. It fails completely against his Heartforce.
- Qianrou reveals the Ninefang Chaos Kingdom has fallen. She offers total submission to Ji Ning in exchange for her life, begging only to avoid a soul imprint.
- Ji Ning refuses to release her but agrees to a soul-scouring instead, which Qianrou has no choice but to accept to survive.
- During the half-day soul-scouring, Ji Ning discovers Qianrou’s extraordinary identity: she is the granddaughter of the Ninefang King himself.
- Her backstory is tragic and vengeful—her mother was a violated Sacred Maiden driven to suicide, and Qianrou later took revenge by driving her father, the Second Prince, mad in the Sea of Ten Thousand Torments.
- The King imprisoned her in the World Prison as punishment, valuing her royal bloodline too much to execute her.
- Ji Ning learns the Ninefang King has conquered twelve Chaosworlds and slain at least three World-level beings, establishing a kingdom far more powerful than the Three Realms.
- Crucially, Ji Ning discovers that techniques surpassing the Heavenly Dao’s limits—like the Five Treasures Sword Art—can only be created by World-level cultivators or above, and are strictly guarded with life oaths in the Ninefang Kingdom.
- This forces Ji Ning to reevaluate Daofather Fuju’s origin: the Five Treasures Sword Art was likely found, not created.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces one of the most critical metaphysical laws of the Desolate Era universe: the hard ceiling on technique creation. Only World-level beings (World Gods and Chaos Immortals) can even *attempt* to forge a method that transcends the Heavenly Dao. Mother Nuwa herself failed at this. This isn’t just a power-level gate; it’s a cosmic law of talent and insight.

The concept of a **Life Oath** is a recurring Xianxia trope. Unlike a simple promise, a Life Oath is a binding soul-contract enforced by the universe itself. Break it, and your soul is instantly obliterated. It’s the ultimate NDA for immortal techniques, and it explains why the Ninefang King’s supreme arts were never leaked, even when his kingdom fell.

Qianrou’s failed **Charm Art** (魅惑秘术) is a classic Xianxia seduction technique—a refined, cultivated spell rather than raw beauty. Ji Ning’s immunity is a direct testament to his fourth-level Heartforce mastery and his Dao-heart’s imperviousness, forged by grief over Yu Wei. In this genre, resisting seduction is a barometer of a cultivator’s spiritual purity.

Finally, the “prisoner vs. jailer” power dynamic here hinges on resource attrition: Qianrou is stronger in theory, but she cannot replenish her divine power inside the World Prison, while Ji Ning can draw endlessly from the outside. This reflects a brutal Xianxia truth—terrain and logistics often trump raw cultivation level.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in “talking scenes” that actually advance the plot. Pay close attention to how Ji Ning *listens*—he doesn’t just seek the cultivation manual; he’s mining for geopolitical intelligence. The backstory of Qianrou and her father is a tragic mini-novel in itself, and it sets up the Ninefang Chaos Kingdom as a terrifying, fully-realized world we only glimpse through fragments.

The lore-dump on the Five Treasures Sword Art is a jaw-dropping moment for long-time readers. It retroactively elevates Daofather Fuju from a powerful but local figure to someone who stumbled upon a cosmic secret. This is the kind of world-building that rewards patient readers.

Also, note the brief author’s note at the end. It’s a peek behind the curtain of Chinese web novel culture—a direct, casual conversation between I Eat Tomatoes and his fans. It reminds us that these stories are serialized, alive, and deeply connected to their community.

## The World Prison: A Xianxia Penal Colony
The World Prison is not just a jail—it’s a metaphysical desert. Prisoners cannot replenish their energy, making even a weaker jailer a mortal threat. The **Heavenly Lock** (天锁) binds directly to the truesoul, not the body, preventing escape through body-sacrifice techniques. It’s a perfect, cruel system designed to break even Elder Gods over eons of starvation. Ji Ning’s position as jailer is one of absolute logistical advantage, not raw power.
