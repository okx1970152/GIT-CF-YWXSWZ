## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a *masterpiece* of Xianxia catharsis. After the stunning debut against Baixue, Ji Ning faces his third Wanxiang Adept opponent: the malevolent ‘Du Xue,’ a master of the Domain of the Dao in *three* Daos. What follows isn’t just a battle; it’s a forge. Under the crushing pressure of a three-Dao expert, Ji Ning’s sword comprehension—sparked in the previous duel—ignites into a full-blown epiphany. His Three Talents Lotus Sword grows sharper, more alive, until Du Xue is defeated. But the real prize comes after: Ji Ning doesn’t stop. He enters a transcendent state, his entire being resonating with the Dao of the Sword, achieving **Sword Heart Illumination**—the first stance of the legendary *Three-Foot Sword* created by Immortal Beixing. The chapter ends with Ji Ning standing tall, challenging the hall again. The sword has found its master.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning vs. Du Xue: Du Xue controls eighteen ‘poisonous hooks’ with three-colored poisonous light, representing his mastery of the Domain of the Dao in three Daos. He immediately overwhelms Ji Ning’s eighteen lotus sword lights.
- Ji Ning enters the ‘state of sword comprehension’ again, his sword light continuously growing in power under pressure. Flamesaint, the slovenly fat youth, and even Jiujie take notice.
- Du Xue, frustrated, unleashes his ace move: all eighteen hooks merge into a single massive ten-zhang poisonous hook. Ji Ning counters by merging his eighteen sword lights into one giant Three Talents Lotus Sword, shattering Du Xue’s attack and piercing his puppet.
- Du Xue concedes; the hall erupts in discussion—even a three-Dao Wanxiang Adept has fallen to a Zifu Disciple.
- Ji Ning does not stand up. He remains seated as a powerful sword aura begins radiating from him. The Wufeng Immortal and the Diancai Immortal realize their junior is breaking through.
- Ji Ning experiences a profound internal revelation: **Sword Heart Illumination**. He understands the essence of the Sword Immortal path—the sword as self, as life, as reliance. He comprehends the first stance of Immortal Beixing’s Three-Foot Sword.
- He stands, challenges the hall once more: “Is there any other senior apprentice-brother or senior sister who wishes to offer guidance?”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Sword Immortal (剑仙) School**: In the vast landscape of Chinese Xianxia, the ‘Sword Immortal’ is one of the most iconic and romanticized archetypes. Unlike a generic cultivator who merely uses a sword as a weapon, a *true* Sword Immortal walks the **Grand Dao of the Sword**—a singular, monomaniacal path where every other Dao (Rainwater, Wind, Fire, etc.) is merely an ingredient to be absorbed into the sword. The result? A cultivator with terrifying offensive power, often capable of defeating opponents a full realm above them. As the slovenly fat youth notes, Sword Immortals are “the most battle-oriented” of all schools. Immortal Beixing and the Diancai Immortal are prime examples.

**Domain of the Dao (道之域境)**: Described in earlier chapters as a persistent field of influence projected by one’s comprehension of a Dao, normally inaccessible before the Primal Daoist stage. A Zifu Disciple wielding even one Domain is called a ‘monster genius.’ Du Xue’s three Daos represent a rare pinnacle—yet Ji Ning, with only one Dao in Domain and an iron will, overcomes him through sheer insight acceleration.

**Three-Foot Sword (三尺剑)**: This legendary nine-stance sword art was created by Immortal Beixing, the most famous Sword Immortal in the Black-White College’s history. Its first stance, **Sword Heart Illumination (剑心通明)**, is not a mere technique but a state of being. The cultivator’s heart becomes one with the sword, allowing intuitive execution of all sword arts. Ji Ning’s breakthrough is the formal initiation into this supreme inheritance. The name ‘Three-Foot Sword’ evokes the classic length of a Chinese *jian* (double-edged straight sword), symbolizing the sword that measures both the opponent and the self.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is pure, concentrated Xianxia fuel—the kind of scene that makes readers stay up until 3 a.m. There’s no grand loot, no world-shaking revelation; just a boy and his sword, facing overwhelming odds and *becoming* the sword. The duel with Du Xue is a textbook escalation: first a probing volley, then a crushing disadvantage, then a slow, inexorable power-up as the protagonist’s talent outpaces the system. The real treat is the aftermath—Ji Ning’s internal monologue as he achieves Sword Heart Illumination is poetic without being preachy. Notice the parallel structure: “The sword is my body / my life / my path.” This is the crystallization of Ji Ning’s Dao Heart—his desire for his parents’ immortality, his love for those close to him, his refusal to be a puppet of fate. He *chooses* the sword as his tool of defiance. If you’ve been binging for the action, pause and savor this moment. It’s the quiet before the storm—and the storm is Ji Ning, now armed with a legendary technique, standing on a stone pillar and asking for more.
