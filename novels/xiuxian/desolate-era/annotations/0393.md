## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, settle in for a masterclass—not in sword art, but in the bow! Last chapter left us with the cryptic riddle of “the arrow flies, the heart flies,” and Ji Ning has been chewing on it like a dog with a bone. This time, we watch our favorite sword prodigy pivot hard into archery, and the result is a beautiful, painstaking grind session. Forget flashy explosions; this is about the quiet, obsessive craft of becoming a true divine archer. Ji Ning finally cracks the code of heartforce and achieves a breakthrough that’s less about raw power and more about refined, soul-deep sincerity. And as a reward for his patience? He finally gets his hands on the legendary Houyi Archery Art and a peek at the Candle Dragon’s Gaze. Buckle up—the world of ranged combat just got terrifyingly real.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Rahu Bow’s “Aha” Moment**: The artifact spirit of the Rahu Bow, spurred by the Woodcutter’s cryptic parting words, suddenly connects the dots between the phrase “let the heart be on the arrow” and the true meaning of “the heart flies.” This simple insight is the key Ji Ning has been missing.
- **The Breakthrough**: Applying this new understanding, Ji Ning enters a state of unity where his mind fuses with the arrow. He experiences the sensation of *becoming* the arrow, gaining rudimentary control over its flight path, though initially only for a limited distance.
- **The Grind**: Ji Ning spends days practicing relentlessly, extending the range of his mind-intent attachment from a mere hundred kilometers to over ten thousand. His Dao-heart itself is tempered through this process, adding a deadly, spiritual pressure to his gaze.
- **Mastery and the Trial**: Ji Ning finally passes the Rahu Bow’s trial, firing one hundred consecutive arrows into a bullseye from ten thousand kilometers away. The Rahu Bow celebrates, and Ji Ning promises to find it a new, superior bowstring to match its innate spirit treasure status.
- **The Inheritance**: Receiving permission from the eccentric Ji Dian, Ji Ning enters the Three Realms Palace and claims the complete Houyi Archery Art. He is stunned by its terrifying power, noting that its peak potential surpasses even his mature Starseizing Hand, albeit with immense heartforce consumption and demanding special arrow requirements.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Power of the Heart (心力)**: This chapter gives us a deep dive into one of Xianxia’s most nebulous yet crucial concepts: heartforce. It’s not magical energy (Ki) or physical strength (Divine Power). It’s the raw power of one’s will, intent, and spirit—their very Dao-heart made manifest. As Ji Ning reflects, a dead Fiendgod’s lingering “Kill!” intent proves this force persists even after the soul has scattered. Mastery of heartforce is what separates a mere marksman from a legendary divine archer. It’s the difference between throwing a rock and bending causality.

**The Heart of Absolute Sincerity (至诚之心)**: Ji Ning realizes his heartforce is initially weak for archery because he lacks a “heart of absolute sincerity” toward the bow. He has this for his sword, but not for archery. This is a classic Daoist concept: power flows from total, unwavering commitment. You can’t fake it. You can’t half-ass it. True mastery in any domain demands you pour your entire being into it. This sincerity is, in many ways, more important than raw talent or comprehension speed. It’s the spiritual fuel for the most powerful techniques.

**The Ten Divine Abilities**: The Houyi Archery Art is ranked as one of the “Ten Divine Abilities since Pangu split Heaven and Earth.” This is the ultimate tier of supernatural techniques in the Three Realms, a collection of legendary arts created by ancient gods that transcend normal cultivation. Starseizing Hand is another one. Getting to learn one of these is a massive legacy event, confirming Ji Ning’s status as a major inheritor of primordial power.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a wonderful palate cleanser and a masterclass in non-combat progression. If you’ve been craving a break from non-stop battles, this is your moment to breathe and appreciate the art of cultivation. Pay close attention to Ji Ning’s internal thought process here. His analytical approach to mastering archery—from theoretical confusion to guided insight to relentless iterative practice—is the exact same methodology he uses for swordplay. It highlights his core strength: a disciplined, sincere, and brilliant mind. Also, mark the Rahu Bow’s character development. It’s not just a sentient weapon; it’s a slightly cocky, very helpful, and deeply invested mentor. The bond between a cultivator and their spirit treasure is being beautifully fleshed out here.
