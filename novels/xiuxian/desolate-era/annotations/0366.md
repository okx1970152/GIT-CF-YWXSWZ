## Chapter Overview
Thirty years of secluded cultivation on Mount Innerheart have transformed Ji Ning. In this chapter, we witness the payoff of that relentless training as Ji Ning finally conquers the eighth floor of the War God Hall, defeating a Void-level Fiendgod in a grueling display of pure combat prowess. More than a simple power-up sequence, this chapter showcases his strategic maturity in treasure selection. He opts for a complete set of nine formation-swords—the *Plowshares* sword array—over a more straightforward powerful single sword, revealing a tactical mind preparing for future mastery of a complex, high-level sword art. The methodical, almost ritualistic, way he processes his victory, chooses his rewards, and plans his next cultivation steps gives us a rare, quiet glimpse into the engine room of a cultivator’s ascent.

## Key Plot Points
- **War God Hall Victory:** Ji Ning defeats the Eighth Floor’s guardian, a raging Void Fiendgod, by unleashing the full spectrum of his divine abilities—Three Heads, Six Arms, and the Starseizing Hand—to force the ancient entity into submission.
- **The Rules of Inheritance:** The yellow-furred bear explains that Daoist Threelives left a strict system: three chances for Immortal treasures, each granting access to a larger pool, with the final selection offering everything.
- **Canny Treasure Selection:** Choosing wisely, Ji Ning passes over a gaudy single sword to select the *Plowshares Sword Formation*, a complete set of nine top-grade Immortal flying swords specifically designed to synergize with a powerful sword art he has already scouted in the palace’s library.
- **Uncle White’s Future Gift:** He also earmarks his previous treasure, the Triple Sealing Immortal Circlet, as a future gift for Uncle White, demonstrating his characteristic pragmatism and care for his spirit-beast allies.
- **The Art of *Plowshares*:** After a month in seclusion revising his signature *Three-Foot Sword*, Ji Ning awakens to summer in the cave world. He heads straight for the Immortals’ Palace, retrieves the *Plowshares* manual, and declares his intention to challenge the ninth-level golem.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The War God Hall as a Tester:** The concept of a dedicated trial hall that limits a cultivator to only a few attempts per lifetime is a classic Xianxia mechanic. It forces the protagonist to exercise genuine caution, not knowing exactly how strong their next realm’s version of themselves will be. It also imposes a hard resource cap—treating “attempts at a challenge” as a non-renewable treasure.
- **The *Plowshares* Sword Art and Daofather Hierarchy:** The manual’s name, *Plowshares*, draws on a sense of elemental control and heavy, inescapable power. The technique is credited to a “Daofather” (Daofather Plowshares), an Ancestral Immortal-level figure who has fully comprehended a Grand Dao of the Three Realms. In the cosmic hierarchy, a Daofather is a being who sits near the top of the local food chain, their creations becoming legendary inheritances sought after by lesser cultivators for millions of years.
- **Elemental Synergy in Sword Arts:** Ji Ning breaks down the *Plowshares* technique as a fusion of Water, Earth, Space, and Sword Daos. This highlights the philosophical depth of Xianxia combat: the most powerful techniques aren’t just about raw power but are conceptual frameworks that weave together multiple fundamental laws of the universe. A cultivator must understand these Daos independently before they can combine them seamlessly.
- **Sunstar and Taiyin Star Projections:** The text explains that every world in the Three Realms has its own sun and moon, which are merely projections of the primordial Sunstar and Taiyin Star. This cosmology reflects an ancient Chinese astronomical concept where the sun, moon, and stars are not independent celestial bodies but projections of a few supreme, formless, transcendent sources of light and energy that exist on a higher plane.

## Reading Guide
This is a “loot and level-up” chapter, the Xianxia equivalent of a character opening a chest in an RPG and meticulously equipping their new gear. But what makes it satisfying is the *thought* behind the action. Ji Ning doesn’t just grab the shiniest sword; he researches a technique, picks a matching set of swords, and even plans a future gift for a comrade. It’s a masterclass in the genre’s obsession with resource management and long-term strategy. Pay attention to the quiet frustration about the War God Hall’s artificial limitations—these moments of imposed scarcity make the eventual upgrades feel genuinely hard-won. This chapter is the calm before the storm: the protagonist has refitted his arsenal, and his final words indicate the next big confrontation is now his primary target.
