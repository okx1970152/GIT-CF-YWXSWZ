## Chapter Overview
After bamboozling a pair of chatty jailers into revealing the secrets of a chaotic new world, Ji Ning sits alone in a pocket of space with a prize he never expected: a bottle of Chaos Nectar. He realizes the precious, universe-born liquid is the key to unlocking the second level of the Taowu Eighteen Fiendgods—a technique that will allow him to maintain eighteen completely independent clones without soul damage. The quiet solitude, scientific analysis of the World Prison’s formation, and the ritual-like emergence of those golden runes all build toward a moment of profound personal power, finally giving Ji Ning the resources to stand against the Seamless Gate.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning commands the two jailers to patrol the World Prison, leaving him alone to process everything he has learned.
- He deduces that Chaos Nectar—distilled over an entire Chaos Cycle by a vast formation inside the World Prison—is a treasure beyond the reach of most cultivators, reserved for Elder Gods and Ancestral Immortals within the Nine Chaos Nations.
- Ji Ning ignores the rule that each jailer is only allowed one bottle, claiming all eighteen for himself and beginning the first level of the Taowu Eighteen Fiendgods.
- The first level splits Ji Ning’s truesoul and soul into eighteen parts, creating eighteen clones with identical memories and independent survival.
- Satisfied with the success of the first level, he immediately begins the second level by consuming Chaos Nectar, feeling its cold, cleansing energy flow through his soul, Pure Yang Golden Core, and divine power.
- After breaking through to the second level, Ji Ning feels he finally has a realistic chance to fight against the Seamless Gate and save his senior apprentice-sister.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Taowu Eighteen Fiendgods is a classic Xianxia body-splitting technique that allows a cultivator to create multiple autonomous clones. Unlike ordinary divine clones—which fragment the soul and risk terminal damage if too many die—this technique grants each clone a full, independent truesoul and soul. The cost is a steep loss of power during the split, offset by rare resources like Chaos Nectar. The concept draws from Daoist alchemical ideals of “one qi transforming into three pure ones,” but the novel pushes the logic further: maintain a swarm of independent bodies so that death of one clone does not ripple back to the original. Chaos Nectar itself is described as an essence extracted from raw primordial chaos, containing the potential to give birth to worlds. In the Xianxia metaphysical framework, chaos is the undifferentiated state before differentiation; its essence is the ultimate all-in-one energy source, capable of boosting truesoul, soul, heartforce, ki, and divine power simultaneously.

## Reading Guide
This is a pure cultivation interlude—no battles, no rescues, no politics. The tension comes from the weight of a single decision: should Ji Ning hoard a once-in-a-cycle treasure or burn it now for massive combat dividends? His choice to upgrade reflects a core Xianxia theme: power delayed is a risk you can’t take in a starving world. Watch how the technique evolves an odd but logical rule: because each clone has a complete truesoul, taking damage is a tactical write-off, not a permanent wound. This is the foundation for Ji Ning’s eventual war footing against the Seamless Gate. If you love slow-but-rewarding power progression, this chapter is satisfyingly focused on that single, transformative process.

## About the Stele’s Hidden Nature
Ji Ning also observes that the stele inside his aquatic estate exists in a space that seems to be outside the jurisdiction of the Three Realms’ Heavenly Daos. This is a quiet, powerful clue: the Stele of the World Prison was forged by a being who “stands above the Heavenly Daos.” In the Xianxia hierarchy, Heavenly Daos are the ultimate, inflexible laws of a given universe. An artifact that evades those laws is either the work of a Chaos Immortal or of an existence near Autarch-level. This hints that the Nine Chaos Nations were not just a minor civilization they were a true cosmic power, possibly rivaling or exceeding the Three Realms in sophistication.
