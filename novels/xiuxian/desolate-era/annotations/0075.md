## Chapter Overview
The prisoner has broken the cage. Chapter 75 shows Ji Ning in full-on savage mode after cracking the mountain-belly formation. There’s no negotiation, no monologuing—just raw Xiantian-on-Xiantian carnage as he tears through Bei Zishan’s underlings. The moment the Six Apprentice Brotherhood try to regroup with **Dao-Soldier Armor**, the battle shifts from a bloody alley-fight to a high-octane formation war. And just when you think Ning has the upper hand, the true horror lurking in the basement decides to take the stage. Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this chapter is pure, unfiltered xianxia combat at its finest, with stakes that go from “survive” to “run” in a single page-turn.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning immediately recognizes that the formation banner is his ticket out: he stores it and goes on the offensive. His first move? Unleash blazing fire on the ordinary servants and personally hunt down the Xiantian lifeforms with his sword. He flash-kills two Xiantian in one breath and scares off a third.
- The six senior apprentice-brothers and sisters realize they can’t beat him individually, so they activate their **Dao-Soldier Armor**—a full ten thousand green-armored soldiers pour out of the mountain caves. Ji Ning identifies them as a stronger grade than the Ji Clan’s Crimson Armored Guards.
- A massive formation battle erupts. Ji Ning counters with Fire and Frost techniques, which decimate unformed squads but are mostly tanked by those who have already formed the Flood Dragon-style units. He then unleashes his trump card: **Water-Fire Lotus** (True Meaning of the Dao), which kills six Xiantian lifeforms instantly from long range, shocking the survivors.
- The Xiantian leaders scramble to reform the formation, linking with the remaining Dao-Soldiers. Because Ji Ning killed so many troops, they cannot merge into a single Flood Dragon. Instead, eight Flood Dragon phantoms emerge from the remaining eight Xiantian leaders.
- Below, a transformed spirit beast alerts Bei Zishan that the Dao-Soldiers are below nine thousand. The old demon is stuck in a dangerous refinement ritual. To buy time, he unleashes his ultimate backup: a **black-furred zombie** refined from a Zifu Disciple’s corpse—a monster that can rival a Zifu-level cultivator. The zombie rises from the coffin and leaps out to hunt Ji Ning.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Dao-Soldier Armor** (道兵甲铠) — This is one of xianxia’s most elegant solutions to the “platoon vs. powerhouse” problem. Instead of a random army, the armor’s runes link a commander with hundreds or thousands of soldiers, amplifying their collective power into a unified dragon phantom. The higher the number of soldiers, the deadlier the formation. At 9,000 soldiers, they can merge into a single **Flood Dragon**. Below that threshold? The beast is crippled, but still lethal. This chapter uses the mechanic perfectly: Ji Ning’s Water-Fire Lotus eliminates just enough soldiers to deny the enemy their ultimate form, a classic xianxia “number-crunch to survive” move.

**Water-Fire Lotus** (水火莲花) — A beautiful technique born from the interplay of Sunstar True Fire and Taiyin True Water. It manifests as interlocking petals of water and fire that spin in opposite directions, crushing anything caught inside. The fact that it contains a **True Meaning of the Dao** makes it far more dangerous than an ordinary spell; it’s not just elemental power but a fundamental principle of the universe being weaponized. This is why even high-level Xiantian lifeforms can be killed from long range by it—a True Meaning elevates a technique from “strong” to “nearly unavoidable.”

**Black-furred Zombie** (黑毛僵尸) — In Chinese corpse-refining lore (Jiangshi tradition), a zombie’s rank is marked by its fur color. Black fur is a high-tier zombie, one step below the legendary silver or golden-furred flying zombies. The fact that this one was made from a Zifu Disciple’s corpse means it possesses both the raw strength of a Zifu cultivator and the undead’s terrifying resilience. Corpse miasma (尸煞) is its signature weapon—invisible to the naked eye but instantly lethal to any living being that breathes it in. For a Xiantian-level warrior like Ji Ning, this is essentially a boss fight he wasn’t prepared for.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in **escalating dread**. It starts with Ji Ning dominating, shifts to him struggling against the formation, and ends with the zombie reveal—a classic “oh, it gets worse” moment. Pay close attention to the balance of power: the Dao-Soldier formation mechanics are a perfect example of how xianxia battles rely on both raw stats and tactical depth. Also, note the Water-Fire Lotus’s range—it’s a hint at Ji Ning’s soul-perception advantage, which will become crucial in the next fight. The zombie’s arrival sets up a confrontation where Ning’s usual speed and precision may not be enough. This is the moment the story reminds us that Bei Zishan isn’t just a fraud—he’s a desperate survivor who’s sunk every last evil resource into staying alive. The tension is sky-high. Don’t blink.
