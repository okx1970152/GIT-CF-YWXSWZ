## Chapter Overview

In one of the most devastating twists yet, Ji Ning’s rampage through the Shaoyin clan takes a catastrophic turn. After tearing apart two Celestial Immortals and dominating the Shaoyin Ancestor’s pet beasts, Ji Ning is outwitted not by raw power, but by a madman’s gambit. Patriarch Fiendbreeze, driven insane by the loss of his disciple and true body, pulls a horrifying final play: he lures ten billion relocated mortals and cultivators into Ji Ning’s attack path, causing a space collapse that slaughters them all. The result? Karmic Hellfire—the universe’s ultimate punishment—descends upon *both* Ji Ning and Fiendbreeze, marking a point of no return in the Shaoyin war.

## Key Plot Points

- The eight Celestial Immortals’ “invincible” formation is shattered, and two of them die under Ji Ning’s hand. The remaining six retreat into a sealed shuttle, their confidence in tatters.
- Chixin and the others are left baffled: Ji Ning’s power keeps skyrocketing in minutes, defying normal cultivation logic. Their earlier ambush data is rendered useless.
- The Shaoyin Ancestor (Shaoyin Chou) unleashes three building-sized venomous beasts—a black serpent, a shelled insect, and a one-legged flying rhinoceros—each with monstrous durability and regeneration.
- Ji Ning, using Three Heads, Six Arms and the fourth-cycle Starseizing Hand, tears through them one by one in a pure, brutal close-quarters brawl.
- Just as Ji Ning gains the upper hand, the supposedly dead Fiendbreeze Patriarch appears via Greater Teleportation and throws hundreds of Star Sand pellets at him.
- Ji Ning’s counter-strike shatters the Star Sand, but it also destroys the hidden space containing the relocated civilians. Ten billion souls are crushed in an instant.
- The universe responds: Karmic Hellfire ignites on both Fiendbreeze and Ji Ning, signifying a sin of cataclysmic proportions. Ji Ning is now marked—whether he survives will depend on his Dao-heart and a miracle.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Karmic Hellfire (Karmic Hellfire) & the Cosmic Courtroom.** This is one of the most feared phenomena in Xianxia. In Daoist-inspired cultivation cosmology, the universe is not a dead void—it is a living, *judging* entity. When a cultivator commits mass slaughter of mortals (especially billions), the accumulated “karmic sin” triggers an automatic response: the descent of Karmic Hellfire. This is no ordinary elemental flame. It burns directly from *within* the soul and Dao-heart, bypassing all physical defenses. Even an indestructible Fiendgod Body Refiner cannot block it with divine power or magic treasure. The only counter is a profound Dao-heart, sincere repentance, or an astronomical karmic merit score to cancel the debt. In the novel’s history, many supreme beings have been reduced to ashes by this very fire.

**The Universe Pearl & Spatial Pocket Treasures.** These are common Xianxia tools for mass relocation. A Celestial Immortal with mastery over the Grand Dao of Space can carve a stable subspace into a pearl or a bag. These spaces can hold vast numbers of people, but they are *brittle*—if the external pressure exceeds their tolerance, the space collapses, grinding everything inside to dust.

**The “Ten Billion Mortal Slaughter” Trope.** In Eastern cultivation epics, the killing of mortals is a different moral calculation than slaying cultivators. Mortals are seen as “innocent” in the karmic ledger. Even a ruthless demon cultivator will avoid such slaughter when possible, because the sin attached to it is mind-bogglingly heavy. Fiendbreeze’s act is the ultimate “mutually assured destruction” move—he drags Ji Ning into karmic hell with him, knowing that even if Ji Ning lives through the fight, the Hellfire may be his true death sentence.

## Reading Guide

Buckle up, folks—because this chapter is a masterclass in **trope inversion**. We’ve been watching Ji Ning power through everything the Shaoyin clan throws at him: formations, ambushes, Celestial Immortal tactics, even divine beasts. But IET (I Eat Tomatoes) reminds us that in Xianxia, *power is not everything*. The true threat isn’t a bigger sword or a stronger monster—it’s a cornered lunatic who has nothing left to lose.

Fiendbreeze is a fantastic villain in this moment. He doesn’t try to outfight Ji Ning; he *out-gambles* him. He uses the one thing Ji Ning never expected: the safety of the relocated civilians as a weapon. It’s cruel, it’s insane, and it’s brilliant. And now Ji Ning is stuck with a literal universe-sized problem. Karmic Hellfire isn’t something you can punch your way out of.

This is a turning point. Will Ji Ning’s remarkable Dao-heart and accumulated karmic merit be enough to pull him through? Or will this “accidental” mass slaughter become the defining weight of his cultivation journey? The answer will test everything we know about his character—and about the rules of this universe.
