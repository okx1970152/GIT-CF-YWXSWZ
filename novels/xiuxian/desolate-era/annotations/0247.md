## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this is the chapter where Ji Ning stops playing nice and officially throws down the gauntlet. When a Primal perfection expert from Snowdragon Mountain arrives with all the arrogance of a living legend, he expects the young Wanxiang Adept to kneel. Instead, he gets a serene lotus, a pair of black wings, and a ten-zhang-tall Fiendgod who laughs in the face of fate. The raw, pulse-pounding clash between Plumed Immortal Xueyu and Ji Ning is not just a fight—it's a statement that the rules of engagement have been rewritten. This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia power scaling, showcasing how a genius with the right foundation, comprehension, and sheer audacity can force even a centuries-old expert to play catch-up. Get ready for a showdown that proves cultivation isn't just about age—it's about the Dao.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning directly accepts the challenge from Plumed Immortal Xueyu, a Primal perfection Primal Daoist, shocking both the Ji clan and Snowdragon Mountain’s forces. His action defies all conventional logic, as a Wanxiang Adept is not supposed to be a match for a peak Primal expert.
- Xueyu opens the battle with a massive sea of Primordial Fire, a standard but powerful Pr Daoist technique. Ji Ning calmly counters it with his Water-Fire Lotus, a defensive technique he has perfected over nine years by merging his insights into Rainwater, Blazing Fire, and the Lotus mysteries discussed with Nine Lotos.
- Unable to burn Ji Ning to death, Xueyu escalates to Heavenly Water—a condensed, superior form of elemental cold. Each droplet is potent enough to kill a Wanxiang Adept at perfection. Ji Ning first uses his sword arts to guide and redirect the water, but Xueyu detonates them, creating a freezing mist.
- From the mist, Xueyu finally unleashes his famous attack treasure, the Mandarin Duck Split-Light Swords. This pair of Heavenly-ranked flying swords is his ultimate killer move, feared across the entire region.
- Ji Ning responds by activating a triple-layer trump card: he unleashes the Heavenly Transformation divine ability, growing to an astonishing ten zhang in height, alongside his Windwing Evasion wings and his Water-Fire Lotus. With a single, explosive strike, he sends the legendary twin swords flying backward, shocking everyone.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Let’s break down the power hierarchy on display here. In Xianxia, a "realm" isn't just a title—it's a fundamental difference in the quality of energy and control. A Wanxiang Adept controls Yuan power, while a Primal Daoist commands a crystallized, far denser Primal energy. Xueyu's "Primordial Fire" (元火) is a perfect example; it's a core technique that every Pr Daoist cultivates, a burning manifestation of their soul and power. The fact that Ji Ning's Water-Fire Lotus can block it without using a sword attack is a massive red flag for Xueyu.

Notice how Ji Ning's technique is built on four pillars: Cold-Sha & Earthfire (from his body), Taiyin & Sunstar divine patterns (from his Crimsonbright Diagram), his comprehension of Rainwater & Blazing Fire Daos, and the Lotus structural secret art from discussing Dao with Nine Lotos. This is classic Xianxia "foundation building"—a pinnacle technique is rarely just one thing; it's a fusion of resources, comprehension, and a bit of luck.

Finally, the "Heavenly Transformation" (法天象地) being ten zhang tall is a critical detail. This divine ability is about *proportionally* magnifying mass, strength, and divine power. A two-zhang transformation is common; a ten-zhang one is a sign of a terrifyingly robust Fiendgod body refining foundation and immense understanding of the art itself. This is Ji Ning’s "Jedi mode" turning on.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch Ji Ning's calm.** When Xueyu rattles off his power, Ji Ning doesn't flinch or boast. He just casts his spells and sends his swords. The contrast between Xueyu's escalating aggression and Ji Ning's serene, analytical response is the core of this chapter's tension.
- **The fight is a test.** Xueyu isn't using his full power from the start—he’s probing. And Ji Ning knows it. Each phase (Fire, Water, Swords) is Xueyu forcing the tempo higher, trying to find a breaking point. The fact that Ji Ning has a flawless counter for each phase reveals the depth of his preparation.
- **The final moment is the payoff.** The chapter ends with the epic image of a ten-zhang giant, wings spread, lotus blooming, batting away a legendary attack treasure. This isn't just a victory in a single exchange—it's Ji Ning proving he can play at the highest level of Primal-tier combat. How Xueyu reacts to having his sword deflected will define the next phase of this conflict.

## Why This Battle Matters: A Deep Dive into Xianxia Power Scaling
This chapter is a textbook illustration of a beloved Xianxia subversion: the "genius defying heaven." The conventional wisdom is that a Primal Daoist at perfection, with centuries of power accumulation, should be an insurmountable wall for a young Wanxiang Adept. Xueyu truly believes his raw power and superior realm will overwhelm Ji Ning immediately.

But Ji Ning isn't playing by those rules. His comprehension of the Dao of Rainwater and Blazing Fire is so deep that he's arguably on the verge of a Grand Dao breakthrough—something more about *quality* than *quantity* of energy. His Fiendgod body refining is so monstrous that his Heavenly Transformation can reach ten zhang, a feat that implies a foundation approaching that of a true primordial Fiendgod. He’s not just accumulating power; he's stacking multipliers (Divine Abilities, high-level Daos, top-tier treasures) that bridge the gap between realms.

This is the classic "one sword can break ten thousand techniques" philosophy: when your Dao is sharp enough and your foundation is broad enough, the numerical advantage in brute force becomes meaningless. Ji Ning is proving he belongs on a battlefield where realm is just a number. The terror in Xueyu’s eyes at the end is the terror of a calculator realizing the equation has changed.
