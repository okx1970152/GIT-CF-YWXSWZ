## Chapter Overview

Tomato breaks the fourth wall in this brief but heartfelt interlude, stepping directly from the role of author into the personal life of the man behind the legend. This is a calm, candid letter to the *Desolate Era* community, explaining a sudden absence. The cause isn't writer’s block or illness, but a universal trial of fatherhood: his infant son, "Little Tomato," fell ill, triggering a harrowing multi-hospital journey from a county clinic all the way to the Nanjing Children’s Hospital. The tone is weary but grateful, contrasting the chaos of emergency-room logistics with the quiet relief of recovery. Crucially, Tomato outlines a new release schedule inspired by his earlier hit, *Swallowing the Starry Sky* — a promise of twelve chapters per week, with one rest day. He also reassures readers about his own health, addressing concerns about kidney stones with a parent’s pragmatic calm. 

## Key Plot Points

- **The Cause of the Hiatus:** Tomato’s three-month-old son, "Little Tomato," fell ill, requiring multiple hospital transfers from a county clinic to Nanjing Children’s Hospital.
- **The Emotional State:** The author is transparent about mental and physical exhaustion, describing around-the-clock childcare and logistical stress but expressing gratitude for his family’s collective effort.
- **Recovery and Return:** The baby has improved and the family has returned home. Tomato announces a gradual return to writing, starting with single chapters before ramping up.
- **The New Schedule:** A formal announcement of a weekly rhythm: five days of double-updates (noon and evening), two days of single updates (Friday and Saturday), totaling twelve chapters per week (matching the *Swallowing the Starry Sky* era).
- **Personal Health Update:** Tomato addresses fan concern about his kidney stones, downplaying them as a manageable condition that requires hydration and exercise.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The "Author’s Note" (作者的话) Tradition:** In the Chinese web novel ecosystem, it is standard practice for authors to post candid, non-fiction updates about their personal lives during hiatuses. This transparency builds a deep parasocial bond with the readership, who are accustomed to treating the author as a real person with real struggles, not a faceless content engine. Tomato’s note is a textbook example: it explains, apologizes, consoles, and even pre-empts speculation about his health.
- **The 12-Chapter Week as a Gold Standard:** For veteran readers of Chinese web novels, Tomato's announced rhythm of "five double-update days plus two single-update days" (totaling 12 chapters weekly) is a beloved benchmark of reliability. His reference to *Swallowing the Starry Sky* (吞噬星空) signals that he is returning to a proven, disciplined formula that his audience already trusts.
- **Tomato’s Authorial Persona:** I Eat Tomatoes (我吃西红柿) is one of the most successful and respected authors in the Chinese web novel sphere. His open, down-to-earth communication style — referring to his child as "Little Tomato" and joking about kidney stones — is a key part of his brand. This note reinforces his image as a hardworking family man, which in turn deepens reader loyalty.

## Reading Guide

This is not a chapter of xianxia action. It’s a meta-textual breather arc — a real-world bridge connecting the mythic realms of the Three Realms to the very human reality of a writer’s life. Approach this as a moment of appreciation for the person behind the epic. The exhaustion and relief conveyed here make the promised 12-chapter schedule feel like a genuine gift rather than an obligation. If you’ve ever wondered what happens when a cultivator of grand stories must put down the brush to tend to a crying baby, this is your answer. Take a moment to appreciate the author’s transparency and know that when Ji Ning’s sword next flashes, it will be fueled by a few nights of hard-won sleep.
