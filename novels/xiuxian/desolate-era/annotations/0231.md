## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a masterclass in tactical evolution. Ji Ning steps into the War God Hall’s third level and faces a golden-armored Fiendgod clone—a being whose true form could blow him to dust with a single breath. But instead of charging in with raw power, Ning pulls back, draws his bow, and grinds the clone down from a distance using Starseizing Hand archery, Water-Fire Lotus, and the upgraded Lesser Thousand Sword Formation. The result? A clean, attrition-based victory that leaves even the ancient estate spirit stunned. This isn’t just a fight; it’s a quiet declaration that Ning’s combat IQ has officially caught up with his raw talent.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning reflects on his incomplete acceptance by the estate spirit: he hasn’t been acknowledged as a true disciple, and the trials are still life-or-death filters.
- The golden-armored general reveals himself as a divine-power clone of a far greater being. He boasts that his true self could annihilate Ning with a breath, but his clone is critically weak in raw divine energy.
- Ning probes the clone’s weakness: cut off from its true body, its divine power is non-renewable. He immediately commits to a strategy of attrition and distance.
- He unleashes a full multi-pronged assault: the ninth-level Lesser Thousand Sword Formation (enhanced by his late-Wanxiang breakthrough), the binding Water-Fire Lotus, and finally the Starseizing Hand with a long-range bow.
- The clone struggles under the relentless barrage. Despite his superior Dao comprehension and defensive hammers, he cannot close the gap or regenerate his power.
- In a final desperate rush, the clone charges Ning—only to be met with four simultaneous arrows. He is shattered into nothing, roaring in unwilling defeat.
- The yellow-furred bear reacts with genuine shock: he had expected a grueling close-quarters brawl, not a ranged demolition. He awards Ning access to the Treasure Hall for an Earth-ranked reward.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Fiendgod Clones (神魔分身):** A staple of high-level Xianxia. A truly powerful Fiendgod can split off a sliver of divine power—or even a single hair—and animate it as an independent combat clone. The catch? The clone is cut off from the main body’s energy source. Once its divine power is spent, it’s gone for good. Ning exploits this ruthlessly: he doesn’t need to overpower the clone; he only needs to outlast it.

**Starseizing Hand Archery (摘星手弓箭):** Ning’s signature divine ability is most famous for close-quarters destruction, but this chapter reveals its second life: long-range archery. In Xianxia, archery is often treated as the king of burst damage—every arrow concentrates all of the user’s strength into one point. The downside is inflexibility, but when combined with area-denial techniques like the Water-Fire Lotus and formation sword-light, Ning turns that weakness into a trap.

**Tactical Maturity:** This chapter is a quiet milestone for Ji Ning’s character. Early Ning would have charged in, swords blazing, to prove his dominance. This Ning analyses, strategizes, and executes a methodical kill from a distance. It’s a sign that his Dao-heart is cooling from reactive fury into patient, predator-like calculation.

## Reading Guide
Pay close attention to the parallel between the golden-armored general’s arrogance and his creeping unease. He starts the fight mocking Ning’s divine sense, but by the time the fourth arrow is in flight, he’s screaming for a close-quarters duel. That’s the sound of a veteran realizing he’s been outplayed by a kid who fights smarter, not harder. Also, note the estate spirit’s reaction at the end: the bear is genuinely surprised. It’s a rare moment where an ancient mentor has to recalibrate his expectations of a mortal disciple. For long-time readers, that silent acknowledgment is more satisfying than any victory roar.
