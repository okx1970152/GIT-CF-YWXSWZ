## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the War God Hall, fellow Daoists! Ji Ning finally steps into the legendary trial that even Celestial Immortal Juhua struggled with—and his first challenge is… a wooden stake. Don’t let the simplicity fool you. This is the Desolate Era equivalent of a “tutorial boss” that makes you sweat blood. We also check in on the Snowdragon Mountain side, where the ruthless Zifu veteran Tong Yu plots his assault on the Ji Clan. The pressure is mounting on both fronts: Ji Ning must prove his Dao in a seemingly absurd test, while the enemies sharpen their fangs. Get ready for a chapter that builds tension through quiet preparation and a single, intense training exercise.

## Key Plot Points

- Mu Si reports the discovery of the massive prime ore vein to Tong Yu, explaining the Ji Clan’s formation defense.
- Tong Yu, a seasoned Zifu Disciple and former rogue cultivator, decides to invite formation expert Nong Zidao to counter the Ji Clan’s arrays.
- Tong Yu reveals his personal background: a Ki Refining dead-end and a slow climb through Fiendgod Body Refining, which earned him the ‘Heavenly Transformation’ divine ability.
- He rides his Earth Dragon toward Yan Mountain, determined to gain merit and hope for sect resources to break through to Wanxiang.
- Inside the aquatic palace, Ji Ning and the black ox discuss the nature of the Star Hall’s acceleration effect—emphasizing daily accumulation over isolated epiphanies.
- The yellow great bear (War God Hall artifact spirit) gives Ji Ning his first trial: cut through a black wooden stake before a stick of incense burns out.
- Ji Ning launches his strongest Rainwater Sword Domain attack, only to leave a tiny wound. The stake’s resilience shocks him, highlighting the gulf between his current power and the level needed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Wooden Stake Trial: A Xianxia Trope Deconstructed**  
At first glance, “chop a log within a time limit” sounds like a mundane chore. But in Xianxia culture, such seemingly simple tests are often profound metaphors for *cutting through the illusions of the world* to reach the truth. The stake here is not just wood—it’s a condensed manifestation of the Dao’s resistance. Ji Ning’s Rainwater Sword Domain, which allowed him to slaughter Wanxiang-level demons, barely scratches it. This is the “hidden gate” concept: the first trial is deliberately easy relative to the palace’s power, yet it still humbles a prodigy.

**The Contrast of Refining Paths**  
Tong Yu’s backstory illustrates a common Xianxia tragedy: a cultivator who invested too heavily in one path (Ki Refining) and hit an insurmountable wall, then pivoted to Fiendgod Body Refining. His ‘Heavenly Transformation’—a signature divine ability—was earned through merit, not inheritance. This echoes the novel’s theme that *resourcefulness and ruthlessness* can sometimes compensate for talent.

**Incense Stick as Timer**  
Using a burning incense stick to mark time is a classic Chinese literary device, symbolizing both the brevity of life and the pressure of cultivation. It’s not arbitrary—an incense stick burns steadily, giving a visceral sense of urgency that a digital clock cannot.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *setup*. On the villain side, we get a deep look at Snowdragon Mountain’s internal politics—how a Zifu elder like Tong Yu maneuvers to maximize his chance at advancement. On the hero side, Ji Ning’s trial seems trivial but holds the key to his next breakthrough. Pay close attention to the yellow great bear’s words: “this will be the easiest trial.” That implies the War God Hall has multiple layers of difficulty, and that Ji Ning’s real test is yet to come. Also note the black ox’s lesson about “daily accumulation”—a nod to the slow-burn progression style of Desolate Era. Enjoy the calm before the storm.
