## Chapter Overview
After the explosive chaos of Shaoyin Nong’s death, our young sword-immortal Ji Ning finally gets a moment to breathe—but danger is still hard on his heels! This chapter is all about the high-stakes “big brother protection program” orchestrated by his newly discovered cousin-sister, Yuchi Xiyue. We get an audience with the legendary and terrifying King Yan (nicknamed “Yan Wang”), who’s equal parts blood-soaked Celestial Immortal and doting grandfather. The chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia political survival: securing an elite Golden Armored Imperial Guard squad as personal bodyguards, snagging a free plot of land in the royal capital, and flexing a million-catty priceless immortal mansion. Plus, Ji Ning’s Dao-heart gets a serious test against Yan Wang’s overwhelming killing intent—and passes with flying colors.

## Key Plot Points
- The Floating Cloud Immortal of the Shaoyin clan tries to tail Ji Ning for an assassination, but is stonewalled when Ji Ning enters the Yan Estate with Yuchi Xiyue—and never comes out.
- Yuchi Xiyue and Ji Ning bond further as “cousin-brother and cousin-sister” through the maternal family line; she personally takes him on a tour of the Yan Estate.
- Yan King (King Yan) summons Ji Ning to Guan Tian Mountain, subtly testing his Dao-heart by releasing his terrifying killing intent without a direct attack.
- Ji Ning’s sword-soul and willpower instantly shake off the effect, earning rare double praise from Yan Wang—a man infamous for calling no one impressive.
- Yuchi Xiyue, acting as Ji Ning’s fierce advocate, strong-arms her grandfather into assigning one squad of Golden Armored Imperial Guards to protect Ji Ning at all times, citing loopholes in the Grand Xia royal regulations.
- Ji Ning chooses a lakeside wasteland as his residence inside the Yan Estate, pulls out his precious portable immortal mansion (worth a million catties of Primordial Liquid), and settles in.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**King Yan’s “Yan Wang” Nickname & Celestial Immortal Madness**  
Yan Wang/阎王 is the Chinese name for Yama, the fearsome King of Hell in Chinese Buddhist-Daoist underworld mythology. King Yan proudly claims this title because his path to Celestial Immortality was drenched in slaughter—his killing intent alone could break a weaker cultivator’s will. This reinforces a key Xianxia trope: **ascension through blood and madness**. The idea that a mortal with only “average talent” can brute-force their way to immortality by courting heart-demons and killing intent is a powerful counter-narrative to the usual “genius with perfect talent” story.

**Golden Armored Imperial Guards & Royal Privilege**  
The Golden Armored Imperial Guard/Golden Armored Imperial Guards (金甲禁卫) are the elite soldiers of the Grand Xia Emperor, analogous to the Qing dynasty’s Imperial Guard system but scaled up to immortal power levels. In Xianxia sociology, forbidding their assignment to non-royalty is a *custom*, not a *law*—so Yuchi Xiyue cleverly exploits this loophole. This dynamic shows how political power in Xianxia works through unwritten rules and individual favor, not rigid legal systems.

**“Cousin-brother” & Chinese Family Hierarchy**  
In Chinese kinship terminology, *biao* (表) denotes relatives through the maternal line, while *tang* (堂) denotes paternal cousins. Yuchi Xiyue calls Ji Ning her *biao-di* (表弟/maternal cousin-brother), which is a slightly more distant relationship than a paternal cousin but still carries strong family obligations. This chapter solidifies this bond as a genuine power connection, complete with familial protection and estate privileges.

## Reading Guide
*Fire up your best popcorn, fellow Daoists—this is the chapter where Ji Ning officially becomes a VIP in the Grand Xia capital’s social ladder!* After the breathless kill-and-run pace of the previous arcs, we get a beautifully paced political interlude with just enough edge to keep you hooked. Watch how Yuchi Xiyue transforms from the grieving, vengeful cousin we met earlier into a sharp, strategic operator who knows exactly how to leverage her grandfather’s authority. And speaking of Grandpa—King Yan is an absolute scene-stealer. His “one look to test your Dao-heart” moment is both terrifying and hilarious in its casual brutality. The chapter’s real joy is seeing the “mad king” melt into a doting, noodle-armed grandfather the second his granddaughter plays the affection card. If you love high-level Xianxia politics, family maneuvering, and the satisfying click of power chess pieces moving into place—grab your wine cup and settle in. Also, *yes*, that mansion is a flex and you know it.
## Guide to Yuchi Xiyue’s Voice Profile
This chapter offers a masterclass in Yuchi Xiyue’s character writing. Her voice shifts from emotionally intense, trembling vulnerability during the private confession of her clan’s destruction to a cold, deep-seated hatred when speaking of the Shaoyin clan. The most telling moment comes when she shifts to iron resolve to announce her vengeance—and later, when talking to her grandfather, a playful, coaxing granddaughter energy surfaces. The translation should reflect these shifts: soft and desperate one moment, fierce and demanding the next, then warm and affectionate. If you only remember one thing: Yuchi Xiyue is never one-note.
