## Chapter Overview
We open with Ji Ning and his crew combing through the spoils of Crippled Moon Realperson, and the mood shifts from victory to cold dread. They uncover standard-issue blood robes, a beast-hide letter referencing “Tianfang World” and “retirement”—clear signs that their victim wasn’t just a petty old villain but a retired soldier of the Anchan Northmont clan’s secret army, the Xuan Army. The team scrambles to erase all evidence, tossing the storage treasure into the depths of the earth’s magnetic field, and then pivots to discuss their next target: Shui Yi, holed up in the heavily guarded Dong clan territory. The chapter ends with a chilling confirmation: Beishan Baiwei’s father, Northmont Blacktiger, has already connected the dots and identified Ji Ning as the killer.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning and Mu Northson analyze the blood robe and realize Crippled Moon was part of a sophisticated military force capable of producing standard-issue formation armor, far beyond Snowdragon Mountain’s means.
- A beast-hide letter reveals the existence of the “Tianfang World” and the concept of “retirement” from this mysterious army, confirming Crippled Moon’s background.
- The group meticulously disposes of the entire storage treasure (except the pure Primordial Liquid) by having Qingqing toss it into the deep terrestrial magnetic light—an irreversible method of destroying any possible tracking marks.
- Ji Ning uses his divine sense to confirm that only three people saw their transformed warship, and they believe the trail is safe.
- The team debates how to deal with Shui Yi: Uncle White suggests luring him out rather than infiltrating the Dong clan’s heavily fortified territory, and Ji Ning begins formulating a plan based on the intelligence they gathered earlier.
- In a parallel scene, the Xuan Army’s red-haired elder declares that their intelligence network in Anchan Commandery is superior to Heavenly Treasures Mountain’s, vowing to avenge their fallen brother.
- The chapter ends with Beishan Blacktiger confronting his son Baiwei, coldly deducing that Ji Ning is the culprit—raising the stakes for our protagonist.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Primordial Fiendgod Worldview**: The novel’s ancient beings, like the secret Xuan Army, operate on a timescale that makes mortals seem like mayflies. Their cautious, learned fear of humanity’s rapid cultivation speed is a recurring theme. Here, the Xuan Army’s reaction to Crippled Moon’s death showcases that even retired veterans are protected by a web of brotherhood that spans worlds.

**Retirement in Xianxia Armies**: The concept of a cultivator “retiring” after millennia of service is a brilliant inversion of standard fantasy tropes. In many xianxia settings, powerful cultivators don’t “retire”—they either die or ascend. But here, the Northmont clan’s secret army allows members to return to their homelands to enjoy their remaining years, only to be killed by a younger generation. This sets up a classic “you killed my brother” revenge cycle that drives the plot forward.

**Terrestrial Magnetic Light**: A mythical phenomenon in Daoist cosmology, often considered the inner magnetic field of a planet or realm. In Desolate Era, it’s used as an impassable barrier and a perfect dumping ground for any trace-able items—no spiritual imprint can survive being tossed into it.

**The Lure Strategy**: Xianxia protocol 101: if you can’t breach the fortress, make the fortress come to you. Ji Ning’s decision to lure Shui Yi out is a classic tactical shift from brute force to psychological hunting, which often yields cleaner results.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in “clean-up after the hit.” Ji Ning’s methodical paranoia—throwing away the jade bottle, deep-sixing the storage treasure in the earth’s magnetic field—shows how much he’s learned from his father’s cold pragmatism. But the real gut-punch comes at the end: no matter how careful you are, the north wind carries whispers. Beishan Blacktiger’s casual deduction is a reminder that in the Grand Xia, information flows like blood through stone veins. For readers, keep an eye on how Ji Ning plans to “lure” Shui Yi—and whether that plan will hold up once the Northmont clan’s secret army catches the scent.
