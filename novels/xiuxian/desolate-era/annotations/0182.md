## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow cultivators, we’ve finally emerged from the meat grinder of the Black-White College trials! And what a beautiful, soul-cleansing chapter this is. It’s a well-earned breather, a victory lap, and a loot distribution sequence all rolled into one. Our boy Ji Ning isn’t just resting on his laurels—he is meticulously consolidating his newfound status, reinforcing his bonds, and setting the stage for the next phase of his ascension. From a heartfelt favor for a friend to a jaw-dropping haul of cultivation resources, Ji Ning is playing the game of Xianxia politics with the same ruthless efficiency he wields his swords. We get to see the immediate social and material rewards of being acknowledged as a true monster genius. Enemies become absent, allies become generous, and the protagonist gets to do the most satisfying thing in any cultivation novel: burn through stacks of Primordial Liquid to fuel an explosive power-up. But it’s not all smooth sailing—a cultivation puzzle emerges that hints at an even more terrifying foundation than anyone expected. Get ready for a chapter of pure, uncut Xianxia dopamine.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Ji Ning remembering a promise he made on the road to Anchan City. He sends his clever retainer, Yunzhou, along with Beishan Baiwei’s men, to find and bring his friend Meng Yan back to the Black-White College, proving that Ji Ning’s Dao-heart values loyalty and reward as much as personal power.

Next, Beishan Baiwei makes his move. Having fulfilled his father’s orders to deliver gifts, he presents his own personal offering: a top-secret map of the entire Grand Xia Dynasty. While only the Anchan Commandery is detailed, this is an invaluable treasure for any cultivator planning to venture out into the dangerous, mysterious world. Ji Ning is genuinely moved by the gesture.

Ji Ning then conducts a full inventory of his haul. He assesses the two new sets of flying swords (a set of seventy-two and a superior set of eighteen), evaluating them for his signature Lesser Thousand Sword Formation. He mentally notes the escalating equipment cost of his cultivation path: Human-ranked, then Earth-ranked, then Heaven-ranked swords as his realm advances.

He refines the other gifts: the new wings for camouflage, a boat for travel, and—most critically—a Miasma Treasure. Ji Ning’s tactical genius shines as he immediately devises a new killer strategy: use the miasma to blind his enemies while relying on his unique Divine Sense to see perfectly, turning a standard escape tool into a terrifying close-quarters assassination method.

Finally, Ji Ning enters seclusion to refine eight hundred jin of Primordial Liquid. He pushes his Ki Refining foundation far past the legendary ‘Celestial Immortal foundation’ baseline of eight hundred jin, only to find that his Zifu still isn’t full. This anomaly leaves even him puzzled, hinting at a foundation that is truly unprecedented.

The chapter ends with a parallel scene. In a poor district of Anchan City, a despondent and injured Meng Yan, who had given up on his cultivation path, is shocked to receive a polite invitation from Yunzhou, who introduces himself as a servant of Master Ji Ning—a moment of supreme vindication and hope.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Primordial Liquid (元液): The Currency of Immortals.**
Primordial Liquid isn't just a valuable resource; it's the high-stakes currency of the cultivation world. It represents the most refined and pure form of natural energy, so potent that it can be absorbed instantly, bypassing the slow process of refining elemental ores. In this chapter, Ji Ning treats eight hundred jin of the stuff like a power drink, which is an astronomical sum. For context, a good Earth-ranked magic treasure is worth ten jin. A high-stakes bet between two young masters of the city was only ninety jin. This shows not only the immense wealth flowing from the Marquis of Anchan but also the sheer, terrifying scale of resources needed for a genius with an ultra-rare foundation to advance. It reinforces the Xianxia trope that true power isn't just about talent—it's about the resources only the top of the power pyramid can command.

**The Zifu [Violet Palace] Foundation: Preparing the Soil.**
The description of Ji Ning’s Zifu lake expanding into an ocean is a critical piece of cultivation theory. In this system, the Zifu is the ‘dantian’ of a Ki Refiner. The size and quality of the ‘sea’ of liquid energy you build at this stage directly determines the quality of your ‘Dharmic Image’ at the Wanxiang stage. A ‘Celestial Immortal Foundation’ (consuming eight hundred jin of Primordial Liquid) guarantees you will condense the best possible Wanxiang Dharmic Image, a massive advantage. Ji Ning surpassing this limit without reaching capacity is a clear signal from the author that his foundation is not just top-tier—it is an anomaly, perhaps a sign of his Godbeast body refining path or his mysterious Nuwa Diagram soul technique synergizing in an unprecedented way.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for one of the most satisfying sequences in a progression fantasy: the loot montage. Pay close attention to Ji Ning’s tactical mind as he looks at the Miasma Treasure. This isn't just him admiring a new toy; it's a perfect demonstration of a Xianxia genius's greatest strength—taking a standard tool and instantly devising a counter-intuitive, devastatingly effective new application for it.

The scene with Meng Yan is the heart of the chapter. It underscores a key theme of *Desolate Era*: Ji Ning’s Dao-heart isn't just about cold, ruthless efficiency. He is a man of his word who rewards loyalty and courage. This small act of patronage will likely pay dividends down the line, showing that building a network of allies is just as important as building personal power.

Also, keep an eye on that cultivation anomaly. Ji Ning has just casually consumed a fortune in resources and hasn't hit a wall. For the author to mention this explicitly is a major flag. This is the kind of setup that usually precedes a shocking revelation about a character's true origins or potential. Ji Ning isn't building a Celestial Immortal foundation; he might be laying the ground for something far more monstrous.
