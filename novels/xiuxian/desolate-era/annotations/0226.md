## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a masterclass in Xianxia patience and cold, surgical planning. We watch Ji Ning transition from a reactive avenger into a methodical predator. His target isn't some mighty sect elder or a great demon; it's the entire Water Clan—a small tribe that serves as the home base for his true target, a Snowdragon Mountain disciple named Dong Qi. Ning's plan is brutally elegant: kidnap the chieftain, force the clan's strongest member to rush home from his post in a faraway super-tribe, and then intercept him in the wilderness. No messy city sieges, no collateral mortal slaughter. Just a clean, isolated kill-box set up in the empty sky. The tension isn't in the fight—it's in the *waiting* and the chilling precision of the trap.

## Key Plot Points
- Ji Ning uses his divine sense to surveil Yi River City, quickly identifying the chieftain, Shui Tianyi, by observing how the tribesmen address him.
- After confirming his target, Ning executes a lightning-fast raid: a soul shock, a sword strike through the roof, and a capture using the 'One-Breath Bag', a Human-ranked treasure designed for taking live prisoners.
- The Water Clan panics, checks the chieftain's life tablet (still intact), and sends an urgent summons for their strongest member, 'Shui Yi', who serves as a Zifu Disciple retainer in the distant Dong Clan.
- Ji Ning calmly waits for over half a month in a hidden puppet cave manor, knowing the trap is already set.
- Finally, Shui Yi arrives, but before he can descend into his home city, a figure blocks his path in the sky: a beast-pelt-clad youth with icy eyes, asking a single, terrible question. The hunt is on.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Politics of 'Root' (根基):** This chapter showcases a classic Xianxia dilemma: a tribe's greatest strength is also its greatest vulnerability. A tribe survives by sending its most talented members to serve powerful external clans. This brings in resources (like Primordial Liquid) and protection, but it also creates a *root* (根基) that the tribe is anchored to. If an enemy targets this root, the distant retainer has no choice but to rush back, walking directly into a pre-set ambush. This isn't just loyalty—it's a structural weakness built into the very fabric of this world. Ji Ning's plan is brilliant precisely because it exploits this system.

**Soul-Based Attacks vs. Physical Defenses:** Ji Ning's opening move—a direct, formless assault on Shui Tianyi's soul—is a hallmark of high-level combat in this setting. Having trained his soul through the Nuwa Diagram and possessing the Divine Sense of a Primal-level cultivator despite his Xiantian body, Ning can bypass all physical armor and body-hardening techniques. In Xianxia, a strong soul is the ultimate trump card, able to defeat a physically superior opponent who has neglected their spiritual cultivation. The fact that Ning uses it as a routine opening against a weaker foe shows just how far ahead of his prey he truly is.

## Reading Guide
Ready for a cold dish of revenge served with a side of total domination? This chapter is all about savoring the *process* rather than the result. Ji Ning is no longer the young boy chasing a fleeting shadow of vengeance. He has become an architect of death, calculating logistics and exploiting social systems. Pay attention to the 'One-Breath Bag'—it's not a flashy treasure, but it perfectly highlights Ning's pragmatic and methodical nature. He didn't need a grand magical weapon; he just needed a tool to do the job cleanly. The real treat here is the mindset shift. He's not just executing a plan; he's sending a message without a single word of threat. He makes Shui Yi *know* that something is terribly wrong, forcing him to come running straight into the jaws of the trap. The stage is set for a satisfying crunch.
