## Chapter Overview

The price of ambition comes due in this chapter, as the Shaoyin clan’s Ancestor Mysterious Mystic grits his teeth and pays the Bloodcloud Hall’s staggering ransom for Ji Ning’s head. Three top-grade Immortal-ranked treasures are the cost of setting a year-long death clock on our protagonist. Meanwhile, Ji Ning himself, fresh from his reunion with his master and the Black-White College, steps into a whole new kind of battlefield: the political drawing rooms of the Grand Xia Imperial Capital. A visit to his cousin, Xiyue Commandery Princess, brings him face-to-face with the Windbinding Island delegation from the Heavenly Realm, where two very different suitors are vying for his cousin’s hand. One is a humble, inferior young man destined to marry into the Yuchi clan. The other is an arrogant prince who treats the former like dirt and doesn’t bother to hide his disdain for Ji Ning’s mysterious background. The knives are out, both literally and socially.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Bloodcloud Hall contract is signed.** Ancestor Mysterious Mystic negotiates the price for Ji Ning’s assassination. The silver-robed maiden wastes no time on courtesy: the risk is not Ji Ning himself, but his unknown master, a potential True Immortal or Empyrean God. She demands three top-grade Immortal-ranked treasures, and despite his protests, the Celestial Immortal pays. A one-year deadline is set.
- **A Dark Timeline.** The narrative casually reveals that Ji Ning’s potential is judged as “at most comparable to a 500,000-year Loose Immortal.” This terrifying assessment comes from a Celestial Immortal who has lived for eons—yet the reader knows Ji Ning’s true growth rate far exceeds even that benchmark.
- **Reunion with the Commandery Princess.** Ji Ning arrives at the Yan Estate in the Imperial Capital to visit his cousin, Yuchi Xiyue. She is overjoyed, but her situation has changed: she is now courted by two Windbinding Island disciples from the Heavenly Realm.
- **The Two Suitors.** Feng Yungu, the humble and inferior young man willing to marry into the family, has already been accepted by Xiyue. Feng Yunpeng, the arrogant son of the Island Lord, openly insults Yungu and treats him as a useless rival, while also showing disdain for Ji Ning’s refusal to reveal his teacher.
- **An uneasy social chess game.** Ji Ning navigates the subtle hostility of Feng Yunpeng with calm brevity, but the tension is clear. The chapter ends with Ji Ning excusing himself to visit Heavenly Treasures Mountain, leaving the political intrigue hanging.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Top-grade Immortal-ranked treasures (仙阶极品法宝).** In Xianxia economies, treasures are ranked from Mortal → Earth → Heaven → Immortal → Pure Yang. Top-grade Immortal-ranked treasures are the peak of what a Celestial Immortal typically carries. Offering *three* of them is not just expensive—it is a statement that the Shaoyin clan considers Ji Ning a long-term existential threat worth bleeding for.
- **The Bloodcloud Hall’s business model.** The assassin guild does not merely charge based on target strength. It evaluates *operational risk*—specifically, the chance that the target’s protector will retaliate. The silver-robed maiden’s calm explanation is a classic Xianxia scene: the price is high not because Ji Ning is strong *now*, but because his unknown master could erase an entire assassination squad in seconds.
- **Dao-companion politics and the Heavenly Realm.** “Dao-companion” (道侣) in Xianxia is more than a marriage; it is a spiritual and strategic alliance. The alliance between the Grand Xia Dynasty and Windbinding Island creates a political marketplace where imperial clan members marry island disciples. A *matrilocal* marriage (入赘), where the husband joins the wife’s clan, is the only way for Yuchi Xiyue to rebuild her ancestral Yuchi family line, and it marks Feng Yungu as a “low-status” husband in the eyes of an arrogant rival like Feng Yunpeng.
- **The Heavenly Realm (天界).** A classic tier in Chinese cosmology, here depicted as a vast upper dimension divided into the Eastern Heavenly Court (Daoist) and Western Spirit Mountain (Buddhist), with countless smaller powers and races like Asuras and Heavenly Beings. Windbinding Island is one such minor power.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a classic Xianxia *breather* before the storm—but it’s a breather filled with political landmines. The assassination contract sets a ticking clock that will define the next arc, while the meeting with Feng Yunpeng introduces a new type of antagonist: not one you can simply kill, but one protected by a fragile alliance. Watch how Ji Ning handles the tension. He doesn’t roar or draw his sword. He simply leaves. The calmest Dao-hearts are the ones preparing the sharpest counter-strike. And for Xiyue Commandery Princess? Her choice of a humble, obedient husband over a powerful, arrogant one tells you everything about the scars her clan’s massacre left on her soul. She will never again marry someone who can control her fate.
