## Chapter Overview

We finally get a real look at Xiyue Commandery Princess—the Yan King’s adopted daughter who lost her entire family, and who just found out she has a *little brother*. But instead of rushing into a tearful reunion, she holds back, watching Ji Ning from a distance. The parallel between his features and her father’s is gut-wrenching. Meanwhile, Ancestor Xuanji has had enough of playing defense. He dispatches an ancient, dying Loose Immortal with a Reincarnation Edict—basically a golden ticket to skip the line in the Netherworld—to kill Ji Ning in the Grand Xia capital. The clock is ticking. And Ji Ning? He’s deliberately exposing himself at the Treasure Competition Assembly like bait, daring the Shaoyan clan to make a move. The chess pieces are all on the board.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiyue Commandery Princess arrives at Heavenly Treasures Mountain with Qingliu the Immortal and asks to locate Ji Ning. She sees him from a distance and is struck by how much he resembles her father, Yuchi Shan. Tears come to her eyes, but she holds back.
- Through Qingliu, we learn the backstory: Yuchi Shan was Xiyue’s father. Yuchi Snow was his sister. That makes Ji Ning Xiyue’s biological maternal uncle—a “little brother” in the affectionate sense.
- Qingliu briefs Xiyue on Ji Ning’s recent behavior: he came to the capital without changing his face, riding Shaoyan Nong’s own carriage, and chased the Shaoyan clan to Many Treasures Peak. It’s an open provocation.
- Qingliu analyzes that killing Ji Ning in the capital would require a top-tier Loose Immortal who can one-shot him in a single breath—and even that is not certain. Xiyue finally relaxes.
- The scene cuts to Ancestor Xuanji, who personally visits an old, dying Loose Immortal named Fuyun. He offers a Reincarnation Edict in exchange for killing Ji Ning within three days.
- Fuyun hesitates, then swears a Heavenly Dao oath, accepts the edict, and prepares to hunt Ji Ning in the capital. Xuanji vanishes into starlight, cold and absolute.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Reincarnation Edict (转世法旨)**: In the grand hierarchy of the Three Realms, major powers—those who have mastered a Heavenly Dao—can issue “Edicts” that carry the weight of their authority. The Netherworld Kingdom, despite being an independent administrative branch of the cosmos, respects these edicts because offending a Heavenly Dao–level power means cosmic trouble. For a desperate Loose Immortal who is about to succumb to the Three Calamities and Nine Tribulations, a Reincarnation Edict is the ultimate golden parachute: a guaranteed shot at a new life without the risk of being judged harshly for past sins. It’s the kind of treasure that makes even a dying old monster willing to charge into the guarded heart of the Grand Xia capital.

**Loose Immortal (散仙)**: When a cultivator fails the Celestial Immortal tribulation but manages to save their Primal soul, they become a Loose Immortal—immortal in the sense that they don’t age, but they still face periodic tribulations (minor ones every 300 years, major ones every 900). Most don’t survive past 100,000 years. Fuyun is at 128,000 years, meaning he is on his last legs. His desperation makes him the perfect tool for a clan elder who values results over sentiment.

**It’s all relative**: Chinese family terms are specific. “弟弟” (little brother) is used here because Ji Ning and Xiyue share blood through the Yuchi family—Ji Ning’s mother is Xiyue’s paternal aunt. In the novel’s emotional logic, finding a surviving relative is like finding a piece of the past that was thought lost forever. Xiyue’s tears speak volumes about her orphaned soul.

## Reading Guide

Get ready, because this chapter pulls double duty: warm fuzzies and cold-blooded assassination setup. Xiyue’s quiet recognition of Ji Ning is one of the most tender moments in this arc—she’s literally watching her dead father’s face on a living boy. But don’t let the softness fool you. Ancestor Xuanji’s response is pure, clinical fury. He didn’t even give the order himself until now, but once Nong’er’s killer started flashing Shaoyan Nong’s carriage around the capital? That’s a direct slap to the clan’s face. The old ox is not done yet. The Loose Immortal Fuyun is a ticking bomb—old, desperate, and armed with a blank check to buy the deadliest disposable treasures he can find. The next bait-and-trap encounter is going to be brutal.
