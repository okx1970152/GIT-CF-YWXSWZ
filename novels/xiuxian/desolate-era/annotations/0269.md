## Chapter Overview

The chapter opens with Ji Ning and his party in the warship, bracing for a final, desperate charge against the wave-soldier formation. Shaoyan Nong pulls out a scroll bearing the single character ‘Still’—and suddenly the world turns to molasses. The wave-soldiers are frozen mid-strike while the warship glides through the gap, unlocking the final void gate. On the other side, the scroll is spent, the treasure is wasted on a single use, and the party has barely escaped. Meanwhile, the goblin commanders across the battlefield piece together the horror of what just happened: a time-distortion treasure. Duohe Immortal, the veteran Loose Immortal, delivers a rallying speech that frames this as the last chance—not just for freedom, but for survival. The battle for control of the Immortal Mansion has just escalated from a desperate raid to an existential, no-retreat war.

## Key Plot Points

1. The warship’s golden shield collapses under sustained assault—the formation is disintegrating and the vessel nearly fails.
2. Shaoyan Nong activates a scroll inscribed with the Dao-Character ‘Still,’ a time-distortion treasure that slows the wave-soldiers to a crawl while the warship dashes through the blockade.
3. The party enters the final branch hall via void gate; the scroll’s power is completely spent—it becomes an ordinary piece of paper.
4. True Treasure Daoist reports the failure to Duohe Immortal; Duohe explains that the goblins’ perception of time was locally dilated—they felt only an instant while the warship traveled for a long duration.
5. Xiangliu Fang escapes laughing as the goblin forces merge into a single enormous wave-army.
6. Duohe Immortal and Wu Sui Immortal confer; they realize the humans’ possession of such a treasure means their background is terrifying. This is the last branch hall, and their only path to survival.
7. Duohe delivers a final speech to the gathered goblins: this is no longer a fight for freedom, but a struggle for the right of their descendants to even exist. The goblins’ morale surges into a determined, desperate battle-cry.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The scroll’s character ‘定’ (dìng) is a classic Daoist and Buddhist symbol for stability, stillness, and the cessation of motion. In Chinese occult traditions, written characters can act as talismans that channel cosmic authority—here, the calligraphy itself radiates the aura of a grand Dao, overpowering the natural laws of the battlefield. The effect is described as a local time dilation, making the goblins feel like only an instant passed while the warship actually covered kilometers. This is a core Xianxia power fantasy: the idea that comprehension of a single word can bend reality. The chapter also implicitly draws on the classic saying “天上一日，地上一年” (A day in Heaven is a year on Earth)—a staple of Chinese mythology that frames time as a subjective, hierarchical dimension. For readers new to Xianxia, this is your first real taste of how treasures in this world can outright *override* physics, turning a losing battle into a clean escape with a single activation.

### Time-distortion treasure

A rare artifact that locally alters the flow of time, often used in Xianxia to escape certain death. Can make an instant feel like a thousand years, or vice versa, depending on the type.

### Dao-Character

A single written character imbued with the comprehension of a Dao, often used as a one-time talisman to invoke a specific law of the universe — here, the concept of "Stillness" or "Stasis."

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in “escalation through revelation.” On the surface, it’s a simple escape sequence—the warship breaks through, the party gets inside. But the real punch comes from the goblins’ reaction. Duohe Immortal’s speech is the emotional anchor: it transforms a tactical defeat into a desperate, world-ending narrative. The goblins are no longer just fighting for a key; they’re fighting for the right of their children to eat, drink, and breathe. If you’ve been treating these goblins as generic mooks, this chapter re-frames them as a besieged civilization with everything to lose. Also note the narrative economy: a treasure that could kill a Loose Immortal is used *just* to buy a few seconds of gap. That tells you how high the stakes are—and how deep the Shaoyan clan’s pockets run. Keep an eye on that scroll’s origin story; it might be a hint of what kind of Dao-level artifacts are still lurking in the Immortal Mansion.
