## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a massive tone shift, fellow Daoists. After the breakneck escape and the devastating loss of our little Feather-Sovereign Duke wannabe, this chapter slows way down to give us the **consequences**. And I mean *consequences* with a capital C. We leave Ji Ning’s perspective entirely and step into the sandals of **Ancestor Xuanji**, a Celestial Immortal who’s just lost the last living piece of his most beloved disciple. This isn’t just a grumpy old man; this is a being who can rewind time and tear open pocket dimensions with his bare hands. And he's *pissed*. The chapter is a masterclass in shifting the power scale from “talented kid in a sect” to **“ancient monster who bends reality.”** The stakes just got a whole lot higher, and the safe feeling of being a Big Fish in the Black-White College? That just evaporated.

## Key Plot Points
- **Grief of an Immortal**: The chapter opens not with action, but with raw, personal grief. Ancestor Xuanji receives the news of Shao Yanong’s death and his first reaction is sorrow for his fallen disciple, Li Shui the Immortal. This isn’t a cold, calculating villain; this is a father-figure who has failed the woman he loved like a daughter.
- **The Unbreakable Rules of the Clan**: We learn why the Shaoyan clan can’t just absorb the loss. The Wu Jiang Immortal Estate trial was for the position of Feather-Sovereign Duke, a clan law that even a Celestial Immortal cannot casually overturn. It establishes that Xianxia clans operate on rigid, inflexible rules that bind even their strongest members.
- **The Power of a Celestial Immortal**: Xuanji demonstrates his terrifying might. He doesn't just brute-force an entry into the Immortal Estate; he *tears open the walls* between pocket dimensions by exploiting the connection point. Inside, he casts **Time Rewind**, a technique that literally replays history, watching Shao Yanong’s final moments like a grim movie.
- **Unmasking the Killer**: Through the re-enactment, Xuanji identifies Ji Ning as the culprit, observing his Windwing Evasion technique and his explosive Domain of the Dao. He correctly identifies Ji Ning’s trump card—the Soulseizing Ring—as a divine artifact from the Primordial Fiendgod Era, and understands that Ji Ning is blessed by tremendous luck.
- **The Condemnation**: Xuanji’s judgment is absolute. He recognizes Ji Ning’s talent but dismisses it as irrelevant. His final line—“No matter how great one’s fortune, it cannot overpower divine abilities”—is a chilling declaration of intent. He knows Ji Ning is a Black-White College disciple, and he’s heading straight for Anchan City to start his investigation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a textbook example of **Delegated Awe (代位敬畏)**, where a character’s power is established not by their own actions, but by the fearful reactions of others and the sheer, impossible scale of their abilities. We already got this from Beishan Xuantang’s awe of his father, but here it’s taken to eleven. Ancestor Xuanji barely does anything physical. He *rewinds time*. That’s not a spell; that’s a philosophical violation of the natural order. In Daoist and Xianxia lore, time is a fundamental pillar of the Heavenly Dao. Only an existence that has transcended the Three Realms—a Celestial Immortal—is permitted to tamper with it. And even then, it costs him “precipitously” in Immortal power. This detail grounds the otherwise god-like power in a system of cost and effort, making it feel like a scientific principle rather than pure magic. Also, note the specifics of his grief: his initial sorrow is for *Li Shui*, not for Shao Yanong directly. Shao Yanong was a vessel for his failure towards his true love (his disciple). This creates a far more tragic, complex motivation for his vengeance than simple revenge for a dead clan member.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is the first major step into the story’s mid-game. Ji Ning has been a big fish in the small pond of Swallow Mountain. He’s a prodigy in the Black-White College. But the conflict here is **clan vs. sect**. A single Celestial Immortal with a grudge against a single disciple can bring immense pressure to bear, even on a premier academy like the Black-White College. Watch how Xuanji operates: he doesn't charge in blindly. He investigates first (at Heavenly Treasures Mountain). He’s methodical. He’s ancient. He knows exactly how the world works. For Ji Ning, this marks the end of a relatively safe period. The “happy” adventure at the Immortal Estate is over. The consequences have arrived, and they’ve brought a primordial monster with them. The game has changed from “can I get stronger?” to “can I survive the attention of a walking ancient god?”

## Deep Dive: The Tragic Irony of the Shaoyan Clan
Shao Yanong’s death is a perfect storm of tragic irony from the clan’s perspective. They carefully vetted an Immortal Estate, determined it to be tough but survivable, and handed their heir the key. They did *everything right* according to their risk-assessment protocols. But they couldn’t account for a variable like Ji Ning, a cultivator whose strength is entirely built on reverse-scheming their own arrogant scion. Xuanji’s initial outburst—“A trial! A mere trial! It was only for the position of Feather-Sovereign Duke!”—is the primal scream of a system that prides itself on being supreme and secure, only to be broken by a single, unpredictable mortal. The Shaoyan clan’s entire political machine, designed to uplift a puppet, was shattered because its tool was too weak to survive a chance encounter with a monster.
