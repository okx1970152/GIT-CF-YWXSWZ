## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a quiet before the storm, and it hits *hard*. The Diancai Immortal has set his tribulation date: nine days from now. Ji Ning scrambles to consolidate his power, pushing his second true body to the late Void stage and prepping his closest allies for the coming trial. But the real weight here isn’t martial—it’s emotional. Ji Ning’s master speaks of death with a smile, and our boy’s usually steel-like Dao-heart cracks just a little. This is classic Xianxia tension: the calm, fatalistic preparation before a tribulation that might erase everything.

## Key Plot Points

- Immortal Wufeng reveals that the Diancai Immortal will face his Heavenly Tribulation in **nine days**, and that only Ji Ning and Wufeng will serve as protectors—everyone else is too weak to matter.
- Ji Ning confidently declares that his swordsmanship now surpasses the legendary Immortal Beixing's, having fully mastered and refined the *Three-Foot Sword*—a statement that stuns even the ancient Wufeng.
- The Diancai Immortal, in a chillingly calm moment, tells Ji Ning he would have “no regrets” even if he fails the tribulation, testing his disciple’s composure.
- Ji Ning distributes **five million jin of Primordial Liquid** each to Uncle White and Xiaoqing, ordering them to break through to late Void without exceeding into completion.
- Ji Ning himself enters the underwater estate, using over twenty million jin of Primordial Liquid to push his own second true body to the late Void stage, fueling the Golden Lotus Primordial Soul to near-maturity.
- The chapter ends with the party assembling at the Black-White College, where Ji Ning vouches for Uncle White’s status as a top-tier formation grandmaster—a key asset for the tribulation’s defense.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### The Heavenly Tribulation (天劫)
This isn’t just a lightning bolt from the sky. In the Desolate Era cosmology, the Heavenly Tribulation is the universe’s ultimate filter—a self-correcting mechanism that tests whether a cultivator is truly worthy of immortality. It’s not a single event but a multi-layered assault: wind, fire, lightning, and the dreaded **heart demon (心魔)**, which attacks the soul itself. The failure rate is astronomical; as the chapter notes, even in the Grand Xia—a realm of countless billions—only a handful of Earth Immortals have succeeded in recent decades.

### The Samsara of Tribulations (三灾九劫)
Below the Celestial Immortal level, cultivators face the *Three Calamities and Nine Tribulations*—periodic disasters every three hundred years and major calamities every nine hundred. But the Heavenly Tribulation is the *final exam*: if you pass, you transcend the Five Elements and achieve true immortality. If you fail… well, you either die outright or, if your soul escapes, you become a **Loose Immortal (散仙)**, doomed to suffer increasingly powerful tribulations until your eventual annihilation. That’s why the Diancai Immortal’s casual “no regrets” carries such tragic weight—he’s walking into a statistical near-certainty of death.

### The Golden Lotus Primordial Soul (金莲元神)
Daoist internal alchemy describes the cultivation of the soul as a process of refinement and growth. The Golden Lotus is a classic symbol of this: rooted in mud (the mortal body), yet growing through the fire (the elemental power of cultivation) toward purity. Ji Ning’s “late Void” breakthrough—where the lotus reaches near-maturity with a single seed—signals that his soul is now only one step away from the threshold of true immortality.

### Heartforce (心力)

A formless, metaphysical power derived from a cultivator’s will and Dao-heart, distinct from Ki or Divine Power.

### Dao-heart (道心)

A cultivator’s innermost spiritual resolve and emotional sincerity; Ji Ning’s is tested by the prospect of his master’s death.

## Reading Guide

This is a *preparation* chapter, but don’t skip it—it’s where the emotional stakes are set. Watch how Ji Ning’s usually cold, clinical demeanor warms, just slightly, around his master. The scene where the Diancai Immortal jokes about failure is a masterclass in Xianxia heartstring-tugging: a senior who has already accepted his probable death, trying to comfort a junior who cannot. If you’re a fan of the bond between master and disciple, this one’s for you. Also, keep an eye on Uncle White’s offhand mention of formation mastery—when a tribulation is involved, no detail is wasted.
