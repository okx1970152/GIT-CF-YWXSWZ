## Chapter Overview

New chapter, fresh terror! Our crew of Wanxiang Adepts has barely escaped the nightmarish army of Dao-soldiers outside, only to stumble into a different kind of hell—the silent, ancient heart of the Wu Jiang Immortal Mansion itself. Shaoyan Nong tries to play the calming host, assuring everyone that this subsidiary hall has been untouched for millions of years. But if you think a dead Immortal’s house is going to be a cozy inn, you’ve got another thing coming. The moment they catch their breath, the mansion’s security system—a set of ancient Qiongqi golems—sniffs them out. One nasty formation later, the team is plunged into a sealed void, under a barrage of uncannily powerful icy arrows, completely cut off from their Fiendgod bodyguard. The message is clear: in a dead Immortal’s home, the traps are very much alive.

## Key Plot Points

- **Safe haven? No, ambush.** After a frantic escape, the group settles in what seems to be an empty, silent hall. Their relief is palpable, but so is their unspoken anxiety.
- **Shaoyan Nong’s responsibility.** The narrative emphasizes that the Fiendgod Xiangliu Fang is still fighting outside to buy them time. Shaoyan Nong can only watch through a spatial hole, unable to help. This frames him not as a spoiled noble, but as a man carrying the weight of his own trial.
- **The eighteen Qiongqi awaken.** Deep in the hall, a line of golem statues crack to life. They are non-living, non-dying guardians who follow the final command of Immortal Wu Jiang: kill all intruders who are not the master or the master’s recognized heir.
- **Formation trap is sprung.** Without warning, the space around the team twists. They are displaced into a sensory void. Shaoyan Nong confirms the worst: the Immortal Mansion Key cannot open a passage here. The Fiendgod cannot enter.
- **The attack begins.** A rain of icy arrows materializes from nowhere, each arrow terrifyingly powerful. Even Ji Ning’s Water-Fire Lotus shatters under the concentrated assault. The chapter ends on a classic cliffhanger, with the implicit promise that someone may die.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **What’s a Fiendgod Bodyguard?** Xiangliu Fang is explicitly referred to as a ‘retainer’ and a ‘Fiendgod’ bound to Shaoyan Nong. Fiendgods in this setting are beings of primordial origin, vastly superior in raw physical power to ordinary cultivators. They are often ancient beings who serve powerful clans in exchange for protection or resources. A single competent Fiendgod can dominate hundreds of mortal soldiers, as we saw in the previous chapter.
- **Golems vs. Living Beings.** The Qiongqi are described as puppets or golems—non-living constructs animated by formations and spirit-energy. Unlike mortals or beasts, they do not age, sleep, or fear death. They are the perfect, eternal guardians. In Daoist Xianxia lore, this is a common trope; Immortals often leave behind silent armies of stone, jade, or metal to guard their secrets for millions of years.
- **The Battle of Fame Setup.** This chapter subtly sets the stage for a potential “battle of fame.” A battle of fame in Xianxia is not necessarily a victory; it is a clash where a rising cultivator survives against, or fights to a near-draw with, a much more powerful entity, thereby announcing their potential to the world. Ji Ning is currently helpless against this formation, but the stage is set for him to prove his mettle.
- **"Immortal Mansion Key" limitations.** The Key grants entry to the mansion but doesn’t grant full authority. It’s like having a skeleton key to a giant fortress—it gets you past the front gate, but the security systems inside will still treat you as an intruder until you find the command center and actually *bind* the mansion’s core.

### Nascent intelligence

The state of a high-grade magic treasure approaching sentience; the artifact can exhibit instinctual behavior (living eyes, draconic fury) but has not yet developed a full, independent spirit or personality.

## Reading Guide

Brace yourself for a Xianxia classic: the “Surrounded and Picked Off One by One” trope. The team is isolated, their heavy hitter is locked out, and a formation is actively trying to murder them. This is where the author separates the main characters from the redshirts. Keep an eye on how Ji Ning reacts—does he panic? No. He analyzes, he warns, but most of all, he *waits*. This passivity is intentional. It sets up a contrasting explosion of violence when he eventually finds his opening. Also, pay attention to the Qiongqi. These tiger-headed, ox-bodied, spiky-winged beasts are mythological monsters straight out of the *Classic of Mountains and Seas* (Shan Hai Jing). Their presence here signals that we have entered the truly ancient, primordial territory of the mansion—no more warm-up acts.
