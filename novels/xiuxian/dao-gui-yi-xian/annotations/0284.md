## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow travelers of the twisted path! Look, I know we’ve been through a lot of bureaucratic horror in this novel, but Chapter 284 is a whole new vibe. It’s the **HR Interview from Hell**—Supervisory Heavenly Office edition. Li Huowang finally makes his move to join the big boys, but nothing in this world comes easy. This chapter isn’t about blood and guts; it’s about **paperwork, bribes, and a terrifyingly cheerful big-headed doll** who moonlights as a celestial headhunter. Get ready for some of the most stressful workplace onboarding you’ve ever read. And oh, yeah—Li Huowang gets his hands on a juicy, classified dossier about his favorite lunatics: the **Zuowandao**. Spoiler alert: they’re even weirder than we thought.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang meets a mysterious figure from the **Supervisory Heavenly Office**, disguised as an unsettling big-headed doll mask. The doll wastes no time, immediately hinting that joining requires greasing the right palms.
- Our boy plays the game well. After slipping the doll a hefty bribe of gold and **yang-life pellets**, he receives an iron token bearing the grade “Gui Zu” (癸卒)—a rank near the bottom of the celestial bureaucracy’s ladder.
- The doll hands him his first assignment: investigate a village for possible **Zuowandao** activity. It’s a test. Pass, and he’s in. Fail, and the token is scrap metal.
- Li Huowang reads the official dossier on the Zuowandao. Key intel: they are organized like **mahjong tiles** (Bamboo, Dots, Characters → Four Joys → Three Fundamentals → the nearly-mythical **Dice**), and their recruitment method is based entirely on **deception**. They trick everyone—even themselves.
- The chapter ends on a contemplative note as morning comes. Li Huowang is left standing alone with a packet of classified intel, a severe lack of sleep, and an overwhelming sense that he’s just signed up for something much bigger than a simple job.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Ten Heavenly Stems (天干 Tiāngān):** When the big-headed doll explains “Gui” (癸), he’s referencing one of the Ten Heavenly Stems, an ancient Chinese system used in calendars, cosmology, and—apparently—bureaucratic hierarchy. The order is: 甲、乙、丙、丁、戊、己、庚、辛、壬、癸. “Gui” is the absolute last, making it the lowest or most junior rank. So Li Huowang, our ever-lucky protagonist, just joined the celestial civil service at the very bottom of the ladder. This “stems and branches” (干支 Gānzhī) system is the same one used to name the Chinese zodiac years, but here, it’s been twisted into a grim organizational chart.

**Mahjong Hierarchy (麻将 Májiàng):** The Zuowandao’s structure is built on mahjong suits—a stroke of genius from the author that makes the sect feel both familiar and deeply alien. In traditional mahjong, the suits are “Bamboo” (条), “Dots/Balls” (饼), and “Characters” (万). The honor tiles are the “Four Winds” (东南西北) and the “Three Dragons” (中發白). The “Dice” (骰子) is the ultimate game-starter, which gives the rumor a chilling logic: if the Dice controls the game, who’s playing it? The author has weaponized a parlor game against us, and it’s terrifying.

**Bribery as Institutional Language:** Li Huowang’s bribe isn’t a crime here; it’s a cultural expectation. The big-headed doll’s words—“think more about other people’s interests”—are a polite, coded demand for a kickback. In many historical and genre depictions of pre-modern Chinese bureaucracy, “tea money” or similar gifts were the grease that kept the gears turning. The horror here is that even a celestial body meant to protect the realm from cosmic threats is just as corrupt as any mortal county office.

### County Gazetteer (县志)

A local administrative record of major events; an empty or sanitized gazetteer in the Dao-Twisted World signals official complicity or denial of supernatural horror.

## Reading Guide

Alright, let’s talk tone. This chapter is a masterclass in **quiet, organizational dread**. There’s no monster under the bed here—just a man in a doll mask and a stack of paperwork. But that’s the point! *Dao Gui Yi Xian* doesn’t always need gore to be scary. The horror here is in the casual normalization of corruption and the realization that Li Huowang is now a tiny cog in a machine that’s probably just as broken as the rest of the world.

Pay close attention to the **Zuowandao dossier**. This is one of the most direct info-dumps we’ve gotten about this group, and the “they-are-all-both-liars-and-deceived” line is a punch to the gut. It implies a recursive, bottomless pit of self-deception and cosmic manipulation. Also, happy morning-after-rooster-crow night shifts! Our man is putting in the work hours now. He’s really earning that corporate healthcare—uh, I mean *celestial occult protection*.
