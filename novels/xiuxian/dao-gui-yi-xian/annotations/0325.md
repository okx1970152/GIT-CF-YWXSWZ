## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang bites off more than he can chew when he goes head-to-head with a Renxiao hive, and the result is a gory, frantic, and utterly desperate battle that redefines the phrase “body horror.” If you thought losing a hand was bad, wait until you see what happens to the rest of him. This isn't a cool cultivator duel; this is a starving animal gnawing off its own leg to escape a trap, mixed with the kind of writhing, visceral metamorphosis that makes you want to take a shower. Get ready for a masterclass in trading flesh for a fighting chance.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is immediately trapped by a Renxiao ritual using black banners that pin his feet and seal his spatial shift ability.
- Swarmed by talismans and attacks, Li Huowang attempts to burn his own skin to fuel a sacrifice to Bashe, a move the lead Renxiao recognizes and interrupts.
- The Renxiao turns its whisk into a Guan Dao, severs Li Huowang’s burning hand, and prepares to deliver a killing blow.
- In a moment of desperate, insane improvisation, Li Huowang severs his own lower body to escape the black banner trap, splitting himself in two.
- The wound does not kill him; instead, a mass of writhing black tentacles (the Black Tai Sui) erupts from the stump, propping up his upper torso like a grotesque octopus.
- The chapter confirms that the lead Renxiao is the mastermind behind the infant thefts and the entire operation.
- The climax reveals a key tactical trick: Li Huowang uses his “walking-out” ability to phase through a fatal stabbing, his real, invisible body standing a zhang away from the illusory one the Renxiao impales.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Renxiao (人魈)** are not just any monsters. The term draws on folk concepts of beings that are “man-like but not man.” In the Dao-Twisted World, they are specifically described as blind, malformed creatures created from humans who suffer into extreme old age, their bodies twisting into something that can only mimic human ritual. They are less a species and more a symptom of a world where piety and suffering curdle into a parody of life.
- **Guan Dao (关刀)** is a traditional Chinese pole weapon, famously associated with the god of war, Guan Yu. It is a heavy, curved blade on a long shaft, meant for devastating cavalry charges. Seeing a Renxiao weaponize a *horsetail whisk* (a Daoist symbol of refined cultivation) *into* a Guan Dao is a perfect example of the novel’s core aesthetic: corrupting a symbol of sacred grace into a brutal instrument of war.
- **Peachwood swords (桃木剑)** are the quintessential Daoist exorcism tool, said to be especially effective against malevolent spirits (and, by extension, walking dead like the Jiangshi). The Renxiao wielding one is a deeply ironic and unsettling image—a monster using the weapon designed to banish monsters.
- **Red-string Copper Coin Sword (红线铜钱剑)** is a classic Chinese ritual weapon, often used in exorcisms. The copper coins are strung on red thread, which itself has apotropaic properties. The fact that it "rejects" the peachwood sword upon contact, sparking and hissing, visually conveys the clashing of two different, incompatible supernatural powers.

### Walking-out

The Heart-Element ability to create a phantom body while one's true self is hidden and intangible at a different location.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow Daoists, strap in. This chapter is a violent, bloody, and brilliantly nasty piece of work. The action is breakneck, but the real treat is the body horror. Li Huowang’s survival strategy isn’t about a new cultivation technique or a secret treasure—it’s about learning to use his own parasitic passenger as a bizarre, new set of legs. The Black Tai Sui is no longer just a curse to suppress; it’s a survival tool, and the way it moves and reacts is wonderfully disgusting.

Keep an ear out for the voice in his head—that fragmented, questioning presence is getting louder. Is it the Black Tai Sui? Danyangzi? Or something else he’s picked up along the way? The confusion adds a lovely layer of paranoia to an already frantic fight.

And that final trick? The fake-out body? That’s Li Huowang’s Heart-Element ability, his “walking-out,” finally being used with deliberate, tactical precision. He’s not just running away anymore; he’s learning to fight dirty with the very thing that makes him a target. This is a turning point—he’s starting to weaponize his own broken reality.
