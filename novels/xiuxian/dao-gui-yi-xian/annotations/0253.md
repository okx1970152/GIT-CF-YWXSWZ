## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow seekers of twisted truths—this chapter is a breather, but don't let the quiet fool you. Li Huowang finally has a *plan*. After squeezing the Luojiao Nascent Soul dry through a long, bloody night, he's got a lead on how to tackle his Heart-Element curse: join the Supervisory Heavenly Office. It's one of those rare pause-and-think chapters where the horror isn't happening *to* him—it's being *engineered* by him. There's even a tender moment between him and Bai Lingmiao, which feels almost too warm for this world. But don't relax. A plan this good in the Dao-Twisted World? That's usually when the floor drops out.

## Key Plot Points
- The interrogation of the Luojiao Nascent Soul yields the Celestial Capital Seal technique, a method to wield the Seven Star Sword.
- Li Huowang confirms the Nascent Soul knows nothing else of value—no talismans, no other Luojiao arts. Just this single prize.
- The Nascent Soul had also dropped a bombshell hint: there is a Heart-Element named "North Wind" who escaped bewilderment, and the key to verifying this lies with the Zuowandao's archenemy, the Supervisory Heavenly Office.
- Li Huowang rejects the simple "just explain who you are" approach—he knows a massive bureaucracy like the Supervisory Heavenly Office doesn't play nice with strangers.
- Instead, he formulates a layered plan: hide his Heart-Element identity, infiltrate the Supervisory Heavenly Office, use their intelligence to track North Wind, and simultaneously gain a powerful institutional backer.
- The chapter ends with Bai Lingmiao gently coaxing a two-days-awake Li Huowang to sleep, a rare pocket of softness before the next storm.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces the *Celestial Capital Seal* (仙都兹摄印), a technique from the Luojiao Sect (罗教). Historically, the Luojiao—or Luo Sect—was a real Chinese folk religious movement founded by Luo Qing in the Ming Dynasty, blending Chan Buddhist, Daoist, and Pure Land elements into a syncretic salvation tradition. In the context of this novel, however, their techniques are pure dirty goods: dark, practical, and tied to the grimy underworld of rogue cultivators and shadowy organizations. Think of it as the "unlicensed sorcery" of the Dao-Twisted World.
Also worth noting: the idea of a Nascent Soul being *removed* from its original body and grafted onto another is a grotesque inversion of the standard xianxia trope. Normally, a Nascent Soul is the proud crystallization of a cultivator's life's work—here, it's a terrified, bargaining parasite that can be scooped out with a ladle.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is all about *strategy*—and for once, it's not just reactionary survival. Li Huowang's decision to infiltrate the Supervisory Heavenly Office shows he's finally learning to play the long game: hide, observe, gather intel, and only then strike. It's the kind of giddy, manic plotting we rarely see from a protagonist constantly on the back foot. But keep that ball of tension in your gut—every time Li Huowang gets *excited* about a plan, the universe seems to take it personally. Also, that quiet, warm moment with Bai Lingmiao? The more tenderness the Dao-Twisted World shows, the more likely it's saving up for a catastrophic payback. Savor the calm, but stay on your guard.

### The Supervisory Heavenly Office: Friend or Foe?
The Office is the novel's first major introduction of a *government-sanctioned* supernatural enforcer. Unlike the fragmented, secretive sects we've seen so far, this is institutional power with a political spine. The chapter hints that their methods are pragmatic to the point of ruthlessness—recruiting Luojiao disciples as disposable chess pieces is standard procedure. Li Huowang's instinct to approach them *not* as an ally but as a infiltrator is exactly the right call. In a world where every institution is rotten, the one that looks most "official" might just be the most dangerous of all.
