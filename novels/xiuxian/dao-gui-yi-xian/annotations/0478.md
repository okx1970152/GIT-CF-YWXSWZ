## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang plays a new, delicate game: he pretends to cooperate with an eager young doctor in the psychiatric ward, using online gaming small talk as both a shield and a potential escape route from constant surveillance. The moment he masters the timing of his own psychotic breaks, he slips back into the Dao-Twisted World, where the meal he eats is split between two realities—his mother feeding him in a hospital bed while he physically chews the braised rice in a small caravan camp. The chapter then pivots to a new investigation: a silent, resin-dripping peach grove that might hold the key to the elusive “Spiritual Scourge.” Zhuge Yuan’s voice returns, carrying a tragic memory of a friend who begged for death, as Li Huowang steps into the uncanny stillness.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Doctor’s Gambit**: Yi Donglai, a younger psychiatrist, tries to build rapport through *League of Legends* talk. Li Huowang sees through the tactic immediately but decides to play along—not as a patient, but as a strategist aiming to loosen his own leash.
2. **Controlled Psychotic Breaks**: Li Huowang demonstrates a chilling new ability: he can *choose* when to return to the Dao-Twisted World, using the hospital dinner as a predictable anchor point. This suggests a growing compartmentalization, not a loss of control.
3. **Double-Meal Scene**: The narrative fractures into two simultaneous feedings—Li Huowang’s mother spooning food into his mouth in the modern world, while his “real” body eats braised rice by the campfire. This is the novel’s most explicit visual of his split existence to date.
4. **Tactical Deception and Emotional Distance**: Li Huowang lies smoothly to Bai Lingmiao (“I can still tell them apart right now”) while knowing his situation is worsening. The trust in his group visibly frays as he avoids deeper explanations.
5. **The Silent Peach Grove**: A new terrain of horror—no birds, no insects, just sticky peach resin and the uncanny stillness of a forest that feels inherently hostile. Li Huowang enters invisible, testing his luck for the third time in his hunt for the Spiritual Scourge.
6. **Zhuge Yuan’s Burial Memory**: For the first time, Zhuge Yuan shares a personal tragedy: his close friend was claimed by a Spiritual Scourge, and he was forced to end his friend’s suffering. This revelation deepens the lore while foreboding Li Huowang’s own possible fate.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Online Gaming as a Psychological Bridge**: Yi Donglai’s use of *League of Legends* references is a specifically modern, clinical tactic rooted in “cultural bridging” therapy. By speaking Li Huowang’s generational language, he attempts to lower defenses. The mention of Tryndamere’s “ultimate” (invincibility) is an ironic parallel to Li Huowang’s own desperate survival mode—both a video game power-up and a real-life trauma response.

**Peach Groves in Chinese Folk Horror**: In Chinese ghostlore, peach wood is traditionally used to exorcise evil. A *silent* peach grove with sticky resin, however, inverts that protective symbolism—the absence of life implies the grove is not warding off spirits but *holding* something inside. The resin (桃胶, *taojiao*), normally a medicinal substance, here becomes a sign of sickness or devouring stillness.

**Spiritual Scourge (灵孽) as Karmic Parasite**: The term “灵孽” combines “spirit” and “calamity/offspring,” suggesting a curse that is both a supernatural entity and a karmic burden. Zhuge Yuan’s story of a friend beyond salvation echoes a recurring trope in xianxia: the binding of a demon so intimate it becomes the self, making death the only mercy.

## Reading Guide
Get ready to see Li Huowang at his most strategically detached. This chapter is a masterclass in compartmentalization—he can now smile at a doctor, chew rice in two bodies, and lie to his own companions with a straight face. The horror isn’t in the monsters but in how smoothly he’s learning to wear masks. Pay close attention to the double-feeding scene: it’s the novel’s most intimate visual of his split between worlds, and it’s *sad*, not triumphant. And that peach grove? The silence is a promise. Something is waiting inside, and Zhuge Yuan’s tragic confession is the first real hint that this hunt might not end with a cure—but with a mercy kill.
