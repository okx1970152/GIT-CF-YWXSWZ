## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, folks—this is the payoff. The Dice is pinned down, half his body already gone, surrounded by the most terrifying Military cultivators in Great Liang, yet somehow he’s the one holding all the cards. Grand Preceptor Huangfu Tiangang decides he wants a word before the kill, and Li Huowang knows that’s the worst possible move. Because giving a Zuowandao a chance to talk isn't an interrogation—it's inviting a master manipulator to set the table. And by the time the Dice is done, he's overturned the entire board.

## Key Plot Points

- Six veteran Military generals surround the Dice, their collective murderous aura so dense it causes hallucinations in Li Huowang. One scarred general is about to finish the Dice with a mace.
- Huangfu Tiangang stops the execution to question the Dice personally, ignoring Li Huowang’s urgent warnings about Zuowandao verbal trickery.
- The hallucination Zuowandao tries to sow doubt in Li Huowang’s mind, suggesting Huangfu Tiangang might be in cahoots with the Dice. Li Huowang dismisses him coldly, threatening lingchi.
- Li Huowang offers to lend Zhuge Yuan a sliver of his “Cultivate the False” technique for a surprise kill, but Zhuge Yuan advises patience, arguing it’s not worth exposing Li Huowang’s Heart-Element identity now.
- The Dice, despite being pinned, reveals he *wanted* to be watched—his role was always to be a decoy. The real strike was elsewhere.
- The Dice drops the bombshell: Ji Lin is actually a woman. Her manhood was secretly installed by the Dice, and she’s a stone-woman (石女), meaning the Great Liang Dragon Vein is effectively severed at her generation.
- Huangfu Tiangang counters that they prepared backup Dragon-line heirs among the commoners. The Dice replies cheerfully that those have all been eliminated—he even mentions the “round white jade pendant” as the identifier, a detail that strikes Li Huowang as disturbingly familiar.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter crystallizes the **Zuowandao philosophy of warfare**: never fight the enemy on their terms. The Dice doesn’t try to match the Military cultivators’ raw strength; he builds a grand scheme that exploits a single institutional weakness—the Dragon Vein’s reliance on male succession. The “stone-woman” (石女) condition, a real-world medical term for vaginal agenesis, is weaponized here as a dynastic curse. By secretly “installing” the appearance of a male heir, the Dice engineered a succession that was doomed from the start.

The **Dragon Vein** (龙脉) is a core concept in Chinese geomancy and political cosmology—it represents the subterranean energy channel that sustains a dynasty’s legitimacy. Tampering with it is equivalent to trying to rewrite history itself. But the Zuowandao’s approach, as the Dice boasts, is more surgical: if you can’t move the Dragon Vein, you destroy the vessel meant to hold it.

The **azurite jade pendant** (白玉圆形玉佩) mentioned by the Dice is a callback to a much earlier plot thread—it’s the same type of pendant Li Huowang used to test interworld transportation. The connection is chilling.

### Void Nian (虚年)

A corrupted Nian beast that drains yang life on contact; named to distinguish it from the traditional folkloric Nian.

### Mandate of Heaven (气运)

In this novel’s context, the Mandate is a stolen fragment of divine power that holds up reality; it can only be supported by a family willing to commit self-slaughter.

## Reading Guide

The Dice is a marvel of theatrical menace in this chapter. Even nailed down with half a body, he keeps winking, laughing, and orchestrating the scene like a conductor. The slow escalation of his revelation—from “I was a decoy” to “I was waiting for this moment” to “the emperor is actually a woman”—is masterfully paced. The chapter also recontextualizes the *entire* arc of Ji Lin’s ascension, transforming it from a political gambit into a dynastic suicide bomb. Pay close attention to Li Huowang’s reaction when the Dice describes the jade pendant—that flicker of recognition is a dead giveaway that he’s already tangled in this web deeper than he knows.
