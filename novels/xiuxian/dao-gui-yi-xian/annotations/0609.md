## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is an absolute *scream*. Li Huowang finally breaks the stalemate, but the victory comes at the exact cost of a punchline that recontextualizes the entire book. He goes from slaying a Siming’s Heart-Pan with a brutal visceral sacrifice to staring at a dying man whose face shatters everything he thought he knew. It’s a masterclass in how *Dao Gui Yi Xian* twists a triumph into a cold spike of dread. Buckle up.

## Key Plot Points

Li Huowang, having turned back “real” mid-battle, immediately massacres the five Heart-Element worshippers by reminding them of their missing heads—a grimly theatrical reversal of their own logic. He then engages the Yin-Yang Doumu Heart-Pan, sacrificing his internal organs to Bashe in an Intercalation of the Five Phases. The gambit works: he pins the Heart-Pan with the spine-sword. The kill triggers a sudden True-False Heavenly Way oscillation. Everyone briefly turns false, then true, then false again. The Heavenly Calamity concludes, signaling that Jizai has taken control of Doumu’s Heavenly Way. Peng Longteng and the monk flicker as illusions. The scene then snaps to the modern reality: a police helicopter hovers overhead. Li Huowang dismisses the consequences and turns to Xu Shou, the mastermind. He steals Xu’s sunglasses and delivers a chilling, dismissive “I don’t blame you.” But as he leans in, he recognizes Xu Shou’s face—it’s the exact face of Abbess Jingxin’s son, Xu’er, the limbless Heart-Element baby raised in her flesh. The chapter closes on Li Huowang’s entire worldview combusting.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Intercalation of the Five Phases (闰置五行):** A brutal forbidden ritual where the practitioner sacrifices their own five viscera (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys) in exchange for a short burst of overwhelming power. It’s a direct corruption of the Daoist Five Phases system (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water), which normally governs bodily health and cosmic harmony. Here, it’s weaponized by mutilation and pain.

**True-False Heavenly Way (真假天道):** One of the core ontological pillars of the *Dao Gui Yi Xian* universe. A Siming mastering a Heavenly Way means they control a fundamental law of reality. The oscillation Li Huowang experiences—everything turning “false” then “true”—is the direct consequence of a Siming battle happening at a level beyond mortal perception. The “Heavenly Calamity” (天灾) is essentially the symptom of this cosmic tug-of-war.

**Great Qi (大齐):** The name of the “Dao-Twisted World,” not just a random dynasty. In Chinese history, the Qi state was a real Warring States kingdom. Here, “sent to the Great Qi” functions as a euphemism for death—an ironic, world-internal way of saying “killed” by someone who can physically sever the connection between a body and its destination.

### Anci Nunnery

The dilapidated nunnery where Li Huowang first encountered Heart-Element cultivation and Abbess Jingxin. It is where he first saw the limbless Heart-Element baby, Xu'er.

### Bashe

The Siming of Pain, the god of the Ao-Jing Sect. Practitioners sacrifice their own flesh and senses to draw power from its domain.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the payoff for a *long*-running thread that many readers may have forgotten. Abbess Jingxin’s “son,” Xu’er—the grotesque, limbless Heart-Element creature kept inside her fat—was introduced way back in the Anci Nunnery arc. His description was so disturbing and so *specific* that seeing his face on the modern-world mastermind, Xu Shou, is a gut-punch of cosmic proportions. It retroactively redefines the entire Anci Nunnery setup as a prequel to the modern conspiracy. Pay close attention to the detail of Xu Shou’s rings and the “wrong number” setup from earlier arcs. Also note Li Huowang’s cold, tactical decision to pierce his own eardrums earlier in the fight, which isn’t shown here but is *implied* by his vulnerability to commands—it’s a testament to how brutally pragmatic he’s become.
