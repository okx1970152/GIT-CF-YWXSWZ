## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow wanderers—this chapter yanks the rug out from under you twice. One moment, Li Huowang is wrapped in a painfully tender reunion with Yang Na, tasting chocolate and almost believing in a future. The next, his stomach is a warzone, and the hallucination shatters like cheap glass. Chapter 123 is a masterclass in the novel’s core cruelty: the sweetest moments are just bait. The transition from fragile domestic calm to visceral, body-horror vomiting is brutal and immediate. This isn't just a scare—it’s a piece of storytelling that weaponizes your own hope against you.

## Key Plot Points

- Yang Na arrives at the hospital, distraught. A raw, emotional confrontation unfolds. She insists on her right to wait for him, rejecting his attempts to “set her free.”
- She gives him a bag of liqueur chocolates—a small, domestic gesture loaded with desperate affection. She force-feeds him one.
- Li Huowang sees his own scarred face for the first time in the hallucination. The moment is quietly devastating, grounding his identity crisis in a tangible, physical mark.
- Just as the conversation reaches a fragile truce, he starts coughing violently. The pain is real, *wrong*, and tears through the illusion.
- He realizes: something is poisoning him in the Dao-Twisted World, and the hallucination has been hijacked. His final accusation—“*Who are you?!*”—hangs in the air before the scene collapses.
- He wakes up back in the cave, vomiting blood. Mixed in with the bile are black tentacle fragments, rusted iron nails, and shredded flesh. The poison has been purged.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter offers a stunning example of a core narrative device in *Dao Gui Yi Xian*: the **two-world bleed**. The hallucination of the modern hospital is not a safe space; it is a permeable membrane. Li Huowang’s physical body in the cave can be poisoned, and that poison will manifest in the hospital reality as a sudden, inexplicable illness. This blurs the line between “real” and “fake” to the point of meaninglessness. The narrative forces the reader to ask not “Which world is real?” but “Which world is *killing him right now*?”

Also worth noting: the **liqueur chocolate** as a signifier of modern, mundane care. In Chinese web novels, a gift of chocolate often carries romantic or affectionate weight. Here, it becomes a Trojan horse—a vessel for a deadly substance that crosses between worlds. The sweetness is a trap, perfectly in line with the novel’s relentless paranoia.

### Cross-contamination

A narrative device unique to *Dao Gui Yi Xian* where events in one world physically manifest in the other. Poison in the cave becomes a sudden illness in the hospital.

### Two-world bleed

The mechanism by which the modern hallucination and the Dao-Twisted World overlap. Neither is fully real nor fully fake; they are mutually contaminating.

## Reading Guide

This chapter hits hardest if you let yourself feel the hope in the first half. Don’t brace yourself. Let Yang Na’s stubborn love feel real. Let the chocolate taste good. Because that’s exactly what Li Huowang is doing—and that’s why the betrayal (of his own body, of the hallucination) cuts so deep. Pay attention to the details of the vomit: little black tentacles, rusty nails. The novel is feeding you clues about what’s *actually* happening to his body in the cave, even as his mind is trying to escape to a sunlit ward. Also, that scar. It’s a permanent mark of his violence-soaked journey, and seeing it through Yang Na’s mirror is the first time *he* has to face the face he wears in her world.

## Deeper Dive: The Poetics of Cross-Contamination

One of the most sophisticated techniques the author employs here is the **emotional cross-contamination** between worlds. Yang Na’s love is real. The chocolate is real. Their shared history is real. And yet, the *poison* in that chocolate—or rather, the poison that uses the hallucinated chocolate as its delivery vector—is also real. This turns the modern world into a horror space not because of monsters, but because of intimacy itself. The things you love can be weaponized against you. The chapter quietly whispers a terrifying truth: *Li Huowang can no longer trust a single moment of comfort, because his own body has become a gateway for horror.*
