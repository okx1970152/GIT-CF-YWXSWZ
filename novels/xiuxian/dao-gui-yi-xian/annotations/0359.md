## Chapter Overview

Chaos and bloodshed erupt in an instant. Fresh from the revelations of his Heart-Element identity, Li Huowang’s paranoia flares to a murderous pitch, and he attempts to kill everyone in sight. But something is very wrong—the others don’t seem to see what he thinks they see. This chapter is a masterclass in cognitive dissonance: the protagonist, convinced he is exposed, discovers that everyone around him is acting on a different, far less lethal script. And at the center of it all sits a tiny, unassuming black spindle—a gift from Zhuge Yuan that rewrites everything Li Huowang thought he knew about his own vulnerability.

It’s a short, sharp entry, but it delivers a massive paradigm shift. Buckle up, fellow Daoists—the spy game just got way more interesting.

## Key Plot Points

- The chapter opens in all-out combat. A Heart-Turbid (心浊) man, identified as the beggar from earlier, vanishes after making eye contact with Li Huowang, and the copper coin mask is hidden away by his ability.
- Li Huowang, now fully convinced his Heart-Element status has been discovered by the Chief Recorder, Hong Da, and the others, sets himself on fire using the *Thousand Greats Record* and attacks with suicidal fury.
- Liu Zongyuan immediately flees through a window. A mysterious force flings Li Huowang backward. He uses a strange light to try to disable everyone.
- The Chief Recorder’s朽木如意 has no effect. Li Huowang is about to cut off his own arm when the White Wuchang’s voice booms from the woods, revealing that Li Huowang is under external control.
- The realization hits: they CAN’T see through his Heart-Element identity. Li Huowang suddenly remembers Zhuge Yuan’s cryptic words when giving him the black spindle—it was a better tool than the mask.
- He stuffs the burning spindle into his own abdominal cavity to keep it safe. The *Thousand Greats Record* is discarded, extinguishing the flames with its removal.
- Using the *True Sutra of the Fire Vestments* to heal himself, Li Huowang experiences a rare moment of warmth thinking of Zhuge Yuan’s genuine kindness.
- After the confrontation, a bizarre gap emerges: none of them can remember what errand the Chief Recorder had called them out for.
- The chapter ends with the group hurrying down the road, still uneasy and not fully trusting each other, but without a coherent mission.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Heart-Turbid (心浊) vs Heart-Element (心素) Dynamic**  
This chapter introduces a crucial counterpart: the Heart-Turbid. While the Heart-Element makes fictions real, the Heart-Turbid hides, negates, and erases things from perception or reality. The two are corrupted opposites—one adds, the other subtracts. Their abilities are absurd in their utility: one can steal your mask off your face without lifting a finger. It's a perfect playground for spycraft and paranoia.

**The Black Spindle (纺锤) and Zhuge Yuan’s Gift**  
Zhuge Yuan is quickly shaping up to be a pivotal figure. His spindle doesn't merely conceal; it actively substitutes for the copper coin mask’s function of hiding Li Huowang’s Heart-Element identity. But the implications are deeper: Zhuge Yuan seems to know exactly what Li Huowang is, and instead of using that knowledge for gain, he gave him a better tool. That’s a kind of trust that doesn’t exist in this world—and Li Huowang is just beginning to realize how rare it is.

## Reading Guide

This chapter pulls off a beautiful double-subversion. We, the readers, are just as paranoid as Li Huowang. When he thinks he’s been exposed, we believe him. When he screams about killing, we don’t question it. But then the twist hits: he’s wrong. No one sees what he thinks they see. His identity is *already hidden*—so well hidden that he forgot it was.

Pay close attention to the moment Li Huowang shoves the spindle into his own body. It’s visceral, desperate, and weirdly tender. He’s protecting a gift from the only person who ever showed him genuine goodwill without wanting something in return. In a world of lies, tortures, and parasites, that quiet moment of trust feels almost sacred.

Also: the Chief Recorder conveniently forgetting why he called them out. Either this man is the most scatterbrained eunuch in the Office, or something else is going on. Memory gaps in *Dao-Guai Yi-Xian* always mean more than they let on.
