## Chapter Overview

Get ready, dear readers—the game board just doubled in size, and Li Huowang is no longer a player; he’s a man trying to pull the escape cord before the whole planet explodes. In this chapter, the Zuowandao reveal their ultimate gambit: they never actually lost. That “capture” of North Wind was a Trojan horse, allowing a crack team of Great Qi Zuowandao—led by four dice-wielding clones—to be imported directly into the Great Liang court. Now it’s a full celestial brawl: the Great Liang emperor and his office of super-cops versus a foreign, fake-cultivating cabal, all beneath the watchful multicolored pupil of something far worse. And Li Huowang? He’s just a guy with a dagger, a self-inflicted divination wound, and a desperate need to kidnap the one person who can body-swap before the sky falls.

## Key Plot Points

- The captured North Wind is revealed to have been a deliberate plant; she transforms from an elderly woman into a living die, confirming that multiple “North Wind” avatars exist and that the Heart-Element one has the ability to body-swap.
- Hong Zhong’s hallucinatory projection gleefully reveals that by turning Zuowandao into Great Qi citizens through Zhuge Yuan, they have successfully summoned reinforcements from an entirely different empire—proving Great Qi is real, not a dream, which shakes Li Huowang’s worldview.
- The Supervisory Heavenly Office’s full power deploys: the National Preceptor summons starlight, and the Celestial Supervisor emerges in red robes with three Deputies, using triple-tiered hand seals and Daoist chants that conjure white lotus imagery to counter the Zuowandao’s “Sitting in Forgetfulness” chant.
- The “Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings” concept is expanded: Li Huowang realizes the strange chant in the sky isn’t a sutra but an incomprehensible force that can destabilize a person’s very soul, and he fears for Bai Lingmiao’s sanity after her earlier corruption.
- Amid the chaotic battle—lotus demons, dark lightning, collapsing golden tree—Li Huowang uses a reversed divination taliban (cutting into his own collarbone) to locate the Heart-Element North Wind, and despite her injuries, he drags her out of the warzone.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Mahjong Metaphor**: The Zuowandao’s “two tables of mahjong” isn’t just a snide taunt—it’s a perfect encapsulation of the novel’s twisted social structure. Mahjong is a game of four players, each trying to complete a hidden hand while disrupting others. By pulling in a *second* table from Great Qi, the Zuowandao double the chaos, making it impossible for the Celestial Supervisor to keep track of all threats. This also reinforces the “Great Liang vs. Great Qi” political layer, where two incompatible realms’ supernatural systems collide.

**Reverse Divination**: Li Huowang uses a divination taliban not to predict the future, but to *probe* for a powerful being’s location. The catch? For ordinary use, the target would sense you. But by stabbing it into his own exposed collarbone, he weaponizes the backlash—the Heart-Element North Wind feels the probe and instinctively reacts, revealing her position. It’s a classic “break the rules to survive” tactic, very in-line with the novel’s rejection of standard xianxia power-level logic.

**Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings**: This is a dark expansion of the Buddhist Eight Sufferings (birth, aging, sickness, death, meeting what we hate, being separated from what we love, not getting what we want, and the suffering of the five aggregates). In the Dao-Twisted World, the Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings are treated as fragile threads that hold a person’s identity together. If a force like the Giant Eye’s chant can rattle them, a person’s very self could unravel—mirroring Bai Lingmiao’s earlier personality shift from corrupted baleful aura.

### Flower Tiles (花牌)

Additional mahjong tiles in some regional sets, including plum, orchid, bamboo, chrysanthemum, god of wealth, cat, and mouse; the author uses them to imply different supernatural rules between Great Liang and Great Qi.

## Reading Guide

- **Pay attention to the “body-swap” rule introduced here.** The Heart-Element North Wind can apparently change faces and identities, which is why eight identical copies appeared. Li Huowang is literally dragging an unknown, shape-shifting hostage out of a celestial battlefield—he has no idea if the person he rescued is actually the one he needs.
- **The war isn’t over.** The toppling of the golden tree and the “dark lightning” suggest the battle is escalating, not ending. Li Huowang’s escape is a temporary retreat from a conflict that now spans two empires. Expect the Zuowandao’s “two-table” gambit to have long-lasting consequences.
- **Great Qi is real.** This is a massive lore drop: Li Huowang’s earlier suspicions are confirmed. The otherworldly realm Zhuge Yuan comes from is not a hallucination or an Upper Realm projection—it’s a separate, tangible empire with its own Zuowandao, its own rules, and presumably its own horrors. The novel just opened a door to an entirely new battlefield.
