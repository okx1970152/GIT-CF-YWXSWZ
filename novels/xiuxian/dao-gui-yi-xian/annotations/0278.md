## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a serious mood whiplash, folks. After the grotesque body-horror of the last few chapters, we finally get a *victory*. The ghost is dead, Pi County is saved, and the people are throwing a party big enough to wake the dead—which, given this world, is probably not a great idea. Li Huowang is treated as a hero. Banners wave, firecrackers pop, and everyone’s stuffing their faces at the finest restaurant in town. But of course, this is *Dao-Twisted World*, so the celebration is only skin-deep. While kids chase dragons and old men get drunk, there are still courtyards with green couplets and weeping families. And Li Huowang isn’t relaxing. He’s got one last piece of grim business to handle: a quiet visit to the mortuary, where a certain corpse isn’t quite as dead as advertised.

## Key Plot Points
- The ghost responsible for the shrinking deaths in Pi County is confirmed dead after Li Huowang’s exorcism. The body is paraded through the streets.
- The town’s collective terror evaporates overnight. Ordinary citizens celebrate by eating, drinking, setting off fireworks, and dancing in the streets. The oppressive atmosphere is replaced by loud, manic joy.
- Li Huowang and his group are treated as revered heroes. Lü Zhuangyuan takes the opportunity to lecture his son about studying hard so he can also enjoy grand feasts one day.
- Bai Lingmiao discreetly prevents Li Huowang from drinking alcohol, swapping it for shark fin soup. Man tou the dog gets a chicken leg.
- Li Huowang shows his cold pragmatism by instructing his group to keep an eye on the drunk magistrate so he doesn’t die of celebration.
- Amid the festivities, Li Huowang singles out the houses of the ghost’s victims—marked with green couplets and white funeral cloth—highlighting the raw grief that the celebration cannot erase.
- Li Huowang slips away to the county mortuary. He dismisses the guards under the pretense of performing a ritual to eliminate any residual ghostly influence.
- Alone with the corpse of the possessed beggar, Li Huowang watches as it opens its eyes. The man tear s off a talisman and greets him, revealing himself as a member of the **Supervisory Heavenly Office**.
- Li Huowang, using the alias “Erjiu” from the Ao-Jing Sect, successfully negotiates the introduction, having deduced the man’s true identity from a single glance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司, Jiān Tiān Sī)**: This is a big lore drop hidden in the last lines of the chapter. Think of the Jiansi as the supernatural equivalent of a shadowy imperial bureau—a government-sanctioned enforcement agency tasked with investigating and managing abnormal phenomena, rogue cultivators, and Heavenly Calamities. They operate under the imperial board of war, making them a kind of *Xianxia CIA* with the authority to sanction or hunt powerful spiritual entities. The fact that one of their agents was already embedded in the county, disguised as a victim, is a masterstroke: it implies the empire has a structured, proactive system for monitoring local supernatural outbreaks, not just reacting to them. This subtly lifts the novel’s scope from the survival of a small group to the high-stakes politicking of the entire Dao-Twisted World.

**County Gazetteer (县志)**: The gazetteer is the local history book—a record of every notable event, census, tax, and disaster that occurs in a county. If a local gazetteer was found blank or suspiciously clean, as hinted at earlier in the story, it becomes a red flag that someone powerful is silencing the truth about supernatural horrors. It’s the mundane horror of bureaucracy weaponized: the denial of reality on paper to maintain order and avoid panic.

**Green Couplets (绿对联)**: In Chinese funeral tradition, red couplets are the norm for auspicious occasions, while white or green couplets are reserved specifically for mourning and the death of a family member. When Li Huowang walks past courtyards with green couplets and white hanging cloth, it’s a silent, piercing reminder that the town’s victory came at a steep cost. This visual contrast—between manic celebration and quiet grief—is pure *Dao-Twisted* thematic blood play.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in tonal contrast—and it’s exactly why I love this novel. You get this huge cathartic release, this “happy ending” vibe where the people finally cheer and the hero gets his due. But the author never lets you settle. The moment you see the green couplets, you remember the cost. The moment the food hits the table, Li Huowang is already walking toward the cold dead bodies. And then, bam—the corpse opens its eyes and introduces a brand-new power player. The **Supervisory Heavenly Office** is a major new piece on the board, and Li Huowang, who’s been flying blind and scraping by on guts and luck, just got himself a live line into the empire’s occult intelligence. The best part? He didn’t stumble into it. He planned this conversation. He faked the exorcism of a “lingering ghost” to get a private audience with a dead man he suspected wasn’t dead. That’s some *cold*, strategic thinking. Also, note how Li Huowang uses the alias “Erjiu” from the Ao-Jing Sect. The boy is building a network of lies that spans multiple secret societies. Get ready—the info war just went national.
