## Chapter Overview
Get ready for the scheming phase, Daoist friends! Chapter 14 is where Li Huowang stops being a passenger in his own nightmare and starts grabbing the wheel—grudgingly, with a side of existential nausea. After being force-fed a pill that gives him a genuine power-up (stronger, sharper senses, the works), our boy has to deal with the moral hangover of *how* he got it: by becoming Danyangzi's errand boy in the human-ingredient trade. But wait—Bai Lingmiao, the soft-spoken albino girl we thought was just a victim, walks in with a backbone of steel and offers to join his rebellion. The two of them piece together crucial intel about the temple's surveillance system (a creepy entity called the Wandering Lord that takes days off on market day), and Li Huowang starts building a network of allies among the doomed ingredients. By the end, he's playing the long game—acting the devoted disciple, learning alchemy as a cover for poison-making, and quietly probing the temple's biggest mystery: if Danyangzi and all his disciples are illiterate, who the hell read him that Heavenly Scripture from Lord Lao?

## Key Plot Points
1. **The pill works** (too well): Li Huowang absorbs a supercharged alchemical pill, gaining increased strength, a more resilient body, and heightened senses—but the source of the power (a human-ingredient sacrifice) nearly chokes him with moral revulsion.
2. **Bai Lingmiao joins the resistance**: She finds Li Huowang after the event, absolves him of guilt ("we know your hands were tied"), and volunteers to help take down Danyangzi. Her quiet stubbornness suggests she's more than a damsel in distress.
3. **Crucial intel on the Wandering Lord**: Bai Lingmiao reveals that Danyangzi uses a summoned entity called the Wandering Lord to keep watch, but it's absent on market days (the 1st and 15th of each month). This gives the conspirators a window of opportunity.
4. **Li Huowang builds a network**: Tasking Bai Lingmiao with scouting the medicine shed for desperate allies, he begins to identify his potential co-conspirators among the human ingredients—people whose only way out is rebellion.
5. **Playing the obedient disciple**: Li Huowang throws himself into temple life, organizing the medicine shed and assisting Danyangzi in alchemy, earning trust while secretly learning how to turn medicines into poisons.
6. **The illiteracy puzzle deepens**: Close observation confirms Danyangzi is not only illiterate but *hostile* to literacy among his disciples. This creates a glaring plot hole: if he can't read the Heavenly Scripture Lord Lao supposedly gave him, who read it *to* him?

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Dantian (丹田) and Meridians (脉络):** In Daoist internal alchemy, the dantian is the body's energy center, located in the lower abdomen. When the pill's "fire" spreads from the dantian along the "meridians," that's standard qi circulation—but in *Dao-Twisted World*, that fire feels less like enlightenment and more like being slow-roasted from the inside. The body *does* get stronger; the cost is the knowledge of what was ground up to make that pill.

**Market Day (赶集) as a Lunar Calendar Institution:** In rural China, market days are traditionally held on fixed dates of the lunar month—often the 1st and 15th. These aren't just economic events; they're social and ritual anchors. The novel weaponizes this: a supernatural entity like the Wandering Lord literally has to clock out and attend market day, giving mortals a small but critical gap in surveillance. It's a delightfully folk-religious detail that turns a mundane rhythm into a survival mechanic.

**Alchemy Taboos (丹药忌讳):** Li Huowang discovers that basic medicinal pills have strict dosage limits—overdose equals poison, and mixing them is lethal. This reflects the real-world Daoist alchemical principle that medicine and poison are two sides of the same crucible. In the Dao-Twisted World, this adds a layer of grim pragmatism: the same alchemy that makes you stronger can kill you if you're careless, so what you learn can be used for healing *or* for murder.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in quiet setup. No big fight scenes, no flashy supernatural showdowns—just Li Huowang swallowing his disgust and turning into a political animal. The transition from victim to spymaster is handled beautifully: the power-up feels like a curse, the first ally walks in of her own accord, and the entire alliance is built on a piece of calendar trivia (market day) and a gap in a surveillance system. That's *good* plotting, my friends.

Keep an eye on Bai Lingmiao. Her stepping up to volunteer is a subtle but critical turning point—she's not asking for permission, she's offering a partnership. Li Huowang's internal conflict (getting stronger through atrocity, then immediately weaponizing that strength against the man who made him complicit) is exactly the kind of morally tangled fuel this novel runs on.

And don't sleep on that closing mystery: the illiterate Danyangzi claims to have received a Heavenly Scripture from Lord Lao, but he can't read it and forbids anyone else from learning. So who interpreted it for him? Or is the whole "heavenly mandate" built on a secondhand lie? That question hangs over the chapter like a blade, and knowing this novel, the answer isn't going to be pretty.
