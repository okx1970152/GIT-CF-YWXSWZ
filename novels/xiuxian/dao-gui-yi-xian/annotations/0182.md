## Chapter Overview
We’re back on the supernatural conundrum train, and this chapter delivers a double whammy of existential dread and folk ritual weirdness. Li Huowang learns from a blind Rice Medium that he’s “missing all Five Phases”—a diagnosis that should be a death sentence, yet he’s standing there, breathing and confused. The chapter dives straight into a divination ritual called *Interrogating the Rice*, where a spirit is summoned into the medium’s body to answer questions. But when the entity that shows up casually identifies Li Huowang as a Heart-Element—and does so with theatrical mockery—the tension spikes hard. This isn’t just a diagnostic session; it’s another reminder that in the Dao-Twisted World, knowing what you are makes you prey.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang’s physical examination by Auntie Cui, a blind Rice Medium, reveals he is “missing all Five Phases.”
- His attempt to recall how his body healed after using Intercalating the Five Phases on the *Thousand Greats Record* yields nothing—no scar, no pain, just a blank memory.
- When he questions whether a person can live without organs and Phases, the medium’s casual confirmation triggers a sudden, agonizing pain that nearly kills him, only to vanish just as abruptly when she hedges her answer.
- The medium performs the “Interrogating the Rice” ritual: bronze mirror, clay tripod, incense, raw eggs, and offerings of wine, tea, and rice.
- A spirit possessing the medium identifies itself with an absurdly grand title: The Golden Que, Nine Vaults, Past Calendars, Han-True, Vaulted High, Sin-Absolving Great Heavenly Venerable—and immediately calls Li Huowang a Heart-Element with a mocking laugh.
- Li Huowang’s sword and killing intent snuff out the incense, ending the ritual abruptly.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**“问米” (Wèn Mǐ / Interrogating the Rice)** is a traditional Chinese folk divination practice, especially common in southern China and among diaspora communities. The medium uses raw rice as a medium to contact spirits or deities. The setup here—bronze mirror, clay incense burner, raw eggs, and offerings—are all classic props from Cantonese and Hakka folk spirit-medium culture. The entity that speaks through the medium often arrives with a theatrical “hiccup” (嗝), which signals a shift in possession and is a staple marker in these rituals.

**五行缺五行 (Missing all Five Phases)** is an extreme variation on the traditional Chinese medical and cosmological concept of the Five Phases (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water). A person is normally born with a balance of all five; to be missing all of them is cosmologically impossible. In the Dao-Twisted World, however, Li Huowang’s use of the *Thousand Greats Record* ritual has placed him outside the normal framework—making him an ontological anomaly, not just a sick person.

**The Sin-Absolving Great Heavenly Venerable** (金阙九穹御历含真穹高赦罪大天尊) is a deliberately over-the-top title, mixing authentic Daoist celestial epithets with invented grandeur. In folk mediumship, spirits often give themselves pompous names to intimidate or impress their audience. The fact that it immediately identifies Li Huowang as a Heart-Element suggests either high-level supernatural awareness or a connection to the broader divine hierarchy that recognizes his nature.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in “information as threat.” The Rice Medium isn’t trying to hurt Li Huowang—she’s a service provider—but the knowledge she uncovers is dangerous simply because it exists. The fact that a random village medium can trigger a cosmic-level entity that recognizes the Heart-Element is a chilling reminder of how high the stakes are. Li Huowang’s body is no longer just his body; it’s a beacon for things that want to eat, use, or mock him. Watch how the chapter uses physical pain as a diagnostic tool—the pain comes and goes with words, not actions, reinforcing the idea that in this world, reality is shaped by what is spoken. And that final sword draw? It’s a reflex now. A good one.

## Flavor Text
“Missing all Five Phases” sounds like a glitch in a shitty RPG character sheet, but in the Dao-Twisted World, it’s a cosmic death sentence that somehow hasn’t been processed yet. And the entity that shows up is less a divine answer-giver and more a smug celestial heckler who shows up to roast the protagonist to his face before getting kicked out by sword aura. Peak folk-horror comedy.
