## Chapter Overview

Forget subtle clues and creeping dread—Li Huowang has a fix on his target and he’s going after it like a man possessed (which, fair warning, he very much might be). This chapter is a straight shot of focused, tactical horror: our limping protagonist uses his demon dog as a bloodhound, tracks down the elusive Black Tai Sui, and executes a messy, high-stakes capture in the dark. But the Dao-Twisted World never gives gifts without a price. A new predator—the “flesh hook”—announces itself, and Li Huowang’s solution to the capture problem requires him to peel his own chest open and use the skin as a living net. It’s brutal, it’s visceral, and it ends with a laugh that might be triumph, or might be the sound of something inside him finally breaking.

## Key Plot Points

- Mantou catches the scent of the Black Tai Sui and leads the group deeper into the darkness. The monk-hallucination interprets the dog’s barks for Li Huowang, earning a harsh rebuke that sends a visible shudder through the rest of the party.
- An unknown entity attacks Li Huowang from the dark—a “flesh hook” that Gao Zhijian barely deflects with his wolf-tooth club. It vanishes before anyone gets a good look.
- The group advances in a tight, defensive formation, step by step, watching the ceiling for the earlier lion-dance threat. The silence between threats is nearly as oppressive as the ambushes.
- They locate the Black Tai Sui: a larger specimen than the Qingfeng Temple’s, writhing with translucent tentacles in a patch of green light. The capture plan is set in motion with chains and a delicate balance of force.
- The plan nearly fails. The tentacles regenerate faster than Li Huowang anticipated, and a new, dissonant sound approaches from the side—*wham-shing*.
- Out of time and options, Li Huowang deploys the *Benevolent Mother’s Heart-Fasting*: a ritual from the Thousand Greats Record that turns a large patch of his own chest-skin into a living, expanding net that completely immobilizes the Black Tai Sui.
- The group escapes with their prize. As Bai Lingmiao tends to his wound, Li Huowang laughs through the pain—he has the key to locking away the hallucination for good.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Black Tai Sui (黑太岁/Black Tai Sui):**
In Chinese folk religion, *Tai Sui* (太岁) refers to the stellar deity of Jupiter, a powerful and often malevolent force that governs fortune in a given year. “Offending Tai Sui” is a common idiom for asking for trouble. The novel takes this and inverts it into a tangible, parasitic entity—a writhing mass of black flesh and tentacles that functions as an alchemical ingredient for warding off madness. Li Huowang is hunting it not for power, but as a grim medical prescription.

**The Benevolent Mother’s Heart-Fasting (仁母心斋):**
This is one of the ritual techniques from the *Thousand Greats Record* (大千录), the Ao-Jing Sect’s scripture of suffering for power. Its name uses sacred-sounding Buddhist language (“Benevolent Mother,” “Heart-Fasting” or mental purification), but its execution is pure body horror: the practitioner literally tears off his own skin and flesh, which then becomes a self-propelled, supernatural binding tool. This is the novel’s core aesthetic in a nutshell—holy names and concepts describing acts of visceral self-mutilation.

**Flesh Hook (肉钩子):**
A new predator introduced in this chapter, glimpsed only as a fast-moving silhouette. The name suggests something from a butcher’s shop repurposed into a living weapon, continuing the novel’s pattern of turning mundane, grotesque objects into animate horrors.

## Reading Guide

Alright, *fellow travelers*, take a breath. This chapter is lean, mean, and gives us exactly what we’ve been waiting for: the hunt and capture of the Black Tai Sui. Notice how Li Huowang’s mindset has shifted—he’s not panicking, he’s *calculating*. Even in the middle of a chaotic battle with a regenerating blob monster, he’s scanning for new threats, adjusting plans on the fly, and making the kind of brutal, self-sacrificing call that has become his signature. The laugh at the end isn’t madness (well, not *just* madness); it’s the laugh of a man who has paid the price and finally gotten his hands on the cure. But with that new unknown sound—*wham-shing*—retreating into the dark, we’re left with the uncomfortable feeling that this world’s debt collectors don’t stay quiet for long.

### The Horror of the “Safe Zone”
One of the most effective tricks in this chapter is the stretch of silence after the flesh hook’s attack. The group waits, moves slowly, and nothing happens. But the reader’s tension doesn’t drop—it curdles. This is a hallmark of the Dao-Twisted World’s horror: even the absence of threat becomes a pressure cooker. The darkness itself starts to feel like a held breath.

### Why the Skin-Sacrifice Works
Li Huowang’s choice to use the *Benevolent Mother’s Heart-Fasting* is a masterclass in *world-logic* problem-solving. He can’t kill the Black Tai Sui, so he needs a method of restraint that is *alive* and *aggressive*. His own flesh, powered by the Record, is both of those things. It’s a terrible price, but it’s a *smart* terrible price. That’s what makes Li Huowang compelling: he doesn’t just suffer, he suffers *efficiently*.
