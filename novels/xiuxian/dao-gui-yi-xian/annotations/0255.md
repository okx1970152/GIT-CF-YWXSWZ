## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, weary travelers! After a harrowing month beneath the earth, this chapter is a breath of fresh—and very, very hot—air. The gang has been on the road for over a month, finally leaving the twisted lands of Qingqiu behind and heading for the relative civilization of Liang. But don't let the pastoral vibes fool you. Li Huowang is still the scarred, paranoid leader we've come to know, sitting on a pile of new, untested power. The chapter is a study in contrasts: the simple joy of a roast sheep versus the dark, calculating mind of a man who has seen too much and trusts too little.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Journey's End (Almost):** After a blissfully uneventful month-long journey, the group is on the verge of crossing the Qingqiu-Liang border. The change in terrain and climate marks a symbolic transition into a new, unknown phase of their survival.
- **The Forbidden Scroll:** Li Huowang has a new prize: a "Seal of the Immortal Capital" cultivation method. But he's too smart to touch it. He knows all too well how a doctored scripture can be a death sentence, remembering his own trick against Danyangzi at Qingfeng Temple.
- **The Unwitting Test Subject:** Needing a guinea pig for the potentially cursed scroll, Li Huowang dismisses the eager Yang Xiaohai. His cold, pragmatic calculus points toward finding an outsider—someone whose loss wouldn't wound the group.
- **A Mother's Gratitude:** A gold box filled with ingots—Sun Baolu's mother's dowry—is found stashed in their tent. A silent, heavy payment for Li Huowang restoring her son's body. He accepts it as pragmatic travel funds.
- **Lost in the Map:** As they approach Liang, Li Huowang tries to pinpoint Bai Lingmiao's home, Ox-Heart Mountain. The crude, hand-drawn map proves to be nearly useless, highlighting the group's need for more reliable information.
- **A Feathered Finale:** The chapter ends on a surprisingly warm note. Li Huowang orders a sheep to be slaughtered for a celebratory feast. We see the group's social dynamics play out: Gouwa's flirtations, Yang Xiaohai's stubbornness, and Zhao Wu's quiet, envious longing for a life that his paralyzed body prevents him from having.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Sanfu (三伏天):** The reference to "sanfu days" is a major clue to the setting's weirdness. These are the hottest days of summer according to the Chinese calendar, a three-stage period that usually ends in August. The fact that it's getting *hotter* after they've passed is a subtle, atmospheric signal that something is still off-kilter with the world’s natural order, long after the main disasters.
- **"Original Soup for the Original Ingredients" (原汤化原食):** Gouwa's joke isn't just a punchline. It's a genuine piece of Chinese folk dietary wisdom. The belief is that the liquid in which food is boiled contains the very essence of that food, making it the best digestif. Subsisting on a diet of mutton and then burning sheep dung to cook said mutton is his crude, hilarious interpretation of this concept.

## Reading Guide
This is a "breather" chapter, but in the *Dao-Twisted World*, even a breather is loaded. The real focus here isn't the action, but Li Huowang's evolution. He's no longer the frantic victim desperately seeking escape. He is now a cautious, measured leader, managing resources, vetting unknown variables (that cultivation scroll), and making politically shrewd decisions (keeping his own people safe from the scroll's potential trap). His paranoia is now a strategic asset. Notice how the social hierarchy is reaffirmed through the simple act of eating. The meat is distributed, the choicest bits are gifted as courtship offerings, and the disabled Zhao Wu is left to silently observe the world of the strong and mobile from the edge of the firelight. It's a quiet, masterful piece of character work that tells you everything about the group's new order.
