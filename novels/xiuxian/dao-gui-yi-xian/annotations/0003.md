## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang's discovery that objects from his hallucinatory world can cross into modern reality shifts his entire perspective. He decides to keep this a secret and treat the hallucinatory world as a resource to exploit. In the cave, he steals Xuan Yang's jade pendant as the next test object, only to stumble upon a group of acolytes—including Xuan Yang—attempting a mass escape. The chapter marks Li Huowang's transition from bewilderment to opportunistic excitement, though he still struggles to reconcile the two realities.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang confirms that the hallucinated candy has become physically real in the hospital, which electrifies him with a sense of opportunity. He resolves to keep this discovery secret from Dr. Li and to experiment further.
- Back in the cave, he demonstrates growing confidence: he jovially rubs a disciple's head and ignores Xuan Yang's provocation, focusing instead on the jade pendant as a potential transport object.
- That night, Li Huowang successfully steals the jade pendant. His internal monologue reveals dreams of financial freedom for himself and Yang Na.
- On his way back, he unexpectedly runs into Xuan Yang and several other acolytes attempting to flee the sect, dressed in hemp robes as common laborers. Xuan Yang silences him before he can say the word 'escape.'

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Jade Pendants (玉佩)** — In traditional Chinese culture, jade pendants were not merely ornaments. They symbolized virtue, status, and often served as talismans believed to ward off evil. The cloud pattern carved on Xuan Yang's pendant (云纹) is a classic auspicious motif representing heaven and good fortune. Li Huowang's plan to sell it for tuition highlights how the novel juxtaposes material longing with the escalating supernatural horror.

**Fluorescent Stones (荧石)** — Fluorite or "night-shining stone" was sometimes used in traditional mining contexts for illumination. Its mention here adds a gritty, low-tech realism to the cave environment, reinforcing that this hallucinatory world operates by its own crude material rules—not magic or qi.

**Layering of Realities** — This chapter firmly establishes a core mechanic: objects from the Dao-Twisted World can physically cross into Modern Reality when Li Huowang presses them against his chest. This bidirectional leakage blurs the boundary between the two realms and suggests that the hallucinatory world is more than a mere delusion.

## Reading Guide

Pay careful attention to Li Huowang's internal voice in this chapter. He oscillates between manic glee (imagining wealth) and tense calculation (planning how to steal the pendant). This aligns with the novel's theme of a protagonist who gradually learns to leverage his condition rather than suffer it passively. The escape plot initiated by Xuan Yang introduces a new layer of social dynamics: even inside the hallucination, not everyone is content to be a passive ingredient. Li Huowang's encounter at the end forces him to choose between revealing his secret (that he can leave anytime) or joining the escape. Note also the subtle character detail that the albino girl (Bai Lingmiao) shows concern for him, echoing his earlier kindness.
