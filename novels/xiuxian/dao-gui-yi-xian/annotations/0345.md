## Chapter Overview

It’s a psychological knife-fight in a bridge tunnel, and Li Huowang almost loses. After Wang Zhilong spills everything—the credit card fraud, the stolen psych patient, the failed gold-extraction scheme—Li Huowang finds himself trapped between crashing tides of doubt: what if this modern nightmare is *real* after all? What if his mother and Yang Na never abandoned him? The pull of that hope is almost strong enough to make him run back into the sunlight. But when he looks down at his blood-soaked hands, still wrapped in a gold chain he almost used as a garrote, he realizes he is no longer the high schooler who fell into this madness. He has become something else—someone who kills without blinking. And that recognition is far more terrifying than any monster.

## Key Plot Points

- **The interrogation breaks:** Wang Zhilong, still pinned, confesses the entire con. He and his crew racked up credit card debt to “save” Li Huowang from the hospital, bribed officials to avoid harassment, and got exactly zero gold in return.
- **Li Huowang’s reality-check:** He stabs himself to test the pain’s veracity, nearly runs for the daylight, then stops cold. The logic he holds onto is brutal but necessary: even if this world is real, his presence would only poison his family further. He chooses the hallucination.
- **The near-murder:** Wang Zhilong, foolishly greedy, pushes one request too many for gold. Li Huowang wraps the gold chain around his throat and pulls. The victim’s face cycles through red, purple, and nearly black before Li Huowang, struck by the memory of killing Xin Chi, lets go in horror.
- **The reflection:** Gazing at his own image in the river, Li Huowang sees a stranger—a ragged, feral creature whose past life feels like a previous incarnation. He recognizes he is being assimilated by the Dao-Twisted World, becoming just as indifferent to life as the Office agents.
- **Return and comfort:** Back in the inn room, Li Huowang coaxes Li Sui out from under the bed, cradles it, and swallows it back down, restoring their symbiotic bond. The chapter ends with him healing Old Wu (who spied on him) with a painful fire-slug, paying for the damaged furniture, and declaring, “I don’t want to be assimilated by this crappy place.”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The self-inflicted wound as reality test:** Li Huowang stabbing himself to feel pain is a grim echo of real psychiatric grounding techniques—methods some patients use to anchor themselves during derealization episodes. In a xianxia context, it also nods to the ancient Chinese principle of “testing the real through the body” (以身证道), where physical experience becomes the ultimate validator of truth.
- **“Don’t want to be assimilated”:** This line is crucial for understanding the novel’s meta-structure. The Dao-Twisted World is not just a backdrop—it is an *active, corrupting force* that rewrites the protagonist’s values. Li Huowang’s refusal to accept silver as “hand-washing money” and his insistence on paying for damages is a moral ritual: a small act of decency performed to push back against the world’s gravitational pull toward cruelty.
- **The gold necklace as murder weapon:** Gold jewelry in xianxia contexts often symbolizes social status, contract (betrothal gifts), or purity. Here, it is literally repurposed as a strangling tool—a stark deconstruction of its symbolic weight, showing that Li Huowang’s world has stripped such objects of their original meaning and remade them as instruments of survival.

### Assimilation (同化)

In the Dao-Twisted World, prolonged exposure to its environment rewrites a person’s values, erasing prior moral frameworks and replacing them with the world’s brutal logic. Li Huowang fears becoming just as indifferent to life as the Office agents.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in what I’d call *self-aware horror*. Li Huowang is not just afraid of monsters—he is afraid of *becoming* one. The moment he nearly kills Wang Zhilong, he doesn’t think “good, that vermin deserved it.” He thinks, “Why did that feel familiar?” That’s the real gut-punch. He recognizes the posture, the rhythm, the cold satisfaction—they are all echoes of Danyangzi. And they are *his* now. The chapter’s emotional climax isn’t the fight; it’s the quiet moment by the riverbank, where he sees a beggar in the water and cannot recognize himself. Also, pay attention to the micro-reconciliation: how he gathers Li Sui back into his body. This is the closest thing to a “romantic reunion” this novel has to offer. And it is *visceral*.

## Why “Fake” Is a Lifeline

For those new to the *Dao-Twisted* psyche: Li Huowang’s mantra “this is all fake” is not a delusion—it is a *survival tool*. By labeling the modern-world hallucination “fake,” he gives himself permission to turn away from the desperate hope that his family is out there looking for him. It’s a terrible, fragile lie, but it keeps him from collapsing. This chapter shows how that tool nearly broke, and why he needs it to stay intact.
