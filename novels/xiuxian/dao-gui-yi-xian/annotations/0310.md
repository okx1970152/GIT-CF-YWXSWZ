## Chapter Overview

Brace yourselves, folks. This chapter is a masterclass in emotional whiplash. We start in Li Huowang’s head—a triumphant, inspiring fantasy where he rallies his broken little family and promises to build a sect from scratch. It’s the dream of every underdog story. But then the rain comes, and the fantasy dissolves like paper skin in a storm. We cut to reality: a hollow, exhausted Li Huowang standing alone in the mud, having stood vigil for an entire day. The Bai family is gone. He knows what happened. And now, he has to face the only person he has left: Bai Lingmiao. Get ready for a chapter that doesn’t hit you with a sword—it hits you with a choice.

## Key Plot Points

1. **The Fantasy: A Leader is Born** — Li Huowang daydreams a grand speech in front of his martial siblings. He declares Ox-Heart Village their new home, promises to teach them to read, to find them cultivation methods, and to build a sect from scratch. For a few paragraphs, it feels like a classic turning point.
2. **The Reality: A Broken Return** — The rain washes the fantasy away. Li Huowang is alone, wearing paper clothes, his skin melting. He has been standing at the village entrance for a day and a night. He is not a triumphant leader; he is a man carrying a corpse’s weight.
3. **The Confrontation: The Lie and the Love** — Bai Lingmiao tends to his wounds with heartbreaking gentleness. She tells him she’s accepted the loss, that they can just wait. But Li Huowang knows the truth. Before he can speak it, Hong Zhong—the Zuowandao trickster—whispers a devastating piece of advice: lie to her.
4. **The Final Decision** — The chapter ends on a knife’s edge. Li Huowang must choose between the brutal truth that will destroy Bai Lingmiao or a compassionate lie that preserves her hope.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“Fellow Martial Brothers” (师兄弟)** — This isn’t just a casual term. In Chinese sect culture (zongmen), calling someone “martial brother” implies a bond stronger than blood in some cases. It creates a parallel family structure. In Dao-Twisted World, where Li Huowang has lost his biological family, these bonds are his only anchor—making the fantasy of building a sect all the more poignant, and the reality of lying to his “family” all the more tragic.

**Paper Skin and Paper Clothes (纸衣)** — Throughout the novel, Li Huowang’s “paper skin” is a visceral reminder of his Heart-Element nature. In Chinese folk religion, paper effigies (纸扎) are burned for the dead. Li Huowang’s body is literally becoming what is meant for the afterlife—a grotesque, material proof that he is no longer fully alive, caught between worlds.

**The Lie vs. The Truth** — This chapter is a direct echo of one of the novel’s core themes: *Cultivate the False into Reality*. Hong Zhong’s advice is a perfect extension of Zuowandao philosophy—the lie is more “real” in its effect than the truth, because it preserves a working, functioning relationship. Li Huowang is being asked to practice the very corruption he hates.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a gut-punch because it’s not about monsters or magic—it’s about a choice. Li Huowang knows the Bai family is dead. Bai Lingmiao doesn’t. He has the power to break her or to preserve a fiction. Hong Zhong, for all his vile trickery, makes a terrifyingly logical argument. The horror here isn’t supernatural; it’s moral. It’s watching a man have to decide whether to become the kind of person who lies to the person he loves to save her from pain. This is the quiet kind of horror that stays with you long after the chapter ends.
