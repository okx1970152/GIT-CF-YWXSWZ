## Chapter Overview
Our boy Li Huowang has gone full murder-blender, and let me tell you, the meat grinder doesn't stop until the last foot is severed. After the cliffhanger of the double-lion dance ambush, this chapter delivers a visceral, teeth-grindingly brutal combat sequence that showcases just how far Li Huowang has evolved from a confused victim into a cold, tactical predator. But beneath all the gore and self-mutilation is a detective’s mind at work—Li Huowang isn't just killing; he's *reading* the corpses, looking for a missing concept called "death." It’s a gruesome puzzle box wrapped in a screaming fight scene.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang engages the second lion dance head-on while the rest of the group handles the other one. He uses his phantom-body switch to completely negate its charge, then counters with his signature self-mutilation attacks—slicing off two fingers and pulling out molars to use as projectile weapons.
- The lion dance reveals its true face: a giant, male face with dozens of eyes crammed into one eye socket, and dozens of bare human legs churning beneath the lion skin. It’s not a costume; it’s a single, nightmarish organism.
- Li Huowang kills both lion dances. The real horror comes from the autopsy: the lion is a single body—the hands holding it up and the feet moving it are all part of one twisted creature.
- Sun Baolu’s worldview shatters. The lion dances he grew up with were never a performance; they were always monsters wearing a friendly skin.
- Li Huowang searches the corpses for something specific: the concept of "death." The lion dance didn’t *die* using death as a power source, which contradicts the sacrificial pattern he observed from the giant cauldron. He suspects a *third* cult is buried beneath the Qingqiu plains.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Lion Dance & Its Dark Inversion:** A traditional Chinese lion dance is a celebratory, auspicious performance meant to ward off evil spirits. It’s performed by two people inside a colorful costume, mimicking a lion’s movements to drums and gongs. In the Dao-Twisted World, this wholesome tradition is inverted into a living, breathing flesh-monster, where the "dancers" aren't people but limbs fused into a single, grotesque entity. This is a classic *Dao Gui Yi Xian* trick: taking a safe, familiar folk practice and peeling back its skin (sometimes literally) to reveal the screaming rot underneath.
**The "Ding" & Sacrificial Systems:** Li Huowang references the giant cauldron (*Ding*, 鼎) he saw earlier. In ancient China, bronze *Ding* were ritual vessels used for offering sacrifices to gods or ancestors—they were symbols of state power and religious authority. In this novel, each major faction seems to have a "signature" sacrificial offering: flesh (Zhengde Temple), decay (Anci Nunnery), and now Li Huowang is looking for "death." The lion dance’s failure to match the *Ding*’s pattern tells him that the city of Qingqiu has more than one horrifying cult lurking beneath its surface.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in *visceral deduction*. The fight isn’t just an action set-piece; it’s Li Huowang gathering data with every swing of his sword. Pay close attention to how he doesn't just kill the lion—he dissects it. He's looking for a metaphysical signature, a *rule* that ties the lion back to the sacrificial model he already knows. The fact that he doesn't find it is the real cliffhanger. Also, soak in that little moment where Li Huowang realizes he's not even surprised anymore. That’s the quiet horror of this chapter: the protagonist has become so desensitized to body horror and reality-warping monsters that gore is just… another data point. That’s not growth. That’s damage.
