## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a tonal whiplash: one moment you’re in a grim grave-robber’s powwow over a cursed corpse-skin artifact, the next you’re watching a blind fortune-teller turn a whole gang into a puddle of red goo. Welcome to Chapter 400, where our token “villain” Lu Xiucai has his brush with the truly unnatural—and where Blind Chen makes a comeback that redefines the term “rescue.” The chapter is lean, mean, and introduces a major philosophical plot bombshell that will reframe everything you think you know about the Dao-Twisted World.

## Key Plot Points

- Zhao Tianhu’s crew debates whether to abandon the “ghostly skin” after losing two men. The short, stocky henchman suggests contacting the Supervisory Heavenly Office, earning a slap and a reminder that they’re grave-robbers.
- Zhao Tianhu reluctantly orders the house sealed until dawn, intending to cut losses. But Lu Xiucai’s continued survival changes his mind.
- Inside the room, Lu Xiucai is chased by a monster with an unnaturally long neck and limbs. It wears a stitched-together “human skin” of multiple monk faces on its chest.
- Lu Xiucai discovers the creature is repelled by his copper coins and tries to retreat to the window, nearly dying when the head lunges.
- A beam of light from outside melts the monster’s head. Blind Chen (the beggar-fortune teller from earlier chapters) nails the loose skin with his banner pole.
- With a flourish, Blind Chen steps to the window, assumes a horse stance, and aims his Bagua mirror. He chants a spell and flashes the light outward.
- The beam reduces Zhao Tianhu, Kui Sanjin, and the entire crew outside to a pool of red slime. Blind Chen calmly retrieves the skin and “rewards” the scene with zero hesitation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Blind Chen’s Return & the Demotion of the “Healer”**
Blind Chen makes his second appearance—and this time he’s not just a beggar-fortune teller; he’s a full-blown ritualist. His banner reads “看相，测字，算命” (physiognomy, character analysis, fortune-telling), marking him as a cheap street diviner. Yet he wields a genuine Bagua mirror with lethal results. In xianxia terms, this signals that “institutional” power (monk lineages) is terrifying, but wild, itinerant practitioners are just as dangerous—and far less predictable.

**The Monk-Skin Artifact (人皮子)**
The “human skin” is a mix of sewn-together monk scalps with ritual *jieba* scars (戒疤) and fake Sanskrit script. This echoes folklore where relics of executed “heretics” or dead monks can become possessed items. The fact that the wearer can stretch his neck and limbs like a snake implies the skin grants supernatural mobility—a classic property of corpse-puppet artifacts in Chinese folk horror.

**Bagua Mirror & Vengeful Ritual**
Blind Chen’s Bagua mirror (八卦镜) is a standard Taoist exorcism tool, typically used to repel evil spirits. In this chapter, he weaponizes it as an offensive AOE—turning people into sludge. This dramatizes the principle that any ritual tool, in the hands of a user who has “seen the true path” (or in Blind Chen’s case, perhaps something darker), can become a weapon of mass destruction.

### Cultivate the True/False (修真/修假)

A central philosophical dichotomy in the Dao-Twisted World, where the generic xianxia concept of "seeking truth" is literalized and opposed by the Zuowandao's power of "cultivating falsehood into reality."

## Reading Guide

This chapter is short but dense with flavor for long-time readers. First, it gives Lu Xiucai his first real encounter with supernatural horror—and he survives, which plants a seed of competence (and future danger). Second, Blind Chen’s sudden lethal precision confirms that the world is not neatly divided into “monk villains” and “beggar saints.” Everyone with a grudge and a ritual item is a potential apocalypse. The biggest shock, however, is the philosophical punchline that sneaks in at the end: the “Cultivate the True vs Cultivate the False” dichotomy. This is the Zuowandao’s core weapon—a claim that their power comes from believing lies into reality. It recontextualizes every single deception, every curse, and every strange rule Li Huowang has faced. This isn’t just a fight between good and evil; it’s a fight between what is real and what you can *make* real through sheer stubborn faith.
