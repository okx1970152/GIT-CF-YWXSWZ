## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, strap in—this is a classic interlude chapter where the gears of our protagonist’s brain turn faster than his feet. Li Huowang has been fruitlessly scouring a county town for any trace of the Jian Tian Si, only to hit wall after wall. Just when the frustration is about to curdle into despair, a flash of tactical brilliance strikes: stop hunting the hunters, and start hunting the *trouble* they’ll inevitably show up to clean. Meanwhile, the daily life of the troupe chugs along—sheep sales, grumpy patriarchs, and a puzzlingly timed breakthrough from a newly minted disciple. It’s quiet, it’s tense, and it’s setting the board for something big.

## Key Plot Points
Li Huowang’s investigation into the Jian Tian Si yields zero results after a full day of legwork, forcing him to rethink his strategy. The breakthrough comes when he recalls how the old lama was drawn to a major supernatural disaster—concluding that the Jian Tian Si only responds to large-scale, systemic threats, not petty hauntings. This insight shifts his search to local rumors of catastrophic events. Elsewhere, Lü Zhuangyuan tries to secure funds to restart his troupe, revealing his stubborn refusal to accept obscurity. Bai Lingmiao quietly presents Li Huowang with a map to her family home, marking a personal milestone. The chapter closes on a startling note: Lü Xiucai has awakened his *shentong*, shouting about it from outside the door, and Li Huowang must now juggle this new development with his higher-priority goals.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Let’s talk about the **Jian Tian Si (监天司)**. The name translates loosely to “Supervisory Heavenly Office,” and in the Dao-Twisted World, they operate like a cross between a celestial policing bureau and a supernatural EPA. They don’t care about one dead body or a possessed farmer—they care about *Heavenly Calamities* (天灾), ontological disasters where the laws of reality break down. Think of them as the guys who show up after the dam breaks, not the ones who patch a leak. Li Huowang’s deduction here is pure tactical gold: if he wants their attention without revealing himself, he needs to find the next big, unnatural disaster before they do. Also noteworthy: the term **shentong (神通)**. In xianxia, this is the catch-all for a cultivator’s supernatural ability—it can mean anything from clairvoyance to telekinesis. Lü Xiucai’s claim to have “made it” with a *shentong* is a huge deal for a novice, though what exactly he's awakened remains deliciously unclear.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is all about **Li Huowang’s tactical recalibration**. He’s not charging in blind; he’s *studying* the system, learning its rules, and adapting. This is the mark of a survivor who has learned that brute force and direct questions don’t work in this world—you have to think several moves ahead. Pay close attention to how he reframes the problem: from “find the Jian Tian Si” to “create or find the conditions that summon the Jian Tian Si.” That’s the kind of lateral thinking that keeps a protagonist alive in a world where every direct answer is a trap. Also, the small moments—Gouwa’s crude humor, Lü Zhuangyuan’s desperate pride, Bai Lingmiao’s quiet relief—are not filler. They are the emotional scaffolding that makes the horror hit harder when it returns. Enjoy the lull. It won’t last.
