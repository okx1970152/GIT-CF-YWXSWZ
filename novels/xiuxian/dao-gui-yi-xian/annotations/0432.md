## Chapter Overview

The golden palace isn't just a display of wealth—it's a trap, a prison, and a ritual array all at once. Li Huowang, still invisible and hunting for the Zuowandao who know the whereabouts of the Heart-Element North Wind, steps into the Great Liang Imperial Palace's Throne Hall only to find that his own memories are being erased in real-time. Forced to rely on desperate self-carved notes on his own skin, he uncovers the horror beneath the gilded surface: every golden hall sits atop a buried Heart-Turbid corpse, sewn shut and used as a living formation eye. And when he finally breaks through to the palace's core, he finds something far worse waiting in the sky.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang enters the golden Throne Hall of the Great Liang Imperial Palace, following bloody footprints left by the Zuowandao.
- He discovers that time and memory are being erased within the hall; his past self has already left carved instructions on his arm because he knew he would forget.
- He finds a wound on his thigh and bone-fracture divination marks, proving he has already attempted to locate the Heart-Turbid without remembering it.
- Following the notes left by his earlier self, Li Huowang digs beneath the central golden brick and finds a buried small shrine containing the corpse of the Heart-Turbid—its body completely sewn with golden thread into a "human log," covered in interlocking swastikas that serve as the formation's anchor.
- He severs the Heart-Turbid from the golden swastika matrix, which dims the entire hall and breaks the memory-erasing effect.
- Hong Zhong reveals that the entire palace complex is an astral constellation formation, with each golden hall potentially housing a Heart-Turbid as its eye.
- Li Huowang pushes further north, finally breaking through to a clearing where a massive black-and-white vertical pupil stares down from the clouds, surrounded by sixty-four bronze hexagrams.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Six-Clawed Golden Dragon (六爪金龙):** In traditional Chinese iconography, the five-clawed dragon (*wulong*) was the exclusive emblem of the emperor, while four-clawed dragons were used by nobles and officials. The novel's use of a **six-clawed** dragon is a deliberate escalation, signaling that this is a twisted or corrupted version of imperial symbolism—nothing in this world follows the orthodox rules of power.

**The Throne Hall (金銮殿):** The *Jinluan Dian* was the most important ceremonial hall in a Chinese palace, where the emperor held court. Its name literally means "Golden Imperial Throne Hall." The novel's version is made of real gold, turning a symbol of earthly authority into a literal and monstrous trap—a terrifically grim inversion of imperial grandeur.

**The Swastika (卍字):** In Buddhist tradition, the swastika (*wan* character) is an ancient symbol of auspiciousness, eternity, and the Buddha's heart-mind. Usually drawn in a clockwise orientation, it appears on the chest of Buddha statues. Here, it is rendered in **golden paint** and used to seal a buried Heart-Turbid corpse. The novel corrupts a sacred symbol into a binding, sealing curse—notably, its use here is aggressive and entangling, the opposite of its traditional liberating meaning. This follows the novel's pattern of taking sacred imagery and weaponizing it.

**Sixty-Four Hexagrams (六十四卦):** These are the core of the *I Ching* (Book of Changes), representing all possible states of change in the universe, each hexagram composed of six stacked lines (yin or yang). Their appearance surrounding the vertical pupil in the sky suggests that whatever is above is not just a monster—it is a **cosmic principle or a complete system of fate**, something that governs or observes reality itself, rather than merely existing within it.

## Reading Guide

Holy gold, am I right? This chapter is a masterclass in "oh no, everything is worse than I thought." Li Huowang walks into an impossibly opulent palace expecting to chase down some Zuowandao, and instead finds a horror labyrinth that **literally eats his own memory**. The image of him discovering his own frantic handwriting on his arm—words he wrote but can't remember writing—is the kind of cognitive terror this novel does best. And the reveal that a captured Heart-Turbid corpse, that ultra-rare and dangerous entity, is just being used as a disposable formation eye? That's the novel telling you, very politely, that the real bosses haven't even shown up yet. Also, that final panel with the vertical pupil and the floating hexagrams? Genuine skin-crawler imagery. Buckle up.
