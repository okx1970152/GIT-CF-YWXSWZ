## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang is back on his feet after three days of unconsciousness, and he marks his return with a grisly but crafty bit of self-tailoring—using his own skin to forge a life-saving talisman from the *Thousand Greats Record*. The scene is pure *Dao Guai Yi Xian*: clinical, methodical, and quietly monstrous. Across town, the Bai family compound is ringing with festive cheer as the group celebrates the New Year. But when the celebrants hear a familiar voice at the door, it's not anyone they expected—it's Bai Lingmiao, back from the dead and wearing a very different demeanor. This chapter is a moment of strange calm before the storm, a brief island of warmth (and pork trotters) in a sea of horrors.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang wakes after three days and finds his peeled human skin intact, guarded loyally by his dog, Mantou.
- Using cinnabar talismans, incense, and dry straw, he performs a ritual that shrinks the skin into a small, waxy palm-sized doll—a life-saving artifact from the *Thousand Greats Record*. It can be recreated infinitely, offering him a desperately needed safety net.
- Li Huowang stores the talisman inside his own body via the Black Tai Sui, and briefly entertains the dark thought that he might finally have a "golden finger."
- At the Bai family compound, Gouwa and the troupe are feasting on a lavish New Year's Eve dinner, with Chun Xiaoman worrying about Bai Lingmiao's absence.
- A figure appears at the door: Bai Lingmiao, alive and seemingly well. Chun Xiaoman's relief turns to surprise as Bai Lingmiao brushes past her, walks directly to the head of the table, and sits down in the seat of honor without asking—toasting the room with bold, uncharacteristic confidence.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Life-Saving Skin Artifact & The *Thousand Greats Record***  
Li Huowang's gruesome crafting scene is a direct application of the *Thousand Greats Record*—the foundational text of a suffering-cult. In Daoist external alchemy (外丹), human skin, hair, and blood have long been used as powerful ingredients. But here, the method is inverted: instead of an elixir of immortality, Li Huowang creates a *paper-thin substitute life vessel*, a charm that can "exchange fate" in a moment of certain death. This is a dark echo of the traditional Daoist "corpse solution" (尸解仙), where a cultivator sheds their physical form like a snake's skin. Li Huowang's version is literal, painful, and made from his own flayed body.  
**New Year's Eve Feast (年夜饭)**  
The spread described—lion's head meatballs, braised carp, preserved pork with mustard greens—is a classic Northern Chinese New Year's feast. Each dish carries symbolic meaning: fish (余) for abundance, meatballs (丸子) for reunion. The Bai family's meal is painted as a moment of warmth and plenty, but the shadow of Bai Lingmiao's return twists it into something stranger, especially when she takes the seat of honor (主位), a position traditionally reserved for the eldest or most respected.

### bianlian (变脸)

A traditional Sichuan opera technique where performers rapidly switch between painted masks; in the Dao-Twisted World, it becomes a trauma trigger for Li Huowang, who associates face-changing with Zuowandao deception.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in tonal whiplash. The opening is pure body horror—Li Huowang's calculated self-mutilation is described with the same clinical precision he'd use for any other chore. But then you get Mantou wagging his tail, and then a full-blown New Year's feast with chicken legs and pig trotters. The real hook, though, is Bai Lingmiao's return. Her entrance is not timid or relieved—it's *loud*, *commanding*, and deeply unsettling. Read the scene again and notice how she ignores Chun Xiaoman's embrace, bypasses the small tables, and sits down in the seat of honor without a word. This is not the same woman who left. Something has changed in her, and it's not just confidence. Keep an eye on her dialogue—she's bold, she's cheerful, and she's toasting like a warlord. That's your first hint that the Purple-Tasseled Sword's corruption has taken root.

## Deep Dive: The *Bianlian* Trigger & Li Huowang's Trauma

One of the chapter's subtle but crucial beats is Li Huowang's reaction to the *Thousand Greats Record*'s ritual. The *Record* is a text about transformation—about shedding, burning, and remaking the self. But transformation is also the domain of *face-changing* (变脸), the Sichuan opera technique where performers swap masks in the blink of an eye. For anyone else, *face-changing* is artistry. For Li Huowang, it's a horror show. Every new face he sees is a potential Zuowandao trick, every mask a lie. This chapter shows him in a rare moment of control—he is the one doing the changing, the one crafting the mask—but the ghost of every *bianlian* trigger he has ever suffered still hangs over him.
