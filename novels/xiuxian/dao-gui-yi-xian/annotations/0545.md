## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, grab your strategist hats! Chapter 544 is a masterclass in *Wheeling and Dealing 101*. Li Huowang has just stolen the spine-sword, and the entire office is baying for his blood. But instead of running, he waltzes into a fine dining establishment, pours the baijiu, and transforms a deadlock into a bargaining chip. This is the chapter where our favorite blood-soaked schemer proves he can play office politics just as well as he plays flesh-horror chess. The stakes shift from sword-fights to soul-crushing dialogue, and the real weapon here isn’t a sword—it’s the offer of a “Heart-Element.”

## Key Plot Points
- Fo Yulu and her crew corner Li Huowang for stealing the Taixu Sword, but she can’t touch him due to Office rules against imperial entanglements—Li Huowang weaponizes her fear of being linked to the succession war.
- After the crowd disperses, Li Huowang drags Hong Da to a tavern, buttering him up with drink and old favours to turn him into a mediator.
- Hong Da tries to get Li Huowang to simply return the sword; Li Huowang refuses, then drops the bombshell: he’ll trade a Heart-Element instead.
- Hong Da’s reaction—casually asking “alive or dead?”—confirms that Heart-Elements are recognized trade goods, and Li Huowang knows he’s found the escape hatch from the deadlock.
- Over drinks, Hong Da reveals massive news: the state of Four Qi has fallen, and its new dynasty abolished the Supervisory Heavenly Office, stirring an ominous feeling in Li Huowang that Great Qi’s fate might be repeating itself.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of a **Heart-Pan (心蟠)** -forged artifact like the Taixu Sword is a deep xianxia lore trope: a sentient or spiritually potent being is refined into a weapon, making it priceless and, frankly, terrifying. Meanwhile, a **Heart-Element (心素)** is a living, breathing rare resource—a “human ingredient” that cultivators value for its reality-warping potential. The fact that Hong Da casually asks “alive or dead” tells you just how commodified human existence has become in this world’s back-alley economy. Also worth noting: the ban on **Supervisory Heavenly Office** members touching imperial affairs is a real historical headache—it mirrors ancient Chinese censorate laws designed to keep state security agencies from becoming kingmakers. Li Huowang using this rule as a shield is a genuine power move.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a palate cleanser that’s anything but clean. It’s a slow-burn negotiation scene that packs a lore bomb and a political landmine into the same bottle of wine. Watch how Li Huowang pivots from cornered animal to cunning merchant—his offer of a Heart-Element is both a lifeline and a confession of what he really is. And that final beat about Four Qi’s fall and the Office’s dissolution? That’s not a throwaway line; it’s the novel’s way of whispering that the cosmic dominoes are starting to fall. Get ready, because the game is being played on multiple boards now.

## Deep Dive: The Art of the Deal in the Dao-Twisted World
In ancient China, wine was the great social solvent—a shared cup meant trust, allegiance, or, at the very least, a temporary truce. Li Huowang using a lavish meal to grease the wheels is straight out of the *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* playbook. But here’s the twist: he’s not just playing the sycophant; he’s reading Hong Da’s reaction like a lie detector. The moment Hong Da asks “alive or dead,” Li Huowang knows the system has a price tag for humanity itself. That’s the real horror—not a monster, but a bureaucracy that trades in souls.
