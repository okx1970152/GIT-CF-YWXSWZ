## Chapter Overview

All right, buckle up, fellow readers—this chapter is a masterclass in *cognitive whiplash*. Li Huowang just pulled off a desperate bluff by forcing two hallucinations (Zhuge Yuan and Peng Longteng) into temporary reality using his Heart-Element ability, and it actually worked long enough to scare off the Supervisory Heavenly Office agents. But the cost? His Primordial Qi goes haywire, and he gets yanked back to the modern-world reality—only this time, not in a hospital bed but in a prison courtyard under his mother’s watch. The real gut-punch comes when Yang Na shows up with her father, and Li Huowang’s fractured mind can’t decide whether she’s real or another hallucination. It’s a raw, painful collision between his desperate survival logic and his equally desperate human longing.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang successfully scares off four Bureau agents by using a marrow-sword to briefly solidify Zhuge Yuan’s hallucination in front of them—his bluff hinges on the agents’ fear of Zhuge Yuan’s actual power.
- The cost of forcing two major hallucinations is severe: his Primordial Qi destabilizes, and his head swells painfully, forcing him to retreat.
- He abruptly shifts back to the modern world, but this time his mother has him in a prison exercise yard, not the hospital ward—suggesting his condition has worsened in the eyes of authorities.
- Using only his teeth, Li Huowang frees himself from restraints, grabs a broom as a makeshift weapon, and hides in a bush, still paranoid that the Bureau agents might track him across worlds.
- He pleads with his mother to trust him, confessing he can’t explain but promising things will be better; she sobs silently, and his guilt over her pain is palpable.
- Yang Na appears outside the fence with her father, visibly emaciated and heartbroken; her father coldly dismisses Li Huowang as a lost cause, dragging her away.
- Li Huowang’s mind splits between two convictions—that this is a hallucination he should ignore, and that this is the real world he must cling to—causing him to physically pound his own head in anguish.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“Forcing the False into Reality” (修真之反用):** This chapter gives us a concrete look at what happens when a Heart-Element pushes their ability too far. Normally, “cultivating the false into reality” is a gradual process of layering belief until a lie solidifies. But here, Li Huowang rushes it—he plants a hallucination of Zhuge Yuan into the world like a flag, using the Primordial Qi as fuel. The result is a short-lived but convincing manifestation that collapses almost immediately. Think of it like a sprint when your cultivation base was built for marathons: you get one explosive burst, then you crash. The swelling head and destabilized Qi are not just flavor text—they’re the body rejecting an overdraw on its spiritual credit.

**The Marrow-Sword as a Medium:** The sword made from Zhuge Yuan’s marrow-skull is more than a gruesome trophy—it acts as a physical anchor, a “bone-print” that makes the hallucination more credible. In Daoist ritual logic, body parts of the deceased retain a resonance with their original owner, like a tuning fork. This is why Li Huowang can summon a Zhuge Yuan hallucination more easily than, say, a random bystander: the marrow-sword is a relic that lowers the spiritual “cost” of the manifestation.

**The Prison Courtyard vs. the Hospital Ward:** The shift from a hospital setting to a prison exercise yard is a chilling detail. In modern Chinese psychiatric care, patients deemed a danger to themselves or others can be placed in “protective custody” with physical restraints and outdoor time under supervision. The presence of iron mesh and the casual way staff have left him in the sun suggests this is not a standard lockdown but a long-term containment arrangement—his mother has had to accept harsher conditions because his behavior has been deemed uncontrollable. The atrophy of his muscles is a medical reality that the Bureau agents on the other side were also observing, which suggests his physical body in one world mirrors his state in the other, even if he doesn’t fully cross over.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *multitool* of emotional weapons. The action is over in the first few paragraphs—the real fight is in Li Huowang’s head, and it’s relentless. Pay attention to the way he *switches mental gears*: one second he’s a cold strategist bluffing Bureau agents, the next he’s a son lying on the ground trying not to make his mother cry, and the next he’s staring at Yang Na and feeling his soul tear in two. The line between “this is fake, ignore it” and “this is real, don’t lose her” is exactly the same line that defines his entire existence across both worlds. That final scream and the self-inflicted pounding are not just dramatic punctuation—they’re the sound of a man whose reality-checkbox has been jammed so hard it’s smoking. If you’ve ever been torn between two truths you want to believe simultaneously, this chapter will land like a punch to the chest. Also, bonus dark humor: Li Huowang’s improvised weapon of choice is a plastic broom. The mighty wielder of reality-bending power, reduced to threatening supernatural agents with a cleaning tool. *Chef’s kiss.*
