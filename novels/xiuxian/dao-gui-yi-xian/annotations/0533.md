## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a gut-punch of cosmic exposition delivered through Zhuge Yuan’s trembling urgency. Forget monsters and demons—the dead human-faced ox just sang a prophecy that shatters the very foundation of reality as Li Huowang understands it. We go from “what is this weird birthing experiment” to “the entire Great Qi dynasty might cease to exist” in the span of a single revelation. And the cost? A desperate, body-breaking ritual that leaves our protagonist bleeding from every hole in his head. It’s one of those chapters where the real horror isn’t in the blood and gore—it’s in the terrifying fragility of history itself.

## Key Plot Points

- Zhuge Yuan identifies the dead human-faced ox as a **Yu**: a prophetic creature born only during times of great chaos, which speaks its omen and dies immediately. Its song predicted the breaking of the Great Qi dragon vein.
- He corrects Li Huowang’s misunderstanding: this is not some twisted eunuch fertility project from Zhengde Temple. The implications are civilization-ending.
- Zhuge Yuan explains that Great Liang existed *because* the Great Qi dragon vein broke once before, a thousand years ago. If it breaks again, Great Qi itself—the entire historical anchor—could be erased.
- The duo escapes the Cold Palace into Youdu, the capital, and Li Huowang realizes the city feels subtly different, though he cannot explain how.
- Zhuge Yuan asks Li Huowang to **Cultivate the False into Reality**—to give him temporary physical form, a process that proves far more taxing than when he did it for the monk or Peng Longteng. His head swells grotesquely.
- Once corporeal, Zhuge Yuan draws the Spine Sword, removes its talisman, and uses it as a ritual writing tool. The other Zhuge (the sword itself) confirms the gravity of the situation.
- He carves a seal into the earth with the sword-blood, then writes a formal document on paper that combusts spontaneously, the ashes swirling away. The ritual is complete just as he fades back to intangibility.
- Behind him, Li Huowang collapses unconscious, bleeding from all seven apertures, held up only by Li Sui’s tentacles.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Yu (寓)** is a fascinating and obscure piece of Chinese folk prophecy lore. The term literally means “to lodge” or “to contain,” and in ancient texts, a *Yu* was a creature born spontaneously during times of extreme upheaval. It would speak one sentence—an omen—and die. It’s the ultimate “messenger from the cosmos,” and the message is never good news. Think of it as heaven’s own desperate telegram, delivered at the cost of a life. In this novel, the Yu’s function is preserved, but its scale is catastrophic: the death of a dynasty itself.

**Dragon Vein (龙脉)** is a core concept in Chinese feng shui and imperial mysticism. A dragon vein is a metaphysical line of geomantic energy running through mountains and rivers, believed to sustain the prosperity and legitimacy of a dynasty. If it “breaks,” the dynasty falls. But here, the concept is taken to its terrifying extreme: the dragon vein isn’t just about luck—it *anchors an entire timeline*. If it breaks, history itself unravels.

**Cultivate the False into Reality (以假修真)** is Li Huowang’s unique Heart-Element ability, and this chapter gives us a crucial look at its limits. Giving physical form to a hallucination is not a free lunch. The more “real” or important the entity, the more it costs. Compared to the monk or Peng Longteng, giving form to Zhuge Yuan—a figure tied to the fate of the dragon vein—nearly kills him. His head swelling like a god of longevity is a grim visual metaphor: the price of making reality is deformity.

The ritual Zhuge Yuan performs—using the Spine Sword as a brush, writing on the earth, the spontaneous combustion of the paper—is reminiscent of Daoist talisman-writing and celestial memorials. The sword-blood sinks into the ground, suggesting he is “sealing” or “reporting” directly to the earth’s spiritual infrastructure. The ash swirling is a classic sign that the message has been delivered.

## Reading Guide

Buckle up, because this chapter is a lore grenade. Zhuge Yuan drops the revelation that the entire political structure of the Dao-Twisted World—the emperors, the dynasties—is not fixed. It’s contingent on a fragile metaphysical thread called a dragon vein. That’s terrifying because it means the world isn’t just mad; it’s unstable. A single bad prophecy can delete a millennium of history.

Notice how Li Huowang’s mind works here: he instinctively connects the dots to the Great Liang imperial temple, but he can’t grasp the full picture. That’s okay—he’s not a historian, he’s a survivor. The real treat is watching Zhuge Yuan transform from a gentle scholar into a desperate, commanding figure. He is *driven*, and the sight of him straining to perform this ritual is a reminder that even the calmest voices in this world carry the weight of impossible knowledge.

Also, pay attention to the physical cost. The “Cultivate the False” ability is not a cheat code. It has a mana system, and the currency is Li Huowang’s own flesh and blood. His head swelling like a grotesque balloon is one of the most visceral images of the chapter—a reminder that power in this world is always, always paid for.

And that ending? Li Huowang collapsing in Li Sui’s arms, silent and broken, without even hearing Zhuge Yuan’s thanks. It’s a cold landing after a white-knuckle ascent. The ritual worked, but the cost is already being tallied.
