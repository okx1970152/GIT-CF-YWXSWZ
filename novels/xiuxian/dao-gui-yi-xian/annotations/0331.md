## Chapter Overview

Two parallel lonely tracks, one dark infernal. In the shadow of Li Huowang's hollow attempt at a Mid-Autumn festival, Bai Lingmiao's service contract with the Bei School is brutally renewed. After her near-death and revival, the true weight of the spirit-dancer's yoke descends—not as a threat, but as a simple piece of homework. Meanwhile, Li Huowang navigates the peculiar folk customs of Silverstar City, only to be stood up by the bureaucracy and left to toast the moon with his ever-present hallucinations and his dog. This chapter is a bittersweet symphony of mundane isolation and cosmic servitude.

## Key Plot Points

- **Bai Lingmiao's Ascension Renewal:** After surviving the hanging, Bai Lingmiao has an out-of-body experience. She finds the starry sky blocked by the Bei School Immortals, who force her to accept a monthly task quota (starting at 'Two'). This confirms her role as an indentured servant of the spirit world, with a penalty for failure that is left chillingly unsaid.
- **The Da Shen's Moment of Humanity:** The entity inhabiting her head, the Da Shen, seems to experience a flicker of empathy or memory when it hears her cry. It pulls out a jade pendant and whispers "Father," a deeply human gesture that suggests a shared history of loss.
- **Li Huowang's Journey Ends:** After a long, frustrating journey through a traffic jam of duck-sellers, Li Huowang finally arrives at his destination, Silverstar City.
- **A Bureaucratic Holiday:** Li Huowang waits for his contact, Tuoba Danqing, only to receive a messenger pigeon declaring he's off for the Mid-Autumn Festival. The mundane reality of the bureaucracy's rest day clashes with Li Huowang's grim purpose.
- **Drinking with Hallucinations:** Li Huowang, in a state of despondent loneliness, orders a feast and drinks himself into a stupor. He toasts the moon alongside his phantom companions, the Monk, Red Central, and his other hallucinations, admitting that the simple meals shared with his old crew tasted better.
- **The Black Tai Sui Emerges:** Li Huowang wakes to find the Black Tai Sui tentacle has emerged from his navel on its own. He casually flicks it back inside, a sign of his growing desensitization to the body horror that defines his existence.
- **Advanced Study:** He resumes his studies of the *Thousand Character Classic*, the next step in his self-education program, and hears a strange, slurred echo repeating the text after him—a new development in his ever-strange condition.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Spirit-Dancer's Contract (跳大神, Tiao Da Shen):** This chapter confirms the brutal reality of this folk practice. The spirit-dancer is not a free agent; they are a vessel, a slave to the "Immortals" (仙家) who grant them power. The monthly "tasks" are a form of debt, a quota of souls or services that must be repaid, or else the consequences are dire. Li Zhi's dying words, "In my next life, I'll never do it again," are the ultimate testament to this draining and hopeless existence.
- **The "Big Character" Task System:** The cold, impersonal number '2' appearing in Bai Lingmiao's mind is a bureaucratic horror. This is not a spiritual test of merit; it is a corporate quota system applied to the soul. The predictable increase in the number is a looming, inevitable dread.
- **The Mid-Autumn Festival (中秋節):** This chapter is steeped in the mood of this major Chinese holiday, which celebrates family reunion and the full moon. The tradition of building "earthen kilns" (土窑子) is a regional custom often associated with the festival in southern China. Children carrying pomelo-skin lanterns is another nostalgic touch. Li Huowang's isolation during this time of family unity is a powerful thematic contrast to the holiday's core meaning.
- **The *Thousand Character Classic* (千字文):** This is a foundational Chinese literacy primer, composed of exactly one thousand unique characters arranged into rhyming couplets. It covers history, nature, and morality. Li Huowang's use of it as a reading exercise for his Black Tai Sui highlights the surreal contrast between classical scholarship and parasitic horror.

## Reading Guide

This is a chapter of quiet horror and profound loneliness. After the high-octane action of recent chapters, things slow down for a character study in desperation. Bai Lingmiao's arc is devastating—her small pride in helping is completely crushed under the weight of her new "promotion." For Li Huowang, the festival is just another night spent with dead people and hallucinations. Pay close attention to the Da Shen. Its moment of sentimental fragility with the jade pendant is our first real hint that it has a history and emotions of its own. Is it a monster, or is it just as trapped as Bai Lingmiao? Also, keep an ear out for that slurred, echoing voice in Li Huowang's recitation. Something is listening, and it's learning to talk back.
