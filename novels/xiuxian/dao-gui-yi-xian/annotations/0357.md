## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow horror-cultivation enjoyers, buckle up. The tension that’s been simmering for the last few chapters finally boils over—but not in the way anyone expected. Li Huowang and the Office squad are deep in the Heart-Turbid’s nest, a village that feels less like a place and more like an unfinished thought. The walls are covered in the Heart-Turbid’s frantic, multi-handed scrawl, and the team is on edge. Then, with almost no warning at all, the Heart-Turbid herself drops right into their laps.

And here’s the kicker: she’s *easy*. Almost pathetically so. Li Huowang stabs her, dislocates her joints, and wraps her up before she can even process what’s happening. But the real nightmare begins when they look around. This isn’t a lair. It’s a grave. A forgotten graveyard of people the Heart-Turbid *meant to save*. This chapter is a masterclass in pivoting from “we caught the monster” to “oh god, the monster is just another victim.”

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Wrong Words:** Tuoba Danqing identifies the handwriting on the walls as belonging to the Heart-Turbid, but notes it looks like it was written by multiple people. This is the first clue that her identity isn't stable.
*   **Instant Capture:** The Heart-Turbid literally falls onto Li Huowang, hanging upside down right in front of him. She is completely shocked that he can see her. Li Huowang captures her in seconds without any struggle, which immediately feels *wrong* to everyone present.
*   **The Living Blindfold:** The Heart-Turbid’s eyes can only be covered by living things. Li Huowang has his Black Tai Sui (Li Sui) use its tentacles to bind her eyes, finally subduing her and earning a major favor from the Chief Recorder.
*   **The Corpse Wall:** Before leaving, Li Huowang reads more of the wall scrawl and discovers a pile of desiccated female mummies hidden under straw. The text explains they are her “wives.”
*   **Twice the Horror:** Li Huowang, noticing the handwriting changes and the contradiction of a “female” Heart-Turbid having wives, asks the million-dollar question: “Could there be another Heart-Turbid?” The room goes dead silent.
*   **The Final Confession:** As they leave, Hong Da reveals the true horror. The Heart-Turbid’s power is a trap for *herself*. She hid people—enemies, family, friends—inside her own karma to save them, but she *forgot*. She forgot the people, then her own memories, then her age, then even her own gender. She isn't one person; she's a new one every hour.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter pivots hard on a concept we’ve touched on before but never fully explored: **the Heart-Turbid (心浊, Xīn Zhuó)**. While the Heart-Element (心素) can “cultivate the false into reality,” the Heart-Turbid does the opposite. It *hides* things—concepts, objects, people—by erasing them from reality. The terrifying folk-logic here is that it isn't a targeted attack; it's a passive, leaking wound in the world. The Heart-Turbid's power is a curse that spreads to everything it touches, including herself.

The detail about becoming a **Renxiao (人魈)** is also crucial context here. The Chief Recorder is frantically eating Lifespan Pills and is terrified of hitting his limit. In this novel, a Renxiao is what happens when a cultivator exceeds their natural lifespan without achieving true immortality. They become blind, malformed monsters, forced to drag out an existence of pure obsession. The Chief Recorder’s panic gives a dark, bureaucratic urgency to the entire mission: this is a man racing against his own biological and spiritual decay.

## Reading Guide

This is one of those chapters that re-contextualizes an entire character archetype. You walked in expecting a terrifying, cunning trickster-spirit that preys on perception. You walked out realizing you’ve just read the diary of a woman who accidentally buried her own friends alive inside her own head. The “Heart-Turbid” is less a villain and more a walking, weeping natural disaster made of apathy and amnesia. The horror isn’t her strength; it’s her weakness.

Pay attention to the way the team reacts after the capture. There’s no triumph. There’s confusion, disgust, and a deep, unsettling *pity*. This is a great example of how *Dao Gui Yi Xian* uses its supernatural elements not just for scares, but for pathos. Also, keep an eye on that line about a “Second Heart-Turbid.” The handwriting on the wall genuinely seems to belong to different people. That seed of doubt is going to bloom into something later. Get ready.

## The Horror of Forgetting

The most chilling part of this chapter isn’t the mummies or the instant capture. It’s Hong Da’s final speech. The idea that a Heart-Turbid’s karmic burden is so heavy, so self-devouring, that it becomes a prison for the *owner* of the power is a deeply Buddhist-cosmic horror concept. It’s the ultimate punishment of the self: to be forgotten by yourself. To become a stranger to your own life, your own grief, your own crimes. The Heart-Turbid didn’t *want* to hurt anyone. She wanted to be a savior. But her power consumed her intention, leaving only a husk that unconsciously buries everything that matters to it.
