## Chapter Overview
Alright, buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a *heavy* one. We’re dropped right into the aftermath with Gao Zhijian on the throne, using the Chief Astrologer’s power to rewatch the final showdown between Li Huowang and the Dice. What we see is a Li Huowang who has transcended his physical limits, casually decapitating Gao Zhijian’s old body to extract something invisible, all while the Dice screams in impotent fury. But the real gut-punch comes after the victory: the Siming *the Unspeakable* is revealed to have been pulling the strings, and in the present day, a mysterious guest named “Ji Zai” has already slipped into Li Huowang’s room. It’s a quiet, creeping horror that undercuts the moment of triumph, reminding us that in this world, no victory is ever clean, and every answer just opens a deeper, darker question.

## Key Plot Points
*   Gao Zhijian, now Emperor, asks the Chief Astrologer to show him the truth of Li Huowang’s battle with the Dice-controlled Dragon Vein.
*   Through a flashback, we see Li Huowang’s unique behavior: he climbs the rigid Dragon Vein, decapitates Gao Zhijian’s former body, and pulls an *invisible* object from the head.
*   The Dice’s desperate claim of being “the real one” is rendered useless against Li Huowang’s inexplicable power.
*   Zhuge Yuan is confirmed to have truly vanished after hearing Li Huowang say “Great Qi is saved.”
*   A mysterious “otherworldly haze” is revealed to have left Li Huowang’s body during the battle, hinting at Doumu’s influence or the Unspeakable’s intervention.
*   Gao Zhijian visits Li Huowang’s residence, only to be told by Bai Lingmiao that another “Chief Astrologer” has already entered.
*   The real Chief Astrologer finds Li Huowang alone in his room, talking to nobody. When asked who he was speaking to, Li Huowang replies, “Ji Zai.”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
*   **Dragon Vein (龙脉) & Mandate of Heaven (气运):** In this novel, the Dragon Vein is not just a geomantic energy line; it is a tangible, parasitic manifestation of a dynasty’s right to rule. The “Mandate of Heaven” is a visceral burden—a stolen fragment of power that props up reality but requires a family to commit unspeakable acts to maintain it. The Emperor and the Vein are one; the Dice possessing the Vein means it *is* the truth of this world, which is why Li Huowang overpowering it is such a staggering feat.
*   **The Unspeakable (说不得):** This entity is a terrifying new addition to the Dao-Twisted World’s cosmology. It is a Siming that operates outside the normal rules—it can descend into the mortal world when other gods cannot. Its name implies that it cannot be named, spoken of, or recorded, suggesting it is a being born from the “void” or from a concept that defies language itself. Its indifference to even a desperate emperor’s plea makes it an uncaring, almost Lovecraftian force of nature.
*   **Siming & Descent:** A key rule is reiterated here: in a world supported by Dragon Veins, Siming (high-ranking gods) cannot normally descend to the mortal plane. The Unspeakable’s ability to do so makes it an anomaly, a loophole in the cosmic order, and a far more dangerous player than others.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in how *Dao Gui Yi Xian* uses its victory laps. There is no triumphant celebration here. Li Huowang wins, Zhuge Yuan is saved, but the chapter is drenched in a profound, unsettling melancholy. The “headless Li Huowang speaking calmly” is a perfect image of the cost of his power—he is becoming something inhuman even as he saves his friends. The introduction of “Ji Zai” (季灾) is the final punch. Who is he? A hallucination? A new Siming? A fragment of Li Huowang’s fractured mind? The chapter ends not on a climax, but on a whisper of dread, forcing the reader to sit in the uncomfortable space between a question and an answer. Pay close attention to the phrase “Ji Zai”; it translates roughly to “Season’s Calamity,” a name that perfectly fits the novel’s theme of cyclical, inescapable disasters.

## The Unspoken Rules of the Dao-Twisted World
The Chief Astrologer’s advice to Gao Zhijian—“If they taught Your Majesty that, then Your Majesty should listen to them”—is a chilling piece of courtly philosophy. It suggests that in this world, reality is a consensus, and those in power can simply *decide* what is true, even if it contradicts the underlying cosmic horror. The fact that Gao Zhijian “stops pressing” is not a sign of faith, but of a weary acceptance that some truths are too heavy to serve as a foundation for rule. This is the deepest horror of the Dao-Twisted World: even the truth is a political tool.
