## Chapter Overview

Oh, snap! The Big Bad Li Huowang is back in Yinling City, but while he was away, the side characters have been cooking up some serious trouble. This chapter is a brilliant breather episode where the "B-team"—Gouwa, Chun Xiaoman, and Gao Zhijian—takes center stage, revealing the deliciously messy aftermath of the Bai family massacre. Gouwa, that scheming little weasel, has struck gold (literally and metaphorically), discovering a hidden trove of Bai family secrets. Meanwhile, Li Huowang’s homecoming is less “hero’s welcome” and more “dog drama at the inn.” It’s a chapter about the spoils of war, the seeds of future conflict, and just how hard it is to be a normal person in a twisted world.

## Key Plot Points

- **Gouwa’s Score:** While the others are grieving, Gouwa is cashing in. He spills the beans about finding a secret stash of books and treasures in a cellar beneath the Bai family ancestral shrine. He’s practically vibrating with greed, dreaming of learning "immortal techniques."
- **Xiaoman’s Veto:** Chun Xiaoman, ever the pragmatist, slams the brakes on Gouwa’s enthusiasm. She confiscates the book (*Hymns of Enlightenment from Bitter Effort*) and lays down the law: they learn the "good" stuff, absolutely none of the murderous, human-sacrificing stuff. She also makes the call to keep it all secret from the grieving Bai Lingmiao.
- **Li Huowang’s Homecoming:** Our favorite paranoid protagonist returns to the inn, only to find his dog, Mantou, has been in a fight. The mystery? Another stray bitch, a male dog, and some very territorial romance. Li Huowang’s worry turns to absurdist frustration when he learns the culprit is long gone.
- **The Next Move:** Settled back in, Li Huowang’s mind turns to his next chess move. He decides to contact Tuoba Danqing to feel out the Chief Recorder’s mood before even *thinking* about broaching the subject of Zhuge Yuan.
- **A Pigeon’s Arrival:** The chapter ends on a cliffhanger, as the cooing of a carrier pigeon announces a new message, promising a hook into the next plot thread.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- ***Hymns of Enlightenment from Bitter Effort* (《苦功悟道诵偈》):** This title is a direct reference to a key scripture from the Luo Sect (罗教), a major Ming Dynasty folk Buddhist sect that deeply influenced the *White Lotus* tradition. The original text is a foundational work of "Bitter Effort" Buddhism, focusing on enlightenment through strenuous practice and repetitive chanting. The book appearing in a White Lotus-connected family’s cellar is like finding a foundational physics textbook in a nuclear physicist’s basement—it confirms their lineage and hints at the dangerous knowledge they possessed.
- **White Lotus Society (白莲教):** This is a recurring boogeyman in the Dao-Twisted World, but it’s rooted in real history. The White Lotus wasn't one single cult but an umbrella term for various secret, millenarian religious societies in China. They were often banned and persecuted for their beliefs that a savior (like the "Unborn Venerable Mother") would come to purify the world. Their syncretic mix of Buddhism, Manichaeism, and folk religion makes their doctrines a perfect source for the twisted, reality-bending techniques in this novel.
- **Human Pillars (人柱):** Chun Xiaoman’s horrified reference to "using living people as foundation fillings" is a grim reality in ancient Chinese (and other) cultures. Known as "human pillars" or foundation sacrifices, it was believed that burying a person (alive or dead) in the foundation of a major structure—a city wall, a temple, or a dam—would appease the earth spirits and ensure the building's stability. It was the ultimate, horrific price for "permanence."

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a perfect example of why *Dao Gui Yi Xian*’s world feels so lived-in. Author Huwei doesn’t just focus on Li Huowang’s cosmic terror; we also get to see the small, human dramas playing out in his absence. Gouwa is officially the MVP of greedy, petty survival, and Chun Xiaoman stepping up to lead the "orphaned" party members is a great character beat. Meanwhile, the Mantou subplot is a masterclass in tonal whiplash—it gives you a moment of absurd, mundane comedy right before plunging you back into the political supernatural thriller.
