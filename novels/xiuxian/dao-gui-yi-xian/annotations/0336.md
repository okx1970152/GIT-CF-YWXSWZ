## Chapter Overview
Get ready, folks—Li Huowang and company are diving headfirst into a Zuowandao-infested nightmare, and the tension is cranked to eleven. The Bone Buddha Temple isn't just a holy site; it's a trap waiting to spring, and the only ally our boy can truly trust is a mute monk who speaks *inside his brain*. The chapter masterfully sets the stage for a high-stakes operation while reminding us that Facai, one of Zuowandao's infamous Three Elements, is a foe whose every word is a poisoned needle. The finale? Chan Master Xinchi reads a situation wrong, and Li Huowang is once again the instrument of a messy, bloody resolution. It's a lean, taut setup chapter that proves even a misstep can kill.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Party Assembles**: The Chief Recorder briefs the team in the jail. The lineup includes the enormous, silent Chan Master Xinchi (who cultivates the Vow of Silence, communicating via Telepathy), the slippery Tuoba Danqing, the ever-reliable Hong Da, and Li Huowang himself.
2. **The Gold Mine of Lore**: The Chief Recorder drops a handbook's worth of rules for dealing with Zuowandao's Facai: don't take her words literally or inversely, but don't ignore them either; believe three-tenths, doubt three-tenths, and keep three-tenths for yourself. The ultimate tactic? Kill her before she can speak.
3. **Road Trip to Horror**: Exploiting the empire's relay system, the party reaches Langzhong City in just two days. The Bone Buddha Temple is described as a bustling, bustling site of worship—exactly the kind of "safe" public space that feels most grotesque in this novel.
4. **The Relic**: The temple's "Front Hall" doesn't house a Buddha statue but a dark red, contorted mummified corpse. Xinchi explains this is a "Flesh-Bone Bodhisattva"—the temple's Living Buddha itself. The name "Bone Buddha Temple" suddenly makes chilling sense.
5. **The False Flag**: Xinchi's voice suddenly turns severe. He orders Li Huowang to kill a kneeling worshipper three steps to the left. Li Huowang strikes without hesitation—bisecting a civilian in a spray of blood and guts. The crowd scatters in horror.
6. **The Mistake**: Xinchi picks up the head, examines it, and with a tone of pious regret, declares: "A sin. This humble monk made a mistake." He tosses the head aside and walks away. The takeaway? Even the fail-safe has its faults.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Flesh-Bone Bodhisattva (肉身菩萨)**: This is the key horror of this chapter. In Chinese Buddhism, "Flesh-Body Bodhisattva" can refer to a Buddhist monk who, through rigorous ascetic practice, prevents their body from decaying after death, becoming a revered relic. However, the way it is described here—a "dark red, contorted mummy"—leans heavily into the grotesque aesthetic of the Dao-Twisted World, twisting a normally sacred relic into something monstrous.
- **The Vow of Silence (闭口禅)**: Also known as "Mouna Vrata" or "Silence Vow." It's a specific ascetic practice in Mahayana Buddhism where a cultivator takes a vow of silence as a means of self-discipline and avoiding speech that could cause karmic harm. This makes Xinchi completely immune to the verbal trickery of Zuowandao, giving him a niche but powerful niche in this squad.
- **Telepathy (他心通)**: One of the Six Supernatural Powers (六神通) in Buddhism, "他心通" literally means "knowing the thoughts of others." But here, it's used more practically: allow the mute monk to communicate. Previously seen at Anci Nunnery, it's a recurring sign of a cultivator with a deep Buddhist background.
- **The Three Elements of Zuowandao (坐忘道三元)**: This chapter confirms that Facai (发财, meaning "Get Rich") is one of the three top-tier members of Zuowandao, alongside the previously mentioned North Wind (北风). The term "One of the Three Elements" ([三] Yuan) implies a trinity of leaders, each embodying a specific vice or thematic branch of the sect's philosophy of deception. This chapter gives us our first proper tactical assessment of Facai: she's a rationalist's worst nightmare.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in "soft horror" setup. There's no monster under the bed—just the crushing dread of walking into a crowded temple where *anyone* could be a shapeshifting liar. Li Huowang is essentially a loaded gun here, forced to wait for a trigger that might be a false positive. The pure coldness of Xinchi's apology—"A sin, a sin"—is the real punchline. The world doesn't care if the kill was justified; it was still messy. It's also a great check-in on Li Huowang's mindset: he's not horrified by the gratuitous violence anymore. He's just kneeling over a corpse, asking for a debriefing.

## On Tuoba Danqing's Act
Don't be fooled by his laid-back "Oh? Cannot ignore them either?" schtick. As the incremental lore update reminds us, Tuoba Danqing is playing a role. His "drunken mistakes" and "casual rambling" are all part of a very long, very slow con. He knows the Chief Recorder is dangerous, and he is wrapping himself in a persona of harmless banter. Count the layers of deception in this chapter: the team versus Facai, and among the team, Danqing versus everyone.
