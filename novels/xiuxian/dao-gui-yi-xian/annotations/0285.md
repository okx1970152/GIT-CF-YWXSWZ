## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang is done running blind. Now that he’s officially inside the Supervisory Heavenly Office, he can finally start working *with* a system instead of against it. But the first real test of his new organizational life immediately pits two competing priorities against each other: the promise he made to that eerie big-headed doll and Bai Lingmiao’s desperate need to go home. The chapter is a quiet, tactical breather—no monsters, no demonic combat—but the emotional temperature rises fast when Bai Lingmiao, who has always been soft and accommodating, plants her feet and refuses to be left behind ever again. The second half shifts to Lü Xiucai’s painful attempts at calligraphy and the final personnel split, setting the stage for a leaner, meaner hunting party heading straight for the Zuowandao.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang realizes the Supervisory Heavenly Office is a massive information network he can exploit to find “North Wind” and learn how to free a Heart-Element from Bewilderment.
- He marks Ganyuan Village on the map but sees it’s not on the same route as Bai Lingmiao’s hometown, forcing a choice between the Zuowandao mission and her return.
- Li Huowang tries to suggest Bai Lingmiao go home ahead of him; she explodes, bringing up the time she had to bury his dead body with her own hands at Anci Nunnery.
- Their argument reveals Bai Lingmiao has fully accepted the Second Spirit as an ally—she even calls it “Sister”—and offers it as a tactical reason to bring her along.
- Lü Xiucai presents his terrible handwriting as “finished work,” mistakenly thinks Li Huowang is about to teach him talisman drawing, and gets thoroughly roasted.
- Li Huowang definitively divides the group: the Lü family troupe and non-combatants head to Ox-Heart Mountain; only Bai Lingmiao, Gao Zhijian, Chun Xiaoman, and Gouwa will follow him to Ganyuan Village.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**County Gazetteer (县志)**—This is not a glossary drop alone; it’s a worldview shaper. A gazetteer is supposed to be the dry, official record of everything that happens in a region: droughts, bandit raids, plagues, births of notable people, rebuilding of temples. In the real Chinese bureaucracy, if something massive happened—a massacre, a harvest failure, a supernatural disaster—it *had* to be recorded for tax remission or imperial inquiry. When a gazetteer is found empty or sanitized, that silence is louder than any monster. It means the local government is complicit, or the horror was so total there was nobody left to write it down. This is peak Dao-Twisted World logic: the bureaucracy itself becomes an unreliable narrator.

**The Second Spirit (二神, Er Shen)**—We’ve seen this figure before: a silent, veiled woman who moves like a puppet, carries out rituals, and never speaks on her own authority. In this chapter, Bai Lingmiao casually says “me and Sister,” which is huge. Accepting the Second Spirit as a person, not a tool, marks a real shift in her relationship with the spirit world. Traditionally, chuma mediums treat their “mount” (the spirit they serve) with formal respect but keep a sharp hierarchy. Calling the Er Shen “Sister” is dangerously affectionate—and possibly opens a path for the Second Spirit to develop its own will.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a *character-routing* chapter, and fans of world-driven stories will love the bureaucratic pragmatism Li Huowang brings to his new position. But the emotional core here is Bai Lingmiao. We’ve seen her cry, comfort, and follow orders. Now we see her refuse. Her red-eyed argument isn’t a tantrum—it’s a survivor telling her loved one “I won’t bury you again.” Li Huowang’s casual attempt to sideline her feels less like cruelty and more like habit, which makes her pushback land harder. Pay attention to the team-split at the end: Li Huowang is deliberately stripping down to only combat-viable people (plus Gouwa as baggage). That’s the move of someone who *knows* he’s walking into a fight, not an investigation. Also, the Lü Xiucai scene is funny on the surface, but his despair at being laughed off as a talisman apprentice is the first crack in his ego—something that may matter when the pressure turns back on.
