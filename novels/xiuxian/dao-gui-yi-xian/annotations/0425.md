## Chapter Overview

Roll credits, because this chapter is a masterstroke of tension and reversal. Li Huowang is dragging his own headless corpse (yes, his own future alchemical ingredient) through a snowstorm, 50% frozen solid and 100% paranoid. The eerie chanting from last chapter turns out to be a city-wide event that left locals clutching their skulls in pain—and when Li Huowang finally returns to the capital, he’s immediately scooped up by the Supervisory Heavenly Office. But what he finds waiting for him? A square-jawed severed head idolized with incense, a suspiciously familiar face twisted into Hong Zhong’s features, and a shocking declaration that flips everything he thought he knew about his enemies. This is the moment where the Zuowandao’s long game finally starts to reveal its teeth, and the implications are *chef’s kiss* terrifying.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Sui emerges from Li Huowang’s navel and complains about there being two dads (one headless, one with a head). Her father is too preoccupied to answer, focused on the supernatural chanting from the capital. The chanting is confirmed as real when commoners around him start collapsing.
- Li Huowang begins his return journey burdened by a headless corpse—his *own* corpse, which he intends to preserve as a future alchemical ingredient once he learns how to refine a Heart-Element. He drains the blood, salts it thoroughly, and carries it on his back.
- During the night, his hallucinations—the monk, Peng Longteng, Hong Zhong, and Jin Shan Zhao—start a conversation. Hong Zhong has been planting ideas in the monk’s head: what if Li Huowang can turn the hallucinations real? The discussion reveals Hong Zhong’s unsettling fascination with Li Huowang’s ability to blur reality and falsehood, and his admiration for Li Huowang’s impersonation of him.
- Li Huowang ignores the bait and sleeps through a snowstorm. He wakes buried in snow, chews some for hydration, and drags his frozen body into the capital.
- He buries his headless corpse on a slope outside the city, preserving it with salt and cold weather, then enters the capital. The city is quiet but not chaotic—the chanting incident didn’t disrupt public order.
- Nangong immediately intercepts him. Li Huowang tries to claim he was deceived by the Zuowandao, but Nangong’s expression warns him. He leads Li Huowang to a grim room in the Supervisory Heavenly Office.
- In that room: a square-jawed severed head sits on a table with three incense sticks before it. The head’s features are contorted into the face of Hong Zhong. A Daoist supervisor with two infant-like Nascent Souls on his neck (a Luo Sect cultivator) tosses the head to Li Huowang after examination.
- Li Huowang inspects the head, then delivers the bombshell: “What?! That Zuowandao Hong Zhong is actually a Heart-Element!?”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Heart-Element (心素, *xinsu*) — The Living Ingredient**  
In the Dao-Twisted World, a Heart-Element is a rare human constitution that makes its bearer an alchemical goldmine. The very concept originates from classical Daoist cosmology’s notion that “form has substance but has not yet become a body,” positioning Heart-Elements as primordial, unfinished matter—which certain twisted cultivators would kill to refine. The horror is that in this world, *you* can be the raw material for someone else’s breakthrough.

**Two Nascent Souls (元婴)—The Luo Sect Mark**  
In standard xianxia cultivation, a Nascent Soul is the condensed life-force of a high-level cultivator; having one is impressive. Having *two* protruding from your neck like conjoined infant skulls? That implies the Luo Sect (罗教), a heretical branch known for mimicking orthodox alchemy through grotesque bodily modifications. It signals that even the Department’s own supervisors may not be playing by the rules.

**Hong Zhong’s Face—The Square Jaw**  
Hong Zhong’s face is consistently described as *square-jawed*—a physically unusual trait that becomes a signature flaw. When Li Huowang impersonated him earlier, he had to mimic that feature. Now seeing it on a severed head means either the Zuowandao is doing something horrifying with duplicates… or that *this* version of Hong Zhong existed separately from the one Li Huowang met.

### Spirit-Summoning Verse (Bang Bing Jue)

A northeastern Chinese chuma ritual chant with an urgent, rapid beat, used to compel spirits into action—referenced earlier in the novel as a key mediumistic practice.

## Reading Guide

*Buckle up, because this is the kind of chapter that makes you want to immediately re-read the last twenty chapters.* The big reveal—that Hong Zhong may be a Heart-Element—completely reframes everything Li Huowang has been doing. If the Zuowandao can manufacture a fake Heart-Element head and have it worshipped with incense in a government building, then whose side is the Office really on? And more terrifyingly: who is the *real* Hong Zhong? The one Li Huowang met on the road, or the one currently sitting under incense smoke?  

Li Huowang’s relationship with his hallucinations also deepens here. Hong Zhong’s curiosity about “turning fakes real” is more than idle chat—it’s a philosophical grenade. If Li Huowang *can* make the unreal real, then where does that leave his identity as the only “real” person in the room?  

Also, quiet appreciation for the moment he salts his own corpse like a holiday ham. The man is thinking four moves ahead, and the casual *pragmatism* of that scene is peak *Dao Gui Yi Xian*—a character so desensitized to body horror that preparing his own future alchemical ingredient is just another task on the to-do list.
