## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a chapter that hits different, fellow Daoists. This isn’t about fighting monsters or brewing pills. Chapter 404 drops the plot hammer and forces us to stare directly at the core philosophical warp of the Dao-Twisted World: **the war between the True and the False**. Li Huowang tries to get a straight answer from Zhuge Yuan about the Great Liang vs. the Great Qi, and what he gets is a Socratic nightmare that questions the very nature of reality itself. If you’ve ever wondered why this world feels like a broken mirror, Zhuge Yuan is about to hand you the biggest piece of the puzzle—and it’s terrifying.

## Key Plot Points

- Zhuge Yuan casually offers Li Huowang some Fasting Pills, and Li Huowang warns him not to move against the minor Zuowandao yet, wanting to use him as bait for North Wind.
- The philosophical clash begins: Li Huowang insists the noodle-seller is a man of the Great Liang. Zhuge Yuan calmly insists he is a man of the Great Qi. Neither is hallucinating—they are seeing two different realities overlaid on the same spot.
- Zhuge Yuan reveals his core stance: the Great Qi has *always* been there, and the Great Liang is a false construct that must be corrected. He refuses to argue, telling Li Huowang to simply watch and learn.
- Li Huowang tries to untangle the mechanics of the Heart-Pivot, realizing that Zhuge Yuan’s bizarre “seen-and-not-seen” state is connected to whatever “Above the Three Bodies” is. He explicitly ties this to the Chief Recorder’s earlier cryptic warning.
- A young Ao-Jing follower named Xia Risheng approaches Li Huowang, admiring him for having completed the Dung-Beetle Ascension three times. Li Huowang brushes him off, wary of exposing his fake identity.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a masterclass in weaponizing a classic Chinese philosophical trope. The argument over the noodle-seller’s nationality isn’t just stubbornness—it’s a direct metaphor for *Zheng Ming* (正名), the Confucian doctrine of “rectifying names.” The idea is that if you call a thing by the wrong name, reality itself becomes disordered. Zhuge Yuan isn’t just wrong or right; he is operating on a different ontological layer where the *true* name of the world is “Great Qi,” and everything else is a corrupt overlay. This is the novel taking the standard xianxia concept of “seeking truth” (修真, xiū zhēn) and literally asking: *whose truth?* The Zuowandao, with their power to “cultivate the false into reality,” are the ideological antagonists to this worldview. Also, the Fasting Pill (辟谷丹, bì gǔ dān) is a real concept in Daoist internal alchemy, where adepts reduce food intake to purify the body; here it’s just a grimly practical travel ration.

### Cultivate the True/False (修真/修假)

The central philosophical dichotomy of the Dao-Twisted World: “seeking truth” (the standard xianxia path) vs. the Zuowandao’s power of “cultivating falsehood into reality,” which literally alters reality by believing in the lie.

### Four Joys (四喜)

The highest-ranked inner circle of the Zuowandao; their presence or mention signals a major escalation in the sect’s involvement.

## Reading Guide

This is a *thinker’s chapter*. If you’ve been breezing through the action, slow down here. The conversation between Li Huowang and Zhuge Yuan is the closest we’ve gotten to a direct statement about the nature of the entire universe we’ve been reading. Pay attention to the emotional beats: Zhuge Yuan is *disappointed* that Li Huowang doesn’t believe him. That’s not the face of a liar—it’s the weariness of a man who sees the real world and can’t make anyone else see it. Also, the introduction of Xia Risheng at the end is a low-key narrative signal: Li Huowang is being perceived as an Ao-Jing big shot, which will either give him leverage or get him into even deeper trouble. And one last thing—Mantou the dog licking the Fasting Pill and walking away? That’s comic relief, folks. Savor it while it lasts. The next storm is coming.

## Lore Deep Dive: Heart-Element, Heart-Pivot, Heart-Turbid

Here’s where the Dao-Twisted World’s cosmology takes a sharp left turn. Remember that Li Huowang is a **Heart-Element** (心素)—a person whose beliefs can literally reshape reality. Now we add the **Heart-Pivot** (心蟠)—an entity around whom the world itself seems to fold, creating layered, contradictory realities. Zhuge Yuan is a Heart-Pivot, and the constant “Great Qi vs. Great Liang” split is the symptom. The **Heart-Turbid** (心浊) is the corrupted counterpart, hiding or erasing things from reality. Together, they form a trio of reality-bending forces that the normal cultivation system simply cannot explain. The scariest part? Zhuge Yuan’s condition seems to be triggered by a higher power called “Above the Three Bodies” (三身旧之上)，and nobody—not even Li Huowang—understands what that entity governs. The Chief Recorder knew about this and warned Li Huowang cryptically. The stage is set for a revelation that may destroy everything we think we know about the map of this world.
