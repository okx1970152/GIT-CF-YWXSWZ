## Chapter Overview

This chapter firmly establishes the novel's dual-world structure. Li Huowang is a patient in a modern psychiatric hospital, yet his hallucinations are not mere delusions—they are a persistent, coherent alternate reality where he serves as an alchemist's apprentice. The visit from his girlfriend Yang Na provides a poignant anchor to normalcy, but the chapter's chilling final image—a piece of alchemical paste from the hallucination physically stuck to his chest—shatters any easy distinction between real and imagined. This is the novel's first overt violation of the boundary between worlds, setting the stage for Li Huowang's central dilemma: he is not merely ill; his reality is actually, terrifyingly porous.

## Key Plot Points

* Li Huowang is discharged from a vivid hallucination where he witnessed his grotesque master "Lai Zitou" murder and use a senior disciple for alchemical ingredients. He reports this matter-of-factly to his psychiatrist.
* His attending physician advises him not to analyze the content of the hallucinations, but to focus on recognizing them as false and maintaining logical thinking.
* Yang Na, Li Huowang's childhood friend and girlfriend, visits him in the hospital. She brings him study materials for the college entrance exam and a handheld game console, offering a glimpse of a normal, hopeful future.
* The couple shares a tender, brief moment together. Their relationship is established as a core emotional anchor for Li Huowang: a promise to attend the same university.
* After Yang Na leaves, Li Huowang's anxiety grows. He worries that worsening symptoms will prevent his discharge before the exams, destroying their shared future.
* In the dead of night, while studying, Li Huowang discovers a strange, dark-brown paste stuck to his chest.
* He tastes the paste and recognizes its bitter-sweet flavor: it is a piece of the alchemical "pill" given to him by the senior sister who was murdered in his hallucination. A physical object from the other world has crossed over into reality.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces the core premise via a modern medical lens, but its horror is rooted in a xianxia/alchemy framework. The hallucination describes the process of "refining a person into a pill" (ren dan / 人丹), a dark inversion of traditional Chinese alchemy where living humans are treated as raw ingredients. This is not a traditional alchemical pursuit of immortality, but a monstrous perversion of it. The master is called "Lai Zitou" (癞子头), or "Bald Scabby Head," a name that immediately signals his low, physically repulsive nature, far from the image of a refined immortal.

Beijing is the setting for the modern reality, grounding the story in a specific, contemporary Chinese context. The hospital and the psychiatric treatment represent a scientific-rational attempt to categorize and control Li Huowang's experiences. However, the novel subverts this by presenting the hallucinatory world with consistent internal logic and, crucially, by allowing physical matter to travel between the two domains. The appearance of the alchemical paste is a direct challenge to the doctor's diagnosis of "hallucinatory perceptual integration disorder." It is the story's first, visceral proof that Li Huowang is not simply insane in a conventional sense.

## Reading Guide

This is a foundational chapter. Pay close attention to how the tone shifts from the clinical detachment of the psychiatric report to the tender warmth of the romance, and finally to the cold dread of the supernatural intrusion. Li Huowang's internal conflict is not yet about survival in a fantasy world; it is about the fear of losing his grip on reality and, by extension, his future with Yang Na. The final image—tasting the paste from the dream—is the novel's thesis statement. From this point forward, the reader must be as suspicious of the "real" world as Li Huowang is. The author is telling you, very early on, that the boundary is permeable. The horror is not just in the other world, but in the fact that it can reach back.
