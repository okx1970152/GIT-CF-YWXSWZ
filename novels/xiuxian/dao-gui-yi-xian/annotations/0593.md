## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a chapter that flips the script on *The Dao-Twisted World*, fellow Daoists. After an eternity of visceral body horror and cosmic dread, our boy Li Huowang finally catches a break—sort of. This chapter is a slow, quiet, and deeply human breather set entirely in the modern-world psychiatric hospital. Watch as a gaunt and trembling Li Huowang struggles through mundane physical therapy, banters with his guards, and savors the fragile warmth of his mother’s care. But don’t let the domestic peace fool you. The calm surface is just bait for a seismic, reality-shaking bombshell that drops in the last few lines. A face from the past returns, and with a single, manic whisper, he threatens to drag Li Huowang—and everything we think we know—right back into the abyss of certainty.

## Key Plot Points
- The chapter opens with Li Huowang stubbornly refusing help to wash himself, a small but vital act of reclaiming his agency.
- His mother, Sun Xiaoqin, is his tireless cheerleader and nurse, pushing him through painful rehabilitation exercises and even massaging his aching muscles.
- The narrative reveals Sun Xiaoqin’s deepest fear: that her son would drift into lifelong madness and die alone and forgotten on the streets. Her frugality was a desperate act of insurance.
- The hospital’s vigilance on Li Huowang is visibly loosening, going from four guards down to two as he proves to be compliant and non-threatening.
- A quiet, almost comedic moment of bonding occurs between Li Huowang and the guard, Xiao Liu, as they debate the merits of TV romance dramas and the status of their respective love lives.
- The tension snaps back into place when Li Huowang recognizes a disguised figure in the bathroom: Wang Wei, his previous doctor who had descended into madness.
- Wang Wei grabs Li Huowang with insane fervor and whispers a revelation that threatens the very foundation of Li Huowang’s current peace: “I know! I know it’s real!!”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Rehabilitation (康复训练):** This chapter offers a starkly real depiction of post-illness physical therapy. For a protagonist who has survived dimension-hopping, flesh-warping horrors, the mundane pain of rebuilding basic motor skills—shaking hands, walking with a stool—is a powerful and grounding counterpoint.
- **The “Prison” Hospital:** The general public’s association of psychiatric wards with prisons is a real and poignant social issue in China. The guards, the handcuffs, the regulated routine—it all mirrors a carceral system, creating a visceral feeling of being trapped even when “safe.”
- **Heart-Element (心素) vs. The Patient:** This chapter starkly contrasts the two “Li Huowangs.” The weak, recovering patient is acutely aware that his other self—the Heart-Element cultivator from the Dao-Twisted World—could crush him with a finger. This is a brilliant narrative trick, making the protagonist feel simultaneously vulnerable and impossibly powerful.

## Reading Guide
Alright, deep breaths. After the relentless chaos of the Dao-Twisted World, this chapter is a cool glass of water—but it’s spiked with a truth serum you might not want to drink. The core tension here isn’t a monster; it’s the terrifying possibility of *peace*. Is Li Huowang’s improvement genuine, or is it just the calm before a more profound mental storm? The chapter masterfully lulls you into the warm, domestic groove of a mother’s love and a boring TV show, only to sucker-punch you with Wang Wei’s return. His single line—“I know it’s real!!”—isn’t just a threat; it’s a promise. It’s the novel reminding you that in *Dao Gui Yi Xian*, no reality is ever truly secure. Pay close attention to Li Huowang’s quiet, almost unsettling acceptance of his “normal” life. Is he truly getting better, or is he just conserving his strength?
