## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang finally relaxes—the scope of the Heavenly Calamity is someone else’s bureaucratic headache. The village throws a beef feast (illegal, delicious), and for a moment, life is almost normal. Then Gouwa kneels with his newborn daughter, and the normal cracks. But the real wound isn’t external: it’s between Li Huowang and Bai Lingmiao. A single well-placed bluff rips open a chasm of mutual deception neither of them can fill. And just as the emotional dust begins to settle, the gate that was locked swings open by itself, and a figure in a burial shroud stands in the corner, its proportions all wrong. The feast is over.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang learns that the Supervisory Heavenly Office is expected to handle the Heavenly Calamity’s darkness, relieving his immediate anxiety.
- The village slaughters an ox and shares a celebratory meal, marking a rare moment of communal warmth.
- Gouwa approaches Li Huowang on his knees, begging for a cure for his daughter’s congenital vitiligo. Li Huowang refuses coldly, stating it is not a supernatural ailment and he cannot help.
- Li Huowang confronts Bai Lingmiao using a bluff, forcing her to admit she has been hiding her worsening eyesight and other physical sacrifices from him.
- The argument escalates into a reciprocal accusation: Bai Lingmiao reveals she knows Li Huowang’s madness has never been cured and that he goes to Ox Heart Mountain to have episodes.
- Their quarrel is interrupted by Mantou’s aggressive, panicked barking at the courtyard gate.
- Li Huowang climbs the wall to reconnoiter and spots a man in burial clothes—grotesquely proportioned, with a giant pear-shaped head—standing motionless in a shadow. He identifies it as an evil entity.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Bluffing as a Detection Skill (诈术):** In Chinese interpersonal dynamics, especially within the jianghu and family settings, a deliberate “bluff” or “诈” is a recognized investigative technique. It is not considered inherently dishonest in the same way a lie is; it is a tactical probe used to confirm suspicion. Li Huowang’s line “You just told me” is a perfect example—he exploits the assumption that he *already knows* to force a guilty party to self-corroborate. This mirrors classical Chinese detective tropes where judges or heroes trick suspects into confessing through staged whispers or empty threats.

**The Significance of Beef (吃牛肉):** Throughout imperial Chinese history, the state often protected oxen as agricultural assets. Slaughtering an ox without official permission was a punishable offense in many dynasties, making beef a rare, illicit luxury. The villagers’ nervousness (“This is against the law”) reflects this deep-rooted cultural taboo against wasting a plow animal. Their excitement at eating beef also reveals the normal human hunger beneath the supernatural dread.

**Vitiligo in Folk Belief:** Congenital skin pigmentation conditions like vitiligo were historically misunderstood in rural China. They were sometimes attributed to curses, bad karma, or spirit interference, though Li Huowang’s clinical, mundane diagnosis here is notable for how it **refuses** to supernaturalize the condition. This aligns with the novel’s theme that the world’s cruelty is often ordinary, hereditary, and carries no mystical cure.

### vitiligo

A skin condition causing permanent white patches; the horror here is that it is purely congenital and natural, with no supernatural cure available.

### bluffing (诈术)

A deliberate investigative tactic common in Chinese interpersonal dynamics where one pretends to know a secret to force a confirmation.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a quiet crucible of domestic tension punctured by a sudden folk-horror intrusion. The beef feast scene functions as a deceptive hearth—communal, illegal, and delicious—before the emotional fire erupts. Pay attention to the asymmetry of secrets: Li Huowang knows about Bai Lingmiao’s eyes and lifespan cost, but she also knows his illness never healed. This mutual surveillance is not betrayal; it’s survival love, where both parties hide the truth to protect the other from worry. The final image of the entity in burial clothes is a textbook Dao Twisted horror hit: a grotesque, silent silhouette that breaks the domestic spell instantly.
