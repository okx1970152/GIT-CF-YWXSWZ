## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang finally reaches Xijing and locates Zhengde Temple—the very monastery that once hunted Danyangzi. But he quickly realizes that stepping into a holy site doesn't mean stepping into safety. From the polite but guarded monk Jian Dun to the albino abbot Xin Hui, every interaction is layered with unspoken tension. Li Huowang lays out the entire nightmare at Qingfeng Temple: Danyangzi's horrific "ascension," the maddening voice in his ear, even the Xi Shen being dragged back into the dark by *something*. And then, with the weight of five golden Buddhas watching over them, the abbot delivers his verdict: Danyangzi didn't become an immortal. He became a Buddha. The implications are as grand as they are terrifying.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang's detective work pays off. He learns that Zhengde Temple is indeed the monastery that hunted Danyangzi, confirming his journey's direction.
- The chapter opens with a vivid contrast: the bustling, incense-shrouded splendor of Zhengde Temple versus the crude, bloody cave of Qingfeng Temple. The piety here *looks* real—but *is* it?
- Monk Jian Dun approaches Li Huowang first, drawn by his conspicuously out-of-place Daoist robe. The conversation is cautious, with Li Huowang opting for a selective version of the truth.
- Li Huowang reveals Danyangzi's death. The simple news causes Jian Dun to drop and scatter his prayer beads—a powerful visceral reaction from a man trained in composure.
- Li Huowang recounts the rest: the hallucinatory voice of Danyangzi after death, the Xi Shen being "pulled back into the darkness," and his growing suspicion that Danyangzi's ascension was *not* a transcendence, but a transformation into something else entirely.
- Jian Dun, shaken, leaves to consult the abbot. The speed of his return underscores the gravity of the news.
- The grand reveal. Inside the main hall, under the gaze of five colossal golden Buddhas (the Five Tathagatas of Wisdom), Abbot Xin Hui greets Li Huowang. Instead of answering his question directly, Xin Hui first delivers a lesson on Buddhist iconography, deliberately building suspense.
- The final blow: "He has become a Buddha." A line that recontextualizes everything Li Huowang—and the reader—thought they knew about Danyangzi's fate.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Zhengde Temple (正德寺).** This is not a remote mountain monastery. It's a powerful, urban temple in the capital city of Xijing. Its prominence is reinforced by the sheer scale: hundreds of steps, thick incense smoke, and overwhelming crowds. In the Dao-Twisted World, such opulence and public piety are often a mask for something far darker lurking beneath.

**Five Tathagatas of Wisdom (五智如来).** This is a Vajrayana Buddhist concept representing five aspects of enlightened wisdom, each embodied by a Buddha in a cardinal direction plus the center. Abbot Xin Hui's decision to name them all before answering Li Huowang's question is deliberate. On one level, it suggests institutional orthodoxy. On another, it's a form of psychological pacing—forcing Li Huowang to sit with the weight of the sacred before receiving an answer that will *break* that sacredness.

**Albinism as a narrative thread.** Abbot Xin Hui shares Bai Lingmiao's condition. In the Dao-Twisted World, albinism is repeatedly associated with unusual spiritual sensitivity or value. This is not a coincidence; it's a subtle thread linking characters who occupy different points on the cosmic horror spectrum of this world.

**"Become a Buddha" vs. "Become an Immortal."** In traditional Chinese religious thought, these are distinct paths. Immortality (Daoist) implies longevity, transcendence, and harmony with nature. Buddhahood implies enlightenment and escape from the cycle of rebirth. In the Dao-Twisted World, both paths can be *corrupted*. Danyangzi craving immortality and supposedly "becoming a Buddha" suggests a grotesque fusion—or simply the next layer of the novel's central mystery: what does it mean for a monster to "ascend"?

### 'Become a Buddha' (成佛)

In this novel's context, a terrifying redefinition of a sacred Buddhist term. It does not here refer to enlightenment, but to a monstrous transformation of a being after death, carrying grave implications for the Dao-Twisted World.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow Daoists, Li Huowang finally hit the jackpot. After weeks of traveling, he's walked right into the lion's den—Zhengde Temple. And let me tell you, this chapter is a masterclass in how *not* to let your guard down just because you're in a sacred space.

You'd think entering a bustling, incense-drenched temple with golden Buddhas everywhere would be a relief after the flesh pits of Qingfeng Temple. Think again. The moment the prayer beads hit the floor and Jian Dun's composure cracks, you know the rot runs just as deep here. Maybe deeper. The piety is polished, the rituals are perfect—and that's exactly what makes it suspicious.

Abbot Xin Hui is the star of this scene. Kind face, pink eyes, elegant speech, and the patience of a saint. But when you're a reader of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*, you learn to fear the gentle ones the most. He doesn't answer Li Huowang's question until he's made him *sit* with the enormity of the Five Buddhas, their cold golden gazes pressing down. That's power play disguised as religious instruction.

And then the punchline. "He has become a Buddha." Not an immortal. Not a demon. *A Buddha.* If you're not already dreading what that means in this universe, you haven't been paying attention. Danyangzi the illiterate madman, the flesh-crafter, the voice that whispers from beyond death—he just got promoted to the highest tier of religious status. And Li Huowang is standing in the temple of the people who hunted him.

Brace yourselves. The theological horror is about to get a whole lot worse.
