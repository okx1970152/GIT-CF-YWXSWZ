## Chapter Overview

Hold onto your seats, fellow Daoists, because this chapter doesn’t just turn the screws—it rips the entire panel out. Li Huowang is trapped, caught in the gaze of the Yin-Yang Doumu herself, and the “re-education” is terrifyingly personal. This isn’t a simple mental attack; it’s a full identity theft, a systematic unpacking of every memory he holds dear to replace them with someone else’s. The chapter is a brutal, high-stakes showdown between Li Huowang’s fragile sense of self and a cosmic entity that treats his life story as a draft script to be overwritten. Get ready for a heart-wrenching sacrifice and a final, desperate gambit that leaves the truth hanging by a thread.

## Key Plot Points

1. **Under the Gaze of Doumu:** Li Huowang realizes he is being targeted by the Yin-Yang Doumu. The ground turns to liquid, the sky inverts, and he is physically pulled toward her.
2. **Identity Collapse:** A cascade of eerie, intrusive memories that feel *too real* begins to flood Li Huowang’s mind—childhood fears, a pawnshop apprenticeship, his fall into poverty, and the origin of his connection to the Zuowandao. These memories are not his own; they are Hong Zhong’s.
3. **Losing His Face:** The logic “you cannot imagine what you have never known” is turned against him. The new memories are proof of a fabricated past. Li Huowang’s face physically melts and reforms into Hong Zhong’s, then into the faces of his companions, as his own identity fades.
4. **Zhuge Yuan’s Stand:** Seeing Li Huowang on the brink of being erased, Zhuge Yuan intervenes. He sacrifices his sword and uses his Infernal Judge’s Brush to write a “year-forbidden” talisman against Siming manifestation, temporarily weakening Doumu.
5. **The Core Revelation:** As Zhuge Yuan urges him to flee, Li Huowang refuses, having deduced a crucial truth: the Dice can only summon Doumu’s *manifestation* via the Wangtian Precious Declaration, not her true body. This implies there is still a way to fight or escape that hasn’t been tried.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Doumu (斗姥):** The Yin-Yang Doumu appears in this chapter not merely as a god but as an **ontological hazard**. In Daoist tradition, Doumu is the Mother of the Big Dipper, a powerful astral deity associated with life, destiny, and salvation. The novel completely corrupts this by presenting her as an impersonal, dissolving force. Directly observing her image is like looking into an abyss that actively unravels your self. Her gaze doesn’t just hurt; it *re-knits your past*, showing that in the Dao-Twisted World, even one’s personal history is not a safe, private archive but an attack surface for cosmic entities.

**Siming Manifestation vs. True Body:** This is a critical lore drop. The fact that the Zuowandao can only summon a *manifestation* (化身) of a high Siming like Doumu, but not her true body (真身), is a core rule of the Dao-Twisted World’s hierarchy. It suggests that divine entities are bound by their own constraints—perhaps a projection is all that can touch the lower realms without collapsing reality. Li Huowang’s desperate use of this knowledge is a key tactical insight.

**Year-Forbidden Talisman (忌日):** Zhuge Yuan uses an old imperial calendar (老皇历) to declare the day “forbidden for Siming manifestation.” This is a wonderful example of turning mundane Chinese folk ritual into a weapon. Imperial calendars were used to determine auspicious and inauspicious days for actions. Zhuge Yuan weaponizes this logic, trying to impose a cosmic taboo directly onto a Siming. It doesn’t defeat her, but it dims her—showing that even gods have to *pay attention* to the rules of fate and time.

## Reading Guide

Brace yourself for a chapter that feels like a fever dream. The horror here isn’t a monster; it’s the idea of your own memories being rewritten in real time, of looking in a mirror and seeing a stranger’s face. This is the deepest attack on Li Huowang’s “Heart-Element” identity yet—not physical pain, but the erasure of his soul.

Zhuge Yuan’s sacrifice is the emotional core. He knows he’s outclassed by a Siming, but he acts anyway, not for a logical reason but for a purely human one: “To know a person is precious in knowing their heart.” In a world of liars, monsters, and cosmic horrors, this simple, stubborn act of friendship is what gives Li Huowang the one thing he truly needs: time to think. The final cliffhanger is less about “will they survive?” and more about “what is Li Huowang now?”

Pay attention to the hallucinatory Zuowandao’s final line. It’s a taunt, but also a piece of intel. The real horror is that he might be right—it might be impossible to fight a Siming’s true body. But the bigger horror for Li Huowang is that he might have to try anyways, because running away means losing the one person who just proved that loyalty still exists in this broken world.
