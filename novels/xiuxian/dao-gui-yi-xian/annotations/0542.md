## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists, because this is the kind of chapter where the *real* horror isn't a monster—it's the broken person who follows you home. Li Huowang is on a time-sensitive quest to find the mysterious Supreme Irrigation Outlet, but he's dragging along a new, unwelcome passenger: Qiu Chibao, a starved, mad woman who died near him and has now become a permanent, hungry hallucination. As he navigates a cold, dark pool in search of a hidden dimension, he's forced to confront the quiet, grinding nightmare that his own mind is now a graveyard for the desperate and the damned. This is a chapter of cold water, colder truths, and the slow, weary acceptance that his hallucinations are a burden he can never put down.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is haunted by a new hallucination: Qiu Chibao, a starving mother who died near him. She is trapped in a cycle of hunger and despair, unable to touch real food, and her frantic wailing is a constant, grating presence.
- Li Huowang tries to silence her by giving her his last rations, knowing it won't actually help—but it's the only way to get a moment of peace.
- He mentally catalogs his growing list of hallucinations (Peng Longteng, Jin Shan Zhao, Zhuge Yuan, the Monk, Jiang Yingzi, and now Qiu Chibao) and tries to find a pattern for how they form, but the rules are inconsistent and incomplete.
- Zhuge Yuan redirects him to the urgent task: finding the Supreme Irrigation Outlet, which can only be accessed during the Hour of the Rat (midnight) via a deep, frozen pool.
- Li Huowang leaps into the pool with a rock to sink faster, using a fluorescent stone to push back the darkness and whatever slimy things lurk within.
- The chapter ends with Li Huowang descending into the abyss, while Qiu Chibao's screams provide a soundtrack of drowning despair.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Hour of the Rat (子时 / Zi Shi)**
This is a key concept in Chinese temporal cosmology. The day is divided into twelve two-hour periods, each ruled by a symbolic animal. The Hour of the Rat (11 PM – 1 AM) is considered the first hour of the new day and is associated with yang energy beginning to grow. In xianxia and folklore, certain rituals, treasures, or portals only appear at specific hours, making time-discipline a survival skill for cultivators.

**The Supreme Irrigation Outlet (上极灌口)**
The name "Irrigation Outlet" sounds like a mundane aquatic structure, but in this novel's logic, it is a hidden, shifting dimensional entry point. The character "灌" (irrigate) carries connotations of pouring, flooding, and channeling water, while "口" (mouth/outlet) suggests a threshold or gateway. The idea that a cosmic portal is disguised as a simple, natural water feature—and moves based on the Chinese clock—adds a layer of eerie, bureaucratic precision to the supernatural.

**Hallucination Mechanics**
Li Huowang's condition as a Heart-Element means his emotional state near the moment of someone's death can "fix" them as a permanent hallucination. This is not a generic power; it is an unwanted curse. The inconsistency of the pattern (some are close, some are strangers; some are clearly dead, some might not be) reinforces the novel's central theme: the rules of this world are broken, and Li Huowang's own cognition is the battlefield where reality and delusion fight to a bloody draw.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in low-key horror. There are no screaming monsters, no explosive battles—just the cold, sinking feeling that comes when you realize you can’t even control the ghosts in your own head. Pay close attention to how Li Huowang handles Qiu Chibao. He doesn’t try to banish her or fight her; he just gives her food to shut her up. That’s a man who has learned to manage his madness, not cure it.

The introduction of the Supreme Irrigation Outlet is a classic Dao-Twisted World touch: a hidden realm that only appears at a specific Chinese hour, accessible only by sinking into a freezing, bottomless pool. It’s part adventure, part suicide attempt. And the final image—Qiu Chibao drowning forever while Li Huowang descends into the dark—is a perfect, haunting blend of horror and pathos. This is the kind of chapter that sticks with you, not because of what happens, but because of what it feels like: being dragged down by the weight of the dead.
