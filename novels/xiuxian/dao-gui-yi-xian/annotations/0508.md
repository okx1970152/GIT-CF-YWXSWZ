## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because Chapter 507 is a masterclass in psychological warfare where the battlefield is Li Huowang’s own mind. After recovering from a suicide attempt, our battered protagonist is now trapped in the Dice’s game, forced to cooperate in a political coup while Bai Lingmiao is held hostage. But the Dice’s most insidious trap isn’t physical—it’s the doubt he’s planted. Li Huowang must now perform an emergency triage on his own hallucinations, hunting for the impostor hiding among his spectral companions. The question of “what is real” takes center stage, and Li Huowang’s answer is as brutal as it is unshakable: he will burn down his own inner world to save the people he loves.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the Dice, in a moment of grotesque domesticity, patching up Li Huowang’s wounds in a carriage—an image as tender as it is menacing. The Dice casually drops the mission: get Ji Lin on the throne. It’s a political chess move, and Li Huowang is the piece being moved. Despite the Dice’s disarming friendliness, Li Huowang sees the trap clearly: return Bai Lingmiao is not part of the plan. The Dice is a liar, and keeping a promise would be an insult to his profession.

Li Huowang’s response is not despair, but a cold, tactical assessment. He knows he needs allies, perhaps Ji Lin or the Supervisory Heavenly Office, but the odds are impossibly stacked. The mountain of pressure feels crushing, yet his hatred fuels a burning resolve.

Then, the true battleground shifts. The faceless hallucination, still trying to dig at him, suggests the Dice might be telling the truth. Li Huowang snuffs that doubt out immediately. The crisis escalates when he confronts the Flesh-and-Blood Zhuge Yuan, who has reanimated from the spine sword. This Zhuge Yuan offers help, but Li Huowang hears deception.

In a frantic, deductive outburst, Li Huowang lays out his new theory: the Second Spirit is not a hallucination absorbed by his Heart-Element powers; she was taken. The Dice’s entire narrative is a lie. He then delivers the final blow: this Zhuge Yuan’s personality doesn't match the original. With a talisman, he silences him, shrinking the head back to a grotesque, fist-sized knot.

But the horror doesn’t end. The *other* Zhuge Yuan—the one he trusts—watches on, calmly deducing the same paradox. Whether he is the fake, the sword is the fake, or both are fakes standing before a real Zhuge Yuan who has already transcended, is a puzzle left hanging in the air. Li Huowang wraps the now-bloated spine sword in blood-soaked cloth, its grotesque contents (peach-wood talismans, bones, hearts, black hair) barely hidden, and gives the command: “To the capital!” The chapter ends with the Dice’s square head reappearing, enjoying the chaos he has wrought.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of “Innate Pneuma” (先天一炁, Xiāntiān Yīqì) is crucial for understanding Li Huowang’s breakthrough earlier. In Daoist internal alchemy, this is the fundamental, undifferentiated energy of the universe that exists before creation. It’s not a power you cultivate; it’s the seed of reality itself. For a Heart-Element to access it is to touch the raw code of existence, which is both a terrifying power and a terrifying vulnerability.

The *Thousand Greats Record* (《大千录》) is also worth revisiting. It’s not a “spell book” in the Western sense. It’s a suffering-cult manual where power is extracted through intense, literal agony, paid to the god Bashe. Every technique is a transaction of pain, and the “Intercalating the Five Phases” ritual is a prime example: it doesn’t just heal you; it overwrites your body’s fundamental nature by replacing your organs with writhing flesh-spirits. Rereading this section after Chapter 507 gives you a whole new appreciation for the cost of Li Huowang’s power.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a psychological slugfest. Don’t skim the dialogue between Li Huowang and his hallucinations. Every line is a feint and a parry. Pay close attention to how Li Huowang uses logic to fight lies, mirroring the rational framework of his doctor from the modern world. He’s doing the same thing—using a system of inference to build a personal truth—except his “truth” is that the supernatural is real.

The true horror here is not the Dice’s power, but the pervasiveness of the doubt he’s injected. Li Huowang is forced to become a forensic psychiatrist of his own soul, and the cost is the serene certainty of the innocent. He can no longer take his own allies at face value. This chapter sets up a new phase for the story: the hunt for Bai Lingmiao will be a war of perception, not just a war of power.
