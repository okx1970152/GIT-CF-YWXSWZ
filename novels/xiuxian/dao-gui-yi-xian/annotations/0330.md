## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow readers—this chapter is a knife to the heart, delivered with the coldest possible hand. Our focus shifts entirely to Bai Lingmiao on the night of the Moon Festival. She returns to her family’s ancestral hall, distributes mooncakes to the spirit tablets of her slaughtered kin, and tries to summon them through the Bei School’s *Crying Smoke-Soul Tune*. The ritual works—briefly, devastatingly—before the silence confirms the worst: they are gone, and she believes they blame her. This chapter isn't about monsters or cultivators; it’s about a grief so bottomless that a woman makes a terrible, quiet choice, with the Da Shen as her sole, unnervingly passive witness. Brace yourself for a masterclass in atmosphere and emotional horror.

## Key Plot Points
1. **Moon Festival Ritual at the Ancestral Hall**: Bai Lingmiao begins by personally placing mooncakes at the spirit tablets of her father, brother, and grandfather, lighting incense and speaking to them as if they are present—establishing the Lunar New Year’s Eve of grief.
2. **Gazing at the Earthen Kiln**: She observes the traditional Moon Festival “kiln burning” in the distance, a sight that triggers a flood of memories about her younger brother, whom she raised.
3. **Summoning via “Crying Smoke-Soul” (哭烟魂调)** : Overcome by longing, she beats her drum and begins to sing the Bei School spirit-summoning tune. This time, she feels a genuine emotional resonance with the lyrics, surpassing simple mimicry.
4. **The Moment of Connection**: The yin wind rises, the spirit tablets rattle, and the ancestral hall seems to come alive. A presence enters the Da Shen through her red veil, and Bai Lingmiao hears her mother’s voice, embracing the entity while pouring out her heart.
5. **The Cruel Confirmation**: When the singing stops, the presence is gone. Bai Lingmiao asks the Da Shen if her mother said anything. The Da Shen’s reply is utterly cold: “She wasn’t your mother…”
6. **Bai Lingmiao’s Confession and Final Act**: Broken, Bai Lingmiao reveals she can’t go on. She asks the Da Shen to take care of Li Huowang after she’s gone. The Da Shen calmly helps her prepare a noose on the roof beam and then sits down to watch the moon as Bai Lingmiao hangs herself in the background.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Moon Festival / Mid-Autumn Festival (月夕)**: The “Moon Festival” is a poetic name for the Mid-Autumn Festival, one of the most important traditional Chinese holidays, centered on family reunion. The irony here is brutal: Bai Lingmiao’s family is dead, and she is “reuniting” with their spirit tablets.
**Bei School (悲家) & The “Crying Smoke-Soul Tune” (哭烟魂调)**: In chuma spirit-mediumship, different “schools” (domains) have specific repertoires. The “Bei School” (悲, meaning “sorrow”) seems specialized in summoning the dead and expressing grief. The *Crying Smoke-Soul Tune* is a ritualized wail used to call upon the souls of the deceased. The novel uses this real folk tradition to create a situation where a medium’s genuine, raw trauma makes the ritual *more* powerful, a chilling inversion of meditative calm.
**The Da Shen’s Role (大神)**: The “Da Shen” is the primary spirit-medium’s possessing entity, a non-human consciousness. In this chapter, the Da Shen acts as a perfect, passive conduit. She doesn’t grieve; she doesn’t judge. She helps Bai Lingmiao prepare for death with the same clinical detachment she would use to light an incense stick. This makes her complicity in the suicide all the more horrifying—it is the act of a being for whom human life is just another ritual step.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is pure, heartbreaking atmosphere. Pay close attention to the sensory details: the taste of the mooncake, the distant glow of the kiln fire, the smell of burnt silk on the Da Shen’s robes, and the final, chilling creak of the rope. The horror here is not jump-scares; it’s the slow, relentless pressure of grief. Bai Lingmiao’s decision to break down only *after* completing the ritual, and the Da Shen’s silent assistance, is one of the most devastating moments in the entire arc. The message is clear: in the Dao-Twisted World, even the gods of comfort are indifferent to your pain. If you’ve been following Bai Lingmiao’s journey from the timid girl of the early chapters, this is the tragic culmination of her growth. It’s not a battle loss; it’s a soul loss.
