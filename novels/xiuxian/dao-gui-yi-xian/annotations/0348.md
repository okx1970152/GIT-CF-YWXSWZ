## Chapter Overview

The chapter opens with the group splitting off in the Bai village. While Bai Lingmiao goes to the ancestral hall to offer incense, a restless Chun Xiaoman follows her. Her curiosity lands on a three-monkey bronze statue, which triggers a hidden wall mechanism, revealing a secret underground chamber carved with an entire heretical mythology. Gao Zhijian is brought in to read the walls. What he translates aloud is nothing less than a full-fledged origin myth of Wusheng Laomu (the Unborn Venerable Mother)—her betrayal by the "blood princes," the theft of her divinity, and a desperate, multi-generational oath of resistance. This hidden scripture re-contextualizes everything about Bai Lingmiao and sets the stage for a cosmic conflict far beyond what Li Huowang has been dealing with.

## Key Plot Points

- Chun Xiaoman follows Bai Lingmiao into the Bai family ancestral hall out of unease and curiosity, breaking her usual habit of avoidance.
- A three-monkey bronze statue (hear no evil, see no evil, speak no evil) is actually a concealed mechanism; when Chun Xiaoman touches it, a hidden wall collapses to reveal a cold, dark staircase.
- Bai Lingmiao is drawn into the opening in a trance, but Chun Xiaoman takes the lead, revealing her assertive, protective instincts.
- Gao Zhijian is summoned and descends fully armed. The hidden chamber proves to be a vast underground space, far larger than the hall above.
- The walls and a preserved book contain a foundational myth of the White Lotus / Wusheng Laomu religion, carved in semi-archaic script.
- Gao Zhijian's stuttered translation fully unveils the narrative: Wusheng Laomu was betrayed by the "blood princes" (天潢贵胄) and the "Unspeakable armies" who emerged from her own dantian.
- The heretical scripture ends with a declaration that as long as one descendant of the blood princes remains, the hope of Wusheng Laomu’s return will endure.
- This chapter acts as a monumental lore drop, reshaping the supernatural political landscape of the Dao-Twisted World.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Wusheng Laomu (无生老母) & the White Lotus**: The "Unborn Venerable Mother" is a central figure in late Ming and Qing dynasty sectarian folk religion, particularly within the White Lotus tradition. She is often depicted as a primordial creator who sent her children (human souls) into the mundane world and longs to bring them back to the "Native Land of True Emptiness" (真空家乡). This chapter's text reads almost like a direct adaptation of those scriptures—a story of a betrayed mother-goddess whose history was erased by usurpers. In the context of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*, this isn't presented as a "good" or "evil" theology; it's a dangerous, hidden truth that recontextualizes Bai Lingmiao's own nature. The text being carved in a secret chamber under the Bai ancestral hall suggests that the Bai family—or at least a branch of it—has been preserving this forbidden history for generations.

**The Three Monkeys**: The bronze statue of three monkeys (covering ears, eyes, and mouth) is the famous "see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil" motif, which is actually of Japanese origin but widely assimilated into Chinese folk art. Its use here as a hidden switch is a clever narrative inversion: the monkeys represent the concealment of a "forbidden truth" that the mechanism is designed to hide.

**"Blood Princes" (天潢贵胄)**: The term *tianhuang guizhou* traditionally refers to members of the imperial clan or highest nobility. In this twisted theology, it's used for the lieutenants or emanations of Wusheng Laomu who betrayed her. The text's claim that they "rewrote history in everyone's hearts" is a devastatingly meta-horror concept—this is a story about a god whose very existence was edited out of reality, waiting to be restored.

## Reading Guide

This is a **worldbuilding lore bomb** straight out of a secret scripture. Unlike previous chapters where Li Huowang was the one discovering hidden rules, here the focus is entirely on Bai Lingmiao's faction. The revelation is framed as a religious text, which means it's *potentially true, potentially propaganda, or potentially both*. The "Unspeakable armies" (说不得大军) are named—and that name alone is a chilling promise. The chapter sets the stage for a "divine restoration" plotline that will inevitably pull Bai Lingmiao into a role far larger than "the girl who cares for Li Huowang." The writing is deliberately archaic and fanatical, so read it as if you're an anthropologist uncovering a forbidden cult's manifesto. And remember: in the Dao-Twisted World, a "religious text" is not just a metaphor—it can be a blueprint for a war.
