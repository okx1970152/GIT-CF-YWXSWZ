## Chapter Overview

*Translation Bites delivers the next critical chapter in Li Huowang’s survival campaign!* Our boy just got promoted from prisoner to **close-door disciple**—and he is already cooking up the most evil chemistry experiment in the Dao-Twisted World. In Chapter 18, Li Huowang puts his plotting into high gear. He seizes the power vacuum left by the Vase-Girl’s death and rewrites the entire Heavenly Scripture to his own advantage. But Danyangzi isn’t a complete fool—the master’s paranoia runs deep, and he now insists they “become immortals together,” a promise that sounds more like a death warrant than an honor. The chapter is a masterclass in calculated deception: from outright fabricating alchemical formulas to maintaining his cover with the other prisoners, Li Huowang is walking a razor’s edge, and every step could be his last.

## Key Plot Points

- Danyangzi, enraged by Zheng Kun’s betrayal, makes a swift decision: he promotes Li Huowang to close-door disciple, skipping all formalities and forcing him to kowtow to the ancestral statues.
- Li Huowang immediately exploits the Vase-Girl’s death, claiming the Heavenly Scripture she interpreted was a complete fabrication—a high-risk gambit that flips the script.
- Danyangzi’s paranoid nature works in Li Huowang’s favor; the more he thinks about the discrepancies, the more he believes Li Huowang’s story over the Vase-Girl’s.
- To prove his loyalty, Li Huowang invents a new cultivation method on the spot, mixing Daoist jargon with intentional deadly ingredients (cinnabar, arsenic, and the *Wandering Lord*).
- Danyangzi, ever the cautious tyrant, insists they cultivate *together*—a clear sign he intends to use Li Huowang as a test subject for the new alchemy.
- Li Huowang returns to the supply room, where the freed prisoners celebrate like it’s New Year’s; a scene of rare warmth in an otherwise cold cave.
- Gouwa claims credit for his earlier performance (the punch that sealed Zheng Kun’s fate), and Bai Lingmiao quietly checks on Li Huowang’s well-being, showing her growing attachment.
- The chapter ends with Li Huowang’s cold resolve: he needs to make the alchemical formula even deadlier, using the *Wandering Lord* and other sinister ingredients to ensure Danyangzi’s complete destruction.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Close-Door Disciple (关门弟子):** This is a huge deal in Chinese sect lore. Unlike ordinary *registered disciples* (记名弟子) who do chores and learn scraps, a *close-door disciple* is the master’s final, most personally taught student—basically getting the entire family recipe. It’s a massive trust signal. Danyangzi is handing Li Huowang the keys to the kingdom (or the poison cabinet). The irony is, the master is putting the fox in charge of the henhouse.

**Internal & External Alchemy (内丹/外丹):** In authentic Daoist practice, *Internal Alchemy* works on refining qi and spirit within the body; *External Alchemy* involves concocting physical pills from minerals, metals, and herbs. Li Huowang’s claim that the Heavenly Scripture lacks Internal Alchemy is a critical blow to Danyangzi’s belief system. The real kicker? The ingredients Li Huowang mentions—cinnabar (a mercury ore), arsenic (a lethal poison), and white croton (a violently purgative herb)—are historically known toxins in improper alchemy. He is literally writing a suicide prescription for his master.

**Heavenly Scripture (天书):** This concept goes way back in Chinese folklore. It’s a celestial text of ultimate secrets, usually said to be received directly from the gods, not written by human hands. Danyangzi’s illiteracy and his reliance on a child to “read” the stone slab reveals the tragic flaw of his whole operation: he’s been worshipping a manual he can’t even read, and now the new translator is deliberately doctoring every line.

**Ritual of Three Bows and Nine Kowtows (三礼九叩):** This is the highest form of ritual prostration in Daoist and Confucian tradition. Doing it to the ancestral statues confirms the master-disciple bond in a binding, almost legal sense. Li Huowang’s performance of this ritual is both a mockery and a shield—it buys him legitimacy while he plots the master’s murder.

## Reading Guide

*Get ready for some serious scheming, cultivators!* This chapter is where Li Huowang officially graduates from punching bag to puppet master. If you loved the tension in the previous chapter, buckle up—because now the lies are getting *layered*. The real treat here is watching Li Huowang’s psychological evolution: he’s not just trying to survive anymore; he’s actively enjoying the game. The way he casually invents a fake cultivation method and watches Danyangzi eat it up is deeply satisfying, but there’s a shadow over every victory. The master’s promise to “cultivate together” is an unspoken threat—he’s already planning to use Li Huowang as a test subject. Pay close attention to the final lines: Li Huowang’s determination to add the *Wandering Lord* to the formula isn’t just clever poison-making. It’s the first sign he’s fully crossing the line from reactive victim to active killer. The ensemble vibe with the other prisoners also gets a beautiful moment of warmth, giving us a brief but needed break from the dread. This chapter is a clinic in how to write a slow-burn betrayal—step by step, ingredient by ingredient.
