## Chapter Overview
Our boy Li Huowang is getting pulled into the big leagues! After a frantic horse ride through rainy Yinling City, he’s summoned by his handler Tuoba Danqing to the city’s most foreboding prison. The mission? It’s finally a hit job against the Zuowandao—the very cult of liars that’s been haunting his every step. But nothing in the Dao-Twisted World is ever as simple as it sounds. Alongside the grouchy debt-credit seller Hong Da, a mysterious "Chief Recorder" who carries a gold abacus and smells oddly sweet, and a prisoner locked in the deepest cell, this team is shaping up to be a powder keg with a very short fuse. The chapter hums with tension as Li Huowang realizes he’s about to step into a job with stakes so high, even a high-ranking eunuch from the capital had to ride out personally.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Summons**: Li Huowang receives an urgent carrier pigeon message from Tuoba Danqing, ordering him to the Yinling Grand Prison—a serious red flag for a routine assignment.
2. **The Assemble**: Li Huowang meets his old acquaintance Hong Da at the prison. They bicker in a way that reveals the Office's surprisingly lax internal hierarchy, though the underlying respect is clear.
3. **The Chief Recorder Arrives**: A fat eunuch official with a gold abacus, powdered white face, and a cloying scent enters. He sizes Li Huowang up and casually mentions a past incident where Li Huowang (as Er Jiu) burned a nest of White Lotus heretics alive. This is a double-edged warning: the Office knows his temper, but they also know his worth.
4. **The Mission Revealed**: The Chief Recorder announces the job is against the Zuowandao. Li Huowang is visibly shaken—this is personal. But the target and details remain hidden.
5. **The Silent Partner**: The Chief Recorder instructs Tuoba Danqing to “invite” the prisoner named Zen Master Xinchi from the deepest cell. This mysterious “monk” is a fellow Office operative, and his presence signals that the operation is anything but a simple raid.
6. **Bureaucratic Horror**: The chapter deepens the unique, unsettling atmosphere of the Supervisory Heavenly Office. It’s a government institution that covers brothel visits and mooncakes but also employs murderers and hints at body-horror rituals (the *Renxiao* mentioned earlier).

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司)**: This chapter gives us our best look yet at the Office’s *operational culture*. It’s a fascinating blend of Ming/Qing dynasty civil service bureaucracy and xianxia-styled corruption. The Office provides generous subsidies (food, clothing, brothel fees, and even holiday mooncakes!) to its operatives, fostering a strange, almost fraternal loyalty. Yet, as the Chief Recorder’s tone suggests, this benevolence comes with a sharp edge: they know your every move, and they will hold it over your head. It’s a family you can’t leave.

**The Renxiao (人魈)**: The Chief Recorder casually refers to “Xuan-word Renxiao” as a measure of power. This confirms the creature’s place in the world’s hierarchy. As the incremental glossary notes, a *Renxiao* is the horrifying result of a person who lives past 120 years old without achieving immortality, their obsession festering into a blind, malformed monster. Being able to kill one with ease is already a serious flex in the Office’s eyes.

**The Chief Recorder (记相)**: This character is dripping with historical flavor. He’s an imperial eunuch, likely a member of the Directorate of Ceremonial (or a similar Ming court institution), but here he's been integrated into the supernatural police force. The gold abacus and the cloying, urine-tinged scent are classic xianxia tropes for a powerful, hedonistic, and slightly repulsive authority figure. His oily politeness is a weapon—don’t let the smiles fool you.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is all about **atmosphere and world-building**, not action. The rain, the cold prison, the cloying smell of the eunuch, and the casual mention of past atrocities all build a sense of dread. We’re seeing Li Huowang step onto a larger stage where the players are far more dangerous than mere cultists. Pay close attention to how Tuoba Danqing navigates the social minefield between the laid-back Hong Da and the terrifyingly polite Chief Recorder—it’s a masterclass in jianghu survival. And speaking of survival, what kind of “monk” has the Office locked away in its own dungeon? Get ready for the next chapter, because this job is going to get messy.
