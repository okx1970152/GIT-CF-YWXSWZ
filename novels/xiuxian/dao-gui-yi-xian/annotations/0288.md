## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang arrives at a new village, hot on the trail of Zuowandao activity, only to find himself doubting his own senses before the tricksters even show up. While investigating a temple, a chance sound—real or hallucinated?—throws him into fresh paranoia. But the real twist comes when a blind old man from the Supervisory Heavenly Office grabs him mid-stride, seeing through his invisibility without eyes. What follows is a tense dance of mutual suspicion: two agents, both hiding something, feeling each other out under the cover of darkness and politeness.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang investigates the village temple but finds nothing; a temple keeper locks the door, forcing him to exit stealthily.
- He resolves to bring his group into the village to verify his senses, aware that his perception is eroding again.
- While walking back, an unplaceable unease stops him. He is grabbed from behind: a blind man named Chen has detected him despite his invisibility.
- The old man introduces himself as a Ninth-Grade Agent (壬卒) of the Supervisory Heavenly Office; Li Huowang lies, claiming to be Er Jiu, a Tenth-Grade Ao-Jing Sect cultivator.
- Blind Chen reveals the village is infested with mud-dwelling evil spirits (邪祟) that nearly attacked Li Huowang.
- The two move outside to talk. Chen admits he cannot handle the entity alone—the “mud-devils” slip away from his blind pursuit—and asks Li Huowang for a way to flush them out, boasting he can handle the fight once they’re exposed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**监天司 (Supervisory Heavenly Office):** This is not just a generic monster-hunting bureau. The term blends two layers: *监天* (to oversee heaven) and *司* (an administrative office). In the novel’s world, it is a government-backed agency that polices rogue cultivators and paranormal incidents—think a supernatural FBI with a Daoist bureaucracy. Its agents carry rank tokens (甲/乙/丙/丁 for seniority, with 壬/癸 for lower grades) that function as both credentials and tracking devices.

**袄景教 (Ao-Jing Sect):** Li Huowang’s alias is not random. This sect fuses two historical foreign religions that entered China: *袄教* (Zoroastrianism, the Persian fire-worshipping faith) and *景教* (Nestorian Christianity, an early Eastern Christian sect). In the Dao-Twisted World, the Ao-Jing Sect is the owner of the horrifying *Thousand Greats Record*—a text that trades ritualized suffering for power from the primordial god Bashe. Claiming membership signals pain-tolerance, self-mutilation, and a terrifyingly transactional relationship with divinity.

**瞎子's Detection:** Blind Chen’s ability to see through Li Huowang’s invisibility is not a counterspell or super-sight. It is a brutally practical method: he *smells* the blood on Li Huowang. This aligns with the novel’s logic that supernatural concealment hides visual forms but not other sensory traces—a hallmark of the grounded, body-horror aesthetic.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in slow-burn horror. No monsters leap out; no Zuowandao cackle in the shadows. The terror is atmospheric and social, built on a single, not-quite-verified sound, a lock that won’t open, and the empty silence of a village that feels *too* ordinary. The real hook is Blind Chen. Is he what he claims to be? His confident boast—“gong against gong, drum against drum”—feels like a test. Li Huowang is the blind one here: he can see perfectly but cannot trust a word or a sound. Pay attention to every pause in their conversation. The lie is layered inside a truth, and the truth is already a trap.
