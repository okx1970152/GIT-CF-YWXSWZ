## Chapter Overview

Our blind and battered protagonist gets an info-dump that reshuffles his mental board of the entire Great Liang political landscape, discovers his own unwitting role in screwing over the Heavenly Office’s calamity plan, and—because there’s no rest for the wicked—sits down to make a good old-fashioned to-do list of existential nightmares. Liu Zongyuan pops in for a chat, offers some juicy gossip about an upcoming Zuowandao purge, and leaves Li Huowang with a critical lead on eye-replacement techniques. Oh, and Li Sui tries to eat a guest. Just another Tuesday in the Dao-Twisted World.

## Key Plot Points

- Liu Zongyuan reveals that the Golden Palace Grand Formation in Shangjing was designed to counter a “Heaven-Devouring Dog Eclipse” Heavenly Calamity, not for the Liang emperor to ascend to immortality.
- Zhuge Yuan, speaking from within Li Huowang, pieces together the larger Zuowandao conspiracy: they used his Heart-Pupil status to link the two branches and steal the Office’s Divine Mountain Ghost Eye.
- Zhuge Yuan blames himself for being tricked from the moment Bai Ban was cut down, revealing the long game the Zuowandao played.
- Liu Zongyuan shares insider info that the Supervisory Heavenly Office is planning an empire-wide extermination of the Zuowandao after the Heavenly Calamity is resolved.
- Li Huowang performs his obligatory “I’m still a badass, take me along!” act, knowing full well Liu won’t accept; his old persona is firmly in maintenance mode.
- Li Sui shows a worrying new aggression: she growls and bares her teeth at Liu when he asks if she’s for sale, suggesting the mantou inside her is warping her nature into something more beastly.
- The chapter ends with Li Huowang single-handedly drawing up his four current major problems: Bai Lingmiao’s failing health, the instability of future calamities, his corrupted past by Doumu, and the constant need to cultivate for Zhuge Yuan’s revival.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Heaven-Dog Eating the Sun (天狗食日)** is a traditional Chinese folk explanation for solar eclipses, where a celestial dog devours the sun. Here, the concept is literalized and weaponized as a catastrophic “Heavenly Calamity”—a world-ending-style event that forces the highest-level powers to coordinate. The Supervisory Heavenly Office’s use of the Divine Mountain Ghost Eye to counter it blends this folk phenomenon with cao-level alchemical warfare.

**Yang Life Pills (阳寿丹)** are a fascinating currency in this world. Since supernatural powers often cost literal years of one’s lifespan, the Office has found a way to solidify and trade this resource. Think of it as the most high-stakes cryptocurrency ever: instead of losing coins, you’re losing heartbeats. Liu Zongyuan’s suggestion to exchange them at the Shangjing treasury for eye-replacement techniques hints at a well-stocked supernatural black market operating under official aegis.

**Heart-Pupil (心蟠)** mechanics are further elaborated here: Zhuge Yuan was used as a “bridge” by the Zuowandao to connect their splintered branches. This suggests that Heart-Pupils have a unique spatial or conceptual linking property that goes beyond just being a powerful cultivator—they are nodes in a metaphysical network that can be hijacked.

### Mang tou / Mantou (馒头)

The parasitic entity fed to Li Sui that is now warping her personality; it adds a bestial edge to her formerly child-like nature.

### Private slave (私奴)

A bonded laborer in historical Chinese society, tied to a household or land; not equivalent to chattel slavery; highlights rigid class hierarchy.

### Human-skin artifact (皮人)

A palm-sized doll made from the cultivator’s own skin and mercury; functions as a phylactery allowing survival past decapitation, and can be mass-produced.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *breather episode* in the best sense—not action-packed, but loaded with world-lore reveals that make the previous arcs click into place like puzzle pieces. Li Huowang’s reaction to the news is a masterclass in guilt-by-association: he helped screw up the Office’s plan without even knowing what the plan was. The dark comedy of his “sure, take a blind guy on a manhunt” performance is a welcome moment of levity. The real gem here, though, is Li Sui’s character development: she’s not a simple tentacle-puppy anymore. Something inside her is waking up, and it’s got teeth. Keep an eye on that.
