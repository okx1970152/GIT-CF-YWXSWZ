## Chapter Overview

This chapter traps Li Huowang in a double bind—physical and psychological. After a brutal escape from the Dharma Sect's endless ranks, he wins a tactical victory at an emotional cost, faces Jin Shan Zhao's desperate plea for help, and picks up a new, grotesque hallucinatory passenger. The "entrapment" isn't just by Earth-based spells anymore; it's the weight of a world that expects him to save it while he can barely save his own mind. The chapter's real punchline lands in that final, bone-tired turn of the head: the dead woman and her rotting infant, another uninvited guest in the carnival of his skull.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is attacked by the Dharma Sect's burrowing spell; a hidden hand in the ground extracts his leg bone, forcing a frantic limb reconstruction with Li Sui's tendrils.
- Zhuge Yuan blocks Li Huowang from escaping to Great Liang via the spine sword, warning that doing so would permanently expose the sword's secret to any diviners in the Dharma Sect.
- A former Zuowandao member, now converted to the Dharma Sect, closes in; this reveals the alarming reach of the Dharma Sect's recruitment.
- Peng Longteng uses Li Huowang's own tendrils as a human slingshot, flinging him high enough to clear the encirclement—at the cost of crossbow bolts and a splitting headache.
- After escaping, Jin Shan Zhao speaks his first word ("Help"), pleading for the Dharma Sect's cannon-fodder followers, and Li Huowang delivers a cold rejection: he is too broken to save anyone.
- Li Huowang turns around to find a new hallucination—a gaunt woman holding a half-rotted infant—has joined the collection standing behind him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Swallowing a Mound (遁地术)** — This term is a catch-all for burrowing into earth. Historical Chinese martial and xianxia fiction often features "earth escape" (遁地) as a basic sorcery or supernatural martial art; the fact that an *organ-extraction* attack rides inside the spell here highlights the novel's unique horror-logic—no clean teleport, just a bone-sucking tentacle in the dirt.

**Dharma Sect (法教)** — Not to be confused with the institutional "Dharma teaching" of Buddhism; in the *Dao-Twisted World*, the Dharma Sect is a homegrown cult whose power is explicitly tied to rock, soil, and earth-raising techniques. The terrifying implication established in this chapter: it has an unprecedented capacity for indoctrination, even absorbing former Zuowandao tricksters.

**Spine Sword (脊骨剑)** — Li Huowang's iconic blade, crafted from his own spine. A "body part weapon" concept found in some dark alchemical lore, but here it's permanent and functional, giving him a direct, portable gate to Great Liang when other escapes fail.

**Slingshot Tactic** — The human slingshot is not a conventional power-up; it's a *desperate mobility trick*. Each use costs Li Huowang physical damage and risks triggering his uncontrollable "innate pneuma" (先天一炁)—a core Daoist internal alchemy term for the pre-differentiated, fundamental energy of the universe, which here has a self-destructive feedback loop.

### Slingshot tactic (甩人)

A desperate mobility technique where Peng Longteng uses Li Huowang's own tendrils to fling him high and far, effectively clearing the encirclement but at the cost of physical damage and risking innate pneuma instability.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a deceptively quiet horror chapter. The big set piece (the earth-mound chase and slingshot escape) is over fast; the real weight lands in the aftermath. Li Huowang's monologue to Jin Shan Zhao is one of the most honest, bleak self-diagnostics he's ever made: he's not refusing to help because he's evil—he's refusing because he has nothing left to give. The symbolism in the final reveal (the dead woman and the rotting baby) is layered: is this his guilt manifesting? A new hallucination from the Dharma Sect pollution? Or just another piece of the world's suffering that has latched onto a Heart-Element's reality? The answer doesn't come in this chapter, but the dread does.
