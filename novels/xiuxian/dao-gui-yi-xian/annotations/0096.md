## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow Daoists—because this chapter gives you no time to breathe before it rips your heart out and shows it to you. Li Huowang hits the absolute floor of his psychological crisis, straddling the kindergarten’s fluorescent lighting and a rotten bamboo grove with equal helplessness. He nearly kills a child. He watches his mother kneel. And when a sniper’s bullet finds his head, the only thing that saves him is the hallucination he’s been trying to escape. This isn’t a fight scene. It’s a spiritual vivisection, and *The Thousand Greats Record* is the scalpel.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang is frozen in the kindergarten, clutching a little girl, completely unable to tell whether either world—or the child herself—is real. His internal dialogue fractures into a shouting match between paranoid self-preservation and horrified guilt.
- Sun Xiaoqin kneels outside the fence and begs. This is the moment that finally shatters Li Huowang’s cognitive walls: *“A Heart-Element is a Heart-Element…”* He now understands that bewilderment is his permanent condition, not an error to be fixed.
- A plainclothes officer distracts Li Huowang while a sniper takes the shot. Li Huowang dodges by pure instinct—but the bullet grazes him, and the impact sends him crashing into the hallucinatory bamboo grove.
- On the Dao-Twisted side, Li Huowang realizes he is alone and surrounded. Layue Shiba has allies: the little girl and the Stove God. The entity tries to force his perception into the dying-old-man frame.
- In a moment of agonized epiphany, Li Huowang weaponizes his own despair. He declares that Layue Shiba’s mistake was killing him *here*—because now his heart pain is strong enough to power *The Thousand Greats Record*.
- The red bamboo slip takes root in the blood-soaked bamboo leaves. The anguish inside him peaks. He reaches for the torture instruments at his waist, ready to complete the ritual with physical pain.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Heart-Element (心素) and Bewilderment (迷惘)** are inseparable in this cosmology. Abbess Jingxin’s teaching—echoed here as a fatal diagnosis—establishes that a Heart-Element cannot escape confusion between realities. This is not a mental illness to be cured; it is a metaphysical anchor. The very nature of a Heart-Element is to be suspended between *is* and *isn’t*. The novel uses this as a haunting inversion of classic xianxia tropes: instead of enlightenment bringing certainty, it brings permanent cognitive fracture.

**The Thousand Greats Record (《大千录》)** appears again with a new layer: it doesn’t just accept pain as fuel—it *resonates* with the user’s raw emotional agony. The bamboo slip’s “rooting” is a visual metaphor for this resonance. In this chapter, Li Huowang discovers that killing him in one reality hasn’t destroyed him; it has sharpened his emotional state to the exact pitch the Record needs. This is the cruelest kind of power-up: one that requires you to be completely broken first.

**Layue Shiba (腊月十八)** is an “evil spirit” with a unique yearly-reset mechanic. It changes appearance, powers, and weaknesses every year on the eighteenth day of the twelfth lunar month. This makes it almost impossible to learn from past encounters. In this chapter, its tactic of forcing Li Huowang into the perception of an old neighbor shows its strength lies in mental *incursion*—it wins by rewriting who you think you are, not by direct combat.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is the climax of Li Huowang’s identity crisis arc. Watch how the *object* of his pain shifts: he starts by fearing for the child’s life, then spirals into self-loathing, and finally lands on a cold, terrible resolve to *use* his own suffering as a weapon. The moment he smiles and says *“It hurts so much I want to die”* is not a heroic beat—it’s the sound of a man who has decided to become the thing that feeds on his own ruin. If you’ve been wondering when Li Huowang would stop being a victim and start being dangerous, this is the exact turning point. But the cost is already visible: he has stopped trying to save the parts of himself that make him human.
