## Chapter Overview
Our man Li Huowang has a lotus root for a head. That is not a metaphor. The morning sun spills into his Baita ward, and he fumbles with a spoon while his mom fusses and Bai Lingmiao hovers nearby. It’s the calmest scene we’ve seen in a while — a slice of almost-normal domestic warmth. But that brittle, hollow shell where his skull should be? That’s a gift from the Chief Astrologer of Siqi, a patch-job from the Fangxian Sect’s “anything-can-be-medicine” school of healing. Forty-nine days, they say, and he’ll have his flesh back. In the meantime, Li Huowang wrestles with a question that cuts deeper than any lotus-scalp: if you excise the fake memories, but the scars and the *Record* and the lingering touch of the spider-god remain, have you really been cured?

## Key Plot Points
- Sun Xiaoqin tries to bribe Dr. Yi before leaving; he de-escalates the situation by placing the envelope in Li Huowang’s gown for return later.
- Li Huowang confirms his head is now an empty lotus root, a temporary replacement from the Siqi court alchemists; forty-nine days for full recovery.
- A flash hallucination of the Chief Astrologer appears, offering a paradoxical statement about “bewilderment” before dissolving.
- The real Chief Astrologer enters the room, confirming the prior figure was a mental projection or phantom.
- Li Huowang’s core anxiety is laid bare: even as the real Li Huowang, the *Thousand Greats Record* and Bashe’s gifts (scars, hyper-perception) persist, tying him to the older cosmic debt.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Lotus Root (莲藕) as a prosthetic**: In Chinese folk religion and Buddhist mythology, lotus roots are famously symbolic of purity and rebirth. The Nezha legend is the most famous — after Nezha’s suicide, his master Taiyi Zhenren rebuilt his body using a lotus root, allowing him to return stronger. The novel weaponizes this sacred imagery: Li Huowang’s lotus head is medical, not mystical, but the parallel is hard to ignore. It’s a grisly inversion of a classic xianxia revival trope.  
**Fangxian Sect (方仙教)**: The “recipe-immortal” school, a term drawn from the historical *fangshu* tradition — alchemists, diviners, and medical masters who served ancient courts. Their philosophy of “anything can be medicine” (万物皆可入药) is a real, radical claim in some classical medical and alchemical texts. Here, it’s literalized into grotesque body-swapping surgery.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is an emotional checkbox disguised as a scene of domestic peace. Li Huowang is finally eating by himself, talking about rehabilitation, and his mother is doing the classic Chinese mom dance of love and anxiety (red envelope, career interrogations, matchmaking). It’s almost *too* comfortable — and that’s the point. The lotus head is a physical reminder that his “cure” is still a patch, not a clean slate. The real hook is Li Huowang’s quiet confession: even without Hong Zhong’s memories, the *Thousand Greats Record* and Bashe’s gifts are *his*, now organic parts of his being. The hallucinated Chief Astrologer’s line about “bewilderment itself being a form of suffering” is the chapter’s thesis — Li Huowang is stuck between being “cured” and still being *infected*. The warm morning light, the porridge, the mother’s chatter… it’s all a beautiful, fragile stage for that single gnawing doubt.
