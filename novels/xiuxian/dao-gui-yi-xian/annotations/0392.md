## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang is thrown into another twisted game of cat and mouse—only this time, the Zuowandao have taken the fight to a whole new level. Trapped in a haunting circular funeral hall deep in a bamboo forest, our paranoid protagonist quickly discovers that the enemy has not only infiltrated Apricot Island but has also somehow **stolen his own supernatural abilities** right out from under him. It’s a desperate, visceral, and skin-crawling confrontation that raises one terrifying question: how do you fight a mirror that has already learned every one of your moves?

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang emerges from the fog to find a massive circular building (a tulou) decorated with white funeral lanterns and paper offerings.
- He enters the funeral hall, shouts into an empty coffin as a test, and confirms that no physical or spiritual presence is hiding inside.
- Frustrated, he issues an ultimatum: reveal yourselves or he will burn the building and the surrounding bamboo forest down.
- As he leaves, he spots a red figure on the second floor—a person who looks exactly like himself.
- Realizing the Zuowandao have already reached Apricot Island, Li Huowang launches into a high-speed, acrobatic assault using his blood talismans and bronze coin sword.
- The enemy turns out to be a phantom decoy. The real attack comes from a misdirection—Li Huowang is stabbed from behind.
- Li Huowang tries to use his own “misalignment” ability to escape, but it fails. The Zuowandao has stolen it.
- In a desperate gambit, Li Huowang crushes his own right eye to buy a moment of temporal distortion and escape the sword.
- He attempts a self-immolation attack using his fire striker, but realizes his skin was already refined into an artifact—he has nothing left to sacrifice to Bashe.
- He rips out his own back teeth and explodes them as shrapnel, covering the entire one-zhang radius of his misalignment ability.
- The Zuowandao is riddled with the shrapnel but still stands. Worse, Li Huowang realizes the enemy has also stolen his regeneration.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **circular building** Li Huowang stumbles into is unmistakably a Fujian *tulou* (土楼)—a massive, fortified earthen structure historically built by Hakka clans. In Chinese folk horror and wuxia/xianxia fiction, these windowless, multi-story rings often serve as claustrophobic stages for supernatural standoffs and closed-room mysteries. Their defensive architecture (designed to keep bandits out) becomes a perfect prison when the enemy is already inside.

The **paper funeral offerings (纸扎)** and **white funeral lanterns (白灯笼)** are standard elements of traditional Chinese mortuary rites. Lanterns and paper effigies are burned or displayed to guide the spirit of the deceased into the afterlife. In this novel’s hands, they become a tasteless, unnerving stage prop—hinting that the Zuowandao are mocking not just Li Huowang, but the very rituals meant to ensure the dead rest in peace.

The “misalignment” ability (**错位能力**) has appeared before as Li Huowang’s signature spatial distortion trick, which lets him displace his body within a one-zhang radius to avoid attacks. Having it stolen is a major tactical shock—it means the enemy has either observed him closely or used a supernatural mechanism to replicate a part of his identity.

You may also note a deeply brutal, Southern Chinese exorcistic logic at work: Li Huowang’s use of his own teeth as shrapnel and the crushing of his own eye to activate a “temporal stun” function connects back to the *Thousand Greats Record* (大千录)—a pain-for-power scripture where any part of the body can be rendered as a weapon if offered with enough conviction.

### Tulou (土楼)

A large, fortified, circular clan dwelling traditional to the Hakka people of Fujian, China. In horror fiction, its enclosed, multi-story ring structure creates a perfect stage for claustrophobic supernatural standoffs.

### Self-sacrifice combustion (自焚术)

A technique from the Thousand Greats Record where the practitioner offers their own skin to Bashe in exchange for a fiery burst of power. Having no skin left to sacrifice renders the technique unusable.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *escalation through identity theft*. For the first time, the Zuowandao aren’t just using masks or illusions—they are literally stealing Li Huowang’s combat tools. This is a terrifying new tactic: the enemy doesn’t need to be stronger, just faster at copying. Readers should also pay attention to the progression of Li Huowang’s tactics. He goes from testing an empty coffin (a surprisingly efficient low-cost recon move), to using a borrowed spatial ability, to finally reverting to raw, flesh-and-blood self-mutilation. It shows a fighter who is still in control of his desperation—but only just barely. Also, keep an eye on that “Four Joys” and “Three Yuans” theory he mutters. If correct, this isn’t just any Zuowandao grunt—this might be one of their inner circle leaders.
