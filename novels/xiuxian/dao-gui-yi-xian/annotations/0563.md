## Chapter Overview

Hold onto your seatbelts, fellow Daoists. Chapter 562, “Thunder Method,” drops us right into the climax of Li Huowang’s deadly cat-and-mouse game with the trickster god-level Zuowandao known as “the Dice.” The Great Liang State Preceptor, Huangfu Tiangong, unleashes the full, terrifying might of his thunder-based magic, turning a palace into a pile of charcoal in a single, explosive salvo. But this is the Dao-Twisted World. Killing the Dice is never as simple as just destroying his body. The chapter is a masterclass in psychological and physical horror—a desperate, visceral struggle where Li Huowang must fight a liar who has already anticipated his every move, all while trying to prevent a powerful potential ally from being poisoned by deception. If you thought the previous fights were intense, you haven't seen anything yet. This is a battle against an idea, and ideas are bulletproof.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Huangfu Tiangong demonstrating his supreme power—five massive bolts of heavenly lightning (五雷) annihilating an entire palace where the Dice was hiding. The Dice, disguised as a dying eunuch, attempts to flee but is cornered by both the State Preceptor and Li Huowang. Realizing escape is impossible, the Dice immediately shifts tactics from evasion to psychological warfare. He deliberately lets Li Huowang capture and strike him, using the proximity to whisper the name “Danyangzi” directly into his mind, exploiting his deepest trauma and vulnerability. This triggers a terrifying possession attempt, with Li Huowang’s own mouth being hijacked. In a brutal act of desperation, Li Huowang resorts to Ao-Jing Sect self-mutilation, impaling himself with hooks and his own ribs to try and purge the invader. Huangfu Tiangong, showing no concern for the host’s life, prepares to incinerate them both with another thunder strike, forcing the Dice to flee Li Huowang’s body and escape in his bisected corpse. The chase becomes a frantic race against time, with the halves of the Dice splitting up. The chapter ends with a new, powerful entity intervening—a hidden archer from the Great Liang court itself—pinning the fleeing half of the Dice to the ground.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Thunder Magic (雷法)** : The chapter's title refers to a real, powerful branch of Daoist ritual magic (道法). In Daoism, lightning is not just a destructive force but a symbol of heavenly judgment, purification, and the cosmic power of the *Zhenren* (Perfected Person). The State Preceptor's ability to summon “Five Thunder” (五雷) aligns with the Daoist *Five Thunder Magic* (五雷法) tradition, a highly esoteric practice used for exorcism, punishing evil, and commanding celestial forces. Its sheer destructive force in this scene underlines the “positive” pole of power in this world, a terrifying but legitimate force of order opposed to the Zuowandao's lawless lies.
- **The Starry Ladder (星宿剑)** : Huangfu Tiangong’s sword. The “Starry Ladder” or *Stellar Ladder* is a concept in Daoist astrology and alchemy, representing the path a cultivator’s spirit takes to ascend through the celestial palaces of the stars. The sword’s name implies it is a conduit for the power of the constellations, reinforcing the State Preceptor's connection to the cold, authoritative order of the heavens.
- **The Dice (骰子)** : As we get closer to this Zuowandao leader, his nature becomes clearer. He is not a “Demon King” in the traditional sense. His power is that of a *Joker* archetype—a narrative disruptor. He doesn’t just lie with words; he manipulates reality by controlling the flow of information and expectation. His ability to “whisper” and cause possession seems to be a form of *Forgetfulness (坐忘)* taken to an invasive extreme, planting suggestions that *are* reality for the victim. His theatrical mannerism, even when half-dead, is a weapon designed to confuse and delay his enemies.

### Green-faced fangs (青面獠牙)

A traditional Chinese description of a demon's or vengeful spirit’s visage, with a blue/green face and protruding fangs. Li Huowang's description fits this archetype.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a high-octane example of what makes the *Dao-Twisted World* so special: it turns the standard fantasy “boss fight” into a three-dimensional chess game of morals, perception, and self-harm. Pay close attention to how the Dice fights. He doesn’t use a secret ultimate technique; he uses *context*. Notice how he first tries to break the alliance between Li Huowang and the State Preceptor, and when that fails, he attacks Li Huowang’s mental stability in the most efficient way possible—by calling him Danyangzi. This is a key moment in Li Huowang’s arc. His solution—deliberately stabbing himself and engaging in brutal self-surgery—is a sickening but logical conclusion to his survival pragmatism. Also, watch the dynamic between him and Huangfu Tiangong. The State Preceptor’s cold indifference to Li Huowang’s life (“I’ll burn him too if I have to”) establishes him not as a friend or mentor, but as an utterly lawful (and terrifying) force who sees Li Huowang as a variable in an equation, not a person. This is the system Li Huowang is trying to join.
