## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang begins to piece together the rules of the Dao-Twisted World while contending with his own horror at the fate awaiting Bai Lingmiao. His attempt to learn alchemy from Danyangzi yields an unexpected clue about the temple's secrets—and a startling revelation about his master.

## Key Plot Points

* Li Huowang learns he must deliver Bai Lingmiao as a human ingredient before the day after tomorrow's zi hour, reuniting him with the earlier time pressure.
* Gouwa reports that Bai Lingmiao is unharmed but reminds Li Huowang of the power his position as storeroom overseer gives him—a power he hasn't yet used.
* Li Huowang visits the alchemy hall and notes its complete lack of written materials, scrolls, or scriptures, reinforcing his earlier observations.
* Danyangzi boasts about receiving the method of immortality from "Lord Lao" through a Heavenly Scripture, hinting at the temple's corrupt religious foundation.
* Danyangzi teaches Li Huowang the Moistening Blood Pill formula, which Li Huowang desperately writes on his robe hem using charcoal.
* When Li Huowang presents the written formula, Danyangzi inexplicably flies into a rage, kicks him, and storms off.
* Li Huowang deduces that Danyangzi may be illiterate—unable to read the characters he himself wrote.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

* **Moistening Blood Pill (润血丹)**: A basic alchemical pill to nourish blood. Its ingredients (cinnabar, cyperus, actinolite) are traditional Chinese medicinal minerals—cinnabar is mercury sulfide, toxic over time. The formula here is fictional but drawn from real pharmacopoeia.
* **Zi hour (子时)**: In traditional Chinese timekeeping, one day is divided into twelve two-hour shichen (时辰), each named after an earthly branch. Zi hour corresponds to 11:00 PM – 1:00 AM. The "day after tomorrow's zi hour" adds a concrete deadline, mimicking the novel's recurring countdown motifs.
* **Chou / Mao hours (丑时 / 卯时)**: Chou = 1:00–3:00 AM; Mao = 5:00–7:00 AM. The precise time specifications for alchemical reactions reflect real inner/outer alchemy procedures in Daoist practice.
* **Lord Lao (老君爷)**: A colloquial name for Laozi deified as Lord Lao, the supreme deity in Daoist pantheon, associated with alchemy and the Dao De Jing. Danyangzi's claim that Lord Lao personally handed him an immortality method underscores his delusional grandeur—or hints at something truly wrong in this world.
* **Heavenly Scripture (天书)**: A mythical text from heaven, impossible for mortals to read; but here Danyangzi claims he can read it, which is either a lie or a sign of his twisted path.

### The Illiteracy of Power

This chapter's quiet climax is not violent but intellectual: Li Huowang realizes that his terrifying master may be unable to read. The alchemy hall's bare walls, the absence of scriptures during lessons, and Danyangzi's fury at seeing written characters all point to a grotesque irony. In a world where alchemy is tied to secret texts (the Heavenly Scripture), the one who claims to possess that scripture cannot decipher the symbols he himself wrote. This is not merely a personal flaw; it suggests that Danyangzi's entire practice is built on a foundation of misinterpretation and ignorance—a typical Dao-Twisted World twist on the venerable theme of the "illiterate sage."

## Reading Guide

* This chapter marks a turning point: Li Huowang shifts from passive victim to active observer, cataloguing environmental details that others overlook. The barren alchemy hall—no books, no scrolls, no writing—is his first concrete clue.
* The revelation of Danyangzi's possible illiteracy is played with dark humor but carries serious implications: a master who cannot read cannot have studied the classics; this suggests his entire alchemical knowledge is either secondhand hearsay or something more sinister.
* The Moistening Blood Pill formula is the first alchemical recipe Li Huowang learns. While it seems minor, its existence as a taught formula gives him a foothold in the world's rules—and a tool he might eventually use.
* Note the contrast: Li Huowang uses writing (a technology of literacy) to record knowledge, while the illiterate master relies on oral tradition and brute force. This clash between literate modernity and oral-ritual culture is a subtle engine of the chapter.
