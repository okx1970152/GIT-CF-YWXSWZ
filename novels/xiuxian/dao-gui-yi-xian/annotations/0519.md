## Chapter Overview
Locked and loaded for a high-stakes assassination, Li Huowang trails the Empress Dowager and the Eldest Princess to a mountain temple. But instead of spilling blood, he gets a front-row seat to a deadly game of psychological ping-pong. This chapter is all about the *wait*—the breathless crawl through a web of hidden guards, the quiet observation of power, and an unexpected detour into filial piety that reeks of a trap. It’s a masterclass in tension before the trigger is pulled.

## Key Plot Points
Li Huowang, invisible thanks to his *Heart-Element* ability, stalks the imperial procession of the Empress Dowager and the Eldest Princess of Great Liang. He analyzes their formation, waiting for the perfect moment to strike. He learns their destination is a temple featuring a colossal male Bodhisattva statue, a place of deep public devotion.

After the two royal women conduct their prayers and the temple settles in for the night, Li Huowang moves in. He skillfully bypasses the outer security cordon, a tense hour-long crawl that tests his patience and nerve. As he prepares to enter the meditation rooms, he witnesses a bizarre scene: maids from the Princess’s quarters carry a young, bald monk to the Empress Dowager’s room, claiming it’s an act of filial piety to ease her fatigue. The chapter ends on this ominous, pre-assassination cliffhanger, with Li Huowang halted by a mystery he can’t ignore.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Male Bodhisattva**: For many Western readers, Bodhisattvas like Guanyin (Avalokiteśvara) are almost always depicted as female deities of mercy. But in earlier Chinese Buddhist art and scriptures, Bodhisattvas were often portrayed as genderless or even male princely figures, transcending earthly duality. The Great Liang Bodhisattva here is a deliberate callback to that older, perhaps more "rugged" iconography. This is a great worldbuilding detail—a visual reminder that the Dao-Twisted World’s Buddhism operates on its own, often corrupted, logic.

**Paying Filial Piety (尽孝)**: Sending a young, good-looking monk to one’s exhausted mother in the middle of the night as an act of “filial piety” is a *massive* red flag in this genre. This isn’t just about getting her a backrub. In historical Chinese court intrigue, “sending a handsome man to a female relative” was a coded way of either planting a spy, providing a secret lover, or setting up a blackmail scheme. The fact that the old eunuch stops them suggests even the palace staff thinks this is a step too far, which makes the entire situation deeply suspicious.

## Reading Guide
A deadly quiet before the storm. This chapter is pure stealth gameplay with the volume turned way down. The tension comes not from action, but from Li Huowang’s internal clock ticking down as he hangs upside down, sword in hand. The big twist is that the target isn’t the most dangerous thing in the room—the family drama might get him first. Keep your eyes peeled, because every detail in a *Dao Gui Yi Xian* chapter like this is either a clue or a coffin nail.
