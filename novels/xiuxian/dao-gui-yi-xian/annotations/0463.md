## Chapter Overview

Some horrors don't need to be seen to destroy you. This chapter is a masterclass in **cosmic dread from above** — Li Huowang survives a close call with Bai Lingmiao's White Lotus crisis, only to stumble into something far, far worse waiting in the sky. The moment he *looks up*, the chapter pivots from rescue drama to ontological collapse. He gouges out his own eyes and it still isn't enough. The enemy here isn't a monster with claws and a mouth — it's the *Heavenly Dao itself* peeling open like a rotten fruit, and our protagonist's body starts singing a song he never learned. Meanwhile, the mundane human drama below — the battle at the Bai compound — feels almost laughably small by comparison. This is the chapter where Li Huowang learns that sometimes the worst thing you can do is *see the truth*. And once you've seen it, you can't un-see it. Get ready for a ride into **the space between the stars**, where even blind men are not safe.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang uses the **Twelve-Grade Merit Jade Lotus** to purge Bai Lingmiao of her White Lotus corruption, restoring her to normal. Zhuge Yuan identifies the artifact and reveals he once searched for it himself.
- Li Huowang carries the weakened Bai Lingmiao out of the underground White Lotus hall and through the Bai ancestral shrine, with the silent Second Spirit following.
- As he emerges and hears continued fighting at the Bai compound, his senses detect *something watching him*. His body begins to tremble uncontrollably.
- The sky "unfolds" layer by layer, revealing a terrifying vision: the **Myriad Lofty Brahma-Aura**, the spinning **Heavenly Dao**, and the **Yellow Path and Purple Way** shining upon a cosmic palace.
- Li Huowang's jaw dislocates as a **screaming crane cry** erupts from his throat, assimilating all surrounding sounds. Roots begin to claw out of his shadow.
- Unable to close his eyes no matter how hard he tries, Li Huowang **gouges out his own eyeballs** — but the severed, bloodied eyes still stare fixedly at the thing in the sky.
- He crushes the eyeballs, the crane cry stops, and he collapses. Bai Lingmiao and the Second Spirit help him, and he forces his jaw back into place.
- He forbids Bai Lingmiao from summoning spirit-immortals, warning that in such chaos, anything she calls might not leave.
- Reciting the **Heart-Calming Mantra** over a hundred times, Li Huowang's hands become glued to his face by dried blood scabs. He tears them off violently, reopening the wounds.
- Lü Xiucai tends to his wounds and reports that the others have nearly cleared the compound of evil spirits.
- Li Huowang heals himself using the *True Sutra of the Fire Vestments*, searing the wounds shut.
- *Climax*: A sudden wave of warmth and cheers signals that **the sun has returned** — the "sky-devouring dog" has been scared off by gongs elsewhere.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**"Tian Wai Tian" (天外天) — Heaven Beyond Heaven**: This chapter's title invokes a classic Daoist and Buddhist concept of layered cosmic realms. In xianxia nomenclature, "Heaven Beyond Heaven" refers to a transcendent space beyond the known celestial order — often the dwelling place of primordial forces or the supreme Dao itself. When Li Huowang *sees* it, his body begins to malfunction like a machine exposed to incompatible data. This is classic Lovecraftian "forbidden sight," wearing a completely Chinese theological costume: the Dao is not safe to look at.

**Twelve-Grade Merit Jade Lotus (十二品功德玉莲)**: A Buddhist-aligned treasure that purges possessing entities by defining what is "impure." The "twelve grades" allude to the twelve levels of Buddhist fruit (achievement), making this a relic of advanced spiritual authority. That it was feared by the White Lotus — a sect that weaponizes the illusion of purity — is deeply ironic and poetically appropriate.

**Heart-Calming Mantra (静心咒)**: A common Buddhist/Daoist protective chant used to still the mind and ward off external psychic interference. Li Huowang's desperate repetition here is a last-ditch "mental firewall" against a vision that threatens to overwrite his consciousness.

**Sky-Devouring Dog (天狗吃日)**: A classic Chinese folk explanation for solar eclipses — a celestial dog eats the sun, and villagers beat drums and gongs to scare it away. The novel inverts this comforting folklore: the "dog" is real, and the darkness it brings is a *Heavenly Calamity*.

## Reading Guide

If you've been waiting for the novel to show you *why* looking at the Dao is a bad idea — here it is. This chapter is a brutal demonstration of the difference between "knowing about cosmic horror" and *seeing it*. Li Huowang has faced demons, flesh Buddhas, and parasitic gods before — but none of them made his jaw unlock and his throat start screaming in someone else's voice. The **crane cry** (鹤鸣) is a particularly chilling detail. Cranes in Chinese culture symbolize immortality and transcendence. Hearing one is supposed to be auspicious. Here, it is a bodily hijacking — a sign that the heavens are singing through him whether he wants it or not.

Also worth noting: Li Huowang's response to the crisis is brutally practical. He doesn't scream and faint. He *gouges out his eyes and keeps moving*. This is the man we've been following for 460+ chapters — broken but never stopped. Even blind, he asks for his bearings. Even blind, he reaches for his sword.

And then the sun comes back. Not because of anything *he* did — but because some random person far away beat a gong. That's the Dao-Twisted World in a nutshell: you can survive cosmic oblivion, and the sky can be saved by a folk superstiton. The universe is both more terrifying and *more absurd* than you can handle.

Fellow cultivators, this is peak *Dao Gui Yi Xian*. Buckle up.
