## Chapter Overview

*Pours one out. Then another.* This chapter hits like a freight train of grief and desperate hope. Li Huowang loses his one true friend in this world, only to find a loophole in the rules of his own cursed existence. It’s a raw, messy, and emotionally devastating pivot where the Heart-Element’s unique nature becomes the only thread left to hold onto. Get ready for tears, violence, and the faintest glimmer of cosmic defiance.

## Key Plot Points

Zhuge Yuan dies in Li Huowang’s arms, prompting a surge of raw grief and impotent rage. In a final, desperate gambit, Li Huowang beheads his friend’s corpse, hoping to force him into becoming a lingering hallucination like the others who haunt him. The attempt initially appears to fail, deepening his despair. As the Intercalating the Five Phases ritual nears its end, Li Huowang forces himself to survive through self-inflicted pain, then successfully negotiates a body-swap back with Bei Feng. Bei Feng, now in a new body, prepares to rejoin the larger battle, but Li Huowang remains frozen, staring at an empty space. To his shock, Zhuge Yuan emerges as a new hallucination, asking for introductions with his characteristic scholarly poise.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The “Intercalating the Five Phases” (闰置五行) is a terrifyingly literal concept drawn from alchemical lore. In traditional cosmology, the Five Phases are cyclical, but to “intercalate” them is to insert an extra, unnatural phase—much like adding a leap month to the calendar. The ritual doesn’t heal; it overwrites. Li Huowang’s hollow chest is a sign that his own physical substance is being consumed, not just as fuel, but as a sacrifice to a higher, unnatural law. The novel’s core twist on “cultivating the false into reality” gives Zhuge Yuan’s resurrection a chillingly plausible mechanism: if Li Huowang’s “hallucinations” are truly real beings born from belief and karma, then a beloved friend can be “remembered” into existence—even if his body is gone.

### Cultivate the False into Reality (修假为真)

The core paradoxical philosophy of this world: lies, if fiercely enough believed and cultivated, can become more real than the truth.

## Reading Guide

*Chef’s kiss at the emotional payoff.* This chapter is a masterclass in emotional payoff wrapped in body horror. The beheading scene isn’t gratuitous—it’s Li Huowang’s *only* way to fight the finality of death in a world where he’s seen the dead linger before. Bei Feng’s snark provides a jarring but necessary tonal shift, reminding us that the supernatural politics haven’t stopped. And that final line—it’s a wink from the author. Zhuge Yuan not *just* a hallucination; he’s proof that Li Huowang’s powers have real metaphysical teeth. Strap in. The game just changed.

## Deeper Dive: The Siming’s Silent Influence

Zhuge Yuan’s death—and his unusual “resurrection”—might be more than just a Heart-Element trick. In Daoist cosmology, a *Siming* (Arbiter of Fate) controls life spans and destinies. Li Huowang’s power to “make fakes real” could be seen as an affront to the natural order these entities maintain. By forcing Zhuge Yuan back into existence, Li Huowang isn’t just grieving; he’s *rewriting fate*. The question lingers: will the Siming let this slide?
