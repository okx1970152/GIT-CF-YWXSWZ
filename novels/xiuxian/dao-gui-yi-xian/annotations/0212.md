## Chapter Overview

This is the chapter where the butcher’s bill comes due. After the unimaginable brutality of the battle against Peng Longteng, “Collect” is the grim, exhausted aftermath—a tally of flesh, sacrifice, and the bitter cost of survival. We see what it means to win in the Dao-Twisted World: not a glorious victory, but a pile of broken bodies, a near-death protagonist held together by rat oil and sheer spite, and the quiet agony of those left maimed. The chapter’s emotional core is not the death of the monster, but the desperate, ugly, beautiful human acts that made it possible. Get ready to feel the weight of every life spent and every limb lost.

## Key Plot Points

- **Impossible survival**: Decapitated Peng Longteng continues to fight, nearly killing Li Huowang before Gao Zhijian heroically intercepts the blow.
- **High-cost rescue**: The Er Shen (veiled beast-head woman) drags Li Huowang to safety, while Gao Zhijian is impaled on Peng Longteng’s halberd.
- **Chun Xiaoman’s sacrifice**: Li Huowang is too weak to use the *Thousand Greats Record*, so Chun Xiaoman takes it upon herself to copy his self-mutilation ritual, chopping off her own arm and unleashing a powerful tentacle attack.
- **Team coordination wins the day**: The Er Shen, with timing from Bai Lingmiao’s spirit-drum and chant, disarms Peng Longteng, allowing the beast-skulls to tear her apart until only an unmoving skeleton remains.
- **The final toll**: Li Huowang blacks out, Gao Zhijian is saved by Bai Lingmiao’s spirit-immortal, but Chun Xiaoman is permanently maimed; the group escapes into Qingqiu territory.
- **A quiet, heavy ending**: Bai Lingmiao tends to Li Huowang’s horrific burns with folk remedies (rat oil) and asks him to share his regrowth ability with Chun Xiaoman, revealing the ongoing, invisible wounds of the battle.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter features some powerful folk-belief elements that deserve a closer look:

- **Rat Oil**: This isn’t just a random gross detail. In traditional Chinese folk medicine, rat oil (rendered from rat fat) was indeed sometimes used as a treatment for burns and cuts. Bai Lingmiao citing her mother’s wisdom grounds this in a specific, rural, and deeply practical folk-healing tradition. It feels visceral and *real* precisely because it *is* a real (if obsolete) practice.

- **The Spirit Chant**: Bai Lingmiao’s chant (“The Ao Fish holds the earth…”) is a fragment of a spirit-summoning verse from the *Bang Bing Jue* (帮兵决, “Helping Soldier Decision”), a traditional ritual text used by Northeastern Chinese shamans (出马仙). The Ao Fish is a mythical giant leviathan (a variation of the Ao turtle) said in Chinese myth to hold up the world on its back. The chant is literally calling upon the earth-shaking power of a primordial entity to disrupt the enemy’s footing. This is not just flavor text; it is a verbal weapon that boosts the team’s coordination.

- **No Golden Elixir Life Saver**: Notice that the “expensive pill” or internal healing method is absent. Li Huowang is not jumping back up after a nap. He is out of commission, lying helplessly as a burned husk. This is the novel’s persistent subversion of xianxia tropes—victory never comes without a devastating, ongoing physical toll. There is no magical fix, only crude rat oil and time.

## Reading Guide

Let’s break down what this chapter tells us about the story’s DNA:

1. **The Cost of the Thousand Greats Record**: Chun Xiaoman’s choice to copy Li Huowang’s self-amputation is a massive character moment. She didn’t just “help”; she permanently crippled herself for the team. This locks in a new dynamic: *anyone* can potentially use the Record’s power, but the price is always the same—it takes a part of you. The ability is never a clean power-up.

2. **Team Evolution**: The fight shows that Li Huowang’s gang has grown from a crowd of frightened survivors into a tactical unit. Gao Zhijian body-blocks, Chun Xiaoman adapts the spell, Bai Lingmiao coordinates with the chant, and the Er Shen provides the killing power. They have developed a working ritual combat system. It’s grim, but it functions.

3. **Li Huowang’s Psychology**: Notice he doesn't feel triumph—only relief and a desperate care for his people (“Save Gao Zhijian”). His smile on the cart is not joy; it’s the smile of a man who is too tired to feel anything except that someone is trying to keep him alive. The emotional weight is on the *debt* he owes the others, which will likely drive his actions later.

4. **The Path Forward**: They have crossed into Qingqiu. The “Fox Hill” territory suggests a new biome and new dangers. But the real question left hanging is: *will Li Huowang teach Chun Xiaoman the regrowth technique?* Bai Lingmiao’s quiet request is a set-up for a future test of his morality and his willingness to share dangerous power.

This chapter is a breather, but it is a breather built on the rubble of a violent climax. Read it for the character development and the heavy silence after the storm.
