## Chapter Overview
Break out the tissues, folks—this one’s a gut-punch. As Li Huowang hallucinates a tender reunion with his mother, Sun Xiaoqin, the chapter weaponizes every scrap of warmth, every ounce of maternal love, and then grinds it all against the hard stone of his obligation. He’s brought gold from the other side—enough to buy back their house, enough to send his parents on that retirement trip they always dreamed of—but it’s a farewell gift. He’s making peace with a version of reality he’s about to leave behind forever. This isn’t a fight scene; it’s a slow, deliberate goodbye, and it hurts. The chapter ends with a brutal, visceral anchor: Li Huowang, back in Qingqiu, weeping while he forces down the raw flesh of the Black Tai Sui. All the hope he just experienced is already turning to ash in his mouth.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang regains lucidity in the hallucinatory hospital and finds his mother Sun Xiaoqin has been caring for him around the clock.
- He gives her a box of gold and a pair of jade earrings, explaining that the gold can buy their house back and fund their retirement dreams. He asks her to pass the earrings to Yang Na as an apology and a message: don’t wait for me.
- His mother panics at the finality of his words and runs to fetch the doctor, but Li Huowang confronts Doctor Wang Wei one last time, proposing a wager: if Wang can “wake him up,” he’ll admit this is reality. He then closes his eyes.
- Li Huowang returns to the Dao-Twisted World in Qingqiu, distributes the gold box to Sun Baolu, and immediately heads to the tent holding the Black Tai Sui.
- He eats the Black Tai Sui’s flesh while crying uncontrollably, watched in silence by his companions.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter showcases a hallmark of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*’s psychological horror: the hallucination is not a monster. It’s a mother. Sun Xiaoqin’s love for her son is genuine, tender, and deeply protective—she’s not a trick or a trap. The horror lies entirely in Li Huowang’s *choice to leave her*. This inverts the classic structure of a temptress-illusion; here, the “temptation” is a safe, warm home and a loving parent, and rejecting it is the heroic (and tragic) act.

The Black Tai Sui also deserves a moment in the spotlight. In Chinese folk tradition, **Tai Sui (太岁)** is a powerful god of the year, often feared and appeased. But here it is rendered as a physical, parasitic substance. Li Huowang consumes it not for power but to suppress his own madness—a grotesque inversion of the classic “eating a divine herb for immortality” trope. Every mouthful is a step away from the warmth he just experienced and into a darker form of existence.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in emotional pacing. The first half, set in the hallucination, is soft, slow, and full of love—Sun Xiaoqin’s anxious fussing, the tangerine peel, the warm weight of the gold box in her hands. It feels like a happy ending. It *wants* you to believe this could be real.

Then Li Huowang turns his head away. He thinks of Bai Lingmiao’s beaten face. And the entire warmth shatters.

Pay special attention to the line where he chooses to return: “I can’t abandon reality for a fake hallucination.” It’s not triumphant. It’s grim. Li Huowang doesn’t reject this reality because he doubts it. He rejects it because he *recognizes* the love he’s leaving behind, and he chooses to do it anyway. That distinction—between ignorance and willful sacrifice—is what makes the final scene so devastating. He’s not fighting a monster. He’s fighting his own desire to be held.
