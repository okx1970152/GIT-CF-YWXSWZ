## Chapter Overview
Homecoming is a beast in any story, but for Li Huowang, it’s a special kind of emotional minefield. This chapter finally delivers on the long-awaited promise of returning to Niuxin Village. But instead of a triumphant hero’s welcome, what we get is something far more human and far more awkward. Li Huowang stands at the same spot where he once contemplated confessing to mass murder, now contemplating how to explain his latest batch of horrors. The village is alive, the people are happy, and for a moment, our perpetually miserable protagonist gets a taste of peace, right before a bombshell named Li Sui drops the word "Mother" and sends the domestic tranquility spiraling.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang returns to Niuxin Village, standing at the exact spot where he previously contemplated confessing to killing Bai Lingmiao’s family.
- The village is visibly more prosperous in spring, with fields full of young, earnest workers.
- Mantou the dog maintains his unyielding hostility toward Li Sui, barking and growling despite repeated scoldings.
- Li Huowang reflects on his relationship with Li Sui, acknowledging he can’t just abandon her and decides to keep her like Mantou.
- The villagers greet him warmly, including Yang Xiao Hai, Gou Wa, Chun Xiao Man, and Zhao Wu, who reports on the village’s finances.
- Lü Zhuangyuan welcomes him with a brazier to ward off bad luck, and everyone joins in a busy preparation for a feast.
- Bai Lingmiao greets him from the second-floor window, seeming to have moved past their earlier conflict.
- Hong Zhong tries to follow Li Huowang upstairs to witness the drama, but Zhuge Yuan blocks him.
- Bai Lingmiao helps Li Huowang with his gear but is shocked and outraged when he introduces Li Sui, who calls her “Mother.” Bai Lingmiao instantly suspects Yang Na is the mother.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The homecoming trope is a staple of xianxia, but *Dao Gui Yi Xian* subverts it beautifully. Li Huowang returns not as a conquering hero with a treasure trove, but as a man carrying a tentacled monster he calls his daughter and a head full of trauma he can’t share. The brazier (火盆, huǒpén) Lü Zhuangyuan sets up is a real-world folk tradition: stepping over a brazier of burning coals after a long journey or a funeral is believed to purify the traveler, burning away bad luck and evil influences before re-entering the home. It's a small, quiet detail that adds a layer of authentic Chinese folk-horror texture to the scene—the world may be twisted, but the rituals of everyday life still matter.

## Reading Guide
Ah, the sweet, awkward tension of a family reunion! This chapter is a masterclass in emotional pacing. After so much pain and cosmic horror, we’re given a moment of pure, unadulterated domesticity, only to have it shattered by a single, perfectly timed line. Li Huowang’s thought that “maybe this is what home feels like” is a dagger of hope, which makes Bai Lingmiao’s subsequent explosion all the more delicious and painful. Notice how the chapter doesn’t shy away from the mundane details—the finances, the pregnant wife, the dog’s stubbornness—it grounds the cosmic in the local, making the weirdness of Li Sui standing there in a rain cloak feel even more jarring. Pay attention to the dynamic between Hong Zhong and Zhuge Yuan, too; it’s a classic “chaos agent vs. moral compass” setup, and knowing their respective factions, this is a powder keg waiting for a spark.

## Deeper Dive: The "Mother" Bombshell
This single line, “Mother,” is a narrative trap door. For Bai Lingmiao, it’s a direct challenge to her role and a revival of deep insecurity regarding Yang Na. For Li Huowang, it’s an impossible explanation that breaks every social norm. For the reader, it’s a perfect blend of comedy and horror—the image of this shapeless, tentacled entity under a rain cloak chirping out “Mother” while a woman loses her mind is pure *Dao Gui Yi Xian* genius. The chapter deliberately never gives Li Huowang the time to explain, dropping us right into the emotional fallout.
