## Chapter Overview

Just when the errand seems done, they run right into the new—and **very** familiar—Emperor of Great Liang. Li Huowang barely avoids a lethal clash with the Grand Preceptor and has to decide in a split-second whether to stay invisible or show his hand. It's a short chapter, but the tension is razor-sharp, and the setup for an unexpected reconnection is brilliant.

## Key Plot Points

- Eunuch Cao takes delivery of the Ling Nie, issuing the treasury receipt with practiced bureaucratic flair. Lian Zhibei's fawning shows she's a pro at palace navigation.
- Li Huowang gets chewed out for forgetting to bring bribe silver for the treasury staff—a grim reminder that even in the Supervisory Heavenly Office, nothing gets paid without "tips."
- The new emperor's procession arrives just as they're about to leave.
- Li Huowang refuses to kneel and turns invisible. *Lucky guess?* Not quite. A white-haired Daoist—the Grand Preceptor—immediately senses him.
- Cornered and about to be stabbed, Li Huowang reveals himself and flashes his Office medallion.
- The Grand Preceptor is *not* amused, but before he can dress down the logistics, the teenage emperor does a double-take and joyfully calls out, "It's you!!"—the same boy who conned Li Huowang into buying a fake cat for 500 taels on the Shangsi Festival.
- The chapter ends with the emperor trying to rush over, only to be blocked by two old eunuchs.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**This chapter drips with imperial court protocol and hierarchical dread.** Let's break down a few key details:
- **The "Grand Steward" (大总管)**: The chief eunuch of the inner palace, usually the most powerful attendant in the emperor's household. That he was a former member of the Supervisory Heavenly Office tells us how much institutional crossover exists between the eunuch bureaucracy and supernatural enforcement—a corruption-generating overlap if ever there was one.
- **"Easy to deal with the King of Hell, but hard to handle the little devils" (阎王好惹，小鬼难缠)**: A classic Chinese proverb meaning the top boss may be reasonable, but you have to bribe every petty clerk along the way. It's a fundamental truth of *guanxi*—networks of personal favors and unofficial payments that grease the wheels of any premodern bureaucracy, and the Office is no exception.
- **Three emperors in a few months (几个月换三个皇帝)**: Li Huowang's offhand observation is a *massive* red flag. In Chinese history, rapid imperial turnover like this signals either a puppet regime, widespread factional poisoning, or a curse-level instability. The Dao-Twisted World's throne is clearly as rotten as its temples.
- **The Grand Preceptor (国师 / Guoshi)**: A title conferred on a supreme religious or Daoist advisor to the emperor. In this world, he's the old Daoist with the star-sword who went toe-to-toe with the Zuowandao. His blind-eye-opening trick and instant ability to lock Li Huowang's body in place confirms he's operating at a realm Li Huowang can't casually ignore.
- **The teenage emperor conning Li Huowang**: Remember that fake "jade cat" back during the Shangsi Festival (上巳节—the ancient Chinese Valentine's Day when people went out to meet lovers)? That was **this kid**—a bored, mischievous crown prince sneaking out to have fun. Now he's the *Emperor*. Hello, dramatic irony.

## Reading Guide

**Classic "oh no, I've met this guy before" payoff.** The chapter is a short, effective scene-setting piece that builds toward a potentially explosive new relationship. The teenage emperor recognizing Li Huowang is a ticking time bomb of political intrigue: will the boy be grateful for the memory, or will he exact revenge for the fake cat? The Grand Preceptor's hostility toward the Office—"You dare let an authorized operative into the palace?"—also hints at a brewing power struggle between the throne, the Office, and the Daoist establishment. Plus, the shift from three months of grind to *this* moment? That's what keeps *Dao Gui Yi Xian* feeling alive: no matter how high the stakes get, the story remembers that politics and personality matter just as much as rituals and monsters. Brace yourselves for some very awkward courtly encounters ahead.
