## Chapter Overview

This is not an action chapter. It is a *surgical* chapter. A chapter where the scalpel's edge is turned inward—on Li Huowang's psychology, on the cost of his new ability, and on the fragile bonds that still hold his group together. Wang Wei, the unorthodox psychiatrist, delivers a brutal, targeted psychological attack designed to break Li Huowang out of his emotional shell, and it works—by provoking a violent reaction that becomes a gruesome object lesson. The chapter pivots from a tense psychiatric breakdown to the quiet, devastating aftermath: a bruised face, a whispered promise, and a chillingly lucid realization about the danger of giving power to a mind that can't control itself. Get ready for some serious emotional damage, fellow travelers. This one hurts.

## Key Plot Points

*   Wang Wei, Li Huowang's new psychiatrist, uses a confrontational, personally targeted provocation, attacking Li Huowang's relationship with his mother and Yang Na. He accuses Li Huowang of being selfish, a coward, and completely unworthy of their sacrifice.
*   The provocation succeeds. Li Huowang breaks free from his restraints and physically attacks Wang Wei.
*   Wang Wei fights back, revealing he was once a patient himself, creating a chilling parallel to Li Huowang's current state. He is then sedated by the staff.
*   Li Huowang regains consciousness in the Dao-Twisted World, hanging upside down. He has unconsciously used his ability to phase through the earth where he was buried, confirming a disturbing new facet of his power.
*   The horror of this ability sinks in. Li Huowang realizes he is a "violent madman holding a knife." The power is not a gift; it's a potential catastrophe.
*   He returns to camp and sees the face of Bai Lingmiao, which he has bruised in a violent fugue state. This physical evidence of his danger is the chapter's emotional climax.
*   Overwhelmed with guilt and fear, Li Huowang begs Bai Lingmiao to stay away from him if it happens again. She agrees, but her silent refusal to truly accept this is deeply telling.
*   The chapter ends with a chilling internal resolution as Li Huowang's gaze falls on the question he carved in the dirt: a legendary Zuowandao Heart-Element who lived to 190. The implication is clear—he is now actively seeking a path forward, one that might not be heroic.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The "Real" World as a Therapeutic Battleground:** This chapter is a masterclass in using the "mundane" half of the story. Wang Wei's tactic is a raw, emotionally manipulative form of "reality therapy" or "provocative therapy." He's not trying to soothe Li Huowang; he's trying to force him to *feel* something real, even if that feeling is rage. The goal is to break through emotional dissociation—a common symptom in severe trauma and psychosis. The "hallucination" of the Dao-Twisted World is Li Huowang's comfort zone; the sterile, confrontational hospital room is the battlefield for his sanity.

**The Power of a "Violent Madman":** This is a crucial thematic beat that inverts the classic xianxia power fantasy. Normally, gaining a new ability like phasing through the ground is a level-up. Here, it's a terrifying liability. The phrase "a violent madman holding a knife" drives home the novel's core horror: power without stable identity is not empowerment; it's a weapon aimed at everyone the protagonist loves. Li Huowang's reaction (self-loathing, the plea for distance) is the opposite of a triumphant cultivator's.

**The Zuowandao Question:** The final line, carved in the dirt, is a rope thrown across the abyss. A 190-year-old Heart-Element who "escaped the hallucinations." Is this a genuine historical fact from the Dao-Twisted World, or another cruel lie from the Zuowandao to give Li Huowang false hope? The question mark at the end (?) is the novel's signature—leaving the answer ambiguous, forcing the reader to inhabit Li Huowang's own inability to trust any narrative.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a emotional gut-punch. Don't skim the fight sequence; feel the raw, ugly desperation of it. Wang Wei isn't a villain; he's a doctor with a ruthless diagnosis. The real horror isn't the punch Li Huowang throws at him, but the one he throws at Bai Lingmiao when he's not looking.

Pay close attention to Li Huowang's internal monologue after he sees her bruised face. The shift is immediate and profound. The fantasy of "having power" crashes head-on into the nightmare of "losing control." The whispered promise he makes to Bai Lingmiao is heartbreaking, but it's a promise he's already planning to break—he's looking for a cure, and that cure might involve becoming even more of a monster.

And that final line? It's not hope. It's a threat. A threat to his own sense of self. The safety of the group has just been found wanting, and Li Huowang is about to start looking for answers in darker places.
