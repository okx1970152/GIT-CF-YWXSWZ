## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a pure psychological duel set entirely within the modern reality (White Tower Psychiatric Hospital). Li Huowang, fresh from the horrors of Siqi, is confronted by his new attending physician, Wang Wei. What starts as a seemingly casual interaction with a chatty nurse quickly unravels into a calculated, diagnostic trap. Wang Wei exposes his own ruse and launches a sophisticated assault on Li Huowang’s identity, using the doctor's ultimate weapon: framing the terrifying, world-shattering abilities Li Huowang has earned in the Dao-Twisted World as mere symptoms of schizophrenia. It’s a masterclass in the novel’s core conflict—is the power real, or is it the disease itself? Wang Wei isn't a physical enemy but a logical one, and his final threat—of abandonment and a forgotten death in a cheap ward—cuts deeper than any claw or claw.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang recognizes a new voice from his previous phone call—Nurse Wang, a man with a mole and a chatty demeanor. His irritation is immediate and palpable.
- After a heated exchange where Nurse Wang mockingly asks about Li Huowang's "her" (referring to Bai Lingmiao), Li Huowang’s anger shifts to cold suspicion. He deduces this "nurse" is actually a psychologist, having sent his mother away to avoid her breaking the act.
- Li Huowang's trap is sprung: he points out the man's lies. The "Nurse" stops his act and calls in Doctor Hou, revealing himself to be the senior psychiatrist, Wang Wei.
- Wang Wei explains his deception: he used a nurse's identity because Li Huowang is resistant to doctors. He then delivers a devastating logical attack.
- Wang Wei uses the very nature of Li Huowang's supernatural "body misplacement" ability—the quintessential Dao-Twisted World power—as *evidence* of a worsening schizophrenic delusion.
- He plays his trump card: a pragmatic threat of economic ruin for his family and eventual abandonment. He warns that Li Huowang’s mother and girlfriend will eventually give up, leading to a lonely incarceration in a cheap institution.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a brilliant inversion of the typical xianxia power fantasy. In most cultivation novels, gaining a special ability (like spatial misplacement) is a triumphant achievement. Here, that same ability is weaponized *against* the protagonist by a modern psychiatrist. Wang Wei isn't lying; *within the framework of his reality*, he is perfectly correct. A delusion that manifests as a "skill" is still a symptom of a worsening condition. This blurs the line between "power" and "pathology" so profoundly that it becomes the novel's central existential horror. The "practice" of the Dao-Twisted World is reframed as the "delusional progression" of a disease. The moment Li Huowang stops seeing himself as a cultivator and starts seeing himself as a patient in this game of psychological Go is the moment he might lose entirely. Wang Wei’s final tactic—appealing to the crushing cost of medical care and the threat of familial abandonment—is a masterstroke of "grounded" horror that is uniquely modern and devastating. It’s not a monster that threatens him, but debt and loneliness.

### White Tower Psychiatric Hospital

The modern-world counterpart to the White Jade Capital in the novel’s meta-structure; a private psychiatric hospital.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a chapter where the only weapons are words and the only battlefield is Li Huowang’s mind. This is a high-stakes psychological confrontation between a trauma-hardened survivor and a calm, professional interrogator. Pay close attention to how Wang Wei shifts his tactics: from the "friendly nurse" persona to the logical examiner exploiting Li Huowang's own beliefs against him. His argument—that the power Li Huowang is so proud of is just a more complex symptom—is a masterfully cruel form of gaslighting. It’s not a battle of fists, but a war of definitions. The true horror here is not the monster under the bed, but the doctor beside it, calmly explaining why the bed itself is the only reality you have left.
