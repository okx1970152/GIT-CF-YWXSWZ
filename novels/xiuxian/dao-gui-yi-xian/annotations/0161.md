## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, hold on tight, because Chapter 161 is a brutal, gut-punch of a reckoning. The game has finally changed. After enduring endless torment and psychological warfare from the Zuowandao trickster Erbing, Li Huowang isn't just fighting back—he's *winning*. The revelation that Jiang Yingzi was real, and that her final message reframes his entire Ao-Jing Sect ordeal as one massive, layered deception, sparks a cold, terrifying fury in our protagonist. We watch him dismantle an enemy not with a complex spell, but by finally understanding what makes a Zuowandao madman *tick*—and then exploiting that weakness with savage, bloody efficiency. But victory here is a hollow, bloody affair. Li Huowang emerges from the haze as a figure of pure, terrifying will, but the cost is measured in the lives he couldn't save and the distance he's placed between himself and his own humanity. This isn't a power-up; it's a psychological breakthrough that leaves everyone around him, including us, wondering just how much further he can fall.

## Key Plot Points
- **Jiang Yingzi's Revelation**: Before vanishing, Jiang Yingzi confirms the Ao-Jing Sect elders were all Zuowandao in disguise, and that Danyangzi is not a hallucination but a real, active entity. This shatters Li Huowang's current understanding of his reality.
- **Erbing's Defeat**: Li Huowang's cold, calculated taunt ("Guess") is his true victory here. By denying Erbing the satisfaction of knowing *how* she was outplayed, he strikes at the very core of a Zuowandao's identity—the game itself. His subsequent execution of her is a visceral, hate-filled act.
- **Li Huowang's Frenzy**: His slaughter of Erbing's body descends into a frenzied, near-catatonic rage, from which Bai Lingmiao must physically pull him back. This moment showcases the deep, unstable well of violence within him.
- **Danyangzi's Confirmation**: As Li Huowang regains his senses, the three-faced Danyangzi appears, smiles, and sinks into the earth. His existence is now an unquestionable fact, a permanent scar on Li Huowang's reality.
- **The Lü Troupe's Departure**: Terrified by Li Huowang's true nature, the Lü family severs their ties, fleeing back the way they came. This is a crucial social consequence to his actions.
- **The Aftermath of Battle**: The chapter closes on a profound note of loss. Two more people are dead. Li Huowang, numb and weary, makes the decision to march back toward Siqi, hinting at a dark purpose yet to be revealed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The Three Corpses (三尸)**: The chapter's closing image, Danyangzi's three-faced form, is a perfect echo of a core Daoist concept. The *San Shi* are three internal, parasitic demons that reside in the human body (head, chest, belly) and are responsible for the Seven Emotions. They crave the host's death so they can become ghosts. 'Cutting off the Three Corpses' is a foundational step to immortality in Daoist alchemy. In *Dao Gui Yi Xian*, this ancient concept is weaponized into a horrifying, externalized entity that stalks the protagonist, suggesting his own internal demons have become tangible.
- **The Psychology of Zuowandao**: The "game" is the key to understanding this faction. Unlike Western trickster gods who might revel in chaos for its own sake, the Zuowandao's joy is derived from the *act of deception itself*. To be proven wrong, to be figured out, is the only true defeat for them. This makes them terrifyingly unpredictable—they will endure any physical pain to maintain the integrity of their 'play.' Li Huowang's epiphany that "some things are more important than life" is his first true step toward combatting them on their own psychological battleground.
- **The Bleak Aftermath**: The chapter subverts the classic "victory celebration." There is no loot, no new technique, no respite. There are only dead companions, terrified allies fleeing, and a protagonist who is more alienated than ever. This gritty, consequence-driven structure is a hallmark of the genre's departure from traditional 'level-up' power fantasy, as established in the core style guide.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a turning point that will leave you feeling hollow and unsettled. Don't expect a triumphant hero's pose. Watch Li Huowang's psychological evolution carefully. He's not just fighting monsters anymore; he's learning to *think* like them. The cold, clinical way he analyzes Erbing's weakness, followed by the animalistic frenzy of the execution, perfectly illustrates the double-edged nature of his survival instinct. Pay close attention to the social fallout—the Lü troupe's terror is a powerful signal from the narrative that Li Huowang is becoming something even the hard-luck characters of this world fear to be near. The decision to return to Siqi is foreboding. He's not going back for healing or peace. He's going back because he has a new, grim purpose. The biggest question this chapter leaves us with isn't *how* Li Huowang will defeat his enemies, but what part of himself he will have to sacrifice to do it.

## Deeper Dive: The Horror of a Solved Mystery
One of the most chilling aspects of this chapter is the revelation about the Ao-Jing Sect. Think about it. Zuowandao didn't just appear to hassle him; they *infiltrated* his earliest safe harbor in this world. They were wearing the faces of the people who were supposed to "heal" him after Danyangzi. This retroactively poisons Li Huowang's entire timeline, making us question every interaction he's had since his escape from Qingfeng Temple. This is a masterclass in crafting dread—not by showing a new monster, but by re-framing the past as a lie.
