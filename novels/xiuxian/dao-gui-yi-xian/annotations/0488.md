## Chapter Overview

After a hard-won healing, Bai Lingmiao can finally “see” again—though her new perception is anything but ordinary. The chapter opens on a rare moment of domestic joy: a family celebration over roast goose, a brief respite from the endless horror. But the peace doesn’t last. Enter Lian Zhibei, the nosy, portly auntie who was supposed to be off fetching Li Huowang’s mother. Instead, she’s back with a sudden change of heart, offering to sever the immortal’s connection to Bai Lingmiao. What follows is a tense, claustrophobic interplay of trust and suspicion, where an intimate medical procedure becomes a stage for cryptic passphrases and watchful paranoia.

## Key Plot Points

- Bai Lingmiao successfully uses her new mind-eye perception, seeing not just surfaces but the inner details of the world—insects inside bark, the movement of life around her. Her joy is pure, and Li Huowang allows himself a moment of earned satisfaction.
- Lian Zhibei returns from her errand abruptly, claiming she no longer needs to bring her mother and can now sever the immortal’s connection to Bai Lingmiao. Her sudden change of plan and eagerness are immediate red flags.
- Li Huowang and the Er Shen have a quiet, loaded conversation outside the treatment room. The Er Shen accepts that removing the immortal will mean her own dissolution back into Bai Lingmiao, a fate she treats as acceptable.
- Inside the treatment room, Lian Zhibei abruptly tests Bai Lingmiao with a cryptic two-line poem that sounds like a passphrase. Bai Lingmiao’s genuine confusion halts the secret exchange.
- Li Huowang catches the suspicious behavior, confronts Lian Zhibei with a veiled threat, and sets Li Sui inside the room as a watchdog.
- The chapter ends with Bai Lingmiao emerging, her body painted in red cinnabar talismans over a yellow paper vest—a ritual preparation that is as eerie as it is intimate.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Lian Zhibei’s whispered passphrase—“Curtain hides the jade peak, storing the night snow; the foundation is laid by blue water, growing autumn moss”—is not random nonsense. It reads like a coded verse typical of sectarian or underground religious networks in Chinese fiction. The “jade peak” and “night snow” are common euphemisms in classical poetry and inner alchemy texts, often referring to hidden or esoteric knowledge. The fact that Lian Zhibei uses it as a recognition signal suggests she may be part of a secret organization—possibly the White Lotus or a related sect—testing whether Bai Lingmiao is an initiate. The failure of the passphrase (Bai Lingmiao’s genuine confusion) tells Lian Zhibei that her target is not “in the know,” which forces her to continue the operation under a different guise. This kind of layered sectarian verification is a staple of historical martial arts and xianxia fiction, where a single misspoken line can mean life or death.

### Mind-eye perception (心眼通)

A supernatural sense that allows a character to perceive qi, auras, and hidden presences; attained via a Buddhist relic in this novel, it reveals the true, unstable nature of beings.

### Passphrase (暗号)

A secret word or phrase used to verify identity or affiliation within a closed group, common in sectarian and jianghu (martial underworld) fiction.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in tension disguised as a domestic scene. The joy of Bai Lingmiao’s recovery is genuine, but the moment Lian Zhibei knocks on that door, the atmosphere thickens. Pay close attention to the Er Shen’s quiet acceptance of her own erasure—it’s a poignant beat that adds moral complexity to what could have been a simple “cure.” And that whispered passphrase? It’s the kind of detail that rewards re-reading. Li Huowang’s suspicion is not paranoia; it’s survival instinct. The chapter ends on a note of ritualized intimacy (the painted body) that is both beautiful and deeply unsettling—a perfect distillation of what makes this novel’s horror so effective.

## Er Shen’s Sacrifice

This chapter deepens the Er Shen’s characterization in a quietly devastating way. She willingly accepts her own dissolution—being reabsorbed into Bai Lingmiao—as a fair price for removing the immortal. It’s a selfless act from a being who has always existed in the margins of personhood. Li Huowang’s hesitation is telling: he doesn’t know if he should see her as a person or a fragment. The Er Shen’s calm logic—the immortal is a ticking time bomb—leaves no room for sentiment. Her fate is left hanging, making this one of the chapter’s most emotionally charged undercurrents.
