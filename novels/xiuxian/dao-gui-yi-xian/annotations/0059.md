## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang finally gets a straight—and deeply unsettling—answer about what a “Chuma Disciple” actually is, and discovers that the price for supernatural help in this world is never money. As he presses the talkative spirit-dancer Li Zhi for details, the real horror isn’t the entity, but the system: you don’t hire an immortal; an immortal hires *you*, and it will take whatever it wants. Li Zhi’s carefree detachment, contrasted with his eerily silent “wife,” the Er Shen, plants a seed of both hope and dread in Li Huowang’s mind.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang learns the origin of a Chuma disciple: a near-fatal illness, a dream-visit from an immortal, and the healer’s fee is lifelong service. The immortals are “not human-shaped.”
- Li Zhi explains the reach of a Chuma: “Anything. Drive off evil, change luck, weddings, funerals, fortune-telling.” He confirms they can handle possession, even “something” as stubborn as Danyangzi.
- The payment structure is split: Li Zhi charges a flat fifty copper coins (new coins, not clipped), but the immortal’s fee is *non-negotiable*. The immortal picks something intangible from the client, and the client is forbidden from ever speaking of what was taken.
- Li Huowang’s hope is tempered by observation: the “Second Spirit” walking beside Li Zhi never eats, drinks, or speaks, and moves with the precise gait of a corpse. Li Zhi calls her his wife.
- Bai Lingmiao voices her unease first, proposing they separate from the duo. Li Huowang decides to wait and observe, recognizing that this might be his best shot at escaping Danyangzi.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Chuma (出马)**: A distinctively northeastern Chinese tradition of spirit-mediumship. A mortal is chosen by an animal-spirit immortal (e.g., fox, weasel, hedgehog, snake) to serve as its bodily vessel. The medium gains supernatural abilities but owes lifelong loyalty and service. The “chuma disciple” is not a cultivator climbing a power ladder; they are a servant in a supernatural debt-bondage system.

**Er Shen (二神)**: The “Second Spirit” is a helper or assistant in a spirit-dance ritual. In this chapter, the Er Shen is presented as a veiled, silent woman who never eats or speaks and walks with robotic precision. Li Zhi calls her his wife—but the text strongly implies she is not human, adding a layer of folk-horror to the concept. According to novel lore, the Er Shen can possess her own will and secrets, capable of independent communication outside the medium’s awareness.

**The Immortal’s Fee**: Immortals do not take money. They pick something intangible—often a portion of the client’s lifespan, health, or luck. The client is forbidden to discuss what was taken, under penalty of bad luck. This “no-talk” rule is a classic folk taboo used to prevent rumors from weakening the spirit’s power.

## Reading Guide
This is the chapter where the “rules of engagement” in the Dao-Twisted World get codified. Li Huowang is no longer just surviving; he’s actively *collecting* system knowledge. Pay attention to his internal shift: he hears the fifty-coin price and immediately runs the cost-benefit math—this is a man who has learned that nothing here comes free. The real terror isn’t the transaction itself, but the taboo on speaking about what was taken. It means we, the reader, might never know what Li Huowang will lose.

Also, keep an eye on the Er Shen. She says nothing, does nothing, moves like clockwork, and is called “wife.” The chills are in the details: the lack of eating, the fixed step length, the complete absence of will. The fact that Li Zhi is so casual about it is its own kind of horror—he’s normalized owning something that was once (probably) a person.

Get ready, fellow Daoists. The bill for Li Huowang’s survival is about to come due.
