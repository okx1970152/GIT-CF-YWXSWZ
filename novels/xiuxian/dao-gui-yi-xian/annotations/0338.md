## Chapter Overview

Oof. Chapter 338 is a brutal, claustrophobic gut-punch that masterfully weaponizes the novel’s core theme: paranoia. After a tense standoff with the repulsive Master Xinchi, Li Huowang finally thinks the group is forming a cohesive hunting party. But when the temple dramatically collapses, the dust settles not on a clear enemy, but on a three-way showdown where **Li Huowang himself** is suddenly the designated target. This isn't a fight against monsters; it’s a viciously efficient character assassination, using the group's own established rules and trust against our favorite paranoid protagonist. Get ready for a betrayal that feels less like a plot twist and more like a logical, terrifying extension of the world's logic.

## Key Plot Points

- The chapter opens with Li Huowang and Master Xinchi butting heads. Li Huowang questions the monk’s infallibility, and shows his ruthlessness by using his blade to expose a small, hideous creature hiding inside a prayer mat. The power dynamic is set: they are allied, but not friends.
- The Chief Recorder steps in to quell the dispute, revealing his authority over both men. The search party continues to wander the Bone Buddha Temple for half an hour, finding nothing but the earlier ambush, which deepens the suspicion that the Zuowandao are either long gone or actively hiding.
- The atmosphere shifts when the Chief Recorder discovers the real reason for his visit: he uses a complex ritual to open a hidden compartment within a mummified Buddha portrait, seeking something called “Xin Zhuo.” The cavity is empty, implying someone has beaten them to the prize.
- The temple hall suddenly collapses. Hong Da, the beggar-like Credit Seller, gives the only warning. After the dust settles, Li Huowang is rescued by Tuoba Danqing, but their brief camaraderie is shattered when Master Xinchi appears and immediately uses his ‘Mind Reading’ ability to accuse Li Huowang of being a fake.
- A deadly standoff forms. Tuoba Danqing and Xinchi corner Li Huowang. Li Huowang’s attempts to argue that the monk could be the impostor are shot down with cold, bureaucratic logic from Tuoba Danqing: the Chief Recorder calculated that learning Mind-Reading takes three hours, so Xinchi is considered verified.
- Master Xinchi confirms his pettiness, admitting he is framing Li Huowang out of spite for their earlier argument. He, Tuoba Danqing, and the third party, Hong Da, all attack Li Huowang simultaneously, leaving him completely cornered and questioning whether this was always a trap meant for him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Mind-Reading (他心通)**: This ability is one of the “supernatural powers” (神通, shentong) in Buddhist and Daoist lore. It literally means “knowing the thoughts of others.” In xianxia, it is a rare and powerful skill often associated with high-level monks or enlightened beings. The Chief Recorder’s use of a strict “three-hour training” rule as a verification method is a perfect example of how this world’s logic is meticulously bureaucratic, even when dealing with the supernatural.
**The Collapse as a Trope**: In Chinese martial arts and xianxia fiction, a sudden, convenient collapse is a classic storyteller’s device. It serves to isolate characters, shift the battlefield, and, most importantly, sow chaos and destroy trust. In the “Dao-Twisted World,” it also serves as an environmental attack—a very mundane, physical horror applied to a supernatural conflict, making it feel both calculated and terrifyingly real.
**The Mummified Buddha (佛骨寺)**: The “Bone Buddha Temple” setting is a corrupted take on a sacred space. In Chinese folk religion and Buddhism, relics—especially mummified remains of saintly monks (肉身佛, flesh-body Buddha)—are objects of veneration. Here, that sacred, preserved power is hollowed out, used as a hiding place for a stolen prize (Xin Zhuo), and ultimately weaponized as a trap. The sacred is literally gutted.

### Renxiao (人魈)

A person who lives past 120 without achieving immortality and forcibly extends their life with elixirs, resulting in a malformed, blind monster. Obsession (执念) must be dispelled before the age of 120 to avoid this fate.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow survivors, let’s talk about this absolute betrayal. This chapter is a masterclass in using *systemic logic* to create horror. Notice how the Zuowandao didn't need to be present to win. They simply needed to plant the seed of the ambush and leave. The group’s own internal rules—the Chief Recorder’s authority, Xinchi’s priestly vanity, Tuoba Danqing’s faith in the system—did the rest.
Also, watch how Li Huowang’s paranoia has been validated. He was suspicious of Xinchi, he felt the Chief Recorder had a hidden agenda, and now he’s been left alone to fight three people at once. The tragedy is that his instincts were correct, but they were too slow for the targeted, personal attack Xinchi launched.
Finally, keep an eye on that “Xin Zhuo” (心浊). The name shares the “Xin” (心) radical with “Heart-Element” (心素). This is a huge clue. The Chief Recorder wasn’t here for the Zuowandao; he was hunting another type of humanoid treasure. This confirms that Li Huowang is only one part of a larger, terrifying ecosystem of people who are less human and more “resource.” The world just got a whole lot bigger—and more dangerous—for him.
