## Chapter Overview
Holy *crap*, folks. After a long and harrowing separation, Li Huowang finally reunites with Bai Lingmiao—but she's not the same woman he left behind. She's the Saintess of the White Lotus Society now, with snow-white eyes, a calm that borders on the inhuman, and a direct line to a deity she calls *Wusheng Laomu*. And here's the kicker: Li Huowang *feels* her. Not as a delusion, but as a genuine Siming. For one dizzying, terrifying moment, he touches the mind of a god who radiates nothing but pure compassion. This chapter is a quiet, philosophical bombshell. It doesn't explode; it glows with an eerie, maternal light that might just be the most hopeful—and most unsettling—thing we've seen in a long time.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang finds Bai Lingmiao among the White Lotus believers on the palace steps. She is blind, serene, and claims she will now protect *him*.
- Lian Zhibei reveals herself: the familiar, fawning woman was a mask; the fervent White Lotus devotee is the real face.
- Bai Lingmiao explains that she was chosen by Wusheng Laomu (the Unborn Venerable Mother), a deity her parents worshipped all their lives. She believes their souls have returned to the "Native Land of True Emptiness."
- Li Huowang initially wants to refute her, to drag her out of the delusion, but hesitates. She is at peace. She was in agony before. He knows what it's like to be forced out of that comfort.
- Through Bai Lingmiao's touch, Li Huowang experiences Wusheng Laomu directly—a presence of immense, almost unbearable compassion, veiled behind immortal floss to protect him from her true form.
- His conclusion is staggering: Wusheng Laomu is a real Siming, and she presides over the Way of Compassion. He contrasts her with his own patron, Bashe, the god of pain.
- Despite the revelation, the immediate threat remains: The Dice. Li Huowang gears up for the fight, wary but cautiously hopeful that Miaomiao's new power might actually be an asset.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Wusheng Laomu (无生老母)** is one of the most important figures in Chinese folk religious sects, especially those associated with the White Lotus tradition. Her name translates to "Unborn Venerable Mother" or "Eternal Mother." In sectarian belief, she is the creator of all humanity, a primordial mother who sent her children into the corrupt, suffering world and awaits their return to the "Native Land of True Emptiness" (真空家乡)—a paradise before creation, free from pain and delusion. Prayers to her often invoke a sense of homesickness. In this chapter, her characterization as a Siming of pure compassion is a masterfully twisted appropriation: the familiar, comforting mother figure is now a *cosmic deity*, and her "love" carries the same ontological weight as Bashe's "pain." The Dao-Twisted World finally has a mother, and she's real.

**The White Lotus Society (白莲教)** was a historical religious movement that existed in various forms from the Yuan Dynasty through the Qing. It was not a single unified church but a loose network of secret societies and sectarian groups. They were often persecuted by imperial authorities for their millenarian beliefs, and rebellions—most famously the White Lotus Rebellion (1796–1804)—were fought in its name. In the novel, this underground, rebellious, and devout energy is preserved, but it is now backed by a very tangible, very real Siming.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a tonal shift, fellow Daoists. After countless chapters of visceral body horror, psychological torture, and the stench of rotting flesh, we get... a mother's embrace. It's warm. It's kind. And it is *terrifying*. The genius of this chapter is that it weaponizes hope itself. Li Huowang's internal conflict is agonizing: does he "save" Bai Lingmiao from a beautiful lie, or let her keep the only comfort she's ever had? The moment he touches Wusheng Laomu, the narrative forces us to completely re-evaluate everything. This is not a hallucination or a scam. Compassion is now a cosmic law, governed by a living goddess. That's a staggering expansion of the world's metaphysics, and it makes the upcoming clash with The Dice feel higher-stakes than ever. Buckle up—the Dao-Twisted World just got a little bit brighter, and a little bit more dangerous for it.
