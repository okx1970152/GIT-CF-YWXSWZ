## Chapter Overview

Our dear Li Huowang finally takes the plunge—not into some grand alchemical ritual, but into the utterly mundane, terrifyingly real fake world of Yang Na’s university. This chapter is a masterclass in self-deception and brutal resolve. Having made peace with the idea that he *must* break free from this hallucination to save Bai Lingmiao, Li Huowang scrubs off the blood, steals some clean clothes, and goes full John Wick on a train ride to the school. But of course, the Dao-Twisted World’s favorite punching bag can’t have a simple talk. The universe throws a bank robbery at him—because why not? It’s *so* absurdly coincidental that it only reinforces his belief this is all a hallucination. Get ready for a tense, heartbreaking hunt through a campus under siege, where every face he doesn’t see is a small knife to the heart.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang emerges from his catatonic state in the bridge tunnel, washes up, and steals new clothes. The act of shaving his matted, bloody hair is a ritual of re-entering the “normal” world.
- He travels to Yang Na’s city and parks himself outside the university, wrestling with his internal voices. The real-world logic of “this is a hallucination” battles with his deep emotional attachment to Yang Na.
- A bank robbery coincidentally erupts *right there*, with hostage-takers storming the women’s dorm. Li Huowang finds the sheer improbability of this event hilarious—it’s the final proof he needs that this is a fake world.
- He enters the campus, trembling, and issues a chilling set of instructions to Bai Lingmiao (the hallucinated version) and Li Sui, outlining a plan for them to inflict ultimate bodily pain on him. This is his self-harm ritual, pre-authorized.
- He searches the dorm for Yang Na, only to encounter the hostage-takers on the stairs. He completely ignores the armed, panicking men and calmly asks a captive girl if she knows Yang Na’s room number. The chapter ends with a gun pointed at him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The “bank robbery disrupts a personal drama” trope is a classic in crime fiction, but here it’s used to weaponize the protagonist’s own worldview. Li Huowang isn’t scared of the gun; he’s *reassured* by it. In the logic of a fabricated hallucination, narrative convenience is a flaw, not a coincidence. This is a brilliant inversion of dramatic irony.

Think of this chapter as a “mental ascension” failure. In xianxia, a cultivator must clear their mind of attachments to reach the next realm. Li Huowang is trying to “cultivate the false into reality” in reverse—he needs to *believe* the world is fake to act within it. This is a twisted form of 修假为真 (Cultivate the False into Reality). He’s using his own doubt as a fuel source. The instructions to Bai Lingmiao and Li Sui are an echo of the *Thousand Greats Record*’s self-harm rituals. Even in a “peaceful” hallucination, his only solution is pain. He’s become a man who can only process reality through agony.

### Self-harm ritual

Li Huowang’s instruction to have his own throat gouged out is a direct reference to the *Thousand Greats Record*’s pain-based cultivation, which he has learned to weaponize.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a brutal showcase of Li Huowang’s psychological armor. The conversation with the hallucinated Bai Lingmiao and Li Sui is going to *haunt* you. He’s not just talking to phantoms; he’s rehearsing a suicide pact with them. The way he casually tells them to gouge his throat out if he can’t move is the coldest, most desperate kind of love. He is trying to save Bai Lingmiao by having her “kill” him in a dream. The *real* horror isn’t the bank robbers—it’s that Li Huowang is now a man who sees a hostage crisis as a *convenient* backdrop to his own self-mutilation. The gun at the end is just a formality; he’s already decided he’s the walking dead in this world.
