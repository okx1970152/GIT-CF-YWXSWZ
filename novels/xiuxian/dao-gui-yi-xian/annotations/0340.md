## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in mounting paranoia and identity collapse. Li Huowang, still invisible, decides to hang back and let the Zuowandao and Heart-Bewilderment tear each other apart. But his spectator’s perch is shattered when a mysterious scholar—who can *see through invisibility*—casually taps him on the shoulder. The scholar, who calls himself Zhuge Yuan, heals Li Huowang’s facial wounds, warns him about the Knife-Pawnbroker’s curse, and leaves a strange black spindle before vanishing. Meanwhile, the identity crisis escalates: a duplicate Li Huowang, a duplicate Hong Da, and a duplicate Heart-Bewilderment all pile into the same side hall, turning the hunt for the missing Chief Recorder into a farce of mirrors. Then the Chief Recorder himself appears—holding the shrunken head of Facai, one of the elusive Three Yuan. The revelation that Facai is already dead hits like a club: whatever killed him is far beyond anyone in this room.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang stays invisible and decides to play the waiting game, reasoning that stepping back is safer than staying in the crossfire.
- Zhuge Yuan, a scholar with calligraphy on his fan, reveals he can see through Li Huowang’s invisibility and even detects the anomaly in Li Huowang’s Five Phases (three elements missing).
- Zhuge Yuan heals Li Huowang’s face, explains the danger of the Knife-Pawnbroker’s blades (they steal one’s fortune), and leaves a black spindle as a gift.
- Despite the obvious goodwill, Li Huowang remains deeply suspicious—he decides the scholar must be a trickster.
- When Li Huowang enters the side hall, he finds a duplicate of himself, a duplicate Hong Da, and a duplicate Heart-Bewilderment already present, creating a three-way identity crisis.
- The two Heart-Bewilderment monks give contradictory verdicts about which Li Huowang is real, destroying any hope of reliable identification.
- The Chief Recorder arrives holding Facai’s shrunken head and announces that Facai (one of the “Three Yuan”) has been dead a long time, shocking everyone.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Zhuge Yuan (诸葛渊)**: This is the first appearance of a character who will become Li Huowang’s sworn brother. His surname “Zhuge” immediately flags him as a worldly-wise, resourceful type—thanks to the legendary Zhuge Liang of the Three Kingdoms. His fan inscription, *Tian Sheng Wo Cai* (“Heaven Gave Me Talent”), is a famous line from Li Bai’s poem *A Toast to a Friend*, expressing soaring self-confidence. The fact that he notices Li Huowang’s missing three Five Phases (Fire and Metal remain; Wood, Water, Earth are gone) places him as someone with profound knowledge of the cosmic order.

**Knife-Pawnbroker (赊刀人)**: In Chinese folklore, the Knife-Pawnbroker (or “Credit Knife Peddler”) is a mysterious figure who loans knives to strangers and later returns to collect the “debt” in the form of the borrower’s luck or fortune. The rusted blades stuck in Li Huowang’s face are not just physical weapons—they carry a metaphysical lien. Zhuge Yuan’s warning is a serious one.

**Chief Recorder (记相)**: A high-ranking eunuch official in the Supervisory Heavenly Office. Eunuch officials in Ming-era court culture often wielded considerable power behind the scenes, and the Chief Recorder’s theatrical, powdered appearance and perfume masking urine are classic signifiers of court corruption.

**Three Yuan (三元)**: A mysterious trio consisting of Facai (发财—“Making a Fortune”), Kuaile (快乐—“Happiness”), and Ping’an (平安—“Safety”). They seem to be powerful figures in the Office’s hierarchy. Facai’s sudden death implies a threat far beyond any Zuowandao trickery.

## Reading Guide

**Buckle up for the wildest identity crisis yet.** This chapter throws three pairs of identical people into one room, and nobody—not even the readers—can tell who’s real and who’s a Zuowandao. The key is to track not appearances but *behavior*: the genuine Heart-Bewilderment is trying to protect himself; the fake one is trying to create confusion. Li Huowang’s tactic of staying disengaged and treating the chaos as entertainment is a sign of his growing coldness and flexibility.

**Pay close attention to Zhuge Yuan.** This is not an enemy, but Li Huowang’s trauma makes him treat every olive branch as a snake. The black spindle will resurface later as a major plot device. The fact that Zhuge Yuan can fully heal Li Huowang’s face (which even the Black Tai Sui couldn’t do fully) marks him as someone on a very different level.

**Why does the Chief Recorder show up now?** His abrupt arrival with Facai’s head suggests that the “facai” (making a fortune) aspect of this mission was doomed from the start. The real danger isn’t the Zuowandao masquerade—it’s the thing that killed a being powerful enough to be one of the Three Yuan.
