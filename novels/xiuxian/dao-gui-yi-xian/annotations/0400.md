## Chapter Overview

Lu Xiucai thinks he’s finally cracked the code. With five coins, a mouthful of bravado, and the faint smell of Li Huowang’s reputation clinging to him, he struts into his first solo exorcism expecting a quick promotion in the supernatural pecking order. What he gets instead is a classic folk-horror setup: a locked room, a dead-silent mansion, a client who’s a little *too* pushy about closing doors, and that final, devastating reveal that no amount of borrowed confidence can prepare you for what’s waiting on the other side of a match-strike. This is a masterclass in building dread through procedural mistakes, and Lu Xiucai makes every single one.

## Key Plot Points

- Kui Sanjin offers Lu Xiucai a lucrative exorcism job with an eighty-percent reward stake, piquing his greed before his caution.
- Despite initial hesitation, a mix of pride and peer pressure (“Li Huowang could do it”) pushes Lu Xiucai to accept the job.
- Arriving at a wealthy, intimidating mansion, Lu Xiucai meets the client—Zhao Tianhu, a thuggish rich man whose “son” is possessed.
- Zhao Tianhu’s men shove Lu Xiucai into a pitch-black room and lock the door behind him, a sign that this is not a simple consultation.
- Desperate but still performing bravado, Lu Xiucai uses Li Huowang’s name as a threat to the unknown entity.
- Attempting to light a lamp, Lu Xiucai’s spark reveals a horrifyingly elongated, pale face staring at him from across the flame—leading to his scream.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Lu Xiucai’s arc here is a textbook example of the difference between *genuine xianxia power* and *borrowed reputation*. The “five coins” are his talismanic weapon, a common motif in Chinese folk-magic—but as a newbie exorcist, he lacks the ritual rigor and spiritual fortitude required to wield them properly. The locked-room trope, where the client or employer is complicit in trapping the exorcist with the entity, has deep roots in Chinese folk horror (*zhiguai* stories often feature well-meaning but panicked clients who seal the door to “prevent escape” of the evil—while forgetting the exorcist’s own exit).

### Locked-room exorcism (闭门驱邪)

A classic trope in Chinese folk horror and xianxia where the client seals the exorcist inside with the possessed, prioritizing containment of the entity over the exorcist’s safety.

## Reading Guide

His transformation from cocky “Master Lu” to a man screaming at a lamp-lit face is the kind of tonal whiplash that makes *Dao-Twisted World*’s horror so effective: it starts with a swagger and ends with a soul-freezing scream. If you’ve ever wondered what happens when a roadside braggart tries to play exorcist, read on.

## Deeper Lore: The Locked Room’s Folk Horror Roots

The “shoving the exorcist into a sealed room with the possessed” is a specific, recurring scenario in Chinese rural exorcism lore. In many cases, the family is terrified that the *evil spirit* will escape into the larger household—or that the *exorcist* will flee the moment he realizes what he’s up against. This isn’t just trap-setting; it’s a grim form of folk logic where the practitioner’s survival becomes secondary to containing the entity. Zhao Tianhu’s “two pageboys were torn to pieces” remark is the classic red flag that Lu Xiucai, in his arrogance, completely ignores.
