## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a raw, gut-wrenching chapter that puts the “hysteria” front and center. After a brief moment of domestic peace with Bai Lingmiao, Li Huowang plunges into a violent psychotic episode that lays bare the terrifying mechanism of his *xinsu* affliction. This isn’t just a spooky vision—it’s a full-blown, externally visible breakdown where he argues with invisible figures, curses out a hallucinated “Wang Wei,” and begs a version of his mother for forgiveness. The horror is made doubly intense by being filtered through the horrified eyes of his companions and the entire Qingqiu village, who can only watch as their leader unravels into something inhuman. It’s a masterclass in how *Dao Gui Yi Xian* weaponizes mental illness as its greatest horror.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang wakes up in a tent with Bai Lingmiao and calmly informs her about Sun Baolu’s mother’s horrific past, making her realize the world is crueler than she imagined.
- He decides they must leave Qingqiu soon but remains pragmatic about their journey to Liang.
- Shortly after stepping outside, Li Huowang’s *xinsu* affliction triggers a severe psychotic break—he starts hallucinating conversations with a figure named Wang Wei and his mother.
- Recognizing the onset, he orders his companions to quickly chain him up to prevent harm, showing terrifying self-awareness amidst the madness.
- His performance disturbs the entire Qingqiu community, who gather in a silent ring to observe the terrifying spectacle of his internal war.
- The episode culminates in a heart-wrenching exchange where he begs his hallucinated mother not to spend money on doctors and ends with a furious roar at Wang Wei to stay out of his family matters.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a deep dive into the novel’s core mechanism: the *xinsu* (心素) constitution. The term comes from classical Daoist cosmology—the “Grand Beginning” transforms into “form,” which has substance but not yet a body. In the novel, this means Li Huowang’s beliefs and hallucinations can *literally manifest into reality*. His madness is not a mere mental illness; it is an ontological wound that allows him to interact with beings that are as real as the grass under his feet. The way he talks to Wang Wei and his mother showcases the “dual-track” horror—the voices are real in his perception, but the physical consequences (his screaming, his convulsions) are real to everyone else. The scene where he says “mental illness means I can’t be charged with murder” is a disturbing inversion of real-world legal logic, weaponizing his condition as a threat.

### xinsu (心素)

A rare human constitution that allows belief to shape reality; classical Daoist cosmology describes it as “form with substance but not yet a body.”

## Reading Guide

Fellow Daoists, this chapter is a pure gut-punch. Watch how Li Huowang’s mental state is depicted not as a power-up or a cool ability, but as a painful, cyclical collapse that alienates him from everyone around him. The “quiet beginning” with Bai Lingmiao is a trap—it lures you into a false sense of peace before the storm hits. And the way the *entire village* watches… that silence is the sound of a protagonist becoming a monster. Pay attention to the names he shouts. Wang Wei is a new, hostile figure, while his mother is a source of overwhelming guilt and love—the same emotional anchors that have defined his journey. The fact that he tries to protect her from financial ruin even while hallucinating shows that his humanity is fighting, even as his mind shatters. This is the cost of being a *xinsu*.
