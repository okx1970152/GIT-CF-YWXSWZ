## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang finally dips his toes into the bureaucratic machinery of the Supervisory Heavenly Office, and — surprise, surprise — it's both exactly what he expected and far more unsettling. He's not here to climb the ranks or make friends; he's hunting prey. Specifically, North Wind of the Zuowandao's Four Joys. What he finds in the Office's dusty records doesn't just confirm how outmatched he is — it forces him to confront a terrifying question he's been dodging ever since he learned he was a Heart-Element. The chapter is a masterclass in *information as horror*: the more Li Huowang learns, the more the walls close in.

## Key Plot Points

- The eunuch explains the Office's inner/outer circle system: freelancers (Li Huowang's tier) get scraps, while high-rankers like the Chief Recorder get longevity techniques and forbidden techniques. But climbing means tighter control.
- Li Huowang rejects the buffet of possible jobs and insists on hunting only Zuowandao. Sima Lan questions him briefly but doesn't press.
- He flips through every available job slip but finds nothing on North Wind — only small fry and "suspected" leads. The eunuch explains that Grade C targets (like the Four Joys) are handled immediately, not left for browsing.
- Pivoting, Li Huowang requests historical records and finally finds North Wind's last confirmed appearance: three years ago in Tanyang County, working with East Wind.
- The records reveal North Wind's terrifying portfolio: instant Bewilderment, body-swapping through eye contact (persistent even after swapping again), weaponizing human darkness to recruit accomplices, and converting a 17-year-old girl into a "Five Bars" Zuowandao in under an hour through pure deception.
- The Office's response cost a Chief Recorder and a Supervisor their lives, plus over a dozen agents. They secured the Heart-Element and killed East Wind, but North Wind escaped.
- Li Huowang realizes a new, crushing layer of vulnerability: North Wind can reach into hallucinations to threaten loved ones. The thought of his mother and Yang Na being used as leverage triggers a visceral, furious reaction.
- He then demands every available record on the Heart-Element itself — determined to finally understand what he actually is.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Four Joys and the Three Yuans (四喜三元)**: These are the Zuowandao's true high-rankers — named after gambling terms. "Four Joys" refers to four winning hands in domino tiles; "Three Yuans" refers to the top three passing scores in the imperial examination. The Zuowandao twist these auspicious names into threats. North Wind is one of the Four Joys, which puts him at the apex of the organization's power structure.

**The Heart-Element (心素)**: At this point in the story, "Heart-Element" is still a puzzle box. We know it's a rare human constitution, valuable as an alchemical ingredient, and capable of belief-driven reality warping. But Li Huowang has never had a clear, authoritative explanation. The Office's archives might give him — and the reader — the first systematic definition. Given that this novel builds its horror around the "Cultivate the False into Reality" principle, whatever the Office has written down is likely to be cold, clinical, and absolutely terrifying.

**Body-swapping in xianxia folklore**: The ability to swap bodies is a rare and deeply unsettling power. Traditional Daoist folklore speaks of "seizing a body" (夺舍) — a technique usually reserved for immortal practitioners whose own bodies have failed. What makes North Wind and East Wind's variant uniquely horrifying is that they can do it *at a glance*, repeatedly, and the ability persists in the new body. It's an anti-cultivation power: it doesn't strengthen the self; it erases the boundary between selves.

### Face-Changing (变脸, bianlian)

A traditional Sichuan opera technique where performers rapidly switch between painted masks; in the Dao-Twisted World, it becomes an immediate psychological trigger for Li Huowang, who associates any change of face with Zuowandao deception.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow cultivators (and the traumatized among you), settle in. This chapter is a slow-burn *information dread* chapter — the kind that doesn't throw a monster at you but makes you realize the monster is smarter, more experienced, and scarier than you could have imagined. North Wind's dossier is a *nightmare*: he doesn't just fight — he *converts* people into his own ranks by weaponizing their worst impulses. That seventeen-year-old girl turned into a Five Bars in an hour? That's not combat; that's *contagion*.

And then there's the punchline: he can reach into your hallucinations. Li Huowang has spent this whole time thinking his mother and Yang Na were safe because they were in a different "layer." North Wind says: no such luck. The moment that thought sinks in, Li Huowang's hunt stops being about revenge and starts being about *prevention*.

Also — the Office bureaucracy is chilling in its own way. The eunuch is polite, efficient, and clearly scared of what Li Huowang might do. And Sima Lan watches him like a hawk. Li Huowang isn't hunting alone; he's being watched by people who could flip on him the second he becomes a liability.

The chapter ends with a new quest: "What is the Heart-Element?" The Office has the answer. The question is whether Li Huowang — or the reader — is ready for it.
