## Chapter Overview
Brace yourselves, fellow Daoists — we're back in the padded room. Chapter 533 yanks Li Huowang out of the Dao-Twisted World and smacks him down onto a hospital bed, electrodes and all. The electroconvulsive therapy worked a little *too* well, and now he's staring at Dr. Yi Donglai, a man who casually mentions the Zuowandao's Dice as if reading the morning paper. The horror here isn't a monster; it's a calm, rational doctor dismantling Li Huowang's reality by *talking about it like it's a delusion*. The chapter is a masterclass in psychological whiplash — we swing from the high stakes of sect warfare to a fluorescent-lit room where the greatest threat is a cup of pills and a reasonable proposition.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Jolt Back**: Li Huowang awakens strapped to medical equipment after intensified ECT. His first instinct is to call for Li Sui and the spine-sword, but the white walls and blue-striped gown tell a different story.
2. **Dr. Yi Donglai Introduces Himself**: A calm, bespectacled psychiatrist steps in, sending orderlies away. He claims to be Li Huowang's doctor, Yi Donglai, and demonstrates an unsettling awareness of the "other side's" events — specifically the Zuowandao's Dice.
3. **The Dragon Vein Bombshell**: Yi Donglai casually suggests the Dice is after Great Liang's dragon vein. This theory instantly clicks for Li Huowang — if the Dice wanted Ji Lin on the throne to manipulate the dragon vein, the consequences could be catastrophic.
4. **The Deal**: Yi Donglai proposes a transaction: if Li Huowang stays "clearheaded" during an inspection, he'll grant privileges — phone, food, freedom. But Li Huowang sees the trap: *keep him grounded in this reality, make the other side feel less real*.
5. **Rejection and Defiance**: Li Huowang bats away the phone and accusation, exposing the psychological manipulation for what it is. The chapter ends with him squarely rejecting the doctor's framework — but the seed of doubt is planted.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Dragon Vein (龙脉)** is a cornerstone of Chinese feng shui and imperial cosmology. It's not just a pretty metaphor — the dragon vein was believed to be the literal energy channel of a dynasty's legitimacy. If it was broken or tampered with, the ruling house would fall. In xianxia and historical fiction, meddling with the dragon vein can shift the course of entire eras, making it a prime target for villains who want to rewrite history.

**Projection Theory**: Li Huowang's instant suspicion that Dr. Yi is a "projection" from Great Qi is rooted in the novel's established meta-rules. The Dao-Twisted World and modern reality are not entirely separate; figures from one side can cast shadows (projections) onto the other. This means the doctor *could* be more than he seems — or Li Huowang's paranoid framework could be right.

**Psychological Warfare via Mundanity**: In Chinese web fiction, the most devastating attacks aren't always fireballs and sword qi. Sometimes, a simple proposition — *"just take the medicine, it's for your own good"* — cuts deeper than any curse. The horror here is bureaucratic: a doctor with a clipboard is harder to fight than a demon.

## Reading Guide
This is a *breather chapter* of the worst kind — no blood, no monsters, just a conversation that makes your skin crawl. Pay close attention to Yi Donglai's dialogue: his calm, reasonable tone is a weapon. He's not shouting; he's *helping*. That's what makes him so dangerous. For Li Huowang, the real fight isn't against the Dice or the Zuowandao — it's against the creeping doubt that *maybe this side is real*. Also, keep an eye on that smartphone. The red +99 notifications on QQ and WeChat are a beautifully cruel detail: the modern world has been trying to reach him, and he's been ignoring it. That's not a glitch — that's a choice.
