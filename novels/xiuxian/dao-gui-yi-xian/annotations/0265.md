## Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we see Li Huowang take on a new role: teacher. But in the Dao-Twisted World, even mentorship is laced with pragmatism and test-subject ethics. On the road to the county town, Li Huowang decides to use Lu Xiucai, whose personality has been dangerously corrupted by the Purple-Tasseled Sword, as a guinea pig for a newly acquired cultivation method. It's a perfect storm of desperation, ambition, and cold calculation—and Lu Xiucai, hungry for power, accepts the deal without a second thought. Meanwhile, the group catches a rare moment of relative peace as they enter a bustling market town, but Li Huowang's agenda is far from idle: he's hunting for the Supervisory Heavenly Office.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang slaps Lu Xiucai for boasting about stealing the sword, then confronts him about his lingering corruption, establishing that the Purple-Tasseled Sword's effects are permanent and non-reversible.
- Li Huowang proposes using the *Xiandu Cishe Seal*, a Luo Sect technique he extracted from Han Fu's Nascent Soul, to flush out Lu Xiucai's killing intent. The plan is explicitly framed as a two-for-one: help Lu Xiucai and test the technique for traps.
- Lu Xiucai, eager for any path to power, kneels and formally takes Li Huowang as his master, kowtowing three times in the dirt.
- Lu Zhuangyuan, after a painful hesitation, agrees, gambling his son's future on the Daoist Master's risky cure.
- A telling character moment: when Li Huowang asks Lu Xiucai if he wants revenge for the slap, Lu Xiucai replies with surprising clarity—he's not a madman, just angrier, and the promise of divine art cancels out any grudge.
- The chapter ends with the group entering a lively county town, splitting up for errands: Bai Lingmiao and Chun Xiaoman seek maps, Gouwa and others sell sheep, and Li Huowang begins his quiet hunt for the Supervisory Heavenly Office.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Xiandu Cishe Seal (仙都兹摄印)**: This is the Luo Sect technique for controlling the Bronze Coin Sword. Like many cultivation methods in the Dao-Twisted World, its "pure" name masks a deeply corrupted origin. Li Huowang's plan to use it as a 'counter-agent' against the sword's killing intent is a fascinating application of xianxia logic—treating supernatural power like a toxin to be neutralized by another toxin.

**Master-Disciple Bond**: When Lu Xiucai kneels and kowtows three times, he is formally enacting a traditional Confucian-style master-disciple relationship. In Chinese martial and cultivation culture, this is not a casual title—it implies reciprocal obligations, lifelong mentorship, and a bond that can be as strong as family. Li Huowang accepts the role with typical grim pragmatism, making no pretense of warmth or guidance beyond the transmission of technique.

**Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司)**: This imperial body is the novel's version of a supernatural police force. Unlike the chaotic sects or corrupt temples Li Huowang has faced, the Supervisory Heavenly Office represents *systemic* power—bureaucratic, institutional, and bound by imperial law rather than personal morality. Li Huowang's decision to actively seek them out marks a new phase in his survival strategy: rather than avoiding all authority, he is now trying to understand and potentially leverage the world's official power structures.

### kowtow (叩头)

A traditional Chinese gesture of deep respect, prostration, or submission, performed by kneeling and touching the forehead to the ground.

## Reading Guide

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a quiet but crucial gear-shift. We've watched Li Huowang survive, scheme, and kill—but now we see him *teach*. And true to form, his mentorship is less about noble transmission and more about opportunistic field-testing. The way he frames Lu Xiucai's training as a "two birds, one stone" gamble is pure Li Huowang: even his kindness is tactical.

But don't sleep on Lu Xiucai's character moment. When he says he doesn't hold a grudge because the teaching cancels out the slap, it's a surprisingly rational response from someone who's been literally corrupted by demonic sword energy. This kid is not stupid—he's just never had power, and now that he can smell it, he's willing to pay any price. Watch him closely; he's got the makings of either a loyal disciple or a future calamity.

And the Supervisory Heavenly Office? We've heard them mentioned in whispers and seen their fear in bandits' eyes. Now Li Huowang is going *looking* for them. That's either a masterstroke or a death wish. Place your bets.
