## Chapter Overview
Get ready, folks—Li Huowang finally knocks on the door of the Ao-Jing Sect, and he finds out pretty darn quick that even the "ask nicely" route has its price. This chapter is all about atmosphere and negotiation: the silent, skinless watchers in their wide gray robes, a charred corpse who might be the sect leader, and one very specific request that makes Li Huowang's blood run cold. Our boy is no longer the clueless victim; he's walking into a den of pain-worshippers with a clear goal, but the game they're playing is on a whole other level.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang investigates two cross-shaped Ao-Jing temples: the first is a ruin, the second is intact, featuring a charred Gu-Spirit head staring up at a chaotic mural representing the entity *Bashe*.
- He is confronted by a silent, skinless sect member in a wide robe and tall hat. After tossing the flesh-skin letter from Abbess Jingxin as a credential, the man wordlessly leads him deeper into the mountains.
- The environment builds dread: other robed figures silently watch him; Dog-head cowers. They arrive at a gourd-shaped, brightly torch-lit cave—a space that makes Li Huowang think of the horror cave at Qingfeng Temple.
- Inside, Li Huowang meets the leader: *Shou San*, a charred corpse lying on the ground with a blood-red *Thousand Greats Record* spread across his chest. The bamboo strips have rooted into his flesh.
- Shou San reveals his interest: he has never seen a Heart-Element perform the *Dung-Beetle Ascension*, and he wants to witness it to find a way to "steal Bashe's power."
- The chapter ends with a critical lore drop: the Ao-Jing Sect does not worship Bashe; they worship the *Gu-Spirit*, which is itself desperately yearning to steal Bashe's power. Pain is just the transaction method.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Ao-Jing Sect (袄景教):** The name itself is a deliberate, chilling fusion of two historically distinct foreign religions that entered China via the Silk Road: *Xianjiao* (祆教), a Chinese designation for Zoroastrianism, and *Jingjiao* (景教), the historical Chinese name for Nestorian Christianity. By smashing these names together, the author creates a fictional cult that feels authentically "foreign" and ancient within the Chinese supernatural landscape. They are a suffering-cult: they do not seek enlightenment or immortality through transcendence, but through the agony of self-mutilation. Pain is their currency, their scripture, their ladder.

**Bashe (巴虺):** The name likely derives from *Bashe* (巴蛇), a mythic giant snake from the *Classic of Mountains and Seas* (《山海经》) said to be capable of swallowing an elephant whole. The novel has twisted this into a high-dimensional, cosmic-horror entity that cannot even be properly depicted—the mural in the temple is a chaotic, indescribable mass of colors and shapes. The Gu-Spirit's desperate longing for Bashe's power, and the sect's transactional relationship with it (pain for power, no worship), creates a unique theological horror: they don't love their god, they want to mug it.

**The Dung-Beetle Ascension (苍蜣登阶):** The "Dung-Beetle" (*qianglang*, 蜣螂) is evocative of filth and base labor. In Daoist alchemical symbolism, different animals often represent different phases of transformation. A dung-beetle rolling its ball of filth is a profoundly humble, unglamorous image to use for a cultivation breakthrough—perfect for this novel's subversion of "cool" cultivation tropes.

## Reading Guide
This chapter marks a fascinating shift in the story's "dungeon" dynamic. Unlike Qingfeng Temple, where Li Huowang was a prisoner and a raw ingredient, here he walks in as a *client* with diplomatic credentials. But Shou San's condition—a live demonstration of the Ascension—instantly flips the negotiation. It's a masterclass in how the Dao-Twisted World operates: even the "civilized" request has a hook in it. Keep an eye on that phrase "steal Bashe's power." It recontextualizes everything we've heard about the Ao-Jing Sect and the *Thousand Greats Record*. They are not faithful servants; they are parasites trying to become predators.
