## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a skin-crawling standoff, fellow travelers! Li Huowang’s escape hits a sudden, nerve-shredding wall when an ancient, bone-legged lama appears out of the black-sheep-studded darkness. This isn’t a fight—not yet. It’s a test of nerve, a conversation through drums, and a chilling reminder that the Dao-Twisted World has its own law enforcement. Li Huowang bluffs with a false identity, Bai Lingmiao’s spirit-drum speaks for the group, and everyone walks away with their lives… this time. But the warning is clear: *don’t do it again.*

## Key Plot Points

- After a harrowing night flight from an unknown black mass, Li Huowang’s group is blocked by a herd of silent black sheep and their master—an old lama in black hide, with pitch-black pupils and a vulture on his arm.
- Li Huowang experiences a brief, horrifying hallucination where the black sheep appear as white-haired, senile old people draped in black sheepskin, leaving him uncertain if it was real or a psychotic break.
- He attempts to bluff his way out by claiming to be “Er Jiu,” a cultivator of the Ao-Jing Sect, testing whether the lama knows his true Heart-Element identity.
- The lama teleports behind Li Huowang, revealing a grotesque lower body made entirely of bone prosthetics, and tries to impose a calming, paralyzing influence on him.
- The confrontation escalates to near-violence, but the lama initiates a drum duet with Bai Lingmiao’s spirit-drum, which serves as a supernatural negotiation.
- After the drum conversation, the lama retreats. Li Huowang pushes the group through an all-night march, and Bai Lingmiao reveals that the lama identified himself as being from the Supervisory Heavenly Office, issuing a final warning with no further action.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces the **Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司)** as a tangible, bureaucratic force. This isn’t a ghostly cult or a rogue sect; it’s a government-sponsored supernatural law enforcement body, likely under the imperial board of war. They don’t appear to judge morality—they maintain stability. The lama’s presence and his terse warning (“let it slide… don’t do it again”) suggests they track significant supernatural events or actors (like a newborn child or a Heart-Element), and they intervene only to prevent major disruptions. Think of them as the cold, pragmatic regulators of the Dao-Twisted World. Also, note the *vulture*. In Tibetan Buddhist tradition, vultures are sacred as sky burial agents, consuming the dead to release the soul. Here, that sacred function is corrupted into a menacing, silent familiar—a perfect example of the novel twisting real-world religious imagery into horror.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *atmospheric standoff*. There’s no bloodshed, but the tension is suffocating. Watch how Li Huowang’s instincts shift: he first tries to ignore the threat (“pretend he isn’t there”), then attempts a verbal bluff, and finally prepares for a suicidal fight. The lama, in turn, doesn’t attack—he *tests*. His teleportation and his paralyzing influence are warnings, not attacks. The real negotiation happens through Bai Lingmiao’s drum, which now serves as a formal channel of supernatural communication. This is a crucial worldbuilding beat: the Supervisory Heavenly Office exists, and they have their own rules. Li Huowang’s reliance on false identities (Ao-Jing Sect) is now a two-edged sword—it may buy time, but it attracts the attention of the very people who can see through it. The *vulture* and the *bone prosthetics* are also worth noting as visual signatures; this lama is ancient, connected to death rituals, and not entirely human anymore.

## Depth: Sky Burial & The Mahasiddha

The lama’s vulture and his grotesque, partially skeletal form evoke the **sky burial** (天葬) practice of Tibetan Buddhism, where the deceased are offered to vultures as a final act of generosity. More than just a funeral, it’s a ritual of detachment from the physical body. The lama himself, with his bone prosthetics and age-covered face, resembles a **Mahasiddha**—a figure in Tantric Buddhism who achieves enlightenment through unconventional, often taboo-breaking means. In the Dao-Twisted World, this sacred figure is corrupted: he is not a liberated sage but a terrifying, bureaucratic enforcer. The *drum conversation* also echoes the use of ritual instruments in Tibetan Buddhist ceremonies to communicate with non-human entities. This chapter quietly layers real religious iconography onto its horror, making the lama feel both ancient and dreadfully *real*.
