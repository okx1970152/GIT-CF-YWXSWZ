## Chapter Overview

Forget everything that brought you here. Li Huowang and his ragtag crew are on the run, and the taste of freedom is bitter and panicked. After the nightmare of Zhengde Temple, our favorite paranoid strategist has come to a chilling conclusion: the rot goes all the way to the top. If a temple this monstrous can operate in the capital next to the Imperial Palace, then there’s no safety in any system run by men. He’s now a man with a map, a plan, and a deep, gnawing distrust of everything—including the gentle reassurances of his own traveling companions. As he forces his group into the woods, we cut back to the source of the horror. In the serene and solemn halls of Zhengde Temple, Abbot Xin Hui is performing his evening ritual, one that is infinitely more gruesome than any sutra. The chapter serves as a brutal narrative pivot, yanking us from the desperate flight of the powerless to the calm, bureaucratic evil of the powerful. Get ready for a quiet, methodical chapter that sets the stage for the next hunt.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Getaway:** The chapter opens with the group in full retreat mode. They are jogging beside the donkey cart, constantly checking over their shoulders. Li Huowang’s mood is grim, setting the tone for a paranoid escape.
*   **The Political Verdict:** Li Huowang and Chun Xiaoman deduce that Zhengde Temple’s impunity in the capital is not because everyone is fooled, but because there is an active collusion or infiltration of the state. This confirms that no governmental authority in the Dao-Twisted World can be trusted.
*   **The New Route:** Li Huowang unfurls a hard-won map of the region. He traces a new, perilous route that cuts through three nations with the ultimate goal of reaching Bai Lingmiao’s homeland, Liang Kingdom. He decides to abandon the main roads for the woods to avoid pursuit.
*   **Compartmentalization and Doubt:** During a meal break, Bai Lingmiao offers comfort, saying the monks in her home are kind. Li Huowang’s internal response is ice-cold; he admits to himself that he trusts *nobody*, filing her statement away as another potential deception. He has officially entered a state of total paranoia.
*   **Secret Instructions:** Under the cover of a seemingly innocent bathroom break in the woods, Li Huowang pulls the Fool aside and gives him a hushed set of orders, revealing that he is already planning his next move for the group's survival.
*   **The Abbot’s Workshop:** The scene shifts to Zhengde Temple at night. The atmosphere is one of organized piety, with monks chanting evening prayers. Jian Dun reports Li Huowang’s departure to Abbot Xin Hui.
*   **The Vase-Girl Ritual:** The abbot is not reciting sutras. He is chanting a strange, alchemical mantra while a baby girl he “saved from drowning” writhes on the ground. The chapter visually confirms that the ‘vase-girl’ is created through a live ritual of constriction and body horror, perfected by the temple.
*   **The Hunt is Ordered:** Upon finishing the ritual, Xin Hui, with serene calm, orders Jian Dun to pursue Li Huowang. He reveals a key piece of information: Li Huowang is a rare “Heart-Element,” a term that explains why both Danyangzi and the temple are so interested in him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **The Heart-Element (心素):** This chapter explicitly introduces a core concept of the novel’s power system. A **Heart-Element** is a rare human constitution. Their “heart” (心), in the sense of their spirit, belief, or will, has a unique property that allows it to affect reality or serve as a potent ingredient in alchemy. It’s not a skill one learns, but a state of being one is born into. Danyangzi and Abbot Xin Hui both recognize it, framing Li Huowang as a highly valuable resource to be used, making his plight even more terrifying than just being a target. His very existence is a commodity for these twisted cultivators.
*   **The Vase-Girl Ritual (瓶姑娘):** We get a front-row seat to the process this time. The chapter draws a direct parallel between the trappings of orthodox Buddhism (prayer beads, wooden fish, chanting) and a grotesque, visceral alchemical act. The mantra chanted by Xin Hui, “The moon hides the jade rabbit… the golden lotus in fire,” is a mishmash of Daoist internal alchemy (Neidan) terms, showing how these sects have bastardized all religious traditions into a singular, horrific pursuit of power.
*   **Geography of the Dao-Twisted World:** The map reveals the political scope of this world: Siqi, Later Shu, Qingqiu, and Liang Kingdom. The novel’s horror isn't local; it spans multiple warring or coexisting states, implying the corruption is systemic on a continental scale. Travel in this world isn’t just a journey; it’s moving between territories with potentially different rules of horror.

## Reading Guide

*   **The Calm of Evil:** Pay close attention to the contrast in tone in this chapter. On one hand, we have Li Huowang’s frantic, gritty escape through the woods. On the other, we have Abbot Xin Hui’s quiet, almost tender, ritual in the temple. This contrast is the novel’s central thesis: that the most horrifying evil isn't a screaming monster, but a patient bureaucrat weaving a baby’s intestines into a prayer bead string while humming a lullaby.
*   **Li Huowang’s Paranoia is a Survival Tool:** His distrust of Bai Lingmiao’s innocent comment about the monks is not just character drama; it is a correct and logical deduction based on the world’s rules. To survive *Dao Gui Yi Xian*, readers must start thinking like Li Huowang: assume every piece of information is a lie until proven otherwise. His paranoia is not a flaw here; it is his only weapon.
*   **Watch the Details:** The “vase-girl” ritual confirms what we suspected: the “non-killing rule” many monks enforce is just a convenient PR statement. The horror isn't just the act itself, but the systematic, ritualized *factory* nature of it. This isn't a rogue monk; this is institutional policy.
