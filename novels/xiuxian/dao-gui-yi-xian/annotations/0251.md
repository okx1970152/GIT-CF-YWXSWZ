## Chapter Overview
Get ready—this chapter is a masterclass in psychological warfare, not martial combat. Li Huowang sits down for a "friendly chat" with the severed little head growing from Han Fu's corpse: Han Fu's Nascent Soul. And by "chat," I mean a calculated, gut-wrenching interrogation using the Ao-Jing Sect's healing arts as an infinite-loop torture enhancer. This isn't a fight scene; it's a dissection of how far Li Huowang has come. He's no longer a terrified victim or a desperate escapee. He's a man who has learned to use the world's cruelest tools—pain, mercy, and even healing—as weapons to extract information. The horror is not in what he does to the creature (though it's plenty horrific), but in how calm, clinical, and almost bored he seems while doing it. This is our protagonist growing into his role as a survivor of a world that has no place for the squeamish.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang systematically interrogates Han Fu's independent Nascent Soul, a small head growing from the corpse's neck, left alive after Bai Sui's attack.
- He uses the True Sutra of the Fire Vestments (火袄真经) to heal the creature's torture wounds, creating a horrific cycle of pain and recovery to break its will.
- The interrogation yields critical world-building data: Yang Life Pills are currency, valued at "one pill = one job from someone like Han Fu." They are refined by killing people born in a triple-yang birth chart.
- The creature identifies itself as Han Fu's Nascent Soul, and its sect as the Luo Sect, based on Snake Bone Mountain in Liang, with 57 members and 224 heads.
- The key revelation: Cultivators see three flames on a person's shoulders; a Heart-Element's flames flicker on and off, making them identifiable. Li Huowang cannot see his own flames.
- The Nascent Soul confirms it has never heard of a Heart-Element who escaped the Bewilderment, and knows nothing about the Zuowandao member "North Wind" beyond their naming scheme (mahjong tiles).
- The chapter ends with Li Huowang preparing a "double-check"—i.e., another round of torture—to verify the information's truth.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Nascent Soul as a Detachable Organ (元婴分离):** In standard xianxia, the Nascent Soul is a condensed, spiritual core of a cultivator's power, typically housed safely within the dantian. It represents the beginning of "immortal" life, a pure energy form. This chapter violently subverts that. Han Fu's Nascent Soul is not a spiritual core—it's a fleshy, vulnerable, screaming tumor growing out of his neck. It has its own personality, memories, and will, but it's also a physical liability that can be captured and tortured separately from the main body. This is classic *Dao Gui Yi Xian*: taking a sacred, lofty concept from Chinese folk cultivation and dragging it down into the mud, making it organic, parasitic, and horrifying.

**The Three Flames (三把火):** This is a genuine piece of Chinese folk superstition. Every person is believed to carry three invisible yang flames on their shoulders and head, representing their life force and spiritual protection. When these flames are strong, a person is resistant to evil spirits and bad luck. When they are weak (e.g., at night, when sick, or after a shock), the person becomes vulnerable to possession or supernatural harm. The novel twists this: for a Heart-Element, the flames flicker, making them "invisible" to normal spiritual sight in a way that paradoxically marks them as prey. It's a brilliant inversion—a protective force that becomes a death sentence because of its instability.

**Yang Life Pills (阳寿丹):** The term "Yang Life" (阳寿) is the standard Chinese term for a person's allotted lifespan. A "Yang Life Pill" is literally a pill made from someone else's remaining years of life. This brings the concept of "life as currency" from a metaphysical idea into a brutal, physical reality. The requirement that the victim be born in a triple-yang configuration (阳年阳月阳日) ties into the same folk system of yin-yang balance and birth-chart destiny. Killing someone specific to steal their "yang quality" for alchemy is a straight line from traditional external alchemy's use of rare minerals, but applied to human beings.

## Reading Guide
Okay, let's be real for a second. This chapter is *rough*. It's a graphic interrogation scene, and Li Huowang is the one holding the tools. If you're squeamish about detailed torture, this is your warning sign. But if you're here for the psychological evolution of a protagonist in a world that breaks everyone, this is peak Li Huowang. Watch how he doesn't rage. He doesn't monologue about justice. He is polite, professional, and utterly terrifying. He treats the Nascent Soul like a broken machine he needs to fix and then break again to get the right output. The most chilling line isn't the scream—it's "I'm not really that skilled at torture" said while holding a barbed hook. Li Huowang has absorbed the logic of this world: pain is information, suffering is a tool, and even kindness (the healing fire-slug) can be the cruelest weapon. This is the cost of his survival, and we're watching him pay it in installments.
