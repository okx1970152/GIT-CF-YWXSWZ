## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a breather chapter that is anything but restful. "The Retreat" picks up with Li Huowang a charred, oozing mess, trying to put distance between himself and a mysterious fat woman who wants his "untainted yang-life." The chapter trades spectacular combat for the grind of survival: raw wounds sewn with thread, badger oil slathered on burns, and the simple act of eating soup turned into a negotiation with a monster-daughter. More than a chase scene, this is a quiet, somber study of Li Huowang's shifting role from survivor to caretaker—and of the daughter who is slowly learning what it means to be a person, not a weapon.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Fat Woman's Gambit**: The obese spirit-medium from the peach grove stalks Li Huowang, offering to buy his five years of "untainted yang-life" at any price. He refuses, intent on saving it for Bai Lingmiao's shortened lifespan. Li Sui's feral display finally drives her away, but she is *not* gone.

2. **Three Days of Unconsciousness**: Li Huowang collapses and sleeps for three days. Bai Lingmiao tends to his wounds, sewing up his chest and waist with thread. He wakes to find the "Spirit-Blight" secured in a wooden box, to his immense relief.

3. **Li Sui’s Evolution**: Li Sui has inherited Mantou’s memories—she eats first, asks for affection—and is learning to draw talismans. Li Huowang instructs her to help her "Second Mother" (the Er Shen) with chores, framing her role as a daughter, not a pet.

4. **The Er Shen’s Secret Life**: Li Sui reveals she and the Er Shen secretly chat, hunt, and share food. This unexpected bond hints at the Second Spirit's autonomy and depth beyond her silent, veiled exterior.

5. **Arrival at Linzhang**: The group reaches a major city, Linzhang, putting them ten days from their final goal. But their respite is cut short when Li Huowang spots the fat woman watching them from a tavern, proving she has not given up the chase.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Yang-Life (阳寿)**: In Daoist cultivation, "yang-life" represents a person's innate lifespan, often measured in years. It can be traded, stolen, or refined, making it a form of supernatural currency. "Untainted" (无垢) yang-life is especially pure and valuable because it lacks karmic debts or negative side effects—perfect for extending the short lives of fragile companions like Bai Lingmiao.

- **Badger Oil (獾油)**: A traditional Chinese folk remedy for burns and scalds, made by rendering the fat of the badger. It is prized for its cooling and healing properties, a pragmatic, ground-level solution in a world of cosmic horror.

- **Talisman Drawing (符箓)**: Li Sui is learning to draw talismans, a core Daoist practice of inscribing sacred symbols on paper or objects to channel spiritual power. For a daughter made of tentacles and claws, this is a deeply humanizing act—she is not just fighting; she is studying a craft.

- **"Second Mother" (二娘)**: Li Sui's term of address for the Er Shen reveals her view of the spirit as a trusted family member, not a mere tool. This reframes the Er Shen as a guardian figure who quietly operates in the margins, forming bonds the protagonist is barely aware of.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in using exhaustion as a narrative tool. Li Huowang is not tested by a new monster but by the mundane weight of his own wounds and the terrifying reality that his resources—yang-life, time, trust—are finite. The real horror here is the psychology of the chase: being so injured that the simplest act of eating becomes a negotiation, and discovering that your stalker has found you again despite your best efforts. Note how Li Huowang's paternal instincts have sharpened: he teaches Li Sui to think of herself as a daughter, not a pet, and he prioritizes Bai Lingmiao's vision over his own rest. His body may be failing, but his role as a protector has never been clearer. The fat woman's reappearance closes the chapter on a perfect note of cold dread: no action, just a threat, patiently waiting.
