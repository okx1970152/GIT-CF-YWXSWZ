## Chapter Overview

This chapter serves up a classic Dao-Twisted double act: a leisurely bowl of fish-soup noodles followed by a sudden, bloody skirmish in the woods. But the real meat is in the conversation that precedes the fight. Zhuge Yuan finally drops a concrete name for the Zuowandao’s patron deity—**Doumu Taiyin**—and drops a bombshell: a Heart-Element who masters the “method of Cultivating the True” becomes the Zuowandao’s natural predator. It’s a rare moment of clear, actionable lore in a world built on lies. Then, just when they try to mind their own business, Zhuge Yuan hears a familiar voice from the middle of a brawl and charges in without a second thought, dragging Li Huowang along for the ride.

## Key Plot Points

1. The chapter opens with Li Huowang pressing Zhuge Yuan on the source of the Zuowandao’s powers. Zhuge Yuan corrects the popular rumor—they didn’t “swindle” their abilities; they belong to a specific Siming.
2. Zhuge Yuan reveals the name of the Zuowandao’s deity: **Doumu Taiyin** (斗姥太阴). He also warns Li Huowang not to dwell on matters of the “Heaven Beyond Heavens” to avoid tangling his own fate.
3. Li Huowang asks to learn Zhuge Yuan’s technique for deflecting supernatural cognition. Zhuge Yuan says it’s an innate ability of a Heart-Pan, but a Heart-Element could learn the “True Cultivation” method to become a direct counter to the Zuowandao.
4. A mundane but tense scene follows: the duo tests their noodles for poison using Li Huowang’s dog, Mantou. Li Huowang casually mentions he’d cut himself open to purge poison if needed—a moment of grim matter-of-factness that Zhuge Yuan finds unsettling.
5. Li Huowang detects a fight in the nearby woods. He identifies one side as the Supervisory Heavenly Office and suspects a Zuowandao member among their enemies—a Miao woman with purple lips.
6. Despite Li Huowang’s hesitation and desire to avoid trouble, they are spotted. A familiar voice calls out “Lord Zhuge!”, and Zhuge Yuan, recognizing it, immediately joins the fray. Li Huowang follows suit.
7. After the brief battle, a survivor kneels to Zhuge Yuan. The masked man is introduced as a “National Hand” of Great Qi—the nation’s top Go player—and a trusted friend of Zhuge Yuan.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Doumu Taiyin (斗姥太阴):** This is a massive lore drop. Doumu (斗姥) is a real figure from Daoist cosmology—the Mother of the Dipper, a high-ranking stellar deity associated with the Big Dipper, life, and the overcoming of calamities. The addition of “Taiyin” (太阴, the Great Yin/The Moon) creates a hybrid name not found in standard scriptures. In the Dao-Twisted World, this name is now tied squarely to the Zuowandao, which means their power doesn’t come from random chaos but from a specific, named celestial authority ruling over an entire domain of “falsehood” or “shadow.” It recontextualizes everything: the Zuowandao isn’t just a gang of pathological liars; they are the mortal arm of a specific Siming’s cosmic domain.

**Cultivating the True (修真) vs. Cultivating the False (修假):** In standard xianxia, “Cultivating the True” (修真) is the generic term for the pursuit of immortality and enlightenment—it means “to cultivate the genuine/true nature.” Here, Zhuge Yuan makes it literal. For a Heart-Element like Li Huowang, mastering the method of Cultivating the True isn’t just about getting stronger; it’s about becoming the *inherent opposite* of the Zuowandao’s Cultivating the False. This sets up a fundamental philosophical axis for the entire novel: the war between making fictions real and stripping reality back to its truth.

**The National Hand (国手):** This title isn’t just a compliment. In historical Chinese culture, “Guo Shou” (National Hand) was a formal, unofficial honorific for the most skilled player of Go (围棋, Weigi) in the entire country. It carried immense intellectual prestige, akin to being a grandmaster in a recognized discipline. The fact that Zhuge Yuan’s friend is both a National Hand and a member of the Supervisory Heavenly Office suggests that strategic genius is highly valued in the Office’s operations—and that Zhuge Yuan’s social network runs deep and eclectic.

### Cultivate the True

A specific cultivation method for Heart-Elements that makes them the natural enemy of the Zuowandao; a literal interpretation of the xianxia term for "seeking truth."

## Reading Guide

Brace yourselves, fellow cultivators. This chapter is the calm before an intellectual storm. The noodles, the dog, the hilarious “stop fanning yourself in winter” roast—it’s all just the prelude to Zhuge Yuan casually dropping the name of the Zuowandao’s patron deity like it’s nothing. *Doumu Taiyin.* That’s the name you need to hold onto. Finally, the “lies” have a source. The “tricks” have a root. This is the kind of detail that transforms a vague, menacing force into a specific, nameable horror. Also, pay attention to how casually Li Huowang offers to gut himself. The man is so deep in the horror that his own body is just another tool. That’s not bravery. That’s desensitization. And Zhuge Yuan, for all his wisdom, has no idea how to respond to it.
