## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up. Chapter 363, “The Solution,” doesn’t give us a clean way out; it gives us the worst possible way out. Li Huowang, surrounded by an inescapable Heart-Turbid trap, makes a choice that will make your skin crawl. He’s going to do a Dung-Beetle Ascension. Again. But this time, he needs the ultimate torment: the anguish of heart. And we all know where that’s hiding. The chapter is a visceral, gut-wrenching descent into self-loathing and brutal calculation, as Li Huowang stands on the edge of an abyss, trying to force himself to take the plunge for the sake of saving his loved ones. It’s raw, it’s painful, and it’s peak *Dao Guai Yi Xian*.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Final Gambit**: With the two-layer Heart-Turbid trap proving inescapable, Tuoba Danqing breaks down and others are paralyzed by despair. Li Huowang, however, refuses to yield and declares his plan: a new Dung-Beetle Ascension to borrow power from the primordial demon god Bashe in the White Jade Capital.
- **The Price of Power**: Li Huowang explains that the ascension requires dual torment: physical agony (easily achieved through self-harm) and the torment of the heart. To get the latter, he must enter his hallucination of the modern world.
- **The Weapon of Guilt**: He instructs Bai Lingmiao to watch over his physical body and introduces her to Li Sui, his parasitic-tentacle “son.” He then transitions into the hallucination, his mind set on what he must do.
- **The Threshold of Atrocity**: In the bridge tunnel, Huanhuan appears with food. Li Huowang tries to steel himself to harm her, reasoning she is just a hallucination. But his hand trembles uncontrollably—he can’t do it to an innocent child he barely knows.
- **The Unbearable Truth**: Realizing he needs a greater emotional wound, Li Huowang writes the names “Yang Na” and “Sun Xiaoqin” on the ground. He physically recoils at the thought, smashing his own head with a rock in a frenzy of self-loathing, screaming that he *must* stop hesitating because Bai Lingmiao is waiting.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Dung-Beetle Ascension (苍蜣登阶)**: This is the corrupt, inverted mirror of traditional Daoist cultivation. “Ascension” in normal xianxia is a glorious, transcendent step. Here, it’s named after a dung beetle, a creature that rolls filth. This ascension is about groveling in the mud of your own suffering, trading agony for a brief connection to a primordial, pain-devouring entity like Bashe. It’s the novel’s thesis that power in this world isn’t earned—it’s *paid for* in flesh and spirit.
**The Hallucination as a Weapon**: Li Huowang’s “Heart-Element” (xinsu) constitution is, on its face, a curse—the inability to distinguish real from fake. But here, he weaponizes it. He uses the hallucination as a targeted emotional “pain generator.” The horror is that the *most effective* weapon is the one aimed at what he loves most. This is a terrifying inversion of the common xianxia trope of the “heart demon”; his heart isn’t an obstacle to overcome, but a furnace he must actively stoke to burn himself alive for fuel. It’s a grim, cynical take on the concept of “using your heart to cultivate.”

### Two-Layer Heart-Turbid Trap (一生一死心浊)

A containment paradox where a dead Heart-Turbid conceals a living one, trapping victims in a fabricated reality they cannot perceive or escape.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in psychological torture. It’s not about giving you a power-up; it’s about demanding a sacrifice you aren’t sure you can make. The entire suspense of the chapter hinges on one question: will he hurt Huanhuan? The answer is no, and that refusal is more painful than if he had done it. It forces him to look at the even deeper, more festering wound: the people he truly loves. The climax isn’t a spell or a fight; it’s a man screaming at himself in a filthy bridge tunnel, trying to force his own hand to commit an atrocity against a memory. This is where *Dao Guai Yi Xian*’s horror is most effective—not in some monstrous god, but in the unbearable weight of love turned into a weapon against the self.
