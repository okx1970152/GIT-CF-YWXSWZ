## Chapter Overview
Fresh off his narrow escape, Li Huowang is in an unexpectedly buoyant mood. The filthy, crude, and boisterous nuns of Anci Nunnery are a bizarrely comforting presence—so much so that he almost forgets the knife at his throat. But the Black Tai Sui is wearing off, and the border between worlds is starting to fray. When he finally meets the terrifying Abbess Jingxin, a blind mountain of festering flesh, her casual revelation that she knows exactly what he is—a Heart-Element—drops him right back into the cold dread he’d been trying to laugh off. And then she drops the real bombshell: she’s intimately familiar with his condition because her own son was a Heart-Element too.

## Key Plot Points
*   **A Fleeting Respite:** After the intense danger of the previous arc, Li Huowang allows himself a moment of levity. The grotesque yet oddly endearing nuns provide a strange sense of safety and humor, a stark contrast to the polished horror of Zhengde Temple.
*   **The Black Tai Sui Fails:** The parasitic entity's effects are fading. Li Huowang feels the modern world’s hallucinations beginning to bleed back in, forcing him to mentally bargain with himself to hold them at bay. This is a critical reminder that his "peace" is only a temporary reprieve from his fractured mind.
*   **Anci Nunnery’s Corruption:** The rural, impoverished corruption of the nunnery is displayed in full force. From using human waste to feed their pigs to the nuns’ selfish laziness, this place is a different breed of institutional decay—less sinister than Zhengde Temple, but far more pathetic and raw.
*   **The First Glimpse of Abbess Jingxin:** The encounter is pure body horror. A blind, morbidly obese nun covered in writhing sores, yet with a voice as gentle as a grandmother’s. The cognitive dissonance is the point, and it effectively establishes her as an unpredictable and deeply unsettling power player.
*   **A Terrifying Revelation:** Abbess Jingxin immediately identifies Li Huowang as a Heart-Element, but instead of coveting him as an ingredient, she drops a bombshell: her son was also a Heart-Element. He went mad, which she describes as "one of the better outcomes," painting a bleak picture of Li Huowang’s own potential future.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Rural Gross-Out Horror of Anci Nunnery:** This chapter introduces a specific flavor of horror distinct from the grand, systemic evil of Zhengde Temple. This is the horror of *poverty, decay, and spiritual bankruptcy*. The nuns are not scheming monsters; they are lazy, gluttonous, and filthy. The pigpen fed by human excrement is a masterclass in disgusting, visceral worldbuilding, emphasizing that in the Dao-Twisted World, even the "holy" is dragged down into the mud. It's a stark reminder that the corruption here is not always magical—sometimes, it's just the rot of a broken and hungry world.

**Heart-Element (心素) as a Known Condition:** This chapter confirms that the Heart-Element is not a unique curse to Li Huowang. It is a recognized, known condition with a tragic prognosis. The Abbess's matter-of-fact tone ("He went mad. He's fine.") is more chilling than any threat. It suggests a world where this kind of cursed existence is a known quantity, with accepted, doomed outcomes.

**The Dilapidated Nunnery:** Abbess Jingxin is a blind, crippled figure, yet she holds authority. This subverts the typical xianxia trope of the all-seeing, powerful hermit master. Her power is not in her vision or her physical strength, but in her knowledge and her disturbing, fatalistic wisdom.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in tonal whiplash, and it works perfectly. After the high-octane terror of the Flesh Buddha, we get a moment of pure, dumb comedy with the crude nuns. Don't let your guard down, though. This is a classic *Daoge* trick: let the reader laugh, let them breathe, and then hit them with a grotesque description that will stick in their craw. The image of Abbess Jingxin, a blind mountain of sad, writhing flesh, is one of the most memorable in the story so far. Pay close attention to the way she speaks—her kindness is a kind of weapon. And her story about her son? That's not just backstory. That's a prophecy.
