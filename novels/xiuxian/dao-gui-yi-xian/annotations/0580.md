## Chapter Overview

All hell breaks loose as the Dice makes his final, devastating move—not a duel, not a ritual, but a full-scale military uprising. With the entire Great Liang army at his command, the false emperor forces our battered coalition to the east wall of Shangjing for a desperate last stand. Amidst the war drums and rising killing intent, Li Huowang faces an impossible choice: can he stop a war without becoming a monster himself? This chapter is a masterclass in escalating stakes, blending personal horror with the crushing weight of dynastic warfare.

## Key Plot Points

- Bai Lingmiao shows her new, serene confidence as a White Lotus Saintess, claiming Wusheng Laomu pities the people—a statement that unsettles Li Huowang, who still associates the cult with body-horror atrocities.
- A tender moment with Li Huowang is violently undercut when Bai Lingmiao’s face momentarily twists into a predatory, alien grin, reminding us her transformation is far from stable.
- Li Sui proudly displays her new human head to Bai Lingmiao, who overrules Li Huowang’s refusal, allowing her to keep it—a small but significant victory for the child-like parasite.
- The Supervisor reveals that the Dice has rallied the entire Great Liang army outside the city walls, planning to overwhelm the coalition through sheer military might rather than supernatural trickery.
- Li Huowang agrees to be used by the Supervisor on one condition: the Dice must die—a vow forged from guilt over Zhuge Yuan’s death and the world’s collapse.
- The chapter ends with war drums sounding and black iron cataphracts being catapulted onto the walls, signaling the beginning of a brutal, full-scale siege.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Dynastic Armies & the "Mandate of Heaven" (气运):** In the Dao-Twisted World, the Mandate is not a celestial reward but a stolen divine fragment that holds up reality—a parasitic burden passed through self-slaughtering imperial families. The Dice weaponizes this by using his legitimate (if fraudulent) imperial status to command the mortal army, turning the state’s own machinery against the protagonists. This chapter reframes the “dynastic war” trope from epic glory into a horror of institutional betrayal.

**The Dice’s Strategy as a Zuowandao Masterstroke:** The Zuowandao are not mere tricksters; they are a conceptual plague born from the existence of lies themselves. The Dice’s gambit—forcing a fight he cannot win just to bleed the coalition dry—perfectly embodies their nihilistic philosophy. He does not need to conquer the world; he only needs to make everyone lose.

**Wusheng Laomu’s Change (无生老母):** Bai Lingmiao’s claim that the Unborn Venerable Mother now “pities the common people” hints at a fundamental shift in this Siming’s nature. Previously a source of fanatical, destructive devotion, she may have been corrupted from a “Way” into something more human—or perhaps more terrifying. Li Huowang’s suspicion that she was once a Way before her current state remains unresolved.

### Mandate of Heaven (气运)

In this novel’s context, the Mandate is a stolen fragment of divine power that holds up reality; it can only be supported by a family willing to commit self-slaughter, making it a curse as much as a blessing.

### Zuowandao (坐忘道)

A secret society whose name means “The Way of Sitting in Forgetfulness”; they are a conceptual plague born from the existence of lies, dedicated to spreading chaos and deception.

## Reading Guide

Get ready, because this is the kind of chapter that makes you forget to breathe. The Dice’s play is absolutely *chef’s kiss*—not some flashy god-battle, but a cold, bureaucratic atrocity: “I am the emperor, and I say they are rebels.” The horror here isn’t a monster with tentacles; it’s the entire apparatus of the state turning against you, all because one liar convinced enough people that he was real. That’s the Zuowandao at their most terrifying. Also, can we talk about Bai Lingmiao’s face-morph? That moment of tenderness turning into predator-mode is pure, uncut *Dao Gui* body horror. And Li Sui, proudly showing off her new head like a kid with a school project, manages to be both adorable and deeply unsettling. This is the calm before the storm—a storm made of iron, blood, and the sound of war drums.
