## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang’s reality-check fails spectacularly in this chapter when he finally learns there is *another* nation—a whole other kingdom with a thousand-year-old veteran—and nobody seems to agree on whether they’re in Great Qi or Great Liang. The noodle-stall test yields three different answers, Zhuge Yuan is being suspiciously evasive, and a Zuowandao Miao woman is biding her time right under the Office’s nose. This isn’t a simple border crossing; it’s a cognitive earthquake, and Li Huowang is the only one feeling the tremors.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang meets the “Great Qi National Champion,” a masked elder who claims to be over a thousand years old, immediately shattering Li Huowang’s understanding of the world’s timeline.
- Elder Qin reveals the Supervisory Heavenly Office has been infiltrated by eunuch spies, suggesting the imperial court’s corruption runs deeper than ever.
- Li Huowang, trying to ground himself, tests the noodle seller with the Great Qi vs. Great Liang question—but the answer *changes* depending on when and with whom he asks.
- Zhuge Yuan deflects Li Huowang’s interrogation with polite evasiveness, pretending nothing is wrong and urging everyone to move on.
- Li Huowang figures out that the shifting reality is likely caused by a Heart-Pupil’s ability, but still can’t determine how or why Zhuge Yuan is involved.
- The Zuowandao Miao woman is deliberately left alive by Li Huowang; he intends to use her as bait to draw out Bei Feng.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “National Champion” (国手) concept**: In ancient China, *guoshou* literally meant “national hand”—the highest-ranked player of a board game like Go or a master of a craft. Here, it’s used as an exalted title for a cultivator or strategist of supreme skill who has served the realm for centuries. Living from Great Qi to Great Liang (roughly equivalent to surviving multiple dynastic cycles) is treated as proof of that status, but also an implicit threat: if he’s that old, what else has he seen and *done*?

**Heart-Pupil (心蟠) reality distortion**: This chapter introduces a new dimension to the Heart-Pupil’s power—it can warp *consensus reality* around a core concept (like “which dynasty are we in?”). This is distinct from simple illusion; it doesn’t fool one person, it changes the answer everyone gives to a question, even when they are the same person asked seconds apart. This is the signature horror of the Dao-Twisted World: not that you can’t trust your eyes, but that you can’t trust everyone else’s eyes either.

### Beifeng (北风)

A Zuowandao leader that Li Huowang is actively trying to bait by leaving the Miao infiltrator alive.

## Reading Guide

This chapter doesn’t give you a flashy fight or a big alchemical reveal. What it gives you is worse: *the world itself starts to feel thin*. The noodle seller’s shifting answers are a smaller, more intimate version of the novel’s central terror—the ground beneath your feet is made of paper, and someone (Zhuge Yuan? A Heart-Pupil? The Zuowandao?) is holding a match. Watch how Li Huowang processes this: he doesn’t panic, he *catalogs*. That cold, methodical suspicion is what keeps him alive. And the thread he’s pulling—leaving the Zuowandao plant in place to catch a bigger fish—shows he’s playing the long game now.

## Deeper Lore: The “Great Qi / Great Liang” Paradox

This is the first clear hint that Zhuge Yuan may be a Heart-Pupil, or at least tied to one. In xianxia, dynastic names are absolute—they’re part of the Heaven’s Mandate. If the noodle seller, an ordinary man, can give three different answers in three minutes, it means the Mandate itself has become *malleable*. This is not a geographical confusion; it’s an ontological one. The implication is that someone (a Heart-Pupil, a high-tier Siming, or even the Great Nuo itself) is *rewriting history in real time*, and Li Huowang is the only one who notices because he asks the question twice.
