## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang gets grilled by the Supervisory Heavenly Office after delivering Hong Zhong's severed head. The rotten-toothed warden, sporting two grumpy Nascent Souls on his neck, interrogates him about the Zuowandao deception. Li Huowang skillfully spins his story, even slicing his own face open to prove he's not a shapeshifting trickster. The Office is suspicious but ultimately gives him a new assignment: try to get close to Zhuge Yuan, the very man whose identity Hong Zhong stole. Meanwhile, Li Huowang learns that the Office isn't just sitting idle—they're assembling agents to crush the Zuowandao once and for all. His plan to pit these two forces against each other is finally working.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang presents Hong Zhong's head to the Supervisory Heavenly Office, but the warden is more intrigued by the fact that Hong Zhong's soul can't be summoned—a strange property of the Heart-Element.
- Li Huowang cleverly reframes his earlier ordeal on Apricot Island, casting himself as the victim whose identity was stolen by Hong Zhong. He discovers that the Zuowandao is a *very* convenient cover story.
- When accused of potentially being a Zuowandao himself, Li Huowang performs an impromptu Ao-Jing face-peel to prove his identity. The brutal self-harm is so convincing that the Nascent Souls immediately back down.
- The warden reveals that Zhuge Yuan's *Six-Shining Comprehensive Guide* was a fake all along—the real one is still safe. Hong Zhong's grand heist was for nothing.
- The Office orders Li Huowang to attempt befriending Zhuge Yuan using his real identity, banking on the confusion Hong Zhong created. Li Huowang is internally dismayed, as he wanted to hunt down the Zuowandao instead.
- Nangong reveals that the Office has already summoned reinforcements from across the empire to eradicate the Zuowandao. Li Huowang's manipulation has succeeded in igniting a full-blown conflict.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Nascent Souls (元婴)**: In standard xianxia, the Nascent Soul stage marks a cultivator forming a miniaturized, spiritual version of themselves inside their dantian. It's a huge milestone—basically building a backup soul for when things go south. But in the Dao-Twisted World, these Nascent Souls are stuck on the warden's *neck* like talking moles, with their own opinions and attitudes. It's a perfect example of how this novel takes a classic cultivation concept and makes it grotesque: your spiritual avatar isn't a majestic glow-child; it's a grumpy pimple that won't shut up.

**Heart-Element (心素)**: We already know Li Huowang is a Heart-Element—someone whose beliefs can warp reality. But this chapter gives us a terrifying clue about how deep this power goes. Even *decapitation* isn't a guaranteed death if the Heart-Element believes they're still alive. It's less "immortality" and more "stubbornly refusing to accept the universe's terms." The downside? It's a fragile, cognitive ability: one reminder from an enemy, and the whole house of cards collapses.

**The Power of a Good Cover**: Li Huowang has discovered that the Zuowandao is the perfect scapegoat. Any weird behavior, any inconsistent timeline, any mysterious ally—just blame the tricksters. "Oh, that wasn't me; that was a Zuowandao impersonating me." It's the supernatural equivalent of "my email was hacked," and it works beautifully because everyone is terrified of the Zuowandao's reputation for chaos.

## Reading Guide

Our boy Li Huowang is getting *good* at this. The way he spins Hong Zhong's story to fit his own survival agenda is a masterclass in improvised deception. He's learning to weaponize the Zuowandao's reputation just as they weaponized his face. There's a darkly comedic moment when he realizes, mid-interrogation, that "the Zuowandao identity is really quite useful." That's your protagonist, folks—a man who now treats being a wanted doppelganger victim as a career advantage.

Also, can we talk about the Nascent Souls? The warden has two of them living on his shoulders like sassy, mortal-hating parrots. One accuses Li Huowang of being a Zuowandao; the other immediately backpedals when Li Huowang starts peeling his face off. They're petty, they argue with their host, and they're probably the most relatable characters in the entire Supervisory Heavenly Office.

The chapter sets up a delicious tension: Li Huowang has to play nice with Zhuge Yuan (a man whose prized book was stolen by his own face), while simultaneously waiting for the Office's hammer to fall on the Zuowandao. His real target—North Wind—is still out there, and Li Huowang is stuck playing political games instead of hunting. The infrastructure of revenge is being built; now he just needs the right moment to light the fuse.
