## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang wasn't just outmatched this time—he was *erased*. Chapter 607 throws our favorite walking wound into a full-blown ontological identity crisis. After facing the Heart-Pan of Doumu, Li Huowang finds himself turned into a "fake"—a ghost that can't touch, hurt, or be seen. The chapter is a masterclass in cognitive horror: what do you do when the rules of reality turn against you? The answer? You grit your teeth, trust the process, and maybe, just maybe, eat a handful of mystery pills to pull yourself back from the void. Get ready for a deep dive into the mechanics of True vs. False, the desperate gamble of the Bewilderment Way, and a brutal reminder that in *Daoguai Yixian*, victory is never a straight line.

## Key Plot Points

+ Li Huowang finally confronts the Heart-Pan of Yin-Yang Doumu inside a grotesque, living wooden ship cabin. The Heart-Pan is a gaunt figure fused to a chair with a monstrously swollen belly, wearing a fanged bronze mask. He has the five captured Heart-Elements chained to his arm.
+ The Heart-Pan and the five Heart-Elements begin chanting a Daoist scripture. This ritual action literally removes something intangible from Li Huowang—his "realness"—making his attacks pass harmlessly through his enemy.
+ Despite using his rift-slash and stabbing the Heart-Pan with both the Purple-Tasseled Sword and the Copper Coin Sword, Li Huowang does no damage. His weapons pass through his opponent and even through himself, proving that the problem is his own state of being, not the enemy's defenses.
+ Li Huowang realizes that he has become a "fake," a non-entity unable to interact with reality. He hits on the idea of using the "Bewilderment" Heavenly Way, which he shares with Ji Zai, but fails to activate it immediately.
+ The chapter cuts abruptly to a different scenario: Li Huowang is badly injured, hiding in a corner of a cruise ship, hunted by Xu Shou's thugs and vicious Bull Terriers. Outmatched and outgunned, he decides to use his last resort—swallowing a handful of red capsules.
+ The pills trigger a shift in his perception: the cruise ship's interior transforms back into wood-grained planks, and Li Huowang finds he can touch his own head again. He is no longer "fake."

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a *deep dive* into the novel's central philosophical horror: the "True-False Heavenly Way" (真假天道). In traditional Chinese philosophy, categories like "true" (真) and "false" (假) are not just about empirical facts; they carry immense ontological weight. The Daoist concept of "Realness" (真) is tied to the Original Qi, the Primordial Spark, the unadulterated self. To be "false" is not to be a liar; it's to be *ontologically deficient*, a shadow with no substance. The Heart-Pan of Doumu is essentially using a scripture (宝诰, Precious Declaration) to ritually "cleanse" Li Huowang's "true" identity out of him, leaving only a hollow shell.

The "Precious Declaration" (宝诰) chanted here is not just magic verse; it is a formal liturgical text from the Daoist tradition, used for praising deities and affirming cosmic hierarchies. By chanting it at Li Huowang, the enemy is literally overwriting his place in the cosmos. The specific names in the chant—"Celestial Terrace," "Gezao," "Jade Capital"—are all locations tied to celestial immortals and peak ascension. The act is a form of ritual gaslighting on a metaphysical scale.

The concept of "Bewilderment" (迷惘) as a Heavenly Way is also a subversion of typical Xianxia. Most cultivation stories have "Dao" or "Heavenly Dao" as a neutral or beneficial natural law. In *Daoguai Yixian*, "Bewilderment" is an active, chaotic force—a cosmic affliction that Li Huowang must learn to weaponize. It's not about understanding the Way, but about *being* the confusion itself.

## Reading Guide

Fellow Daoists, buckle up. This chapter is where Li Huowang's power ceiling gets a harsh reality check. The "He kills everything with one slash" era is officially over. The Heart-Pan of Doumu isn't a monster you can just stab; he operates on a different *layer* of reality. This is the Mid-Boss that teaches the protagonist (and the reader) that *Daoguai Yixian* isn't about climbing a power ladder—it's about surviving a reality that is actively trying to delete you.

Pay close attention to the sudden scene transition. Is the cruise ship Xu Shou scene a flashback? A projection of his fight with the Doumu Heart-Pan? The line between them is blurred on purpose. The pills he takes might be a way to "re-enter" the Dao-Twisted World, or they might be antipsychotics re-framing his hallucination. The chapter's structure mirrors the theme: the reader is just as disoriented as Li Huowang.

The key takeaway? Li Huowang's "Bewilderment" Way is not an instant-win button. He has to *feel* it, *become* it. The battle is moving from physical combat to a war of cognition and belief. Keep reading, because the next chapters will show us whether he can weaponize his own madness to claw his way back to "realness."
