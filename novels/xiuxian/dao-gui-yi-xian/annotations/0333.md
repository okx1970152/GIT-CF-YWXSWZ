## Chapter Overview
Our man Li Huowang is back in the lion’s den, and he’s got a mouthful of tentacles and bad vibes. This chapter is a masterclass in the subtle art of the “official dinner,” where the food comes with strings attached and the wine is laced with political calculation. Tuoba Danqing, our friendly neighborhood spymaster, is testing Li Huowang’s loyalties with all the finesse of a boa constrictor offering a hug. Between the grotesque spectacle of “Skin-Lip Cups” and the silent call for revenge, Li Huowang walks a razor’s edge, trying to figure out just how much of his act Tuoba is buying. Buckle up—this is the part where the real scheming begins.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang deliberately reveals the Black Tai Sui tentacle from under his copper coin mask to Tuoba Danqing, framing it as a gruesome but convincing deterrent—it successfully explains his mask and confirms Li Huowang’s suspicion that Tuoba has no experience with such phenomena.
- Tuoba Danqing warns Li Huowang about the dangers of rogue cultivation, but his friendly tone is layered with calculation, probing for cracks in Li Huowang’s story.
- Tuoba demonstrates his “privilege” by summoning four mute, deafened women (“Silent Beauties”) from the Linglong Tower for a *Skin-Lip Cup* drinking ritual, a grotesque display of power and dehumanization that vividly illustrates the Dao-Twisted World’s systemic cruelty.
- Li Huowang refuses the women, citing his burning need to avenge his fallen fellow disciples, which seems to strengthen his position in Tuoba’s eyes. Tuoba orders him to settle in Yinling City and promises to call on him for future assignments.
- Alone at the inn, Li Huowang reflects on his new rank token (壬伍 / Ren Wu) and realizes he can use the Supervisory Heavenly Office itself as a weapon against the Zuowandao.
- Red Center reappears to mock his naivety, but Li Huowang fires back, claiming he now believes he *is* Red Center from the Three Joys. The two share a manic, disorienting laugh that ends in mutual mockery, leaving the reader uncertain of who is manipulating whom.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces two deeply unsettling examples of how the powerful treat the powerless in this world: the *Skin-Lip Cup* (皮儿杯) and the *Silent Beauty* (幽伶). Both are horrifying literalizations of historical Chinese customs of objectifying human beings.
- **Skin-Lip Cup (皮儿杯)**: This is not a standard historical term found in official records, but a piece of “jianghu” lore popularized in martial arts novels. It refers to a grotesque drinking game where a woman holds wine in her mouth, and a man drinks it directly from her lips using her tongue as a funnel. In the seedy underbelly of the jianghu, it was a symbol of ultimate decadence, reducing a person to nothing more than a living vessel.
- **Silent Beauty (幽伶)**: These are women who have been deliberately deafened and made mute specifically to serve as high-end, disposable pleasure objects for the elite. This echoes histories of “sing-song girls” and enslaved courtesans, but pushes the horror one step further: the “user” doesn’t want them to be people, he wants them to be tools that can’t talk back or leak secrets. It’s the logical endpoint of feudal class cruelty.
- **Three Corpses (三尸) and “Sitting in Forgetfulness” (坐忘)**: Li Huowang’s sudden alignment with his inner hallucination, Red Center, raises a crucial xianxia question. In traditional Daoist cultivation, the **Three Corpses** are inner demons that must be purged for ascension. Here, Li Huowang is starting to *agree* with his demon, blurring the line between the cultivator and the evil he is cultivating against. This is the corrupt “Sitting in Forgetfulness” in practice—not forgetting to find peace, but forgetting which self is the real one to achieve a twisted goal.

## Reading Guide
First off, pay close attention to the silent duel happening *underneath* the dialogue. Tuoba Danqing is the perfect “smiling tiger” — every compliment is a probe, every offer is a test. Li Huowang’s refusal of the Silent Beauties isn’t just moral outrage; it’s a tactical move to present himself as a man single-mindedly driven by revenge, a simpler creature than he actually is. Does Tuoba buy it? The chapter leaves that wonderfully ambiguous.
Secondly, Li Huowang’s conversation with Red Center at the end is the chapter’s dramatic highlight. He’s no longer just passively hallucinating; he’s actively engaging his psychosis as a strategic resource. The fact that his plan to pit the Office against the Zuowandao is *actually smart* makes his shared excitement with Red Center terrifying. This fusion of lucid strategy and shared delusion is the heart of the novel’s horror.
Finally, keep an eye on that “壬伍” (Ren Wu) token. Li Huowang is climbing the ranks, and with institutional power comes the ability to move pieces on the board. The question is: is he climbing the ladder, or is the ladder a trap?

## The Spider’s Web: Tuoba Danqing’s Game
Tuoba Danqing isn’t just a friendly drunkard. Every “drunken mistake” or “casual mention” is a calculated probe. When he treats the Silent Beauties as disposable perks, he’s testing Li Huowang’s character. When he warns against rogue cultivation, he’s testing Li Huowang’s story. This is a man who plays the long game, and Li Huowang is a very interesting new piece on his board.
