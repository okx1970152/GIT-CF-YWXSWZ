## Chapter Overview

Get ready, folks. After the stomach-churning execution field and a trip to a thoroughly unnerving prison, Li Huowang is handed the key to fixing Bai Lingmiao. It’s a tiny black coffin. Simple as that. Open it for three breaths, and she’s cured. And it works. The malevolent qi is gone, Bai Lingmiao is back to her old self, and there’s a moment of pure, tearful relief. But this is the Dao-Twisted World. Happiness is always a poisoned bait. The very second she’s “cured,” her hand shoots for the Purple-Tasseled Sword on Li Huowang’s back. The cure was real. The cost, as always, is everything else.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang returns from the execution ground, mentally repulsed by the image of the elixir-stuffed corpse and connecting it to the Emperor’s likely obsession with immortality, which parallels his own world’s history of alchemical hubris.
- He reports to a visibly busy and irritated Battalion Commander Cao, who casually hands over a tiny black coffin as the cure for Bai Lingmiao.
- The coffin’s light, almost papery weight and the ominous “寿” character immediately clue Li Huowang into its nature – it’s not a Military Path artifact, but something from another, likely darker, school.
- After a brief moment of paranoid self-doubt (wondering if everyone is in on a plot to trap him, the Heart-Element), Li Huowang decides the logic holds and returns to the inn.
- The “cure” works, but he notes the horrifying side effect it has on *him* – it starts sucking away not just his fear, but his attachment to living.
- Bai Lingmiao is restored, but her very first conscious act is to reach for the weapon that corrupted her, openly begging Li Huowang not to take away its sustaining, poisonous influence.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Emperor and Elixirs**: Li Huowang draws a direct line from the executed high official’s pill-popped corpse to the common historical practice of Chinese emperors seeking immortality through alchemy. It’s a grim tradition with real-life costs (from the First Emperor’s mercury pills to the Tang dynasty’s alchemical-poisoned rulers). In this novel, that quiet desperation is scaled up to a cosmic horror level.
- **The “寿” (Longevity) Character**: The bright red “寿” character on the black coffin is a stark contrast of symbolism. In Chinese culture, this character is almost universally positive, used for birthdays and well-wishing. Placing it on a coffin, a symbol of death, creates a nauseating cognitive dissonance—as if the container for a corpse is wishing it a long life.
- **The “Papery” Weight**: Li Huowang noticing the coffin feels like paper, not wood, is a subtle but powerful nod to funerary paper offerings (纸扎). In Chinese folk religion, paper houses, money, and items are burned for the dead to use in the afterlife. A “paper” coffin, then, feels not like a real container for a corpse, but like an *effigy* or a tool meant to function in a space between life and death. This makes its effect—sucking away life and spirit—even more uncanny.

### Two-Layer Heart-Turbid Trap (一生一死心浊)

A containment paradox where a dead Heart-Turbid conceals a living one, trapping victims in a fabricated layer of reality, unable to perceive the true threat that has already taken root.

## Reading Guide

Alright, let’s talk about the big, ugly elephant in the room. The horror isn’t that the coffin fails—it’s that it *works perfectly*. Li Huowang gets exactly what he wanted from Commander Cao. The problem is that the cure only addresses the physical “煞气 (malevolent qi)” but ignores the spiritual addiction. Bai Lingmiao is back to her old self, but that old self is now a *broken* self. Her first instinct isn't relief, it’s to grab the sword and beg. This is the novel at its most brutal: it’s not about finding the right tool; it’s about living with the wounds the wrong tool left behind. This chapter also showcases Li Huowang’s growing, jaded professionalism. He suspects a trap, tests the coffin on someone else, and executes the procedure with cold precision. He’s getting better at this world. And that’s perhaps the saddest thing of all.

## Deeper Dive: The Two-Layer Trap

This chapter sets up a crucially important concept for the coming arcs. Li Huowang is walking right into a trap that isn't a trap at all—a "Two-Layer Heart-Turbid Trap (一生一死心浊)." The "dead" layer is the solved problem: Bai Lingmiao is cured. The "living" layer is the problem that was hiding *underneath* the cure: that the cure itself was never going to fix what was truly wrong. The coffin fixed the surface; the sword’s corruption is now the new, deeper reality. For the Dao-Twisted World, this is the most insidious kind of horror. It’s a paradox where the living sickness is perfectly concealed by the dead, solved one. The reader, and Li Huowang, can only watch as the thing they feared most—that Bai Lingmiao was beyond saving—reveals itself in a new form.
