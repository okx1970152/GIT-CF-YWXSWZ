## Chapter Overview

Ladies and gentlemen, we've got ourselves a classic "rags-to-riches" chapter, Dao-Twisted World style! Our favorite grumpy opera patriarch, Lü Zhuangyuan, finally gets his due. After a journey paved with blood, horror, and unexpected alliances, he's gifted a magnificent three-story opera garden in the heart of Shangjing City by none other than the new Emperor—Gao Zhijian! But this isn't your typical feel-good retirement party. While Lü Zhuangyuan is busy losing his mind over the gilded carvings and getting slapped back to reality by his son, the chapter pivots to our other survivors as they prepare to enter the palace, with the ambitious Lü Xiucai hitching a ride, convinced his master will teach him "powerful techniques." It's a chapter of sharp contrasts: the dreamlike sweetness of a lifetime's ambition realized, set against the underlying, unspoken weight of what it *means* that the simple-minded Gao Zhijian now sits on a throne built on stolen Mandates.

## Key Plot Points

1. **The Grand Gift:** Lü Zhuangyuan arrives in Shangjing City and is presented with the "Guangde Garden," a lavishly decorated, three-story opera house by the Emperor's eunuch. He is given ownership and a full resident troupe, the Feng Family Troupe.
2. **Loss of Composure:** Overwhelmed by his new fortune, Lü Zhuangyuan hysterically touches every surface of his new stage, behaving like a man possessed. His son Lü Xiucai has to slap him back to his senses after he runs around like a madman.
3. **Generational Divide:** The chapter highlights the different reactions among the Lü family. Lü Zhuangyuan is ecstatic, Lü Juren is cautiously impressed, and Lü Xiucai is utterly unimpressed, impatient to move on to his own pursuits.
4. **A Sneaky Ambition:** Lü Xiucai rejects the life of an opera house heir, running toward the palace with the excuse of "visiting his master" (Li Huowang). He confidently believes Li Huowang will now share his ultimate powers with him.
5. **Palace Bound:** The narrative follows Lü Xiucai as he catches up to the sedan chairs of Chun Xiaoman, Zhao Wu, Yang Xiaohai, and Gouwa, who are formally entering the palace. He barges into Gouwa's sedan, trading insults and stirring up the old dynamic.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **Shangjing City (上京城):** This is not the historical capital of Beijing, but the name of the capital within the Dao-Twisted World's Great Qi dynasty. It's a classic xianxia trope for the seat of imperial power, a place of extreme wealth, danger, and supernatural politics.
*   **A Gift Fit for a... Subject:** The Emperor gifting a property and a household (the Feng Family Troupe) to a subject is a classic display of imperial favor in Chinese history and fiction. Lü Zhuangyuan's initial refusal ("this will shorten my lifespan!") is a ritualized form of politeness—a sign of deference, not genuine rejection. Accepting too readily could be seen as greedy and disrespectful.
*   **Hitting to Wake Up:** Lü Xiucai's method of slapping his father to snap him out of his trance isn't just random violence. This is a bit of Chinese folk logic and comedic trope—a shock to the system (often a slap) is the prescribed cure for hysterical blindness, possession, or a "soul-loss" state.
*   **Low-Class Status (下九流):** Lü Zhuangyuan's observation that he "could never have come here" in the past reinforces the rigid social hierarchy of the *Nine Classes*. A traveling opera troupe leader was at the very bottom, forbidden from socializing with scholars or officials. This gift is not just wealth—it's a *revolution* in his social standing, a tear in the fabric of his world.

### Nine Classes (九流)

The traditional Chinese hierarchical class system mentioned in Lü Zhuangyuan's thoughts, placing traveling performers at the very bottom (lower class).

## Reading Guide

*   **Watch for the Irony:** Savor the sheer, unearned joy of Lü Zhuangyuan's moment. This is a man who spent his whole life as a bottom-rung performer. Will he enjoy this peace, or will it sour? The Dao-Twisted World has a nasty habit of making "happy endings" feel deeply suspicious.
*   **Lü Xiucai's Dangerous Hope:** Pay close attention to Lü Xiucai’s unwavering confidence that Li Huowang will teach him what he wants. This is a powder keg. Xiucai sees his master's monstrous power, but completely misunderstands the agonizing price and psychological torment it costs. He wants the "trick" without the "truth." This ambition is going to be a major problem.
*   **The "Bad King" Shadow:** Never forget *how* Gao Zhijian became Emperor. This gift isn't a simple act of charity from a good man; it's a reward from a mentally simple pawn who just inherited a throne built on the corpses of a dynasty, a throne that literally feeds on the life force of its occupants. The joy of this chapter is a fragile, painted layer over a pit of cosmic horror.
*   **Where are the Heavy Hitters?** Notice who *isn't* rushing to see the Emperor: Li Huowang, Bai Lingmiao. The chapter subtly shows our core team splitting. The "war heroes" are getting their rewards, but the "mad general" is elsewhere, probably dealing with fallout that the rest of the world is blissfully ignoring.
