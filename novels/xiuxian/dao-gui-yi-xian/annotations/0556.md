## Chapter Overview
The desolate, post-cataclysm landscape of the Great Qi finally sinks in for Li Huowang—and the weight of it is crushing. While searching for the next Upper-Stage Filling Gate under Zhuge Yuan's navigation, he stumbles upon a scene that cuts through his pragmatic shell: a single, hoarse-voiced orphan trying to pull a trapped family member from under a collapsed mountain. This chapter trades supernatural combat for a stark, human-scale tragedy, forcing Li Huowang to confront the limits of his power and the bleak, uncrossable boundary between the "real" Great Qi and the "fake" Great Liang.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang and Li Sui are traveling through the Great Qi, which has been completely reshaped by recent cosmological upheavals; Zhuge Yuan must re-evaluate feng shui to find the next Upper-Stage Filling Gate, which requires a specific time alignment.
- A small, mute girl leads Li Huowang to a village crushed by the splitting of a mountain; she is the sole survivor and wants him to rescue her family members pinned under the rock.
- Li Huowang is deeply shaken by this direct encounter with civilian suffering, realizing his deliberate avoidance of the Great Qi's human cost can no longer be maintained.
- He tries to take the girl back to the Great Liang but Zhuge Yuan explains it's impossible: the Great Qi is the real world, and the Great Liang is the fake one, so travel only works in one direction.
- Zhuge Yuan proposes a temporary solution: Li Huowang draws a talisman to summon Zhuge Yuan's friend to take the girl; the friend is still alive.
- Li Huowang seals the miraculously calmed girl in a crevice with rations, hoping for a rescue he can't guarantee.
- At the temple, Shangguan Yuting appears, enters the Upper-Stage Filling Gate, but the cracked clay deity and ominous sounds from the shadows hint this ascent differs from the last.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Geomancy (Feng Shui) and "Splitting Gold, Fixing Water" (分金定水):** In traditional Chinese geomancy, a great terrestrial catastrophe—such as mountains splitting—would violently alter a region's *feng shui*, the metaphysical flow of energy across the land. Unlike a simple topographical map, a cultivator's spiritual compass must be recalibrated, which is why Zhuge Yuan (a scholar of such arts) must painstakingly "re-split the gold and re-fix the water" to locate the hidden gate. This isn't just navigation; it's a metaphysical re-mapping of reality itself.
- **Earth God Temples (土地庙) & Deity Statues (泥塑):** The *Tudi Gong* (Earth God) and *Tudi Po* (Earth Goddess) are among the most ubiquitous local deities in Chinese folk religion, usually a small, benevolent pair. A generic temple in this setting would have two separate statues. The fact that they have *merged* into a singular, one-eyed clay monstrosity is not just decay—it is a grotesque indication that the very principles of local spirit-protection have been corrupted or collapsed under the Heavenly Calamity.
- **The "Real" vs the "Fake":** Zhuge Yuan's line—"the Great Qi is the real one, and the Great Liang is the fake one"—is the thematic core of the entire novel. This isn't about which world is more important; it's a metaphysical prison. The false can visit the true, but the true cannot enter the false, trapping the innocent of the Great Qi in their own hell. This is a devastating inversion of the standard xianxia trope where the "higher" or "realer" plane is a place of escape and power.

## Reading Guide
Brace yourself. This chapter is the heaviest kind of horror—the kind that has no monster to fight and no ritual to undo. Li Huowang has been running on pure survival instinct and cold calculation, but the little girl’s silent, tear-streaked face breaks through every wall he's built. The true terror of the Great Qi isn't the Siming or the aberrant beasts; it's the endless, indifferent suffering of ordinary people he can't possibly save. And the worst part is the logic: he can't take her back with him because his "real" world is a lie. It’s a gut punch of a chapter that re-contextualizes the entire journey, making you feel the hopeless vastness of a world that has already lost.
