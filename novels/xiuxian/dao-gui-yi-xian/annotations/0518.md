## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up—because this chapter is a masterclass in “what fresh body horror did we just sign up for?” After the political scheming and the weight of Ji Lin’s mission, Li Huowang goes right back to the one thing this novel does best: making you feel every single layer of your own skin being peeled off. But this time, he’s not just doing it for survival—he’s doing it for *homework*. Ji Lin gave him an assignment, and Li Huowang is determined to ace it. The chapter starts with the gruesome process of crafting a substitute talisman from his own Heart-Element skin, and it ends with a terrifying new power-up: a symbiotic fusion with Li Sui that turns him into a walking, blade-wielding horror show. It’s gritty, it’s painful, and it’s absolutely brilliant.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang prepares to craft a substitute talisman from his own skin, using liquid mercury for the peeling process—a task assigned by Ji Lin to help deal with the Zuowandao threat.
- He takes a moment to comfort his hallucinated mother before the procedure, showing his fractured awareness and the emotional toll of his condition.
- The peeling ritual is brutal: Li Huowang screams, nearly passes out, and is left in excruciating pain even after healing via the True Sutra of the Fire Vestments.
- Li Sui comforts him by wrapping her tentacles around his body like a second skin, helping him sleep through the worst of it.
- The next morning, Li Huowang gets a new idea: let Li Sui enter his body temporarily to boost his combat power, just like the Luo Sect uses Nascent Souls.
- The fusion results in a grotesque, bloated form: black tentacles fill his blood vessels, burst through scabs, and writhe out of his body. He looks unrecognizable.
- Li Huowang tests the synergy with Li Sui: he wields the Purple-Tasseled Sword while Li Sui controls the Bronze Coin Sword and talismans, proving their combined combat efficiency.
- To complete the disguise, he dons a tattered gray bedsheet cloak, abandoning his signature red Daoist robe for stealth.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter revolves around two core xianxia conceits, both twisted into the viscera-soaked horror of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*.

First, the **substitute talisman** (替身法器). In standard cultivation fiction, substitute talismans or life-saving artifacts are common tropes—a magical item that takes a fatal blow for the user. Here, the process is brutally literal: Li Huowang must peel off his own skin and use mercury as a preservative, turning his Heart-Element flesh into a physical escape hatch. Mercury (水银) itself has a long history in Daoist external alchemy (外丹术), used in elixirs and as a preservative for corpses—the ultimate imperial vanity project for immortality. The novel weaponizes this historical lores into body horror.

Second, the **symbiotic fusion** with Li Sui. This echoes the “Nascent Soul empowerment” technique used by the Luo Sect (罗教), where cultivators use their internal spirit babies to amplify their strength. Li Huowang’s version turns that refined, internalized power-up into something external and monstrous: a visible, writhing parasitic armor. The idea of a cultivator and a spirit beast or artifact merging is common in xianxia (e.g., soul-bonded beasts, artifact possessors), but here it is depicted as viscerally wrong—tentacles bursting through every blood vessel, the body bloated like a corpse stuffed into a suit that doesn’t fit. It’s a brilliant subversion of the standard “power-up” scene.

## Reading Guide
So why is this chapter such a treat for long-time readers? It shows Li Huowang evolving—not just in power, but in *method*. He’s no longer just reacting to horror; he’s weaponizing it. The substitute talisman is a tactical safety net. The Li Sui fusion is a combat multiplier. He’s thinking like a strategist, not a victim.

The emotional beat with his mother is also worth pausing on. Even in the midst of a literal skin-peeling, he takes the time to comfort a hallucination. That’s not weakness—it’s a lifeline. He knows that if he lets that last human thread snap, he might never find his way back.

And finally, the image of Li Huowang in that gray bedsheet cloak, dripping with tentacles and wielding two swords like a blender from hell—that’s the kind of visual that makes *Dao Gui Yi Xian* unforgettable. This is not a protagonist who levels up with a golden glow. He levels up by becoming something that even he finds hard to look at.
