## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang begins his life as Xuan Yang, a registered disciple of Qingfeng Temple. He participates in the morning lesson, meets his fellow disciples, and receives guidance from Senior Brother Xuan Yuan. Though Danyangzi appears satisfied with his outward composure, Li Huowang's true intent is vengeful: he memorizes every detail of the temple's layout, personnel, and routines, seeking an opening to kill his master. The chapter introduces the hierarchy of inner vs. registered disciples, the division of labor, and the fact that many previous disciples died or fled, leaving the survivors overburdened.

## Key Plot Points

1. Morning lesson chants and the three statues Li Huowang commits to memory.
2. Danyangzi assigns Xuan Yuan to mentor Li Huowang (now Xuan Yang).
3. Introduction of Zheng Kun, the only remaining inner disciple after the rebellion of Zheng Kan and Zheng Zhen.
4. Li Huowang learns that Danyangzi's supernatural perception may enable him to know everything that happens inside the cavern—a major obstacle.
5. The five registered disciples are gathered: Changming, Changren, Xuan Yin, Xuan Yuan, and the new Xuan Yang.
6. Li Huowang observes the other disciples' different temperaments and speculates they may have been former human ingredients.
7. The tour reveals each disciple's duty: managing boy attendants, external purchases, feeding the Black Tai Sui, or multiple roles.
8. Li Huowang resolves to treat this world as permanent and use his new knowledge to plan Danyangzi's death.
9. Xuan Yuan casually reveals that alchemy depends on celestial timing—not every day is suitable.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Registered disciples (记名弟子) vs. inner disciples (内门弟子):** In traditional Chinese martial arts and xianxia sects, a registered disciple is nominally accepted but does not receive core teachings; they often perform mundane labor or serve as outer members. An inner disciple receives direct transmission of the master's advanced arts. This hierarchy creates bitter jealousy (as seen in Xuan Yuan's tone) and a sense of being used as expendable labor. 

**Morning lesson (早课):** Daoist monasteries hold daily morning and evening recitations of scriptures. The text chanted here appears to be a ‘Precious Declaration’ (宝诰) praising a deity. Such liturgical forms are common in the Dao-Twisted World but often twisted to serve corrupt ends.

**Black Tai Sui (黑太岁):** A parasitic entity used in Danyangzi's alchemy. Traditional Chinese folk religion reveres Tai Sui as a spirit of the year, but here it is inverted into a flesh-eating, wriggling horror that consumes rebels.

**Celestial timing for alchemy (炼丹与天象时辰):** Traditional Chinese alchemy (both internal and external) places heavy emphasis on synchronizing pill-refining with specific cosmic rhythms—lunar phases, seasonal nodes, and hourly cycles. This is consistent with historical Daoist practice, though in the novel it serves as mundane background detail for the urgency of the plot.

### The Hierarchy of Qingfeng Temple

Qingfeng Temple's structure mirrors a typical xianxia sect: one master (Danyangzi), one inner disciple (Zheng Kun), and five registered disciples (Xuan Yuan, Xuan Yang, Xuan Yin, Changming, Changren). The stark inequality in training and status creates a breeding ground for resentment and potential betrayal. Li Huowang's location at the bottom of this ladder gives him few allies but also fewer eyes watching him—an advantage for a hidden predator.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a quiet but essential setup. Li Huowang's internal voice shifts from helpless rage to calculated observation. Pay attention to the small details he notices: the statues, the jealousy among disciples, the master's omniscience. These will become crucial later. The introduction of alchemical timing hints that Li Huowang may soon need to act on a specific day. The fact that the previous batch of disciples died or fled underscores the constant threat of death. Xuan Yuan's helpfulness may hide his own agenda; his jealousy toward inner disciples could be exploited.

## Human Ingredients and the Black Tai Sui {#human-ingredients-black-taisui}

The previous batch of disciples is implied to have been either turned into human ingredients or fed to the Black Tai Sui. This reinforces that within the temple, human life is a consumable resource. Li Huowang's own survival so far—surviving Danyangzi's initial tests—marks him as more resilient than the others, which may be why Danyangzi has kept him alive as a registered disciple rather than using him up in the cauldron.
