## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists. This chapter is a masterclass in psychological horror that has nothing to do with monsters and everything to do with the collapse of your own memories. Li Huowang is hit with the most devastating weapon in the Zuowandao arsenal: *a favor you don't remember owing*. After a gut-wrenching reveal about Zhuge Yuan's persistence and a hallucinated memory of killing the Second Spirit, our broken protagonist finds himself at the mercy of the Dice—a corpulent, grinning strategist who treats the world like a mahjong table. Every layer of truth is peeled back to reveal another lie, and by the end, Li Huowang is forced to make a deal with his own personal devil.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with a shocking revelation: Zhuge Yuan is still "alive," having grown from Li Huowang's spine-blade into a mass of mismatched, living organs. He calmly explains that as a Heart-Pith (心蟠), his soul and emotions are tied to the "Three-Bodied One" and cannot simply be absorbed into an illusion. This single line shatters Li Huowang's understanding of his own powers.

Immediately, Li Huowang's memories begin rewriting themselves. He now "remembers" a different version of the Second Spirit's death: not a sacrifice, but a murder he committed by choice, with her smiling consent, so he could later "cultivate her from false to true." This false memory is presented with such emotional weight that the reader feels the ground shift under their feet.

The scene transitions to Li Huowang pleading with the Dice—a fat-faced oil-fritter vendor—to erase the painful memory of killing the Second Spirit. Dice agrees, but only if Li Huowang repays the favor later. This "favor" becomes the trap's trigger.

Back in the "present," Li Huowang confronts Bai Lingmiao, demanding to know how the Second Spirit "really" died. Her answer—that she vanished with the Immortals—initially seems to confirm the false memory. But then she begins to turn transparent, and she disappears, leaving Li Huowang screaming her name.

The Dice strolls over, grinning, and explains the trick. Because Li Huowang "didn't repay his favor" (the memory erasure), Dice is no longer obligated to help. He then casually implies that Bai Lingmiao might also be a false reality that Li Huowang "cultivated from nothing," planting the seed of impossible doubt: can he ever trust that anyone is real again?

The chapter's climax sees Li Huowang attempting the Dung-Beetle Ascension in desperation, but he realizes it's useless—Dice's true body isn't here. In a moment of cold, tactical clarity, he stops, shoves his own viscera back into his stomach, and agrees to Dice's deal: help put Emperor Ji Lin on the throne in exchange for Bai Lingmiao's return. The chapter ends with Li Huowang bitterly realizing that the entire trap—perhaps even back to the Shangsi Festival—was Dice's careful design.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**"Cultivate the True without the False leads to Qi Deviation"**: This is a central philosophical conflict in the *Dao-Twisted World*. In standard xianxia, "Cultivating the True" is the only path—pursuing an authentic, enlightened state. But the Zuowandao have flipped the script. They argue that rejecting the False (illusions, lies, deceit) makes you brittle. Li Huowang, a Heart-Element who can make illusions real, is the living embodiment of this dilemma. The Dice's taunt—"how do you know Bai Lingmiao isn't one you made real?"—exposes the terrifying loophole: if the power to make fakes real exists, then *anyone* could be a fake, and you'd never know.

**The Dung-Beetle Ascension (苍蜣登阶)**: This recurring ritual from the *Thousand Greats Record* is one of the novel's most memorable body-horror set-pieces. The practitioner hooks their own intestines and impales them onto the skin-script to summon a primordial god (Ba She). It's a perfect inversion of "chrysalis" or "phoenix" imagery—instead of emerging from a cocoon reborn, you emerge from your own entrails as a sacrifice. Li Huowang wisely stops this time, realizing the enemy isn't physically present. "Knowing when *not* to self-mutilate" is a genuine character growth moment in this world.

**Heart-Pith (心蟠)**: This is a refined lore term. While a Heart-Element (心素) is a raw "ingredient" or reality-bending node, a Heart-Pith is a more advanced state where the entity's soul and emotions are permanently anchored to a higher authority (the "Three-Bodied One"). This explains Zhuge Yuan's persistence: as a Heart-Pith, his very existence is a "license" issued by a cosmic power, not a fragile, local life. You cannot "absorb" him with mere illusion magic.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the climax of the Zuowandao's psychological warfare against Li Huowang, and it works best if you read it with a sense of *dread*. The Dice is not a physical threat; he's an information-threat. Every sentence he speaks is a trap, and he plants "favor" debt like a scammer plants malware.

Pay close attention to the memory revision sequence. The novel deliberately blurs the line between what "really" happened (the Second Spirit's sacrifice) and what the Dice made Li Huowang *want* to believe (killing her himself). This is the ultimate horror: your own wishes become weapons against you.

The final realization—that the entire chain of events, perhaps going back hundreds of chapters, was a setup by the Dice—is the emotional gut-punch. It re-contextualizes Li Huowang's victories as moving pieces on a board he didn't know existed. Get ready for the political arc ahead; Li Huowang is about to become a kingmaker for Ji Lin, and we all know how well "helping emperors" works out in *this* story.
