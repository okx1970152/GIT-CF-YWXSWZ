## Chapter Overview
Alright, buckle up, because Chapter 551 throws Li Huowang into the worst kind of two-front war. Fresh off dealing with the skin-clad Void Nian, he now finds himself face-to-face—or rather, face-to-wood—with the bizarre Imperial Carriage of Great Qi's zombie child-emperor. And yes, riding inside that carriage is a chubby, giggling boy who is somehow fused with the Dragon Vein itself. Li Huowang tries fire, tries the spine-sword, tries everything, but nothing sticks. The pressure is immense, the despair real, and the chapter leaves him buried in dirt, utterly outmatched. If you thought his luck had hit rock bottom, guess again—there's always a deeper floor in the Dao-Twisted World.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang's bronze coin sword pierces the Void Nian's skull, but the creature remains unfazed and immediately retaliates by aging his left foot with a red spike.
- The Void Nian's intelligence is highlighted when it sacrifices a limb to trap Li Huowang, showing it's a tactical fighter, not just a beast.
- Heavy footsteps announce the arrival of the Imperial Carriage of Great Qi, an ornate but decayed wooden structure pulled by skeletal eunuchs.
- The child-emperor of Great Qi is revealed as a living, giggling child while alive, but his body is *literally fused* with the pile of Dragon Veins beneath his dragon robe.
- Li Huowang attempts a double-pronged attack, but the Dragon Vein is immune to the spine-sword's "cut between two histories" ability for the first time.
- The Void Nian and the Dragon Vein seem hostile to each other, giving Li Huowang a brief window to try escaping to Great Liang via a fissure—but the Dragon Vein shatters it, unwilling to let him slip away.
- After a devastating blow that flattens his shoulder, Li Huowang is left burying himself in soil, with his hallucinations flickering and his attack options exhausted.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The **Imperial Carriage** (*tiānzǐ chēlù*, 天子车辂) was the supreme ceremonial vehicle of the Chinese emperor—a mobile throne mounted on an ox-cart platform, draped in dragon-and-cloud fabrics, and festooned with jade, bells, and ritual ornaments. Only the Son of Heaven could ride in one. Seeing it here, decayed but still moving, screams that the authority of Great Qi is broken and undead at the same time—perfectly in line with the novel's theme of hollowed-out institutions.

The **Dragon Vein** (*lóngmài*, 龙脉) is a central concept in Chinese geomancy (fēngshuǐ) and imperial ideology. It's the underground "qi channel" that determines a dynasty's luck and lifespan. Cutting or blocking it can topple an entire empire. In this chapter, the author takes that abstract idea and makes it terrifyingly literal: you can *pile multiple Dragon Veins together* under a living child-emperor, making him a walking, giggling nexus of the dynasty's total qi reserves. The horror isn't just that he's a zombie—it's that he's the living embodiment of a nation's soul.

The **Void Nian** (*xūnián*, 虚年) is a corrupted spin on the traditional Nian beast from Chinese folklore. In legend, the Nian was a monster that would descend on villages at New Year's until people learned to scare it away with firecrackers and red paper. Here, that cyclical terror is hollowed out into something that drains *yang* life-force on contact and fights with cruel cunning—a year-beast that has forgotten why it came, but never how to hunt.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in "scale escalation without power fantasy." Li Huowang throws everything at the problem: sword-through-the-head, self-immolation (literal), strategic targeting of the softer enemy, and even his signature spine-sword fissure. And every single weapon bounces off. The Void Nian laughs off a skewered brain; the Dragon Vein doesn't even flinch at an attack that previously cut through reality itself. The point here is to make the reader feel as helpless as Li Huowang feels—the Qi emperor isn't just *strong*; he's operating on a totally different tier of existence. And that smile on the child's face is somehow worse than any snarl. Get ready for a real low point.
