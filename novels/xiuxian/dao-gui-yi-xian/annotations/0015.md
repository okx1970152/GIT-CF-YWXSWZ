## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang's grim daily reality continues as he escorts another human ingredient to the alchemy hall, feeling the terrible weight of his guilt slowly dull into a familiar numbness. Searching desperately for a chink in Danyangzi's armor, his scheming is interrupted by an aggressive confrontation with Danyangzi's only surviving inner disciple, Zheng Kun. The clash reveals two crucial pieces of information: Danyangzi trusts no one with his immortality method, not even his inner disciples, and Zheng Kun's bitter resentment might be a lever Li Huowang can finally use.

## Key Plot Points
This chapter kicks off with yet another sacrifice, the eighth human ingredient sent to Danyangzi's cauldron. Li Huowang catalogs what he knows about his captor: the Wandering Lord is absent on the 1st and 15th, Danyangzi is illiterate and relies on an unknown entity to read the Heavenly Scripture, and the temple's hierarchy is paper-thin. He briefly weighs assassination and poisoning but dismisses both as suicide. Then Zheng Kun appears—a gloomy, iron-hard inner disciple who radiates menace. When Li Huowang intentionally needles him, implying that Danyangzi might share the immortal method with the literate newcomer instead of his senior disciple, Zheng Kun snaps, beating him savagely. But Li Huowang's gambit works: he confirms that Zheng Kun has never been taught the core secrets, and he sees the naked envy and resentment in the man's eyes. Later, the kind-hearted Xuan Yuan helps him up, revealing in his confusion that even he knows nothing about the Wandering Lord. The chapter closes with Li Huowang spotting a potential tool in Zheng Kun's discontentment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Inner disciples vs. Registered disciples:** The distinction here is brutally clear. An inner disciple (内门弟子) like Zheng Kun receives direct, one-on-one instruction from the master and is trained in advanced techniques. A registered or ‘named’ disciple (记名弟子) gets basic teaching and does temple chores but is kept out of the real secrets. In traditional Daoist schools, this was often a formal marker of commitment and seniority; here, Danyangzi twists it into a system of deliberate ignorance—hoarding his ‘immortal method’ as leverage.  
**Dao names:** Li Huowang's temple name is Xuan Yang (玄阳), meaning 'Mysterious Yang.' In Daoist tradition, adopting a new name upon entering a sect symbolizes rebirth into the spiritual lineage. The hierarchy is often reinforced through these names, with the shared ‘generation character’ linking master and disciples.  
**Wandering Lord (游老爷):** This entity functions as Danyangzi's surveillance system, an invisible enforcer that keeps the temple's occupants in line. The fact that even Xuan Yuan doesn't know about it shows how carefully Danyangzi hoards information, making his regime feel like a web of secrets where no one knows the full pattern.

## Reading Guide
This is the first time Li Huowang really starts fighting back—not with force, but with information. He uses Zheng Kun's pride and insecurity like a probe, deliberately poking until he gets a reaction that reveals data. That moment of eye contact, when he clocks Zheng Kun's raw resentment, is the chapter's electric jolt. If you've been feeling the slow crawl of despair in these opening chapters, take heart: this is the shift from passive suffering to active scheming. Li Huowang is learning how to read people, and that's his real weapon. Keep an eye on Zheng Kun going forward—he's a powder keg, and Li Huowang just lit the fuse. Also, note how the cruelty in this world feels terribly quiet and bureaucratic: a man forced to recite his own will before walking to his death. That silence is part of the horror.
