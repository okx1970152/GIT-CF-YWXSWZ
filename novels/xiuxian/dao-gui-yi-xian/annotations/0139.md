## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists! This chapter is a massive lore drop that will completely reframe how you see the Dao-Twisted World. Li Huowang receives a crash course in cosmic geography from the gloriously vulgar Abbess Jingxin, introducing the concept of the Five Buddhist Realms and the terrifying hierarchy of gods that exist within them. While the protagonist’s mind is being blown by the true scale of the universe’s horror, the quieter B-plot shows us a refreshing moment of normalcy: Chun Xiaoman’s dedication to her sword training reveals a character hungry for agency, while Yang Xiaohai and Gouwa provide a look at the group’s internal dynamics. It’s a chapter of big reveals and small, character-driven moments, perfectly balancing cosmic dread with grounded survival.

## Key Plot Points

*   Li Huowang learns that the Joy God (Xi Shen) he encountered is only a minuscule fragment of the far more powerful Double Joy God (Xi Shen), establishing a terrifying hierarchy among deities.
*   Abbess Jingxin outlines the Five Buddhist Realms: Adamantine, Fragrant, Joyful, Utmost Bliss, and Lotus. The Joy God and Double Joy God are identified as wandering spirits from the Joyful World in the south.
*   Danyangzi’s hallucination violently manifests for the first time in a while, seething with contempt and dismissing the "Five Buddhist Realms" as the ignorant fantasy of dogmatic monks, a stark contrast to the abbess’s explanation.
*   The abbess reveals that different sects have different names for what is essentially the same celestial place—some call it a unified Penglai, while others claim it’s all a fabrication, highlighting the chaotic and information-poor nature of the world.
*   Li Huowang asks where the entity known as Ba She comes from, but the abbess, not being a member of the Ao-Jing Sect, has no answer. This leaves a vital piece of the cosmic puzzle tantalizingly out of reach.
*   In the chapter’s domestic scenes, Yang Xiaohai watches Chun Xiaoman practice her sword with envy. She is dedicated, practicing constantly, and decides to go to town to find a real opponent at the escort agency, asserting her own path to power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter throws a massive curveball by introducing a structured cosmology that seems to borrow from various Buddhist and Daoist traditions. The concept of multiple "Buddha-worlds" (佛土/佛国) is a core tenet of Mahayana Buddhism, particularly in the *Avatamsaka Sutra* (Huayan Jing), which describes a cosmos of infinite, interpenetrating realms. The specific list given—East (Fragrant), South (Joyful), West (Utmost Bliss), North (Lotus), and Center (Adamantine)—is a direct nod to this, with the "Utmost Bliss World" (极乐世界) being the famous Pure Land of Amitabha Buddha.

However, the Dao-Twisted World perverts this. The "Joyful World in the south" isn't a land of enlightenment; it’s home to terrifying "wandering spirits" like the Double Joy God. It takes a sacred, orderly cosmology and turns it into a hierarchy of hungry, alien horrors. This is a classic move in the novel: using the authentic framework of Chinese religious vocabulary to build a universe that feels blasphemously wrong.

Also, note the abbess’s mention of “Penglai” (蓬莱), a legendary isle of immortals in Daoist mythology. The fact that different sects can’t agree on whether it’s one place, five places, or a lie is a fantastic piece of worldbuilding. It tells you that no single religion has the full map, and the protagonists are navigating a universe where the instruction manual is incomplete, contradictory, and probably written by demons.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a "lore dump" done right—it's delivered through a character whose personality is so strong (the filthy, snoring, utterly pragmatic Abbess Jingxin) that it never feels like a textbook. Watch how Li Huowang reacts. He's not just filing information; he's trying to *connect* it. His mind immediately goes to Ba She and the Zhengde Temple incident, proving he’s learning to see the patterns in the madness.

Pay close attention to the contrast between Abbess Jingxin and Danyangzi’s hallucination. The abbess is indifferent and grounded ("I can't be bothered to argue"), while Danyangzi is furious and grand ("Only when you truly see the Celestial Realm..."). One is a survivor dealing with her immediate problems; the other is a ghost obsessed with a false, abstract glory. This perfectly encapsulates the choice Li Huowang faces: adapt and survive in the real (and horrifying) present, or chase a beautiful lie that will destroy him.

Finally, don’t sleep on the ending scene with Chun Xiaoman. While Li Huowang is grappling with the infinite, she’s taking practical action. Her decision to find a real opponent at the escort agency is a small but crucial step from practicing to applying—a practical, direct strategy that stands in stark contrast to Li Huowang’s endless metaphysical struggles.
