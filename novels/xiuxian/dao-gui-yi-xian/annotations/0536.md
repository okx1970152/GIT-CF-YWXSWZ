## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang recharges in the mundane safety of his capital home—eating a rock-hard bun, listening to Li Sui's chanting, and trying not to think about the white-walled prison waiting for him if his Innate Pneuma slips again. But the dread of the Dragon Vein and Dice's endgame claws its way back in. Just when the hunt seems hopeless, Zhuge Yuan drops a name: Shangji Guankou. A cryptic entity that may or may not be human, a mountain gate that may or may not be a sect, and the only lead that promises to pinpoint the most wanted trickster in two empires. Li Huowang doesn't hesitate for a second.

## Key Plot Points
1. Li Huowang wakes up at home (not in the asylum)—a small victory that immediately comes with a price: lingering cognitive damage from forcing Zhuge Yuan's Cultivate the True, which threatens to destabilize his Innate Pneuma and drag him back to White Tower.
2. Zhuge Yuan dispels Li Huowang's worry about Dice corrupting the Dragon Vein: inside the palace, the Dragon Vein is the protector, not the protected—Dice can't win a direct confrontation with the True Dragon Son of Heaven.
3. Li Huowang presses on Great Qi's severed Dragon Vein, which visibly unsettles Zhuge Yuan; the dead scholar admits he died before the severance and has no answer, leaving the mystery dangling.
4. Zhuge Yuan pivots the strategy from guessing Dice's plan to hunting his location passively, revealing his solution: seek out Shangji Guankou in Great Qi—a being/place that can track Dice down but is impossible to describe in words.
5. Li Huowang is shocked that the Supervisory Heavenly Office never tried this route; Zhuge Yuan calmly explains that Shangji Guankou only exists in Great Qi, not in Great Liang—a sobering reminder of how much knowledge died with the fallen empire.
6. The chapter ends on a rare moment of forward momentum: Li Huowang, reinvigorated, sets out beyond the city walls to cross into Great Qi with single-minded determination.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Shangji Guankou (上极灌口):** This arcane name is not a random invention. "灌口" (Guankou) is a real geographic term—an irrigation sluice or canal head—but in Chinese mythology, the most famous Guankou is the temple of Erlang Shen (二郎神), the fearsome god with a vertical third eye. The name itself hints at a transcendent vantage point: a "mouth" or "gate" at the extreme upper limit, from which all things can be seen. It also echoes the idea that some beings exist *at the border of description*—which is why Zhuge Yuan says "in some eyes it's a person, in others a demon, and in others a mountain gate."

**The "Can't Be Spoken" (说不得) Trope:** In Chinese folk supernatural literature, certain names are taboo because speaking them *defines* them into a form too narrow for their nature. It's a cousin to the folk belief that knowing a demon's true name gives you power over it—and that by extension, labeling a transcendent being wrongly is dangerous. Zhuge Yuan's refusal to define Shangji Guankou is not coyness; it's a cautious survival instinct.

**Dragon Vein (龙脉) as Political Geomancy:** In traditional cosmology, a dynasty is not just a political entity—it is a living body sustained by underground energy channels called Dragon Veins. A thriving dynasty has a strong, unimpeded Dragon Vein; a severed one means the mandate of heaven has been revoked. That Great Qi's Dragon Vein broke means more than just an empire falling—it means the land itself *rejected* its rulers. Li Huowang's question hits Zhuge Yuan where it hurts: his civilization not only died but was cosmically invalidated.

## Reading Guide
**High-energy setup chapter disguised as a breakfast scene.** The pacing is deceptive: the first half feels like domestic downtime, but every beat—the headache, the burnt buns, the silent tension with Zhuge Yuan—is laying groundwork for the second half's sharp pivot. Enjoy the breather, but stay alert; the real meat is crammed into the final pages.

**Watch how Li Huowang's approach has matured.** Earlier in the story, a lead like this would make him charge in half-cocked. Now he asks the hard questions ("If it's that simple, why didn't the Office do it?") before grabbing his dao robe. He's actively stress-testing his own hope.

**Zhuge Yuan's discomfort with the "child" subject is telling.** He dodges the question about Great Qi's Dragon Vein with visible strain—not because he's hiding something, but because his own knowledge died with him. This is a rare moment where Zhuge Yuan's composure cracks, reminding us that even the dead are limited by what they witnessed.

**Why the "private" setting matters.** The chapter opens in Li Huowang's own house, a space the novel rarely uses. It creates a false sense of safety—but the headache, the hospital's gravitational pull, and the constant threat of his Innate Pneuma slipping all remind us that *nowhere is truly safe*.

**Buckle up for an operation with no support.** Zhuge Yuan makes it clear: Shangji Guankou can find Dice, but dealing with him afterward is entirely on Li Huowang. This is a solo reconnaissance mission into a dead empire, chasing a god-tier manipulator, with only a ghost for backup.
