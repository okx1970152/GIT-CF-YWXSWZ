## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow initiates—this chapter is a gut-punch wrapped in a philosophical bomb. After a harrowing escape via skin-artifact possession, Li Huowang finds himself back in the sterile white nightmare of his hospital room. But this isn’t just a breather between fights. His mother, Sun Xiaoqin, has managed to secure a caregiver’s spot, and her quiet, desperate love pulls him into a moment of terrifying clarity. The core question of the entire novel gets an existential upgrade: what if the real world isn’t the fake one? What if his own Heart-Element powers have been rewriting *both* sides of his reality all along? It’s part family drama, part cosmic horror re-contextualization, and absolutely not to be missed.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang survives decapitation by using the skin-double artifact, which inflates from his shadow to form a new, intact body.
- Li Sui remains trapped in the old body, leaving Li Huowang without her hallucination-suppression abilities.
- He snaps back to the modern world hallucination, restrained in a straitjacket, just as his mother enters the ward.
- Sun Xiaoqin shows maternal cunning: she plugs the room’s microphone hole with rice and warns Li Huowang to never reveal the gold’s origin.
- Li Huowang begins to seriously question his own foundational assumption: the gold and uncanny coincidences might be products of his own unconscious Heart-Element reality-bending, not proof that the modern world is fake.
- The chapter ends on a cliffhanger of radical doubt, suggesting the “real” world could be just as valid as the Dao-Twisted World.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter weaponizes a central xianxia-philosophical concept: **Cultivate the True vs. Cultivate the False (修真/修假)**. In standard cultivation novels, “seeking truth” (修真) is a given—a path toward enlightenment and ultimate reality. The *Dao-Twisted World* introduces its monstrous twin: “cultivating falsehood into reality,” the ability of a Heart-Element to make fiction concrete. Li Huowang’s breakthrough here is brutal: if both worlds can be *made real* by his own power, then neither has an inherent claim to being the “base reality.” This upends the entire prior framework of the story and forces the reader to inhabit the same radical uncertainty as the protagonist. The scene where Sun Xiaoqin plugs the microphone with rice is also a wonderful, grounded detail from real-world Chinese prison/hospital lore—a mother’s practical cunning against an indifferent system.

### Cultivate the True/False (修真/修假)

A central philosophical dichotomy in the Dao-Twisted World; a Heart-Element does not merely *follow* truth but can *create* it from fiction.

### Skin-double artifact (皮人法器)

A talisman that stores a copy of the user's form in a deflated leather skin; when released, it re-inflates by consuming the user’s shadow.

## Reading Guide

This is one of those chapters where the action is minimal but the stakes are existential. Don’t breeze past the final paragraph: that question is the novel’s engine recalibrating. Pay close attention to the tonal whiplash—one moment Li Huowang is a headless corpse in a demonic world, the next he’s being spoon-fed rice by his mom. That jarring transition is the *point*. The horror here isn’t a monster; it’s the creeping suspicion that *you can never know what is real*. Also, note how Zhuge Yuan’s earlier exposition about Heart-Element abilities is finally paying off in an agonizing way. This chapter recontextualizes everything that came before it.
