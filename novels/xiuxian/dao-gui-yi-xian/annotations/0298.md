## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow survivors of the Dao-Twisted World! This chapter is a masterclass in dread done differently—no tentacles, no screaming rituals, just a drunk dinner conversation between new “friends” that cuts Li Huowang deeper than any blade. Our boy gets a firsthand look at what a Heart-Element *looks like* when it’s been harvested, pickled, and bagged for the imperial treasury. And spoiler alert: it looks exactly like him. The horror here isn’t jump-scares; it’s the slow, creeping realization that the people you’re toasting with would gut you for spare parts if they knew what was sitting across the table. Li Huowang plays the fool beautifully, masking his shock with false admiration, but inside? The rules of survival just got rewritten. Get ready for a chapter that’s heavy on atmosphere, light on action, and absolutely crushing in its implications.

## Key Plot Points
- Tuoba Danqing drunkenly shows off a severed Heart-Element head and organs as a trophy from a border operation, explaining that a live one was stolen before they could secure it.
- Li Huowang, hiding his horror, feigns ignorance and pumps Tuoba for information about Heart-Element lore, learning that every part (tongue, flesh, bones) is a rare alchemical material worth centuries of yang-life.
- After the feast, Tuoba pretends to sleep and secretly evaluates Li Huowang, approving of his lack of visible greed—confirming that the Office is constantly testing him.
- Alone with Bai Lingmiao, Li Huowang’s mask drops. He admits the crushing truth: the Supervisory Heavenly Office is not a safe haven; as a Heart-Element, he is walking prey that any “ally” would sell the moment profit outweighs caution.
- The chapter closes on a somber note of fragile reassurance as Bai Lingmiao tries to offer comfort, but the weight of Li Huowang’s self-reliance vow hangs heavy.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Turn your reading glasses on, because this chapter is dripping with lore nectar. First up: **Heart-Element (心素)**. In classical Daoist cosmology, the *xinsu* was a concept from the *Huainanzi*—the primeval substance before form becomes body. This novel weaponizes that ancient philosophy: a “Heart-Element” isn’t a cultivator type; it’s a *living ingredient*, a walking piece of primordial matter that everyone wants to grind into pills or weapons. The horror is that Li Huowang’s very existence is valuable as raw material, not as a person.

Next: the **“Fortune” (招财)** euphemism. Tuoba explains that “fortune” means “tongue.” This is jianghu code-talk, a sign that the Office operates with professional slang to keep uninitiated ears from understanding the true value of what’s being discussed. It mirrors real historical Chinese secret societies (like the White Lotus or Triads) where coded language was survival tech.

Also noteworthy: the **imperial treasury (国库)** mention. In the Dao-Twisted World, the central government actively collects and hoards supernatural resources. This isn’t a lawless frontier; it’s a *system* that treats Heart-Elements as state assets. That makes the Office both a protection racket and a direct threat—they don’t just want Li Huowang’s loyalty; they want his *body* on a shelf.

Finally, note the drinking culture. Feasts that last from the second watch (9–11 PM) to dawn were standard among the elite and martial underworld in pre-modern China, serving as both bonding ritual and intelligence-gathering operation. Tuoba’s drunkenness is partly real, partly performance—a classic test to see if Li Huowang’s tongue loosens first.

## Reading Guide
This is a “quiet burn” chapter, and it’s one of the most important turning points in the arc so far. If you came here expecting a fight scene, recalibrate. The real battle is happening inside Li Huowang’s head. Watch how he processes the evidence of a dead Heart-Element: he doesn’t panic, doesn’t run, doesn’t even flinch visibly. Instead, he *studies* it—asking the right questions, filing away every detail about the tongue, the organs, the value. That’s the survival instinct of a man who knows his only weapon is information.

Also pay attention to Bai Lingmiao’s role here. She’s no longer the timid girl from earlier chapters. Her immediate concern when Li Huowang returns isn’t “where’s the food” but “you scared me.” She reads his mood instantly and offers comfort without demanding explanations. That’s quiet growth—and a lifeline for a protagonist who just realized he can’t trust anyone in power.

If you’re a lore hound, this is your feast. The way the Office talks about Heart-Elements as “celestial treasures bound for the imperial treasury” should send chills down your spine. This isn’t a monster-of-the-week world; it’s a world with *institutions* that have perfected the art of harvesting human-shaped resources. The horror is bureaucratic, transactional, and smiling at you over a wine cup.

Final thought: Li Huowang’s mental shift from “find allies” to “trust no one” is a dark necessity. But note that he doesn’t paint Bai Lingmiao with that brush. She remains the exception. That distinction matters—it’s the thread keeping him from turning into a complete monster.
