## Chapter Overview
Two worlds collide—and the collision has never been more brutal. In the Dao-Twisted World, Bai Lingmiao’s sight is failing, her fingers are raw from a kitchen accident, and her quiet grief is a slow, drowning thing. She is becoming a burden and she knows it. Meanwhile, in the modern world, Li Huowang is having lunch with his mother. It’s warm. It’s sweet. It’s a trap. When doctors arrive to take him for “rehabilitation,” his lucid mind immediately recognizes the danger: if they wheel him out that door, the body on the other side will wander off somewhere he can’t control. The horror here isn’t a monster with fangs—it’s the slow realization that being *sane* is its own kind of vulnerability.

## Key Plot Points
- Bai Lingmiao’s vision continues to degrade; she can now only perceive blurry colors and shapes, relying on the Second Spirit to lead her by touch.
- She burns her hand while trying to light a fire, and the Second Spirit silently pushes her aside to finish the work—a moment of quiet, devastating humiliation.
- She admits to Li Huowang (in her head) that she has been lying too: her heart has not let go, and she wonders if they would both be better off apart.
- In the modern world, Li Huowang enjoys a peaceful meal with Sun Xiaoqin, who has rented a place nearby and visits him regularly.
- Two doctors and three orderlies enter and begin removing his restraints for “rehabilitation exercises,” revealing his shockingly atrophied body.
- Li Huowang’s panic spikes: he cannot leave this room while lucid, because doing so would sever his anchor to the Dao-Twisted World, where his body is currently walking into the woods.
- The bald doctor deduces Li Huowang’s lucid state and presses him on exactly why he cannot leave—making the threat feel clinical and inevitable.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The **parallel-reality anxiety** in this chapter is a hallmark of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*’s structure: Li Huowang’s body exists in two places at once, and control of one seems to abandon the other. The modern doctors are not supernatural, but their *orderly kindness* is a weapon of its own—they have no idea they are about to destroy his anchor to a second life. His terror is not that they mean him harm, but that they mean him *no harm at all*, and their well-intentioned exercise will still end up killing him on the other side. This is a rare inversion of the “hospital as safe haven” trope: the sterile white room has become a prison he *needs* to stay in. Also noteworthy is Bai Lingmiao’s narrative: her blindness is not a result of violence but of slow, degenerative corruption—a kind of quiet body horror that mirrors Li Huowang’s mental atrophy. The Dao-Twisted World is rotting her senses as surely as the hospital bed is rotting his muscles.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is pure psychological tension—no battles, no spells, no monsters. The threat is a wheelchair and a doctor’s patient voice. It asks you to sit in the gut-churning dread of a man who is *too sane* for his own good. Pay close attention to the dialogue split: Bai Lingmiao’s internal confession (“My heart hasn’t let go, either”) echoes Sun Xiaoqin’s maternal nagging, creating a strange emotional resonance between the two women who love him in different worlds. The doctor’s final question—“What are you afraid of?”—is the chapter’s thesis. Li Huowang is afraid of exactly what the doctor offers: health, freedom, rehabilitation. Because that would mean leaving the loop, leaving the other world, and leaving the people there to rot. Sometimes being trapped is the only thing keeping you safe.
