## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a heavy one, fellow travelers. Chapter 247 is a *quiet* chapter—the calm after a storm of flesh and madness—but don’t let that fool you. This is where the weight of everything Li Huowang has been through settles onto his shoulders like a mountain of wet stone. He’s healed up, the group is safe in Qingqiu, and life is almost... normal. But for a man who can’t tell which world is real, “normal” is the most terrifying thing of all. We see him obsess over a small gold box, hoarding it like a lifeline, and the reason behind it cuts deeper than any blade. The chapter ends with a transfer to the hospital reality—and a mother sleeping by his bedside. It’s a quiet hammer blow right to the heart.

## Key Plot Points

Li Huowang stops himself from eating the Black Tai Sui, hesitating. He asks about the group’s gold reserves and, finding them insufficient, taps Sun Baolu to borrow more from his clan. Baolu returns with a stunning, antique gold box—his mother’s dowry from a wealthy family—which she offers as payment for her son’s life. Li Huowang is unsettled by her eagerness to close the debt. He insists it's only a loan.

Over the following days, he keeps the box glued to his side like a dragon with a hoard. That night, Bai Lingmiao asks the million-li question: does he *hope* the other world is real, or does he hope it’s fake? Li Huowang admits he doesn’t know. Time passes. His face heals. As he’s about to deal with Han Fu, Sun Baolu brings a proposal: the women rescued from the stockade are interested in settling down with local Qingqiu men, and the chief needs Li Huowang’s blessing.

Li Huowang agrees readily—it’s a safer life for them. But before he can finish the thought, reality shifts. He feels the world twisting and screams for Zhijian to bring the chains. The tent vanishes. He’s in a hospital room, clutching the gold box. His mother is asleep on a chair beside him, covered in a small blanket. His final, broken word: “Mom.”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Beauty Paper (美人纸)** – In feudal China, extreme luxury and dehumanization often went hand in hand. “Beauty Paper” refers to a historical practice where a wealthy noble would employ a beautiful young woman to serve as a living, reusable piece of toilet paper. Yes, you read that right. It represents the absolute pinnacle of class-based cruelty and explains why Sun Baolu’s mother comes from a family so wealthy they could casually gift an ornately inlaid gold box as a dowry. The mention of “Beauty Paper” subtly underscores the horrifying social inequality embedded in the world—and why she might be eager to sever ties with reminders of that past.

**The Gold Box as a Talisman** – Why is Li Huowang so fixated on gold? In the Dao-Twisted World, gold isn’t just currency; it’s a physical anchor. Li Huowang is terrified of losing himself to the hallucinatory reality, and the gold box is his escape pod. He’s not being greedy; he’s stockpiling a “get out of jail free” card for his next mental slide. The box itself, with its precious stones and imperial weight, symbolizes the last shred of material certainty he can cling to. When he transfers with it *still in his hands* at the end of the chapter, it becomes proof that the boundary between worlds is porous, and that objects—like trauma—can travel both ways.

## Reading Guide

This chapter gives us one of the most achingly human moments in the entire arc. Bai Lingmiao’s question—*do you hope it’s real, or do you hope it’s fake?*—is the core existential dilemma of the entire novel. Li Huowang wants the other world to be just a hallucination so he can stay sane, but he *needs* it to be real so his actions there have meaning. The horror of “I can’t tell” is amplified not by monsters, but by love. Seeing his sleeping mother in the final scene is a gut punch; it transforms the abstract dread into a visceral ache. Pay attention to how Sun Baolu’s character has opened up since his rescue. His hesitation and stammering also reveal that Li Huowang’s reputation in Qingqiu is becoming something… darker. The people are scared of the man who came back from the dead with grotesque trophies. This chapter is a masterclass in horror-through-stillness.
