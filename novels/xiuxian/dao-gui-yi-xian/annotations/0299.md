## Chapter Overview
After the gut-wrenching revelations of the previous chapter, Li Huowang finally catches a break—or so it seems. He rallies his ragtag crew—Gouwa, Gao Zhijian, Chun Xiaoman, and Bai Lingmiao—and heads for what he hopes will be a safe harbor. But homecoming in the Dao-Twisted World is never that simple. As the group approaches the rustic, picture-perfect Niuxin Mountain with Bai Lingmiao bubbling over with joy, Li Huowang lets himself believe, for just a moment, that he can finally put down roots. But the moment he arrives, an anxious Lü Zhuangyuan pulls him aside with a look that says *everything is wrong*. The chapter ends on Bai Lingmiao’s desperate, unanswered calls for her parents—a gut-punch reminder that in this world, peace is always borrowed, never owned.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang snaps out of his funk and commands the team to move out, heading to Bai Lingmiao’s hometown on Niuxin Mountain.
- The cart journey is surprisingly warm and lighthearted, with Gouwa’s bickering, Chun Xiaoman’s sharp tongue, and Bai Lingmiao’s infectious excitement.
- Bai Lingmiao paints a vivid, almost painfully wholesome picture of her family and village, filling Li Huowang with cautious hope.
- The Lü troupe is already there and safe—no casualties—but Lü Zhuangyuan is **visibly nervous**.
- Lü Zhuangyuan insists on speaking to Li Huowang alone, flashing a meaningful look toward Bai Lingmiao.
- Bai Lingmiao reads the situation instantly and runs off to find her family, only to be met with silence and emptiness.
- The chapter closes on her tearful cries echoing through a village where every door is shut and every room is empty.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Niuxin Mountain (牛心山)** — Literally “Ox-Heart Mountain.” The name sounds rustic and quaint, like something out of a pastoral folktale. But in the Dao-Twisted World, the very sweetness of the name should make your skin crawl. The more beautiful the promise, the more gruesome the disappointment.

**The “live-in son-in-law” (赘婿)** — Li Huowang jokes about becoming a live-in son-in-law, a historically stigmatized role in traditional Chinese society. A man who moved in with his wife’s family was often seen as having no prospects of his own. Li Huowang’s self-deprecating acceptance of this role shows just how exhausted he is—he’s given up on pride if it means safety. In xianxia terms, this is a massive retreat from the usual “dominant cultivator” fantasy, and that’s exactly the point.

**The silent village trope** — A classic horror setup in Chinese folklore and modern web novels alike. Bai Lingmiao’s empty home, with every door closed, evokes the ancient image of a *ghost village* or a place abandoned to something worse. The lack of signs of struggle or violence is often more terrifying than gore—because it implies the disappearance was deliberate or inexplicable.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, folks—this chapter is the classic “calm before the storm,” executed with surgical precision. After chapters of non-stop body horror and betrayal, the author gives you a gentle cart ride through sunny fields, a cute girl bragging about her grandpa’s free melons, and the faint promise of a happy ending. You know what that means. **It’s a trap.**

The real horror here isn’t a monster—it’s the *absence* of one. The gut-punch is emotional, not visceral. Bai Lingmiao runs through her own village, calling for her parents, and no one answers. No blood. No signs of struggle. Just silence. And for a character as sweet and hopeful as Bai Lingmiao, that silence hits harder than any flesh buddha ever could.

What really stings is that Li Huowang *let himself believe*. For the first time in ages, he let himself hope. And the novel punished him for it immediately. This is *Dao Gui Yi Xian*’s signature move: it gives you just enough daylight to make the next shadow cut deeper.
