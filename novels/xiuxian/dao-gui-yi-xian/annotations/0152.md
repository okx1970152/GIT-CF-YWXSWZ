## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow travelers of the Dao-Twisted World, because Li Huowang finally gets to *push back*. No more running, no more hiding in shadows. A mysterious assassin armed with a creepy shadow-puppet comes calling in the dead of night, and our boy greets him with a brand-new sword—one that makes even seasoned creeps hesitate. Li Huowang is starting to carve out a foothold in this nightmare, and it feels *good*. But the chapter isn't all triumphant blade work. It’s also a masterclass in the novel's signature whiplash: one moment you’re swinging steel and shouting “KILL!”, the next you’re lying rigid in the dark, feeling cold serpentine fingers crawl up your leg while a voice whispers “Husband~” in your ear. Pure, undiluted *Dao Gui Yi Xian*.

## Key Plot Points

- A sinister shadow-puppet entity attacks Li Huowang through a paper window in his inn room. It immediately recognizes him as a “Heart-Element,” a term that now signals danger louder than any war drum.
- Li Huowang draws his new military sword for the first time in real combat. The sword’s killing intent surges through him, intensifying his aggression and turning his vision red, driving a devastating, direct attack.
- The attacker operates by manipulating a puppet frame and wearing a worn bronze mask. His technique is more stealth than raw force—a lone rogue, not a high-ranking sect.
- The attacker retreats and even hesitates upon seeing the sword, asking about Li Huowang’s connection to the “Right Family of Siqi.” Li Huowang immediately files away this clue about the weapon’s origin.
- Li Huowang rushes back to check on Bai Lingmiao, demonstrating his shifting priorities and dependence on his group’s safety, but finds her unharmed.
- A tense confrontation with Bai Lingmiao’s Second Spirit follows: it thanks him for acknowledging its help, but then attempts a disturbingly intimate advance, which Li Huowang violently shuts down.
- The next day, Li Huowang calmly analyzes the night’s events over a meal, displaying a new, tactical mindset. He hypothesizes the attacker is a lone rogue and decides to stay in town rather than flee into the wilderness.
- Lü Zhuangyuan arrives in high spirits, having apparently won an argument with the local Nuo opera troupe, providing a moment of mundane, almost comedic relief.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Shadow Puppets (皮影戏):** This chapter weaponizes a very ancient form of Chinese folk theatre. Shadow puppetry, or *piyingxi*, involves manipulating flat, articulated figures behind a screen to tell stories—often martial epics or ghost tales. In the Dao-Twisted World, the art itself becomes a vector for supernatural assault. The fact that the puppet is a projection, its master safely hidden behind a mask, makes it a deeply unnerving threat. It’s not a monster you can simply walk up to and stab; its body is a trick of light and shadow.

**Heart-Element (心素):** The chapter confirms that the term “Heart-Element” is now a universal red flag. It’s not just Danyangzi’s private obsession anymore; the supernatural underworld has a bounty on them. This reinforces the novel’s core horror: Li Huowang himself is a resource, a raw material, and now, anyone with the right knowledge can read the price tag on his existence.

**The “Right Family” Clue:** The attacker’s question about Siqi’s Right Family is a breadcrumb about the sword’s pedigree. In the feudal-like social structure of the Dao-Twisted World, a hereditary “family” (家) of warriors or sword-smiths implies a tradition, a code, and a network of obligations. The sword is not just a weapon; it’s a calling card with a reputation attached, good or bad.

**Bang Bing Jue (帮兵决):** While not used in this chapter by a character, Bai Lingmiao’s “Spirit-Summoning Verses” are a crucial part of her kit. These rhythmic, urgent chants are the core incantation of the *chuma* (出马) spirit-medium tradition, used to invite a possessing immortal to ride the medium. They represent a form of vocalized ritual authority that directly contrasts with the silent, manipulative art of the shadow-puppeteer.

### Bang Bing Jue

A traditional northeastern Chinese spirit-summoning verse with a rapid, urgent rhythm belonging to the chuma medium tradition.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a milestone. After volumes of being prey, Li Huowang has finally acquired a weapon that grants him a *status effect*. The sword’s murderous aura isn’t just for show—it physically changes him, painting the world red and dialing his aggression to eleven. The author is telling us that power here is not clean; it stains the user. Watch how that bloodlust might linger.

The second half, though, is where the real horror lives. The Second Spirit’s behavior is a classic *Dao Gui Yi Xian* moment. It was helpful, so Li Huowang can’t justify smashing it (yet). But its “affection” is a grotesque violation of boundaries, a constant reminder that nothing in this world—not even the protectors—is safe or sane. The tension between being grateful to the hand that warned you and wanting to cut it off as it crawls up your leg is the very soul of this novel.

Finally, keep an eye on that “Right Family of Siqi” thread. In this world, a name like that is never just a passing detail. It’s a hook for a much larger conflict, or an alliance, or a blood debt that will eventually come due.
