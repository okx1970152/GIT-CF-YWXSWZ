## Chapter Overview

*Chapter 240: Fengdu* is a cold plunge into the uncanny: a decompression chamber that turns out to be a trap. Li Huowang finally puts a name to the dark, nested caves beneath Qingqiu—Fengdu—and just as the exit appears, the guide he never trusted reveals his hand. What follows is a masterclass in slow-burn horror that escalates from a history lesson on Heavenly Calamities to a gut-drop identity reveal: his traveling companion isn’t just a weird Daoist, he’s a two-headed variant who’s known Li Huowang’s secret all along. This chapter isn’t about gore; it’s about the terrible moment a friendly face splits into two.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang learns the name of the cave network: **Fengdu** (the underworld in Chinese mythology). Han Fu clarifies the rule of escape: always go up.
- A conversation on the **Yuan family’s “eating the heirless inheritance”** practice reframes the Rope Spirit from a cheap trick into a nasty folk-horror institution.
- Han Fu drops massive lore: the last Heavenly Calamity was the disappearance of **Lies**, which drove the Zuowandao to suicidal panic. This confirms the world’s reality-cycles are recurring.
- As the group nears the exit, Han Fu nonchalantly knocks off Li Huowang’s copper coin mask, revealing his face.
- His “guess what you are” moment: **the reveal of his true form**—a double-headed Daoist with a tiny baby head poking from his collar. The one talking in the dark was never Han Fu alone.
- Li Huowang draws steel the moment Han Fu seals himself into shadow, but the Daoist vanishes before the blade lands.
- Final beat: Li Huowang commands a frantic sprint for the exit, fully aware they’re prey in a hunter’s territory.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Fengdu (酆都)** is a direct lift from traditional Chinese cosmology—it is not a “hell” in the Biblical sense but rather a sprawling, bureaucratic underworld city where the dead are judged and death-demons administer punishment. In the *Dao-Twisted World*, Fengdu is repurposed into a dark, layered cave system beneath Qingqiu, one that twists the geography of the underworld into a hostile, nest-like maze. The word choice is deliberate: it carries all the dread of a “journey into the underworld” without needing a single skeleton.

**Eating the Heirless Inheritance (吃绝户)** is the grim folk-custom that drives the Rope Spirit subplot. In traditional Chinese rural society, if a family died out without a male heir, the community often divided the estate among themselves. This novel literalizes that process: they ritually *feed* the old person to a spirit so they can legally and supernaturally claim the property. It is class exploitation dressed as exorcism, and the Dao-Twisted World treats it as business as usual.

**The Heavenly Calamity cycle** is introduced here as a recurrent, systemic phenomenon. The fact that “Lies” once vanished is a terrifying peek at the world’s cosmological logic—these aren’t natural disasters, they are *ontological lapses*, periods where some fundamental rule of reality—like rot, like truth—just ceases to function. For the Zuowandao, a world without lies is a world in which they cannot exist; their panic is both a punchline and a clue.

### Eating the heirless inheritance (吃绝户)

A rural folk custom where a community claims the property of a family with no male heir; the novel twists it into ritualistically feeding the elder to a spirit.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterful **slow-burn unmasking**. Every friendly detail about Han Fu—his cynicism, his chatter, his claim that the little girl died—is retroactively poisoned by the double-head reveal. When you reread, notice how the *same voice* shifts between adult and childish tones without warning; that’s the two heads trading lines. The real horror here is not the monster, but the *trust meter that drained too slowly*. Li Huowang catches the mask a beat late, and suddenly the door is gone. Also, pay attention to the “up is the way out” rule—it’s one of the few survival guidelines in this world that works. Don’t forget it.

## Deeplore Note: The Hierophany of the Heavenly Calamity (天灾)

The term “Heavenly Calamity” is the novel’s stand-in for a theological term: a *hierophany gone wrong*, a moment where the fabric of reality tears because the Siming (司命, Arbiter of Fate) that governs that layer is either dead, sleeping, or fighting someone bigger. The “disappearance of Lies” means that the Dao of Deception itself was suppressed. The Zuowandao’s reaction—desperation, suicidal thoughts—is not mere panic; it is a sect whose patron deity has been cut off from the world. Every Lost Calamity is a war report from the heavens.
