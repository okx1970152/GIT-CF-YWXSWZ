## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers one of the most devastating psychological gut-punches in the arc so far. Li Huowang thinks he has finally caught a break, but Bai Lingmiao’s confession of her new, clear-eyed mindset turns her into a stranger wearing his lover’s face. Just when he begins to rally, hoping to cure her, the chapter pivots hard into a new supernatural nightmare. The *twist* regarding the two Heart-Turbids recontextualizes everything the group has been through, leaving them—and the reader—staring into an abyss of horror. Buckle up, because “It’s over” isn’t just a chapter title; it’s a statement of fact.

## Key Plot Points
- Bai Lingmiao openly acknowledges that Li Huowang killed her family, but declares she has made peace with it because they were White Lotus members who would have killed him. She claims this new clarity makes her painless and decisive, but Li Huowang sees her transforming into a version of the brutal Peng Longteng, which terrifies him.
- Overwhelmed by the change in Bai Lingmiao and the Second Spirit’s sudden intimacy, Li Huowang extricates himself on a flimsy excuse and privately asks the Chief Recorder for a connection to a military cultivator, hoping to find a way to dispel the martial killing intent corrupting Bai Lingmiao.
- The Chief Recorder agrees to the introduction but is focused on getting himself transferred out of the Supervisory Heavenly Office, hinting that the danger they just survived was merely routine for him.
- Li Huowang and the Chief Recorder detect a hidden observer in the woods. Li Huowang mutilates himself to quickly disable the entity, only to find a gaunt, skeletal, barely human creature that seems mindless.
- Hong Da recognizes the creature in a sudden panic: it is a person that the *other* Heart-Turbid had hidden away. He realizes he himself has been eating dirt, confirming that he, and possibly the entire group, has been “hidden” inside some kind of fabricated reality.
- The horrifying truth emerges: Li Huowang’s memory was correct all along. There was a female Heart-Turbid at the start. The male Heart-Turbid they killed had hidden her *inside* his own corpse, while the living Heart-Turbid is hidden *inside* the dead one, creating an inescapable loop that traps everyone. The Chief Recorder collapses in despair, realizing they are all doomed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Heart-Turbid (心浊)** is the dark mirror of the Heart-Element. Where the Heart-Element can make fiction real, the Heart-Turbid can erase, hide, or seal things away—including concepts, objects, people, and even memories. In this chapter, we witness the truly terrifying application of this power: a dead Heart-Turbid used as a vessel to hide a living one, and a living one used to hide the dead. This creates a containment paradox that is nearly impossible to solve, as finding either one requires solving the other first. It's a folk-horror take on the classic logic puzzle brought to life with flesh and screaming.

**Karmic Obstacles (业障)** here refers to a layer of hidden reality crafted by the Heart-Turbid. In traditional Buddhism and folk religion, *yezhang* are obstructions caused by past misdeeds that cloud perception. The Heart-Turbid weaponizes this concept, literally tucking a living nightmare into a pocket dimension made of its own corpse. The physical act of Hong Da eating mud with no taste confirms that the group has been trapped in this fabricated layer, unable to perceive the actual world or the true threat.

**Military Path / Killing Intent (兵家煞气)** is a major theme this chapter. Bai Lingmiao’s corruption is framed as an invasion of *sha qi*—killing intent—from the Purple-Tasseled Sword. In Chinese martial and xianxia lore, weapons soaked in bloodshed develop a will of their own. The “killing intent” can corrupt a user’s personality, making them cruel, manic, and bloodthirsty. Li Huowang’s plan to consult a military cultivator mirrors real-world “exorcisms” for haunted weapons, though the method here suggests a need to bleed out or redirect the aggressive qi rather than a simple cleansing.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in “turning victory into ashes.” The triumph of the previous chapter is immediately undercut by Bai Lingmiao’s transformation. She doesn’t just sound different; she *is* different. Pay close attention to how her vocabulary and sentence structure mirror Peng Longteng’s pragmatic, even brutal clarity. This is a sign of the Artifact’s corrosion altering her soul, not just her mood. Li Huowang’s fear is not of her anger, but of her newfound, cold rationality.

The real horror of the chapter, however, lies in the hidden mechanics of the Heart-Turbid trap. The author uses a classic “diversion” structure: the group assumes the threat is over, the attention shifts to internal drama (Bai Lingmiao), and then the nature of the real enemy is revealed to be far more insidious. The phrase “It’s over” (完了) isn't just the title—it’s the collective epilogue of everyone present. Watch how the Chief Recorder’s cool falls away instantly. His collapse is a crucial signal to the reader: this isn’t a setback they can fight through. This is an extinction-level event for the group.
