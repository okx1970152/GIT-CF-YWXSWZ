## Chapter Overview

*And there it is.* The moment Li Huowang finally gets to turn the tables. After being mentally cornered, identity-swapped, and nearly erased by Layue Shiba's insidious cognitive tampering, our favorite (only?) Heart-Element patient finally figures out the one thing that piece-of-trash evil spirit *can't* handle: a mind that's already broken in two different directions at once. This chapter is pure dopamine for anyone who's been waiting for Li Huowang to stop being a victim and start being a hunter. He's not just surviving anymore—he's chasing. And he's *enjoying* it. The chase is on, and the red is running.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang deduces Layue Shiba's core weakness: it can only modify *one* cognitive framework at a time. As a Heart-Element, Li Huowang possesses **two**—his hallucinatory reality and his modern psychiatric reality—making him uniquely resistant.
- He realizes that if his identity had fully accepted death as the old man, he would have died for real, bypassing even Danyangzi's protection mechanism.
- Back in the hospital hallucination, Li Huowang exploits his familiarity with the psychiatric ward to escape: he fakes a bathroom request to get untied, then bolts the moment the nurse leaves to notify the doctors.
- Ignoring the "real" world around him (which he now firmly classifies as an illusion), he runs to the location of the Wu Family Courtyard—which in the hallucination is a busy road intersection.
- He shouts instructions into the traffic, directing Bai Lingmiao to ring the Daoist bell and summon the Wandering Lord to attack the fake Li Huowang. The car horns confirm she is acting—they are the bell sounds distorted by the hallucination.
- A red sports car driver yells at him, and Li Huowang senses a gaze—he identifies the red vehicle as Layue Shiba's physical manifestation within the hallucination.
- Before he can attack, the "red" focal point shifts to a distant red electric scooter. Layue Shiba has been injured and is trying to escape by shrinking and moving its awareness.
- Li Huowang gives chase, commanding the unseen disciples to pursue the fleeing red.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Layue Shiba: The Annual Reset Spirit** — This is one of the more creative folk-horror constructions in the novel. Layue Shiba (腊月十八) is named after the eighteenth day of the twelfth lunar month, suggesting a spirit whose nature, appearance, and even vulnerabilities *completely reset* every year on that date. Compare this to the legalistic rule-based horror in classical Chinese ghost stories, where knowing the spirit's name and its "taboo day" gives the protagonist leverage. The fact that it's a date-name implies it has been encountered before—and will be encountered again, entirely different.

**The Heart-Element's Cognitive Duality** — Li Huowang's triumph here is rooted in the novel's core ontological horror. As a Heart-Element (心素), he doesn't just "see things"—he *holds two contradictory realities in his head at once*. Layue Shiba's power is to replace a target's identity by overwriting their cognitive schema. But if the target already possesses two schemas, the spirit can only overwrite one, leaving the other intact as a lifeline. In xianxia terms, this is not a contest of cultivation realms—it's a contest of *perception structures*, and Li Huowang's illness is his strength.

**"Red" as a Supernatural Marker** — In Chinese folk religion and fengshui, red is traditionally a *yang* color associated with life, joy, and warding off evil (red envelopes, red wedding clothes, cinnabar talismans). Layue Shiba's manifestation as "red objects" (sports car, electric scooter) is an inversion: the protective color becomes the signal of the predator. In the Dao-Twisted World, no symbol is safe from corruption.

## Reading Guide

If you've been gritting your teeth through the last few chapters of Li Huowang getting his identity scrambled like a bad hard drive, this is your pay-off. The pacing here is *exhilarating*—note the contrast between the cold, deliberate calculation in the hospital room and the manic, almost feral energy of the street chase. Li Huowang's confidence is back, but it's not the brittle, fearful confidence of earlier chapters. This is the confidence of a man who has *understood the rules* and is now exploiting them. The hospital hallucination is no longer a prison—it's a command center. And that grin? That bared-teeth, "I found you" moment? That's Li Huowang discovering that hunting feels a lot better than being hunted. Watch the red. It's running.
