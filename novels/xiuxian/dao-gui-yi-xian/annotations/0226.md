## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow travelers—we finally reach a milestone that feels almost too good to be true. After weeks of blood, pain, and the constant fear of being hunted or consumed, Li Huowang's ragged group crosses the final ridge and delivers Sun Baolu home to his Qingqiu tribe. This isn't just a rest stop; it's an emotional avalanche. The chapter wrings every drop of warmth from the reunion—a mother’s milk pail hitting the dirt, a lover’s embrace, a feast of roasted lamb and ant honey—before quietly reminding us that Li Huowang still has a half-healed wound, a paranoid mind, and a heart that doesn't quite know how to process kindness. This is the *afterglow* of survival, fragile and bittersweet.

## Key Plot Points
- Bai Lingmiao and Chun Xiaoman share an intimate bathing scene and a bed-talk that reveals both Bai Lingmiao’s shyness and Chun Xiaoman’s blunt, caring nature.
- Li Huowang wakes from another nightmare, checks the horizon for danger one more time, and cautiously decides they are safe from the Lion Dance Palace apprentices.
- Sun Baolu’s excitement grows as he recognizes landmarks: a slope where he broke a tooth, a field where he saw a white wolf, tent-marks in the grass.
- The group crests a hill and sees the Qingqiu encampment with sheep, cattle, and tiny figures moving among the tents.
- Sun Baolu runs ahead, sobbing. A Qingqiu woman drops her milk pail at the sight of him; they embrace in a raw, tearful reunion that draws the entire tribe’s attention.
- An elder with a ceremonial necklace calms the crowd. Sun Baolu introduces Li Huowang to his people, and a feast erupts around the fire.
- Li Huowang samples “ant honey” and is approached by a fair-skinned woman—Sun Baolu’s mother—who bows to him and thanks him formally for bringing her son home.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Ant Honey (蚁蜜)** – This isn't just poetic license. Certain ant species in Asia, like the weaver ant (*Oecophylla smaragdina*), produce a sugary substance stored in their bloated abdomens, which is harvested as a delicacy in some rural communities. The novel leans into the exotic and rare, contrasting the sweet, cloying honey of bees with a sharper, purer sweetness from the insect world—a small but vivid detail that grounds the fantasy in believable, lived-in folk knowledge.

**Qingqiu Culture** – The Qingqiu are depicted as a nomadic, livestock-herding people with their own language and distinct customs. Their geographic displacement, observable physical differences (slightly darker skin, distinctive clothing), and the presence of a tribal elder with a ceremonial necklace all mark them as a culturally distinct group within the Dao-Twisted World. The author uses neutral but alienating details to reinforce Li Huowang’s persistent outsider status, even in moments of welcome. The tribe’s warm, noisy, physical reunion is a stark contrast to the cold, transactional bonds Li Huowang has survived by elsewhere.

**Corpse Solution Immortal (尸解仙)** – A corrupted Daoist concept briefly alluded to in earlier chapters. In orthodox Daoist mythology, "Corpse Solution" (*shijie*) describes a method of transcendence where an adept sheds their mortal body like a cicada shell. The novel inverts this: decay, rot, and putrefaction become the path to immortality, not liberation from the flesh. This is a classic example of the book weaponizing familiar religious vocabulary against the reader.

## Reading Guide
Buckle up, because *this* is the emotional payoff you’ve been waiting for. After all the sacrifices, lies, and literal skin-of-the-teeth escapes, we finally get to see something *good* happen. Sun Baolu, the excitable kid with the sheep, is home. His mother’s tearful prostration before Li Huowang is a rare moment of unironic gratitude in a world that usually punishes kindness.

But watch Li Huowang. He doesn't join the feast. He watches. He sips the honey drink. He accepts the thanks with quiet, guarded composure. Li Huowang is learning to accept warmth, but he hasn’t forgotten what he carries inside him. The chapter’s closing image—a mother bowing to her son’s savior—is beautiful, but it’s also a reminder: this man walked through hell to get here, and he knows he’s bringing hell back with him.

This is the calm before whatever storm comes next. Treasure it.
