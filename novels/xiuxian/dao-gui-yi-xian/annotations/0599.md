## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang has been transferred to Kangning Hospital, a new, shiny cage. The easygoing Dr. Wu Cheng from the morning pulls a 180-degree turn, initiating an aggressive “talk therapy” session designed to re-trigger Li Huowang’s hallucinations of the Dao-Twisted World. Li Huowang barely resists, but his refusal to cooperate causes Wu Cheng to threaten indefinite hospitalization. The situation escalates: Li Huowang is put under higher surveillance, and a new, suspicious red pill is added to his medication. Suspecting a conspiracy linking Wu Cheng to the treacherous Wang Wei, Li Huowang feels the walls closing in, ending with orderlies storming his room after he secretly dumps the new drug.

## Key Plot Points
This chapter is all about the psychological siege tightening in the modern world. Key beats:
- **Wu Cheng’s Betrayal**: After seeming agreeable, Wu Cheng launches a destabilization test, trying to force Li Huowang’s mind back into the Dao-Twisted World, contradicting Yi Donglai’s gentler approach.
- **The Refusal & Isolation**: Li Huowang puts his foot down, demanding to call Yi Donglai. His request is denied, and the rules of the institution are weaponized against him.
- **Paranoia Confirmed**: Li Huowang’s already frayed trust shatters. He correctly deduces Wu Cheng may be in league with the enigmatic Wang Wei, making the hospital itself a battlefield.
- **Escalation of Control**: He’s put on higher alert (orderlies watching him), and a new, unknown red capsule is introduced to his regimen.
- **The Gambit**: Li Huowang secretly flushes the new pills, but his defiance is discovered, leading to a brutal, confrontational ending where orderlies physically demand he take his medication.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Hospital Hierarchy as a Prison System**: For Western readers, it’s crucial to understand that in Chinese mental health institutions, the doctor’s authority is nearly absolute. When Wu Cheng uses “rules are rules” to block communication, it’s not just petty bureaucracy—it’s a clinical form of confinement. The power dynamic is brutally one-sided.
- **The “Evaluation” Trap**: The “evaluation” Wu Cheng threatens is a real, high-stakes procedure. A patient’s discharge relies entirely on a subjective psychiatric assessment. Failing it—especially by showing agitation—can lead to a permanent stay. Li Huowang isn’t just fighting a delusion; he’s fighting a system.
- **Pharmaceutical Warfare**: The mysterious red capsule is a classic trope in Chinese psychological thrillers. It represents the ultimate loss of control: the patient doesn’t know what’s being put into their body, and “just taking it” is framed as an act of submission, not healing. The fear here is visceral and real, not supernatural.

## Reading Guide
Welcome to the *other* horror of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*: the sterile, bureaucratic horror of the mental health system. Forget the flesh Buddhas for a moment; this chapter proves that a locked ward with a bad therapist can be just as terrifying as a demon. The tension here is masterfully built through small details: the tone of the door opening, the way the orderlies stare, the number of pills in the cup. Li Huowang’s paranoia isn’t just justified—it’s his survival instinct screaming at him. What’s truly chilling is the question the chapter leaves us with: *Is Wu Cheng a traitor working for a grand conspiracy, or is he just a doctor who genuinely believes Li Huowang is still sick?* Either answer is terrifying.

## Psychological Horror Breakdown
This chapter is a masterclass in *institutional horror*. The “villain” isn't a monster; it's a process. The new hospital, “Health and Peace,” is a white-walled trap. The isolation from allies, the feeling of being watched, the unexplained new medication—these are tools of control that feel authentic to real-world psychiatric settings. Li Huowang’s fear isn’t of a Vision but of being chemically subdued and losing his agency. The most frightening moment is when he realizes the gaze of the orderlies has shifted from passive observation to active surveillance. The chapter asks a bleak question: How do you fight a system that has the power to define your sanity?
