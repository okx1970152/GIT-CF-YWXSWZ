## Chapter Overview

After the frantic, soul-burning climax of the last arc, this chapter pulls us into a space of stark, unsettling calm. We follow Li Huowang through the clinical, bureaucratic process of admission into a new psychiatric hospital. It’s a masterclass in quiet horror. There’s no supernatural entity here, no ritual circle drawn in blood. Instead, the dread comes from the mundane: a mother's anxious love, the cold click of handcuffs, a circle of patients sharing their diagnoses. But this quiet is a trap. The chapter ends with a chilling, mundane *wrongness* as a fellow patient recognizes Li Huowang from a horrific news report, shattering the fragile illusion of a fresh start and reminding us that his past isn't something he can simply check at the door.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is admitted to Kangning Hospital, a private psychiatric institution, after his mother is convinced to leave him by himself.
- He undergoes a routine full-body search and intake process, surrendering control to the hospital’s system.
- The chapter details a group therapy-like session where patients share their diagnoses (schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, depression), creating a mundane yet poignant atmosphere.
- Li Huowang, asserting he is cured, refuses to participate earnestly, causing a stir among the other patients.
- A shocking reveal occurs when a depressed girl identifies Li Huowang as a public figure from a school massacre, ending the chapter on a note of chilling recognition.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Kangning Hospital (康宁医院):** The name "Kangning" literally means "Health and Peace." This is a significant, ironic choice. It represents Li Huowang’s hope for a peaceful, normal life. However, given his track record, this "peace" feels less like a sanctuary and more like a well-decorated cage. This chapter also hilariously deconstructs the Xianxia trope where his "notorious reputation" would be a source of power or fear. Here, his fame from the "Danyangzi incident" only gets him a private room, a very modern, bureaucratic perk.

**The "Psychiatric Ward" Setting:** This chapter is unique as it is set entirely in our modern reality. In the world of Xianxia and folk horror, a psychiatric hospital is the ultimate mundane horror. It’s the place where your cosmic truths are diagnosed as "delusions" and your "cultivation" is treated with pills and therapy. The terrifying, lawless Dao-Twisted World is replaced by a prison of order, routine, and clinical labels, which for Li Huowang, might be its own form of hell.

**"Fellow Daoist" in the Ward:** The term "classmate" (同学, *tóngxué*) used by the nurse’s aide and the doctor is a fascinating cultural detail. In China, it's common to call strangers "classmate" as a friendly, neutral term. Here, it creates a jarring sense of parody. This is not a sect or a school; it's a mental ward. The word feels almost sarcastic, highlighting the gulf between the world Li Huowang has left and this new, "normal" reality he is trying to inhabit.

## Reading Guide

Welcome to the calm before the storm, fellow Daoists! Or is it the storm pretending to be calm? After the explosive body-horror of the previous chapters, get ready for a slow-burn psychological descent. This chapter is all about *mood* and *atmosphere*. Pay attention to the details: the sterile hallways, the routine questions, the casual way patients describe their own broken minds. The true horror here isn't a monster, but the terrifying ordinariness of Li Huowang’s new life. He’s trapped in a system that has a label for everything he’s been through, but can never understand it. And just when you start to feel that strange, almost boring sense of safety—BAM. A single line from a depressed girl brings the entire weight of his past crashing back down, reminding us all that you can’t outrun a reputation carved in blood and fire. This chapter is a masterful reset, dropping us into a new, suffocating setting to build tension for what comes next.
