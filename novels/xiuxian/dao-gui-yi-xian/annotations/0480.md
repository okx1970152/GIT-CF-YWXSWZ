## Chapter Overview

Alright, grab your tea, fellow horror-reader, because Chapter 479, “Counter,” is a masterclass in *nope*. Li Huowang, fresh off sacrificing his skin to old Ba-she, goes full flaming top-tier-boss on the Spirit-Blight. But this isn’t your average monster-hunt. The Blight proves itself a cunning, slippery little gremlin, possessing fire, then hijacking Li Huowang’s own body in a meltdown scene that makes “body horror” feel like an understatement. It’s a desperate, scrappy fight where our boy uses his own chopped-off lower half as a trap. The high-stakes, tactical, and deeply grotesque nature of the conflict is the main course.

## Key Plot Points

Li Huowang ignites himself to burn the nine-colored stag (Spirit-Blight). He searches the charred remains but finds it’s escaped. He sets the entire peach forest ablaze to smoke it out, but still no luck. The Blight, having possessed the fire itself, enters his bloodstream through an open wound. Li Huowang’s body begins to dissolve and melt—a horrifying process where every sensation is felt—as the entity’s pure malice tries to overwrite his consciousness. Desperate, he uses his ‘Cultivate the False into Reality’ ability to forcibly restore his body and runs. He then enlists Li Sui, who engages in a two-on-one flesh-wrestling match against the entity inside his body. The Blight is expelled but remains invisible. Unable to perceive it because he carries the ‘Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings,’ Li Huowang tracks its footprints. With a plan, he lures the invisible entity into what it thinks is his fleshy leg, but which is actually a disembodied lower half he’s using as bait.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter leans hard on the concept of **Spirit-Blight (灵孽 / Língniè)** . This isn’t your typical ghost or demon. In the Dao-Twisted World, a Spirit-Blight is less a creature and more an entity born from pure, concentrated sin or negative karma. It has no physical body of its own and is utterly invisible to anyone who still carries the **Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings (十情八苦)** —in other words, anyone who is still a living, feeling human being. This makes it a uniquely horrifying Xianxia-adjacent threat, bypassing normal sight and requiring sensory or deductive workarounds. Also notable is Li Huowang’s use of **Bashe’s flesh-sacrifice** without his skin as a tactical choice, a reminder that power in this world always has a visceral, physical price.

Another fascinating detail is Li Sui’s combat style. She literally disassembles her physical form into a mass of tentacles and flesh to engage in a grapple with the entity. This leans into the Black Tai Sui’s nature as a parasitic entity built for possession and reshaping, turning her into a monstrous, adaptable weapon rather than a simple fighter.

### Cultivate the False into Reality (修假为真)

Li Huowang’s ability to make his own “lies” or false reality temporarily real. He uses it here to forcibly restore his dissolving body, fighting off possession with sheer disbelief.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a classic “hero fights an invisible, unbeatable foe and wins through an unexpected trick” moment. Watch how Li Huowang’s tactic evolves from brute-force fire to baiting with his own severed body part. It’s gross but brilliant. Also pay attention to the two-on-one dynamic with Li Sui; their relationship is now a tactical partnership, not just a ‘parent and child’ deal. The real gem here is the whispered lore about the Spirit-Blight’s invisibility—it’s a reminder that in this world, your very humanity can be a handicap. And if you’re feeling a bit peckish, maybe don’t read the part about mushy, dissolving flesh while eating.
