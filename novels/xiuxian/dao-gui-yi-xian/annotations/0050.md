## Chapter Overview
Holy mother of *something*—the Flesh Buddhas are out of the bag, and they are *not* here for enlightenment. This chapter is a brutal, visceral turning point where Li Huowang’s desperate bid for power collides head-on with the systemic horror of Zhengde Temple. Gone are the cryptic riddles and veiled threats; the monks drop the pious act and reveal their true form: seven towering, incense-studded, flesh-and-blood abominations that transform into even more nightmarish “Thousand-Armed” entities. This isn’t a debate or a negotiation. It’s a straight-up supernatural showdown in a cold, dark forest, with Li Huowang’s last real weapon—the Wandering Lord—pitted against a sutra-chanting wall of corrupted Buddhist power. The chapter masterfully escalates from psychological dread to full-blown body horror, and the price tag for failure just got a lot higher. Get ready for a chapter that redefines what “monstrous” means in this already twisted world.

## Key Plot Points
- **Cold Open in the Dark**: Li Huowang is alone in a thicket, suppressing a fire and taking stock of his meager resources. The last of Danyangzi’s pills are gone; the Daoist bell is his only card.
- **A Vow of Desperation**: In a moment of raw, snarling internal monologue, Li Huowang resolves to become stronger by any means necessary—beg, borrow, steal, or kill. He literally touches his own neck, a nod to his earlier suspicions about Danyangzi’s lingering influence.
- **The Chanting in the Dark**: Ethereal sutra chanting shatters the night. The monks are here, and they are closing in. Li Huowang orders a chaotic retreat, telling the Fool to carry Bai Lingmiao and instructing everyone to scatter.
- **The Reveal of the Flesh Buddhas**: As Li Huowang runs, he is cornered on a dark road by seven massive entities. They appear as giant, potbellied Buddhas with incense sticks stuck directly into their flesh. Their leader has the face of Jian Dun.
- **The Transformation**: The pretense is dropped. With a sickening sound, the Buddhas sprout dozens of skinless, fleshy arms from their backs, becoming the “Thousand-Armed Flesh Buddhas.”
- **The Battle of the Bell and the Sutra**: Li Huowang summons the Wandering Lord, unleashing its reality-warping chaos. The Buddhas prove immovable at first, but the Wandering Lord is able to possess and destroy two of them.
- **The Corporealization and the Kill**: The remaining Buddhas’ chanting is so powerful it forces the Wandering Lord into a physical, fleshy form—a writhing, tangled mass. This newly-solidified entity is immediately crushed into paste by a giant palm.
- **The Final Stand**: With its weapon obliterated, Li Huowang is left facing the remaining five, monstrous Thousand-Armed Buddhas. They offer a final, hollow “invitation” to return to the temple.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Thousand-Armed Flesh Buddha (千手肉佛)**: This is a direct, grotesque perversion of the Buddhist *Guanyin* (Avalokiteśvara), who is often depicted with a thousand arms as a symbol of limitless compassion. Here, that holy iconography is twisted into a horror of raw, parasitical flesh. The 'arms' are skinless and sprout from a corrupted form, a visual metaphor for the temple's worship of “the Flesh Buddha” from earlier chapters.
- **The Power of Chanting**: In Buddhist tradition (and Daoist ritual), chanting sutras is a meritorious act that can purify, protect, and generate spiritual power. This chapter literalizes and weaponizes that concept: the monks’ chanting is a direct counter to the Wandering Lord’s hallucinatory, reality-bending ability. It “locks in” reality and forces the intangible into a physical form, showing how institutionalized, collective faith can dominate a powerful but disorganized entity like the Wandering Lord.
- **Swastika (卍) on the Palm**: This is the ancient *manji* symbol, a traditional auspicious sign in Buddhism often found on the chest or palms of deities. Corrupting this symbol onto the palm of a flesh monster emphasizes the complete inversion of sacred imagery that defines the Dao-Twisted World.

## Reading Guide
Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up. This chapter is a *massive* step up in the horror scale. The monks of Zhengde Temple just revealed their final form, and it’s not a joke. Pay very close attention to the cost of Li Huowang’s actions. His transformation from a confused victim to a determined, ruthless survivor is fully underway. That internal vow he makes? It’s a pact with himself, and this world *loves* to make people pay for their oaths.

Also, note the symbolism of the incense. Those aren’t just decorations; they look like offerings *embedded into* the entity. It’s a powerful image of how worship can sustain a monster. And the Wandering Lord’s fate is a critical lesson: even the most useful tool can be overwhelmed. Li Huowang just lost his biggest advantage. What’s a schemer to do when his only ace is turned to mush? The answer is chilling.
