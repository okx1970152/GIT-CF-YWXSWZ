## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a masterclass in paranoia. Li Huowang and Blind Chen put their heads together to deal with the mud-devils of Gan Yuan Village, and our boy Huowang even busts out the Daoist bell and the Wandering Lord to flush them out. But just when you think the plan is working, Chen drops the mother of all bombshells: he never called for backup. The person standing right next to him? He’s convinced it’s a Zuowandao trickster wearing Li Huowang’s face. Get ready, because the walls of trust are about to come crashing down.

## Key Plot Points
- Blind Chen recounts his previous encounter with the village’s evil spirits: three faceless entities crawled from the mud, imitated human speech, and asked him to read their bones. He killed one and wounded another.
- The villagers, far from grateful, chased him out with farming tools—Blind Chen concludes they’ve been “possessed” by the spirits.
- Li Huowang devises a plan to use the Wandering Lord to force the mud-devils out of hiding, using a misdirection technique to lure away a group of four heavily armed outsiders first.
- The two approach the Mud Bodhisattva Temple, and Li Huowang unleashes the Daoist bell, splitting the Wandering Lord into eight entities.
- In a stunning reversal, Blind Chen tries to assassinate Li Huowang mid-ritual, revealing that he never actually called for reinforcements—and that he suspects Li Huowang is a Zuowandao agent.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a brilliant example of how *Dao of the Bizarre Immortal* weaponizes the reader’s own trust. The entire sequence is built on the assumption that Li Huowang is telling the truth, but Blind Chen’s final accusation flips the script completely. Zuowandao (坐忘道) is literally the “Way of Sitting in Forgetfulness”—a perversion of a Daoist meditative concept where one “forgets” the self. But here, “forgetting” means erasing boundaries between truth and lies, friend and foe. The horror isn’t in a monster; it’s in the total collapse of reliability. You can’t trust what you see, what you hear, or even who you’re standing next to.

Also worth noting: the Wandering Lord (游老爷) is summoned through the Daoist bell, which traditionally calls upon spirits for exorcism or aid. Li Huowang’s use of mud as a medium (literally eating it to speak the spirit’s language) echoes shamanic practices where earthly substances serve as bridges between worlds. It’s gritty, folk-horror magic—no fancy incantations, just dirt and willpower.

## Reading Guide
This is a pivotal chapter for genre-savvy readers. Up until now, we’ve mostly seen Li Huowang as the unreliable point-of-view character, questioning his own sanity. But here, for the first time, *another* character accuses *him* of being the unreliable one. Blind Chen’s logic is sound—if he never sent for reinforcements, then the person claiming to be Li Huowang must be a fake. The twist forces us to ask: what if our perspective has been compromised all along? Pay close attention to Li Huowang’s off-screen conversation with the outsiders—could he have been replaced during that brief moment? Or is Blind Chen simply too paranoid to see the truth? Either way, welcome to the Zuowandao nightmare, where no one is who they say they are.
