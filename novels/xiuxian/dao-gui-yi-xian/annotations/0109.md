## Chapter Overview

“You said I couldn’t do anything, didn’t you?” This chapter is the final, bloody showdown of the Danyangzi arc—and the moment Li Huowang proves that the most dangerous weapon in the Dao-Twisted World isn't a talisman or a mantra, but a man who’s willing to disembowel himself to win an argument. The confrontation erupts when Danyangzi reveals he is *still alive*, physically living inside Li Huowang’s belly alongside the Black Tai Sui. What follows is a chaotic, visceral battle where nuns turn into fly-Bodhisattvas, tentacles drag Li Huowang around like a puppet, and Danyangzi spits "immortal truths" about having seen the Southern Heavenly Gate. But the real punch comes at the end: Li Huowang, locked in a battle of wills and trapped in a stalemate, reaches inside his own gut, yanks out his own stomach, and stabs it, screaming that this isn't over. It’s not a victory. It’s a declaration of war—against Danyangzi, against destiny, and against the very idea that anything in this world is pre-ordained.

## Key Plot Points

- **Danyangzi’s Survival Confirmed**: Li Huowang learns for the first time that Danyangzi didn't just corrupt him spiritually—he has been physically alive inside his body all along. The tentacles emerging from Li Huowang’s mouth are Danyangzi’s organs, twisted into weapons.
- **The Nuns’ Counterattack**: Jingxin and her sisters attempt to suppress Danyangzi through their Clear Mind link, but fail because Danyangzi is inside Li Huowang, blocking the connection. They resort to summoning the fly-Bodhisattva and trapping Danyangzi in a pile of rotting flesh.
- **Danyangzi’s “Truth”**: When Li Huowang taunts Danyangzi by revealing that the ascension method was fake and that the man has been twisted into a monster by evil spirit flesh, Danyangzi does not panic. Instead, he claims he *has* become a half-immortal and has seen the Southern Heavenly Gate and its celestial inhabitants.
- **Li Huowang’s Desperate Gambit**: Disarmed of the *Thousand Greats Record* and cornered, Li Huowang chooses self-mutilation over submission. He stabs himself, pulls out his own stomach, and stabs it repeatedly, showing he would rather destroy a part of himself than let Danyangzi use it.
- **The Stalemate Breaks**: Danyangzi is trapped by the nuns’ Bodhisattva-flesh, and Li Huowang is bleeding out. The chapter ends on a cliffhanger of mutual hatred and an unresolved fight.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Severing the Three Corpses (斩三尸)**: In Daoist cultivation lore, the "Three Corpses" (or Three Worms) are malevolent entities that live inside the human body, craving decay and tempting the cultivator toward death. A common xianxia trope involves "severing" them as a step toward immortality. Here, Danyangzi twists this concept into a parasitic cohabitation, using it to justify his continued existence inside Li Huowang. It’s a brilliant corruption of a respectable cultivation concept into a body-horror nightmare.

**The Southern Heavenly Gate (南天门)**: In Chinese mythology, the Southern Heavenly Gate is the main entrance to the Heavenly Court, guarded by the Four Heavenly Kings. Mortals who see it are usually either immortals or about to die. Danyangzi’s claim to have seen it—and to have seen other “gods” inside—is the novel’s most direct hint that the Dao-Twisted World’s version of ascension is a horrific perversion of the traditional myth.

**Self-Surgery as Resistance**: Li Huowang’s act of pulling out his own stomach is not just shock value—it is a tactical, ritualistic self-harm that mirrors real-world folk practices of “washing the stomach” or “cleansing the body” in Daoist and Buddhist ascetic traditions. In the Dao-Twisted World, where the body is currency, destroying part of yourself is the ultimate act of defiance.

## Reading Guide

*Get ready, folks—this is the chapter where Li Huowang stops being a passenger in his own body and starts acting like a man with nothing left to lose.* The horror here isn't the tentacles or the fly-Bodhisattva (though those are nasty). The horror is Danyangzi’s casual, smug confidence. He calls Li Huowang a “stray dog” he can kick to death. And Li Huowang’s response? He pulls out his own stomach. It’s not a power-up; it’s a **cry of defiance written in viscera**.

If you've been waiting for Li Huowang to take control, this is the chapter. But don’t expect a clean win. This is the kind of victory that leaves you needing stitches—and a lot of questions. Is Danyangzi really a half-immortal? Is the Southern Heavenly Gate real? And what happens when Li Huowang runs out of body parts to sacrifice?

Buckle up. The price of rejecting destiny just went up.
