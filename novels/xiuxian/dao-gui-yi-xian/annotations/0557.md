## Chapter Overview
This chapter breaks the action wide open. The "negotiation" with the Supreme Extremity Confluence turns out to be a full-on supernatural heist staged by the Supervisory Heavenly Office. Shangguan Yuting gets forcibly upgraded into a living archive of stolen secrets, and Zhuge Yuan drops a knowledge bomb about a terrifying new faction: the Unspeakable. Li Huowang, now in possession of the official's proxy, catches his breath, grabs a horse, and sets his sights on the capital—and on Dice.

## Key Plot Points
- Shangguan Yuting's earlier confession about being "traded" is revealed as a cover story; the real process involves direct, violent seizure.
- The Supreme Extremity Confluence manifests as a funnel of living written characters attempting to flee, but the office official's mirror-sky and staring-shadow trap it completely.
- Shangguan Yuting transforms into a grotesque vessel: her face is replaced by a mass of live eyes, confirming she now carries the stolen secrets.
- Zhuge Yuan identifies the official's likely sect as "the Unspeakable"—an entity born from the void that cannot be named, seen, or recorded by mortals.
- The chapter ends on a logistical beat: Li Huowang secures provisions (a horse, in exchange for the jade pendant from his Qingfeng Temple days) and pushes toward the capital, his body held together by Li Sui.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Unspeakable (说不得)**: Zhuge Yuan explicitly states this being's name cannot be spoken by humans or written down. This taps into a deep xianxia trope—and real Daoist taboo—where certain divine names or True Names carry power. To utter them is to invoke or bind. The fact that the Unspeakable "was born from the void, dwells in the void, and dies in the void" aligns it with primordial chaos (Hundun) mythology, making it less a god with a personality and more a principle of oblivion that happens to have will. Mortals can only glimpse it at the edges of death or extreme grief—a classic horror framing.

**Shadow eyes and mirror skies**: The official's technique uses a shadow that becomes "a mass of countless eyes," reminiscent of the *Shan Hai Jing*'s omniscient monsters, but weaponized here as a capture net. The mirror-sky inverts the earth, canceling any dimensional escape. This is a high-end application of Daoist wuji (the limitless) and xiangke (counteraction) logic: the void traps the void.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a lore bomb that recontextualizes everything. The Unspeakable isn't just another monster—it's a faction that cannot be discussed, recorded, or named. That's the kind of meta-horror *Dao Gui Yi Xian* does best. This also sets up a fascinating parallel: Li Huowang has been dealing with entities that *refuse* to fit into language, and now he's about to work *for* one. The visual of Shangguan Yuting's face becoming a lattice of living eyes is pure body horror, but it's also a narrative signal: she's no longer a person; she's a storage medium. And Li Huowang's casual trade of the jade pendant—that last relic from his disciple days—for a horse is a quiet marker of how far he's come. He's burning every bridge, one artifact at a time.
