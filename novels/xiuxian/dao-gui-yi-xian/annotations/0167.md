## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang reaches the final, ultimate gambit of his existence. With Danyangzi now puppeting his body in a full-blown war against the five Zuowandao elders, Li realizes he has one last card to play—not to win, but to ensure that after the fighting is over, there is no "him" left for Danyangzi to take back. This is not a fight for victory; it is a fight to give himself a death no one can steal. The chapter is a single, breathless descent into self-annihilation, where every wound is a word in a tragic language only Bashe can read. No turning back, no clever escape—just a boy deciding that the only way to save himself from being a monster is to stop being anything at all.

## Key Plot Points
- The "Sorrow-Body" elder attacks Li Huowang with rusty nails, but the possessed Li—now fully Danyangzi—dodges easily and begins slaughtering Ao-Jing followers with contemptuous ease.
- The Zuowandao elders recognize Danyangzi's possession and counter with a Joy Sutra, which forces Danyangzi to laugh uncontrollably until his features start to loosen. Danyangzi retaliates by conjuring black mist to muffle the chant.
- Li Huowang, observing the stalemate from within his own body, decides to escalate. He wants both sides to destroy each other—but realizes he needs to ensure that even if Danyangzi wins, there will be no host body left for him.
- Li Huowang stabs himself in the chest, then slices open his abdomen in a crude self-surgery. The amplified pain is almost unbearable, but he forces himself to stay conscious.
- He reaches into his own wound and pulls open his cavity, exposing his internal organs to the air. He then begins a ritual from the *Thousand Greats Record*: the Intercalating of the Five Phases.
- One by one, Li Huowang offers his Liver (Wood), Lungs (Metal), Spleen (Earth), and Kidneys (Water) to Bashe, with root tendrils from the *Record* sustaining them. Ao-Jing followers try to intervene but are blocked by the flying bamboo slips.
- Finally, he offers his Heart (Fire), completing the sacrifice. His body becomes an empty shell, and he collapses.
- The ritual is not an ascension but a replacement: the flesh-worms that formed the *Thousand Greats Record* swarm into his hollow cavity, filling him completely. He stands back up, now filled with writhing worms, no longer in pain, but unable to tell where the worms end and he begins.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Intercalating the Five Phases (置闰五行):** The phrase "置闰" (zhì rùn) comes from the Chinese calendar system—an intercalary month inserted to realign the lunar and solar years. Here, it is grotesquely repurposed into a ritual that "re-aligns" the body's Five Phases by replacing the organs with parasites. The ritual is not a promotion but a substitution: the body is kept "functional," but its original nature is overwritten by the *Thousand Greats Record*. This is the Dao-Twisted World's ultimate logic: nothing is gained without something else being eaten from the inside.

**The Five Viscera (五脏) and Five Phases (五行):** Each organ is linked to a phase: Liver (Wood), Heart (Fire), Spleen (Earth), Lungs (Metal), Kidneys (Water). Traditional Chinese medicine views them as interdependent systems of vitality. Sacrificing them one by one is not just physical destruction—it is the systematic dismantling of the self's foundational structure, leaving only the worms to "play the part." The fact that Li Huowang feels "no pain" at the end is not a blessing; it signals that the self that could feel pain is already gone.

**"Joy to the point of loosening the features":** The Zuowandao's counter-chant targets the body's structural integrity through extreme emotional force. This reflects a Daoist idea that excessive emotion can damage the organs and unseat the spirit. The elders weaponize this principle: they force Danyangzi's body to "laugh itself apart," forcing the spirit to either lose control of its host or dissipate. Danyangzi's black mist is his countermeasure—smothering the sound vibration that drives the attack.

## Reading Guide
This is not a fight scene; it is a suicide note written in blood and bone. Li Huowang's actions are cold, methodical, and absolutely final. Readers should pay attention to the shift in his internal tone from desperation to a kind of grim satisfaction—he is not collapsing into despair but *succeeding* in his plan. The horror of this chapter is not that he is dying, but that he is completing a transaction. When the worms fill his body, pay attention to the ambiguity: he can still think, still stand, still act—but he is no longer "Li Huowang" in any sense that matters. The disappearance of pain is the first sign that the self has been hollowed out and refilled with something *else*. If you're looking for a heroic last stand, you won't find it here. What you'll find is a boy deciding that the only way to stop being a puppet is to become a stage.
