## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up—this one is a *ride*. Chapter 462, “The Lotus Seat,” starts with Li Huowang fighting a desperate two-front war against the Heavenly Calamity: outside the walls, a swarm of grinning, corpse-brick zombies; inside his own people, the maddening *possession* of Bai Lingmiao and the Second Spirit by rampaging Immortals. Li Huowang’s solution—pasting his own face onto theirs as a Zuowandao trick— buys a few seconds at best. But then Zhuge Yuan drops the real game-changer: a twelve-grade Merit Jade Lotus, a relic that can apparently *cleanse* the Immortals out. The problem? It nearly wipes the patient out of existence along with the parasite. This chapter is a masterclass in triage horror: when you have a cure that’s almost as lethal as the disease, and the clock is ticking on an apocalypse.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang’s temporary fix—pasting his own face onto the possessed Second Spirit and Bai Lingmiao—fails as the masks degrade and the madness resurfaces.
- Zhuge Yuan diagnoses the root of the Heavenly Calamity: the Immortals (仙家) are revealed to be fundamentally “evil entities” (邪祟), and their simultaneous corruption with the external surge proves the calamity is entity-driven.
- Desperate for intel, Li Huowang sends a sparrow with a letter to Liu Zongyuan, seeking answers from the Supervisory Heavenly Office.
- A new wave of enemies appears: bodies folded over like “flesh bricks,” all grinning, forcing a three-front fight with Li Huowang, Bai Lingmiao, and the Second Spirit holding the walls.
- The Second Spirit breaks free and flees into the Bai family ancestral hall, revealing a hidden White Lotus sanctum.
- In a final, tearful confession, the Second Spirit reveals the Immortals’ true goal: to use their bodies as vessels to descend into the mortal world.
- She passes on the White Lotus’s “Possessed Medium” (神打乩童) powers to Li Huowang as a posthumous inheritance.
- Zhuge Yuan directs Li Huowang to use the twelve-grade Merit Jade Lotus, a relic that successfully purges the Immortals but nearly dissipates the patient themselves.
- The process is successful for both the Second Spirit and Bai Lingmiao, with the malignant qi being the final thing to leave Lingmiao’s heart.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The White Lotus (白莲教) & Wusheng Laomu**: The Bai family ancestral hall has been hiding a White Lotus sanctum all along. Historically, the White Lotus was not a single cult but a long-lived, syncretic secret society tradition, often built around the worship of the “Unborn Venerable Mother” (無生老母). This novel weaponizes that history: the sect’s powers are real, hereditary, and terrifyingly effective.

**Possessed Medium (神打乩童 / Ji Tong)**: This is a real, extreme folk practice where a spirit medium invites a deity into their body, often through self-mutilation (whipping, cutting) to prove the spirit’s power. In *Dao Gui*, this tradition is literalized into a combat-relevant “power inheritance” that the Second Spirit can pass on.

**The Twelve-Grade Merit Jade Lotus (十二品功德玉莲)**: This is likely a nod to the twelve-grade lotus pedestals in Buddhist cosmology, which represent levels of enlightenment and spiritual purity. In the Dao-Twisted World, it functions as a double-edged exorcism tool: it can purge entities, but treat the “self” as a removable impurity too.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for emotional whiplash. The chapter title “The Lotus Seat” sounds peaceful, but the content is a frantic, ugly battle against corruption. The real punch to the gut comes from the Second Spirit’s final speech: she’s *laughing* while confessing their impending death, crying while passing on her powers. This is a core theme of the novel—moments of profound human connection are forged in the heat of absolute horror. Li Huowang’s solution—using a face as a band-aid—is as broken and desperate as the world itself. Pay close attention to Zhuge Yuan’s diagnosis: he’s not just a scholar; he’s the only one who can see the big picture while everyone else is panicking.

## Deep Dive: The Lotus & the White Lotus Tradition
The “twelve-grade Merit Jade Lotus” is a brilliant piece of worldbuilding. “Jiupin” (Nine Grades) lotus pedestals appear in Buddhist sutras as a measure of a bodhisattva’s spiritual rank. By upping it to *twelve* grades, the novel implies a degree of purity beyond standard Buddhist cosmology—perfect for a heretical sect like the White Lotus. But the horror twist? In the Dao-Twisted World, purity is indistinguishable from erasure. The lotus doesn’t simply drive out the evil; it threatens to drive out the possessed person’s *existence* itself. This weaponizes the very concept of salvation: being “saved” might mean being *deleted*.
