## Chapter Overview
This is a setup chapter — and in the Dao-Twisted World, setups are never safe. Li Huowang is finally acting on a lead: black-robed nuns to the south who might be able to extract the parasite Danyangzi from his soul. But before he can reach them, he has to cross a lake. And on this lake, the rules are simple: pay the captain, trust his twenty-year experience, and hope the darkness under the water stays down there. Spoiler: it doesn’t. The chapter ends with the boat stranded in a reed marsh, the crew vanished, and a flotilla of armed small-craft closing in. The captain reappears with a knife and a question—and you just *know* neither “boiled knife-cut noodles” nor “wonton soup” is on the menu.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang tries to pawn the jade pendant in Siqi but is disgusted by the offered price (three taels). He briefly considers robbing the pawnshop, then physically punishes himself for the thought — a telling moment of self-control.
- He gathers the group for a new direction: south, following Li Zhi’s final clue about a convent of black-robed nuns. His priority is clear — Danyangzi must be dealt with before anything else.
- Chun Xiaoman joins them after sending her farewell note home via an escort agency. She reveals her mother did not report her to the authorities.
- Chun identifies the likely location: Mount Heng Hua, famous for a Golden Body Bodhisattva and an unusually fat order of nuns.
- The group purchases supplies and joins the general southward exodus of people fleeing a rumored coming war.
- At a massive, black-water lake, they board a ferry helmed by a surly, barefoot captain.
- During the crossing, the captain and boatman vanish mid-voyage in a reed marsh. Panic breaks out.
- Small armed boats emerge from the reeds, circling the stranded ferry.
- The captain reappears on one of the boats, cleaning his nails with a dagger, and asks Li Huowang a chilling question with a smile.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**“Boiled knife-cut noodles” (滚刀面) and “wonton soup” (馄饨面)** are not real menu items here — they are coded threats used by bandits on river/ lake routes. Knife-cut noodles implies *cutting* the victim’s body. Wonton soup implies *drowning* the victim (the word “wonton” sounds like “swallow whole” in this context). It’s a folk-horror equivalent of “your money or your life,” but with graphic, visceral imagery that fits the Dao-Twisted World’s aesthetic of physical consequence.

**Golden Body Bodhisattva (金身菩萨)** refers to a gilded or “golden” statue of a Bodhisattva, a sign of significant temple wealth and devotion. The fact that the nuns are described as “very fat” is a genuine rural Chinese observation — it implies either the temple has excellent food (good alms) or something more sinister is at play.

**The lake’s black water** is a classic horror-suspense motif in Chinese folk narratives: dark, still water is said to be a hiding-place for *shui gui* (水鬼, water ghosts) or *jiao* (蛟, dragons/ serpents), and crossing such bodies without proper appeasement is a standard gamble. The novel exploits this folk anxiety by grounding it in a concrete scene.

## Reading Guide
Buckle up, fellow Daoists — this chapter is the *calm before the lake-demon*. The real tension here isn’t the lake; it’s watching Li Huowang manage his crew and his impulses. That failed pawn? He almost goes full criminal-in-the-making and then decks himself. That’s our guy. He’s aware of his own erosion, and he’s fighting it with self-inflicted violence.

The worldbuilding nugget about nuns being “very fat” is deliciously weird. It’s such a specific, mundane observation that it *has* to mean something awful. Either they’re genuinely well-fed and safe, or the “fat” comes from a source nobody wants to think about. Read this and start counting the days until that “something” is revealed.

And that ending — the captain’s false cheer, the reeds closing in, the armed families appearing — this is *textbook* folk horror. The lake isn’t haunted by monsters. It’s haunted by *bandits*, which in this world might be even worse. Because monsters have rules. Bandits just have daggers and appetites.
