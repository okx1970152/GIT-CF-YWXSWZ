## Chapter Overview

Well, well, well. Our boy Li Huowang was finally having something resembling a decent day, and what does the Dao-Twisted World do? It serves him a steaming plate of *human meat* for lunch. That’s right—this chapter opens with a classic black inn trope, but it quickly spirals into something far more unsettling. From the visceral horror of discovering the “mutton” is really long pig, to a shocking revelation about the very map Li Huowang has been using, this chapter is a masterclass in tonal whiplash. Buckle up, fellow Daoists—the Great Liang is gone, and we’re now in the Great Qi. And nobody bothered to tell the guy who lives here.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang identifies the stir-fried lamb as human meat mid-meal, triggering a brutal one-man slaughter of the inn’s staff. His black tentacles emerge, brandishing multiple weapons, shocking even the jaded Supervisory Heavenly Office agents.
- Despite his victory, Li Huowang is disgusted and exhausted. He forces himself to vomit, even rinsing his stomach in wine to purge any residual knockout drug.
- Xia Dongsheng reacts by yanking ritual hooks embedded in his own acupoints, showing the group’s readiness for combat.
- Hongzhong pops up to taunt Li Huowang with a crucial piece of information: this is now the *Great Qi*, not the Great Liang. The Heart-Pan’s ability has literally rewritten the world’s geography and political structure.
- Li Sui, growing bolder, throws a toddler-level tantrum demanding swords to “help Dad.” Li Huowang pacifies her with his torture kit, which she accepts as a new toy.
- Zhuge Yuan reassures Li Huowang that reinforcements from the Nether Capital are coming to escort Elder Qin.
- While consulting his map, Li Huowang discovers that the city he bought the map in—the Upper Capital—does not appear on the map. Instead, the Nether Capital, capital of the Great Qi, is marked. The map has *changed* to reflect the new reality.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Cultivate the True/False” Dichotomy (修真/修假)**

This chapter subtly prepares the ground for one of the novel’s most devastating philosophical reveals: the conflict between “cultivating the true” and “cultivating the false.” In standard xianxia, every cultivator pursues the *True Way* (修真)—a path to authentic transcendence. But the Zuowandao world operates on a terrifying inversion: here, lies and falsehood can be “cultivated” into reality. The map that changes itself, the world that has been overwritten by a Heart-Pan’s belief—these are symptoms of that deeper sickness. When this secret finally drops, it will retroactively color every chapter you’ve read so far.

**The Heart-Pan (心蟠) Ability**

The Heart-Pan is not just a powerful being—it is a *reality editor*. While a Heart-Element can blur the line between truth and falsehood through sheer cognitive weight, a Heart-Pan can impose their version of reality onto the world at large. The Great Liang becoming the Great Qi is not a political event; it is a *rewrite*. The fact that Li Huowang’s map has physically changed to match the new reality shows how deep the alteration goes.

**Li Sui’s Development**

Li Sui is the mutated Black Tai Sui that resides inside Li Huowang. In this chapter, her behavior shifts from simple survival instinct to something approaching *child-like personality*. She throws a tantrum, demands toys (swords), and is pacified by a bag of torture instruments. This is adorable and terrifying in equal measure—a monster-child growing into awareness while playing with tools designed to break human bodies.

### Cultivate the True/False (修真/修假)

A central philosophical conflict in the Dao-Twisted World; “cultivating the true” is the standard xianxia path, while “cultivating the false” is the Zuowandao’s ability to turn lies into reality.

## Reading Guide

- Pay close attention to Hongzhong’s taunts. He is a Zuowandao agent, and every word out of his mouth is a breadcrumb of truth wrapped in a shell of malice. His reminder that this is the “Great Qi” is not a casual remark—it’s a needle buried in the reader’s subconscious, preparing you for the big reveal.
- The map scene is deceptively simple. Li Huowang’s confusion mirrors the reader’s: reality has *moved* while he wasn’t looking. The map didn’t change; *the world did*.
- Li Sui’s tantrum is a rare moment of dark humor in this otherwise grim chapter. Treasure it—because the next time she asks for a toy, it might be a god’s severed head.

**Chapter Themes:** Identity erasure, reality contamination, parental horror (raising a monster toddler), and the slow erosion of the old world by an even older lie.
