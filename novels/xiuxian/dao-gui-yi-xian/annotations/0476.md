## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang finally has a plan, but it comes with a price tag that makes his eyeballs sweat. After learning that the Buddhist relic to save Bai Lingmiao’s sight costs a staggering four hundred Yang-Life Pills (and the Dharma Eye is even more expensive at five hundred), our broke protagonist heads straight to the Supervisory Heavenly Office’s job board. He’s not here to make friends—he wants the highest-paying gig available. And that, dear readers, is where things get interesting. He bypasses the tempting Zuowandao bounties (too much baggage, he’s learned his lesson) and zeros in on a mysterious entry: **Spirit Perversion**. The reward is juicy, but the job description is a terrifying lottery ticket. The chapter ends on a surprisingly peaceful note—a month and a half later, with Bai Lingmiao washing clothes by a riverside, tapping a bamboo pole in front of her. The calm before the storm.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang learns the prices for the treatment options: 400 Yang-Life Pills for the relic, 500 for the Dharma Eye. He’s flat broke.
- He accepts the grim reality of valuing Bai Lingmiao’s sight over any cost, mentally noting that he’s already killed her entire family—what’s a little more sacrifice?
- He returns to the main hall of the Supervisory Heavenly Office to check the job board, still wearing his stoic “I survive everything” face.
- He sees a wall of Zuowandao bounties, including one for Hongzhong, but deliberately avoids them. The lesson with Zhuge Yuan is still fresh.
- He discovers the “Spirit Perversion” (灵孽) job—430 Yang-Life Pills plus five years of Stainless Yang-Life. A massive payout.
- Nanguan warns him that Spirit Perversions are wildly unpredictable; their danger depends entirely on what they’ve latched onto. They could be a pushover or a nightmare.
- Li Huowang accepts the job, takes the map with four possible locations, and refuses to recruit partners to keep all the reward for himself.
- A time jump of a month and a half. The first two locations are duds. No Spirit Perversion found.
- The chapter closes with a quiet domestic scene: Bai Lingmiao washing clothes by the river, tapping a bamboo pole ahead of her as she walks back to the wagon. She is blind.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Spirit Perversion (灵孽 / Líng Niè)** — This is not a standard xianxia term. “Líng” (灵) usually means “spirit” or “numinous,” while “Niè” (孽) means “evil” or “sin.” Together, they denote a kind of malignant, parasitic entity. The big twist in this novel is that Spirit Perversions are opportunistic horrors: their power changes based on the host they’re attached to. A Spirit Perversion on a grass blade is a joke; one on a millenia-old demon is a death sentence. This makes them a perfect “gacha” hunt — you never know if you’re pulling a common card or a world-boss.

**Yáng-Life Pills (阳寿丹) & Stainless Yáng-Life (无垢阳寿)** — Yang-Life Pills are a standard supernatural currency in the Dao-Twisted World, each representing a unit of lifespan. But “Stainless Yang-Life” (无垢阳寿) is a rarer, more valuable grade—lifespan that is “unstained” by sin, karma, or curse, making it far more potent for rituals and healing. Li Huowang’s hunger for this specific reward tells you he’s aiming for something that requires *clean* life energy, not just bulk years.

**The Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司) as the ‘Black Market Government’** — Li Huowang’s bitter observation that “no one in the world can outdo the Supervisory Heavenly Office when it comes to greed” is a key piece of worldbuilding. The Office operates as a state-sponsored supernatural contract agency, but they are ruthless capitalists. They pay cheap for blood and sell expensive for salvation. It’s a grim mirror of real-world systems: the people who clean up the mess also set the prices.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a classic *preparation arc* with an emotional gutpunch twist. The plot is simple: Li Huowang needs money, takes a dangerous job. But the execution is all about cost. The staggering gap between the *four pills* Li Zhi once had and the *four hundred* Li Huowang needs now visually represents how far he’s fallen into this world’s economy. He’s no longer a novice scraping by—he’s a veteran who understands the value of human life, and it’s terrifyingly expensive.

Also, pay attention to what Li Huowang *refuses*. He could take the Zuowandao bounties — they’re plentiful, well-paid, and direct. But he’s learned that killing one of them brings a swarm of vengeful colleagues. His refusal is a rare sign of maturity and caution. He’s not just a berserker anymore; he’s thinking about consequences.

And then there’s the final image. Bai Lingmiao, washing clothes by a river, tapping a bamboo pole. She’s blind. The carefree domestic moment is a lie—Li Huowang hasn’t found the cure yet, and time is running out. The bamboo pole is her lifeline, and the river is her only privacy. That’s the real horror of this chapter: the ticking clock disguised as a sunny afternoon.
