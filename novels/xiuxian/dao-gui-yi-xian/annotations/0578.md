## Chapter Overview
Alright, buckle up, disciples of the weird. Chapter 577 is the briefing room before the final boss run against That Freaking Dice. With the Great Liang Dragon Vein literally kidnapped by a Zuowandao trickster wearing the emperor's skin, our heroes are scrambling for a plan. The Ao-Jing sect is twitchy, Wusheng Laomu's people are murmuring, and everyone's gaze keeps sliding sideways. The Head of the Supervisory Heavenly Office, Xuanpin, proposes a plan so audaciously insane it involves Li Huowang's Xinsu ability being used as a Reality-Acceptance Engine. And in the middle of this war council, we get a quiet, ominous glimpse of Bai Lingmiao that suggests the weight of being a White Lotus Saintess is settling on her shoulders in ways Li Huowang can’t see.

## Key Plot Points
- **The War Council Convenes**: Xuanpin silences the Ao-Jing believers with a single gesture, but the tension is palpable. The problem: The Dice is now the legally crowned Emperor of Great Liang, making any direct attack an act of rebellion.
- **The Dilemma of the Military Cultivators**: The National Preceptor explains that without the troop deployment tally, the military cultivators are off-limits. The risk that The Dice, a master of manipulation, could turn them against the party is too great.
- **Li Huowang’s Inside Intel**: Li Huowang shares that The Dice is physically present as a dice inside Gao Zhijian’s skull. He proposes using a resurrection talisman (like the skin artifact he once gave Gao Zhijian) to kill him and bring him back. Xuanpin dismisses this as too simple.
- **Private Conference**: Xuanpin shrinks himself and Li Huowang down to ant-size for a private talk. Li Huowang senses a strange familiarity with the figure.
- **The Insane Plan Revealed**: Xuanpin’s plan is pure madness: Li Huowang must face The Dice first and, no matter what the trickster says, *treat it as truth* and use his ‘Cultivate the False’ ability to make it real, while Xuanpin handles the rest. Li Huowang is dubious about his own power.
- **Aftermath and Unease**: The plan is set. Li Huowang leaves with Bai Lingmiao, but she seems distant, emotionally opaque. Li Sui crashes into him, and he has to frantically explain her monstrous form is just Li Sui.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The Dragon Vein (龙脉)**: We’ve covered this before, but it bears repeating. A Dragon Vein isn’t just a river of power; it’s the physical, spiritual, and geopolitical backbone of an empire’s legitimacy. The Dice hasn’t just kidnapped an emperor—he is the emperor, and he has stolen the very thing that makes Great Liang real. This puts the entire realm in ontological danger.
- **The Xinsu Reality Engine**: Xuanpin’s plan is a terrifying application of the novel’s core metaphysics. Li Huowang doesn’t just *believe* things; his nature as a Xinsu means his belief has causal weight in reality. The plan is to weaponize him as an “acceptance node”—whatever The Dice claims, Li Huowang will make true, presumably to overload the lie or force it into a shape Xuanpin can exploit. It’s a brilliant, horrifying inversion of the Zuowandao’s own game.
- **The Emperor’s Blade**: The National Preceptor’s point about the military cultivators (兵家) being the “emperor’s blade” is a classic Chinese political logic. In a centralized bureaucratic empire, you cannot turn the standing army on the sovereign, even if you know the sovereign is a fraud, because the institutional oath binds them. The fact that The Dice has legally ascended the throne gives him the ultimate defensive shield: the very machinery of state.

## Reading Guide
So, the stage is set for what promises to be a mind-bending confrontation. The plan to use Li Huowang’s Xinsu power as an “acceptance node” for The Dice’s lies is a stroke of dark genius from the author. It’s the perfect counter to a Zuowandao: you don’t try to see through their lies; you become the mechanism that makes their lies *real* and watch them lose control of their own game. Pay close attention to Bai Lingmiao’s silence at the end. She’s not just tired; she’s carrying the weight of a world that’s about to break. And the bit about Li Huowang feeling familiar with Xuanpin? That’s the kind of detail that makes Taoist weird fiction so rewarding. Don’t just read the plot—feel the dread of the coming crossroads.
