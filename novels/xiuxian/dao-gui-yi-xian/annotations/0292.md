## Chapter Overview

You thought you knew what was coming. Li Huowang is on the brink of death, the Zuowandao double is about to saw his head into thirds, Bai Lingmiao is weeping behind a red veil, and Gao Zhijian is swinging a spiked club at a horde of living mud statues. It looks like a straightforward slaughter. Then the *other* voice inside Li Huowang’s head finally answers—and the answer comes out his mouth in a spray of black tentacles and blood. The source of the auditory hallucination, the one Li Huowang has been shouting at for chapters, isn’t madness. It’s the Black Tai Sui. And it’s ready to fight.

## Key Plot Points

- The Zuowandao impostor reveals himself as the sole orchestrator of Ganyuan Village’s nightmare. Every clay statue is his puppet; every threat was his performance. He even gives Li Huowang an ironic offer: let *him* be the new Hongzhong.
- The Zuowandao’s brutality hits a new peak. He doesn’t just stab Li Huowang—he *lifts* him by the knife in his chest and begins a deliberate, horizontal cut toward his skull. The scene is visceral and butcher-like.
- Li Huowang’s auditory hallucination, treated for many chapters as a symptom of his madness, is confirmed as a real entity communicating from inside his body. When he finally roars at it to shut up, it obeys—and then erupts from his mouth.
- The Black Tai Sui physically manifests: black, mucus-slicked tentacles burst from Li Huowang’s throat, latch onto the Zuowandao’s face, and rip it off—along with his double-pupiled eyeballs. The Zuowandao’s final scream is one of genuine, unscripted terror.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Bang Bing Jue (帮兵决)** — Bai Lingmiao’s incantation is a northeastern Chinese spirit-summoning verse used by chuma mediums. The lyrics invoke the ghostly officials of the underworld (Ox-head, Horse-face, Black and White Impermanence) to drag a soul to judgment. In this chapter, it’s not just atmosphere—it’s her *counter-spell*, the attempt to pull Li Huowang back from the brink of death by divine protocol.

**The Black Tai Sui (黑太岁)** — A parasitic entity with no fixed shape, sourced from the corrupt alchemy of Danyangzi. Earlier, Li Huowang kept it suppressed because it was his own contamination. Now he weaponizes it. The horror here isn’t just the tentacles—it’s that the voice in his head all along had *been* this thing talking from the inside. The novel has been giving you the answer for a hundred chapters; you just didn’t know it.

**Zuowandao self-deception trick** — The impostor’s ability to perfectly mimic Li Huowang (down to the double pupils) suggests a technique where the liar genuinely believes their own disguise, making it undetectable even to supernatural senses. The only thing that outs him is his theatrical glee. He enjoys the reveal too much.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a setup-and-payoff machine. The Zuowandao monologue? Infuriating. The mud statues overwhelming the group? Tense. But the real hook is the *riddle you forgot you were reading*. Li Huowang has been hearing voices since nearly the beginning. The doctor told him it was schizophrenia. Danyangzi told him it was the system being weird. But in this chapter, the answer slams into you with the taste of blood and mucus: it was the Black Tai Sui, and it was always listening from inside his chest. The chapter doesn’t give you a new power-up—it gives you a *recontextualization* of the entire previous arc. The tentacle vomit is bonus.
