## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang's paranoia goes into overdrive when he tails a trio of Nuo-opera performers to their hidden home—a creepy sunken courtyard built right into the earth. But instead of a confrontation, he gets a tense standoff with unseen watchers behind doors and windows, while the grotesque masks on the walls seem to *watch* him too. After a cautious retreat, the family makes a fateful decision: strike first. Meanwhile, the friendly old monk tagging along starts looking less like a lucky charm and more like a walking coincidence that's getting *way* too convenient.

## Key Plot Points
- The three Nuo performers remove their masks, revealing entirely ordinary young faces—but Li Huowang can't shake the image of them juggling teeth and stays on high alert.
- Spotting Li Huowang's surveillance, the trio hastily packs up and flees, leading him and the old monk on a chase through town.
- They lose the trail, but the monk's scream draws Li Huowang to a bizarre underground residence: a sunken square pit with doors, windows, and Nuo masks hanging from the walls like sentinels.
- Li Huowang detects multiple hidden watchers and attempts to de-escalate with ritual greetings and underworld code talk ("spring canon"), but receives no reply.
- After a tense stalemate, he retreats with the monk, but the family—led by a furious grandfather—vows to strike first and eliminate the "red-robed Daoist."
- Li Huowang privately questions whether the monk's presence is still coincidental, given that he's been at the center of two dangerous discoveries.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
### The Sunken Courtyard (地坑院)
This chapter features a traditional architectural form called a *dikengyuan* (地坑院) or "sunken courtyard"—a dwelling literally dug into the earth, common in the Loess Plateau regions of northern China. These homes are naturally insulated, cool in summer, and warm in winter. In the Dao-Twisted World, however, the same structure becomes a claustrophobic, trap-like space where the walls themselves feel alive, and masks on the walls act as surveillance tools. The contrast between mundane folk architecture and its uncanny misuse is a recurring horror technique in the novel.

### Nuo Opera Masks & Folk Horror
The Nuo masks in this chapter are not just props—they carry a living gaze. In Chinese folk tradition, Nuo masks were believed to house the spirit of the deity or exorcist being portrayed. Storing them improperly, or having them face the wrong way, could invite bad luck. Here, the masks actively *watch* Li Huowang, turning a ritual object into an unsettling surveillance tool—a brilliant subversion of their original protective function.

### The "Spring Canon" (春典)
Li Huowang's attempt to "open the spring canon" is a reference to the secret jargon used by traveling performers, thieves, and underworld operatives in traditional China. Using the right code words could mark you as "one of us" and defuse hostility. The family's refusal to respond signals either that they do not belong to the same network—or that the breach is too deep for words.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, fellow Daoists—because this chapter is all about **escalation through stillness**. There's no fight, no monster, no explosion. Just Li Huowang standing in a pit, feeling watched by wooden masks, waiting for someone to blink. And that's *more* unsettling than any direct attack.

The real tension here is psychological: Li Huowang is no longer a reactive victim. He's actively hunting for threats, testing social boundaries, and choosing retreat over confrontation when the odds are unclear. That's growth—but it also means he's drawing *more* attention, not less. The family's decision to "strike first" is a beautiful bit of dramatic irony: Li Huowang thinks he's being cautious by leaving, but his caution has just made him a confirmed target.

And then there's the monk. Twice now, the old man has been Li Huowang's accidental guide into mortal danger. Is he a plant? A pawn? Or just the unluckiest holy man in the Dao-Twisted World? The novel keeps us guessing—and that doubt is the point.

Keep your eyes on the masks. They're watching.
