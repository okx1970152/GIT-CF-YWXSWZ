## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists—because this chapter is a non-stop, pedal-to-the-metal assassination sprint. Li Huowang has completely abandoned stealth and gone full berserker mode, tearing through the Great Liang palace like a one-man army. The chapter kicks off with a visceral, high-stakes attempt on the Empress Dowager’s life, showcasing a brutal new combat dynamic between Li Huowang and Li Sui. But the palace's defenses are no joke, featuring a seductive Hu Ji assassin whose abilities hit Li Huowang right where it hurts—his emotions. It’s a raw, bloody spectacle of pain-as-power, culminating in a chilling face-off with a figure from the Ao-Jing Sect that leaves us hanging on a razor’s edge.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang executes a brutal assassination attempt on the Empress Dowager, using his own eye as a freezing weapon and a severed hand as a projectile to pierce through her guard.
- Li Sui demonstrates her advanced coordination, retrieving and reattaching Li Huowang’s severed hand with her tentacles, establishing their seamless combat synergy.
- The Eldest Princess’s guards use a diversion tactic to buy her time, but Li Huowang’s sheer aggression overwhelms them.
- A mysterious Hu Ji dancer uses a seductive art that transfers Li Huowang’s love for Bai Lingmiao and Yang Na onto her, nearly incapacitating him with pleasure.
- Li Sui saves Li Huowang by forcing her barbed tentacles out of his mouth to counter the Hu Ji’s kiss, tearing her face off in the process.
- Li Huowang increases his shared pain-link to critical levels, forcing the Eldest Princess and her entire retinue to collapse from the agony.
- A black-veiled figure with layered hand scars blocks Li Huowang’s path, identified as likely from the Ao-Jing Sect due to his immense pain tolerance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter showcases a fascinating and brutal application of the novel’s unique magic system: the **sympathetic pain link**. Li Huowang’s ability, tied to his nature as a Heart-Element, allows him to share the physical suffering he inflicts on himself with everyone in a certain radius. It’s a terrifying area-denial and crowd-control tool. The tactic of using his own ribs as ritual daggers is a direct callback to the **Ao-Jing Sect’s** *True Sutra of the Fire Vestments*, which weaponizes self-immolation and agony. The Hu Ji's “golden thread” seduction is a classic xianxia charm technique, but uniquely twisted here to target Li Huowang's specific emotional anchors—his love for Bai Lingmiao and Yang Na—making it an incredibly personal and devastating attack. The appearance of a figure who can endure such pain to stand is the hallmark of a high-ranking Ao-Jing practitioner, setting up a clash between two masters of suffering.

## Reading Guide
This is a pure action chapter, but it’s also a major character moment for Li Huowang. Notice how he no longer hesitates, no longer questions. He is a cold, efficient predator. The horror here isn’t in the monsters, but in the protagonist himself. The real terror is watching a man who has completely become the weapon he was forced to forge. Pay close attention to the dynamic with Li Sui—she is acting less like a passenger and more like a perfect combat partner. The final reveal of the Ao-Jing Sect member is a classic cliffhanger. This isn’t just a random guard; this is a cultivator from a sect that *revels* in the same pain Li Huowang uses. This fight will be less about dealing pain and more about who can *endure* it longer. Get ready for a collision of two immovable objects.
