## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, buckle up—the tension dial is jammed past eleven this chapter. Our favorite walking disaster Li Huowang, still invisible from last time, crashes headfirst into a full-blown Supervisory Heavenly Office operation. He’s got one goal: grab Zhuge Yuan’s bone-sword from the purple-lipped woman and ghost. But between a swarm of thumb-sized golden Buddhas that preach the *absolute worst* gospel and four triangular banners that nail his shadow to the ground, escape is looking less like a plan and more like a prayer. The chapter builds from a surgical heist into a standoff where Li Huowang’s only backup is a headless corpse he can barely control. And when the Office Daoist breaks out his special brush, the reveal lands with the weight of a guillotine. *How is this possible! That’s Zhuge Yuan’s brush!*

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang, still invisible via his skin technique, is detected by the Office agents despite his stealth, confirming that their methods can bypass his standard tricks.
- He cuts off his own fingers to deflect a blade-studded robe and uses his detached face as a disposable decoy against Hong Da—both acts showing his willingness to sacrifice body parts as tactical tools.
- The purple-lipped woman is revealed to be a Buddhist practitioner when a thumb-sized golden Buddha emerges from her sleeve, shattering Li Huowang’s dagger with its head.
- Li Huowang refrains from drawing his own sword (the one Hong Da knows) and instead uses ribs torn from his chest as a self-inflicted weapon to induce shared pain, immobilizing everyone except himself.
- After crushing her wrist and recovering the marrow sword (Zhuge Yuan’s bone), he attempts to flee but is trapped by four apricot-yellow banners that pin his shadow to the ground.
- All former Dharma Sect enemies have escaped, leaving only the Office agents surrounding Li Huowang, which escalates the standoff.
- Li Huowang tries a belly-voice bluff, but the Office agents don’t buy it and set a blood-soaked net to prevent escape.
- The Daoist activates a special brush that begins erasing Li Huowang’s color, turning his hair gray.
- Li Huowang summons the headless corpse of Peng Longteng as a last-minute shield, stunning the Daoist, who recognizes that the brush should have made this impossible.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Thumb-Sized Golden Buddhas (拇指佛陀)** — These tiny, weeping or laughing golden figures clinging to the woman’s face are a grotesque corruption of a standard Buddhist motif. In xianxia and folk horror, miniature Buddha emanations usually signify advanced enlightenment or pure merit, but here they act like parasitic symbiotes that can *wield their own spells* (“lay down the butcher’s knife and become a Buddha” is actual Buddhist doctrine weaponized as a mind-control nudge). The horror comes from seeing a beautiful ideal (Buddha-hood) reduced to a swarm of maggots that physically mold themselves into a male face on a female body.

**Shadow-Pinning Banners (杏黄旗)** — The four triangular apricot-yellow flags that nail Li Huowang’s shadow to the ground are a classic Daoist formation tool. In Chinese magical folklore, controlling a target’s shadow is equivalent to controlling their physical movement—shadow and body are linked as yin-yang reflections. The banners are not just restraining his feet; they’re anchoring his *light-based self*, preventing invisible or phasing escapes. Very common in xianxia tactical magic, but always costs the caster effort (the side characters don’t free up easily).

**Judge’s Brush / Kui Star Brush (魁星笔)** — The Daoist’s brush that erases a person’s color from reality is directly named after Zhuge Yuan’s weapon. This isn’t a magical paintbrush; it’s a tool that literally *smears away* the target’s existence—taking their vitality, their connection to the world, one brushstroke at a time. The graying of Li Huowang’s temples is the visual symptom. The shock the Daoist feels when Li Huowang summons Peng Longteng despite being half-erased signals that the brush is supposed to suppress all supernatural abilities, and Li Huowang just broke that rule.

### Judge's Brush / Kui Star Brush (魁星笔)

A martial and ritual weapon named after the god of literature; it can erase a target’s color and vitality from reality, suppressing supernatural abilities. Zhuge Yuan’s personal tool.

### Shadow-pinning banners (杏黄旗)

Small triangular banners used in Daoist formations; when placed at the four corners of a target’s shadow, they anchor the target to the ground by controlling their light-based reflection.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in escalating limitations. Li Huowang starts invisible, confident, and surgical—then every layer of his advantage gets systematically peeled back. His invisibility is seen through. His face trick is countered by a brush. His weapons break against a Buddha. His shadow gets pinned. The result is a slow-building claustrophobia where the protagonist’s only winning move is to *cause himself more pain than everyone else*—a strategy that is as darkly hilarious as it is desperate.

Also, pay close attention to the brush scene: the Daoist’s panic is not just fear of a strong enemy. It’s *theological* shock. He has full faith in Zhuge Yuan’s brush and cannot reconcile that with Li Huowang still standing. That moment hints that Li Huowang’s condition (his Heart-Element nature, his parasitic passengers, or even his blurred reality) operates on rules outside the Office’s normal framework. The horror here isn’t just the gore—it’s that the system of supernatural law the Office relies on has just met something that does not fit.

And the final line, echoing the title *Manifestation*, belongs to Peng Longteng. She is *manifested* where she shouldn’t be, and that changes the battlefield’s rules entirely. Get ready for the fallout.
