## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang makes a terrifying discovery: Danyangzi is not just a ghost haunting his mind—he’s actively taking over. Each appearance is stronger, more invasive, and more personal. As the crew tries to escape the Yuan family’s stronghold with their share of treasure, they discover that the boat captain, Yuan Er, isn’t dead. Worse, he’s armed with a grotesque, self-mutilating ritual powered by a mysterious red bamboo slip. What follows is a desperate struggle where Li Huowang’s summoning of the Wandering Lords meets its match—and the bamboo slip itself awakens into something far beyond human.

## Key Plot Points

- **Danyangzi’s growing control** is a key chapter theme: Li Huowang identifies a clear three-stage escalation (voice → vision → full emotional/intellectual manipulation) and decides he will never allow full possession, even if it means his own death.
- **The group’s greed reveals character**: Li Huowang prioritizes practicality (only gold, no heavy silver) over Gouwa’s desperation for wealth, showing the contrast between survivor’s logic and petty greed.
- **Li Huowang’s respect for the dead**: He takes time to retrieve the drowned child-disciples’ bodies, intending to cremate them and return their ashes home, demonstrating that even in crisis, he refuses to dehumanize the innocent.
- **Yuan Er returns**: The boat captain survived and has turned himself into a living ritual weapon, using his own teeth, eyes, and throat-flesh to power a mysterious bamboo slip.
- **The Wandering Lords are neutralized**: Yuan Er’s eye-sacrifice creates an invisible binding field that pins the spirits, revealing a limitation of Li Huowang’s current trump card.
- **The bamboo slip awakens**: Yuan Er’s final self-sacrifice—carving his own throat-flesh—feeds the slip, which starts to speak with his stolen tongue, delivering a cryptic, powerful incantation that signals a supernatural entity beyond mere mortals.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Bamboo Slips (竹简)**—Before paper became widespread in China, texts were written on strips of bamboo bound together with cords. In the Dao-Twisted World, a bamboo slip is not just a historical artifact; it can be a vessel of forbidden ritual power. Yuan Er’s slip is no ordinary text—it has been corrupted and activated through a blood offering, turning it into a mystical weapon that consumes the caster’s own body parts as fuel. This blends the ancient scholar’s tool with the horror writer’s imagination: the very medium of knowledge becomes a devourer.

**The Verbal Incantation**—The bamboo slip’s final lines (*“One tongue turns the wheel…”*) are written in a style reminiscent of Daoist and Buddhist ritual verse. The phrase “tongue turns the wheel” alludes to the Buddhist concept of the “Dharma Wheel” (*法轮*), which represents the teaching of the Buddha. Here, it is twisted into a declaration of dominance—the Wheel is turned not by wisdom, but by a living tongue ripped from a dying man’s throat. The “supreme lord” referenced is a warning that something far more powerful than Yuan Er is now listening.

**Self-Mutilation as Power Source**—Yuan Er’s methods (teeth, eye, throat) follow a grim logic: in many folk traditions, the body is a microcosm of the world, and sacrificing its parts (especially sensory organs like eyes or voice) can unlock supernatural force. This recalls real-world shamanic practices where extreme physical sacrifice is believed to open a channel to higher powers. The cost is permanent, irreversible, and intensely personal.

## Reading Guide

Grab your ritual bell and hold your breath—this chapter is a masterclass in escalation. Li Huowang is getting smarter about Danyangzi’s threat, and that internal dread makes every external fight feel like the countdown to a bomb. Meanwhile, Yuan Er’s grotesque bamboo-slip technique is one of the most haunting displays of body horror so far: teeth, eyes, throat, and blood all turned into projectiles and fuel. It’s visceral, it’s desperate, and it sets up a terrifying new power dynamic. Plus, that final incantation? That’s not just a spell—that’s a name being called in a realm you don’t want to answer. Fellow readers, this is the part where the Dao-Twisted World shows you there’s always something older, stranger, and hungrier behind the curtain.
