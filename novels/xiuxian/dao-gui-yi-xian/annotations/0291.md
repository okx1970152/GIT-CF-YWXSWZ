## Chapter Overview

Holy *crap*. Forget monsters and demons—this chapter introduces the most terrifying enemy Li Huowang has faced yet: **a Zuowandao who can lie to himself so perfectly that even supernatural truth-detection fails**. And he learned it from *Li Huowang*. That’s the kind of insult that leaves a mark deeper than any sword wound. Get ready, because the Way of Sitting in Forgetfulness just pulled out an entirely new tier of psychological warfare, and it hits like a freight train wrapped in a mirror.

## Key Plot Points

Li Huowang’s group fends off a wave of animated clay statues, with Blind Chen using his blood-infused Eight Trigrams Mirror to incinerate them with a beam of holy light.

Taking shelter in a woodshed, Li Huowang tries to convince the wounded and deeply suspicious Blind Chen to stay with them, but is interrupted by the persistent return of the clay figures.

Just as Li Huowang turns his back to urge his ally to prepare, Blind Chen stabs him through the chest from behind.

The horrifying twist: Blind Chen is genuinely horrified by his own actions. His body is suddenly hijacked, and his face melts and reforms into an exact copy of Li Huowang’s—complete with heterochromatic eyes.

The impostor proudly announces it’s a **Zuowandao** who has perfected a new technique: *lying to itself* so deeply that even its own body believed the disguise, bypassing all normal detection. The kicker? It learned the method from Li Huowang himself.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Zuowandao (坐忘道) – The Way of Sitting in Forgetfulness**: Don’t let the classical Daoist name fool you. In the original Daoist canon, “sitting in forgetfulness” (*zuowang*) was a lofty meditative state where the practitioner emptied the mind and dissolved the boundaries of the self. It was all about transcending ego and merging with the Dao. Beautiful, right? Well, this novel takes that beautiful concept and stomps it into something truly horrifying. The Zuowandao are a mad cult of liars who weaponize falsehood itself. They don’t just deceive others—they live inside the lie, making every interaction a minefield of uncertainty.

**The “Trick Yourself” Gambit**: This chapter introduces a terrifying new escalation. Standard Zuowandao trickery involves fooling the victim; a good eye and sharp instincts might catch the lie. But if the liar fools *themselves* first—genuinely believes their own false identity—then what happens? The lie becomes psychically true for the liar. Spells that reveal truth won’t trigger. The body physically transforms. And when the mask is dropped, the reveal hits with the force of a sledgehammer. This is a logical extreme of the novel’s central horror: if belief shapes reality, then what happens when a malicious actor weaponizes total self-deception?

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a gut punch of both action and psychological horror. The fight against the clay statues is a solid warm-up—a classic xianxia struggle against endlessly respawning minions that keeps the tension high. But the real show is the betrayal scene. The genius of it lies in the accomplice’s genuine terror; Blind Chen’s panic makes the deception exponentially more disturbing. You *know* a twist is coming, but the execution—the face-morph, the arrogant confession, the implication that this trick originated from Li Huowang’s own methods—lands with brutal precision. This isn’t just a plot twist; it’s a thesis statement about the power of lying and the erosion of the self in the Dao-Twisted World. Pay close attention to how the impostor speaks. The casual, almost affectionate tone (“Red Center Boss”) is pure Zuowandao performance: they treat cosmic terror as a game, and you, the reader, are now a player.
