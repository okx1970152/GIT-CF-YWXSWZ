## Chapter Overview
The Zuowandao have gone conspicuously quiet, and Li Huowang’s paranoia sharpens to a razor’s edge. While the chapter may seem like a transitional breather—travel, inn stops, awkward new worshipers—it actually drops a lore bomb that quietly reshapes everything we thought we knew about the *Thousand Greats Record*. Li Huowang discovers that three Dung-Beetle Ascensions have altered his relationship with Bashe at a fundamental level. He no longer needs the book. He *is* the offering point. And while that sounds like a power-up in any normal xianxia, here it feels like moving one chair closer to the furnace.

## Key Plot Points
- Xia Risheng, a young Ao-Jing disciple, kneels to Li Huowang and addresses him as “Great Elder,” claiming that three Dung-Beetle Ascensions make him the sect’s highest authority. Li Huowang bluntly denies it and walks away.
- The chapter’s core revelation: Li Huowang tests if he can still perform flesh-and-blood techniques without the *Thousand Greats Record*. He can. His fingernail flick splinters a tree at three *zhang* with no intermediary book—proving he has internalized the sacrificial link to Bashe.
- Li Huowang acknowledges this is both an advantage (no more vulnerability to having the *Record* stolen, as with Peng Longteng) and a terrifying unknown (what else has Ascension changed about him?).
- Zhuge Yuan buys a cart for Old Man Qin and explains his philosophy of friendship: he chooses companions by fate and instinct, not status. The chat is cut short when food arrives.
- Li Huowang broods over the Zuowandao’s silence. He suspects they are planning something large-scale, possibly involving the Four Joys and North Wind.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The **Dung-Beetle Ascension** (*Cangjia Dengjie*, 苍蜣登阶) takes its name from the dung beetle, an insect that feeds on filth and transforms it into a ball of life-giving sustenance. In xianxia terms, this is a “corrupted” or “inverted” path to power: instead of refining purity, the practitioner refines suffering and rot. The beetle’s ball becomes the cult’s paradoxical symbol—a grotesque, sacred burden. Three completed Ascensions apparently mark a user as having become so saturated in Bashe’s logic that the physical *Thousand Greats Record* is no longer required. The flesh itself becomes the scripture.

The **Great Elder** title in the Ao-Jing Sect is noteworthy. In the novel’s earlier arcs, all “Great Elders” encountered were actually Zuowandao impostors. That a genuine sect member now recognizes Li Huowang as one clarifies that the title corresponds to a real, in-world rank—its authenticity was only ever corrupted by the impostors, not the office itself. The hierarchy likely runs: ordinary disciple → Ascension-level elder → Great Elder → something higher we haven’t seen.

## Reading Guide
If this chapter feels strangely quiet, that’s exactly the point. The calm before a storm is a staple of the genre, and here the silence of the Zuowandao is *deafening*. Li Huowang’s logic is airtight: they tried small strikes, got nowhere, and are now probably rolling out the heavy artillery—the Four Joys. But the bigger gut-punch is the personal cost. Li Huowang gets a power-up he never asked for, one that moves him closer to the entity he least wants to be close to. Every “advantage” in this novel comes with a shadow. Enjoy the breather, reader—the butcher’s knife is definitely being sharpened off-screen.

Also, Zhuge Yuan buying a cart for an old chess buddy is a moment of genuine warmth in a world built on filth and betrayal. Savor it. Those are rare.
