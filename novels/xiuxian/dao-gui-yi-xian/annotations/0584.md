## Chapter Overview

Hold onto your meridians, fellow daoists, because Volume 3 wraps up with a bang—or rather, with the chilling, triumphant laughter of a man losing his mind on purpose. Li Huowang makes a terrifyingly bold play: he decides to become a Siming, an Arbiter of Fate, by sheer force of belief. He gambles everything on the idea that his future, more powerful self will come back to save him *right now*. It's a gamble that breaks reality, confuses gods, and lands him smack in the middle of a therapy session with Dr. Yi Donglai. The lines between the two worlds blur more than ever, leaving us with a cured (?) Li Huowang, a vanished Zhuge Yuan, and a mother's tearful joy that feels just a little too good to be true. Get ready for a finale that’s part cosmic horror, part psychiatric breakthrough, and entirely mind-bending.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang rejects the Dice's taunts and Doumu's coercion, declaring he will become a Siming himself.
- He drains his Innate Pneuma to "cultivate the false into reality," attempting to make a future, more powerful version of himself exist.
- The Void year entity attacks, draining Li Huowang's yang life and nearly killing Gao Zhijian, but Gao Zhijian's latent power awakens, unleashing his Dragon Vein.
- During his struggle, Li Huowang experiences a vision of the Chief Astrologer, who confirms he is on the path to becoming the Siming of Bewilderment.
- The vision collapses, and Li Huowang is transported to a hospital room with Dr. Yi Donglai.
- Using hypnosis, Yi Donglai helps Li Huowang resolve the mental conflict, pulling an eighteen-faced dice (symbolizing the Dice's influence) out of a ceiling light.
- The Dice's possession of Gao Zhijian ends, and Zhuge Yuan finally fades away, his duty fulfilled.
- Li Huowang declares his illness cured, and the doctors and his mother celebrate, ending Volume 3 on a note of fragile, uncertain victory.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Siming (司命) & Heart-Pan (心蟠):** This chapter deepens the novel's already staggering cosmology. We already knew Siming were high-dimensional, reality-bending "Arbiters of Fate." Now we learn the term *Heart-Pan* (心蟠). A Heart-Pan is a mortal who acts as a Siming's "past" and "seed"—its connection to the lower world to ensure its own existence. Think of it as a Siming's anchor or its larval stage. Being a Siming's Heart-Pan means being their puppet, a role Li Huowang violently rejects, preferring to become the thing itself.

**The Way of Bewilderment (迷惘天道):** Li Huowang is told his future Siming domain will be "Bewilderment Itself." This is a perfect, tragic irony. Bewilderment—the inability to tell truth from falsehood, illusion from reality—has been the curse of his life *and* his greatest weapon (his Heart-Element power to "cultivate the false"). His future ascension means his power will literally grow from the very thing that tortures him most.

## Reading Guide

This is a masterclass in weird fiction's central trick: making the solution feel as terrifying as the problem. Li Huowang "cures" himself not by destroying his hallucinatory world, but by leaning so hard into its logic that he forces it to rewrite its own rules. The "Winning by Losing" strategy is in full effect. He accepts his future as a Siming of Bewilderment, which sounds like a nightmare, but he frames it as a victory.

The final scene with Dr. Yi Donglai is pure genius. The Chief Astrologer shed his red robe to reveal a white lab coat. What does that tell us? Are the gods of the Dao-Twisted World just psychiatric constructs? Or is modern psychology just another form of divine exorcism? The novel refuses to give a clear answer, but it gives us a powerful symbol: the line between the healer and the prophet has been completely erased.

And that eighteen-sided dice? That's the Dice's meddling, literally unscrewed from a light fixture. It's a perfect, bizarre, and mundane solution to a supernatural problem. The real horror, however, lingers in that final question: is Li Huowang truly better, or has he just found a more comfortable lie to live in? Volume 3 ends, and the only thing certain is uncertainty.
