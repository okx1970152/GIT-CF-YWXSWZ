## Chapter Overview

Alright, grab your cult-survival gear, folks, because Chapter 174 takes us on the wildest ride yet — straight into outer space, past some truly nightmarish cosmic horrors, and right up to the pearly gates of… a bureaucratic misunderstanding. Danyangzi is convinced he’s won. He’s broken free, sucked Li Huowang dry, and is ready to ascend to immortal glory. What he finds, however, is less a welcoming party and more a celestial immigration checkpoint with a very tricky name. This chapter is the ultimate payoff for a hundred plus chapters of body horror and twisted alchemy, a masterclass in using cognitive dissonance as the final, cruelest weapon.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Li Huowang, now appearing as an illusion, taunting Danyangzi. He reveals that all the faces Danyangzi sees are projections from his own unsevered consciousness, a hell of his own making. When Danyangzi tries to cut them down, they pass right through. He is trapped in a psychological cage made of his own contemptible actions. In a moment of clarity, Danyangzi realizes he can’t win this game and, in a final act of defiance, chooses *not* to sever his *san shi* (Three Corpses), vowing that the “true self” will deal with Li Huowang. He strikes himself, shouting “I am Li Huowang!” causing the illusions to briefly flicker.

The scene shifts. A mangled, battle-worn Danyangzi (having just escaped the Xi Shen) descends and absorbs the “Danyangzi” part of the struggling youth. The one-armed youth dies, and the version of Danyangzi we have been following feels whole for the first time. Now, truly believing himself an immortal, Danyangzi ascends into the cosmos. He flies past horrifying, Lovecraftian entities—a grey-white entity covered in incense, a blind golden *pusa* with tentacles, and wailing black cranes among them.

Finally, he reaches the legendary *Nantian Men*, the Southern Heavenly Gate. The gate is a formless jade plaque suspended by colossal, nameless beings. He pushes past other wailing monsters and tries to enter, but is blocked by an invisible barrier. As he rages against the celestial order, he suddenly realizes he can read the characters on the plaque. They do not say *Nantian Men*. They say *Mao zhi Men* — the Ox-Head Gate. His final ambition is shattered in a moment of chilling, absurd revelation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is an absolute goldmine for hardcore xianxia and folklore fans, as it takes established tropes and gives them a thoroughly *Dao Gui Yi Xian* spin.

First, the **Southern Heavenly Gate (南天门, *Nantian Men*)**. In traditional Chinese mythology and most xianxia, this is the main celestial gateway into the Heavenly Court, the seat of the Jade Emperor and all the major gods. Reaching it is the culmination of a cultivator's journey. The novel completely subverts this. Here, it’s not a glorious entrance but a formless, terrifying jade plaque suspended in a void of chaos. It’s not guarded by celestial generals but by wailing, formless abominations. The name being wrong—*Mao zhi Men*—implies that even the fundamental geography of heaven is different or has been corrupted, or that Danyangzi was always aiming at the wrong door.

Second, the concept of the **Three Corpses (三尸, *San Shi*)**. In Daoist internal alchemy, the Three Corpses or Three Worms are malevolent, parasitic spirits residing in the human body (head, chest, belly). They crave decay and death, and they report a person's sins to the heavens to shorten their lifespan. A key step in immortality is to “cut off” or “kill” these corpses through meditation and purification. The novel literalizes this into a full-blown psychological horror. Li Huowang, as a *xinsu*, weaponizes the concept, trapping Danyangzi by forcing him to confront the question of identity until the *San Shi* become a prison of his own consciousness. Danyangzi's final act of defiance is to *refuse* the purification process, highlighting that immortality paths in this world are not about moral perfection but brutal, unforgiving mechanics.

Finally, Danyangzi’s journey is a perverse mirror of the classic **Ascension (登阶/飞升)**. The horrifying entities he passes—the incense-covered mass, the blind *pusa*, the screaming cranes—are all failed or trapped cultivators, the ones who never made it. This is a deeply unsettling addition to standard lore. It implies that the path to heaven is not a clear road but a bio-horror gallery of your own potential future, and that even the “gods” and “immortals” are locked in a state of permanent, tortured stasis.

### Ascension

A classic xianxia trope where a cultivator transcends mortality to reach a higher realm. In this chapter, it is depicted as a journey through a cosmic horror gallery before a final, absurd denial.

## Reading Guide

This is a colossal, game-changing chapter, so take a moment to let it sink in. First, savor Danyangzi’s psychological breakdown. The novel has been building him up as this unfathomable tyrant, but here we see him reduced to a babbling, trapped animal having an identity crisis. It’s a fantastic payoff for all his hubris.

Second, the cosmic horror section is key. Don’t just gloss over it. The entities Danyangzi flies past are a curated list of the novel’s ultimate stakes. They are the answer to the question “What happens to everyone?” The *pusa* with tendrils, the wailing cranes — these are not random monsters; they are the end-state of various failed cultivations. Think of them as a celestial bestiary of failure that Danyangzi arrogantly ignores.

Finally, the punchline of *Mao zhi Men* (毛之门, the Ox-Head Gate) is perfect. It’s a moment of brilliant, bleak comedy. The entire chapter is a brutal comeuppance. Li Huowang didn’t need to kill Danyangzi; he just needed to get him to the wrong gate at the wrong time. This confirms a terrible new rule of the Dao-Twisted World: The labels on things matter, and even the most sacred names can be a lie.
