## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow cultivators—because this chapter pulls the rug out so hard you’ll feel your own worldview crack. “Red Center” is a masterclass in rug-pull escalation: after chapters of political maneuvering and courtly tension, Li Huowang suddenly reveals himself as a Zuowandao—specifically, its highest-ranked “Four Joys” member, Red Center. The scene vaults from desperate escape, to cold-blooded assassination, to grotesque body horror, to triumphant laughter as his face literally reshapes itself into the Chinese character 中. It’s a tonal detonation that recontextualizes everything you thought you knew about his recent behavior, his motives, and the very nature of the conflict.

## Key Plot Points

1. The eunuch rejects the anti-Empress Dowager coalition, revealing a deeper political deadlock.
2. As the Trap-and-Smash Camp besieges the Liang estate, Zhuge Yuan faces a coordinated magical assault involving blood sorcery, four Daoist altars, and Buddhist chants.
3. Zhuge Yuan counters the attack with his own brush-and-almanac technique, neutralizing the four priests.
4. In a sudden move, Li Huowang steals Zhuge Yuan’s almanac and flees toward the Supervisor.
5. He assassinates the Supervisor mid-handoff using a hidden blade in the almanac.
6. He declares himself “Red Center of the Zuowandao,” revealing himself as the enemy all along.
7. He forces Li Sui to reshape his face into the character 中, grotesquely confirming his identity.
8. The disguised Zuowandao soldiers celebrate, and Li Huowang laughs in triumph over the corpse.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Four Joys (四喜) and Three Yuans (三元)**: These are the highest echelons of the Zuowandao, named after mahjong tiles. “Four Joys” corresponds to the four wind tiles (East, South, West, North) or the four “joy” patterns. “Red Center” (红中) is one of them—the middle tile with the red character 中, often wildcard or bonus tile. This isn’t random; the author weaponizes a beloved gambling game’s symbols into a ranking system for a cult that treats reality as a game to be rigged. Each “JoY” and “Yuan” is a master trickster, and seeing one deployed signals that the Zuowandao has committed a serious asset.

**Cultivate the True vs. Cultivate the False (修真/修假)**: This is the deepest philosophical wound in the Dao-Twisted World. In generic xianxia, “cultivating truth” (修真) is about seeking immortality through enlightenment. Here, the Zuowandao’s secret art is “cultivating falsehood into reality” (修假为真)—the power to make lies come true if you believe hard enough. This chapter’s reveal is that Li Huowang—or the entity wearing his face—is not just a cheat; he is a direct agent of that philosophy. The shock isn’t just about betrayal; it’s about the very nature of reality being contested.

**Face-Bending Body Horror**: The method Li Huowang uses to reshape his face—black tentacles from Li Sui, forced bone-cracking, eyeballs popping out—isn’t just gruesome for its own sake. It calls back to the earlier body-horror aesthetic of the novel: faces are not fixed, identities are not stable, and the physical self is a tool to be warped, not a sacred vessel. The “中” character as a facial mosaic is both a signature brand and a visual punchline—memorable, shocking, and deeply unnerving.

### Cultivate the False into Reality (修假为真)

The central secret art of the Zuowandao, allowing a practitioner to turn lies into truth through sheer belief and performance.

## Reading Guide

This is one of those chapters you’ll want to reread immediately after finishing, just to catch all the clues you missed. Li Huowang’s “absent-mindedness,” his sudden reluctance to engage with the political plot, his oddly eager question about whether Zhuge Yuan needed help—all of it reads differently now. The author plays fair: the behavior isn’t out of character; it’s just that the *character* is wrong. For first-time readers, the whiplash is the point—you’re meant to feel as stunned and betrayed as Zhuge Yuan does. For returning readers, savor how smoothly the Red Center impersonated Li Huowang’s voice, his paranoia, his dry concern. The Zuowandao’s power isn’t just violence; it’s performance.
