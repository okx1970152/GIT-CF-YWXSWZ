## Chapter Overview
Our weary band has finally crossed the border, leaving the surreal nightmares of Qingqiu behind for the (mostly) familiar soil of Daliang. But new places bring new faces, and not all are friendly. Li Huowang’s paranoia spikes as he deciphers the cryptic message from the mysterious black-clad lama, linking him to the death-tainted aura of the White Jade Capital. While Bai Lingmiao chatters happily about returning home, Li Huowang is already playing three steps ahead—using the ever-resourceful Lü Zhuangyuan to scout for local intel. The chapter ends with a rare, fleeting moment of visual peace: a golden harvest stretching to the horizon, promising both sustenance and the next leg of their unpredictable journey.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Lama’s Secret Revealed**: Li Huowang has a sudden, jarring awakening from a nightmare. He realizes the black-clad lama’s aura was identical to the death-aura of the White Jade Capital. This reframes the underground cauldron ritual as a sect-specific power, not a random event.
- **A New Threat Assessment**: Li Huowang concludes the lama’s sect is immensely powerful, capable of wielding death itself. He’s relieved they didn’t come to blows.
- **Domestic Moments in Chaos**: A brief, tender interaction where Li Huowang scolds Bai Lingmiao for not wearing her eye-covering, proving that even in a world of cosmic horror, the mundane problems of attraction and blindness still matter.
- **The ‘Wheat-Harvesters’**: The party encounters a group of traveling laborers (麦客) heading for the autumn harvest. Their presence signals a shift from desolate wilderness to organized agricultural society.
- **Strategic Intel Gathering**: Li Huowang delegates Lü Zhuangyuan to be his forward scout. The old troupe leader’s social skills prove invaluable, extracting information about a nearby big village (大庄子) and a county town beyond.
- **The Golden Field**: The chapter ends with a stunningly beautiful, peaceful vista of golden crops. This serves as a powerful contrast to the horror of the previous arc and a visual promise of food, rest, and a new direction.
- **A Fragile Hope**: Bai Lingmiao’s joy at being close to her homeland is palpable, adding a rare note of personal emotional stake to the group’s logistical survival.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**麦客 (Mài Kè / Wheat-Harvesters)**
These weren't just laborers; they were a unique and real social phenomenon in traditional China, especially in places like Shaanxi and Gansu. Driven by poverty, they would travel hundreds of miles on foot, carrying their own sickles and bedding, from poorer regions to richer ones to help with the harvest. Their appearance here is a masterful stroke of world-building, injecting a dose of historical authenticity into the Dao-Twisted World. It signals that beneath the supernatural chaos, the real-world cycles of agriculture and poverty still grind on.

**监天司 (Supervisory Heavenly Office)**
We finally get a clearer picture of this mysterious bureaucratic entity. It’s not a demonic sect; it’s an *office*—a government agency under the imperial board of war. But in the Dao-Twisted World, even 'law enforcement' looks weird. The Qingqiu branch employs a black-clad lama who threatens high-ranking cultivators with cryptic messages. This reinforces a key theme: the supernatural world has its own politics, and this office prioritizes *stability* over morality. It’s a supernatural version of the imperial censor, and its presence changes the game for our protagonist.

**The Golden Harvest (金灿灿的稻谷)**
The visual of a golden ocean of grain is a classical symbol of peace and prosperity in Chinese literature and painting. In a xianxia context, a good harvest means stable prices, less famine-driven chaos, and a safer environment for mortals. For Li Huowang’s group, it’s a tactical relief—food becomes cheaper and more accessible. For the reader, it’s a breathtaking respite from the claustrophobic terror of Qingqiu, a reminder that beauty and bounty can still exist in a twisted world.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a classic ‘breather’ episode, and a vital one at that. After the relentless psychic and physical pressure of the Qingqiu arc, the author gives us a chance to catch our breath. But don’t be fooled by the calm—this is a **tactical rest**. Li Huowang isn’t relaxing; he’s re-evaluating the map of the world’s power structure. The biggest takeaway? The Supervisory Heavenly Office is now on his radar, and that’s both a comfort and a curse.

Pay attention to the little details of daily life—the scolding about the eye covering, the old troupe leader negotiating with farmers. These scenes are where the novel’s soul lives. In a world of flesh-buddhas and death-aura lamas, the fact that people still worry about going blind in the sun is what makes the horror *matter*. Enjoy the golden fields while they last, fellow Daoist. You know it won’t stay this peaceful for long.
