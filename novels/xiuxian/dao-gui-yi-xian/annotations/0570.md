## Chapter Overview
And we’re back! After that heart-stopping dive into Great Qi’s ancient horrors, Li Huowang pops out of a well only to find that things have somehow gotten *even darker*—literally. The Great Qi sun is now just a ghostly outline being devoured by black rot. The Heavenly Calamity is starting up again, and Li Huowang knows he’s on the clock. His mission? Find the palm-sized, translucent baby emperor he literally stabbed and tossed into the woods last time. No pressure, right? What follows is a masterclass in trauma-hardened detective work as Li Huowang applies his Zuowandao-derived perception to the most ordinary of hunting grounds: a sleepy village full of gossiping, child-beating, ginseng-hunting peasants. Buckle up, because this chapter is all about the quiet, creeping dread of a search that’s going nowhere fast.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang emerges from the well and immediately senses the Great Qi Heavenly Calamity restarting, seeing the sun nearly consumed by black corruption. The sensation of the Yú’er Shén’s gaze forces him to retreat to Great Liang.
- He swiftly returns to the forest where he threw the Little Emperor and begins a meticulous search, using his heightened senses to track any trace.
- Finding no blood or evidence, Li Huowang expands his search and discovers an old man hiding in the bushes, who claimed he was stalking a ginseng root.
- Li Huowang forces the old man to take him to his village, which turns out to be the same one where he once traded a jade pendant for a horse.
- They arrive to find the old man’s grandson being publicly beaten with willow branches for defecating in the family well—a moment of grimly comedic folk justice.
- Li Huowang scales a tree and announces a reward of ten gold taels for finding his "premature nephew," using his Zuowandao ability to scan the villagers’ faces and their Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings for any sign of deception.
- The scan yields nothing; no one in the village has found the Little Emperor.
- The chapter ends with an old villager launching into an irrelevant, rambling story from thirty years ago, which the other villagers do their best to ignore.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a fantastic showcase of the novel’s ability to pivot from cosmic body horror to a grounded, folk-horror investigation. Li Huowang’s method of searching is pure *xianxia* detective work: he uses his Zuowandao-derived ability to sense the "Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings" (十情八苦) of the villagers. This isn’t magic mind-reading; it’s a twice-corrupted Buddhist concept that allows him to perceive the emotional "taste" of a person’s karma. If someone were hiding the emperor, their emotional signature would spike. The fact that he finds nothing is its own kind of horror—it means the trail is truly cold.

The side scene of the boy being beaten for “shitting in the well” is a staple of Chinese rural folklore. A well was a community’s lifeline, and polluting it was seen as a grave sin that could bring bad luck or illness on the entire village. The public, ritualized beating, with the father, grandfather, and even the bystanders all taking turns, is a grimly dark comedic image that grounds the supernatural story in very real, tangible village life. It’s a reminder that in the Dao-Twisted World, the horrors are often social and economic, not just monstrous.

Also worth noting: Li Huowang’s call for a *ginseng* that just “ran off” is a clever nod to the folk belief that wild ginseng is a living, semi-sentient creature that can sense being stalked and will try to escape. ## Reading Guide
Pay close attention to the mood here. After the high-octane chaos of recent chapters, this one is deliberately slow and procedural. Li Huowang isn't fighting monsters; he's tracking footprints and interrogating a terrified old man. The tension comes from the *absence* of the emperor. Where could a bleeding, half-dead baby go? The easy answer (a beast ate him) is the most horrific, but the lack of any evidence suggests something even weirder is going on. The chapter’s final image—a solitary old man telling an ignored story to the wind—perfectly encapsulates Li Huowang’s growing desperation. He’s running out of time, out of leads, and out of hope. Keep this quiet, despairing energy in mind; it’s a deliberate contrast to the explosive climax we’re all waiting for.
