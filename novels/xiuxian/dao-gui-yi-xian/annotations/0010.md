## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang spends most of the chapter in a catatonic state, curled in a corner of the ingredient storeroom after the traumatic events of the previous day. Bai Lingmiao’s gentle persistence—offering him a coarse grain bun and returning his jade pendant—gradually pulls him back. The discovery that he kicked Senior Brother Wang to death (mirroring his hallucination of killing Old Liu) deepens his existential confusion but also anchors him in the brutal reality of the Dao-Twisted World. When Bai Lingmiao asks him to deliver a message to her parents in exchange for her gold anklet, Li Huowang finds a new purpose. The chapter ends with him accepting help from a sycophantic fellow prisoner, Gouwa, and resolving to kill Danyangzi.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is discovered catatonic; Bai Lingmiao covers him with dried grass and offers food.
- She returns his jade pendant, which he remembers giving to Yang Na—this triggers a crisis of reality.
- Bai Lingmiao explains the jade pendant came from Li Huowang himself, deepening his confusion.
- Li Huowang learns he killed Senior Brother Wang with a kick, blurring the line between hallucination and reality.
- He returns Bai Lingmiao’s gold anklet, but she asks him to keep it as payment for a favor.
- Bai Lingmiao asks him to take a message to her parents on Oxheart Mountain if he escapes.
- Li Huowang accepts the task, regaining a sense of purpose.
- Gouwa, a flattering fellow prisoner, offers Li Huowang more buns and water, seeking protection.
- After eating, Li Huowang stands and fixes his mind on killing Danyangzi, shifting from despair to vengeful resolve.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Jade pendant (玉佩)**: In traditional Chinese culture, jade is not merely jewelry; it symbolizes virtue, status, and metaphysical protection. Li Huowang's confusion over the pendant's origin shows how core symbols of identity and memory have become untrustworthy.
- **Gold anklet (金脚环)**: A traditional ornament often given to daughters as a betrothal gift or family heirloom. Bai Lingmiao's willingness to part with it underscores her desperation and her trust in Li Huowang.
- **Coarse grain buns (杂粮馒头)**: A basic, rough bread made from mixed grains, common among the poor. The buns Li Huowang eats are dry and hard, emphasizing the meager, harsh conditions of their captivity.
- **Human ingredients (药引)**: Danyangzi treats his disciples and captives as raw materials for alchemy. This literal reading of 'human ingredient' turns the xianxia trope of 'elixir ingredients' into visceral horror.
- **'The third watch' (三更天)**: Traditional Chinese timekeeping divides the night into five watches. The third watch roughly corresponds to 11 p.m. to 1 a.m., often associated with supernatural activity in folklore.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a turning point for Li Huowang's character arc. From complete numbness and self-hatred, he is pulled back by the smallest act of human kindness—a bun, a word, a request. The chapter also introduces Bai Lingmiao's self-sacrificing nature: she offers her last possessions to bribe Li Huowang into a dangerous promise. Observant readers will note the parallel between the kick that killed Senior Brother Wang and the hallucination of killing Old Liu, suggesting that Li Huowang's madness and the Dao-Twisted World may be more connected than he realizes. The appearance of Gouwa, a quick-witted sycophant, hints that Li Huowang's newfound resolve may attract allies and enemies alike. The chapter ends on a note of grim determination, setting up the conflict with Danyangzi as the immediate goal.
