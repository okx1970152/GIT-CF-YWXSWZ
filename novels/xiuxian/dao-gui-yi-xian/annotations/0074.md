## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a brutal, somber inventory of loss—the aftermath of the Yuan family compound massacre. There’s no glorious victory, no triumphant plunder. Instead, we see raw, visceral grief: Li Huowang spiraling into self-harm, the desperate tally of the drowned (the Fool, a human ingredient, an acolyte—and only one lucky bastard spits his way back to life). The reed stockade reeks of death, and the psychological agony hits harder than any monster attack. The chaos has settled, but in its place creeps a cold, hollow quiet—broken only by the crackle of a campfire and the slurping of hastily boiled pork. It’s the quiet after the storm, and it stinks of regret.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang, in a fit of psychotic self-loathing, stabs his own hand through with a dagger. Bai Lingmiao stops him by grabbing the blade and embracing him, which finally calms him down. This moment highlights his fragile mental state and her role as his sole anchor.
- The group takes stock of the dead: out of twelve, five are gone. Two missing, three drowned. The Fool is among the drowned, and Li Huowang is struck by the pointless, worthless nature of their deaths.
- In a sudden, grotesque reversal, the stone-cold corpse of the Fool violently vomits lake mud and miraculously revives. The others are stunned; Dogwa jokingly praises Li Huowang’s ‘resurrection’ powers.
- Li Huowang attempts to revive the other drowned victims, but fails. Only the Fool returns, reinforcing the novel’s brutal, arbitrary luck.
- The survivors regroup in the kitchen, build a fire, butcher a pig from the stronghold, and eat. The chapter closes with Li Huowang on a sour note: noting that a bandit lair this big must have more hidden loot, his mind already shifting from mourning to practical opportunism.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- Li Huowang’s self-harm here is a significant character beat: it is a physical, intentional act of grounding himself against overwhelming psychological or spiritual pain. This mirrors a real-world psychiatric symptom and reinforces the novel’s core theme of a mind under siege.
- The sudden revival of the Fool is a classic *daoguixian* subversion of a trope. In many novels, a drowning death is permanent. Here, the corpse vomiting mud and springing back is a *visceral, humiliating resurrection*—the opposite of a glorious “ascension.” It underscores that in this world, “alive” is just a state, not a merit.
- “宰猪/butchering a pig” is a very grounded, rural practice. In this surreal, corpse-littered world, the simple act of boiling meat and sharing a fire grounds the narrative in the reality of human survival, creating a sharp contrast with the earlier horror.

## Reading Guide

- *Note:* If you were expecting a power-up or a “clean” victory feast, this chapter will make you deeply uncomfortable. Good. That’s exactly the point. The novel is punishing *us* alongside Li Huowang.
- Pay close attention to the Fool’s revival. It feels random and almost goofy, but it’s a *deliberate* narrative choice. The Dao-Twisted World doesn’t operate on clean rules—sometimes people just vomit mud and wake up. It’s the opposite of a convenient save; it’s a horrifying fluke that reminds us how cheap life is here.
- Li Huowang’s final thought about looting the stronghold is a classic sign of his survival-driven, opportunistic mindset. Even when he’s at his most broken, his brain immediately jumps to resource acquisition. That’s what keeps him alive.
- The emotional heart of the chapter is Bai Lingmiao’s simple act of holding him and saying “Please.” In a world full of monsters, fake Buddhas, and identity gaslighting, that one human touch is the only real exorcism that works on Li Huowang. Don’t take it for granted.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The survival of the Fool**: A grotesque face-slap at the genre. The resurrection is not earned—it just happens. In Xianxia, revival is a reward for cultivation. Here, it’s a biological accident of stomach compressions and lake mud. The novel mocks the very notion of a “deserved” life.
- **Dogwa’s comic relief**: Dogwa’s light-hearted reaction to the Fool’s revival provides a necessary foil to Li Huowang’s wrecked mental state, preventing the chapter from becoming an unbearable slog.
