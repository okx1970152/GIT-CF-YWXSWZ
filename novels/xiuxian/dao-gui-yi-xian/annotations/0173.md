## Chapter Overview
Hold onto your gut, because this chapter puts us inside the skull of one of the most monstrous figures in the Dao-Twisted World: Danyangzi. And guess what? He’s terrified. After escaping the caves, our favorite alchemical butcher finds himself trapped in a waking nightmare populated by the ghosts of everyone he’s ever wronged. This isn't a battle of spells and sword-qi; it’s a psychological siege. The dead disciples he crushed, the villagers he sold, the brother he betrayed—they’re all here, and they’re not impressed by his “True Immortal” act. Our guide for this horror show is Danyangzi himself, and it’s a rare, uncomfortable privilege to see the predator become the prey. Get ready for a masterclass in karmic karma, served with a side of existential dread and the final, chilling appearance of Li Huowang.

## Key Plot Points
1. Danyangzi, separated from his main body, is haunted by a vision of his former favorite disciple, Zheng Kun, whose head he personally crushed. He immediately attacks, but the vision dissipates harmlessly.
2. Fleeing the cave, Danyangzi sets up a protective Nine Palaces and Eight Trigrams Formation in a nearby ghost town of grave mounds. This is a tactical retreat, not a rout—he plans to wait for his main body.
3. Zheng Kun reappears *inside* the formation, completely unaffected by its power. His voice is fawning and familiar, which is far more terrifying than a hostile ghost.
4. A flood of apparitions follows: all his dead disciples (Xuan Yin, Changming, Changren), old rivals, victims, and betrayed allies. The voices turn from respectful greetings into a cacophony of accusations.
5. Danyangzi’s martial power proves useless. A lightning-powered strike turns the town to ash but leaves the crowd untouched. His desperation peaks when he stabs his own ears to block out the laughter, only to have it intensify.
6. The chapter culminates with Li Huowang stepping forward. He is the only one not laughing. His question—“Master, how is this disciple’s body treating you?”—directly invokes Danyangzi’s greatest fear: losing his “host” and his opportunity at immortality.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Face of One’s Karma:** This chapter is a brutal showcase of the karmic debt that drives much of the Dao-Twisted World’s horror. Danyangzi is not just haunted by guilt; he is being judged by the metaphysical weight of his own crimes. The ghosts don't need power; they just need to *be*, and their inability to be harmed reflects the nature of this tribulation. It’s a spiritual attack that no amount of Qi or cultivation can cut through.

**Ritual Precision vs. Reality Decay:** Notice how Danyangzi’s rituals are flawlessly executed: the blood talismans, the incantations, the true methods with his sword. The spells should work. He’s a master of his craft. That they have *no effect* is what makes the horror so profound. This isn’t a failure of his power; it’s a failure of his *person*. The ritual’s efficacy depends on the purity of the Daoist, and in this twisted world, impurity is a one-way ticket to damnation.

**The Laughing Judgement:** The crowd’s mockery is a particularly Chinese form of social and spiritual execution. It’s not just anger or grief; it is the loud, total rejection of a man’s entire life’s work. To be laughed at by the spirits of those you killed is the ultimate spiritual humiliation, denying Danyangzi even the dignity of being feared. This directly ties to the concept of “Face” (Mianzi), where social respect is a form of reality. If everyone thinks you are a joke, in the Dao-Twisted World, you *might just become one*.

## Reading Guide
This is a chapter to savor for its psychological tension. Pay close attention to *how* the horror is built. It doesn’t start with a scream or a jump scare. It starts with a simple, quiet greeting from a dead man. The accumulation is what makes it so chilling. Also, note the specific vocabulary of guilt: the fat man who taught him a trade, the wife he sold, the father of the family he killed. Danyangzi's path to power was paved with an endless stream of specific, named tragedies, and now they are all coming to collect. This entire sequence sets the stage for a critical moment where Danyangzi’s absolute confidence begins to crack and the line between his identity and Li Huowang’s starts to blur. The final question is a knife-edge—it confirms the nature of the trap but doesn't solve it. Enjoy the show; you won’t see a more satisfying spiritual beatdown in a while.

## Style & Horror Notes
When a character’s identity is shattered by revelation, the narrative holds a tight focus on their cognitive dissonance. Danyangzi cannot process the failure of his art; he knows the spells, he feels the power, but the world has stopped obeying him. His terror is born not from the enemy’s strength, but from the collapse of his own understanding of reality. This is a textbook example of how to make a powerful monster feel small.
