## Chapter Overview

Well, well—turns out the plot was thicker than a bowl of winter stew. Our boy Li Huowang, fresh from the revelation that Bai Lingmiao's cozy family portrait may have been… *a little* retouched, decides to march straight into the reeds to demand a happy family reconciliation. Spoiler: Nobody wants to reconcile. The Bai clan, possessed by some very unfriendly spirits, greet him with a full-contact demonstration of "we don't trust you, your Office token, or your stupid face." This chapter serves up a brutal reality check: the in-laws from hell are literal. What follows is a tense, claustrophobic confrontation where Li Huowang is forced to hold back his lethal training while the Bai family pulls out folk-possession tactics and even the **Wandering Lord** makes a ghostly cameo. It’s family drama with body horror, and nobody brought a casserole.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is stopped by the masked Office agent, who warns him that the Bai family now knows Li Huowang is connected to the Office.
- Despite the warning, Li Huowang’s trust in Bai Lingmiao’s description of her family overrides his caution. He enters the reeds alone, calling out for a peaceful talk.
- The Bai family sends out two young men who perform a naked ritual chant, summoning flashy silhouettes to possess them.
- The possessed youths attack with unsettling, segmented movements—their fingers writhing like independent snakes.
- Li Huowang avoids using lethal force, but when they grab him and an ally tries to stab him with the **purple-tasseled sword**, he is forced to explode his **bronze coin sword**, severing their fingers in self-defense.
- The chapter ends with a wider reveal: nearly half the Bai clan has been possessed. The single-toothed elder, supported by Bai Sai, begins to chant, summoning a blurry but recognizable version of the **Wandering Lord**.
- Li Huowang connects the dots—this elder is the grandfather who told Bai Lingmiao the stories about the Wandering Lord, implying her seemingly innocent knowledge was a family inheritance of horror.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### Divine Possession vs. Generic Exorcism
The Bai family uses a chant that explicitly calls on *all the gods* to enter the mortal body. This is a stylized version of **spirit-possession (神打 / shén dǎ)** where a practitioner invites a celestial or martial spirit into their own body to gain combat prowess. The key feature here is the *visible process*—a shimmering silhouette rising from the ground and merging with the host. In real-world folk practice, this is often an ecstatic trance state; the novel literalizes it into a visible, hostile entity transfer.

### The Wandering Lord's Return
Yes, the **Wandering Lord** is back. But notice the condition—no bell, no vibration, just a blurry shape. The elder’s chant produces a pale echo of the full entity, confirming that even without the summoner’s ritual tool, the knowledge of how to call it exists independently within the Bai clan. This retroactively reframes Bai Lingmiao’s earlier knowledge—she wasn’t just a curious girl; she was the granddaughter of a man who could command evil spirits.

### The Bronze Coin Sword’s Explosive Mode
Earlier in the novel, the bronze coin sword could scatter and reform as a flexible whip. Here, Li Huowang uses a specific activation word (“Azina lingguang dashe”) to *detonate* the sword’s coins as a cutting net. This marks a tactical evolution—the sword is not just a melee weapon but a versatile tool with explosive options, at the cost of losing the binding structure temporarily. A reminder that every tool in the Dao-Twisted World has both utility and cost.

## Reading Guide

If you came here expecting Li Huowang to finally have a quiet, normal conversation with his girlfriend’s family… welp, sorry, you picked the wrong novel. This chapter is all about *the reveal that his trust was misplaced*—not in Bai Lingmiao, but in her perfectly normal-storybook family. The shift from “let’s talk it out” to “everyone around me is possessed and trying to kill me” happens almost in a single breath. Pay attention to the old man with one tooth. He doesn’t just know the chant; he wears it like a family heirloom. That moment when Li Huowang realizes the source of Bai Lingmiao’s harmless little bit of lore is the same mouth that’s now summoning the Wandering Lord—that’s the kind of creeping horror *Dao Gui Yi Xian* does best. Also: Li Huowang holding back his killing moves while getting swarmed? Chef’s kiss. He is no longer a desperate survivor lashing out blindly. He’s a restrained fighter trying *not* to massacre his girlfriend’s relatives. Character growth, baby.
