## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang’s plan pays off: the jade pendant from the hallucinatory world is appraised at 400,000 yuan, turning a dreamlike asset into real money. His excitement clashes with Yang Na’s concern for his health. But the celebration is cut short when an escaped patient attacks Yang Na. Desperate to protect her, Li Huowang consumes one of Danyangzi’s pills, unleashing a brutal strength that quickly turns from defense to rage. The chapter ends with Yang Na screaming for him to stop, highlighting the thin line between empowerment and loss of control.

## Key Plot Points

- Yang Na confirms the jade pendant’s authenticity and value; Li Huowang feels validated for the first time.
- Li Huowang vows to continue treatment despite dreaming of using the money to reduce hospital stays, but Yang Na insists on his health first.
- The hospital goes on alert when Old Liu escapes the locked ward.
- Old Liu and a fat orderly attack Yang Na; Li Huowang is knocked down by a thrown paving stone.
- In desperation, Li Huowang takes one of Danyangzi’s pills, gaining superhuman strength that lets him brutally beat both attackers.
- Yang Na’s horrified cry ends the chapter, signaling that Li Huowang’s newfound power may be slipping beyond his control.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The pill Li Huowang consumes is likely a form of crude External Alchemy (外丹) — a Daoist tradition of ingesting mineral or herbal concoctions to gain supernatural strength, longevity, or heightened senses. In folklore, such pills often came with dangerous side effects: toxicity, madness, or irreversible bodily change. Here, the pill bridges the hallucinatory world and reality, blurring the line that Li Huowang desperately tries to maintain. The riot fork (防暴叉) is a standard tool in Chinese psychiatric hospitals for restraining violent patients, adding realistic detail to the modern setting.

### The Bond Between Li Huowang and Yang Na

Yang Na represents Li Huowang’s last anchor to normalcy. Unlike the hospital staff or his parents, she believes him—at least enough to help. Her insistence on continuing treatment despite the money shows she prioritizes his health over wealth. The chapter reinforces that this relationship is his most important connection, and his violent outburst is triggered by the threat to her safety. The contrast between her gentle, caring demeanor and his blood-soaked rage underscores the cost of crossing between worlds.

## Reading Guide

Li Huowang's shift from joyful disbelief to violent protector mirrors his growing willingness to use the hallucinatory world’s resources for real-world problems. Yet his anger escalates beyond self-defense into near-lethal rage, worrying both Yang Na and the reader. Note how the phrase “I’m not sick” turns from a cry of relief into a warning sign as his actions grow increasingly unhinged. The chapter asks: if power crosses over between worlds, can the user control where it leads?
