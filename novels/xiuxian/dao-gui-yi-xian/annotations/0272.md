## Chapter Overview

If you thought body horror was just about tentacles and gore, welcome to Pi County. This chapter peels back the lid on the town’s central terror, and it’s not a monster lurking in the shadows. It’s an epidemic of existential dread that crawls under the skin—literally. Li Huowang, invisible and shivering, witnesses two starkly different but horribly mirrored scenes: a wealthy family torturing their own son to stop his manhood from vanishing, and a poor couple locked in a desperate, painful argument over the same terrifying process. What unites them is a shared, obsessive fear of their own bodies betraying them. Our boy Li Huowang is officially out of his depth. He doesn’t know what causes this *retraction*, and the locals, locked in a shroud of terrified silence, won’t tell him. Frustrated and running out of patience, he realizes the clock is ticking, and being polite isn’t an option anymore. Get ready for a cold, calculated shift in strategy.

## Key Plot Points

*   Li Huowang, still invisible, observes a magistrate’s family physically restraining and torturing their son. The father is about to surgically insert a “lock” into the boy’s body to prevent his “bird” (penis) from shrinking away.
*   The boy’s desperate pleas to “not be a man” are heartbreakingly rejected. The lock is presented as a brutal but necessary last resort to preserve his gender identity and future.
*   Startled by Li Huowang’s “presence,” the magistrate screams for guards, convinced “that thing” has arrived.
*   Li Huowang then witnesses a poorer couple’s panic. The husband is terrified his penis is shrinking, while his wife reveals she must also lock her breasts with a rusted, bloody iron chain to prevent them from “shrinking in.”
*   Li Huowang connects the dots. This isn’t just a male problem. It’s a systemic, town-wide affliction of both sexes—a bizarre, collective *genital retraction*.
*   Realizing he can get no information through stealth or goodwill, Li Huowang hardens his resolve. He decides to abandon soft tactics and use “force” to squeeze answers out of the locals.
*   Returning to the inn, he observes the Lü troupe performing an erotic play for a sparse audience. He coldly orders Gouwa to summon the waiter to his room, signaling the beginning of his aggressive interrogation plan.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Genital Retraction Syndrome (缩阳症, Suo Yang Zheng):** This chapter is a masterclass in weaponizing a real-world cultural phenomenon. *Suo Yang Zheng*—also known as *Koro*—is a genuine psychiatric or culture-bound syndrome predominantly reported in parts of East and Southeast Asia. The sufferer experiences an intense, panicked belief that their genitals are retracting into their body, and that fatal death will follow if they fully disappear. The treatments are as terrifying as the belief itself: herbal remedies, but also physical restraints like ropes, pegs, and even surgical “locks” to physically anchor the organ. The novel brilliantly transposes this clinical anxiety into a supernatural curse, turning a mass-psychogenic illness into an epidemic of collective dread. It’s not a monster you can stab; it’s a fear you can’t prove wrong.

**The Wu Zetian Fallacy:** This seems like an odd name, but it fits. There’s a persistent myth (often falsely attributed to the ruthless Empress Wu Zetian) where she collected men for stamina tonics. The core idea—that male vitality is a finite, consumable resource—underpins many “retraction” beliefs. This chapter modernizes it: the body is not just under attack, but is *being subtracted from*. The horror is that this isn't a quick death, but a slow, humiliating erasure of one’s very identity as a man or a woman.

### Suo Yang Zheng (缩阳症)

A culture-bound syndrome where a person experiences an intense fear that their genitals are retracting into their body, often believed to be fatal. Traditional “cures” include physical restraints and locks.

### Genital retraction

The central horror of this chapter, a literal and terrifying shrinkage of body parts affecting both men and women, fueled by a collective panic.

## Reading Guide

Okay, fellow Daoists, switch your brains into puzzle-solving mode. This arc is a different beast. Forget going nova with the Thousand Greats Record. Li Huowang is dealing with a curse that has *no punchable face*. The genius here is how the horror is social and systemic. It’s not a ghost haunting a house; it’s a fear haunting an entire population. Their silence isn’t rudeness; it’s a survival mechanism. In a world where admitting truth might make it real, nobody wants to be the one to break the spell.

Pay attention to Li Huowang’s shift in tactics here. He’s not just lashing out. He’s being cold, methodical, and threatening. He’s going to interrogate the waiter, probably the weakest link in the town’s chain of silence. This is the new Li Huowang—the one who uses his terrifying presence as a weapon instead of a liability. The chapter ends on a quiet, menacing note that promises things are about to get very, very messy. Strap in.

## Deeper Lore: The Geography of Fear

Pi County isn't just a random town. Its very emptiness is a clue. The lack of records, the silence of the magistrate, the universal terror—it points to a curse that isn’t supernatural in the normal sense. Think of it as a *miasma* of belief. The Dao-Twisted World often operates on consensus reality. If everyone believes the yang energy of the town is being drained, then technically, it *is* being drained. The horror is that Li Huowang might be as vulnerable to this belief-loophole as anyone else.
