## Chapter Overview

Welcome back to the quiet (and not-so-quiet) rhythms of Niuxin Village! This chapter is a wonderful breather after all the mountain-hopping body-horror—a chance to see how Li Huowang’s motley crew has settled into their new home. But even as morning dawns with roosters crowing and breakfast porridge steaming, some things never change: Lü Zhuangyuan is still shouting at his family, Lü Xiucai is still seething with resentment, and Li Huowang is still covered in blood scabs at the breakfast table. Yet beneath the domestic comedy lurks a genuine surprise—an old acquaintance from the Supervisory Heavenly Office has tracked him down, bringing a fleeting moment of camaraderie from a shared, bloody past. Get ready for a chapter that’s equal parts slice-of-life, simmering tension, and a very familiar face in a wooden mask.

## Key Plot Points

- Morning routine in Niuxin Village: Lü Zhuangyuan wakes his entire household and his apprentices with his signature loud and grumpy style.
- Luo Juan (his daughter-in-law) complains about the free breakfast arrangement; Lü Zhuangyuan shuts her down harshly.
- At the Bai family compound, Lü Xiucai’s presence immediately provokes his father’s open hostility, while the son mutters threats under his breath.
- Gouwa arrives with his heavily pregnant wife, proudly announcing she’s full-term; he needles Lü Xiucai about having no son, sparking a tense exchange.
- Li Huowang comes down for breakfast, his skinless appearance now taken for granted by the group.
- Bai Lingmiao nags him about eating too fast, being covered in blood, and scaring her at night.
- After a hasty meal, Li Huowang heads up Niuxin Mountain with Li Sui in tow—but stops short when white mist rolls in.
- From the fog emerges Liu Zongyuan, a fellow Office operative from the Heart-Turbid hunt. Only two of the original five are still “around.”
- Liu Zongyuan explains he came on his own initiative to investigate signs of a new White Lotus settlement; no official errand ties to Li Huowang.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Three-meal vs. Two-meal culture**: Historically, many commoners in pre-modern China ate only two meals a day (breakfast and an early supper). Three meals were a luxury for harvest season or the wealthy. Niuxin Village offering free breakfasts to everyone is treated as an extraordinary act of generosity, reinforcing Bai Lingmiao’s benevolent status.
- **“A flood washing out the Dragon King’s temple” (大水冲了龙王庙)**: A classic Chinese idiom meaning that a conflict has erupted between people who are actually on the same side. The Dragon King (龙王) is a powerful water deity; even his temple wouldn’t be spared by the flood. It’s a folksy, almost jocular way to say “what a terrible misunderstanding.”
- **Begging for a title (讨口封)**: While not used in this chapter directly, the broader folk horror context is worth noting. In Chinese folklore, a spirit or demon may ask a human to confirm its nature or level, and the human’s answer can empower or bind it. Li Huowang’s past with such traps (the two-faced woman) makes every new encounter a potential threshold.

### Private slave (私奴)

A bonded laborer in historical Chinese society, tied to a household or land; not equivalent to chattel slavery, but still reflecting a rigid class hierarchy.

## Reading Guide

This is a classic “calm before the storm” chapter. The tone shifts from domestic comedy (Lü Zhuangyuan banging on doors, Gouwa bragging about his son, Bai Lingmiao’s affectionate nagging) to a quiet but pointed reunion with an old ally. Keep an eye on the subtle power dynamics: Lü Xiucai’s growing resentment and his purchased wife’s silent thoughts suggest future trouble. And the appearance of Liu Zongyuan—a survivor of the same doomed mission—reminds us that the Office’s eyes are always watching, even when there’s no formal assignment. The chapter’s real value lies in establishing that Niuxin Village is becoming a known node in the supernatural world’s web. Li Huowang’s cover is not as clean as he’d like to think.
