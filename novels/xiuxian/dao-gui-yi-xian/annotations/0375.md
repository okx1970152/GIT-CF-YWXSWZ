## Chapter Overview

Ah, the sweet smell of bureaucratic horse-trading in a demon-haunted world! This chapter is a masterclass in *quid pro quo* as Li Huowang and the Chief Recorder try to get the prison's help and instead get handed a side-quest. Forget monsters and blood rituals for a moment—the real horror here is government red tape. Commander Cao won't lift a finger unless Li Huowang does *him* a "small favor" first. And what's that favor? Escorting a prisoner for the military. The chapter then pivots into a poignant farewell, where the Chief Recorder, fearing the coming chaos in the capital, makes his exit while leaving Li Huowang with a promotion, a token of command, and a terrifyingly valuable artifact. It’s a quiet, tense chapter that sets the board for the next move, reminding us that in the Dao-Twisted World, every favor has a hidden cost, and every gift might be a goodbye.

## Key Plot Points

- Commander Cao accepts incense to the prison god but refuses Li Huowang's lifespan pellets, demanding that Li Huowang first do *him* a "small favor" before he'll help with Bai Lingmiao's condition.
- The "small favor" is revealed to be escorting a prisoner for the military; the Chief Recorder assures Li Huowang it will not harm his official career as long as he doesn't seek promotion.
- Back at the courtyard, Li Huowang confronts the Chief Recorder about his intention to leave, extracting a frank confession about the danger brewing in the capital and the Chief Recorder’s decision to flee for his life.
- The Chief Recorder promotes Li Huowang to the Gengqi rank, giving him a new token that grants him autonomy to choose jobs from the Supervisory Heavenly Office and command lower-ranked operatives.
- The Chief Recorder reveals his final gift: the rotted-wood Ruyi with its Heart-Element tongue, a priceless artifact he has left behind for Li Huowang.
- In a quiet epilogue, the Chief Recorder reunites with his gray-haired sister and departs the capital by carriage, his debts and favors neatly squared away.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Prison God (狱神)** – Many traditional Chinese prisons had a small shrine dedicated to a "Prison God," often believed to be a deified historical figure like Xiao He, the Han dynasty chancellor who codified laws. It’s a piece of folk religion you’d only find in the grimmest of places, adding a layer of grim piety to Commander Cao’s character—even a jailer has his rituals.

**The *Bi'an* and *Pixiu* (狴犴与貔貅)** – The Chief Recorder’s insult is pure gold. The *bi'an* is a mythical creature said to be the seventh son of the dragon, often carved on prison gates to symbolize justice and oversight. The *pixiu* is a greedy, mouth-only beast that eats everything and never excretes—a symbol of insatiable greed. Telling a prison to replace its *bi'an* with a *pixiu* is a devastating burn, accusing them of being all take and no give.

**The Art of Saying "But" (打官腔)** – This chapter perfectly captures a classic Chinese bureaucratic move: agree enthusiastically to a request ("Of course I'll help!"), let a pregnant pause hang, then drop the killer word "but" (可是) to introduce a new condition. It’s a negotiation tactic as old as the imperial examination system itself, and seeing it used in a demon-infested fantasy world is a deliciously grounded touch.

### Rotten-wood Ruyi (朽木如意)

A corrupted sacred artifact in the Dao-Twisted World, consisting of a dried Heart-Element tongue nailed to a piece of rotting wood. It retains dangerous cognitive or spiritual properties.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a tonal shift, fellow Daoists. After the visceral horror of the Flesh Buddhas and the mental warfare of the Zuowandao, this chapter is a slow-burn political interlude. Don't let the lack of action fool you—the information here is critical. The Chief Recorder's farewell is a masterful piece of character work. He knows the capital is about to become a "live game of chess," as he implied earlier, and he's cashing out. The promotion to Gengqi and the gift of the Ruyi aren't just charity; they're him making sure his investment (Li Huowang) is well-positioned. Pay close attention to the mention of "His Majesty" and the trouble brewing in the northeast—these are major plot seeds being planted. And what is this mysterious military escort mission? The way everyone is brushing it off as "nothing important" is the biggest red flag of all.
