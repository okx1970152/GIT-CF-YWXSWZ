## Chapter Overview

No grand exorcism. No cosmic battle. Instead, this chapter pulls us into the quiet, gnawing aftermath of Bai Lingmiao's absence—and what *she's* doing while she's away is suddenly the biggest unspoken question. Coward Gao Zhijian, the gentle giant with mud between his ears, is sneaking off to cultivate a military manual that should have gone to the grave with its original owner. Chun Xiaoman is practically vibrating with anxiety, trying to chant her way into a spirit-medium breakthrough and failing. And then a letter arrives: Bai Lingmiao is in Shangjing, she's fine, and she's busy. But whose handwriting is on the page? Oh, just Li Huowang's. No big deal. (It's a very big deal.) Get ready for a chapter that weaponizes domestic calm like a knife hidden in a steamed bun.

## Key Plot Points

- Gao Zhijian is secretly training in a military cultivator's art, lifted from Peng Longteng's possessions. The blood-sealed text only reveals its true content when smeared with fresh blood; his qi circulation produces visible blood-qi and an insect-dropping glare of killing intent. His cover story: "I test dangerous things first so I can warn the others."
- Chun Xiaoman chants a protective verse (*二十四味诸天扶助*) inside the hidden hall beneath the Bai family shrine, trying to awaken her spirit-medium abilities. She fails, gets impatient, and only stops short at Gao Zhijian's insistent "Eat first" routine.
- A letter from Bai Lingmiao arrives, carried by the ever-sycophantic Gouwa. Chun Xiaoman's months of dread dissolve into relief—until Gao Zhijian identifies the handwriting as Li Huowang's. The chapter ends with Gouwa's laughing jab about "newlyweds fighting and making up," leaving the reader to sit with the implications.
- The domestic vignettes—Yang Xiaohai managing the kitchen crew, a dog missing its master, the quiet routine of a base without its core figure—paint a picture of a team holding together but fraying at the edges.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Military Cultivation (*兵家修炼*)** is a distinct path in Chinese xianxia lore, separate from Daoist internal alchemy or Buddhist cultivation. It draws from the *Military Strategy School (兵家, Bing Jia)* of the Warring States period—think Sun Tzu's *Art of War*—but twists it into a supernatural discipline. The core resource here isn't qi but *killing intent (杀意, sha yi)*, a palpable force generated by slaughter, battlefield rage, and the will to dominate. Gao Zhijian's blood-sealed manual is straight out of this tradition: it demands blood to activate, and its visual effect—red-tinged qi, a glare that kills insects—is a classic sign that the practitioner is condensing battle-field essence into raw psychic pressure. This is not spiritual enlightenment; it's learning to become a living weapon.

**The Protective Verse Chun Xiaoman Chants**: The *二十四味诸天扶助* incantation belongs to the *Bang Bing Jue* tradition in Chinese folk spirit-mediumship—specifically the northeastern *chu ma* or "flag-striking" school. These are not elegant Daoist incantations; they're earthy, rhythmic summons to local tutelary spirits, often using visceral imagery (iron rulers, dragging blades, goose feathers turning soft from hard). The "twenty-four flavors of heavens" (*二十四味诸天*) is a folk-Buddhist/Daoist hybrid concept where different heavenly departments are analogized to medical ingredients—each "flavor" corresponds to a specific celestial bureau or protective spirit. Chun Xiaoman's failure to activate it suggests she hasn't yet achieved the correct state of belief, a classic hurdle in possession-based cultivation paths.

### Bang Bing Jue (帮兵决)

A traditional northeastern Chinese spirit-summoning verse; its rapid, earthy rhythm belongs to the chuma medium tradition, using visceral imagery to call local tutelary spirits.

### blood-sealed text

A cultivation manual that only reveals its true content when smeared with fresh blood; common in forbidden or military cultivation paths.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *showing without telling*. Gao Zhijian trains in secret. Chun Xiaoman chants alone until she chokes. A dog misses Li Huowang. A letter arrives in his handwriting but with Bai Lingmiao's name on it. Nobody shouts; nobody monologues about how worried they are. The horror here is structural: someone is missing, someone else is changing in the dark, and the letter that should be a relief is actually a question mark dressed up as news. Pay attention to Gao Zhijian's line about testing dangerous things before the others—this is the same gentle soul who used to pull a cart for the group. The fact that he's hiding a military cultivation breakthrough is a ticking clock. And that handwriting? You already know it means more than a simple "she's fine."
