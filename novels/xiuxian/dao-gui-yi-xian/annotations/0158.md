## Chapter Overview

Get ready for one of the most psychologically brutal chapters in the Dao-Twisted World so far. Li Huowang’s grip on reality is systematically dismantled as he’s told that the monk traveling with him—the one who’s been whispering "you're a delusion named Peng Zhi" for the last arc—was invisible to absolutely everyone else. The result is a raw, violent, and deeply existential confrontation where Li Huowang doesn’t just fight an enemy; he has to affirm his own identity against a world that keeps telling him he isn’t real. This is a chapter about will, not logic.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang frantically asks his companions if they see the two monks (the one beside him and the one in the distance), only to be told there are *zero* monks inside the wagon circle.
- Yang Xiaohai tearfully confesses that Li Huowang "often talks to people nobody else can see," shattering Li Huowang’s confidence in distinguishing reality from illusion.
- The false monk (who has been impersonating a wise advisor) seizes the moment to push the "Peng Zhi" narrative: Li Huowang is a delusional fragment named Peng Zhi from the Three Corpses myth, and should surrender his identity.
- Li Huowang tests the false monk's claim by passing his hand through the distant monk, confirming it is a hallucination—but instead of breaking down, he *attacks the advisor monk anyway*.
- In a stunning act of pure existential defiance, Li Huowang declares he doesn't need evidence or logic to be Li Huowang. He stabs the advisor through the gut, rejecting the "Peng Zhi" label completely.
- The dead monk's body twists into a square, mahjong-tile face, revealing her true identity: Two Dot of the Zuowandao. The entire "cutting the Three Corpses" trap was a lie.
- Two Dot escapes as it becomes clear she orchestrated all the bandits and Nuo performers; the chapter ends with Li Huowang's furious realization that the whole ordeal was staged by the deception sect.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Three Corpses (三尸 / San Shi) and Peng Zhi (彭质):** This chapter plays directly with a core Daoist concept. The Three Corpses are three parasitic "corpse demons" said to reside in every human's body—one in the head (Peng Zhi), one in the chest (Peng Ju), and one in the belly (Peng Jiao). They crave the host's death so they can roam free, and they are blamed for tempting humans toward greed, lust, and delusion. In Daoist cultivation, "cutting off the Three Corpses" through fasting and meditation is a prerequisite for immortality. The Zuowandao weaponizes this very real mythology against Li Huowang: by calling him "Peng Zhi," they frame his identity itself as a parasitic delusion that must be eliminated. Knowing this context makes Li Huowang’s defiant "I don’t care if you’re Peng Jiao" even more powerful—he is rejecting an entire religious framework that would deny his very being.

**Zuowandao (坐忘道) and the Mahjong Face:** The sect’s members are named after mahjong tiles. "Two Dot" is one of the lower suit tiles (the dots/circles suit), and their faces can reshape into these geometric patterns during transformations. This visual cue—a face turning into a tile with two round circles—is a signature calling card, signalling that the Zuowandao’s power is tied to deception, games, and luck manipulation. The author's choice of mahjong tiles as identifiers is deliberate: mahjong is a game of chance, skill, and hidden information, perfectly mirroring the sect’s philosophy that reality is a game to be played with.

**The "Li Huowang" Identity as a Battlefield:** This chapter marks a critical turning point. Up until now, Li Huowang has doubted his sanity. But here, he makes a *meta* decision: even if all evidence points to him being "Peng Zhi"—a delusion demon—he will *will himself* into being Li Huowang. This is not rational; it’s an act of pure phenomenological defiance, reminiscent of existentialist philosophy ("existence precedes essence"). In the Dao-Twisted World, where belief can rewrite reality, this may be the most powerful move he could make.

## Reading Guide

This is a chapter you read with your heart pounding, not your head analyzing. The Zuowandao’s trap is brilliant because it uses a real-world religious concept (the Three Corpses) to gaslight Li Huowang into questioning his own existence. The genius of the payoff isn’t that Li Huowang finds the "real" truth, but that he *chooses* to be himself despite the truth. That moment—"I don’t need any reason!"—is a thesis statement for the entire novel. Pay close attention to the way Danyangzi’s three-faced form watches from the sidelines with unimpressed commentary; his presence is a reminder that even when Li Huowang wins against one deceiver, older, deeper monsters are still lurking. The scene also reveals a dark new impulse in Li Huowang: after affirming his identity, his mind jumps to "if the *other* Li Huowang in the modern world exists, I’ll kill him and take his place." The protagonist is becoming more dangerous to himself.
