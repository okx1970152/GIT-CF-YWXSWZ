## Chapter Overview

Mistaken for troublemakers, Bai Lingmiao and Chun Xiaoman get the cold shoulder from an old escort chief whose wife has been raging in a locked room for three decades. Just when it looks like their good intentions have backfired, the arrival of the silent, red-veiled Er Shen (Second Spirit) sends the hardened bodyguards scrambling for their apotropaic ink-lines and black dogs. A dramatic about-face from the chief follows once a whisper reveals the girls are spirit-dancers, turning their polite dismissal into a desperate plea for help. This chapter beautifully showcases how folk ritual knowledge can flip social power in the Dao-Twisted World.

## Key Plot Points

- Zhao Qin, the head of the escort agency, initially refuses Chun Xiaoman's offer to help his possessed wife, believing she is beyond cure.
- Bai Lingmiao’s pity for the screaming woman leads her to press the issue, which nearly results in a violent confrontation.
- The sudden, eerie appearance of the Er Shen (Second Spirit) in her red wedding veil causes panic, and the bodyguards deploy folk countermeasures: ink-lines and black dogs.
- Bai Lingmiao clarifies the Er Shen is her harmless ‘sister’ and attempts to leave, but Zhao Qin’s attitude shifts drastically upon learning they are spirit-dancers.
- He invites them back with full ceremony and respect after they return with their senior brothers.
- The final scene depicts the possessed wife: an old woman chained in a padded room, having been in this state for three decades.
- Bai Lingmiao initiates the summoning ritual: she removes the Er Shen's outer veil, places it on her own head, hands over the drum, and the Er Shen begins the chant with a single drum tap.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Jumping the Gods / Tiao Da Shen (跳大神):** This is a northern Chinese shamanistic ritual, distinct from Daoist liturgy. A ‘Lead Spirit’ (大神) acts as a medium, possessed by a spirit, while a ‘Second Spirit’ (二神) plays a hand-drum and guides the ceremony with chants. It’s a folk practice that blends exorcism, healing, and communication with the spirit world. In the Dao-Twisted World, the Er Shen is often a silent, veiled figure—an eerie embodiment of the liminal space between the living and the dead.

**Apotropaic Measures: Ink-Lines and Black Dogs (墨斗与黑狗):** In traditional Chinese folk religion and Fengshui, an ink-line (墨斗) soaked in cinnabar or black ink is used for marking straight lines, but is imbued with power to exorcise demonic entities. Black dogs, specifically those with black fur and no other colors, are believed to be powerful spiritual guardians whose barking or blood can ward off evil. The swift deployment of these items by the bodyguards shows the deeply embedded folk knowledge of the common people.

**The Red Wedding Veil (红盖头):** In Chinese wedding tradition, the red veil is a symbol of chastity and transition. In the Dao-Twisted World, it takes on a morbid significance. It’s used here to conceal the true nature of the Er Shen, almost like a shroud. When Bai Lingmiao places it on her own head to perform the ritual, she is symbolically transforming herself into the vessel for the spirit—a chilling inversion of a wedding.

### Tiao Da Shen (跳大神)

A northern Chinese folk shamanistic ritual. A "Lead Spirit" (大神) becomes possessed by a spirit, while a "Second Spirit" (二神) plays a drum and guides the ceremony. It is a form of exorcism and healing, deeply rooted in local supernatural traditions.

### Apotropaic

Any object or rite intended to ward off evil or bad luck. The ink-line and black dogs used by the bodyguards are classic examples of apotropaic folk magic.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in "the social power of the folk practitioner." Watch how Bai Lingmiao and Chun Xiaoman go from being dismissed as clueless girls to revered spiritual experts, all because of a single whispered word. Also, take note of the 30-year timeframe. Zhao Qin’s suffering isn't a recent incident—it’s a lifelong burden, which deepens the tragedy of his initial hostility and the desperation of his final plea. The appearance of the Er Shen is perfectly timed—not to attack, but to act as silent proof of their expertise, turning the bodyguards' fear into respect at the last possible moment. And keep an eye on that final ritual tableau—the drum, the veil, the silent Second Spirit tapping the skin. It’s pure, uncanny folk horror.
