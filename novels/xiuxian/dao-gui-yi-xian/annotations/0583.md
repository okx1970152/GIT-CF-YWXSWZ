## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers the payoff of the Supreme Extremity Confluence in spectacular—and literally headless—fashion. Li Huowang's desperate consumption of Shangguan Yuting's entire head grants him a moment of terrifying cosmic clarity, where the true nature of Simings, Heart-Pans, and the Chessboard of the gods is laid bare. But awakening to higher truth comes at a cost: his own head explodes, reducing him to little more than a mouth on a tentacled stump. The chapter balances visceral body-horror with a massive lore dump, revealing that both Doumu AND Bashe have been angling to make Li Huowang their Heart-Pan all along. Meanwhile, Bai Lingmiao’s White Lotus powers fully manifest—she splits into two upper bodies, chants alongside her Second Spirit, and chews through Martial’s elites like a dual-faced goddess of war. Get ready, because this is the chapter where the universe's true game board is finally shown, and Li Huowang is not a player—he is the prize.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang completes his consumption of Shangguan Yuting, achieving a transcendental flash of insight from the Supreme Extremity Confluence. His head swells, shrinks, then explodes, leaving only a mouth behind.
- Through this vision, Li Huowang deduces the core truth of the Dao-Twisted World: Simings exist through traces left in the past, and changing one's past is a way to change their future—making past and future fungible for divine beings.
- He realizes his identity as "Red Center" was granted by Doumu, which retroactively altered his own history. The Four-Qi Supervisor's earlier words on karma are validated: without causal connection, there is no karma.
- Li Huowang sees Doumu's Eye of the Mountain God in the sky and understands the trap: if he becomes her Heart-Pan, Doumu is essentially his own future self, making the crisis solvable through a predestined path.
- More chillingly, he recognizes that Bashe has been playing the same game from the start—ever since he touched the *Thousand Greats Record*, both Simings have been competing to claim him as their Heart-Pan.
- Bai Lingmiao triggers "Rise, Possessed Medium" (起乩), splitting her upper body into two halves—one merciful, one savage—and fighting alongside her Second Spirit with wolf-bone attacks, buying time for Li Huowang.
- The Dice's taunting ("Your head really is gone!") contrasts the chapter's grim revelations with a touch of Zuowandao-style mockery, leaving Li Huowang's fate uncertain.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Mandate of Heaven and Siming Economics**
This chapter reveals that Simings (司命), the high-dimensional fate-arbiters of the Dao-Twisted World, are not eternal beings. They exist through *traces*—causal imprints left in the world's past. If those traces are erased, the Siming vanishes. This is a masterful corruption of Buddhist-dependent origination (缘起) and Daoist concepts of destiny (命). Li Huowang's realization that "changing the past changes the future for gods" turns the entire novel's metaphysics into an arms race over *history as territory*.

**The Heart-Pan Trap (心蟠)**
The term "Heart-Pan" (心蟠) is explicitly positioned here as a causal anchor—a mortal whose existence serves as a Siming's "seed" or "past" in the mortal realm. Doumu and Bashe both want Li Huowang as their Heart-Pan, which would essentially merge his identity with theirs. This is not mere possession; it is a retroactive rewrite of reality where the mortal becomes the god's younger self. The implications are staggering—and horrifying.

**Possessed Mediums (乩童)**
Bai Lingmiao's "Rise, Possessed Medium" (起乩) ritual draws from real Chinese folk religion. A possessed medium (乩童, jītóng) is a practitioner who invites a deity into their body, often through self-mutilation (striking the forehead with a knife, as shown here) to prove the deity's presence. The body becomes impervious to pain as a sign of divine possession. Bai Lingmiao's dual-body manifestation, with the Second Spirit chanting from beneath the red veil, elevates this folk practice into full-blown supernatural warfare.

**The Dice's Taunt**
The Dice (骰子), a high-ranking Zuowandao member possessing Gao Zhijian's body, continues his signature blend of casual mockery and tactical provocation. Even when Li Huowang's head is literally gone, the Dice's instinct is to weaponize the absurdity of the situation—a perfect reflection of the Zuowandao's philosophy that even truth can be twisted into a lie if delivered with enough bravado.

## Reading Guide

This is a lore-dense chapter—one of those rare moments where the fog lifts, and the map of the Dao-Twisted World's true cosmology is revealed. If you've been confused about what Simings actually want, or why Li Huowang has been such a hot commodity, this is where everything clicks. But be warned: the clarity is a double-edged sword. The more Li Huowang understands the cosmic game, the more he realizes he was never really a player. The revelation that both Doumu AND Bashe have been angling for him from early on recontextualizes the entire story. His laugh—"I'm still a hot commodity around here, huh?"—is tragic, defiant, and darkly funny all at once. Also, props to Bai Lingmiao for becoming an absolute terror on the battlefield; her dual-faced, wolf-bone-wielding form is one of the most visually striking moments since Zhengde Temple's Flesh Buddha. Finally, the chapter's cliffhanger—Li Huowang reduced to a headless, tentacled mouth still screaming defiance—is pure *Dao Gui Yi Xian*: the ultimate assertion of being over form.
