## Chapter Overview

All right, fellow survivors of the Dao-Twisted World, this chapter is a pure *negotiation chamber* special—and I don't mean that in a boring way. After the chaos of the Buddha Bone Temple fiasco, Li Huowang gets a summons from the one person he least expected to still be in town: the Chief Recorder. That pale-faced, sweet-smelling eunuch who bolted like a spooked rabbit last time? Yeah, he's back, sitting in a shabby study like he owns the place, abacus clicking, withered human tongue on display like a *conversation piece*. The old fox lays his cards on the table: the Office failed to retrieve the dead Heart-Mud, so now they're going after a *live* one—and he wants Li Huowang on the team. The bait? Access to the Four Repositories' Zuowandao records. The catch? Everything about this screams *trap wrapped in opportunity*. Buckle up, because this is the kind of chapter where a single conversation redraws the entire map of the story.

## Key Plot Points

- The Chief Recorder reveals he is *still* in Yinling City and hasn't fled back to the capital, immediately signaling that the stakes of his failed mission are higher than a simple reprimand.
- Li Huowang enters a humble study—a deliberate contrast to Tuoba Danqing's gaudy style—and is confronted by the desiccated tongue of a Heart-Element, mounted like a trophy, reminding him exactly what kind of creatures the Office harvests.
- The Chief Recorder admits the Buddha Bone Temple retrieval failed and that he *cannot* return to the capital empty-handed. His solution: find a living Heart-Mud to substitute for the dead one.
- He invokes an existential threat: if this chain of tasks fails, everyone on it—from the Recorder down to Li Huowang—could be "uprooted," suggesting the Office's internal politics are brutally hierarchical.
- Li Huowang presses for information about Zhuge Yuan, the mysterious figure who intercepted the Heart-Mud. The Chief Recorder dismisses the threat—claiming Zhuge Yuan only needs one Heart-Mud—but dodges the deeper question of his allegiance.
- The Recorder finally reveals Zhuge Yuan's nickname: "The Storyteller." He describes a scholar who recites *fabricated history*—a nine-hundred-year-old alternate timeline where the Great Qi never fell—positioning Zhuge Yuan as someone who treats reality as a narrative he can rewrite.
- Li Huowang accepts the deal, not out of loyalty, but out of desperation for the Zuowandao intel that might break his identity crisis.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Chief Recorder (记相):** This isn't just a fancy title. The term *jì xiāng* historically refers to a specific type of Ming Dynasty eunuch official attached to the Directorate of Ceremonial—essentially the emperor's personal scribes and secretaries. Their power was immense because they controlled the flow of information. Translating this as "Chief Recorder" captures both the bureaucratic weight and the slightly eerie, clerical horror of a man whose job is to *record your fate*. Eunuchs in Ming-era fiction often carry this dual valence of refined courtly grace and physical decay (hence the sweet perfume masking urine), and this novel leans into that with gusto.

**The Four Repositories (四库):** This is a loaded term for anyone familiar with Chinese literary history. The *Sì Kù Quán Shū* (Complete Library of the Four Treasuries) was the largest encyclopedia compilation project in Chinese history, commissioned by the Qianlong Emperor. It was meant to collect *all* knowledge—but also *censor* it. By promising to access the Four Repositories, the Chief Recorder is offering Li Huowang a key to forbidden knowledge, the kind that the empire itself tried to control. The irony is thick: the very institution that hoards truth is now offering it as a bribe.

**The Storyteller's Alternate History:** Zhuge Yuan's claim that the Great Qi never fell and that a new emperor reigns under the era name *Ying Hui* is more than just madness—it's a direct challenge to the foundational narrative of the Dao-Twisted World. In Chinese dynastic history, the *legitimacy* of a dynasty rests on its unbroken succession. By claiming a parallel history where Qi survived, Zhuge Yuan is essentially performing an act of *historical counterfeiting*—which, in a world where "Cultivate the False into Reality" is a cosmic law, may be more than just a delusion; it might be a *ritual*.

## Reading Guide

So here's the juicy bit: this chapter is a masterclass in *the deal you can't refuse but shouldn't take*. Li Huowang is being offered a direct path to the one thing he craves—knowledge about the Zuowandao—in exchange for hunting down a creature that is basically his mirror image: a Heart-Mud, alive, somewhere out there. The Chief Recorder is smooth, using flattery, camaraderie, and the ever-present threat of "uprooting" to pressure him. But watch how Li Huowang's sympathy flickers the moment he hears about a *living* Heart-Mud. That's not sentiment; that's a crack in his armor. He sees himself in the prey.

And then there's Zhuge Yuan. The name itself—*Zhuge*, as in Zhuge Liang, the legendary strategist—is already a red flag. But a *storyteller* who recites a fake history? In a world where belief shapes reality, a man who *narrates alternate timelines* may be the most dangerous kind of being: an author who doesn't agree with the current draft. Keep an eye on this scholar with a fan. He's not just a plot device; he's a walking *revision* of the world's source code.
