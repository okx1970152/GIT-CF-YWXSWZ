## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow travelers of the twisted path! Chapter 78 is one of those heavy, *quiet* chapters where the screaming has stopped, but the silence is worse. After the explosive horror on the island, we find Li Huowang holding the bloody bamboo slip—the *Thousand Greats Record*—and facing a moral decision that cuts to the core of his soul. Keep it for its brutal utility, or sink it to spare the world its poison? You already know which choice a survivor makes. This is a chapter of aftermath, of cold calculation, and of a final, shocking act that proves Li Huowang is learning the Dao-Twisted World’s lessons a little too well.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Temptation of the Record**: Li Huowang seriously considers throwing the blood-soaked bamboo slip (the *Thousand Greats Record*) into the lake. He recognizes the evil of Bashe’s power. But pragmatism wins. He tucks it away, acknowledging that in the future, he may need a dangerous substitute for Danyangzi’s power. This is a chillingly rational decision.
- **The Boatman’s Plea**: A young bandit, Yuan Fu, tries to save his skin by acting as a guide. He claims he was forced into banditry and wants to take the imperial exams. Li Huowang uses him for information about the nuns on Mount Heng Hua, learning they are “fat, lazy, and stinky”—a far cry from holy maidens.
- **The Burning of the Dead**: The group disposes of their three drowned companions. The grim ritual of burning the bodies and collecting the ashes is a somber, grounding moment for the exhausted survivors.
- **The Dying of the Light**: In the chapter’s final, brutal beat, as the sun rises and the group prepares to move on, Li Huowang casually slits Yuan Fu’s throat. No hesitation. No rage. Just a clean, efficient execution. Li Huowang is no longer the man who jumped into this world.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Bashe (巴虺)**: This is the name of the entity whose power is channeled through the *Thousand Greats Record*. In Chinese mythology, Bashe is a legendary python said to swallow elephants. In the Dao-Twisted World, it has been corrupted into a deity of suffering and flesh-horror. Borrowing its power always comes at a physical cost.
- **The Three Don’t-Rob Rule (三不抢)**: This piece of bandit lore is a fantastic worldbuilding detail. The fact that the Yuan family bandits have specific *spiritual* prohibitions—black-clad nuns, bird-keeping monks, and drum-carrying spirit-dancers—suggests that these groups are recognized as dangerous, protected, or simply too troublesome to mess with. It gives the world a sense of lived-in rules.
- **The Nuns of Mount Heng Hua**: Described by Yuan Fu as comically obese and filthy, this description is deeply unsettling in context. Combined with Li Zhi’s earlier claim that they are “barely decent people,” the reader is left with a profoundly uneasy image. In the Dao-Twisted World, physical grotesquerie often masks (or is a direct result of) spiritual power.

### Thousand Greats Record (《大千录》)

A foundational artifact of a suffering-cult, a bamboo slip containing methods that trade extreme physical agony for power from the entity known as Bashe.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in quiet dread. The most terrifying moment isn't a monster; it's Li Huowang’s calm decision to slit the throat of a teenage boy who was just being useful. Pay attention to how his internal justification has shifted. He’s not acting out of rage or fear anymore. He’s acting out of cold policy. The “good” Li Huowang is being buried one small, practical decision at a time. Also, savor the description of the nuns. “Fat, lazy, and stinky” is such a mundane, peasant-perspective description that it practically guarantees the reality is going to be far, far stranger.
