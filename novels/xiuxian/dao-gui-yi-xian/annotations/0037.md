## Chapter Overview

Welcome to Jianye Town, folks! After days of trudging through creepy forests and dealing with supernatural horrors, Li Huowang’s ragtag group finally reaches civilization. But if you thought they’d get a warm welcome, think again. This chapter pulls back the curtain on the Dao-Twisted World’s social structure—and it is *brutal*. From constables chasing them for looking too scary to Zhao Wu casually dropping the Nine Classes system like it’s common knowledge, we get our first real look at how this messed-up world organizes itself. Li Huowang, ever the opportunist, learns a valuable lesson about keeping his Daoist robe on. It’s a quieter chapter, but one that builds the world brick by horrifying brick.

## Key Plot Points

- The group’s approach to Jianye Town is marked by increasing signs of civilization: worn paths, footprints, and animal waste.
- In a dialogue-heavy section, Zhao Wu explains the **Nine Classes (九流)** social hierarchy, ranking everyone from emperors to actors and prostitutes. This is our first major info-dump on the world’s societal structure.
- Li Huowang learns that Daoists, as part of the “Three Teachings,” are outside the Nine Classes and generally respected. He decides to keep his Daoist robe, realizing it’s a pragmatic asset for travel.
- He also probes about high-level cultivators like Danyangzi, discovering that ordinary people in remote towns have no idea such beings exist—until they’re captured by one.
- The group enters the town but is immediately stopped by a constable, Constable Yan, who warns them to cover up their monstrous appearance to avoid scaring the townsfolk.
- Li Huowang buys veiled bamboo hats for the group, confirming his role as a pragmatic leader.
- Zhao Wu, overwhelmed with emotion, returns home to his family’s rice shop. His reunion is joyful with his brother and little sister, but Li Huowang notices something off: the rest of the family inside the shop looks strangely indifferent.
- The chapter ends on a quiet, ominous note as Li Huowang senses something is wrong but chooses to stay silent for now.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Nine Classes (九流)** is a traditional Chinese social hierarchy that has roots in pre-imperial and imperial-era thought. Here, it’s hardened into a rigid caste system. Note: “Clergymen and sect members” (Fourth Class) include Buddhists and Daoists, but Daoists get special treatment as “outside the nine.” In the real world, actors and prostitutes were indeed near the bottom, subject to legal and social discrimination. The novel uses this as a tool to amplify the stakes for characters like Lü Zhuangyuan and his family.

**The Three Teachings (三教)** refers to Confucianism, Buddhism, and Daoism. Being “outside the Nine Classes” is a huge privilege; it means you’re judged by your spiritual calling, not your birth. Li Huowang’s decision to keep his Daoist robe is a classic xianxia protagonist move: adapt the local system’s rules to your advantage.

**The Veiled Bamboo Hat (斗笠)** is a real historical item used by travelers and commoners for sun and rain protection. Adding black veils is a pragmatic fix for the group’s “monstrous” appearance, but it also evokes the trope of masked wanderers—a classic wuxia/xianxia visual.

## Reading Guide

Okay, so this chapter is mostly **worldbuilding with a side of mystery**. The action’s light, but the lore is heavy, and that’s a good thing. Pay attention to the Nine Classes: it’s not just flavor text. This system will come back to haunt characters, and knowing it helps you understand why Lü Zhuangyuan is so grumpy and why Zhao Wu was treated like nothing. Also, that chilling note at the end? Li Huowang seeing Zhao Wu’s family acting *flat*? That’s a classic horror setup. Something’s not right in that rice shop. Get ready for the next chapter—it’s probably not going to be a happy homecoming.
