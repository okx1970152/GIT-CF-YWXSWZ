## Chapter Overview
Welcome back to the ward, folks. This chapter drags us out of the Dao-Twisted World and back into the sterile, fluorescent-lit reality of the White Tower Psych Ward. And guess what? The *real* world isn't any safer. Our boy Li Huowang thought he was done with his past-life delusions, but a familiar face from his medical history, the therapist Wang Wei, has crawled out of the woodwork with a dangerous obsession. Wang Wei has read Li Huowang’s confidential case files, seen the security footage of his gold-conjuring trick, and has become convinced that the Dao-Twisted World is real. He's not here to offer a handshake—he’s here to demand a partnership, backed by a threat that could shatter Li Huowang's fragile freedom. Get ready for a tense, low-key psychological showdown that proves the horror isn't always hiding in caves and temples; sometimes, it's wearing a white coat and holding a USB drive.

## Key Plot Points
*   **An Unexpected Visitor:** Li Huowang is cornered in the prison bathroom by Wang Wei, his former therapist. Wang Wei looks haggard and unhinged, a stark contrast to his professional demeanor from earlier chapters.
*   **The Threat:** Wang Wei reveals he has video evidence of Li Huowang making gold appear in the hospital ward. He threatens to upload this proof of Li Huowang's "special ability" to the internet.
*   **A Man's Last Warning:** Li Huowang's response is chilling. He leans in and whispers a dark reminder: he's still a registered psychiatric patient, and killing someone under that legal umbrella has very different consequences. The threat is so visceral that it backs Wang Wei down, for now.
*   **A Proposal for Partnership:** Wang Wei clarifies he isn't there to blackmail, but to "cooperate." He believes Li Huowang can retrieve life-extending Yang Life Pills from the Dao-Twisted World, just as he retrieved the gold.
*   **New Year's Eve:** The chapter shifts into a quieter, more atmospheric segment. The ward prepares for the Chinese New Year, with dumpling-making and plans to watch the Spring Festival Gala. Sun Xiaoqin is cheerfully present.
*   **The Silent Deadline:** During the festivities, Li Huowang feels that familiar chill. He excuses himself to his room, knowing Wang Wei will come. Just as he expects, Wang Wei appears, having infiltrated the locked ward in a patient's gown.
*   **Evidence and a Revelation:** Wang Wei throws a stack of papers onto the bed: Yi Donglai’s full research thesis on Li Huowang's case, documenting every hallucination and delusion. He demands a demonstration of the Yang Life Pill.
*   **A Question of Karma:** Before complying, Li Huowang asks a pointed question. He recalls being kidnapped from the hospital by people who also wanted his gold. He now suspects Wang Wei was the inside man who leaked his secrets to them.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Spring Festival Gala (春晚 / Chūn Wǎn):** This is a massive, multi-hour variety show broadcast by China Central Television (CCTV) on every Lunar New Year's Eve. It's a cultural institution watched by hundreds of millions. The characters complaining about the "terrible shows" is a universal tradition—everyone watches it, and everyone complains about it. Its presence in this scene grounds the horror in a painfully recognizable, mundane Chinese reality.

**Yang Life Pills (阳寿丹):** A recurring item in the Dao-Twisted World. "Yang Life" refers to a person's ordained lifespan. An "Yang Life Pill" would be an elixir that artificially adds years to one's life. In the novel's cosmology, lifespan is a tangible resource that can be harvested, traded, and consumed. Wang Wei's desire for it reflects the ultimate human greed—not for gold, but for more time.

**The Legal Loophole of "Mental Illness" (精神病杀人):** Li Huowang's threat is a dark play on a common social perception (and legal nuance). China's Criminal Law states that a mental patient who commits a crime during periods when they are incapable of recognizing or controlling their own behavior shall not bear criminal responsibility. Li Huowang is weaponizing his own diagnosis to create a zone of legal impunity.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in shifting the axis of horror. The threat isn't a ten-armed Flesh Buddha or a flesh-warping curse; it's a man with a clipboard and a theory. Wang Wei is terrifying because he is *plausible*. He has proof. He has Li Huowang's own words. For the first time, Li Huowang is faced with someone from the "real" world who accepts the Dao-Twisted World as fact—and wants to exploit it. This creates a new kind of trap for our protagonist. He can't just kill a monster or escape through a portal. He has to outthink a human predator who operates by the same rules of law and evidence that should be his only safety net. Watch how Li Huowang operates: he doesn't panic. He issues a cold, calculated counter-threat, then distractingly plays the part of the cooperative patient. He's learning to be a strategist in both worlds. The final question—"Were you the one who sold me out?"—is key. It shows Li Huowang's paranoia is now his greatest investigative tool. He's not just surviving; he's looking for the source of the leak.
