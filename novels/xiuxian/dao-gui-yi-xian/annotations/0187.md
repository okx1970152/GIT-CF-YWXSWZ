## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow travelers on the Dao-Twisted road. After the relentless horrors of Zhengde Temple and the blood-soaked weddings of the past few arcs, Chapter 187 finally gives us a moment to **breathe**—and it’s one of the most quietly devastating chapters in the entire novel. No monsters, no rituals, no screaming. Just Li Huowang lying in bed with Bai Lingmiao, talking about the future like a normal couple… until the hallucination drags him back to a sterile hospital room.

This chapter is a masterclass in emotional contrast. The first half is domestic warmth so tender it hurts: Li Huowang’s awkwardness with Gao Zhijian, his quiet affection for Bai Lingmiao, and his fragile hope for a settled life at Niuxin Mountain. Then, without warning, the swap hits. He’s in a restraint ward, calling Yang Na, and we’re reminded that *he has no idea which world is real*. The Zuowandao’s taunt—that a Heart-Element named North Wind lived to 190 without going mad—echoes through his thoughts like a poison he can’t spit out. But the real gut-punch? Yang Na’s careful, heartbroken “sorry… I didn’t mean to upset you.” This chapter doesn’t need a monster to terrify you. It just needs a phone call.

## Key Plot Points
- **Gao Zhijian’s awkward confession**: Li Huowang orders him to speak in the yard, but Gao Zhijian stammers, flushes, and retreats inside without finishing. His secret (a “huang” something) is left hanging. Li Huowang dismisses it as a private matter, not worth prying into.
- **Domestic warmth with Bai Lingmiao**: Li Huowang blows out the candle. They talk about the fool’s destination (Qingqiu then Liang), Li Huowang muses on settling at Niuxin Mountain, and he realizes his relationship with Bai Lingmiao has grown slowly, like a stream carving a lake. It’s comfortable, unforced, and deeply fragile.
- **The Heart-Element anxiety**: Li Huowang admits he still fears losing himself. He recalls the Zuowandao’s dying claim that a Heart-Element named North Wind lived to 190 without bewilderment. He suspects it’s a trick, but he can’t stop chewing on it.
- **The swap to the psychiatric ward**: Li Huowang senses the hallucination coming and orders Bai Lingmiao to chain him down. He wakes in a restraint ward—transferred, apparently. A male nurse, Nurse Wang, offers to patch a call through.
- **The phone call with Yang Na**: Li Huowang requests Yang Na’s number. She answers with her familiar morning grumpiness. He asks about her birthday. She softens, apologizing for “upsetting” him, and says she’ll be waiting for his birthday present. The exchange is painfully normal, and that’s what makes it horrifying.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Heart-Element (心素) and the bewilderment trap** — For those just joining us: the “Heart-Element” isn’t just a fancy constitution. It’s a fundamental category of being in the Dao-Twisted World. A Heart-Element’s *belief* has the power to make things real—which means the line between genuine reality and self-generated hallucination is porous, terrifying, and always shifting. Li Huowang’s fear of “bewilderment” (迷惘) is not a simple worry about going crazy. It’s a concrete, existential dread that he might accidentally *believe* the wrong world into existence, abandoning everyone he loves. The Zuowandao’s claim that “North Wind” lived to 190 without this happening is therefore not just a taunt—it’s a psychological weapon designed to undermine his self-trust.

**The psychiatric ward as a mirrored world** — Note the deliberate architectural detail: Li Huowang recognizes the *restraint ward* layout. This isn’t a random hospital room. It’s specialized for patients who might need to be physically confined. In the context of the novel’s meta-structure, the “White Tower Psychiatric Hospital” is explicitly the modern-world counterpart of the White Jade Capital. The more sterile and clinical this world feels, the more it functions as a mirror inversion of the bloody, ritual-filled Dao-Twisted World. The fact that Li Huowang can name the room type and proactively order himself chained shows he has learned to *game* his own hallucinations—a chilling form of self-surveillance.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a **palate cleanser made of emotional glass**. After the grand guignol of Zhengde Temple and the grotesque Flesh Buddhas, we’re given a quiet, almost *normal* scene. Li Huowang talking about marriage. Bai Lingmiao nestling closer. A future that sounds peaceful. And that’s exactly why it cuts so deep. We know—and Li Huowang knows—that this peace is built on a fault line. The phone call with Yang Na is a masterstroke: she’s warm, she’s familiar, she apologizes for accidentally hurting him. And that *normalcy* is the most destabilizing thing in the entire chapter.

Pay close attention to the structural mirroring. Li Huowang feels comfortable with Bai Lingmiao (“like a stream carving a lake”). Then, in the psych ward, he deliberately calls Yang Na, only to immediately feel guilt and confusion. The two women are not rivals; they’re anchors in two different worlds, and Li Huowang is terrified of choosing wrong. The Zuowandao’s parting shot—the “North Wind” claim—hangs over everything like a question mark. Is it a lie? A truth meant to trap him? Or a clue he’s too paranoid to accept?

For the reader, this is the quiet before the storm. Enjoy the warmth while it lasts. The Dao-Twisted World doesn’t do peace for long.
