## Chapter Overview

Ever had one of those nights where you set yourself on fire, cut off your own fingers, fight a pair of demonic mahjong goons, and then find out your buddy—who you thought was a bit of a mystic—has apparently been running the world’s most exclusive interdimensional transport service? No? Just Li Huowang, then. This chapter throws us directly into the flaming chaos, but the real fire isn't on his skin. It's in his head. The Zuowandao attack is a brutal, visceral brawl, but it’s the aftermath that hits harder: a revelation that rewrites everything we thought we knew about the world. Get ready for a face-palming, mind-breaking lore bomb, fellow daoists.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is ambushed by two flame-covered Zuowandao wielding a massive changdao. The fight is raw and desperate, with Li Huowang using his Purple-Tasseled Sword to ultimately win by cutting off his own fingers as a distraction.
- Li Sui fights alongside him, her burning tentacles taking severe damage, showcasing her loyalty and the heavy physical cost of the battle.
- The two Zuowandao are revealed to be suicidal pawns; they were not immune to the fire but simply fought until they burned to death, frustrating Li Huowang’s plan to extract information.
- Upon returning to Zhuge Yuan, Li Huowang discovers the other Zuowandao are gone. Zhuge Yuan matter-of-factly states he sent them all back to Da Qi.
- The philosophical bombshell drops: Da Qi is not just a concept or a localized illusion. It is a real place, accessible by forgetting Da Liang and believing in its existence. This shatters Li Huowang’s understanding of reality.
- As a final, shocking reward, Zhuge Yuan unfurls his scroll and coughs up the wanted man, Northern Wind, alive and beaten. The hunt is over before it began.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Let's talk about the **Heart-Turbid (心浊)**. In the standard xianxia lexicon, you have your elements and your spiritual roots. But the Dao-Twisted World likes to play dirty. The Heart-Element (心素) is the ability to *make the false true*, to will things into reality. Its dark mirror, the Heart-Turbid, works in reverse—it *hides* the true and makes the false *forgotten*. Zhuge Yuan’s scroll isn’t just a magic bag; it’s a conceptual eraser. He isn’t teleporting people; he’s *un-making* their connection to this reality (Da Liang) and *re-asserting* their belief in another (Da Qi). Think of it less as a transportation spell and more as a targeted ontological reboot. For a western audience: this is less like a portal and more like convincing someone their entire life was a dream, and when they “wake up,” a new life is waiting for them.

And what of **Da Qi** and **Da Liang**? A classic xianxia trope is the “real world” versus the “false world,” or a pocket dimension. This novel corrupts the trope. Zhuge Yuan has been preaching that *this* world, the one we know, is the fake one. But seeing it in action—watching people simply march off to a different country because they’ve been made to forget—is a whole new level of horror. It raises a terrifying question: if you can be made to forget your entire identity and remember a different one, what is “real” at all? This is the core philosophical conflict of the novel, now laid bare: **Cultivate the True (修真)** vs. **Cultivate the False (修假)**. Does one refine their connection to a pre-existing truth, or does one have the power to build a new one with belief alone? The Zuowandao are the champions of the latter.

### Cultivate the True (修真) vs. Cultivate the False (修假)

A central philosophical dichotomy in the Dao-Twisted World. One is the standard xianxia path of refining truth; the other is the power to make false beliefs into reality, championed by the Zuowandao.

The highest-ranked inner circle of the Zuowandao. Their appearance signals an escalation in the enemy's threat level.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in tonal whiplash. We go from a grimy, all-in, flesh-burning street brawl where Li Huowang is chopping off his own fingers, right into a quiet philosophical argument on a rooftop. Let that sink in. Li Huowang is literally covered in soot and blood, and Zhuge Yuan is calmly explaining the metaphysics of reality with a fan in his hand. This is the essence of the Dao-Twisted World: cosmic horror doesn’t pause for you to catch your breath.

Here’s the kicker for Li Huowang: his desperation has evolved. He’s no longer just reactive. When he finds the Zuowandao are gone, his frustration is so sharp he *wishes* they would come back just so he could fight them. That’s a new low (or high?) for our protagonist. He’s resourceful, grim, and increasingly cold. The chapter closes on a note of anticlimactic triumph and profound confusion. He got what he wanted—Northern Wind—but the method (a man getting spat out of a scroll) only deepens the central mystery. The hunt for a person has turned into a hunt for the nature of reality itself. Strap in. The answers are only going to get weirder.

## Deeper Lore: The Four Joys (四喜) of the Zuowandao

For the first time, we get a hint of the Zuowandao’s internal hierarchy. The two fighters, marked by the Eight and Nine Strip mahjong tiles, are described as “pawns.” This implies a culling of forces, with the true leaders remaining hidden. The **Four Joys (四喜)** are the rumored highest-ranked members of the society. Their names are a perversion of the “Four Joys of Life” in Chinese culture (rain after a long drought, meeting an old friend in a distant land, the wedding night, and passing the imperial exam). For the Zuowandao, their “joy” is likely a twisted, cosmic punchline at the expense of all reality. The appearance of a True Joy member would signify a level of threat far beyond what we’ve seen so far.
