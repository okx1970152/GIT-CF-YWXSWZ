## Chapter Overview

Oh boy. If you thought Li Huowang was done dealing with terrifying sovereigns, think again. This chapter introduces the *actual* head honcho of the Zuowandao—the Dice (骰子)—and let’s just say his “friendly favors” hit different than a celestial emperor’s warmongering. Our boy gets cornered in broad daylight over fried dough sticks, and the exchange is a masterclass in smiling dread. The chapter then slides into a tense wilderness isolation arc (because when the Dice says jump, you don’t ask how high, you *run*), before ending with the sound of unsolicited combat in the forest—because of course Li Huowang can’t catch a break.

## Key Plot Points

1. The Dice casually intercepts Li Huowang at a market stall and reveals he knows about the Great Liang Emperor’s recruitment offer last chapter—so much for secrecy.
2. The Dice wants Li Huowang to take the emperor’s offer and secretly help him ascend the throne as a Zuowandao insider.
3. Li Huowang bluntly refuses, triggering the Dice’s “you owe me a favor” gambit. The favor involves storing something for him a month ago that Li Huowang has no memory of—which is either a lie or evidence that the Dice has already started messing with his timeline.
4. Li Huowang stands his ground, gambling that the Dice won’t escalate to open conflict while the Supervisory Heavenly Office is still hunting them.
5. As Li Huowang leaves, the Dice shouts a cryptic warning about only cultivating the True and not the False, linking back to the novel’s core philosophical axis.
6. Li Huowang goes full survival mode: no contact with people, foraging for food, fleeing into the wilderness to avoid hidden Zuowandao traps.
7. Late night, everyone hears combat sounds from the eastern woods. Li Huowang, Bai Lingmiao, and Li Sui go to investigate as a unit to avoid being tricked by a “divide and conquer” tactic.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Dice (骰子)**: He’s not just “another powerful trickster.” The Dice is the *leader* of the Zuowandao, a rank so high it’s rarely even named. His entire combat style is psychological warfare—he uses favors, memory manipulation, and social pressure as his weapon. Unlike martial cultivators, he doesn’t need to *hit* you; he just needs you to *owe* him.
- **Cultivating the True vs. the False (修真 vs 修假)**: This is the core metaphysical axis of the Dao-Twisted World. Standard cultivation (“seeking the True”) is about aligning with reality’s inherent rules. The Zuowandao’s “Cultivation of the False” is about making a lie so solid it overwrites truth. The Dice’s final warning—“if you only cultivate the True and not the False, watch out for Qi Deviation”—is a terrifying inversion of conventional wisdom: in this world, refusing to lie might *hurt you spiritually*.
- **Qi Deviation (走火入魔)**: A classic xianxia term for a cultivation accident where the practitioner’s inner energy goes out of control, often leading to madness, self-destruction, or monstrous transformation. The Dice weaponizes this term here as a threat: by refusing to “play the lie,” Li Huowang is leaving himself vulnerable to internal spiritual imbalance.

## Reading Guide

Li Huowang makes a *critical* survival decision here: he refuses to engage in the Zuowandao’s game at all. He doesn’t try to out-con the Dice or lay a counter-trap—he simply *leaves*. That’s the mature version of his old paranoid self: instead of trying to win every fight, he recognizes that the safest move is not to enter the ring. But the Dice’s parting shot about “cultivating the True vs. the False” is a world-building bomb that will echo forward. Pay attention: this isn’t just a philosophical quip. It’s a warning about the very nature of power in this twisted reality.

## Deep Dive: The Dice’s “Favor” Mechanic

This chapter introduces a terrifying Zuowandao recruitment technique: the fabricated favor. The Dice claims Li Huowang “asked him to store something a month ago”—a month during which, from Li Huowang’s perspective, nothing happened. But in the Dao-Twisted World, memory is already unreliable. The Dice might be lying, or he might actually be planting false memories in Li Huowang’s timeline. This is the horror of a trickster sect that doesn’t need to fight you—it just needs to make you *doubt your own past*.
