## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up. This chapter is a masterclass in high-stakes politicking and raw, ruthless opportunism. Li Huowang has the secret everyone wants, and suddenly, the big players are coming out of the woodwork. Forget the slow burn—this is where the Imperial Preceptor himself steps in, cutting the Supervisory Heavenly Office right out of the deal. The air crackles with tension as Li Huowang, cornered and desperate to save Bai Lingmiao before Ji Lin’s coronation, has to make a devil’s bargain with a terrifyingly powerful stranger. It’s fast, it’s tense, and it reveals just how deep the rot—and the mysteries—of this world truly go.

## Key Plot Points

- The Grand Liang Imperial Preceptor, Huangfu Tian’gang, intercepts Li Huowang, offering to kill Dice in exchange for the Supreme Extremity Confluence secret, effectively stealing the prize from the Supervisory Heavenly Office Director.
- Li Huowang, realizing the leverage he holds, successfully negotiates for one of Shangguan Yuting’s eyes as a second “secret” of the illusion, allowing him to track both Dice and Bai Lingmiao.
- The Imperial Preceptor accepts the deal and instructs Li Huowang on how to lie to the Director when she returns, forcing Li Huowang deeper into a web of political debts.
- Using the secret’s water-reflection technique, Li Huowang locates Dice, who is disguised as a young eunuch preparing for Ji Lin’s enthronement ceremony inside the Grand Liang Imperial Palace.
- Huangfu Tian’gang reveals a shocking truth about the Zuowandao: the sect can never be truly eradicated because it is born from the very act of lying itself, persisting as long as a single lie exists in the world.
- In a dramatic final sequence, the Imperial Preceptor uses his Constellation Sword to fly them both to the capital and ignites ritual altars around the palace with heavenly thunder, initiating the hunt.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Mandate of Heaven” as a Parasitic Burden:** This chapter builds on a core, twisted lore concept. Here, the Mandate of Heaven (气运) isn’t a glorious blessing; it is a stolen fragment of divine power that holds up reality itself. It can only be maintained by a family willing to commit self-slaughter and endless atrocity, making it a curse disguised as a crown. The Imperial Preceptor’s weary attitude and the old emperor’s dark amusement are symptoms of this parasitic system.

**The Unkillable Nature of the Zuowandao:** A terrifying bit of world-building is dropped here. The Zuowandao (坐忘道) aren’t just a sect that can be destroyed. They are a conceptual plague born from the existence of lies. As long as one person on earth tells a single lie, a new Zuowandao can be born. This reframes them from a mere antagonistic faction into a fundamental, inescapable law of the Dao-Twisted World’s reality—a truly cosmic horror concept.

**Supreme Extremity Confluence (上极灌口):** This technique is confirmed as a divination tool that uses water reflections. By reciting a target’s name and touching the water to one’s forehead, the user gains a temporary, overlapping perspective with the target. It’s a powerful investigative tool, but the disorienting dual-perspective effect highlights the cognitive price of using such dangerous knowledge.

**Constellation Sword (星宿剑):** The Imperial Preceptor’s weapon. It can be thrown, kicked for propulsion (a classic xianxia sword flight technique), and used as a platform to ride. This chapter showcases high-level martial prowess, from sword flight to summoning heavenly thunder (天雷) to ignite ritual fire on dozens of altars simultaneously.

### Mandate of Heaven (气运)

A stolen fragment of divine power that holds up the reality of the Dao-Twisted World, maintained through endless sacrifice by a single family.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a complete shift in momentum. This chapter is all about *speed* and *audacity*. You’ll feel Li Huowang’s frantic, desperate calculation as he weighs the risk of betrayal against his need for immediate power. The Imperial Preceptor is a fantastic new addition to the stage—an ancient, powerful player who is both terrifying and, in a weird way, pragmatic. Pay close attention to the lore-drop about the Zuowandao. It changes everything you thought you knew about them. The final sequence, from the water-vision of the palace to the sword-flight chase and the thunder igniting the altars, is pure, cinematic action.

## Deeper Lore: The Wu and the Old Emperor’s Burden

This chapter is also a strong companion to the massive world-building revelations about *The Wu* (巫). If you recall, the Wu were ancient beings who farmed humans as livestock. Humanity’s ancestors escaped and used the stolen Mandate to build their own world. The old emperor’s lineage is the one holding up this stolen reality. His dialogue is not the ravings of a mad tyrant, but the exhausted confession of a man forced to commit atrocities just to keep the sky from falling. The Imperial Preceptor’s actions aren’t just politics; they are the *maintenance* of this broken system. The hunt for Dice is a small part of a much larger, more horrifying picture.
