## Chapter Overview

Bai Lingmiao goes solo, and she *nails* it. Our girl has her first real exorcism—a possessed corpse in a burial shroud—and handles it with a mix of ritual precision and the kind of raw nerve that makes you sit up and cheer. No Li Huowang to hide behind, just her, a drum, and a Second Spirit who gives terrible relationship advice. But while Bai Lingmiao's star is rising, the chapter's real gut-punch comes from a blind exorcist on the road. One touch to Li Huowang's face, and she drops a diagnosis that hits harder than any curse: this man is missing all Five Phases. Buckle up, folks—the cosmology just got personal.

## Key Plot Points

- Bai Lingmiao confronts the Second Spirit's quiet accusation: her entire identity is tied to Li Huowang. She doesn't argue it; she just stings and carries on.
- A door is smashed in. A corpse in a red burial shroud enters with stiff, hopping steps—her shoes are sewn together, her ankles bound. Classic jumping-corpse logic.
- Bai Lingmiao's ritual is precise: Bang Bing Jue, self-igniting incense that fizzles on contact, a hedgehog-defense that pierces the corpse's chin, and fox fur that invades its seven orifices. The possessing shadow flees.
- Her victory is real, and her confidence is earned. She asks the Second Spirit how to improve and agrees to learn the vocal chants—a small but significant commitment.
- An old blind woman on the road is furious to learn the exorcism is already done. She spits curses that could strip paint.
- Li Huowang asks about her background. She approaches, touches his face, and reveals a diagnosis that reframes everything: he is missing all Five Phases. Not just one. Not two. All five.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Bang Bing Jue (帮兵决)**: This is the traditional summoning verse of the Chuma (出马) spirit-medium tradition in Northeast China. It's not just noise—the rhythm is sharp and military, designed to call down "immortal soldiers." The drum isn't accompaniment; it's a *tactic.*
- **Possessed corpses with bound ankles**: In some Chinese folk traditions, corpses that are "walking" due to possession or incomplete burial are described as hopping because their feet are bound or their ankles are tied. It's a visual shorthand for "this is a body being puppeted, not a person walking."
- **The Seven Orifices (七窍)**: The eyes, ears, nostrils, and mouth. A common route for invading spirits or corrupting energies. The fox fur's attack here is textbook spiritual combat—block the orifices, starve the possessor.
- **The Five Phases (五行)**: The foundation of Chinese cosmological science. Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water. Every person has a natal imbalance, but *missing all five* is an ontological impossibility. It means Li Huowang isn't just unlucky—he's a hole in reality.

### Chuma (出马)

A spirit-medium tradition from Northeast China in which a human serves as a "mount" for animal or spirit patrons; the body is lent to entities for rituals, exorcisms, and healing.

## Reading Guide

- Watch Bai Lingmiao here. This isn't just a side character moment. She moves from passive follower to active practitioner, and the narrative respects her growth. She's scared, but she performs. She's proud, but she asks for help.
- The blind exorcist's diagnosis isn't just a cool line. In the Five Phases system, saying someone "lacks all five" is the equivalent of saying they don't exist in the natural order. It's a violation of the world's grammar.
- The chapter's pacing is deceptive. It starts with solo exorcism (a win), then ends with a quiet, devastating piece of worldbuilding. The horror isn't in the dark—it's in a blind woman's casual observation.

## Deeper Dive: The Five Phases (五行) and Why "Missing All Five" Is a Novelty

The Five Phases aren't just "elements." They're a dynamic system of mutual generation and control—Wood feeds Fire, Fire creates Earth (ash), Earth bears Metal, Metal collects Water, Water nourishes Wood. Every person, place, and object has a Phase constitution. A fortune teller might say you're "Wood-heavy" or "Fire-deficient." That's normal. But "missing all five"? That's not a deficiency; it's a null set. It suggests Li Huowang's fundamental nature is *outside* the cosmic framework of the Dao-Twisted World. He's a glitch. A piece of data that shouldn't exist. And in a world where belief shapes reality, being an impossibility is both a vulnerability and a terrifying advantage.
