## Chapter Overview

Alright, buckle up, folks—this chapter is a *masterclass* in tension and maneuvering. Li Huowang has just finished processing the Renxiao incident, and he comes to a very important, very pragmatic decision: the Black Tai Sui isn't Danyangzi. It's a parasite he can't live without, so he might as well try to domesticate it. He officially names it "Li Sui" and proposes a tentative alliance. But the real meat of the chapter is the dinner with Tuoba Danqing. What starts as a friendly, boozy catch-up quickly turns into a psychological fencing match. Danqing drops a massive lore bomb about the nature of Renxiao and Yang Life Elixirs, casually probing Li Huowang's deepest wounds, and then finally—*finally*—demands to know what's under the mask. Li Huowang's solution? Show him the grossest, most unsettling truth he can physically produce. It's a brilliant, horrific misdirect, and it works perfectly.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang formally names the Black Tai Sui "Li Sui" and establishes a new, pragmatic policy of coexistence with the entity inside him. They are now, in his words, "grasshoppers tied to the same rope."
- Tuoba Danqing arrives for their routine debriefing and delivers Li Huowang's promotion token (Ren Wu), signaling his official advancement within the Office.
- During their meal, Danqing explains a critical piece of world lore: forcibly extending life past 120 with Yang Life Elixirs inevitably turns a cultivator into a Renxiao, unless they can first dispel their inner obsession.
- The most dangerous (and "best") method to dispel that obsession, according to Danqing, is using the "Bewilderment of a Heart-Element"—a bombshell of information that rattles Li Huowang.
- Danqing subtly pressures Li Huowang about his constant wearing of the copper coin mask. Red Center confirms this is a deliberate, probing interrogation.
- Li Huowang decides to preempt the suspicion by "showing" the truth: he lets the Black Tai Sui's tentacles and eyes emerge from under the mask, presenting his parasitic horror as a grotesquely physical explanation. The gamble pays off.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a treasure trove for worldbuilding. First, the concept of the **Renxiao** is formally explained. "Renxiao" is a folk term for a person who has lived miserably into extreme old age, becoming a blind, hairy, pitiful creature. The novel weaponizes this: if a cultivator doesn't achieve immortality and simply forces their way past 120 with life-prolonging pills, they don't become wise—they become *this*. It's a brutal inversion of the "long life" dream, turning it into a biological and spiritual horror.

Next, Tuoba Danqing outlines a key pillar of xianxia mythology: the distinction between the body's age and the soul's "obsession" (执念). In Daoist cultivation, a long life isn't just a matter of accumulating years; it requires *purging attachments*. The stronger the obsession, the more impossible transcendence becomes. That's why the Heart-Element's "Bewilderment" is so valuable—it's not a drug; it's a kind of existential confusion that can overwrite a dying cultivator's stubborn will to cling to life. It's horrifying and brilliant.

Finally, the **Supervisory Heavenly Office** is fleshed out. It's a bureaucracy with ranks (like *Ren Wu*), it's connected to the Ministry of Rites, and it has an internal economy where Office-earned Yang Life Elixirs are the currency of choice for accessing rare artifacts unavailable in the "ghost markets." This gives the Office a tangible, political weight, grounding the cosmic horror in a system of mundane power and privilege.

### Cuo Jin Dan

A pill containing a concentrated dose of Yang Life Elixir doses; Tuoba Danqing mistakenly uses this term during his drunken ramble.

## Reading Guide

If you're reading this for the first time, pay very close attention to the subtext. Tuoba Danqing is *not* a friendly drunk. His "lapses in judgment" (like asking about the mask) are calculated. His "slips of the tongue" (like mentioning the Heart-Element) are traps. He's treating this as an interrogation disguised as a banquet. This is a test, and Li Huowang passes it with a masterful show of visceral body horror. The chapter perfectly balances the long-form lore drop (explaining the world's rules about lifespan and obsession) with the immediate, intimate threat of being exposed. It's a character piece for Li Huowang's new, pragmatic ruthlessness: naming his parasite and weaponizing it to keep his secrets safe.
