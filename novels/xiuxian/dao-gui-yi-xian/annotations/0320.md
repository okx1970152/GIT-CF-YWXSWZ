## Chapter Overview

This chapter is equal parts stomach-turning horror and a brilliant detective breakthrough. As the crowd of Cangshui roars in approval at a public execution by a thousand cuts, Li Huowang makes a chilling connection—not only are the stolen babies and defiled women connected, but all of it points to a hidden *alchemical recipe*. The small, grisly details of a dying man’s curse, a hexagram carved in flesh, and a list of stolen herbs suddenly snap together into a much larger, more horrific picture. Your boy is no longer just hunting a pervert; he’s stumbled onto someone using the entire county as a cauldron.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang eats a bizarre regional noodle dish with thorn-embedded green noodles while overhearing two locals brag about sleeping with a “female ghost,” a braggart’s tale that will later prove important.
- He tries to repeat his talisman divination but fails—it works inconsistently, likely due to his inexperience with the manual.
- After his divination fails, Li Huowang resorts to a stakeout, but the baby-snatching mysteriously stops the moment he starts watching.
- The captured embroidery man is publicly executed by slow slicing (lingchi) in the town square as a massive, cheering crowd watches.
- During the execution, the dying man furiously curses the crowd, claiming that none of the women he raped had their “falling red” (hymenal blood)—meaning they were all already defiled before him.
- Li Huowang notices that the cuts on the man’s body form the exact same hexagram from his earlier bone divination, treating the curse as a supernatural confirmation rather than a rant.
- He demands the stolen goods list from Magistrate Lou and, after examining it, adds the missing pieces: red pills, five nectars, and bone-to-rot.
- Li Huowang realizes the entire pattern of abductions and thefts fits a single *pill recipe* involving herbs, blood, and stolen bodies.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Lingchi Execution (凌迟)**: This is *not* just any execution—it’s “death by a thousand cuts,” a real historical method of execution in imperial China reserved for the worst crimes (treason, patricide, etc.). The number of cuts varied, but the goal was to prolong suffering. The crowd’s enthusiastic participation here is intentional: in the Dao-Twisted World, public torture is treated as communal catharsis, not tragedy.

**“Falling Red” (落红)**: The Chinese folk term for the blood from a ruptured hymen, traditionally tied to the first night of marriage. It was often fetishized as “proof” of virginity, a deeply problematic but culturally persistent belief. In this context, it’s being used as a forensic clue—the serial rapist never saw blood, meaning someone else had already been systematically taking it.

**The Alchemical Connection**: The final ingredients Li Huowang lists (hymen pill, five nectars, bone-to-rot) point toward a corrupt *alchemical recipe* involving virgin blood, sexual fluids, and ground infant bones. This is a direct nod to the darker corners of traditional Chinese alchemy, where “elixirs” were sometimes rumored to involve body parts and sexual essence. In the Dao-Twisted World, this isn’t just a rumor—it’s a live formula being executed right under the county’s nose.

### Lingchi (凌迟)

A historical Chinese method of execution by slow slicing, reserved for the most heinous crimes. The body was systematically cut apart over a prolonged period, with the number of cuts sometimes specified by law.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a chapter that shoves both horror and “aha!” moments right in your face. The public execution scene is deliberately hard to stomach—it’s supposed to feel grotesque and carnival-like, because that’s exactly how the Dao-Twisted World normalizes atrocity. But don’t let the blood distract you. The real treat here is watching Li Huowang’s brain make the jump from “this guy’s a disgusting liar” to “wait, *his body matches the divination hexagram.*” That moment of deduction is pure gold: he uses folk-belief logic (a dying man’s curse carving a prophecy into his own flesh) as a genuine investigative tool. And the final realization that all these thefts form a *pill formula*? That shifts the villain from a random degenerate to a careful alchemist who has been harvesting the entire county like a field. This chapter doesn’t advance the plot through combat; it advances it through cold, brilliant deduction.
