## Chapter Overview

This is the chapter where the hallucination starts *making sense*, and that might be the most terrifying thing of all. Li Huowang, having fully given up on parsing the logic of his modern-world vision, is suddenly confronted with something he can’t ignore: a grubby little scheme that proves his hospital reality isn’t just random mental noise—it has actors, motives, and a payoff. The chapter leans hard into paranoid detective work as Li Huowang interrogates, ambushes, and systematically breaks down the truth from a pack of low-rent criminals who thought they could milk a “lunatic” for gold. The emotional arc here is a brutal seesaw: first the crushing relief of knowing his family didn’t abandon him, then the rapid spiral back into suspicion—because if the whole world can be a lie, trust is a poison you can’t afford to drink.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang is stunned to learn the fat man watching him is just a hired hand for someone named “Dalong,” who specifically instructed the watcher to report if Li Huowang produced objects out of thin air.
- The fat man confesses he took the job because it paid 2500 a month and was easier than factory work, and that Dalong hasn’t paid him in two months.
- Li Huowang calls Dalong using the fat man’s phone, luring him and his crew to the culvert.
- A brutal, short fight erupts: Li Huowang is stabbed in the arm but turns the knife on his attackers, quickly subduing all four men.
- Under water-boarding interrogation, Wang Zhilong (Dalong) confesses he had Li Huowang stolen from the hospital, believing he could force him to produce gold.
- A powerful wave of relief hits Li Huowang when he realizes he wasn’t abandoned by his loved ones—but the feeling is short-lived as paranoia resurfaces.
- Li Huowang accuses Dalong of lying, pointing out the logical hole: if Dalong wanted to control him, why was Li Huowang left free on the streets?
- Dalong hysterically insists they *couldn’t* hold him—ropes slipped off, locked doors didn’t hold—reinforcing that Li Huowang’s “rules” don’t obey normal physics even in this world.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter gives us a meta-puzzle unique to the *Dao-Twisted World*: when a character’s hallucinations start forming a coherent, physical conspiracy, does that mean the hallucination is *more real*, or is the hallucination just getting better at lying? This reflects a core folk-horror and xianxia concept—the "Heart-Element" (心素) condition where belief and reality twist together. In Daoist-inspired cultivation settings, the line between inner mind and outer world is always thin; a sufficiently powerful delusion can manifest physically, a concept tied to "Cultivating the False into Reality" (修假为真). Here, Li Huowang isn’t fighting ghosts or gods—he’s fighting the *plausible fiction* of a mundane kidnapping scheme, which is arguably more disturbing because it forces logic upon chaos. Also notable: the symbolism of false gold (fake chain) vs. real gold (the supposed output of his ability) mirrors the novel's central obsession with truth, counterfeit, and value in a broken world.

### Heart-Element (心素)

A rare human constitution where belief and reality blur; those afflicted can make objects appear from nothing, but their perception of truth is permanently unstable.

### Cultivating the False into Reality (修假为真)

A core Dao-Twisted World principle: if a falsehood is cultivated with enough conviction, it can become physically real.

### Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司)

A government body that polices supernatural activity; its agents often wield bureaucratic and ritual power.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for the most unsettling kind of relief. This chapter offers Li Huowang a rare moment of emotional clarity—knowing his family didn't abandon him—only to rip it away with the same question he always asks: "Is this real, or is this just a more convincing lie?" The fight scene is shorter and more raw than the usual supernatural carnage; it’s a street brawl with a kitchen knife, shoe leather, and cobblestones. The real horror here is **systematic suspicion**: Li Huowang can’t even enjoy the truth because his trauma has taught him the truth is just a well-disguised trap. Pay attention to Dalong’s desperate explanation about the ropes and locked doors—it’s a tiny but terrifying clue that the horror of Li Huowang’s condition isn’t just internal; his presence breaks reality in ways that frighten even the crooks who try to cage him.
