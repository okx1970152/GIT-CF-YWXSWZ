## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow travelers, because this chapter is a mind-bender even by *Dao-Twisted* standards. The Dice, a top-tier Zuowandao, is captured and mutilated, but instead of breaking, he uses his final moments to plant a devastating seed of doubt in Li Huowang's mind. The revelation hits like a truck: Li Huowang's own memories might have been tampered with, suggesting he was the Zuowandao agent "Red Center" all along, manipulating events from the very beginning. The chapter is a masterclass in psychological horror, where the enemy's greatest weapon isn't a spell, but the terrifying possibility that the truth itself can be a lie. As one villain dies laughing, the foundation of our protagonist's identity is violently shaken, forcing him to claw his way back to reality with Zhuge Yuan's help, just in time to witness the desperate, grisly endgame of the Great Liang court.

## Key Plot Points

- The Dice, a high-ranking Zuowandao, is captured by the Great Liang Imperial Preceptor, Huangfu Tiangang. Even with his limbs severed and body charred by lightning, he remains defiant and cheerful.
- The Dice reveals that the Zuowandao's method is not to use lies, but to use truths in a way that leads victims to their own destruction. He claims Li Huowang is actually "Red Center," a Zuowandao agent who deliberately joined Danyangzi to eliminate Xuan Yang, a descendant of the dragon bloodline.
- Stricken by these planted memories, Li Huowang enters a severe identity crisis, unable to distinguish between his own past and the implanted persona of "Red Center."
- The hallucinatory Zuowandao mourns the Dice's death, demanding a monk perform Buddhist funeral rites.
- Zhuge Yuan snaps Li Huowang out of his spiral by urging him to think of his loved ones (Niuxin Village, Li Sui, Bai Lingmiao) and prevent Great Qi's fate from befalling Great Liang. He reveals the Dice's puzzle is not flawless and there's a way to reattach the Dragon Vein.
- Huangfu Tiangang enters the throne room, violently pulls Emperor Ji Lin from his dragon throne, and begins drawing talismans on him.
- A parasitic entity hidden inside Ji Lin is exposed and crushed under the Imperial Preceptor's foot, revealing the extent of the Zuowandao's infiltration within the imperial court itself.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Mandate of Heaven (气运 / Qiyun):** In traditional Chinese political philosophy, the Mandate of Heaven is the divine right that legitimizes a dynasty's rule. It is believed to be granted by heaven to a virtuous ruler and can be withdrawn if the ruler becomes corrupt, leading to natural disasters and rebellion. In *Dao-Twisted World*, this concept is given a visceral, parasitic twist. The Mandate is not a celestial blessing but a stolen, bleeding shard of divine power that holds up reality. It is a curse passed down through a family line that must be constantly maintained through sacrifice, including ritual suicide and self-mutilation. This warped interpretation turns a cornerstone of Chinese dynastic lore into a source of body horror and existential dread.

**The Dragon Vein (龙脉 / Longmai):** Closely related to the Mandate of Heaven, the Dragon Vein is a concept from Chinese geomancy (feng shui) and political cosmology. It refers to the terrestrial energy channels that run beneath the land, particularly associated with mountains and rivers. The strength and integrity of a dynasty's Dragon Vein are believed to determine its stability, prosperity, and lifespan. Tampering with or severing the Dragon Vein is considered a catastrophic act that can cause a dynasty to fall. In the novel, the Dice's claim that the Great Liang Dragon Vein is "already severed" signals an ontological disaster that threatens the very fabric of the country's reality, not just its political structures.

**The Constellation Sword (星宿剑):** This is not a generic "star sword." In Chinese cosmology, the constellations (星宿 / Xingxiu) are not just points of light; they are celestial mansions or official bureaus that govern specific aspects of earthly life and fate. A sword named "Constellation Sword" implies a weapon that can channel the authority and power of these celestial offices. The Imperial Preceptor's ability to summon purple lightning from it suggests he is wielding the punitive power of the heavens themselves, making his role as a protector (and potential executioner) of the dynasty's Mandate all the more terrifying.

**The Ziwei Star (紫微星):** The Ziwei Star (Polaris) is the most important star in Chinese astrological cosmology. It is the celestial emperor's throne, the center around which all other stars revolve. A dynasty is said to rise or fall based on the brightness and stability of the Ziwei Star. When the Dice claims the Dragon Vein is broken and the Yu'er Shen has arrived, he is essentially declaring that the emperor's celestial anchor is gone, and the entire cosmos is beginning to tilt into chaos.

### Yu'er Shen (于儿神)

A deity from the *Classic of Mountains and Seas* described with a human head and two serpent bodies; in the novel, it is a death-cult’s patron whose arrival signals the final collapse of a dynasty.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sits perfectly at the nexus of high-concept horror and brutal, practical power struggles. The Dice's death is a masterpiece of "evil villain exit," where he refuses to give the hero the satisfaction of a clean victory. Instead, he weaponizes his own death, leaving behind a psychic bomb that detonates inside Li Huowang's head. The horror here isn't from a monster tearing a body apart; it's from the idea that your own sense of self can be re-written, that you might have been a puppet without knowing it. Pay close attention to how Zhuge Yuan anchors Li Huowang—not with philosophy, but with a list of concrete, loved names. This is a key survival tactic in the Dao-Twisted World: when abstract truth becomes a weapon, you root yourself in the tangible and the emotional.
