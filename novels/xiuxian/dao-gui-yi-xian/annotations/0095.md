## Chapter Overview

Holy *crap*, fellow Daoists. Chapter 95 is not a chapter about fighting a monster. It is a chapter about fighting your own brain while your brain shouts *“TRICKED YA!”* at you from inside a kindergarten. Li Huowang is locked in a desperate chase with Layue Shiba in the modern world, but every time he gets close, reality hits him with a sucker-punch: his homeroom teacher, police cars, and finally—his mother kneeling on the pavement. The chapter doesn’t just show him losing his grip; it shows him clawing for purchase while the line between “real” and “illusion” gets flayed open. And that final scream? Buckle up, because it’s going to stick with you.

## Key Plot Points

1. **Chasing the Red**: Li Huowang chases Layue Shiba through the streets, noting that the entity has no eyelids—it is forced to “see” everything passively, which gives him a tactical edge (and some excellent folk-horror food for thought). The chase moves from public streets into a kindergarten.
2. **The Teacher Interrupts**: His high school homeroom teacher stops him mid-lunge. Li Huowang initially hesitates but then violently dismisses the encounter as “illusion”—the first crack in his certainty.
3. **Pursuit Into the Kindergarten**: Li Huowang scales a fence, knocks down a teacher, and grabs a little girl by her hair clips, which he identifies as Layue Shiba. His voice is cold and predatory, calling her out as a disguise.
4. **Police Intervention**: Cops swarm in with guns drawn. Li Huowang sneers at them, completely convinced this is part of the ruse. The chapter leans hard into his psychological isolation.
5. **The Mother's Entrance**: Sun Xiaoqin pushes through the crowd and kneels in front of the guns, begging the police not to shoot. She pleads with her son, telling him she sold the house and has nowhere left to turn. This is the breaking point.
6. **The Collapse**: Li Huowang’s voice breaks entirely. He drops to his knees, clutching the child, and screams a confession that cuts to the bone: “Mom!! I can’t tell!!! I really can’t tell them apart anymore!!!”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Layue Shiba Refresher**: The entity’s name literally means “The Eighteenth Day of the Twelfth Lunar Month,” tying it to the Chinese lunar calendar as a time-bound curse or pestilence spirit. In folk tradition, certain evil spirits are tied to specific calendar dates and lose power or change form outside those windows. The red motif—cherry hair clips, hats, store signs—is a classic folk-horror trick: it weaponizes a lucky/protective color (red wards off evil in mainstream Han culture) by making it the *signature* of evil, creating an ominous dissonance.

**The “Bewilderment” Thesis**: The chapter’s title, “Bewilderment,” is not just a sad feeling—it’s a metaphysical state in this universe. The Dao-Twisted World runs on *迷惘* (mí wǎng), a term that means disorientation but also connotes being lost in a fog, unable to tell path from pitfall. Unlike generic “madness,” *bewilderment* in this context is a cognitive trap: Li Huowang is not insane in the sense of losing logic; he is *over-logical*, applying hyper-rational doubt to everything, including the people who love him. In classical Chinese philosophy, “迷” is often the opposite of “觉” (awakening)—which makes his mother’s appearance a kind of tragic Bodhisattva move: she’s not fighting the monster; she’s trying to wake him up.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a **therapy-read nightmare**. If you’ve ever wondered what it feels like to have your brain gaslight itself in real time, this is it. Li Huowang is not a badass here; he is a raw, weeping, knee-buckled person whose only remaining anchor is his mother—and even she might be “fake.” Pay close attention to the structure: every time he *almost* reaches certainty, someone real shows up. The chapter weaponizes mundane fragility (a homeroom teacher, police sirens, a mom kneeling) against his fantasy logic. The horror is that he can’t tell if his mom is real, and *neither can we*. That’s the Dao-Twisted World’s deepest trick: it doesn’t show you fake things; it shows you real things that feel fake, and then watches you break trying to decide.
