## Chapter Overview

Welcome to Shangjing, folks—the crown jewel of Great Liang and the hornet’s nest where the Supervisory Heavenly Office calls home. Our battered little caravan finally rolls through the gates, and the sheer scale of it all is dizzying: thousands of heads, a drum tower you can barely see in the haze, and enough depth of history to make your head spin. But this isn’t a sightseeing trip. Between a bizarre “Seize the Bun Mountain” ritual that stinks of unspoken sacrifice, and an inn-side face-changer who picks the absolute wrong mark to show off, this chapter is a masterclass in **mismatched signals**. Bai Lingmiao is flaunting her new, sword-corrupted boldness—lounging on the cart roof, sassing Li Huowang, threatening to swap out her own eyeballs—while Li Huowang is quietly, desperately checking every corner for threats. And when that actor’s face literally shifts in front of him? The wrong trigger gets pulled, and we’re reminded that this guy is still *this close* to the breaking point. It’s a chapter of gorgeous world-building, a splash of Xianxia street fair, and a slow-burn dread that says: something’s watching, something’s waiting, and Li Huowang is walking right into its nest.

## Key Plot Points

- The group arrives at Shangjing, the ancient capital of the fallen Great Qi and the current seat of Great Liang. Li Huowang’s carriage—coffin-like enough to spook any guard—only passes inspection thanks to his Supervisory Heavenly Office token.
- Bai Lingmiao, now corrupted by the Purple-Tasseled Sword, behaves with bold, manic confidence: she sits atop the cart, openly complains about her eyes, stretches in Li Huowang’s lap, and mocks the Shangjing folk for wasting white buns.
- Li Huowang witnesses the “Seize the Bun Mountain” festival—a chaotic ritual where young men tear apart three towers of stacked buns, which he immediately notes resembles three incense sticks, implying a sacrificial undertone.
- After finding an upscale inn with a stage, Li Huowang and Bai Lingmiao settle down to eat. The group’s wandering dog, Mantou, is given food.
- A face-changing opera performer approaches Li Huowang to impress him. When the actor’s face shifts from old-sheng to dan to xiaosheng—an impressive stunt—Li Huowang reacts with violent paranoia, pressing a sword to the man’s throat and screaming, “What face-changing are you doing?! Who told you to change faces?!”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Face-Changing (变脸)** is a real and cherished art in Sichuan opera where performers change their painted masks in the blink of an eye—traditionally with a sweep of a fan or a toss of the head. It’s a jaw-dropping display of skill designed to electrify the audience. But in *Dao-Twisted World* logic, “faces changing” is *not entertainment*—it’s an existential alarm bell. Li Huowang has seen Zuowandao liars peel off identities like layers of skin. He’s watched Danyangzi’s three-faced body bloom from a single skull. So when a grinning stranger leans in and *switches his face without warning*, every trauma response fires at once. The *biaolian* (变脸) stunt becomes a horror trigger, not a party trick.

**Seize the Bun Mountain (抢包山)** is loosely inspired by real Chinese folk traditions where communities scramble for steamed buns from tall wooden frames during temple fairs, competing for good luck. The *longevity* character (*shou*, 寿) stamped on the buns reinforces the auspicious motive. But Li Huowang sees **three white towers** arranged like **incense sticks**—and in a world where every ritual might be feeding something hungry, that image makes the whole celebration feel like an offering disguised as fun.

**Shangjing (上京)** serves as the seat of the Supervisory Heavenly Office, a quasi-imperial supernatural law enforcement agency. The city itself is a palimpsest: layers of Great Qi foundation beneath Great Liang’s modern pomp. This “depth” (*diyun*, 底蕴) is exactly what makes it so dangerous for a wanted Heart-Element. The gates aren’t the barrier—the political and spiritual scrutiny inside them is.

### Purple-Tasseled Sword (紫穗剑)

A weapon of the "military path" that corrupts a normal user’s personality with overwhelming aggression; Bai Lingmiao’s boldness and manic cheerfulness in this chapter reflect its influence.

## Reading Guide

You can practically *hear* the tension in this chapter. On the surface, it’s an almost cozy travel log: new city, local festival, inn with a stage, dinner. But beneath that, every beat is a war drum. Bai Lingmiao’s voice has *completely* shifted—you’ll notice she’s no longer the tentative, ribbon-twirling girl. She’s sharp, she’s brash, she snarks at the locals and calls her own eyes “useless.” That’s the Purple-Tasseled Sword talking through her. Pay attention to how Li Huowang handles her: he’s gentle but firm, pats her head, doesn’t indulge her outbursts—a man trying to manage a ticking bomb he still loves. And his reaction at the end isn’t just PTSD. It’s the inevitable collision between a man trained to see deception in every change and an art form that *celebrates* change. If you’ve read this far, you know this doesn’t end with the actor simply walking away. The Office is here. The rituals are everywhere. And Li Huowang’s trigger finger is *tight*.
