## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists — Li Huowang’s head is doing backflips again. Fresh off a near-capture of the Zuowandao named North Wind, our favorite mental-patient-on-a-cosmic-scale gets yanked back to the White Tower hospital, and the first thing that hits him is a terrifying thought: *What if this world is real?* The chapter is a masterclass in emotional whiplash, swerving from paranoid metaphysical speculation to a raw, heartbreaking conversation with his mother about Yang Na’s depression. It’s a quiet chapter — no blood, no tentacles — but the stakes are personal, existential, and somehow even more brutal.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang, now back in the modern-world hallucination, wrestles with the possibility that the hospital reality might be genuine. He recalls his nature as a Heart-Element and speculates that he might have unconsciously used his Primordial One Qi.
- He attempts to will gold into existence and move objects with his mind, but fails completely, deepening his confusion.
- Sun Xiaoqin’s arrival triggers a painful irony: even if she’s a hallucination, Li Huowang cannot bear to see her aged face or her worry.
- His questioning reveals that Yang Na has been taken out of school by her parents due to depression. The revelation hits Li Huowang like a physical blow, twisting his guilt and sense of helplessness.
- Sun Xiaoqin unties his restraints against the guards’ frantic objections, giving him a moment of physical freedom.
- Li Huowang stands at the barred window, reaches for a patch of sunlight, and confesses his longing for this world to be real.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The chapter hinges on the concept of **Primordial One Qi (先天一炁)**, a foundational pillar in Daoist internal alchemy. In classical practice, this is the primal cosmic energy that precedes the division of yin and yang — the raw stuff of creation. To tap into it is to become a living bridge between the unmanifest and the manifest. In *Dao-Twisted World* terms, this is what makes a Heart-Element so terrifyingly valuable: they don’t just *use* qi; they *are* a leaky faucet for reality itself. Also worth noting: Li Huowang’s failed attempt to produce gold mirrors a classic *fangshu* (方术/recipe-scholar) trope — using esoteric arts to transmute base metals. His failure here becomes a subtle but crucial piece of evidence for his own self-doubt.

## Reading Guide
This is a breather chapter that somehow hurts more than a monster fight. Watch how Li Huowang applies the same paranoid, interrogation-hardened skills he learned against the Zuowandao to his own mother — and still can’t tell if her lies are real or scripted. The real horror here isn’t a cosmic truth bomb; it’s the quiet, mundane grief of realizing someone you love is suffering because of you, and you can’t even be sure they exist. The final image — a man behind bars reaching for sunlight — is pure *Dao-Twisted* poetry: beauty and confinement, hope and impossibility, all tangled together.
