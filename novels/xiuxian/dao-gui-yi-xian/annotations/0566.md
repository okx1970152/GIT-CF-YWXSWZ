## Chapter Overview

Okay, buckle up, Dao-disciples. Chapter 565, “Trap Within a Trap,” is the emotional implosion we’ve all been dreading and waiting for. The mask comes off, but not the one we expected. Li Huowang finally pieces together the giant, invisible puzzle that’s been running under his nose, and the person holding the last piece is the one person he thought he could trust absolutely: Zhuge Yuan. The chapter opens with Ji Lin suffering a horrifying, gender-flipping transformation (yes, you read that right), courtesy of Dice’s scheme. The national preceptor is furious and helpless, but Li Huowang has a potential solution—one that requires the bloodline of Great Qi. And that’s when the gears in his head, oiled by Hong Zhong’s memories, start to turn. What follows isn’t a monster fight; it’s a brutal, philosophical knife-fight between two sworn brothers, where trust, sanity, and the very nature of “self” are put on the operating table. Get ready for a chapter that hurts.

## Key Plot Points

- **Ji Lin’s Apotheosis Reversed:** The national preceptor’s attempt to pass on the Mandate backfires catastrophically. Ji Lin is physically transformed into a woman as the power is forcibly ripped away and absorbed by the current emperor, the lizard-man. The process is visceral and disorienting, confirming that the Mandate of Heaven in this world is a predatory, parasitic burden, not a divine gift.
- **Huangfu Tiangang’s Despair:** The national preceptor, seeing his centuries-long plan undone in an instant, is consumed by a cold rage. He’s not just defeated; he’s outplayed on a level he didn’t foresee. He dismisses Li Huowang’s initial attempt to help, believing the situation to be hopeless.
- **The Great Qi Gambit:** Zhuge Yuan proposes a radical, and seemingly insane, solution: use the bloodline of the *Great Qi* emperor—the very man Li Huowang killed—to reconnect Great Liang’s dragon vein. He insists the emperor isn’t truly dead, having been forged into an immortal, soul-woven body by his mother.
- **The Trap of Coincidence:** It’s this very “solution” that triggers Li Huowang’s final breakthrough in suspicion. He realizes that every step of his journey—finding the Supreme Extremity Confluence, meeting the Great Qi emperor, bringing him to Great Liang—was a path paved by Zhuge Yuan. The “coincidences” are too perfect.
- **Zhuge Yuan’s Confession:** For the first time in the novel, Zhuge Yuan raises his voice at Li Huowang. He admits he knew about Dice’s plan all along. He confesses that the Void Nian was his friend and that he orchestrated everything—not to hurt Li Huowang, but to use Dice’s trap as a bridge to save the dying world of Great Qi.
- **The Ultimate Breach of Trust:** Li Huowang is shattered. He demands to know why Zhuge Yuan didn’t just ask for his help. The answer, delivered with brutal honesty, is a single, devastating word: “Yes.” Zhuge Yuan admits he never fully trusted Li Huowang, fearing he might truly be Hong Zhong, the Zuowandao master of lies.
- **The Skeleton Acts Alone:** In a final, chilling moment, the Zhuge spine-sword on Li Huowang’s back tears itself free, carves a message into the floor, and sends it flying after the national preceptor. It acts on Zhuge Yuan’s will, independent of Li Huowang’s consent, proving that the plan is still in motion, trust or no trust.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Mandate of Heaven as a Parasite:** This chapter solidifies one of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*’s most terrifying subversions of classic Chinese political philosophy. The "Mandate of Heaven" (天命), which in Confucian thought is the divine right and moral duty of a ruler, is here revealed as a *stolen fragment of divine power that physically supports reality*. It cannot be passed on like a crown; it must be forcibly implanted, and the price of hosting it is the systemic cannibalization of the host’s bloodline. The old emperor isn't a ruler; he's a screaming, lizard-skinned battery whose family must commit self-slaughter to keep the whole show running. It’s a bitter, cynical inversion of the “celestial bureaucracy” trope.

**Zhuge Yuan’s “Phoenix” Ambition:** His hissed quote, “Though poor, my ambition remains firm; I will never lose my lofty aspirations” (穷且益坚，不坠青云之志), is a direct lift from the Tang dynasty poet Wang Bo’s “Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng” (《滕王阁序》). It’s a line about maintaining noble character in the face of poverty and obscurity. In this context, Zhuge Yuan uses it to defend his actions: his “impoverishment” is his damaged, flickering state of existence, and his “lofty aspiration” is the salvation of an entire world. It’s a beautiful, tragic boast from a man who has become a ghost for his cause.

### Mandate of Heaven (气运)

In this novel's context, the Mandate is a stolen fragment of divine power that holds up reality; it must be supported by a family willing to commit self-slaughter, making it a curse as much as a blessing.

A high-ranking member of the Zuowandao known for setting elaborate, long-term schemes, often involving dynastic politics and reality-warping lies.

## Reading Guide

This is a character-defining chapter for *Zhuge Yuan*. For the longest time, he’s been the anchor, the voice of reason, the calm in the storm. Here, we see the steel beneath the scholar’s robe. He is not just a good man; he is a ruthless strategist who was willing to deceive his own sworn brother, manipulate events on a geopolitical scale, and bet everything on a single, fragile coin. The question isn’t whether he’s good or evil—the novel has always rejected that binary. The question is whether the weight of his goal justifies the betrayal.

Pay close attention to the final scene with the spine-sword. That sword is Zhuge Yuan’s literal backbone, his will made manifest. The fact that it acts independently is a powerful signal. Li Huowang has spent this entire novel terrified of being puppeted by others. Now, even his most trusted friend’s legacy is acting without his input. This is the low point of a relationship that has been the story’s only genuine emotional safe space. How does Li Huowang reconcile with having been played by the one man he loved as a brother?
