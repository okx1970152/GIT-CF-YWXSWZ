## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a masterclass in psychological warfare wrapped in a dragon-scaled, limb-tearing nightmare. The Dice, now puppeting Gao Zhijian's monstrous Hundred-Soul Transformation Body, gloats to Li Huowang about how their entire partnership—the destruction of the Ji clan, the manipulation of Zhuge Yuan—was all a Zuowandao setup. But when Li Huowang tries to attack, he's completely outclassed. The Dice tears through Peng Longteng's summoned form like wet paper and proceeds to give Li Huowang a brutal five-horse dismemberment in the middle of the capital. Just when our favorite madman is about to be pulled apart, a miracle appears: sky lanterns bearing Lotus Children chanting White Lotus hymns, and descending from the clouds… Bai Lingmiao. She saves him, bandages him, and reveals a cold, distant truth that cuts deeper than any dragon claw.

## Key Plot Points

- The Dice, controlling Gao Zhijian’s body, reveals that Li Huowang was his unknowing accomplice in destroying the Great Liang Ji clan and framing Zhuge Yuan. He frames their "friendship" as a one-sided manipulation.
- Li Huowang attempts to betray the Dice by cutting off his own hand to attack him, proving he never truly believed the alliance.
- The Dice's possessed body is overwhelmingly powerful: even Peng Longteng is ripped apart in seconds.
- Using his color-manipulation ability, Li Huowang slows one of the Dice's claws, but is quickly overwhelmed by multiple limbs and subjected to a five-horse dismemberment.
- Just as Li Huowang is about to die, massive lotus-shaped sky lanterns appear, and Lotus Children chant a White Lotus hymn. The hymn unsettles the dragon's spine and forces the Dice to react.
- Bai Lingmiao descends from the sky like a bodhisattva, saves Li Huowang, and applies a miraculous healing balm.
- Li Huowang’s joy at seeing her turns to ice when she asks, coldly, if he received her letter—implying he ignored a message she sent, and that the separation was at least partly his doing.
- The Dice taunts Li Huowang one last time: "You believe the words of a Zuowandao?"—his only truth is that everything he says, even his "truths," can be a lie.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**White Lotus & Wusheng Laomu Hymns**: The Lotus Children chant, "The Azure Yang rises, the White Yang ascends, Wusheng Laomu saves all beings! The Red Yang falls, the Purple Yang dies, Nurtured Ease, Ultimate Bliss, Eternal Still Light!" This is a corrupted eschatological hymn drawn from the White Lotus tradition. It speaks of cosmic cycles (Azure, White, Red, Purple Yangs) leading to a final paradise—a "Nurtured Ease" (养安) and "Ultimate Bliss" (极乐) that mirrors the Pure Land of Buddhism. The "Eternal Still Light" (常寂光) is a term from Tiantai Buddhism for the ultimate reality. In the Dao-Twisted World, this hymn isn't prayer—it's a weaponized belief-system that physically disrupts the dragon's spine and forces a Zuowandao to react. It's the first time we see Bai Lingmiao's White Lotus affiliation not as a passive curse, but as an active, terrifying power.

**The Five-Horse Dismemberment (五马分尸)**: This was a real, ancient Chinese execution method where the victim's limbs and head were tied to five horses or carts, which were then driven in opposite directions, tearing the body apart. The Dice turns this historical atrocity into a public spectacle, shouting for the capital's citizens to watch and "throw money in the ring" like a street performance. It reinforces the Zuowandao's core philosophy: cruelty is entertainment, and the world is their stage.

### Five-Horse Dismemberment (五马分尸)

A historical Chinese execution method where the victim's limbs and head are tied to five horses (or carts) and pulled apart.

### Zi Yang (紫阳) / Red Yang (红阳) / White Yang (白阳) / Azure Yang (青阳)

Terms from Chinese Buddhist-Manichaean eschatology representing cosmic cycles of destruction and renewal, co-opted by the White Lotus tradition.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a brutal gut-punch in three acts: first, the cold realization that Li Huowang was a pawn; second, the visceral horror of watching him get torn apart; third, the emotional whiplash of Bai Lingmiao's return. Pay close attention to Bai Lingmiao's dialogue—specifically the line about the letter. This isn't just a misunderstanding; it's a deliberate act of narrative gaslighting by the Dice, who has already demonstrated he can make people *think* things happened. The biggest question the chapter leaves us with is whether Bai Lingmiao is truly "back" or if she's another layer of the Dice's trick. After all, as the Dice himself says: "You believe the words of a Zuowandao?" Trust nothing. Not even the rescue.
