## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in *Dao Guai Yi Xian*’s signature tension: the calm after the storm, where victory feels more like a precarious mask than a triumph. Li Huowang is bleeding, exhausted, but thinking faster than ever. He puppets Princess Anping’s severed head with Li Sui’s help—a grotesque masterpiece of political misdirection—while a bewildered Gao Zhijian arrives at his own palace gates, only to be treated like a trespasser. Meanwhile, back in the throne room, a paranoid and grief-raging Ji Lin dispatches a courtier for merely delivering bad news. The chapter sets up a three-way collision: a fake princess, a real amnesiac emperor, and a volatile tyrant who refuses to believe the obvious. Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this is the quiet before the storm, and the splatter pattern is already taking shape.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang survives the fight against the unknown Daoist who killed Princess Anping, but at a heavy cost. His wounds reopen and worsen, and he acknowledges the Daoist’s lightning arts were nearly as strong as Danyangzi.
- Peng Longteng savagely pulverizes the already-dead beggar-Daoist in a rage, smashing his body into paste with a rockery before dissipating when Li Huowang orders her to stop.
- Li Huowang uses Li Sui’s Black Tai Sui parasitic nature to puppet Princess Anping’s severed head, then her entire corpse, disguising her as still alive. The scene is vividly horrific: a beautiful head suspended on tentacles, a corpse hollowed out and re-inhabited.
- Gao Zhijian enters Shangjing City but finds himself completely lost. He has no memory of the capital and is stopped by the imperial guards at the palace gate, who nearly kill him when he tries to identify himself as the Emperor.
- Inside the palace, a terrified courtier reports to Emperor Ji Lin that Princess Anping is still missing. Ji Lin, in a fit of rage, orders the man beheaded. An old eunuch tries to soothe him with divination results, but Ji Lin refuses to accept them, demanding physical proof.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **Emperor/Zheng Tong** (正统) concept is central here. In Chinese imperial history, a “legitimate” emperor wasn’t just a bloodline claim—it was backed by the **Mandate of Heaven** (天命), a cosmic approval that could be demonstrated through rituals, omens, and the enforcement of social order. Gao Zhijian’s failure to be recognized by the palace guards isn’t just a plot hurdle; it’s a spiritual crisis. If the court doesn’t know him, does his Mandate of Heaven even exist? This hits the core confusion of a man who was told he was a king, only to be treated like a random stranger at his own front door.

The **Princess Anping puppet** sequence is a direct callback (and inversion) of traditional “Painted Skin” (画皮) ghost stories from *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio*. In those, a demon wears a beautiful woman’s skin to seduce victims. Here, Li Huowang weaponizes the same horror as a political tool—making a *dead* princess look alive to stall for time. Li Sui’s Black Tai Sui parasitism being used to control a severed head is a perverse twist on the “undead puppet” trope, made even more unsettling by the princess’s pretty head smiling and blinking naturally atop a writhing mass of tentacles.

The **eunuch’s divination**—a “single-lamp offering” (一灯供奉)—is a practice drawn from folk Daoist and Buddhist syncretism. A solitary lamp or candle could be used for spirit communication, especially to confirm a death. The fact that the eunuch already knows the outcome before Ji Lin accepts it reinforces the theme that *truth* and *political necessity* are often at war in the imperial court. Ji Lin doesn’t want facts; he wants a scenario he can control.

### Void Nian

A corrupted Nian beast in the Dao-Twisted World that drains yang life on contact, distinguished from the traditional folkloric Nian by its dissonant fusion of human and beast features.

### Supreme Extremity Confluence

a Daoist cultivation concept manifested in the novel as a funnel of living written characters, blending the abstract nature of text with the visceral, physical power of wind.

### Alabaster Corpse

A state of death where a cultivator’s body is preserved perfectly, maintaining a lifelike appearance, often found in high-level xianxia settings.

### Thunderclap Eclipse

a Daoist combat technique that converges multiple lightning strikes into a single devastating point of impact.

### Thousand-Armed Flesh-Buddha

A corrupted transformation of Zhengde Temple monks; a massive mound of pulsating, fleshy limbs and organs representing the perversion of Buddhist iconography into body horror.

### Bamboo Slip Corpses

Corpses that have been bound, interred, or displayed with bamboo slips, often marking them as part of a dark ritual or as containers for forbidden knowledge.

### Great Nuo (大傩)

The name of the mad cosmic entity controlling the Dao-Twisted World, derived from an ancient Chinese exorcistic ritual tradition, here inverted into the source of the world’s sickness.

### Doumu (斗姥)

A high Daoist deity, the Mother of the Big Dipper; in the novel, she is a Siming whose form appears as an infinite yin-yang ocean, causing mind-splitting horror on direct observation.

### Divine Blood Pearl (神血珠)

A valuable alchemical substance extracted from a god or high-level cultivator’s blood; used for rituals and weapon forging.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a perfect example of *Dao Guai Yi Xian*’s unique mode: the grim comedy of political horror. Li Huowang is literally using a dead princess’s face as a marionette to fool the city, while Gao Zhijian—the rightful Emperor—can’t even get past the gate. The juxtaposition is absurd, but the tone is dead serious. Pay close attention to the small details: the white silk ribbon around Li Sui’s neck to hide the stitching, the eunuch’s composed demeanor while his colleagues tremble, and Ji Lin’s pearl crown tassels shaking with his rage. These are the telling human touches that make the cosmic horror feel intimate. And if you thought the chapter was just a breather after the fight… think again. Every scene here is a fuse being lit. The fake princess parade, the wandering sovereign, and the furious tyrant are on a collision course, and the next chapter promises fireworks.
