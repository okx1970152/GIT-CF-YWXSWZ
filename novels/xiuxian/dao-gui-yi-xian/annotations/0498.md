## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow travelers of the Dao-Twisted World, because this chapter is a masterclass in emotional gut-punches wrapped inside a high-stakes heist. Li Huowang is just trying to mind his own business, scoping out a routine Office operation against a Child-God cult, when he spots something that turns his blood to ice: Zhuge Yuan's remains, desecrated into a spinal-cord sword wielded by the new Chief Recorder. What follows isn't just a tactical evaluation of an enemy—it's a raw, visceral collision between cold pragmatism and the burning need to honor a friend. The chapter balances battlefield observation, supernatural detective work, and a deeply moving internal conflict, all while setting up a confrontation that feels both personal and cosmically significant. If you've been missing Zhuge Yuan's presence, this chapter will hit you right in the heart—and then hand you a reason to fight.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Office vs. Dharma Sect:** Li Huowang observes a Supervisory Heavenly Office operation against a sect worshipping the Child-God Yú'er Shén. The Office is winning decisively, confirming their superior combat power.
2. **Zhuge Yuan's Judge's Brush:** Li Huowang spots a Daoist in the Office ranks wielding Zhuge Yuan's old Judge's Brush, confirming that the Office collected his belongings after his death.
3. **The Bone-Spine Sword & New Chief Recorder:** Li Huowang identifies the leader as the new Chief Recorder, a purple-lipped woman. Her weapon is the horror: Zhuge Yuan's spinal cord, refined into a sword with a shrunken head as its hilt. Bai Lingmiao sees it too.
4. **The Sword's Ability Revealed:** When the woman slashes with the bone-sword, Bai Lingmiao sees a rift open in space—a rift containing farmland and mountains. Zhuge Yuan deduces it leads to Great Qi. The sword is a key to a history-altering dimension, slicing enemies in half by closing the rift.
5. **Li Huowang's Decision:** Despite Zhuge Yuan's philosophical pleas to let it go—"a stinking bag of skin"—Li Huowang refuses to abandon his friend's remains. He camouflages himself as a Dharma Sect member, becomes invisible, and begins his approach.
6. **The Ambush Triggers:** As Li Huowang lunges at the Chief Recorder, her defensive artifact activates—a tattered robe covered in blades attacks him from all sides.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Judge's Brush (判官笔, Pànguān Bǐ):** This isn't just a fancy name. In Chinese mythology and wuxia, the Judge's Brush is the weapon of King Yama's judge, who writes fates in the Book of Life and Death. As a martial weapon, it's a sharpened metal stylus used to strike pressure points—very elegant, very lethal. Seeing it here, wielded by an Office Daoist, confirms that Zhuge Yuan's belongings are being used as standard-issue Office gear. It's a grim detail that reinforces the Office's merciless efficiency: nothing goes to waste.

**Heart-Pivot (心蟠, Xīn Pán):** The glossary reminds us that a Heart-Pivot is a rare human constitution, even more valuable than a Heart-Element. A Heart-Element makes fictions real; a Heart-Pivot is a central node of reality that can anchor an entire history. Refining a Heart-Pivot's spinal cord into a sword is not just desecration—it's turning a person's very essence into a weapon that can cut between worlds. The horror here isn't just emotional; it's ontological. They've weaponized a friend's soul-logic.

**The "Stinking Bag of Skin" Philosophy:** Zhuge Yuan's dismissal of his own body as "a stinking bag of skin" (臭皮囊) is a direct reference to Daoist and Buddhist attitudes toward the physical form—the body is impermanent, a vessel at best. In any other xianxia, this would be profound detachment. In the Dao-Twisted World, it's heartbreaking because Zhuge Yuan is *right*, and yet Li Huowang's refusal to accept it is the most human thing he's done in chapters.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a beautiful, brutal example of how *Dao Guai Yi Xian* weaponizes *memory* and *friendship* as plot drivers. Li Huowang doesn't have a stat sheet telling him to reclaim the sword; he has a ghost whispering in his ear and a debt that can never be repaid. Pay close attention to how his rage is *cold* rather than hot—he doesn't charge in blindly; he observes, calculates, and disguises himself. This is the Li Huowang who has survived by learning, and now he's applying those lessons to an act of love.

Also, watch Bai Lingmiao's role here. She sees the rift. She sees the horror. She doesn't scream or faint—she reports what she sees with alarming clarity, reinforcing her growth as a witness and potential combatant. And the Zuowandao hallucination's taunt? That's the narrative winking at you: "You thought this was a simple rescue mission? Think again."

Final note: the chapter ends on a classic "bait-and-switch" cliffhanger. Li Huowang thinks he's the ambusher, but she's already triggered a defensive formation. The question isn't "Can he win?"—it's "What is he willing to pay to get that sword back?" And knowing this novel, the answer is going to be horrifying.
