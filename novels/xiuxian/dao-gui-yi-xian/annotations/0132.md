## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow survivors. Last chapter was a baptism of fire—literally. This time, Li Huowang wakes up in the charred aftermath of Dead Town, only to discover that the line between ‘healed’ and ‘haunted’ is thinner than his brand-new, excruciatingly sensitive skin. Our boy is raw, both physically and mentally, and the chapter gives us a brutal, quiet moment of penance before yanking us right back into the cognitive mayhem. If you thought Danyangzi was gone, think again. The ghost isn’t just in the machine anymore—he’s in the mirror. And he brought a friend.

## Key Plot Points

- **A New Body:** Li Huowang wakes up after a full day unconscious to find his charred skin has flaked away, replaced by a pink, tender new layer. His recovery ability has skyrocketed, but it comes with a price—his pain sensitivity is now dialed up to eleven. Every movement, every breath, every *draft* is agony.

- **Penance in Dead Town:** Alone with only his dog Mantou for company, Li Huowang wanders the ruins. In a used-clothing shop, he finds a Daoist robe and then, in a grim act of atonement, begins burying the dead. He digs graves for the headless shopkeeper, a mother and daughter, and others, carving rough symbols on the doors he uses as grave markers because he’s forgotten how to write.

- **The Return of Danyangzi:** Just as he’s about to enter another house with a bamboo cradle, Danyangzi’s voice hits him like a thunderbolt. He sees the three-headed abomination perched on the rubble, watching him with that familiar, mocking smirk. The entity isn’t gone—it’s just been waiting.

- **Confrontation at the Cave:** Panicked and bleeding, Li Huowang sprints to the cave of the Ao-Jing Sect. He screams at the elders, demanding to know why Danyangzi is still there. The elders claim they’ve done their job and that they see nothing. But Li Huowang does.

- **A Vision of Two Horrors:** As his panic peaks, Li Huowang sees not just Danyangzi, but Jiang Yingzi—broken, mutilated, and staring at him with undiluted hatred. The chapter ends with the cold, clinical dismissal from the Ao-Jing elder: they handled the dead master; the living visions are the Heart-Element’s own problem.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**为什么袄景教说“不关我们事了”？(Why the Ao-Jing Sect Claims Irrelevance)**

This is a key moment for understanding the novel’s internal logic. The Ao-Jing Sect specializes in dealing with the *supernatural*—the soul, the divine, the literal pollution of a parasitic spirit. They used their rituals on Danyangzi’s presence, which they see as an external entity that has been successfully expelled.

But Li Huowang’s problem is now classified as a **心素幻境 (Heart-Element Hallucination)**. The novel’s mechanics distinguish between an external threat (a ghost you can exorcise) and an internal breakdown (a hallucination your own mind produces). To the Ao-Jing sect, the latter is a neurological or karmic sickness that falls outside their jurisdiction. They drew a clear line in the sand before the ritual: “We handle your master; your Heart-Element craziness is on you.” This is a brutal reminder that the Dao-Twisted World has hard rules. Just because something is *real* to Li Huowang doesn’t mean it’s real to the world, and the world’s “professionals” will abandon him the moment his problem crosses into the territory of his own fractured perception.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in pacing—a quiet, painful exhale after the explosive climax of the previous arc, but one that deliberately sets your teeth on edge. Li Huowang’s burial spree is not heroic. It’s self-flagellation. He’s physically torturing himself because the pain is the only thing that makes him feel less like a monster. The fact that he carves *cloth patterns* instead of words because he’s *forgotten how to write* is a subtle but devastating sign of his degradation.

And then the other shoe drops. The return of Danyangzi is horrifying, but the addition of Jiang Yingzi’s vengeful ghost is a new, cruel twist. Is she really there? Is his guilt so powerful it’s manifesting a ghost from his own trauma? The old man’s final line—“we don’t handle Heart-Element visions”—is the thematic gut punch. Li Huowang has won the battle against the external enemy, only to be left completely, utterly alone to fight the war inside his own skull.
