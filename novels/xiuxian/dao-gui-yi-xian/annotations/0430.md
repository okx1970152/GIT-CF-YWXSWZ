## Chapter Overview

Sometimes, you just can't catch a break. Fresh off a hallucinatory identity crisis, Li Huowang finds himself literally skewered together with the very people he's trying to kill—the Zuowandao—because the Supervisory Heavenly Office can't tell the difference. This chapter is a claustrophobic, roof-shattering brawl where Li Huowang fights with the desperate rage of a man screaming *“I’m not one of them!”* while everyone around him assumes he is. It’s messy, bloody, and ends with him standing over a pile of dead agents and asking a terrifying question about the one person he's been searching for.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Li Huowang snapping out of his internal fog and immediately attacking the three Zuowandao members who have been impersonating his companions. He kills one of them mid-fall, but is quickly trapped by the arriving Supervisory Heavenly Office agents, who use an Eight Trigram formation to cage all three remaining combatants together.

In a desperate attempt to prove his innocence, Li Huowang tears off his own face—a gruesome, visceral act of self-vindication. It doesn't work. The agents only tighten their assault, skewering him and the two surviving Zuowandao members together with flag-spears and applying sealing talismans.

When the agents think they have him subdued, Li Huowang, his rage hitting a fever pitch, unleashes Li Sui's tentacles to break free. Using Li Sui’s ability to share his pain with others (by stabbing her own ribs into her lungs), he incapacitates the agents and proceeds to slaughter every single one of them. The chapter concludes with him confronting the surviving Zuowandao members, demanding to know the location of the mysterious "Heart-Element North Wind."

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Eight Trigram (八卦, Bā Guà) Formation:** This is a classic defensive and binding technique in Chinese fantasy, derived from the ancient divinatory symbols of the *I Ching*. The Eight Trigrams (Heaven, Earth, Water, Fire, etc.) represent cosmic forces, and their arrangement can be used to create a reality-warping prison, trap, or battlefield. In this chapter, the purple-robed Daoist uses a "mine-type" version, collapsing the roof to trap his targets. Note the chanted invocation of the "Six Jia" and "Six Ding"—these are powerful spirit-generals in Daoist ritual, often called upon to act as a celestial strike force.

**The Flag-Sword and Point-Sealing:** The length-changing, homing flag-spear and the talisman-to-the-forehead combo is a textbook example of how xianxia handles "restraining" techniques. The *acupoints* (穴) are vital energy nodes in the body; piercing them is the go-to method for a magical body bind. Here, the agents use it on both Li Huowang and the Zuowandao, treating them all as a single threat—a massive tactical blunder.

**Li Sui’s Shared Pain Attack:** This is a fascinating application of the Black Tai Sui’s parasitic bond. By damaging her own body, she forces a sympathetic reaction on everyone who was magically linked to Li Huowang. It’s less a combat move and more of a tactical "reset button," exploiting the very bind the agents created to break it.

## Reading Guide

This is pure, unfiltered Li Huowang: a man pushed past the point of reason, fighting not for victory but just to be *heard*. The sheer frustration of being mistaken for a trickster deity when you're just a broken guy with a sword is palpable. The chapter is a brutal highlight reel of his power: the instinctive violence, the grotesque willingness to self-harm as proof, and the terrifying symbiotic bond with his parasite-daughter, Li Sui. If you've been waiting for him to stop just reacting and start *demanding* answers—this is your chapter. The cliffhanger question is a game-changer.
