## Chapter Overview

Holy *crap*, this chapter throws down in the Imperial Palace like a heavyweight title match between every major faction in the Dao-Twisted World. The Dice, using Gao Zhijian's body as a puppet, is holding the entire Great Liang Dragon Vein hostage, and Li Huowang is forced into a corner where killing his old friend would doom a dynasty—but letting him go means unleashing a Zuowandao with cosmic leverage. Meanwhile, Bai Lingmiao returns with a full White Lotus entourage, the Ao-Jing Sect shows up, a mountain-sized Buddha statue wakes up to join the brawl, and the Supervisor finally steps in. Get ready: this is one of those chapters where the world's political and supernatural chessboard gets flipped upside down.

## Key Plot Points

- Bai Lingmiao returns with her White Lotus followers, now powerful enough to perform the Incense Spirit Possession ritual—forcing a god *into* the possessed Gao Zhijian to buy Li Huowang an opening.
- Peng Longteng hurls Li Huowang at Gao Zhijian for a repeat of their childhood duel, but the Dice taunts him mid-strike: "If I die, the Dragon Vein goes with me." Li Huowang's blade stops an inch short.
- The Dice boasts that he has outsmarted everyone: "I'm the Dragon Vein now. I can shut my eyes and let you kill me, and you won't dare." His identity-protection gambit renders Li Huowang powerless.
- The Ao-Jing Sect (led by Zu Yi) and countless other sects fill the Imperial Palace grounds—the entire cultivated world has gathered for this crisis.
- A living Buddha statue carved from a mountain descends to confront the Dice, revealing itself as the true Supervisor (Profound Feminine).
- The Dice laughs at the Supervisor, calls him "too late," and swallows the entire Dragon Vein—including the emperors—before vanishing from reality.
- The hallucination collapses: only the Ao-Jing Sect, the White Lotus, the Supervisor, and the wounded Huangfu Tiangang remain. Everything else was fake.
- Huangfu Tiangang, healed by the Supervisor, immediately tries to kill Li Huowang, blaming him for the Dice's rampage. The Supervisor talks him down—barely.
- Gao Zhijian is gone, the Dragon Vein is gone, and the Dice has won this round.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Dragon Vein (龙脉)** is a concept from Chinese geomancy and political cosmology—the underground energy channel that determines a dynasty's legitimacy and fate. In standard xianxia, it's a passive resource. In *Dao Gui Yi Xian*, it's a *living chain of corpse-emperors* that the Dice can eat. This chapter explicitly treats the Mandate of Heaven not as divine favor but as a *parasitic burden* that a family must bear by committing horrors.

**The Incense Spirit Possession (香神起乩)** is a corrupted version of the classic *Qitong* (乩童) ritual, where a mortal invites a deity to inhabit their body through self-mutilation. Here, the White Lotus disciples stab themselves in the head and pierce their cheeks—proof of authenticity through blood—before summoning a god into their enemy. It's a risky tactic because the vessel must be a body, and they're shoving a spirit into an unwilling host.

**The Supervisor (司天监)** has been a shadowy background figure for a while, but this chapter reveals his name: Profound Feminine (玄牝). This is a direct reference to the *Dao De Jing*, where 'Xuanpin' is the mysterious, generative female principle of the Dao—the root of heaven and earth. Using it as the name for a cosmic-level exorcist is a delicious piece of Taoist irony.

### Mandate of Heaven (气运)

In this novel's context, the Mandate is a stolen fragment of divine power that holds up reality; it can only be supported by a family willing to commit self-slaughter, making it a curse as much as a blessing.

### Void Nian (虚年)

A corrupted Nian beast that drains yang life on contact; not seen in this chapter but previously established as a threat from the Zuowandao's arsenal.

## Reading Guide

This is the chapter where the Dice's long con pays off. He didn't come to fight—he came to *claim a seat at the highest table*. By swallowing the Dragon Vein, he has effectively made himself the emperor of the empire's fate, holding Great Liang's entire future hostage inside his stomach. Li Huowang is not just outmatched physically; he's outmatched *conceptually*. Killing the Dice means killing Gao Zhijian and collapsing a dynasty. Not killing him means letting a Zuowandao run the world.

Bai Lingmiao's return with full White Lotus power is a major character beat. She's no longer the helpless girl. She has a sect, rituals, and the confidence to back Li Huowang without asking permission. But this also sets up a tension: her new power is tied to a cult Li Huowang has every reason to distrust.

The final confrontation between Huangfu Tiangang and Li Huowang is raw and personal. The Preceptor is not being unfair—the Dice did follow Li Huowang here. But the Supervisor's calm intervention shows that he sees the bigger picture. The question hanging over this chapter: what does a Zuowandao do when they have *everything*?
