## Chapter Overview
Our boy Li Huowang is back in the psych ward, and he’s just pulled the slickest move of his career: he outed his scheming ex-doctor Wang Wei to the hospital authorities, getting him hauled off by prison guards without laying a finger on the guy. But no sooner has the dust settled than the Sitian Jian (Ji Zai) pops in with some *alarming* news: the Zuowandao are wiped out, and now it’s time for Li Huowang to help a Siming stage a cosmic coup against Yin-Yang Doumu. The storm is brewing, and even the gods are scared.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang outmaneuvers Wang Wei: Instead of confronting or using him, he simply reports Wang Wei’s obsessive scheming to Dr. Yi Donglai, getting him arrested. It’s a cold, elegant win that shows Li Huowang has learned to work the system.
- The Sitian Jian reveals an existential threat: The calm is broken. Something bigger than Yu’er Shen is stirring in the White Jade Capital, and even Simings are feeling the pressure.
- Li Huowang learns he must help seize the True and False Ways from Yin-Yang Doumu, starting by finding and eliminating her hidden Heart-Pans. The target: North Wind, who was just arrested.
- Ji Zai drops a horrifying implication: If he (Ji Zai / future Li Huowang) is destroyed, everything that has happened will be rewritten. The stakes are no longer local—they’re reality-spanning.
- Li Huowang is left to plan alone, concluding that he must build power quietly while he still can, to protect the people he loves.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Sitian Jian (司天监)**: In historical China, this was a real official title for the director of the Imperial Observatory, responsible for astrology and calendar-making. In the Dao-Twisted World, it’s been elevated to a cosmic role—a guide between mortals and the divine, sometimes appearing as a vision or possessing a person. Think of it as the celestial CEO of timekeeping and fate-spotting.
- **Heart-Pan (心蟠)**: A mortal who serves as a Siming’s “anchor” in the physical world—the god’s causal connection to reality. If a Siming is the software, the Heart-Pan is the hardware it needs to boot. Destroy the Heart-Pan, and the Siming loses its foothold.
- **Yin-Yang Doumu (阴阳斗姥)**: The Siming who controls the True and False Ways. In Daoist mythology, Doumu is the Mother of the Big Dipper, a star deity associated with life, death, and cosmic balance. Here, she’s been corrupted into a multi-Way entity Ji Zai wants to *overwrite*.
- **The True and False Ways (真假天道)**: A cosmic dichotomy that maps onto the novel’s core tension—what is real vs. what is cultivated into being real. If the Zuowandao were the cult of falsehood, Doumu is the archon of that domain.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a textbook example of Li Huowang’s quiet evolution from reactive victim to strategic predator. He doesn’t rage, doesn’t panic, doesn’t even get his hands dirty—he just makes a phone call and lets the system eat his enemy. And then, in the second half, he gets dropped into a cosmic conspiracy so big that his only response is weary acceptance. The contrast is the point: the world’s threats are scaling up exponentially, but Li Huowang is meeting them with the same cold pragmatism he just used on Wang Wei. Also, the “you of the future didn’t tell you of the present” line is pure lovecraftian horror—knowledge itself becomes a contaminant that reveals position to higher beings.
