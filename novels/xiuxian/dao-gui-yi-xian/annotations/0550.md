## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up. This chapter is a masterclass in *atmospheric dread*. Li Huowang has just made a deal with the Director’s Office to give them a secret about the Upper Extremity Gateway, in exchange for the Director taking care of the Dice. But the moment he thinks he has the rules of this new game figured out, the narrative flips the board. No monster fight—just a quiet, devastating revelation that his perception has been tampered with, and the Director is playing chess five moves ahead.

## Key Plot Points

- Zhuge Yuan, the monk apparition, and the Zuowandao ghost all simultaneously warn Li Huowang that the woman beside him, “Shangguan Yuting,” is a fake, placed there without his awareness.
- Li Huowang realizes she has the face of Princess Anping, indicating the Director’s Office can perfectly project an illusory person into real space, completely bypassing his senses.
- Confronted, the fake Shangguan Yuting calmly reveals that *she herself* is the “secret” being traded for the deal, implying the Director’s true purpose is surveillance more than exchange.
- Li Huowang enters Great Qi, only to find the world plunged into a perpetual, crushing darkness caused by a Sky Dog Devouring the Sun Heavenly Calamity.
- In the darkness, Li Huowang is attacked by giant claws and other unseen entities, forcing him to fight blind by relying on touch and instinct.
- Over three days, the Calamity persists, and the evil spirits grow more powerful. Li Huowang discovers that looking at certain entities triggers his Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings.
- The chapter ends with Li Huowang detecting a new, unknown threat through the ground, signaling the hunt is far from over.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter anchors its horror in two classic Chinese supernatural tropes. First, the **Sky Dog Devouring the Sun (天狗食日)** is an ancient folk explanation for a solar eclipse, where a celestial dog is said to swallow the sun. In the Dao-Twisted World, this is not a natural astronomical event but a *Heavenly Calamity*, an ontological disaster that turns the world into a hunting ground for evil spirits. The sun’s absence is normalized as a state of persistent, unending night, which is far more terrifying than a temporary eclipse.

Second, the **Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings (十情八苦)** is a twisted expansion of the Buddhist Eight Sufferings. In orthodox Buddhism, the Eight Sufferings (birth, aging, sickness, death, parting from what we love, meeting what we hate, unattained goals, and the suffering of the five aggregates) are the path to enlightenment. The Dao-Twisted World weaponizes this concept: instead of seeking transcendence, the Cultivate the False philosophy exploits these very emotions to fuel supernatural horror. Looking at a creature can trigger these internal “sufferings,” warping the body from the inside out, which is why Li Huowang fights blindly.

## Reading Guide

This is a perfect example of how *Dao Gui Yi Xian* turns bureaucratic paranoia into a weapon. The real horror here isn’t the monster in the dark; it’s the quiet realization that the Director’s Office has a technology (or ability) to seamlessly insert an illusory person into your reality, and you have no way of knowing they’re there until someone *else’s* hallucination tells you. The Director’s casual admission—“I’m the secret”—is a power move that recontextualizes the entire transaction. Li Huowang wasn’t buying help; he was buying a leash.
