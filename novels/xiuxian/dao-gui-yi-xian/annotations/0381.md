## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a lore bomb wrapped in a panic attack. Li Huowang finally gets his hands on the Supervisory Heavenly Office's official files on the Heart-Element—but what he finds isn't a solution, it's a completely alien description of what he *should* be capable of. The gap between the book's mythical "reality-warper" and the broken, hallucination-ridden man he actually is has never been wider. And when Sima Lan, the Mohist scholar, casually drops that North Wind is a Zuowandao "Four Joy," Li Huowang's world tilts again: the one person who might have answers is the one person whose entire existence is a lie. This ain't a power-up chapter—it's a reality-check from hell.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang pores over the Supervisory Heavenly Office's book collection, desperately seeking a way to solve the Heart-Element problem. He finds nothing but horrific instructions on how to *use* a Heart-Element's body parts as crafting materials, leaving him disgusted and empty-handed.
- Sima Lan catches Li Huowang with the books and questions his interest. Li Huowang covers by saying he was researching North Wind as a potential enemy.
- Sima Lan launches into a classical, abstruse lecture on the Heart-Element's metaphysical nature (Primordial Qi, the Grand Beginning). Li Huowang bluntly cuts him off to get a straight answer.
- The blind bird translates: a Heart-Element has an extra "Primordial Qi" on top of normal emotions, allowing them to "overturn mountains and seas" with a thought. This terrifying power, however, is only triggered when they are pushed into absolute desperation.
- Red Center reappears to mock the lecture, pointing out that Sima Lan has never actually seen a living Heart-Element—he's just reciting dogma. The irony that the "monster" is sitting right in front of him is not lost on Li Huowang or the reader.
- Li Huowang's mind fractures as he compares the book's superhuman description to his own pathetic, hallucination-plagued reality. The disconnect is deeply unsettling.
- Sima Lan confirms North Wind is a Zuowandao "Four Joy" and admits the Supervisory Heavenly Office has no effective countermeasure, leaving Li Huowang to grapple with the terrifying implication that his greatest source of information is as lost as he is.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Mohist Connection (墨家):** Sima Lan is identified as a follower of the Mohist school, a philosophical tradition from the Warring States period. In this novel, the Mohists seem to function as a bureaucratic, knowledge-keeping arm of the Supervisory Heavenly Office, distinct from the more mystical Daoist or Buddhist sects. Li Huowang's blunt "I’m a crude man" response clashes with their scholarly formality, highlighting the class and education gap that defines much of this world's social friction.

**Primordial Qi and the Grand Beginning:** The bird's explanation that a Heart-Element possesses an extra "Primordial Qi (先天一炁)" is a direct lift from classical Daoist cosmology. In texts like the *Liezi*, the "Grand Beginning (太始)" is a primordial state before form and substance. The novel takes this abstract philosophy and weaponizes it: a Heart-Element’s emotions can literally *become* this fundamental force, allowing them to rewrite reality. But Li Huowang’s own experience—seeing dead people, body dysmorphia, hallucinations—suggests the “official” version is a sanitized myth that ignores the catastrophic psychological cost.

**The Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings (十情八苦):** This is a deliberate expansion of the standard Buddhist concept of the "Eight Sufferings" (birth, age, sickness, death, etc.). By adding two more emotions, the novel creates a unique metaphysical framework where a Heart-Element's internal, private pain is the very fuel for their world-breaking power. It makes Li Huowang's suffering not just a tragedy, but a loaded gun.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in cognitive dissonance. The horror here isn't a monster—it's the *disconnect* between what Li Huowang is told he is and what he experiences himself. The "overturning mountains and seas" line is supposed to be awe-inspiring, but when he glances at his spectral followers (Red Center, the nun, the headless warrior), it just sounds like a sick joke. Pay close attention to Sima Lan's subtle disdain when Li Huowang admits his illiteracy—it’s a quiet reminder that in this world, knowledge is power, and knowledge is guarded by social class. Finally, the revelation that the target he's been hunting (North Wind) is so dangerous that even the Office has given up should leave you feeling exactly the way Li Huowang does: trapped between a terrifying myth and a painful reality, with no clear path forward.

## Deeper Dive: The Zuowandao "Four Joys"

Sima Lan casually drops that North Wind is one of the "Four Joys (四喜)" of the Zuowandao. This isn't just a fancy title; it places North Wind among the sect's highest echelons. The term "Joy (喜)" is a direct, horrifying inversion of its normal meaning—it refers to the corrupted Deity of Joy (喜神), an entity that feeds on laughter and mirth born from madness and deception. For North Wind to be a "Four Joy" means they are not just a random liar, but an apex predator of cognitive terror, a master of the game whose very existence validates the Zuowandao's philosophy of "cultivating the false into reality."
