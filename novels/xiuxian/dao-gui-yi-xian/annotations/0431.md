## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a high-octane, blood-pumping chapter, folks! Li Huowang, fully in his Hongzhong persona, is on the warpath. Fresh off butchering a few low-level Zuowandao tiles, he's got one thing on his mind: hunting down the Heart-Element known as "North Wind." This chapter is a brutal, fascinating look at our protagonist's descent into a cold, pragmatic hunter. He's no longer just surviving; he's actively infiltrating, interrogating, and executing. And that final reveal? The golden palace isn't just a luxury flex—it's a promise of the insane scale of the power structure Li Huowang is about to dive headfirst into. Buckle up, this ride's going deep.

## Key Plot Points

*   Picking up from the last chapter, Li Huowang is pressuring a group of Zuowandao for North Wind's location. When one tries to play dumb, Li Huowang decapitates him on the spot without a second thought, establishing his new, ruthless authority.
*   He interrogates the last standing *pai* tile—a woman whose skin shifts to resemble a *lan* flower. Under his strangling grip, she reveals North Wind is at the Imperial City's East Gate with most of the other high-ranking Zuowandao. She dies whispering a love poem, a final act of psychological manipulation that briefly unnerves Li Huowang.
*   Li Huowang switches his disguise, swapping Er Jiu's skin for the *pai* woman's face and abandoning his red robes. He then gallops towards the Imperial City, confirming the info by grabbing and threatening two more Zuowandao on the way.
*   He arrives at the East Gate to find a chaotic battle between the Zuowandao and the Imperial Guard. He watches from a rooftop, trying to spot North Wind among the fighters.
*   A lone Imperial Guard opens the gate to wave for help, drawing the Zuowandao inside. Li Huowang suspects this guard is the swapped consciousness of North Wind. He becomes invisible and follows the trail of blood and bodies into the depths of the palace itself.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **"The mountain has trees... and the trees have branches..."**: This is a famous line from an ancient Chinese poem, "Yue Ren Ge" (越人歌). It's a declaration of unrequited love. The Zuowandao's *lan* woman reciting it is the ultimate mind game—cloaking a cruel, deceptive life in the language of genuine, tragic romance. It deliberately blurs the line between a performance of love and a final expression of a twisted, real emotion, designed to haunt the killer. This is peak Zuowandao psychological warfare.
*   **"Lan (兰)" as a Metamorphosis**: In Chinese lore, a *lan* (orchid) is a symbol of nobility, elegance, and refined beauty. Its mutation here into a fleshy, face-morphing flower used by the Zuowandao is a perfect example of the novel's theme of corruption. It takes something pure and repositions it as a grotesque tool for deception.
*   **The Golden Palace**: Historical Chinese imperial palaces did use a vast amount of gold leaf and gilding, but a palace entirely made of solid gold is a fantastical exaggeration. In the context of the Dao-Twisted World, it signals the pinnacle of worldly power, a place so saturated with "yang" energy and authority that it becomes a beacon for high-level cultivators and monsters alike. It's not just a building; it's a supermassive power node.

## Reading Guide

This is a fantastic chapter to watch Li Huowang's transformation in real-time. Notice how he justifies his actions? Each kill is a pragmatic step towards his goal. But the Zuowandao's final trick—dying with a poem on her lips—shows they understand *him* better than he understands himself. They know he's not a pure monster yet, and they exploit that flicker of humanity. The chapter keeps you guessing about North Wind: is he the fleeing soldier, someone among the green-masked figures, or is he already long gone? The final description of the golden palace is a classic *Dao Guai Yi Xian* setup: a breathtakingly beautiful, awe-inspiring place that we *know* is soaked in an even more sinister kind of horror than what we've seen before. That's your cliffhanger.

## Additional Lore & Mythology

*   **The Brocade Guard and the Imperial City**: The "Brocade Guard" (锦衣卫) is a real historical institution from the Ming Dynasty, serving as the imperial secret police. Their presence here, especially one potentially possessed by a Heart-Element, adds a layer of historical dread to the supernatural chaos. The Imperial City itself, as the seat of the Emperor, is the ultimate hub of worldly power and order in the Great Liang Empire, making it the perfect target for the chaotic, nihilistic forces of the Zuowandao.
