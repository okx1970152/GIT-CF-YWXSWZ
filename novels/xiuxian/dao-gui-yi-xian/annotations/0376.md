## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a chapter that moves fast and hits hard—not with gore, but with the cold, bureaucratic brutality of the court. Our boy Li Huowang finally finds a gig that gets him out of the city, and it's basically a prison escort. The catch? The prisoner is a screaming, delusional man claiming to be the Emperor of the Great Liang Dynasty. The whole thing is over in a flash: a quick trip to the Meridian Gate, a red token thrown, and a head rolling. But as always in the Dao-Twisted World, the real story is in the details—the golden mask, the belly full of pills, and the citizens swarming the blood like it's a blessed feast. This chapter is a masterclass in showing, not telling, the sheer weight of political horror and how the supernatural bones of this world are buried just beneath the surface of everyday life.

## Key Plot Points

- Ji Xiang, still processing the sheer scale of what Li Huowang did, explains to an old woman that the kid survived the Dung-Beetle Ascension, a secret art from the Ao-Jing Sect that almost always kills its user. This cements his suspicion that Li Huowang is not just a petty vengeful schemer but something far more dangerous—and he wants nothing more to do with his debt.
- Li Huowang reports for his escort duty, finding a motley crew of outsiders: a kid with a blind man, and a man riding a cow backward while chanting to a clay Buddha statue. It's clear the court is scraping the bottom of the barrel for people willing to touch this job.
- The prisoner is revealed: a man in a strange, archaic golden mask who claims to be the "Emperor of the Great Liang Dynasty." His words are met with total indifference from everyone except Li Huowang, who feels a brief, sharp jolt of unease.
- The execution is brutally efficient: a single swing, a head rolling, and the golden mask is whisked away to the palace. The soldiers vanish instantly, and the citizens swarm the corpse to soak their buns in the blood.
- Li Huowang, ever the opportunistic investigator, cuts open the dead man's distended belly and discovers it is packed full of alchemical pills. The "emperor" was literally being consumed from the inside by his own greed for immortality.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Emperor Who Never Was: Why "Daliang" Matters.** The man screams he is the "Emperor of the Great Liang Dynasty" (大梁皇帝). While "Liang" is a common enough dynasty name, in the context of the Dao-Twisted World, it's a political landmine. For a prisoner to claim this title, he's either a delusional pretender or a puppet being purged. The "Great Liang" might have no connection to our history at all—or it could be the name of a fallen dynasty that the current imperial family is trying to erase. Either way, calling yourself an Emperor in a world where Siming and gods play with thrones is a one-way ticket to the chopping block.

**The Buns of Anguish: Blood as Folk Medicine.** The citizens swarming to soak steamed buns (馒头) in the blood of an executed "emperor" isn't just mindless cruelty—it's a grim echo of real-world Chinese folk belief. For centuries, people believed that the blood of a hero or a powerful being (especially one executed unjustly) could cure illness or bring luck. The most infamous literary example is in *Lu Xun's Medicine*, where a family buys a bun soaked in a revolutionary's blood to cure their son's tuberculosis. Here, the Dao-Twisted World takes that superstition and twists it into something even more grotesque and transactional.

**Golden Masks and Sanxingdui Ghosts.** Li Huowang directly compares the prisoner's mask to a Sanxingdui artifact. This is a hefty cultural nod. The Sanxingdui civilization (c. 1600–1046 BCE) left behind incredibly surreal, abstract bronze masks with protruding eyes and geometric features. They were likely used in shamanistic rituals to connect with gods or ancestors. The sight of such an archaic, sacred artifact being worn by a condemned man is a power move—and a hint that this "emperor" might have been involved in something far older and more dangerous than mere politics.

## Reading Guide

This is a deceptively quiet chapter. Don't let the short distance to the Meridian Gate fool you—the horror here is systemic. Li Huowang is learning a brutal lesson about the cost of power in the capital. That screaming man wasn't just a pretender; he was a symbol. His mask, his bloated belly of pills, the way the soldiers evacuated the scene—it all points to a purge orchestrated by a higher power, possibly even the Siming or the current emperor himself. Pay close attention to Li Huowang's final act: his decision to cut open the corpse. He's no longer a startled newcomer. He's a forensic investigator of the grotesque, looking for clues in a world where even the dead are screaming warnings. The real question isn't if Li Huowang will be next, but when a decree is signed that sends *him* to the chopping block.
