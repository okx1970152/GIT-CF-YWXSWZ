## Chapter Overview
Blood, lies, and mahjong tiles fly in a three-way free-for-all that reads like a fever dream. Li Huowang, with a newly formed hand of writhing worms, joins Danyangzi and the Zuowandao in a chaotic dance of shifting alliances. Every few moments, the team-ups flip: one moment Li Huowang and Danyangzi gang up on the Zuowandao, the next Danyangzi and the Zuowandao turn on Li Huowang. It’s not about winning anymore—it’s about dragging everyone else down with him. The Zuowandao, desperate to survive, try a classic psychological gambit, but Li Huowang’s paranoia has evolved into something sharper: he treats everything they say as a weapon, and swings back just as hard. Get ready, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a brutal masterclass in using lies as both shield and blade.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang uses worm-flesh to replace his severed hand, gaining a grotesque but functional new limb from the Bashe-granted power.
- The three-way battle is established: Danyangzi (demigod-tier), five Zuowandao members (psychological tricksters), and Li Huowang (suicidal opportunist).
- Li Huowang’s strategy is explicit: balance the fight so no side wins, and *everyone* dies with him.
- Yao Ji attempts to trick Li Huowang by claiming he is secretly Hongzhong, a Zuowandao boss who sealed his own memories for fun.
- Li Huowang fakes belief, exposes Yao Ji’s lie mid-sentence, and kills him with a sword through the throat.
- With Yao Ji dead, Li Huowang immediately turns to help the Zuowandao weaken Danyangzi, chopping off his child-head.
- Danyangzi regenerates the lost head instantly, confirming his demigod-level resilience.
- Yi Wan tries to ally with Li Huowang by pointing out that Danyangzi is their common enemy, but Li Huowang’s response is a bloody spit and silence.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Zuowandao (坐忘道):** *The Way of Sitting in Forgetfulness.* In Daoist cultivation, "zuowang" is a meditative state of emptying the mind and forgetting the self. In this novel, it's grotesquely inverted into a sect of pathological liars who weaponize falsehood and treat reality as a gag to be rewritten. Their "holy scripture" isn't sacred—it's a punchline. When a Zuowandao member speaks, every sentence is a potential trap; the safest approach (as Li Huowang demonstrates) is to treat it all as shit.

**Mahjong as Fate and Fear:** The Zuowandao see the world through mahjong tiles and dominoes, treating each fight like a hand of a game. This isn't just aesthetic—each tile (Hongzhong, Yao Ji, Qi Wan, Yi Wan) is a codename that ties their identity to a specific trick or role. In Chinese gambling culture, mahjong is both luck and skill, but here it becomes a metaphor for manipulating outcomes: they deal the cards, and you're the one who loses.

**Demigod Body (半仙之躯):** This term appears often in xianxia to describe a cultivator who has partially transcended mortality but is not yet a true immortal. In *Dao-Twisted World*, it means Danyangzi can regenerate lost heads like it's nothing, and conventional attacks are almost useless. It's a terrifying reminder that raw power matters in this world, and Li Huowang's tricks work best against mortals—not demigods.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a turning point in Li Huowang's psychological arc. The way he handles the Zuowandao's lies now—treating every word as a weapon to be parried—shows a ruthless pragmatism that's evolved from his earlier paranoia. His tactic of "believe nothing, stab everything" is crude but effective. When Yao Ji tries the "you're one of us" trick, Li Huowang doesn't even hesitate. He plays along just long enough to get the kill, then moves on without a second thought. That's not madness anymore—it's cold, predatory intelligence.

The three-way stalemate also sets up a deeper narrative tension: Danyangzi's power is clearly greater, but Li Huowang's self-sacrificial gambit is keeping the balance. How long can he keep this up? The answer is bleak—until the ritual ends and he dies. The chapter's dark humor (the "you stole my woman" line) only highlights how little he cares about survival; he's shopping for company on the way out.
