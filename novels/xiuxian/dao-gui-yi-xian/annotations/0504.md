## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in quiet dread before the storm. We get a deceptively peaceful sequence: Li Huowang teaching Bai Lingmiao, a brief encounter with bandits (handled by the increasingly capable Li Sui), and a much-needed father-daughter talk about the ugliness of human nature. But the real show is the nightmare that follows—a hallucinatory descent that drags Li Huowang through a school exam, a shattered reflection, and a heartbreaking cliffhanger with Zhuge Yuan. The chapter's true horror isn't external; it's the slow, creeping realization that even in his dreams, Li Huowang cannot escape the weight of his own mind. Prepare for feels, not fights.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang gives Bai Lingmiao a copybook, a quiet gesture of intimacy and teaching. Her perception is now sharp enough to hear distant disturbances from inside the carriage, marking her growth as a spirit-medium.
- A group of refugees-turned-bandits attacks two fleeing carriages. Li Huowang deduces they're likely not Zuowandao—the scale is too small for the Dice—but still has Li Sui handle the response as a precaution.
- Li Sui's first combat deployment: she uses the bronze coin sword and a talisman, terrifying both victims and bandits with her monstrous appearance. Li Huowang uses the encounter as a teaching moment on human deceit.
- The carriage resumes its journey. Li Huowang cultivates at night, explaining the danger of uncontrolled Innate Pneuma distorting his surroundings.
- The nightmare sequence begins: a school exam, Yang Na's help, then a surreal collapse into a puddle-reflection that shatters. Li Huowang's desperate attempts to affirm his own existence ("I'm here!") spiral into madness.
- The dream's climax: Li Huowang clings to a cliff, holding Zhuge Yuan's hand. Despite his desperate efforts, Zhuge Yuan falls into the abyss, crying out in agony. Li Huowang wakes with a gasp to the sound of rain.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### Innate Pneuma (先天一炁)
This is a heavyweight term from Daoist internal alchemy (内丹). "Innate Pneuma" or *Xiāntiān Yīqì* refers to the primordial, undifferentiated energy that exists *before* the separation of heaven and earth—the raw stuff of the cosmos before it gets all messy with yin and yang. In cultivation fiction, it's the holy grail: the purest form of power you can channel. But here, Li Huowang treats it like a loaded weapon—he can't use it casually because he has *no control* over what it will twist his companions into. That's not just a limitation; it's a character beat. His power is so inherently *wrong* that even its basic function is a threat to the people he loves.

### The "Yu'er Shen" (于儿神)
Mentioned by the bandits, this is a nod to the *Classic of Mountains and Seas* (《山海经》). Yu'er Shen is a deity described with a human head and two serpentine bodies. In the Dao-Twisted World, it's being used as a focus of worship by a "Fa Sect"—likely a corrupted cult that has forced these people from their homes. This is a subtle piece of worldbuilding: the novel is pulling from actual Chinese mythology, but twisting it into a source of terror and displacement.

## Reading Guide

Alright, settle down, fellow travelers. This chapter is a *mood piece* disguised as a filler episode. The first half is all about Li Huowang as a reluctant teacher—he doesn't just tell Li Sui not to trust the bandits; he explains *why* with a weary cynicism that speaks to his own scars. It's a small, beautiful moment of character growth wrapped in ugly pragmatism.

But the nightmare. Oh, the nightmare. This isn't just a bad dream; it's a microcosm of Li Huowang's entire psychological war. The exam panic is a classic anxiety-trope, but the reflection in the puddle? That's pure *Dao Gui*—the desperate self-affirmation that turns into a scream. And Zhuge Yuan's fall… that's the knife that's been buried in Li Huowang's heart for a very long time, finally being twisted again.

Pay attention to the rain. It frames the chapter. It starts as the setting for the nightmare's disintegration, and it ends as the real-world sound that drags him back. The line between the real and the hallucinated is paper-thin, and this chapter reminds us that Li Huowang's worst enemy isn't the Dice or the Fa Sect. It's the hole inside him where Zhuge Yuan used to be.
