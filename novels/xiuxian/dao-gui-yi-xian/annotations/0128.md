## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow survivors of existential dread. Chapter 128 is a masterclass in psychological demolition. Forget monsters; the real horror here is the *name*. After a brief hope that he could rationalize away the slaughter of an entire town, Li Huowang is confronted by three characters written in blood—his own name. The chapter opens with him staring at Yingzi’s corpse, his mind cascading through denial, rage, and finally a shaking, tear-soaked breakdown. He tries to use forensics (bone analysis!) to prove his innocence, but the evidence just keeps stacking up against him. And just when he hits rock bottom—literally kneeling among skeletons—the Ao-Jing Sect’s Shou San shows up with a literal fire extinguisher of agony. This chapter is a brutal, necessary reset: it takes the “am I a monster?” theme and stamps it on Li Huowang’s soul with a red-hot iron. Get ready for a ride that’s more therapy session than fight scene.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Weight of a Name**: Li Huowang is paralyzed by the accusation that he murdered Yingzi and her family. He alternates between frantic self-defense and horrified self-loathing, a classic Li Huowang spiral.
- **Shou San’s Unwelcome Reassurance**: Shou San tries to calm him down by saying the Ao-Jing Sect doesn’t “kill randomly,” only pointing out Li Huowang is… special. This is the worst possible comfort; it only confirms Li Huowang’s worst fears.
- **The Forensic Escape**: Li Huowang, in a desperate bid for sanity, runs to the corpse-strewn town to *inspect the evidence*. He examines multiple skeletons and concludes each death style (blunt force, fire, wall-crushing) doesn’t match his known capabilities. He feels a wave of relief.
- **The Damning Document**: Just as he’s about to relax, he finds a bloodstained letter. He can barely read it, but he recognizes the final three characters brushed on it: “Li Huowang.” His denial shatters instantly, replaced by a screaming breakdown.
- **The Final Push**: Kneeling in the middle of the carnage, Li Huowang is pushed over the edge by a “fur-covered pill” that amplifies his pain. Shou San then walks in, snaps his fingers, and engulfs them both in fire. This isn’t a fight; it’s a catastrophic emotional *and* physical conflagration.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Recognizing Characters in Late Imperial China**: Li Huowang’s difficulty in reading the bloodstained letter is a key detail. In a world before mass literacy, recognizing only your own name was a common reality. The novel uses this fact to weaponize the moment of recognition; it’s not a scholarly deduction but an instinctual, horrifying identification.
- **The Ao-Jing Sect’s M.O.**: The Ao-Jing Sect is introduced here as a fusion of Zoroastrian (袄教) and Nestorian (景教) elements—both foreign religions that arrived in China along the Silk Road. The “Healing Fire” shown here (snapping fingers to create flame) inverts the usual Buddhist or Daoist exorcism, replacing gentle purification with grotesque, physical cleansing. This aligns with the novel’s thesis: the “sacred” is always corrupted into something painful.
- **Self-Harm as Grounding**: The “fur-covered pill” and the subsequent immolation are not random torture. In the Dao-Twisted World, intense physical pain is often used as a *mechanism* to stabilize or suppress overwhelming spiritual/psychological symptoms. It’s a perverse, corrupt version of a psychiatric treatment, turning “shock therapy” into a bloody ritual.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the Voice**: Pay attention to how Li Huowang’s internal monologue degrades. It starts with logical defense (“That’s not how I fight”), shifts to desperate hope (“She must have been wrong”), and then collapses into hysterical, repetitive denial (“No, I didn’t, I didn’t!”). This is a textbook case of cognitive dissonance under acute stress.
- **Don’t Trust the Evidence… Yet**: The skeleton analysis *is* logically sound. It’s the written name that breaks him. This is a key learning point for readers: in the Dao-Twisted World, “proof” isn’t just about facts—it’s about what sticks emotionally. Li Huowang can question violence, but he can’t question his own name.
- **Shou San’s Role**: Notice how Shou San doesn’t attack. He waits, he observes, and he *intervenes* at the exact moment of maximum despair. This marks him as a sophisticated manipulator, not just a brute. His fire isn’t an attack; it’s a “cure” for the broken man. Keep your eyes on this guy—he’s playing a longer game.
- **The Emotional Arc**: This chapter is a closed loop: accusation → denial → forensic logic → hope → final evidence → breakdown → external intervention (fire). It’s a tight, emotional rollercoaster that leaves the reader feeling as shattered as Li Huowang. The horror here is the *uncertainty*—he might be the monster, or he might just look like one. Either way, he’s trapped.
