## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang is on the warpath, and this chapter proves exactly how far he’ll push himself when the people he loves are at stake. After learning that Bai Lingmiao’s entire family has been taken—possibly by the Supervisory Heavenly Office, possibly by something wearing their face—he rides like a man possessed. The chapter is a masterclass in pacing: the first half is pure, grinding pursuit through night and fog, and the second half pulls the rug out with a supernatural encounter that feels less like a monster and more like an *institution* manifesting its authority. That wooden head drifting out of the mist? That grinning face descending from above? You’ll feel it in your bones. Buckle up, because the Supervisory Heavenly Office just became way more terrifying.

## Key Plot Points
- Captain Cao drops the bombshell: the Bai family was taken by the Supervisory Heavenly Office, shattering Li Huowang’s assumptions.
- Li Huowang briefly entertains the possibility of impersonators—both possibilities are equally dire.
- Magistrate Wang refuses to get involved, demonstrating the Office’s hands-off policy towards local authorities.
- Red Center reappears and taunts Li Huowang with riddles about truth and lies, deepening the suspense.
- Li Huowang tracks the convoy for days, finally locating the Bai family in a forest, all wearing heavy cangues.
- There are no visible guards—an immediate red flag.
- A strange *clack-clack-clack* sound heralds the approach of a wooden doll’s head in the mist.
- The chapter ends with a massive, grinning, pale face descending from above—the true overseer of the Bai family’s capture.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司)** is a recurring force in the Dao-Twisted World, and this chapter cements its role as a bureaucratic horror entity. Think of it as the imperial government’s supernatural police force—except it answers to no local magistrate, follows its own internal logic, and prioritizes “stabilizing” supernatural threats over saving lives. In Chinese web novel tradition, organizations like this often embody a very real historical anxiety: when the state’s secret police decide you’re a problem, there is no court of appeal. The Office’s *modus operandi* is cold, professional, and terrifyingly efficient. The fact that Li Huowang cannot even be sure whether the people who took the Bai family are the *real* Office or impostors is the novel’s signature move—making even the forces of “order” fundamentally unstable and suspect.

## Reading Guide
This is a prime example of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*’s ability to build dread through atmosphere alone. The first half of the chapter is all about pursuit—the rhythmic hooves, the sleepless nights, the tension of not knowing whether you’re hours or days too late. The reader feels every mile. Then, when the supernatural element finally arrives, it’s not a lightning strike—it’s a gradual, fog-bound creep. That *clacking* sound is going to stick with you. Pay close attention to how the author uses the mist: it’s not just scenery, it’s a narrative veil that separates Li Huowang (and the reader) from the truth. And when that enormous face finally appears, it’s the payoff for all that slow-burn tension. Also, don’t sleep on the Magistrate’s reaction—his refusal to get involved is a quiet but important clue about how the Office operates. The world’s rules are becoming clearer, and they are not kind.
