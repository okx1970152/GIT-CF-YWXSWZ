## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang has a strange encounter trying to communicate with the Black Tai Sui inside him, only to discover it’s repeating sounds it overheard—like a baby babbling its first words. With no quick fix, he settles on the absurd plan of teaching a mute blob of tentacles to read. That’s one problem. The other: collecting his first paycheck from the Supervisory Heavenly Office. After a tense meeting in a stable with the Big-Headed Doll, Li Huowang finally gets a taste of something he hasn’t had in ages—a little bit of human decency, even if it might be an act. For once, the world isn’t trying to kill him. Yet.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang removes the cotton wadding from his ears and endures a cacophony of sounds to locate the Black Tai Sui’s voice. He realizes the entity can only repeat a handful of simple words—exactly the ones he taught Lü Xiucai.
- After multiple failed attempts at quick fixes, he decides on the long-term, “clumsy” method: teach the Black Tai Sui to speak and read from scratch, hoping to turn it into a controllable ally or hidden weapon.
- He forces the group to resume travel despite objections, negotiating a compromise with Bai Lingmiao to ride in a cart. His chest wound has fully healed, but his missing eye and ribs are still regenerating.
- Arriving in Yinling City, Li Huowang heads straight for the same inn. He returns to the stables in the second watch to meet the Big-Headed Doll.
- The Big-Headed Doll confirms Li Huowang has passed the trial, praising his bloodless handling of Gan Yuan Village. He then removes his mask, revealing a round, smiling face beneath.
- The man introduces himself as Tuoba Danqing. As a gesture of trust, he returns the five Life Span Pills. Li Huowang accepts, feeling a rare flicker of warmth and belonging.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The ‘Clumsy Method’ of Teaching a Silent Horror.** Li Huowang’s plan to teach the Black Tai Sui to read and write is a prime example of the novel’s approach to cultivation. There is no quick-fix cheat code. Progress is slow, absurd, and requires the protagonist to sit down and do the unglamorous work. In traditional xianxia, this would be the moment Li Huowang finds a hidden manual or a spiritual teacher. Here, his “teacher” is a parasitic entity he’s stuck with, and the “curriculum” is whatever word he can get it to parrot.
**The Big-Headed Doll and the Supervisory Heavenly Office.** The “大头娃娃” is a literal big-headed doll mask—a deliberately childish and unsettling guise for a representative of the *Supervisory Heavenly Office*, the empire’s shadowy supernatural enforcement agency. Tuoba Danqing removing the mask is a symbolic gesture of transparency and trust. Returning the Life Span Pills—previously given as a "security deposit" against Li Huowang’s failure—means he is now vouching for Li Huowang’s future, binding their fates together in the eyes of the Office.
**Naming and Clan Identity.** The surname Tuoba (拓跋) is historically significant. It was the ruling clan of the Northern Wei dynasty, a non-Han dynasty from the Xianbei steppe nomads. Using a compound surname like this immediately signals that Tuoba Danqing is from an old, prestigious lineage that has been sinicized for centuries. For him to offer his full name and invite Li Huowang to call him “Brother Tuoba” is a huge gesture of familial acceptance in a world built on suspicion and blood ties.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a breather chapter—and no, that doesn’t mean nothing happens. After the relentless horror of the past few arcs, Li Huowang finally catches a break. The tension in this chapter isn’t from a monster about to eat his face (though there’s a tentacle blob in his gut). It’s the quiet, awkward tension of someone who has forgotten what human kindness feels like. The scene where Tuoba Danqing hands back the Life Span Pills is deceptively warm. Fellow Daoists, don’t let your guard down yet. But for now, let Li Huowang have this moment. A fake smile from a fat man with a silly mask might be the most sincere gift he’s received all year.
