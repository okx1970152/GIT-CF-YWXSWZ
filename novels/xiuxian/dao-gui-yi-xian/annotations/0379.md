## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow travelers of the Twisted Dao! In this chapter, our boy Li Huowang finally gets his feet wet inside the Supervisory Heavenly Office, and let me tell you—it’s not quite the grand, imposing celestial bureaucracy you might have imagined. We get a crash course in Mohist sectarian politics (yes, Mozi’s descendants are *also* having a messy breakup), a tour of a surprisingly moldy intelligence hub, and a crucial first step in Li Huowang’s plan to hunt down the mysterious North Wind. Get ready for some shadowy screens, cryptic bird-speak, and a protagonist who’s learning to play the game *very* carefully.

## Key Plot Points

*   Li Huowang learns from Sima Lan’s talking black bird about the split in Mohism: **Qi Mohism** (the orthodox branch) and **Liang Mohism** (the heretical branch that allied with the Eastern Quake Hall and became “neither human nor ghost”).
*   Li Huowang observes the Office’s internal communication system: a moldy “bamboo spider” structure that distributes messages from messenger birds to eunuch-sorters, revealing a decrepit yet functional intelligence apparatus.
*   Sima Lan guides Li Huowang to the main hall, a space filled with folding screens that cast human silhouettes, creating an atmosphere of Kafkaesque paranoia where identities are hidden behind shadows and muffled voices.
*   Li Huowang registers his token (Enjiu) with the Astral Secretary, Nangong, and confirms that the Chief Recorder (Erniu) had already informed the office of his arrival.
*   In a crucial proactive move, Li Huowang asks Sima Lan how to take assignments, learning that the Office operates on a self-selection model—he can choose his own work from a eunuch-assistant, rather than being assigned by a superior. This gives him the freedom to steer his own investigation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Mohist Splintering:** The mention of “Qi Mo” and “Liang Mo” is a fictionalized extension of a very real historical event. After the death of Mozi (the founder of Mohism), the school did indeed fracture into three main branches: Xiangli氏, Xiangfu氏, and Dengling氏. Each claimed to be the true inheritor. The novel’s use of this historical memory of division creates instant texture—most readers know that philosophies don’t stay pure; they get twisted by new leaders and political alliances. “Liang Mohism” allying with the “Eastern Quake Hall” (东震堂) is a perfect fictional corruption, suggesting a sect that used Mohist discipline for horrific, self-serving ends.

**The “Bamboo Spider” and Office Technology:** The Office’s mail system is a brilliant bit of clockpunk-tinged folk horror. It’s essentially a pneumatic tube system made of bamboo, powered by falling weights and wooden gears. The *mold* is the key detail—it’s not just flavor text. Mold implies stagnation, decay, and a system that’s rotting from within. A celestial bureaucracy that’s literally growing fungus is a perfect visual metaphor for the novel’s theme of corrupted institutions.

**Screen-Shadow Paranoia:** The hall of screens with anonymous silhouettes is a classic trope of wuxia and xianxia bureaucracy, reminiscent of the “shadow court” in many martial arts novels. It creates an information asymmetry where you can’t be sure who’s listening, who’s watching, or who’s your enemy. Li Huowang’s immediate reaction—“Is this a ghost market?”—is the correct one. The Office is meant to be a refuge of order, but it operates on the very same principles of deception and concealment that the rest of the Dao-Twisted World uses.

### Face-Changing (变脸, bianlian)

A traditional Sichuan opera technique where performers rapidly switch between painted masks; in this novel’s context, any change of face triggers Li Huowang’s Zuowandao-rooted paranoia.

## Reading Guide

Okay, so this chapter is mostly setup and worldbuilding, but don’t you *dare* skim it. This is where Li Huowang transitions from “drifting infiltrator” to “active agent.” The key moment is when he interrupts Sima Lan’s tour to ask a specific, procedural question: “How do I get work?” This is a *huge* character beat for our boy. He’s not just passively absorbing info anymore; he’s starting to look for the levers he can pull.

The self-selection model for assignments is a massive green flag for the plot. It means Li Huowang can theoretically choose missions that put him in proximity to the North Wind’s case, or that allow him to investigate specific types of supernatural phenomena. The eunuch-assistant is now his gateway to controlled intelligence.

Also, let’s talk about the *silence*. There are no monsters, no kowtows, no rituals in this chapter. The horror is purely atmospheric—the mold, the muffled voices, the hidden faces. It’s the horror of a system that’s *supposed* to be your ally but feels like a trap. Pay attention to how Li Huowang’s paranoia is just below the surface; he’s one wrong look away from bolting. The tension is quiet, but it’s there.
