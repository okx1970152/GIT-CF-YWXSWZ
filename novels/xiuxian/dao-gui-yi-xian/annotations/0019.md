## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang levels up his scam game in this chapter, and it’s a thing of twisted beauty. Armed with folk horror lore from his fellow captives—the shape-shifting Great Grandmother, the lethal Husband-and-Wife Fish—he weaves a fake immortality formula so convoluted that Danyangzi, trapped in his own prison of superstition, falls for it hook, line, and sinker. The old Daoist even starts *backfilling the pharmacology himself*, proving you don’t need to believe in gods to be devoutly wrong. But just when Li Huowang thinks he’s won, Danyangzi pulls out a dented bell and does something that makes the edges of reality bend, warp, and *multiply*. Get ready for a masterclass in desperate deception and the cost of pretending to know what you don’t.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang questions Bai Lingmiao about local folk tales. She shares a gruesome story about child-snatching skin-walkers but admits her knowledge is mostly generic country superstition—only the ‘Wandering Lord’ is real and specific.
- Fellow inmate Zhao Wu, a crippled man with a peddler uncle, offers Li Huowang the juicy stuff: the Great Grandmother (a perception-warping entity that makes *you* become it) and the Husband-and-Wife Fish (a hot-natured poison). Li Huowang realizes these unknown confections will make his fake formula sound *more* authentic to Danyangzi.
- A mentally disabled inmate tries to share his own ‘knowledge,’ but Li Huowang brushes him off, revealing his cold pragmatism.
- The next morning, Li Huowang recites his fabricated immortality recipe to Danyangzi, larding it with the newly learned folk ingredients. The old man immediately buys the logic, even supplying his own fake pharmacological reasoning.
- The chapter’s climax: Danyangzi shakes his damaged bronze bell, the sound distorting Li Huowang’s senses. The edges of all objects in the room writhe like living things and coalesce into a single entity—then, when Li Huowang shakes his head, it *splits into two*. The summoning has gone wrong.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a brilliant deconstruction of ‘superstition’ in a world where the supernatural is real. Li Huowang’s internal monologue nails it: superstition isn’t about believing in false gods; it’s about *believing in your own closed system* so deeply that you can’t see the truth. Danyangzi doesn’t trust Li Huowang, but he *does* trust his own ability to reason about elixir balances, which is exactly what makes him exploitable.

- **The Great Grandmother (大姥姥)**: This is a pure folk horror invention—a mimetic entity that looks different to everyone and converts people by proximity. In traditional Chinese folklore, there are many ‘name-hearing’ creatures (mentioning them summons them), but the Great Grandmother is a uniquely modern-feeling take on memetic infection.
- **The Husband-and-Wife Fish (公婆鱼)**: A fabricated ‘hot-natured’ poison, playing on the Chinese medicinal yin-yang theory of foods and toxins. The idea that a fish’s gills are *yang* enough to balance arsenic’s *yin* is exactly the kind of folk-logic Danyangzi would worship.
- **The Daoist Bell (道铃)**: Used in both orthodox Daoist ritual and zombie-controller folklore. Danyangzi’s bell is *dented*, which explains why the summoning goes unstable. A spirit that splits when you shake your head is a beautifully low-tech consequence for damaged ritual tools.

## Reading Guide
This is where the *Dao-Twisted World* shows its shiniest, most corrupt teeth. Li Huowang isn’t fighting with swords—he’s fighting with *bullshit*, and he’s winning. The real horror here isn’t the flesh monsters; it’s the way Danyangzi’s mind works. He is a brilliant fool, a man who can deduce chemical balances from thin air because his foundational premise (that Li Huowang is reading a real Heavenly Scripture) is pure house-of-cards. If you love watching a protagonist out-think a monster by weaponizing its own delusions, this chapter is *chef’s kiss*.

The final scene—the writhing edges of reality coalescing into a creature that *duplicates*—is a perfect microcosm of the novel’s aesthetic: visceral, disorienting, and drenched in the feeling that nothing in this world follows clean rules. Pay attention to how Li Huowang’s internal voice shifts here: from smug conman to genuine, bone-deep terror in a heartbeat. That’s the ride.
