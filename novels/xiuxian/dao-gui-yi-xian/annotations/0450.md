## Chapter Overview
Crikey, folks. Just when Li Huowang thought he could catch a break—emotional gut-punch followed by a drunken blur followed by a demonic ritual in the ancestral hall. This chapter is a masterclass in tonal whiplash that only *Dao Gui Yi Xian* can pull off. We pick up right where the banquet left off, but the warmth of the reunion is immediately undercut by the cold, aching distance between Li Huowang and the Second Spirit. And just when you think you’ve processed that, Bai Lingmiao casually strolls into the family basement to find a frothing, self-flagellating Lv Xiucai turning himself into a living god vessel. It’s intimate, tragic, and pants-wettingly intense all at once. Strap in.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang lifts the Second Spirit’s red veil. Her face has changed: the fox features now dominate, and her human half aches with an expression he remembers too well. He reaches out, she grabs his hand—then lets go, covering her face and walking away sobbing.
- Drunk and burdened by the emotional tangle of feeling like he’s been given “two Bai Lingmiaos,” Li Huowang stumbles back to her room and into an embrace.
- The narrative cuts to Bai Lingmiao and Chun Xiaoman walking through Niuxin Village at dusk. The villagers are happy, splitting up leftover meat; the atmosphere is almost domestic.
- Bai Lingmiao discusses logistics: the Lu troupe’s spring opera (explicitly tied to *hūn sāng*, “happy funerals”) is making good money in Daliang.
- The pair enter the Bai family ancestral hall and descend into the underground chamber, where Lv Xiucai is mid-ritual: stabbing his own head with a double-spiked short sword, covered in blood, chanting a graphic medium-invocation verse.
- He succeeds. His body becomes impervious to blades—Bai Lingmiao tests his skin with a magic sword and can barely scratch him.
- Manic and ecstatic, Lv Xiucai declares his intent to kill his father for past beatings. Bai Lingmiao calmly tells him to go ahead, but his bravado visibly falters as he approaches the door.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Ji Tong (乩童, Possessed Medium):** This is not your standard xianxia power-up. A *jitong* is a medium who invites a deity to take over their body—often through self-mutilation (whipping, piercing, cutting the head) as proof of divine protection. The practitioner doesn’t cultivate their own power; they *borrow* the god’s flesh. That’s why Lv Xiucai’s body goes “metal-hard.” The chant here is a traditional spirit-summoning formula, but retrofitted for this novel’s bleak cosmology.

**Spring Opera (粉戏, Fěn Xì):** The “spring” in this term is the *pink* that implies erotic or bawdy content. A “spring opera” troupe performs lewd plays for rowdy audiences. The fact that this degeneracy is being commissioned for *happy funerals* (喜丧, where the deceased is old enough to die without grief) is a perfect, grimly comedic detail that the novel loves to weaponize.

**Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings (十情八苦):** A twisted expansion of the Buddhist *Eight Sufferings*. In this novel’s White Lotus context, it implies a cultivation path that doesn’t seek to *transcend* suffering, but to *weaponize* a more granular spectrum of emotional pain. The Second Spirit’s foggy aura literally blocks this perception—she’s an emotional blind spot to Li Huowang’s Zuowangdao sight.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a quiet storm. If you’re here for action, you’ll get one burst of horror at the end, but the real meat is in the emotional geometry. Pay close attention to how Li Huowang processes the Second Spirit: he doesn’t see her as a monster, *but he also doesn’t see her as wholly Bai Lingmiao*. That unresolved tension is going to fester. Meanwhile, Bai Lingmiao’s casual authority in the village—ordering meat, blocking Chun Xiaoman, green-lighting Lv Xiucai’s revenge—shows how much she’s grown into her role as a leader. The *terrifying* question is whether she’s also growing into something more monstrous. And Lv Xiucai? His rage is real, but his *follow-through* is already cracking before he’s even out the door. That self-doubt is the first sign that his new power is unstable—and that somebody else is already planning to use him.
