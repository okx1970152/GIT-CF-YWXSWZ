## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow pilgrims on the crooked path. Chapter 394, “True and False,” is a dense, dialogue-heavy masterclass in worldbuilding that peels back another layer of the Dao-Twisted World’s cosmological horror. Forget flashy fight scenes; the real battle here is fought with words over a Go board in a tranquil bamboo building. Li Huowang finally gets the chance to voice the question that has been gnawing at him since he learned he was a Heart-Element: *Why is Zhuge Yuan helping him?* The answer he receives is both disarmingly simple and terrifyingly profound. But that’s just the appetizer. The main course is a devastating lore drop that re-contextualizes the entire conflict between Heart-Elements and the Zuowandao, culminating in a single, breathtaking revelation that will have you slapping your forehead and shouting, “Of course!” Get ready to have your understanding of *cultivation* itself turned upside down.

## Key Plot Points

*   Li Huowang learns that “Heart-Pan” (心蟠) means “coiled together” (蟠结), and that Zhuge Yuan is “coiled together” with something called the “One Above the Three Bodies” (三身旧之上), which is a forbidden epithet for a Siming in the White Jade Capital.
*   Li Huowang finally asks the burning question: why is Zhuge Yuan helping him? Zhuge Yuan’s answer—that it is simply out of the goodness of his heart and his nature to help anyone in need—shocks Li Huowang, whose paranoid mind immediately suspects a hidden agenda.
*   Li Huowang proposes his plan: to stay by Zhuge Yuan’s side and help him fight the Zuowandao, including the mysterious Heart-Element “North Wind,” in exchange for a chance to study North Wind’s method of escaping his hallucinations.
*   Zhuge Yuan raises a critical doubt: Is North Wind’s method even suitable? It could be a form of extreme self-deception or a vile cult technique, given that North Wind is a Zuowandao.
*   Zhuge Yuan reveals a forgotten secret: there exists a book that details a cultivation technique specifically for Heart-Elements to control their innate One Qi, thereby eliminating their hallucinations.
*   The chapter’s climax hinges on Zhuge Yuan’s deduction: the Zuowandao’s hatred of Heart-Elements is not random—it’s because the Heart-Element’s technique is a direct, natural counter to the Zuowandao’s own method.
*   The chapter ends with the ultimate question and answer: The Zuowandao “cultivate the false.” What do Heart-Elements cultivate? Zhuge Yuan’s answer, spelled out on the Go board, is “True” (真). The unspoken full answer is **Cultivate the True (修真)**.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### Cultivating the False vs. Cultivating the True (修假 vs. 修真)

This chapter throws down the thematic gauntlet of the entire novel. The term “Cultivate the True” (修真, *xiū zhēn*) is the actual Chinese name for the entire “xianxia” cultivation genre itself! Every xianxia novel, from the most generic to the most epic, is fundamentally about “seeking/practicing the True.” The author of *Dao Gui Yi Xian* has performed a masterstroke of metatextual horror by pitting this generic genre label directly against the Zuowandao’s invented counter-method: “Cultivating the False” (修假, *xiū jiǎ*).

In the context of this world, the Zuowandao’s “False” isn’t about karmic illusions or shadow clones. It’s an ontological principle. Their power lies in the act of **replacing reality with a lie**—to the point where the lie *becomes* the new reality. This is the true meaning of their sect’s name, “Sit and Forget the Dao.” A Heart-Element’s power, which makes their own beliefs and delusions real, is thus the perfect counter. A Heart-Element doesn’t just see through lies; their very existence threatens the foundation of the Zuowandao’s entire power structure. This reframes the Zuowandao’s persecution of Li Huowang not as bullying a helpless victim, but as a desperate act of war against their natural predator.

### The “One Above the Three Bodies” and Unutterable Names

Zhuge Yuan’s reference to his “coiling” with the “One Above the Three Bodies” is a brilliant way to evoke the classic Lovecraftian trope of the un-nameable entity. In Chinese folklore and Daoism, especially within the traditions of the White Lotus and other secret societies, the true names of gods and demons were considered vessels of immense power. To utter a name was to call its owner. To *know* a name was to hold a sliver of power over it. By refusing to speak the name of the Siming above the Three Bodies, Zhuge Yuan is both showing profound respect and communicating pure, terrified caution. The fact that he can only use a cryptic epithet tells us this is an entity whose attention is worth avoiding at all costs—a common theme when dealing with the world’s high-level, reality-bending powers.

## Reading Guide

*   **Pay attention to the Go game.** The game itself is a metaphor for the conversation. Li Huowang’s stones are “donkey’s lips not matching a horse’s jaws”—scattered, illogical, desperate. Zhuge Yuan’s plays are deliberate, patient, and aimed at a final revelation. The final revelation that the scattered stones form the character for “True” is a beautiful piece of narrative staging.
*   **Li Huowang’s character development.** This chapter shows Li Huowang at his most desperate but also his most strategically-minded. He has moved beyond simple survival into actively trying to solve his own condition. However, his paranoia is a chronic illness. The fact that he instinctively distrusts genuine kindness from Zhuge Yuan is a heart-breaking display of how trauma has permanently scarred his worldview.
*   **The true nature of sects.** This chapter clarifies the great schism in the Dao-Twisted World. We have the traditional cultivators (Daoists, Buddhists, etc.), the Abberational (Heart-Elements, etc.), and the philosophical predators (Zuowandao). This is not a world of simple good vs. evil, but a battlefield of competing *ontologies*. The Zuowandao’s sin isn’t that they are evil; it’s that their entire way of existence is a weapon against the very nature of reality.
*   **The “forgotten book.”** This is a classic xianxia trope: the lost manual that holds the key to ultimate power or salvation. However, here it’s presented not as a cheat code, but as a piece of missing history that explains a cosmic conflict. It suggests a deeper, older layer to the world’s rules, a layer that the Zuowandao are actively trying to erase by eliminating Heart-Elements.
