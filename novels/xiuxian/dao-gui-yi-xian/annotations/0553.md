## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang has finally met his match. This chapter throws him into a brutal, no-win fight against the Dragon Vein—a writhing, screaming tower of stacked Great Qi emperors that feels less like a creature and more like an ontological insult to the concept of life. Everything he throws at it fails. His talismans, his swords, even his borrowed Zuowandao tricks and Ao-Jing agony-path get swatted aside like toys. It’s the first time the story has made him feel *this* powerless since the early days of Danyangzi. But the chapter’s real highlight? An unlikely, teeth-gritting team-up with the Void Nian, the very beast that was trying to kill him moments before. Desperation makes strange, hairy bedfellows.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang confirms that the Dragon Vein is completely immune to his standard toolkit: talismans, spine-sword, copper-coin sword, Zi Sui sword, Zuowandao abilities, and even Ao-Jing techniques.
- He recalls Zhuge Yuan’s warning that the Zuowandao deliberately avoid interfering with the Dragon Vein, underscoring its unique threat level.
- The Void Nian, previously an enemy, is now being crushed by the Vein; Li Huowang saves it out of pure tactical necessity, realizing he needs a partner against a shared threat.
- The Void Nian speaks again (“Kill the small one”), and they form a rough plan: the Nian holds the old emperors, Li Huowang targets the child emperor at the top.
- The Void Nian’s special ability activates: a field of red afterimages that accelerates time for everything inside it. Wounds heal and re-open in rapid cycles; the Dragon Vein’s ancient emperors begin to de-age and destabilize.
- Li Huowang’s body is a ruin: half a face gone, one arm missing, a chunk of his waist torn out. Peng Longteng is his only shield, and she’s starting to flicker under the strain.
- The chapter ends with the Vein’s emperors starting to shake apart, suggesting the “rejuvenation” is actually their undoing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Dragon Vein (龙脉)** in Chinese geomancy and political cosmology is the underground energy channel that determines a dynasty’s legitimacy and fate. Normally it’s a metaphor about landscape. This novel does what the novel does best: it literalizes the metaphor into a screaming, clawing monster made of dead emperors. The “lawful” structure of imperial succession becomes a physical chain. And the fact that the Zuowandao—the ultimate cosmic trolls—won’t touch it says everything about how deeply the Vein’s rules are baked into this world’s operating system.

**The Void Nian (虚年)** is a corruption of the traditional *Nian* beast from Chinese folklore. The original Nian was a lion-like monster that preyed on villages during Lunar New Year, eventually driven away by the color red and loud noises. Here, it’s given a tragic makeover: a woman’s face on a beast’s body, a creature that feeds on *yang life* (阳寿). The ability shown in this chapter—the red afterimages that fast-forward time—is a brilliant inversion of the folkloric “red scares away Nian.” Instead, the red *is* the Nian’s power, and it doesn’t scare anything; it ages things to death.

## Reading Guide
Okay, this chapter is a *grind*. It’s not a flashy power-up moment. It’s Li Huowang getting pulverized for pages while working together with an enemy he just met. If you’re reading this for the victory lap, you’re in the wrong genre. This is *Dao Gui Yi Xian* logic: the only way out of a trap that defies all your tools is to find someone else who’s also trapped, and hold your nose while shaking hands. Pay attention to the Void Nian’s “rejuvenation” field—it’s not a heal effect. It’s time acceleration. That’s a terrifying ability, and one that perfectly mirrors the novel’s obsession with making *aging* a weapon rather than just a state of decay. Also, note the body horror progression: Li Huowang is now down half a face and an arm, and he’s still fighting. The man simply refuses to die, even when his body has all but given up.
