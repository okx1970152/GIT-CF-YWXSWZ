## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists, because the lid just blew off Danyangzi's kitchen pot! This chapter is a masterclass in tension as the old alchemist discovers his sacred vase has been shattered. "Found Out" isn't just a title—it's a promise fulfilled. The serene, calculating facade cracks wide open, revealing the raw, murderous tyrant underneath. We watch our boy Li Huowang sweat through the ultimate test of nerves, while the cave temple's fragile social order collapses into a bloodbath of paranoia and betrayal. It's a claustrophobic, visceral payoff that redefines the stakes: in this world, a single mistake doesn't just kill you—it does so with theatrical, grinding cruelty.

## Key Plot Points

- Danyangzi erupts in unimaginable rage upon discovering the broken vase-girl, slaughtering innocents to vent his fury before interrogating the entire temple.
- He methodically questions his disciples: Changming, Changren, Xuanyuan, and finally Li Huowang, who maintains his lie about being in the material room under immense pressure.
- Danyangzi then turns to the human ingredients, dangling the ultimate bait—freedom and travel money—to identify the culprit.
- Bai Lingmiao first confirms Li Huowang's alibi, but Gouwa, the sharp-faced sycophant, betrays Li Huowang, claiming he was never in the material room.
- Danyangzi brutally slaps Gouwa down, seeing through his lie and using the failed accusation to pivot toward his real target: Xuanyin.
- Xuanyin, cornered by Danyangzi's relentless pressure, breaks down and confesses that Zhengkun forced him to frame Li Huowang.
- Zhengkun makes a desperate break for the entrance but is monstrously and systematically dismembered by Danyangzi's curse through a bronze coin.
- The chapter ends with Zhengkun, now a limbless, bloody worm, begging for mercy until Danyangzi literally crushes his head, splattering blood across Li Huowang's face.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter demonstrates the brutal internal hierarchy of a "cultivation sect." Danyangzi isn't just a master—he's a god-tyrant. His authority is absolute and enforced through violence that makes even his inner disciples disposable. The interrogation scene reveals a core xianxia dynamic: the master holds all the cards, and betrayal is met with spectacular, public punishment meant to terrorize everyone else into loyalty. The bronze coin curse is a subtle but powerful example of "Daoist sorcery"—ritual objects (coins, bells, swords) are not just tools but vessels for order-based supernatural power. Zhengkun's instant dismemberment, mirrored by the coin's destruction, visually reinforces the concept of sympathetic magic bound to strict rules. Also note the chilling use of "sending home" as a reward—for these mutilated captives, home is a psychological weapon more potent than any threat.

### Sympathetic curse

A Daoist sorcery technique that links a target's fate to a ritual object, such as a coin; when the object is destroyed, the target suffers the same effect.

## Reading Guide

Watch Li Huowang's game face in this chapter. He's not a hero; he's a survivor playing a high-stakes bluff. The real tension is in the *near misses*: Danyangzi's gaze passes over him, Gouwa's lie is too clumsy to stick, and Xuanyin's panic saves him by redirecting the target. This is the kind of desperation-chess that defines the best survival horror. Also, pay close attention to how the "human ingredients" act—they are not props. Bai Lingmiao's quick defense shows her growing agency, and even the terrified Gouwa makes a rational, if cowardly, choice. The true horror isn't just the bloodshed; it's that every person in that cave is calculating how to use another's life to save their own. Li Huowang wins this round, but the blood on his face is a promise: he's a step closer to the abyss.
