## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a gut-punch of a chapter, fellow Daoists. "Yang Na" drops the explosive action and horrifying rituals of the previous arcs for something far more insidious: a quiet, warm, and deeply unsettling reunion. Li Huowang, with his sanity apparently restored, meets his girlfriend Yang Na in the sterile visiting room of Kangning Hospital. On the surface, it’s a sweet scene of care packages and shared dreams for the future. But underneath the tender words, the forced smiles, and the doomed plans, a single, black thread of *Dao Gui Yi Xian*’s true horror is beginning to unravel. This isn’t a chapter about monsters; it’s about the chilling moment when the safe, ordinary world starts to tremble under the weight of the *Dao-Twisted* one. Yang Na’s casual question about a birthday gift sends a tremor straight through the reality Li Huowang had just begun to trust.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Reunion:** Yang Na, having been told by Li Huowang’s mother of his recovery, pays him an anxious and deeply emotional visit at Kangning Hospital. She is immediately distressed by the iron grille and handcuffs separating them.
- **A Fragile Peace:** Li Huowang reassures her, painting a picture of a model institution and his imminent recovery. He carefully builds an image of normalcy and a shared future.
- **The Burden Shared:** Yang Na reveals she is also being treated for an illness. Li Huowang’s internal monologue provides a stark, medical, and heart-wrenching explanation of major depressive disorder, framed as a persistent “black dog.” He vows to be there for her.
- **Plans and Presents:** Yang Na shows him gifts—snacks, daily necessities—and unveils her post-discharge plans: a tour of all the places they’ve missed. The mood lifts, if only for a fragile moment.
- **The Crack in the Mirror:** As the visit nears its end, Li Huowang asks about a scar on her chest—a visible mark of the violence he may have brought into her life. Yang Na deflects with gentle reassurance.
- **The Fatal Question:** Smiling, Yang Na points to a jade earring she’s wearing, a supposed birthday gift from Li Huowang. She asks where he bought such an expensive piece. Then she mentions the **jade pendant**—worth four hundred thousand yuan.
- **Collision Course:** Li Huowang’s world freezes. A gift he has no memory of giving. An impossible purchase. The jade pendant—an artifact from the **Dao-Twisted** world. The careful separation between his two realities has just been breached.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **The "Black Dog" Analogy:** The narrator’s explanation of depression through the metaphor of a "black dog" (黑狗, *hēi gǒu*) is a well-known Western idiom popularized by Winston Churchill and later adopted globally. In this context, Li Huowang uses it to describe Yang Na’s condition with clinical precision and a devastating, intimate understanding. It grounds the supernatural horror of the *Dao-Twisted World* in a very modern, tangible kind of suffering—the internal struggle against one's own mind.
- **Jade (翡翠, *fěicuì*) and Social Status:** Jade has been the most revered material in Chinese culture for millennia, symbolizing purity, virtue, and status. A high-quality jade pendant or earring is a significant luxury item. Yang Na’s friend identifying the earring as "expensive" is a realistic social cue, but the context adds a terrifying implication: the objects from the *Dao-Twisted* world appear to possess immense monetary value in the real world, further blurring the line between madness and a tangible, dangerous alternative reality.
- **Four Hundred Thousand Yuan (四十万):** This is a very large sum of money (roughly $55,000 USD). The mention of such a specific, colossal value for the "impossible" jade pendant transforms a gentle question from a girlfriend into a chilling interrogation of reality. It’s a number that can’t be explained away by a patient’s hallucination; it suggests a transaction, an existence, a truth that Li Huowang’s "recovered" mind cannot account for.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in misdirection and tonal shift. After chapters of visceral horror and frantic action, "Yang Na" feels like a breath of fresh air—a tender, human moment. **Don’t let yourself relax.** Pay very close attention to Li Huowang’s internal narration. His clinical analysis of depression is not just heart-warming; it’s a display of his sharpened, paranoid, and deeply analytical mind. He’s scanning for threats even while holding his girlfriend’s hand. The key detail is the **jade pendant**. At this point in the story, you should know that a jade pendant of immense value is a classic artifact from the *Dao-Twisted* world. The fact that Yang Na possesses it—and that Li Huowang has no memory of giving it to her—is the first, terrifying sign that his "cure" might be a fiction, or worse, that the *Dao-Twisted* world is actively bleeding into his modern reality with physical proof. Enjoy the warmth, but feel the cold draft of the horror that’s about to flood in through that single, opened crack.

## Deeper Dive: The Psychology of a Diagnostician
Li Huowang’s detailed breakdown of Yang Na’s depression is one of the most grounded and compassionate passages in the novel. It reveals a crucial facet of his character that often gets buried under the gore and the screaming: he is a profoundly intelligent and empathetic person. He has been forced by his own experience to become a master diagnostician of the mind. He can see the difference between a mood and a disorder just as he can see the difference between a real god and a *Siming*. This moment isn't just romantic fluff; it's a demonstration of his core competency outside of sword-fighting and ritual sacrifice. He is using the very skills he developed to survive the *Dao-Twisted World* to understand and love the people in his own. It makes the intrusion of the supernatural into this pure, human moment all the more tragic and terrifying.
