## Chapter Overview

Get ready, because this chapter throws a heavy, sacred punch right in the feels. Lian Zhibei — that sloppy, foul-mouthed, firecracker-chomping fat woman we met before — finally shows us what a full-blown "Lord Tiger Possession" really looks like. It’s brutal, it’s visceral, and it’s surprisingly effective. She’s here to do one thing: rip Bai Lingmiao's Immortal connection out by the roots. But the ritual works *too* well, and the silent companion who has lived in Bai Lingmiao’s shadow — the Second Spirit — is suddenly facing total erasure. What starts as a violent exorcism turns into a desperate, heartbreaking standoff where Li Huowang must choose between saving Bai Lingmiao’s power or saving the part of her that is a separate person.

## Key Plot Points

- Lian Zhibei performs a self-mutilating ritual called "Lord Tiger Takes the Possessed": she pierces her cheeks with incense, shoves hooks through her lips, and detonates firecrackers in a cloud of smoke, channeling a tiger spirit.
- The possession works: Lian Zhibei’s eyes go white, her body contorts into animal postures, and she begins jumping over Bai Lingmiao's head, “biting” at something invisible — the Immortals’ flesh.
- Bai Lingmiao’s body patterns writhe and shrink as the Immortals are devoured. Li Huowang hears distant, pained screams (the Immortals) and is initially relieved, thinking the procedure is working.
- Bai Lingmiao’s mind-eye perception suddenly registers the danger: the Second Spirit is disintegrating alongside the Immortals, because the Second Spirit exists *within* that same spiritual flesh.
- Li Huowang rushes into the courtyard to see the Second Spirit’s face half-gone, her body collapsing. She gives him a final, intimate warning: “Live well. Don’t let her down.”
- Li Huowang tries to stop Lian Zhibei, but she has lost awareness. After a chaotic fight, Li Huowang stabs her through the chest while she tears off his arm.
- As the incense burns out, Lian Zhibei returns to consciousness, bewildered and in pain, not knowing what went wrong.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Let's talk about "Possessed Medium" (Qì Tóng 起乩 / 乩童). This is a real, living tradition in Chinese folk religion that has survived for centuries. A *jitong* is a person who becomes the physical vehicle for a god — their body is essentially rented. The violent self-mutilation (piercing cheeks with spikes, stabbing lips with hooks) is a crucial part of the *proof*: because the deity is occupying the body, the practitioner feels no pain and sheds blood without injury. If they do not bleed unnaturally clean, the possession is either failed or false. Lian Zhibei's entire performance — the tiger growl, the gavel slam, the firecracker-snorting — is exactly how a rural folk-medium would have invoked a martial guardian spirit in historical China.

The "gavel of the crouching tiger" she slams is a miniature judge's block (jīngtángmù), usually used in court or ritual theater to command spirits and declare authority. Combined with incense and firecrackers — which both clear "yang" space and drive away lesser residual yin — the ritual creates an aura of absolute force.

Now, why does this hurt the Second Spirit? Because in the *chuma* / *jitong* system, the "Second Spirit" or *er shen* is not a separate ghost; she is a fragment of Bai Lingmiao’s own consciousness that has grown a personality around the Immortals. The Immortals' flesh is literally part of what makes her exist. When Lian Zhibei destroys one, she destroys the other. It's a tragedy woven into the metaphysics: exorcism, when pushed too far, becomes amputation of the self.

### Mind-Eye Perception (心眼通)

A supernatural sense attained by Bai Lingmiao after consuming a Buddhist relic; allows her to perceive qi, auras, and the true state of beings — including the Second Spirit's dissolution.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *consequence*. Li Huowang, for all his tactical smarts, misreads the situation at the worst possible moment. He hears the Immortals scream and thinks *victory* — but the price tag turns out to be the silent woman in the red veil who has watched over Bai Lingmiao from the shadows. This is the Dao-Twisted World reminding us yet again that no power comes without cost, and that the ones who pay are not always the ones who choose.

The fight scene between Li Huowang and Lian Zhibei is deliberately chaotic. It’s not a clean duel — it’s two wounded animals reacting to a broken ritual. The fact that both end up impaled and dismembered, only for Lian Zhibei to return to her senses and ask "What happened?" in confusion, is *perfect*. There is no villain to hate here. Just a tool that cut too deep, and a user who lost control.

Pay close attention to the Second Spirit’s final line. She scolds Li Huowang for being a weepy little bitch. That’s not just a barb — it’s her last act of *caring*. She knows she’s dying, so she gives him the one thing that might keep him fighting: a sharp, loving insult. That’s good writing.

Oh, and one more thing: Li Huowang’s self-mutilation is back. He shoves his own sword into his mouth to weaponize his teeth as projectiles. This is becoming a signature — when logic fails, he pays in flesh. The pattern is solidifying: Li Huowang is a man who is learning to treat his own body as a consumable resource.
