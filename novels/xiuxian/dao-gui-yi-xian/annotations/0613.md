## Chapter Overview

After a brutal stretch of body horror and ontological warfare, this chapter pulls back the curtain on something almost stranger: domestic life. Li Sui is happily attending school, charming the villagers with her eerie beauty, and learning how to be a “girl” under Bai Lingmiao’s gentle guidance. But the chapter’s real weight sinks in as Bai Lingmiao confesses her fears about motherhood and her quiet envy of Li Sui’s innocence—all while the supernatural world (White Lotus, Sitian Jian) presses in just beyond the window. It’s a slice-of-life breather that tastes like ash and sugar mixed together.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Sui proudly attends the village private school, reciting the *Three Character Classic* and delighting in the fact that people no longer fear her.
- Her breathtaking appearance distracts both the teacher and the students, filling the usually half-empty school to the brim.
- After class, she returns to the Bai compound, where Bai Lingmiao intercepts her, explaining that Li Huowang is meeting with an important guest.
- Bai Lingmiao gives Li Sui a bath and teaches her feminine hygiene and dress, serving as an unexpected maternal figure.
- During the bath, Bai Lingmiao confesses her grief: she longs to bear Li Huowang a child but fears passing on her white-fur condition.
- Bai Lingmiao also admits that while she was initially disturbed by Li Sui’s monstrous body, she has learned to accept her as family.
- A White Lotus agent appears at the window, pressing for a meeting with the Sitian Jian. Bai Lingmiao dismisses them curtly, asserting her autonomy.
- Liu Zongyuan, leaving Li Huowang’s room, spots a tentacle under Li Sui’s skirt, panics, then pretends he saw nothing after realizing it’s just her.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The *Three Character Classic* (三字经) that Li Sui recites is one of the most foundational texts in Chinese literacy education, used for centuries to teach basic Confucian morals and character recognition. The very first line—“Men at their birth, are naturally good”—is a direct reference to Mencius’s theory of innate human goodness. Hearing it chanted cheerfully by a shapeshifting tentacle-monster in human skin is exactly the kind of tonal dissonance *Dao Gui Yi Xian* excels at.

The term “Sitian Jian” (司天监) historically was an imperial office responsible for astronomy, calendar-making, and interpreting celestial omens. In this novel’s setting, it has evolved into a bureaucratic organization that polices the supernatural—a fitting counterpart to the White Lotus Society’s underground messianic structure. The fact that they want to “talk” with Bai Lingmiao as the White Lotus Holy Maiden suggests escalating political tension behind the scenes.

Also worth noting: the “human skin” Li Sui wears is not metaphorical here. She is a Black Tai Sui entity that learned to craft a human appearance from flayed skin. Bai Lingmiao’s gentle acceptance of this reality—even washing the skin alongside her—shows how far the Bai compound has come from screaming to domesticity.

### Three Character Classic

The most famous Confucian children’s primer in Chinese history, teaching moral principles and basic literacy in neat three-character verses. Its opening lines about innate human goodness are a direct reference to Mencius.

## Reading Guide

This is one of the most emotionally delicate chapters in the entire novel so far. It rewards careful reading by contrasting monstrous biology with deeply human tenderness. Pay particular attention to Bai Lingmiao’s confession: her fear of passing on her “defect” to a child echoes real-world anxieties about hereditary illness and disability, and her acceptance of Li Sui—initially hesitant, now warmly maternal—shows a quiet maturation that the novel rarely pauses to dramatize. If you’ve been craving Li Sui development and Bai Lingmiao inner life, this chapter is a small feast.

On the flip side, don’t miss the threat lurking at the edges. The White Lotus agent’s intrusion, Liu Zongyuan’s quick cover-up, and the looming Sitian Jian meeting all remind us that this sanctuary is fragile. The bath scene is the eye of a storm, not the end of one.
