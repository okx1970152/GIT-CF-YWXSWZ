## Chapter Overview

Chapter 5, 'Alchemical Pills,' marks a pivotal turn in Li Huowang's relationship with the hallucinatory cave world. After the chaos of the previous night, Danyangzi promotes Li Huowang to registered disciple, gives him the Dao name Xuan Yang, and hands him five supposedly miraculous pills. But the real shock comes when Li Huowang returns to his hospital room—the pills and the jade pendant have come back with him. This tangible evidence forces him to abandon the doctor's simple solution and adopt a pragmatic dual-reality strategy. The chapter closes with him summoning Yang Na as an accomplice, revealing his growing willingness to engage with both worlds.

## Key Plot Points

- Danyangzi uses fear and reward to control his disciples after the rebellion: he promotes Li Huowang to registered disciple with the Dao name Xuan Yang, implying the previous holder's death is now irrelevant.
- Li Huowang receives five black pills that Danyangzi claims grant ten years of life and temporary superhuman strength. Li Huowang briefly sees one pill pulse as if something is inside, then dismisses it.
- Returned to the hospital, Li Huowang discovers both the jade pendant and the pills physically present on his bedsheet, shattering his belief that the cave world is just a figment.
- After an agonizing internal debate, Li Huowang devises a strategy: he will treat the cave world as real for the sake of survival, but verify the cross-dimensional objects through testing rather than blind consumption.
- He decides against using fellow patients as test subjects and instead contacts Yang Na to pawn the jade pendant for money to buy lab mice.
- The chapter ends with Yang Na arriving, suspicious but willing to help, and Li Huowang closing the door and curtains to speak in private.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Many readers may wonder about the concept of a 'registered disciple' (记名弟子). In traditional Daoist sects, disciples were often divided into inner disciples (内门弟子)—those who received direct training in core arts—and registered disciples (记名弟子), who were formally acknowledged students but taught only basic or outer practices. Here, Danyangzi uses the promotion as a political tool: by giving Li Huowang the same Dao name as his predecessor (Xuan Yang), he erases that disciple's death and claims continuity. The name itself—Xuan (玄, profound/dark) and Yang (阳, sun)—follows the pattern of alchemical nomenclature where opposites are combined.

The pills given to Li Huowang are called 'immortal elixirs' (神仙丹药). In Chinese alchemical tradition, external alchemy (外丹) involved compounding minerals and herbs into pills believed to grant longevity or supernatural powers. However, the novel consistently twists this tradition into body horror: these pills are made from human ingredients, as implied by the earlier mention of a senior sister being 'refined into a pill.' The slight movement Li Huowang sees—the pill pulsing—hints that something alive or undead is contained within.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the first major test of the novel's central question: if objects can cross between worlds, is the cave world merely a hallucination? Li Huowang's decision to treat both as real while maintaining a cautious verification process shows his growing cunning. Pay attention to his internal dialogue—he still uses doctor-recommended phrases like 'it's all fake,' but his actions betray a man who now suspects otherwise. The introduction of Yang Na, a character from his modern life, creates a tangible bridge between the two realities. The chapter also reinforces Danyangzi's manipulative nature: he knows exactly how to use rewards (status, pills) to bind his remaining disciples after a bloody purge.

## The Black Cauldron as a Symbol of Corrupted Alchemy {#black-cauldron}

The Black Cauldron (黑鼎) mentioned in Li Huowang's flashback is a recurring symbol of how traditional alchemical apparatus has been perverted in the Dao-Twisted World. In classical Daoist external alchemy, the cauldron (鼎) was a sacred vessel for compounding elixirs, often associated with transformation and refinement. Here, it is used to devour disciples alive—a literal consumption that mirrors the alchemical process but inverts its purpose. Instead of refining minerals into immortality, the Black Cauldron refines human beings into raw material. The fact that the previous Xuan Yang was 'devoured by the Black Cauldron' adds a sinister layer to Li Huowang's new Dao name: he is inheriting not just a title, but a fate that may consume him.
