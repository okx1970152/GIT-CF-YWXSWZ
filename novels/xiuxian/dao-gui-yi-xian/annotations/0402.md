## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers a brutal one-two punch: first, a grim object lesson in the Dao-Twisted World’s raw power hierarchy through Lü Xiucai’s traumatizing encounter with Blind Chen; then, a quiet, lore-dense carriage ride where Zhuge Yuan casually drops a worldview-shattering truth about the Zuowandao’s divine backing. The whiplash between Xiucai’s visceral terror—his self-worth shattered, his ambitions twisted into a hungry, envious spiral—and Zhuge Yuan’s Socratic calm while lecturing a dog on history is exactly the kind of tonal contrast this world excels at. By the end, Li Huowang is forced to confront a fundamental question that recontextualizes his entire struggle: the Zuowandao have a Siming too.

## Key Plot Points

- Lü Xiucai witnesses Blind Chen massacre Kui Ye and his men effortlessly. His attempt to beg for mercy using Li Huowang’s name fails when Blind Chen dismisses the name entirely.
- Blind Chen spares Xiucai only because he pays with five spiritually-imbued copper coins, leaving Xiucai alive but psychologically shattered.
- In the aftermath, Xiucai’s fear transforms into a burning, envious ambition: he wants to become *that powerful*—and resolves to beg Li Huowang to teach him the divine arts.
- The scene cuts to Zhuge Yuan lecturing the dog Mantou on the founding of Great Qi, in a transparent attempt to teach Li Huowang history.
- When they pass a roadside noodle stall, Li Huowang’s paranoia about a Zuowandao ambush is dismissed by Zhuge Yuan, who calmly states he can *see the threads* connecting the Zuowandao to their Siming.
- The revelation lands: the Zuowandao are not chaos-worshipping nihilists without higher backing—they have their own Siming, just like the Heart-Embedded.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Cultivate the True vs. Cultivate the False (修真 vs. 修假):** This chapter subtly deepens one of the novel’s core philosophical conflicts. The standard xianxia idiom “cultivate truth” (修真) refers to a practitioner’s quest for the authentic dao, often through enlightenment, alchemy, or martial perfection. The Zuowandao’s inversion—“cultivating falsehood” (修假)—literally weaponizes illusion and deception as a methodology for power. But if they, too, draw their abilities from a Siming (a higher-order Arbiter of Fate), then the dichotomy is not “truth vs. lies” but “whose higher power backs whose version of reality.” Zhuge Yuan’s quiet revelation reframes the Zuowandao not as clever liars but as a rival cult with its own celestial patron.

**The Founding of Great Qi:** Zhuge Yuan’s history lesson to Mantou includes the Black Heavenly Calamity (黑天灾) as a founding event—a cataclysm that reshaped the land and forced a migration led by General Qi Lu. This is a classic xianxia-world-establishing trope, except the novel leaves the nature of the “Black Heavenly Calamity” deliberately ominous. The mention that the Supervisory Heavenly Office was founded by Qi Lu’s grandson, Qi Heng, ties the current bureaucratic order directly to this traumatic origin.

## Reading Guide

This is a deceptively quiet chapter. Don’t let the noodle-stall domesticity fool you—Zhuge Yuan dropping “they have a Siming” is a paradigm shift. Up until now, readers (and Li Huowang) have treated the Zuowandao as a rogue sect of malicious tricksters, power-hungry agents of chaos wearing human faces. But a Siming is serious business. It means that the “lies” of the Zuowandao are structurally equivalent to the “reality” of the Heart-Embedded world—both are backed by a higher-dimensional patron. The question becomes: whose truth is more true? This chapter plants a seed that will bear fruit later, especially regarding the Four Joys and the upper echelons of the Zuowandao. Meanwhile, Lü Xiucai’s corruption arc receives a crucial push: from self-pitying coward to an ambitious predator-in-training, driven by the lesson that power is the only currency that matters in this world.

## Deeper Lore: The Threads of the Siming

Zhuge Yuan’s ability to “see the threads” connecting the Zuowandao to their Siming is a major hint about the Heart-Embedded’s nature. A Heart-Embedded (*xinpan*, 心蟠) is a person whose soul or destiny has been occupied by a Siming, making them a vessel or anchor for a higher power. This grants them cognition beyond mortal limits—including, apparently, the ability to perceive the *lines of influence* connecting a practitioner to their patron. This is different from mere supernatural detection; it suggests that the relationship between a Siming and its followers has a visible, almost topological manifestation. For Zhuge Yuan to see these threads on a Zuowandao who is not even actively using their power implies that this connection is always present, a kind of spiritual umbilical cord. For Li Huowang, this raises an uncomfortable question: is he himself also “wearing” threads that others can see?
