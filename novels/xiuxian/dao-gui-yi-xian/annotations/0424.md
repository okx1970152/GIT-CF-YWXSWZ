## Chapter Overview
Bai Lingmiao is back in Niuxin Village, and she is *done* being gentle. This chapter is a quiet, domestic power shift: she signs contracts, plans to fill the village, and slaughters a pig for her new “family.” But the real action is in the hidden chamber at night, where she wrestles with the White Lotus legacy, scolds her weeping Second Spirit for pining over Li Huowang, and stumbles into a hallucinatory ritual triggered by a jade lotus. The mural shifts from a single lotus to a “twin lotus”—a symbol that promises connection but may deliver something far more dangerous.

## Key Plot Points
*   Bai Lingmiao returns to Niuxin Village and immediately systematizes her rule: contracts, handprints, long-term laborers. She is building something permanent.
*   Lü Zhuangyuan beats and berates his son, Lü Xiucai, for stealing money and attempting to flee; their public shouting match provides a crude, human counterpoint to the more supernatural tensions at play.
*   Bai Lingmiao organizes a feast for the new workers, slaughtering a pig; Yang Xiaohai cooks the entire animal. It’s a rare moment of communal warmth.
*   In the ancestral hall’s secret chamber at night, Bai Lingmiao examines the White Lotus manuals with a sharp, pragmatic mind. She bluntly dismisses the Second Spirit’s longing for Li Huowang, accusing her of being a burden.
*   The jade lotus on the cabinet begins to flicker, triggering a strange, multi-voiced chant that overpowers Bai Lingmiao, threatening to dissolve her emotions and murderous intent.
*   She embraces the lotus for relief, and when the vision ends, the mural on the wall has transformed from a single lotus into a “twin lotus” (并蒂莲), suggesting a split, duplication, or a bond that has become something else entirely.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The **twin lotus (并蒂莲, bìngdìlián)** is a classic Chinese symbol of marital harmony and inseparable love—two flowers growing from a single stalk. In the novel's hands, this auspicious symbol becomes deeply unsettling: the single entity worshipped by the Bai family has *split*, and the implications are ominous rather than romantic. The strange chant Bai Lingmiao hears (“Great Qian甘露 Realm… Seven Treasures Fragrant Forest…”) is a pastiche of Daoist and Buddhist liturgical language, deliberately garbled to evoke a corrupted sacred text. This is a recurring technique in *Dao Gui Yi Xian*: holy language hollowed out and repurposed by something that is *almost* divine but not quite right.

The **White Lotus Society** and its connection to **Wusheng Laomu** are central to this chapter. Bai Lingmiao’s secret chamber is filled with the sect’s forbidden texts, and the jade lotus is clearly a vessel for a higher power. The chanting and visual distortion suggest that the “Unborn Venerable Mother” is not a passive idol but an active, hungry presence that communicates through sensory-overload “revelations.”

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a *respite* chapter that feels anything but restful. Bai Lingmiao’s evolution is the highlight: she is no longer the shy girl following Li Huowang’s orders. She is pragmatic, sharp-tongued, and building her own power base without asking permission. Her cruelty toward the Second Spirit is striking—“Would you die without him?”—and reveals a hardened survivor who has no patience for weakness. The jade lotus vision is a classic *Dao Gui* hallucination: beautiful, terrifying, and layered with meaning that the characters themselves don’t yet understand. Watch for the “twin lotus” as a major symbol going forward. The chapter also lowers the pressure, letting readers breathe before the next wave of horror; cherish the pig feast while it lasts.

## Deep Dive: Chuma Mediumship and the Second Spirit
This chapter offers a rare glimpse into the mechanics of the **chuma (出马) spirit medium tradition**, which is deeply rooted in Northeastern Chinese folk religion. In this system, a living medium (the “lead spirit”) enters a pact with an immortal entity (often an animal spirit like a fox, weasel, or snake). The medium provides their body as a vessel; in return, the immortal grants power, healing, or protection. The **Second Spirit (二神)** is the assistant who helps guide the ritual and acts as a bridge between the medium and the spirit world.

Here, the “Second Spirit” is a silent, puppet-like woman in a red bridal veil—a personification of the role. The horror comes from her liminal status: she is not fully human (or is she?), and she acts with an uncanny, independent will (foraging, moving, inserting herself into sleep). Bai Lingmiao’s harsh treatment of her reflects a deeper truth: in this world, relationships with spirits are transactional and often cruel. The Second Spirit’s longing for Li Huowang is both a sign of her latent humanity and a dangerous weakness that Bai Lingmiao ruthlessly exploits.
