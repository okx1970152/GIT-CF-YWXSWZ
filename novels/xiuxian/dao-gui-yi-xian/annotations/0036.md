## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a *breather*—and anyone who's been reading *Daoist Twisted Immortal* knows just how dangerous that word is. After the life-or-death confrontation with the two-faced woman, Li Huowang and his ragged band of former human ingredients finally have a moment to rest, reorganize, and stock up. He buys a donkey cart with his hard-won silver, shares a proper roast chicken with the group, and even starts teaching Zhao Wu to read. But beneath the mundane rhythms of travel preparation, this chapter quietly builds toward something more ominous: the first real test of whether these broken people can build something resembling a life—or whether the world's brutal hierarchies will crush them all over again. The appearance of a spoiled rich boy who wants to run away with the opera troupe, only to be violently dragged back by his father, serves as a stark reminder that in the Dao-Twisted World, even dreams come with chains.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang buys a donkey and cart to haul supplies, marking his transition from fleeing survivor to someone who can actually plan for the road ahead.
- He shares a roast chicken with the group—a small but meaningful gesture that builds morale among the former human ingredients.
- Bai Lingmiao touches Li Huowang's hand, and for a moment, there is real intimacy between them; he starts to pull away, but she holds on.
- Zhao Wu shocks everyone by refusing to learn alchemy; instead, he begs Li Huowang to teach him to read, revealing a desperate hunger for knowledge and dignity.
- The Lü family and even the other human ingredients listen to Li Huowang's literacy lesson with awe, treating it as a sacred act.
- Young Master Hu, a wealthy boy from Wuli Gang, tries to join the opera troupe out of passion for the art, only to be publicly slapped and berated by his father for wanting to enter the "lowest of the low" profession.
- Lü Zhuangyuan tries to speak to Li Huowang after the incident but fails, walking away with his back bent lower than before—a silent moment of shared understanding and shame.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Nine Classes (九流)**. This chapter brings a core social structure of the Dao-Twisted World into sharp focus. Traditional Chinese society, especially in novels, sometimes classified people into three broad categories (upper, middle, lower), but here the system is harsher and more rigid. The "lowest of the low" that the father screams about refers to actors and prostitutes, who were legally restricted in terms of marriage, official examinations, and burial rights. In the Dao-Twisted World, this is not just social snobbery—it's a life sentence. Lü Zhuangyuan's cowed silence after the incident is not weakness; it's the posture of a man who has internalized a lifetime of being told his profession makes him less than human.

**Teaching literacy as sacred ritual**. The reverence with which the group watches Li Huowang teach Zhao Wu might seem exaggerated to a modern reader, but in a world where books are rare, knowledge is power, and most people—especially the poor and disabled—are locked out of literacy by a brutal system, watching someone learn to read *is* a kind of miracle. This is also why Zhao Wu chooses literacy over alchemy: alchemy makes you useful to a master; literacy lets you *think* for yourself.

**The donkey as practical travel tech**. Donkeys are slower than horses but cheaper, hardier, and better suited to rough terrain. In the Dao-Twisted World, a donkey cart isn't just transportation—it's a lifeline that frees the group from carrying their supplies and lets them move faster if danger chases them.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *palate cleanser*—a quiet stretch of road before the next storm. If you've been binge-reading and your heart rate needs a break, this is the place to breathe. But here's the trick: **pay close attention to the quiet moments**, because this author loves to hide character development in plain sight. Bai Lingmiao holding Li Huowang's hand after he tries to let go is a small gesture that speaks volumes about how she sees him—not as a monster, but as someone worth reaching for. Zhao Wu's desperate plea to learn to read, over the more "practical" skill of alchemy, tells you everything about what he values: not just survival, but *meaning*.

And then there's Young Master Hu. You'll laugh at his comic-opera enthusiasm, but the violence of his father's reaction is a slap that echoes far beyond the scene. The most chilling line: *"That's the lowest of the low! The dregs of the nine classes!"* It's not just a father's anger—it's the mouthpiece of an entire world telling you where people like the Lü family stand. And when Lü Zhuangyuan walks away without saying what he wanted to say, his silence is heavier than any speech. Get ready—the road to Jianye Town is still long, and the deeper we go into this world, the more it's going to bite.
