## Chapter Overview
Who needs flashy sword techniques when you can weaponize a crack in reality? Get ready, fellow veterans of the strange—this chapter is a masterclass in *how not to assassinate a warlord who’s been expecting you*. Our favorite paranoid patient springs a trap on the Mercenary King, only to find that the target was wearing a whole dungeon of backup plans. Li Huowang’s ambush turns into a frantic scramble against two masked Wealth Gods, a paper-thin escape artist, and an army of shadow puppets. The fight is fast, brutal, and ends with our hero scrabbling for a portal to another dimension. Not bad for a guy whose left hand just became a projectile weapon.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang catches the Mercenary King mid-performance, engaged in a grotesque, sung sex-act with a male Dan. His opening strike is clean—the Meridian King is cut in half along with his partner—but the job is not done.
- A palm-sized skin-man (a life-preserving artifact) crawls out of the Mercenary King’s hair and begins to regrow, forcing Li Huowang into a chase. This is our first clear confirmation that high-value targets in the Dao-Twisted World carry insurance policies no apothecary would approve.
- Shadow-puppet entities grab Li Huowang’s shadow to immobilize him. Li Sui counters by destroying the candlelight, which bans the puppets—a small but clever supernatural rule: shadow-puppets need shadows, and shadows need light.
- A Civil Wealth God (mask, giant gold ingot) and a Martial Wealth God (mask, silver mace, riding a leopard) appear in the smoke. The Civil Wealth God immediately takes control of Li Huowang’s copper-coin sword, reversing an asset into a liability.
- Li Huowang cuts off his own left hand mid-combat, using the severed arm as a ranged claw-attack to create an opening. He re-equips his spinal sword, but the enemy presses him from both sides.
- Outnumbered and out-timed, Li Huowang rips open a large fissure to the Great Qi realm as an escape route. He shoves himself toward the gap with Li Sui’s help and talisman-enhanced speed, but the chapter ends before he reaches it.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Wealth Gods (财神)** aren’t mere mythology here—they’ve been weaponized into bodyguard archetypes. In Chinese folk religion, the *Civil Wealth God* (文财神, usually Bi Gan or Fan Li) represents wisdom and prosperity, often depicted with a golden ingot. The *Martial Wealth God* (武财神, Guan Gong or Zhao Gongming) is a warrior-deity of fortune, often mounted and armored. The Mercenary King’s summoners have replicated both, turning blessings into bludgeons. The Civil Wealth God’s ability to forcibly redirect copper-coin talismans and weapons is a clever twist on the folk belief that a Wealth God *owns* all monetary objects.

**Skin-man (皮人)** artifacts appear in Daoist and folk tales as a crude form of phylactery—a practitioner severs their consciousness into a miniature replica of their own body, often made from their own shed skin or hair. If the main body dies, the skin-man can regenerate. In this chapter, the Mercenary King’s escape via skin-man confirms that he’s not just a mundane bandit-king; he’s a cultivator with back-up plans that violate the flesh.

**Shadow-puppet entities (皮影影人)** draw on the mechanics of *piying*, the ancient art of shadow puppetry. But here, the puppets reverse the art: instead of manipulating light-cast shadows, they seize the *real* shadow and immobilize the living body. The counter—snuffing the candles—is a logical, horror-rule exploit that Li Huowang has learned to calculate on the fly.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is pure action-thriller with a supernatural armor set. If you’ve been missing the breathless, tactical combat sequences from earlier arcs, this is your feast. The pacing is frantic—every parry is followed by a new trap, every kill by a replacement body. Pay close attention to the **Great Qi fissure** mechanic: Li Huowang doesn’t just use it as a one-hit-kill sword wave; he now treats it as a *dimensional door*, a trick that could reshape his entire mobility playbook. The ending is a cliffhanger (will he reach the tear before the Wealth Gods pin him?), but the real takeaway is Li Huowang’s growing ruthlessness: self-amputation as a tactical throw is a new low in his cost-of-doing-business ledger.
