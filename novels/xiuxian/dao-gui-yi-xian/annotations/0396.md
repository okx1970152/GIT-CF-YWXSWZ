## Chapter Overview
Oho, fellow Daoists, strap in because Chapter 395 is a *heavy* lore dump served with a side of existential dread. Li Huowang finally gets a name for the nameless horror of his existence: he’s not just a freak with hallucinations; he’s a **Heart-Element**, a being that *cultivates the true*. And in a world where the Zuowandao are busy *cultivating the false*, that makes him their natural nemesis. But here’s the kicker—the grand reveal that Danyangzi’s accidental immortality was entirely *Li Huowang’s fault*, an unconscious act of reality-bending power. Zhuge Yuan plays the gentle Socratic tutor, leading our baffled protagonist through a minefield of self-discovery, while Li Huowang spirals into the mother of all identity crises: if he can make things real without knowing, how much of his own story is even *his*?

## Key Plot Points
- The chapter opens mid-debate: Li Huowang grapples with the revelation that "Heart-Element" means he cultivates the true, making him the Zuowandao’s ideological opposite.
- Zhuge Yuan dismantles Li Huowang’s denial by presenting the Danyangzi elixir formula. He argues that Danyangzi’s success was not due to the crude formula but because Danyangzi’s still-living flesh inside Li Huowang’s body *borrowed* his Heart-Element ability to fulfill its obsession with immortality.
- Li Huowang experiences a paradigm-shattering moment: his hated enemy’s ascension was his own unintended doing, proving his power is real even if he can’t control it.
- The philosophical trap is set: Li Huowang tries to retroactively determine which events in his past were real and which he *made* real. The boundary between “what happened” and “what I willed into happening” blurs fatally.
- Zhuge Yuan confirms there is a method for using this ability, but admits he only read about it once and doesn’t know where the full text is, leaving Li Huowang with a tantalizing, frustrating lead.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**“Cultivate the True” vs. “Cultivate the False” (修真 vs. 修假):** This is the chapter where the novel finally *names its core philosophical war*. In standard xianxia, “cultivating the true” (修真) is the default path—a seeker refines their spirit and body to achieve alignment with the Dao’s ultimate truth. The novel weaponizes this generic term by giving it a literal, terrifying edge: a Heart-Element *makes things real through belief alone*. The Zuowandao’s “cultivating the false” is its dark mirror, a path that *unmakes* reality by injecting lies into the fabric of existence. It’s not just a power system; it’s an ontological arms race.

**The Heart-Element as an Unwitting Creator:** This chapter echoes a classical Daoist paradox: the sage who acts without acting (无为, wu wei). Li Huowang’s power works best when he doesn’t know he’s using it—like a child’s unfiltered belief making a drawing come to life. The horror is that this power doesn’t require *skill* or *morality*; it only requires proximity to a sufficiently strong *obsession* (执念, zhí niàn). Danyangzi’s obsession was so potent that it hijacked Li Huowang’s body as a reactor, not unlike how a parasitic wasp uses a host to incubate its young.

**Demigod (半仙):** The novel carefully avoids calling Danyangzi a true immortal. A “demigod” or “half-immortal” occupies a liminal space—still bound by karma and flesh, but beyond normal human limitations. The fact that a *fake* formula combined with a *real* Heart-Element could produce even this result sells the sheer, terrifying potency of Li Huowang’s innate nature.

## Reading Guide
This is a *sherlockian deduction chapter*, not an action scene. Watch how Zhuge Yuan guides Li Huowang without handing him answers—he lays out the evidence (the written formula) and lets Li Huowang’s mind make the horrifying connection. The subtext here is masterful: Zhuge Yuan isn’t just a helpful scholar; he’s a diagnostic tool, a mirror that reflects the truth Li Huowang has been avoiding. Pay attention to the moment Li Huowang tries to sift through his past to find other instances of “false-to-true” creation. That silence, that pause, is where the real novel lives. The question “What is real?” is no longer a philosophical exercise for Li Huowang—it’s a *forensic audit of his own memory*. Also, note the loaded reference to “North Wind”: it confirms that Li Huowang’s original plan (seeking help from another Heart-Element) is still on the table, but now he has a second option: mastering his own terrifying, inborn power.
