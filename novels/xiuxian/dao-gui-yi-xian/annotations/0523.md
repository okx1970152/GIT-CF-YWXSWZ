## Chapter Overview
Turns out being the emperor’s personal assassin doesn’t come with a “no strings attached” clause. In this chapter, Li Huowang collects his spoils (a gold token and a royal IOU), but reality sinks in fast as he realizes a crown is just a bigger target. The new Great Liang Emperor, Ji Lin, is all fire and vengeance, but even he can’t just point and shoot. Palace politics is a different beast from the wilds, and Li Huowang is already smelling a trap. Meanwhile, back at home, Li Sui tries her hand at domestic bliss (emphasis on *tries*), and a certain scorched-paper man from the late princess’s kitchen makes an entrance. Let’s just say: the web is getting stickier, and Dice’s silhouette looms larger with every move.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Morning After the Coup**: Ji Lin vents his fury in court over the deaths of his mother and sister, ordering a manhunt for his family’s killers. The officials are either too scared or too complicit to speak up, and a heavy atmosphere of paranoia hangs over the throne room.
- **Li Huowang Collects His Due**: Ji Lin offers rewards, but Li Huowang declines official titles and gold—he wants an unpaid favor instead. Ji Lin grants him a Gold Life-Saving Token, a pass that will only work as long as Ji Lin stays alive and on the throne.
- **The Zuowandao Thread**: Li Huowang probes Ji Lin about his hatred for the Zuowandao. Ji Lin confirms his father died by their hand. This gives Li Huowang the opening he needs to eventually use imperial power against Dice. But Zhuge Yuan soothes Li Huowang’s fear that Ji Lin himself might be a Zuowandao pawn—the Supervisory Heavenly Office watches that closely.
- **Home Front**: Li Huowang returns to find Li Sui has made steamed buns. They are burnt, lumpy, and inedible. He eats one anyway. This quiet domestic breather is immediately interrupted by Zu Yi—the burned, enigmatic figure who sided with Li Huowang against the Eldest Princess.
- **The Scorched Man Arrives**: A veiled, charcoal-smelling man enters the courtyard, seeking an audience with the “Great Elder.” Li Huowang and Li Sui both sense danger, but the game is still unfolding.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Gold Life-Saving Token (免死金牌)**: A metaphor-as-trope in imperial Chinese fiction. It’s a real historical concept—emperors sometimes granted these to meritorious subjects as a symbol of ultimate imperial favor. The catch, as Li Huowang immediately sees, is that it only works under the emperor who issued it. Change the dynasty, and the token becomes a paperweight.
- **The Politics of a New Emperor**: Ji Lin’s rage is genuine, but his problem is not knowing *who* to trust. In a coup, most officials either side with the victor or hide their true allegiances. Ji Lin cannot simply order a purge—he needs to shift the *political base* of his enemies to his side first. That is why he tells Li Huowang that killing is only half the job.
- **Homey Horror Reprieve**: The scene with Li Sui and the burnt buns is a classic Dao-Guai-Yi-Xian breather. The world’s horror seeps even into domesticity: a skull-headed “daughter” trying to please her adoptive father, a failed meal, a deadpan rejection. It grounds cosmic terror in the mundane, which makes the return of danger (Zu Yi at the door) hit harder.
- **Zu Yi (足一)**: This name means “Foot One” or “First Step.” Given his connection to the Eldest Princess’s rebellion and his defection to Li Huowang, this feels like a code-name or a numbered identity. The “scorched” smell suggests he was literally burned in some past event—possibly related to the fire-based alchemy of the Ao-Jing Sect.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the slow burn of Ji Lin’s character**: He used to be a blunt, hotheaded young man. Now he’s a ruler who can switch from stage rage to cold calculation the moment the doors close. That is not just character growth—it’s survival instinct. Li Huowang is right to be suspicious of anyone who learns to wear masks that quickly.
- **Li Huowang’s new role as “Great Elder”**: This title appears here for the first time. If Li Huowang is building some kind of faction, calling him “Great Elder” implies a leadership position within a sect or cult. Given his growing crew of misfits (Li Sui, Gouwa, the burned man), a power structure is quietly taking shape.
- **The meta-anxiety of this chapter**: Li Huowang cannot move without asking: “Would Dice have anticipated this?” That is the real horror of this section. It is not the monster in the room—it is the invisible puppeteer who might already be three moves ahead. This kind of mental chess is a hallmark of *Dao Guai Yi Xian*’s unique tension.
- **Don’t gloss over the domestic scene**: The burnt bun moment is not filler. It shows Li Sui trying to be normal, Li Huowang allowing himself a tiny moment of warmth, and the inevitable intrusion of darkness via a knock at the door. That rhythm—breathe, then choke—is the novel’s signature pacing. Savor it before the next storm.
