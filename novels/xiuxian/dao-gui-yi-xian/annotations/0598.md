## Chapter Overview

Alright, buckle in, fellow travelers—this chapter is a palate cleanser of the highest order, and I mean that in the most unsettling way possible. We're back in the modern reality, and Li Huowang is navigating the bizarre social ecosystem of his new group therapy at Kangning Hospital. The chapter's primary tension isn't a monster or a Siming—it's the terrifying prospect of being *known*. A fellow patient outing him as "that guy from the news" nearly sparks a riot. But before things go off the rails, Dr. Wu Cheng steps in with a masterclass in crowd control, reframing Li Huowang from a menace into a success story. The rest of the chapter is a slow, awkward dance of introductions, small acts of human decency (Li Huowang calming a guilt-ridden depressive girl), and a final, chillingly mundane line from Wu Cheng that drags the metaphysical horror right back through the front door: "Your treatment starts this afternoon." It's quiet, it's clinical, and it's utterly unnerving.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Outing Incident:** A female patient in the therapy group recognizes Li Huowang from a past news report about his violent psychotic break. She screams in fear, causing a near-riot among the other patients who all remember the story.
*   **Wu Cheng's Intervention:** Dr. Wu Cheng skillfully de-escalates the situation. He uses Li Huowang as a "success story," suggesting that if a man that crazy can be cured, there's hope for everyone. He restores order with practiced, charismatic authority.
*   **Social Navigation:** Li Huowang finds himself an outcast again, but he's learned to live with it. He participates in introductions, learning the names and conditions of his new group: Zhang Shui'e (anxiety), Wei Shidi (bipolar), Zhao Ting (depression), Wang Wangshu (schizophrenia), and Zhang Jinzhong (autism).
*   **The Compassionate Act:** During a break, the depressive patient Zhao Ting is crushed by guilt for outing him. Li Huowang, recognizing the spiral of self-blame, takes the time to console her, demonstrating a hard-won empathy for the intricacies of mental illness.
*   **The Jianghu of the Asylum:** In a conversation with Wu Cheng, Li Huowang explains the petty social dynamics of the ward—the small cruelties and thefts that doctors never see. He frames it with a worldly observation: "Wherever there are people, there's a social order."
*   **The Final Hook:** As Li Huowang finishes getting his lunch, Wu Cheng casually informs him that his "treatment" begins that afternoon. This directly contradicts Yi Donglai's earlier promise that he just needed to wait out his time, planting a new seed of dread.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **The Jianghu of the Psychiatric Ward:** Li Huowang's comment, "Wherever there are people, there's a *jianghu*," is a classic piece of Chinese cultural shorthand. *Jianghu* (江湖), literally "rivers and lakes," is the name for the chaotic underworld of martial artists and adventurers in wuxia novels. It implies a society with its own unwritten rules, hierarchies, and conflicts operating beneath the surface of the law. By applying this term to a psychiatric hospital, Li Huowang acknowledges that human nature—and its petty cruelties—is universal, even in a place meant for healing. It's a small but potent moment of a xianxia protagonist applying his grim, worldly wisdom to the "real world."
*   **Group Therapy Dynamics:** The structure of the therapy session seen here—group introductions, facilitated conversation, "icebreaker" games—is a standard part of modern psychiatric treatment. The novel uses this realistic, clinical setting as a stark contrast to the visceral, rule-based horror of the Dao-Twisted World. The horror here is social, psychological, and mundane, making the sudden intrusion of the supernatural (the threat of "treatment") all the more jarring.

### jianghu (江湖)

A term meaning "rivers and lakes," it refers to the underground society of martial artists, wanderers, and outlaws in Chinese wuxia culture, defined by its own codes and conflicts.

### Kangning Hospital (康宁医院)

The name of a private psychiatric hospital, meaning "Health and Peace." A new, more refined cage for Li Huowang.

## Reading Guide

*   **Read the Subtext:** Wu Cheng is a brand-new character, introduced as Yi Donglai's junior. His smooth handling of the riot is impressive, but his final line about "treatment" is a massive red flag. Is he a genuine doctor, or is there something else going on? We're reading a horror novel, so trust that instinct of suspicion.
*   **Character Consistency:** Note how Li Huowang handles the depressed patient, Zhao Ting. This is a far cry from the paranoid, violent man who tore his own face off. He's learned compassion, or at least, he's learned what *looks* like compassion. Is this genuine recovery, or is it just a better mask?
*   **The Quiet Before the Storm:** This is a chapter of building tension in a low-key setting. The hospital acts as a cage, but one with soft walls and fluorescent lights. The core question is: is this place safe, or is it just another trap with a kinder face? The final line suggests the latter. Watch for what "treatment" means.

## The Art of the Slow Burn

This chapter is a masterclass in the "quiet horror" that makes **Dao Guai Yi Xian** so unique. The Dao-Twisted World is all blood, pus, and screaming gods, but the modern reality is often where the psychological knife digs in deepest. Here, the horror is social isolation, the weight of a reputation, the guilt of a stranger, and the ominous implication of a doctor's simple sentence. Don't let the lack of monster attacks fool you—this is the part of the story where the *real* wounds are being prepped.
