## Chapter Overview

Welcome to Yinling City, folks! After a long, brutal slog through a world that's been chewed up and spat out by cosmic horror, our emotionally constipated protagonist strolls into a place that looks… normal. Like, clean-streets, multi-story-buildings, normal-normal. And it’s *weirding him out*. This chapter is a breather, a palate cleanser of sheer civilization after so much visceral gunk. But don’t let the cobblestones fool you—this is still the Dao-Twisted World, and Li Huowang’s mind is working overtime. We get a slick display of his new coin-sword, a painfully awkward master-disciple negotiation, and Bai Lingmiao actually being chatty for once. It’s the quiet before the storm, and that storm is going to hit *hard*.

## Key Plot Points

- **Coin Sword Mastery:** Li Huowang demonstrates his proficiency with the new bronze coin sword, learning to use it effectively against both stationary targets and to deliver a remotely-focused strike through a stone obstacle. He recognizes its practical value as a long-range, sidebar-free weapon.
- **Xiucai Negotiation:** The Xiucai’s fawning admiration is met with cold pragmatism. Li Huowang’s primary goal (testing the technique for traps) accomplished, he stalls the eager disciple by forcing him to learn basic literacy, framing it as a prerequisite for learning more techniques.
- **Arrival at Yinling City:** The group reaches the major city of Yinling, their last major stop before Niu Xin Mountain, Bai Lingmiao’s hometown. The sight of its towering walls and prosperous streets creates a moment of awe and dissonance for the group, a jarring reminder that civilization still exists in this broken world.
- **Bai Lingmiao’s Chatter:** For the first time in a while, Bai Lingmiao is openly excited and talkative, sharing secondhand stories about the city's wealth and food. This marks a rare moment of normalcy and cheerful anticipation for her.
- **Strict Inspection & Unease:** The city's military checkpoint is rigorous, causing tension for the group who carry the Black Tai Sui. Their successful entry into this orderly, prosperous space leaves Li Huowang feeling a deep, unsettling disconnect.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Bronze Coin Sword (铜钱剑):** This isn't just a cool weapon Li Huowang found; it's a real thing in Chinese ritual practice! A *copper coin sword* is a Daoist exorcism tool. Coins from a specific historical period (often from the more prosperous eras, as they carry stronger *yang* energy) are strung together on a red cord to form a sword shape. The red cord itself is a protective element, channeling yang and warding off evil. You'll see these in Taoist temples during *jiao* offerings, used by priests to drive out demons. Li Huowang weaponizing it with the Qingfeng Temple technique is a brilliant blend of folk ritual and combat practicality—the novel is saying your grandpa's talisman is a deadly piece of artillery if you know the right chant.

**The Barbican (瓮城):** That double-gated entryway the group passed through is a *wengcheng*, or barbican. It's a brilliant piece of ancient Chinese military architecture. The name literally means "jar city," because once enemies breached the outer gate, they'd be trapped inside this small, walled courtyard, where defenders could rain arrows and hot oil down on them from all sides. It was an urban kill-box. The novel's casual detail of the echoing sounds is a nice touch, giving it a massive, imposing feel.

## Reading Guide

Alright, gear up, because this calm is a setup. We're at the doorstep of a major story beat. The fussy guards, the massive walls, Li Huowang’s cold reaction to his own “successful” disciple—everything is teed up. The vibe is Zen with a knife behind your back. This is a classic "the party enters the big city" sequence, and in a story this grim, that almost always means we're about to meet a new faction, learn a horrifying new rule of the world, or walk straight into a trap the size of a city block. Bet your bottom coin that the peace of Yinling is a lot more fragile than a well-swept street suggests.
