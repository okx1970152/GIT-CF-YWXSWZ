## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow travelers of the twisted path! Chapter 408, "The Arrival," pulls us into a strange lull between storms. Li Huowang finds himself a bored, reluctant spectator at a high-stakes political conspiracy. The Great Qi loyalists are scheming under Zhuge Yuan's guidance, but our boy couldn't care less about their rebellion—he's got bigger fish to fry. The real tension here is quiet, personal, and stomach-churning: Li Sui is growing up, and that ticking clock is louder than any war council. Then a bombshell announcement shatters the evening: the little emperor, who Li Huowang was *pretty* sure was a rotting corpse, has just walked in. Get ready for a chapter that feels like the calm before a seismic shift.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang follows Zhuge Yuan into a gathering of Great Qi loyalists, where they debate military strategy and political restoration.
- Feeling completely detached from the proceedings, Li Huowang zones out, comparing the schemers to actors playing house.
- During a break, Li Sui exhibits increasingly mature behavior—she asks questions about what she doesn't understand and worries about her dad being hungry, signaling her rapid development.
- Li Huowang's mood sours as he realizes that if Li Sui matures before he can force a Zuowandao to give him the cure for his hallucinations, he will be trapped in delusion again.
- He privately admits, with grim irony, that he has *never* wished for a Zuowandao to show up so badly.
- At the banquet, Li Huowang ignores protocol, eats alone, and asks Zhuge Yuan bluntly: when do they strike and who dies?
- Zhuge Yuan calmly explains the need to wait, gather more military support from fencesitters, and promises to help Li Huowang find a true cultivation method once the restoration is complete.
- The chapter ends with a eunuch's cry: "His Majesty arrives!"—a shocking detail given Li Huowang's earlier knowledge that the Great Qi emperor should be a walking corpse.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Walking Corpse Emperor:** This is a *huge* detail that casual readers might miss. In Chinese folklore and xianxia, a "walking corpse" (行尸) isn't just a zombie. It refers to a body that continues to move and act *as if alive* even though the soul or spirit has left it—often a puppet controlled by a darker force. If the Great Qi emperor was *supposed* to be one, and now he's making a public appearance, it means either the earlier information was incomplete, or something *else* is wearing his skin. Both possibilities are equally terrifying.

**Political Restoration and "Fencesitters" (墙头草):** The term describes people who sway with the wind, supporting whoever seems strongest. In the context of Chinese court politics, a successful rebellion often hinges on winning over these neutral military officials, since they control the actual armed forces. Zhuge Yuan's strategy of patience and coalition-building is textbook classical Chinese political wisdom—think *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* diplomacy, not hack-and-slash fantasy.

**Li Sui's Maturation as a Countdown:** The Black Tai Sui growing more sentient is a *horrifying* realization for Li Huowang. In Daoist and folkloric traditions, parasitic entities that "mature" inside a host often undergo a transformation—either fully integrating into the host's body (permanent fusion) or becoming an independent entity that *tears its way out*. Li Huowang's desperation to find a cure before she "grows up" is a classic body-horror countdown.

## Reading Guide
This is a *slow-burn setup chapter*, and if you're here for non-stop action, you might find your attention wandering as much as Li Huowang's does. But don't skip it! This is the kind of chapter that plants landmines for later explosions. Pay close attention to:
- **Li Huowang's emotional state**: He's restless, detached, and increasingly desperate. His internal clock is ticking.
- **Li Sui's development**: Every line she speaks is a clue to how fast she's evolving.
- **The promise of help**: Zhuge Yuan's offer to find a "true cultivation method" is the first sign of a potential way out—but in this world, any promise comes wrapped in thorns.
- **The cliffhanger**: That emperor's arrival? It's not just a surprise party. It's a sign that *nothing* in this world is what it seems.
