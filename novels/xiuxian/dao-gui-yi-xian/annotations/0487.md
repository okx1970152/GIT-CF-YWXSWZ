## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because Chapter 487 serves up a masterclass in *transaction horror*. Li Huowang navigates an imperial audience with a Grand Preceptor who doesn’t trust him, snags the cosmic-level “pure yang life” voucher, and then walks into the Office’s creepy treasure vault to trade 400 human-life pills for… a human finger bone. But the real zinger? He feeds that bone to a blind Bai Lingmiao, who wakes up to a new sense she never signed up for. This chapter is all about trades, trust, and the uneasy feeling that every gift in the Dao-Twisted World comes with invisible strings attached. The prose leans into quiet dread — no screaming monsters, just the slow drip of Li Huowang’s paranoia and Bai Lingmiao’s fragile hope.

## Key Plot Points

- The Grand Preceptor of Great Liang pretends to buy Li Huowang’s story, but secretly plants a speech-restriction curse in his tongue root. Li Huowang doesn’t fight it — he never planned to talk anyway.
- Li Huowang retrieves the jade token for pure yang life from Lian Zhibei, then physically seizes it to force her to bring her mother first — he won’t let her run off without delivering.
- He enters the Office’s inner repository, trades 400 Yang Life Pills for the “relic” — which turns out to be a crystalline human little finger bone stored in a seven-tiered agate box.
- The coin-eyed eunuch repeats a bizarre warning: “You must have a kind heart.” Li Huowang files this away as ominous but proceeds anyway.
- Back home, Bai Lingmiao is blind, groping in the dark. Li Huowang tricks her into swallowing the bone with “rootless water,” causing violent coughing.
- After the ordeal, Bai Lingmiao gains a new perception — she can “sense” the qi of objects and people, but Li Huowang registers as a flickering, trembling mass with unstable afterimages. Her final line hints that she sees something deeply wrong with him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“Rootless Water” (无根水)** — A common Daoist and TCM term for naturally fallen rainwater that has never touched the ground. Often used in alchemical recipes and ritual medicine; it’s considered “pure” because it hasn’t been contaminated by earthly yin. The fact that Li Huowang uses it here as a chaser for human bone is a grim bit of alchemical authenticity.

**Buddhist Relics (舍利, śarīra)** — In real-world Buddhism, relics are cremated remains of enlightened masters — bone fragments, crystals, or pearl-like granules — believed to be proof of spiritual cultivation. They are venerated, not eaten. The novel perverts this by turning the relic into a consumable medicine, reinforcing its theme of sacred things being weaponized into body horror.

**Mind-Eye Perception (心眼通)** — A Daoist/Buddhist concept of “inner sight” distinct from physical vision; it allows a cultivator to see the true nature of things, including qi flows and concealed presences. In this chapter, Bai Lingmiao attains a corrupted version — she can now detect Li Huowang’s unstable, flickering state, which she might not have wanted to see.

**The implied taboo around bone-eating** — In Chinese folk and religious practice, consuming the remains of a corpse is generally taboo (pollution). The fact that Li Huowang feeds this to his wife without explaining what it is marks a cold, pragmatic boundary in their relationship. He chooses her survival over her consent — a morally gray choice that fits his arc.

### Sarira (relic, 舍利)

In Buddhism, cremated remains of an enlightened master, often pearl-like or crystalline; venerated as sacred objects. In the Dao-Twisted World, they are consumed as medical ingredients.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a slow-burn horror of *consent and consequence*. Bai Lingmiao regains a sense she never expected and immediately sees something terrifying about Li Huowang — his qi is unstable, fragmented, flickering between states. This is more than a new power-up; it’s a dramatic irony bomb that will likely explode in their relationship later. Pay attention to the pattern: every transaction Li Huowang makes here — with the Grand Preceptor, with Lian Zhibei, with the Office, with his wife — involves a hidden cost. The bone was “free” in exchange for pills, but the real price might be Bai Lingmiao’s trust and sanity. Also, that Grand Preceptor curse is a ticking clock. Li Huowang has no intention of talking now, but if he ever *needs* to speak the truth? That’s when it’ll bite.
