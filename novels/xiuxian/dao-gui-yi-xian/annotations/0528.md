## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in tension and misdirection. While the previous volume was a full-tilt sprint through hell, this one slams on the brakes, forcing Li Huowang to sit in the crushing weight of his own indecision. The entire first half is a frenetic, one-sided argument with the ghost of Zhuge Yuan—a back-and-forth that feels like Li Huowang is interrogating his own reflection in a cracked mirror. Every line is a diagnostic of his paranoia: Is Bai Lingmiao safe? Is the Dice's letter a warning or a trap? Are they all already dead and just don't know it? The second half is a masterful pivot. A new imperial summons and the grotesque, practical logistics of burying two Heart-Element corpses ground the philosophical dread in immediate, physical action. Get ready for a chapter that weaponizes doubt, where the greatest horror isn't a monster but a choice that can't be unmade.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Argument with Ghosts**: Li Huowang holds a heated, one-sided debate with an imagined Zhuge Yuan. He argues that the Dice's letter was a threat, not a request, and that any possibility of a trap, no matter how remote, is a certainty when Zuowandao is involved.
- **The Trap's Rationale**: Li Huowang articulates his core dilemma: he can't gamble on Bai Lingmiao's safety, and even if the letter were real, Zuowandao could have already imprisoned her mind without her knowledge. Inaction = death.
- **The Zhuge Yuan Gambit**: A key breakthrough is implied. Li Huowang suddenly brightens when speaking to the empty window. Zhuge Yuan has apparently offered a plan to test the situation without committing to a full assault, giving Li Huowang his "chance."
- **The Imperial Edict**: An old eunuch delivers a scroll from Ji Lin, ordering Li Huowang to *not* enter the palace; the Emperor will come to him instead. The scroll self-destructs after reading.
- **The Corpse Inventory**: Before leaving, Li Huowang retrieves two precious Heart-Element corpses from his cellar: one skinless, the other headless. He stuffs them into pickling jars, a brutally pragmatic act of asset management.
- **The New Mission**: Li Huowang meets Ji Lin at a gambling house. The Emperor is badly wounded—half an ear and a deep facial scar from an assassination attempt. He tasks Li Huowang with a new job, presented as a rolled painting.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Heart-Element (心素) Corpses**: In standard xianxia, a cultivator's corpse is just a decaying husk. In the Dao-Twisted World, a Heart-Element's body—even a skinless one or a headless one—is a "precious heavenly material" (天材地宝). It retains a terrifying potential for alchemy, ritual, or even reanimation. Li Huowang's hoarding of his own dead flesh isn't macabre hoarding; it's hard-nosed resource management, because in this world, the dead can be more useful (and more dangerous) than the living.
**Speak of the Devil (说曹操曹操到)**: Li Huowang uses this classic Chinese idiom—literally "speak of Cao Cao, and Cao Cao arrives"—in reference to an opportunity presented by Zhuge Yuan's insight. It's a cultural shorthand for perfect, almost supernatural timing, a line that feels particularly sharp in a world where coincidences are never just coincidences.
**The Self-Destructing Edict (圣旨自燃)**: Imperial edicts in Chinese web novels often possess a ritualistic, almost supernatural quality. This one's spontaneous combustion upon being read is a sign of Ji Lin's involvement in high-stakes, secretive court politics. It's a visual cue that the transmission of information is as dangerous as the content itself.

## Reading Guide

Buckle up, because this chapter is a masterclass in "the problem of doubt." Li Huowang isn't fighting a monster; he's fighting a logical paradox. The Dice's trap is perfect because it's *ambiguous*. Any action might be the wrong one, but inaction is also a choice. This is the kind of psychological warfare where Zuowandao thrives. Watch how Li Huowang's frantic, circular logic finally snaps into place when he talks to Zhuge Yuan. It's a quiet, pivotal moment—a sign that even in his deepest doubt, his survival instinct can still find a new tool. Oh, and keep an eye on those pickling jars. If you've read this far, you know a "Heart-Element corpse in storage" is not a detail that stays in the background. The pile of dead flesh is growing, and in the Dao-Twisted World, that pile has a nasty habit of starting to move on its own.
