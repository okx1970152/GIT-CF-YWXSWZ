## Chapter Overview

Alright, buckle up, fellow travelers of the twisted path. This chapter is a masterclass in psychological friction. After the explosive revelations and visceral horrors of previous arcs, we get a breather—but not a peaceful one. The air is thick with tension as Li Huowang finally sights his quarry: the big carriage barreling toward the eastern gate of the Imperial Capital. But before he can sprint off on his solo mission, he has to deal with the human anchors trying to slow him down. Lü Zhuangyuan’s nagging about opera houses, Gouwa’s prattling, the phantom interruptions from his own mind—all of it is the mundane static of a world that refuses to let him focus on his bloody objective. The chapter then pivots to Niuxin Village, showing the quiet desperation of those left behind, and culminates in a chillingly professional confrontation with the Supervisory Heavenly Office. Li Huowang’s honesty is a calculated weapon, and the stranger’s response reveals a terrifying truth: the system already knows everything.

## Key Plot Points

Li Huowang, at the gates of the Imperial Capital, is waylaid by the Lü Troupe and Gouwa. He gets so annoyed he physically shuts Gouwa up and issues a brutal ultimatum: go back to Niuxin Village immediately and stop looking for him, or else. The threat hangs heavy; the group is terrified by this new, hard-edged version of the Little Daoist. He then takes off, leaving them in stunned silence. Back in Niuxin Village, Chun Xiaoman is frantic with worry. She’s calmed by Zhao Wu, but the real movement comes from Gao Zhijian, who decides, with his simple, crushing logic, that *he* will go find Li Huowang and Bai Lingmiao. His declaration—"I am the emperor! I can find them!"—is a poignant echo of his backstory, a broken title wielded as a tool of hope. The main plot kicks into high gear as Li Huowang, chasing the carriage, is intercepted by the Supervisory Heavenly Office. The lead agent, Er Jiu, coldly recites Li Huowang’s entire dossier: his ascension records, his sect affiliations, even the theft of the Taixu Sword. He knows everything. Li Huowang tries to sell his truth about the Zuowandao, but the agent dismisses his claims about Shangji Guankou as “preposterous.” Finally, Zhuge Yuan’s spectral voice cuts in, reframing the agent’s ignorance not as a challenge to Li Huowang’s sanity, but as the exact reason why the world is rotting.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司)** makes a powerful, bureaucratic return here. These aren’t just cultivators; they are an intelligence agency. The agent, Er Jiu, pulls up a complete file on Li Huowang as if reading from a police database. This introduces a terrifying new layer to the Dao-Twisted World: it is *administered*. The chaos isn’t total; there is a shadow government that knows, files, and judges. This chapter also reinforces the concept of **“Shangji Guankou”** as a kind of living, receding myth. Zhuge Yuan’s defense—that ignorance of a thing doesn’t make it unreal—is a philosophical counterpoint to the Office’s rigid, empirical worldview. It’s a battle of truth vs. lived reality.

### Deng Jie (登阶)

The process of ascension through suffering, tied to the Ao-Jing Sect’s cultivation. Li Huowang is noted as a “Three-Time Ascension” elder.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a gear-shift. After the raw, personal terror of the Danyangzi arc, we’re entering a phase where the world-system itself becomes a hostile force. Pay close attention to the Office’s dossier on Li Huowang—it’s the first time he’s been seen not as a freak or a target, but as a *known variable* on a bureaucratic ledger. The real horror here isn’t a monster; it’s the institutional weight that makes his personal, painful truth feel like a footnote in someone else’s file. Also, savor Gao Zhijian’s quiet resolve. His character arc is one of the most unexpectedly tender threads in this brutal story. And watch out for the Dice. Li Huowang has finally run out of patience for games. The hunt is on.
