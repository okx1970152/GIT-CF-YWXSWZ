## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up. This chapter is a masterclass in high-octane, body-horror-infused combat. We finally get a direct confrontation with the elusive Heart-Turbid, an enemy whose ability isn't just about hiding things—it's about erasing them from existence, including memories. But our good friend Li Huowang, never one for subtlety, finds a gruesomely ingenious workaround. This isn't a battle of flashy sword arts; it's a gritty, desperate chess match where the pieces are made of flesh and bone, and the penalty for losing is being forgotten. Get ready for some of the most visceral problem-solving we've seen yet!

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with a bizarre puzzle: a rusty knife appears in Li Huowang's hand from nowhere, causing immediate, high-alert confusion. Tuoba Danqing’s theory that the Heart-Turbid’s karma is about “hiding, not giving” is quickly proven wrong as the ritual to force the creature out begins. Shentu Gang and the Chief Recorder’s combined ritual is visually spectacular, causing the sky to darken and perceptions of quantity—number of swords, number of objects—to become completely unreliable. The Heart-Turbid manifests as a dirty, bedraggled woman. The fight is swift and brutal. Li Huowang’s initial attacks (fingernails, flung skin) are negated by her power, and a disguised Hong Da is used as a human shield. In a stunning moment of tactical desperation, Li Huowang breaks his own ribs and uses them as weapons. By stabbing the ribs into himself, he establishes a direct pain-link with the Heart-Turbid, allowing him to torture her through his own self-mutilation (using barbed spikes in his nail beds). The link breaks her will, and she flees, but Li Huowang fails to catch her before she vanishes. In the aftermath, the exhausted ritualists recover, and Li Huowang experiences a terrifying moment of amnesia before remembering his purpose. The chapter ends on a chilling cliffhanger—Tuoba Danqing finds writing on the wall, suggesting the Heart-Turbid’s past or a deeper trap.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The term **Heart-Turbid (心浊, Xīn Zhuó)** is a direct parallel to the **Heart-Element (心素, Xīn Sù)** , presenting a fascinating duality. While the Heart-Element’s ability is to make the false real through pure belief (a kind of ontological creation), the Heart-Turbid’s ability is to render things nonexistent (a form of ontological erasure). This is a classic Daoist pairing of creation and destruction, being and non-being. The battle itself is a brilliant tactical feat. Li Huowang uses a twisted form of **sympathetic magic (触染巫术, Chù Rǎn Wūshù)** —the principle that like affects like. By breaking his own ribs and linking his pain to the enemy, he bypasses her direct ability to hide things. He can’t hide *her* pain if it’s happening to *him*. This is a deep, clever application of folk ritual logic, turning his own body into a voodoo doll. The ritual performed by Shentu Gang—the "Four Golden Star Lords" and "Eight Immortals"—draws on the **Earthly Branch system (地支, Dìzhī)** that governs time and direction, which is used in traditional Chinese geomancy (feng shui) and exorcism to fix locations and entities, which is the perfect counter to a being that tries to “un-fix” reality.

### sympathetic magic

The folk principle that 'like affects like'; an action performed on one object (or body) can affect another it is linked to. Li Huowang weaponizes this logic.

### Bang Bing Jue

A traditional northeastern Chinese spirit-summoning chant used by chuma mediums; its rhythmic, urgent verses are designed to compel spirits to manifest.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a rollercoaster, so pay attention to the lulls. The opening confusion with the “free” knife is a great example of the novel’s rule: in the Dao-Twisted World, *anything* that appears out of nowhere is a trap or a weapon. NEVER pick up strange items. The key moment is Li Huowang’s dark epiphany: to fight an enemy that can hide things, he must become the thing that cannot be hidden—his own pain. His self-surgery is not just a shocking visual; it's the most logical and brutal solution to a tactical problem. Also, note the moment of amnesia. The Heart-Turbid didn’t just hide his sword; she almost hid his *purpose*. This highlights a terrifying new dimension to this enemy: she doesn’t just make you lose your keys, she makes you forget why you're even in the room. Finally, savor the ending. Tuoba Danqing finding writing on the wall is a classic horror trope, and in this world, it’s a promise that the mystery is only getting deeper. Buckle up, the real hunt is just beginning.

## Deeper Dive: The Logic of the Link

Li Huowang’s solution to the Heart-Turbid is a fantastic example of the novel’s internal consistency. Her ability isn’t “mind control” or “teleportation”; it’s **conceptual occlusion (概念屏蔽, Gàiniàn Píngbì)** . She “hides” the concept of an object from reality. So, Li Huowang can’t hide her concept by using an outside object; he has to make *himself* the offensive concept. By physically linking their bodies via a shared, inflicted wound (the broken ribs), he forces her to be “present” in his pain. This is brilliant, macabre tactical thinking that feels deeply rooted in folk magic logic rather than generic video game mechanics.
