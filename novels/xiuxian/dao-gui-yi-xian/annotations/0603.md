## Chapter Overview
Li Huowang finally flips the board, but it’s not a flashy power move—it’s a desperate, bloody scramble to seize the initiative. He brutalizes Wu Cheng for intel, swaps identities with the man who was trying to drug him, and calls the one doctor he still trusts—only to hear that trust tested. This chapter is Li Huowang at his most cornered: calm when he needs to be cruel, clear-headed enough to lie to the entire hospital, and terrifyingly determined to take the fight directly to Xu Shou before his family pays the price. The question hanging over every breath: is this a breakout or a breakdown?

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang tortures Wu Cheng with a fountain pen to extract Xu Shou’s location: a white yacht at Beihai New Port. No other safe houses are known.
- He saws off his own restraints, swaps clothes with Wu Cheng, forces him into the iron mask and restraint gown, and stuffs a handful of antipsychotics down the man’s throat to leave him docile and mute.
- He calls Dr. Yi Donglai using Wu Cheng’s finger to unlock the phone, hoping to find an ally. Yi’s response sounds like he suspects Li Huowang has relapsed.
- Li Huowang begs Yi to warn his parents and Yang Na to hide, since they think he’s just a paranoid psychotic. Yi is clearly unconvinced and urges him to stay put.
- He hangs up, blocks Yi’s number, then stages a fake emergency—leaving a bloodied, masked Wu Cheng in the room while he slips out as the “doctor” who ran for help.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
A psychological ward in modern China isn’t just a medical setting—it’s a layered symbol. Mental illness carries heavy stigma, and a “mental patient’s word” is almost automatically discredited in both legal and interpersonal contexts. That is why Li Huowang’s plan is so precarious: every move he makes looks to outsiders like a psychotic break, not a justified response to a real conspiracy. The white coat he steals isn’t just a disguise—it’s the one uniform that society trusts, while the striped gown and iron mask he forces onto Wu Cheng are visual shorthand for “unreliable narrator.” When a doctor asks if you’ve “forgotten to take your medication,” the question itself becomes an accusation: reality is up for debate only if you’re the one holding the pills.

## Reading Guide
Think of this chapter as an emergency lesson in *why you never corner a man who’s already been broken by a world that won’t stop lying*. Li Huowang is not a slick escape artist; he’s a man who has learned to weaponize the things everyone else takes for granted—a fountain pen as an interrogation tool, a doctor’s coat as camouflage, a psychotic’s mask as a jail he can lock someone else inside. The scene with Yi Donglai is especially painful: here is the one professional who helped him, and even he reads Li Huowang’s desperation as a sign of relapse. This is *Dao Gui Yi Xian’s* real horror—not the monsters, but the way the truth sounds exactly like madness when you’re the one shouting it. Keep your eyes on the white yacht. That’s where the next trap is waiting.
