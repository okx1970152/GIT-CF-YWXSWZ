## Chapter Overview
Out of the frying pan and into the main hall! After all the scheming, running, and body horror, Li Huowang finally gets a front-row seat to the Supervisory Heavenly Office’s big game hunt. This chapter is pure setup—but it’s *atmospheric* setup. Snowy streets, early curfews, and a gathering of grim-faced agents all set the stage for a major crackdown on the Zuowandao. But the Office isn’t just powerful; it’s paranoid, efficient, and ruthlessly thorough. Before the Vice Supervisor even gets to his speech, he’s already sniffed out and neutralized five infiltrators. The message is clear: in the capital, the Office plays chess while everyone else plays checkers.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang receives a bird-delivered message that the operation is finally beginning, and he rushes to the Supervisory Heavenly Office headquarters.
- The main hall is kept deliberately dark, creating an oppressive atmosphere where only those who get close can be clearly seen.
- The gathered agents number in the hundreds, drawn from all six circuits of Liang, with additional specialists borrowed from the Qingqiu branch, including a possible familiar face—a lama with black sheep.
- Li Huowang learns the Office’s chain of command from Sima Lan: five Vice Supervisors (Si Tian Shao Jian), with the highest being the Supervisor (Si Tian Jian). The top brass are absent, reportedly called to the palace.
- The Vice Supervisor makes his entrance with a dramatic gong and a soft moonlit glow; his mere presence exerts a crushing pressure.
- Without saying a word about the plan, the Vice Supervisor demonstrates his power by causing five Zuowandao infiltrators in the crowd to collapse, their faces exposed by iron hooks to reveal the mahjong-tile patterns.
- His declaration confirms the Office’s goal: the Zuowandao killed an Office Supervisor, and now they will pay.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Supervisory Heavenly Office’s Chain of Command**  
An old hand in the Office explains the hierarchy: five Vice Supervisors (司天少监, *Si Tian Shao Jian*), and one overall Supervisor (司天监, *Si Tian Jian*). In ancient Chinese bureaucratic terms, “少” (shao) means “deputy” or “vice,” while “监” (jian) means “supervisor” or “inspector.” This mirrors real historical government agencies like the Directorate for Astronomy (司天监), which was responsible for celestial observation and calendar-making. Here, the titles are repurposed for an agency that polices the supernatural.

**The Capital Curfew & Authoritarian Control**  
The early curfew and the gloomy, snow-covered streets create a classic wuxia/xianxia “storm before the battle” atmosphere. Historically, Chinese dynasties imposed strict curfews during times of unrest, enforced by night patrols. Here, the Supervisory Heavenly Office is using its authority to lock down the capital, turning the entire city into a hunting ground.

**Iron Hooks & Face Exposure**  
The agents use small iron hooks to peel back the skin of the collapsed infiltrators, revealing the mahjong-tile patterns on their faces. This is both a practical identification method (the Zuowandao’s disguise is skin-deep) and a visual cue steeped in folk horror: the face as a mask that can be “lifted off.” It echoes the novel’s recurring theme that identity itself is a thin, removable layer.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a classic “amassing the troops” scene, but with a *Dao Gui Yi Xian* twist. The Office isn’t just gathering its forces—it’s demonstrating its institutional terror. The ease with which the Vice Supervisor identifies and neutralizes the infiltrators is a power move designed to cow everyone present, including Li Huowang. Notice how the chapter emphasizes *atmosphere*: the darkness, the murmuring, the suffocating pressure. This isn’t about fighting; it’s about establishing that the Office operates on a scale and with a ruthlessness that makes even a Heart-Element like Li Huowang feel small. The fact that the top brass are all at the palace hints at an even larger political game beneath the surface—one that the Zuowandao may be just a piece of.
