## Chapter Overview

Li Huowang enters a tense negotiation with the persistent fat woman, Lian Zhibei, who offers him a deal he literally cannot walk away from. What starts as a blunt refusal to sell his Immaculate Lifespan turns into a life-altering bargain when she reveals she can remove the Celestial Immortal from Bai Lingmiao. And just as he’s about to say no, the Second Spirit drops a bombshell that makes the choice for him: the Celestial Immortal has been threatening Bai Lingmiao and forcing her to spirit-dance all along. Cue a furious Li Huowang striking the deal on the spot, a paranoid road trip back to the capital, and a final power play at the Supervisory Heavenly Office where he learns that bureaucracy, not monsters, is the real gatekeeper of his reward.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang meets Lian Zhibei in a tavern where she has prepared a feast; he immediately establishes dominance by unmasking himself and Li Sui, clearing the floor of other diners.
- Lian Zhibei pleads her case with genuine sorrow (confirmed by the Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings) about her mother’s death and her need for Immaculate Lifespan.
- Li Huowang initially refuses and prepares to leave.
- Lian Zhibei reveals she can remove the Celestial Immortal from Bai Lingmiao, which catches his attention.
- The Second Spirit arrives and whispers that the Immortal has been threatening Bai Lingmiao and forcing her to perform—a secret they’ve been hiding from him.
- Infuriated, Li Huowang agrees to the deal on the spot: five years of Immaculate Lifespan from the reward in exchange for removing the Immortal.
- Lian Zhibei follows the group all the way back to the capital, paranoid that Li Huowang will try to escape.
- At the Supervisory Heavenly Office, Nangong tries to take the Spirit-Blight box for processing through official channels, but Lian Zhibei argues that the palace eunuch will skim the Lifespan.
- Li Huowang takes the box back and decides to deliver it directly to the palace.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The “Celestial Immortal” (仙家) attached to Bai Lingmiao is a classic fixture of northeastern Chinese folk mediumship (chuma / tiao dashen). In folk belief, a Celestial Immortal is often an animal spirit (fox, weasel, snake) that chooses a human host and grants them supernatural abilities in exchange for servitude—usually lifelong and non-negotiable. Forcing a spirit-dance is essentially the Immortal asserting control over the host’s body, demanding ritual performance to strengthen its connection. This is far from a benevolent contract; it’s a parasitic relationship wrapped in the language of “divine selection.” The fact that Li Huowang learns the Immortal has been *threatening* Bai Lingmiao reframes spirit-dancing from a quirky folk practice into a hostage situation where the hostage can’t even speak about her captor.

The “Ten Emotions and Eight Sufferings” (十情八苦) is a twisted expansion of the Buddhist “Eight Sufferings” (八苦), which traditionally cover birth, aging, sickness, death, parting from loved ones, meeting hated ones, unfulfilled desires, and the unrelenting nature of the Five Aggregates. The novel’s version adds ten granular emotional states, turning it into a lie-detection and status-inspection system—Li Huowang can literally see whether someone’s sadness is real. This transforms a philosophical concept from “things every mortal must endure” into a practical tool for survival in a world where everyone is lying.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *offering a deal the protagonist would be an idiot to refuse.* Lian Zhibei plays it perfectly: she shows up openly (removing suspicion of ambush), picks a public venue with the magistrate’s office across the street (establishing safety), and appeals first to pity before pivoting to the one thing Li Huowang can’t ignore—Miaomiao’s well-being. The true horror here isn’t any monster but the realization that the person you trusted has been hiding a deadly secret from you for your own good. Li Huowang’s fury isn’t at the Immortal; it’s at being kept in the dark. The chapter ends on a brilliantly pragmatic note: he’s learned that in this world, even the reward you’ve rightfully earned can get eroded by bureaucratic middlemen before it ever reaches your hands. *That* is a horror that needs no ghosts.

## Deeper Dive: The Supervisory Heavenly Office & The Palace Eunuch

This chapter gives us a rare glimpse into the operational reality of the Supervisory Heavenly Office. It's not just a monster-hunting agency; it's a government bureaucracy tangled with the palace’s eunuch power structure. Nangong's suggestion to process the Spirit-Blight through normal channels is met with immediate suspicion from Lian Zhibei, who knows firsthand that “handling fees” and “skimming” are just how things work here. The fact that Li Huowang ultimately chooses direct delivery to the palace rather than trusting the Office reveals a deepening cynicism: even the org that’s supposed to police the supernatural can’t be trusted not to pilfer your Lifespan. This is a world where not even metaphysical currency is safe from corruption.
