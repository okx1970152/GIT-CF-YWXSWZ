## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a gut-punch of a chapter, my fellow Daoists. After the festive warmth of the Shangsi Festival, Li Huowang makes a brutal, self-inflicted sacrifice to pay for his mistakes. This chapter is a masterclass in tonal whiplash: we go from a bustling street fair and a tipsy, happy Bai Lingmiao to a raw, tearful breakdown, and then straight into a morning of cold, pragmatic self-mutilation. It’s a quiet, devastating reminder that in the Dao-Twisted World, every moment of peace is borrowed, and the bill is always paid in flesh.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huowang and Bai Lingmiao attend the Shangsi Festival. He casually demonstrates his street-smart knowledge by exposing a jade cat scam, noting the Zuowandao use the same layered “everyone you meet tells you the same lie” technique.
- Bai Lingmiao gets drunk and experiences a sudden, intense emotional collapse, crying and beating Li Huowang’s back as all her suppressed grief over her ruined eye and her corrupted identity comes flooding out.
- The next morning, without a word of farewell, Li Huowang gouges out his own left eye. He places the bloody organ in the Second Spirit’s hand as a message, telling her to inform Bai Lingmiao he’s gone to find a cure for her eye.
- Li Huowang travels to the Supervisory Heavenly Office’s inner vault to search for a healing artifact. He finds his old copper coin mask there, a trophy from his previous decapitation while impersonating Hong Zhong.
- The guardian of the inner vault is revealed to be a eunuch with copper coins sewn into his empty eye sockets, setting an ominous tone for the treasures held within.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Shangsi Festival (上巳节):** While often romanticized in media, this was a real ancient Chinese festival for cleansing and warding off evil. It’s a bit of cruel irony that Li Huowang takes Bai Lingmiao to a “purification” festival right when her soul and body are most contaminated by the White Lotus Wusheng Laomu. He’s literally treating the symptom (a fun day out) while the disease (her avatar-hood) festers.

**The “Everyone Says the Same Lie” Con:** The Zuowandao’s strategy is a literalization of a classic psychological phenomenon called *gaslighting*. By seeding a single mark on a target, every subsequent contact “confirms” the false narrative, creating a social reality that overrides the individual’s perception. Li Huowang’s bitter realization—that the lie eventually convinced *him* to become Hong Zhong—is a terrifying commentary on how the Dao-Twisted World erodes selfhood.

**Eye-Gouging as Communication:** Removing one’s own eye is a visceral act of extreme penance and a brutal form of message-passing in the Dao-Twisted World. It’s not just self-harm; it’s a tactical, symbolic gesture. Li Huowang *gives* his eye to the Second Spirit, using the organ as a physical token to convey a promise he can’t bear to say out loud. It’s as sincere as a suicide note, but written in blood and cartilage.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a quiet storm. Keep an eye on Li Huowang’s *process*. He doesn’t hesitate. He doesn’t monologue. He just hooks his fingers in and yanks. This is a man who has fully integrated the Dao-Twisted World’s logic into his daily life: *all debts are physical.* His casual attitude toward self-surgery is the final proof that he’s no longer a normal person struggling to survive—he’s become a native of this world of horror.

Also, pay close attention to Bai Lingmiao’s breakdown. The Shangsi Festival wasn’t a cure; it was a bandage. The moment the festivity stops, the grief over her lost eye and her role as Wusheng Laomu’s vessel comes roaring back. Li Huowang’s response? To find a *physical* fix (a new eye) instead of confronting the *spiritual* rot. That’s his blind spot—and it’s going to cost him everything.

## The Metaphor of the Fake Jade Cat
Here’s a juicy layer for the deep-thinkers among us. The jade cat scam Li Huowang exposes is a **perfect microcosm of his entire existence.** The cat is a fake constructed from three separate pieces; Li Huowang’s identity—Heart-Element, Hong Zhong, Danyangzi—is also a fake constructed from separate, conflicting pieces. The scam works because *everyone* tells the victim the same lie. In the Dao-Twisted World, *everyone* tells Li Huowang he is Danyangzi. *Everyone* tells him the White Lotus is true. The pressure is so immense that even he eventually believed it. He didn’t just expose the scam; he *identified* with it on a meta level. The world itself is the biggest con of all, and he’s the mark who can never leave the table.
