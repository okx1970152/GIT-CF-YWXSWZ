## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists—Chapter 38 is a masterclass in *mundane horror* and *human wreckage.* This chapter doesn't throw a single monster at you, but the face-to-face with a broken family, a woman sold like livestock, and a fleeting vision of Yang Na will leave you feeling like you've been socked in the gut. Li Huowang finally gets a moment to breathe, and it smells like ash. He successfully delivers Zhao Wu home, only to be met with his father's cold indifference—a man who treats his returning son like an inconvenience. And then there's the hirsute woman, whose quiet confession cuts deeper than any blade: *I was sold by my own parents for two silver taels.* This chapter cements one of the novel's cruelest truths: in the Dao-Twisted World, betrayals of the heart are just as monstrous as any tentacled god. It's a quiet, ugly, achingly human chapter, and it will stick with you.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang escorts Zhao Wu back to his family's grain shop in Jianye Town. Zhao Wu's father is cold and dismissive, treating his son's return from captivity as a nuisance.
- Li Huowang intimidates the father by splitting the counter with a sword and then pays for rice with a deliberately theatrical shaving of silver, establishing his new, pragmatic ruthlessness.
- Inside the courtyard, Li Huowang hands Zhao Wu the messages from the deceased prisoners, tasking him with sending them through a reliable escort agency.
- The hirsute woman, a "human ingredient" rescued from Danyangzi, whispers to Zhao Wu that he might not have been kidnapped but sold by his own father.
- When Li Huowang questions her cynicism, she breaks down and reveals she was sold by her own parents for two taels of silver. Bai Lingmiao comforts her, but the damage is done.
- The group checks into the Eastern Welcome Inn. Li Huowang finally has a quiet moment to observe the bustling street life, but the peace is shattered by a hallucination of Yang Na.
- He panics, thinking he's seeing his childhood sweetheart, but it's just a stranger with a similar face. The brief vision leaves him shaken, questioning his sanity.
- Bai Lingmiao visits his room with a lotus-leaf-wrapped bundle of steaming meat buns, a simple but touching gesture of care.
- The bombshell ending: Li Huowang asks where she got the money. Bai Lingmiao reveals she pawned her gold anklet without telling him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Pawning the Gold Anklet:** In traditional Chinese culture, a gold anklet (金脚环, *jīn jiǎo huán*) was often given as a betrothal gift or a family heirloom. Pawning it signals a deep, often painful, personal sacrifice. Bai Lingmiao's act here is a powerful, silent declaration of loyalty and a demonstration of her growing agency. She is no longer a passive victim; she's making her own choices to support the group.

**The Nine Classes (九流) Background:** This is a historical social hierarchy system, not just a novel invention. Actors, prostitutes, and entertainers occupied the absolute bottom. This system is still lurking in the background, shaping why Zhao Wu's father treats him poorly and why the hirsute woman was considered so disposable. In the Dao-Twisted World, your social class isn't just a label—it's a death sentence or a shackle.

**Family Betrayal as Folk Horror:** The hirsute woman's confession ("a tiger doesn't eat its cubs, but a man is crueler") draws on a classic Chinese proverb (虎毒不食子, *hǔ dú bù shí zǐ*). In the twisted reality of this world, the proverb is weaponized to show that human cruelty has surpassed even the animal kingdom. The horror here isn't spiritual; it's economic and social. You can be sold by the people who should love you the most, and it's just business.

### acting like a wasp

A Chinese idiom describing someone who acts aggressively or recklessly, like a wasp swarming and stinging. Li Huowang uses it to describe his own behavior of intimidating others with silver and a blade.

### roughrider

A literal translation of a low-level thug or mercenary who travels by horse. In this context, it refers to the armed escorts in the shop who are quickly dismissed by Bai Lingmiao's straightforward attitude.

## Reading Guide

This is a "breather chapter" in the worst sense of the word. It gives you a moment to breathe, but the air is thick with new, unspoken dread. Pay close attention to the hirsute woman. For most of the journey, she's been a silent, anonymous presence. This chapter retroactively fills in her silence with a terrible history. Her story about being sold is not just background flavor—it's a direct, ugly mirror to what Li Huowang will inevitably face if he keeps dragging people through this nightmare world.

The hallucination of Yang Na is also a critical character beat for Li Huowang. His reaction is pure panic. He hasn't become numb to the visions; they still shatter his composure completely. This tells us that his "modern world" anchor is still his most vulnerable point. Finally, watch Bai Lingmiao's arc. Her kindness isn't passive; she *acts*. Pawning the gold anklet is a quiet act of defiance and partnership, signaling that she is becoming a true ally, not just a damsel.
