## Chapter Overview

After a tense period of hiding and recovery, our battered crew finally catches a fragile breather. Lü Xiucai has achieved a breakthrough with his coin-manipulation technique, buzzing with manic, vein-popping excitement. Li Huowang, meanwhile, continues his grim, methodical hunt for information—only to stumble upon a very different kind of performance. The Lü family troupe, ever the survivors, have found a new, highly profitable angle: *spring opera*. And in the midst of this bizarre slice-of-life breather, a stray whisper about a plague in a neighboring county lands with ominous weight, foreshadowing the next horror on the horizon.

## Key Plot Points

- **Lü Xiucai’s success**: After his recent breakdown, Lü Xiucai bursts into Li Huowang’s room, having successfully made a single bronze coin tremble through incantation. His excitement is raw, almost feverish, but Li Huowang remains wary—he suspects Han Fu’s Nascent Soul may be setting a delayed trap.
- **Bai Lingmiao’s quiet worry**: While Li Huowang insists everything is fine—thanks to the Black Tai Sui suppressing his hallucinations—Bai Lingmiao sees right through him. Her silent embrace and whispered concern reveal her deep unease about his state of mind.
- **Li Huowang’s dead-end search**: Having scoured the entire county without finding any trace of what he’s looking for, Li Huowang decides today is his last day here. His patience is grinding thin.
- **The spring opera revelation**: Curiosity draws Li Huowang to a massive crowd gathered around the Lü family troupe’s stage. He discovers they’ve resorted to performing *spring opera*—a lewd, risqué folk performance aimed exclusively at men. Lü Zhuangyuan’s survival instincts are as sharp as ever.
- **A plague in Pi County**: As Li Huowang turns to leave, he overhears two men mention that Pi County has been hit by a plague. This throwaway line lands with sinister purpose, hinting at the next disaster awaiting the group.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Spring Opera (粉戏, fěn xì)** – This isn’t a formal xiqu genre but a pejorative label for any opera performance that leans heavily into erotic or bawdy content. In traditional Chinese society, such shows were considered low-class, often performed by struggling troupes trying to draw a male-only audience. The term “spring” (粉) literally means “powder” or “rouge,” carrying connotations of female beauty and sexual innuendo. For the Lü family troupe, resorting to this is a desperate but clever survival tactic—when you can’t compete on skill, you adapt to the market.

**Bronze Coin Sword (铜钱剑, tóng qián jiàn)** – A ritual weapon in Daoist exorcism, made by stringing old bronze coins together with red thread. Each coin carries the seal of a specific dynasty, symbolizing the accumulated power of state authority and circulation. In the Dao-Twisted World, Lü Xiucai’s ability to make even a single coin tremble is a major milestone—these objects are spiritually heavy, and manipulating them requires genuine cultivation.

**Nascent Soul (元婴, yuán yīng)** – In xianxia cultivation, the Nascent Soul is a condensed, infant-like form of the cultivator’s core essence, representing a major breakthrough beyond Core Formation. Han Fu’s Nascent Soul is a special case—it was severed from his main body and now exists as a sentient, extremely anxious entity. In this universe, even severed souls have their own agendas.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a deliberate *breather*—a moment of false calm before the storm. Don’t let the relaxed atmosphere fool you. The plague mention at the very end is dropped with surgical precision, like a single chess piece moved into position. Lü Xiucai’s “success” is also deeply suspicious: Han Fu’s Nascent Soul has already proven itself a tricky little gremlin, and Li Huowang’s caution is well-founded. And on a thematic level, watching a once-proud troupe descend into *spring opera* is a bitter reminder that in this world, dignity is a luxury no one can afford for long. Enjoy the crude jokes and the ox-dung scavenging while it lasts, fellow Daoists—the next horror is already warming up backstage.
