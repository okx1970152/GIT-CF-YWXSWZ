## Chapter Overview

Beware. This chapter is a masterclass in quiet dread. After a nightmare involving Li Zhi's clay Protecting Immortal, Li Huowang wakes with a single, burning question: what did the veiled Second Spirit (Er Shen) try to tell him before she was interrupted? His midnight investigation leads to a secret meeting, a blood-written warning on a red bridal veil, and an explosive confrontation with a suddenly territorial Li Zhi. The chapter pivots on that brief, terrifying glimpse Li Huowang catches: scales beneath the veil, yellow-green mucus on Li Zhi's lips. The Heart-Element term gets a new spine-chilling modifier (“Immortal”), and the easygoing facade of the traveling spirit-dancer cracks just enough to let the rot show. Li Huowang is left with more questions than answers, and Bai Lingmiao’s sleepy concern is a quiet reminder that she is watching him change.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang dreams of the wooden bridge and a giant version of Li Zhi's Protecting Immortal, only to be saved from its grasping hands by the Second Spirit's whispered command: “Don't look.”
- He wakes in the dark, dwells on the Second Spirit's earlier attempt to communicate, and decides he cannot proceed with the spirit-dance ritual without understanding her secret.
- He sneaks to Li Zhi's room, finds the Second Spirit standing upright and motionless beside the bed like an object, and silently beckons her outside.
- In the yard, she writes on her own veil with her own blood: “Beware. You are. Heart-Element. Immortal.”
- Li Zhi bursts out, grabs her aggressively, and kisses her inside the veil, revealing yellow-green mucus and scales on her skin—clear indications she is something far from human.
- Li Huowang returns to a confused Bai Lingmiao, lies that nothing happened, and lies awake pondering the new, ominous term: *Heart-Element Immortal*.
- The next morning, Li Zhi's demeanor has shifted from easygoing to hostile and possessive, marked by the silent treatment at breakfast.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The term **Heart-Element Immortal (心素仙)** is a significant expansion of the novel's internal vocabulary. Here, *Heart-Element (心素)* was already established as a rare, innate constitution that makes its bearer a valuable alchemical ingredient—a living reagent. Now, the Second Spirit appends *Immortal* to it, suggesting that from her perspective (or from the perspective of the supernatural order she belongs to), Li Huowang is not a victim or a reagent but an *entity* of a certain rank. This could mean his condition is more ontological than physiological, or that the line between “rare human” and “low-tier deity” has become dangerously thin.

The **Second Spirit (Er Shen / 二神)** is a ritual assistant in the Northeast Chinese chuma spirit-dance tradition. In orthodox practice, the lead spirit-dancer (*Zhu Shen*) enters trance and channels the immortal, while the Second Spirit plays the role of translator and anchor—singing, drumming, and ensuring the lead returns safely. Here, the trope is inverted: Li Zhi is loud and controlling, while his “wife” is silent, autonomous, and capable of independent supernatural action (writing in blood). The red veil, the long black nails, and the revealed scales mark her as a fusion of bride, puppet, and something eldritch. The act of a medium kissing his Second Spirit as an assertion of ownership weaponizes the folk intimacy of the performance into a display of grotesque control.

## Reading Guide

This is your classic “the guide is not what he seems” pivot. If you, fellow Daoist, have been enjoying Li Zhi’s talkative charm and bottomless appetite, prepare for a rude awakening. The scales and mucus are the physical tells, but the real shift is in power: Li Zhi doesn’t treat the Second Spirit like a partner; he treats her like property, and his sudden rage at Li Huowang’s approach reeks of a secret he’s terrified will get out. The blood-written message is our first hard confirmation that the Second Spirit is working against her handler’s interests, which means she might be Li Huowang’s most valuable—and most dangerous—source of intel.

Also, keep an eye on Bai Lingmiao. She’s not doing much here, but she’s *watching* Li Huowang lie to her, and that kind of silent observation tends to pay off later. This chapter is rich with atmosphere: the creaking bridge dream, the moonlit yard, the blood sinking into fabric. Let each image settle before you rush to the next page. The horror here is in the *texture*—the grit of the clay, the stickiness of the mucus, the quiet absorption of a warning written in its author’s own blood.
