## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow travelers of the twisted path! Li Huowang thought he’d seen it all—demonic alchemists, flesh Buddhas, heart-element hunts—but tonight, the Dao-Twisted World decides to crank the difficulty knob to eleven. The sky has gone permanently dark, and evil spirits are popping up like discount fireworks in every single window of Cow Heart Village. Just when Li Huowang starts to think he has a handle on this new threat, he discovers the worst possible breach: Bai Lingmiao has been infected, turning into a monstrous, cackling, flesh-gobbling entity alongside her Second Spirit. This chapter delivers a concentrated dose of pure folk-horror panic, forcing Li Huowang to wield a terrifying new technique to pull his ally back from the brink of bestial madness. Get ready for claustrophobic dread, a shocking character transformation, and a ritual that blurs the line between self and other in the most literal way possible.

## Key Plot Points
Li Huowang and Zhuge Yuan confirm that the sudden proliferation of evil spirits is a direct symptom of the Heavenly Calamity, suggesting a systemic breakdown of reality. Li Huowang engages and dispatches a grotesque, arm-extending evil spirit with clinical efficiency, demonstrating his hardened combat reflexes. The real horror begins when green-lit windows start appearing in every house in the village, and then one lights up in Bai Lingmiao’s room on the second floor. Rushing inside, Li Huowang finds Bai Lingmiao and the Second Spirit devouring a corpse on her bed. Bai Lingmiao’s appearance has mutated horrifically—covered in scales, spikes, and fangs—and she is compulsively laughing and eating, overwhelmed by the mad joy of the spirits possessing her. To stop her, Li Huowang uses a cell-based technique: he presses a domino tile to his own forehead, removes his own face like a mask, and presses it onto Bai Lingmiao’s, finally causing her to freeze. This act reveals a profound new layer to his arsenal—one that comes at a heavy existential cost.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The chapter’s claustrophobic horror is built on a classic *jianghu* trope: the sudden, overwhelming proliferation of *xié suì* (邪祟, evil spirits) during a time of celestial imbalance. In Chinese folk religion, a *xié suì* is a generic term for a malignant or unclean spirit, often born from violent death or resentment. Their mass emergence here signals that the Heavenly Calamity is not merely a natural phenomenon but an ontological disaster—the barrier between the living and the dead is breaking down. The green light from the windows is a classic descriptor for *yīn qì* (阴气, yin energy), the cold, spectral force associated with ghosts and the underworld. Li Huowang’s use of orpiment paper (姜黄纸) and blood talismans is a core ritual practice, as orpiment is a yellow mineral believed to have warding properties. His final act—using a domino tile and a hand seal to “remove” his own face—is a visceral, literalized version of the alchemical concepts of *duàn yuán* (断缘, severing attachments to the mundane) and *tài dìng* (泰定, calm stillness from the *Zhuangzi*). He is not casting a spell; he is *lending his own humanity* as a stabilizing anchor for Bai Lingmiao, a terrifyingly intimate act of cultivation.

## Reading Guide
This chapter moves at a frantic, breathless pace. Pay attention to how Li Huowang’s response to the first evil spirit shows his growth—he is no longer afraid, just annoyed and efficient. The real punch comes from the violation of a safe zone: Bai Lingmiao’s room. Her transformation is one of the most visceral body-horror moments in recent chapters. Her line, “The Immortals are just too happy! They’re affecting me!” is a crucial clue. This is not a simple demonic possession; the spirits are *ecstatic* about the Calamity, and their joy is a contagious, corrupting force. Li Huowang’s solution—pressing his own face onto hers—is a raw, sacrificial tactic that leaves the reader wondering what he has just given away. The chapter ends on a freeze-frame of terrifying uncertainty: is Bai Lingmiao saved, or merely paused? The author masterfully uses the visual of the green windows to create a sense of being surrounded by an inescapable, hungry audience.
