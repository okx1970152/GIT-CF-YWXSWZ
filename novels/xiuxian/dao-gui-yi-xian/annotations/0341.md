## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow guides and travelers of the strange! Chapter 341 is one of those explosive instalments where the political board gets overturned and a new, terrifying player is name-dropped into the game. The dust hasn’t even settled from the rush for the Heart-Murk, and already the Zuowandao are bleeding. Their man Facai is dead, and the Chief Recorder is furious. But here’s the kicker: the killer is none other than the scholarly fanboy we glimpsed earlier, Zhuge Yuan. Now, the hunt is on, but not everyone is playing by the rules. Get ready for a brutal execution, a beautiful bribe, and the dawning realization that Li Huowang has just waded into a chess game between cosmic forces he doesn’t even know the names of yet. The tension is palpable, and the mystery is deepening faster than a knife into a monk’s gut.

## Key Plot Points

- The chapter opens with the reveal of Facai’s severed head. The Chief Recorder loses his cool, demanding to know who took the Heart-Murk from the remaining Zuowandao twins.
- His fear is tangible. He suspects a new player, a name that makes even this eunuch official nervous.
- The group splits to pursue the fleeing Zuowandao. We see a coordinated, if reluctant, manhunt.
- Li Huowang corners Xin Chi. He uses his Black Tai Sui tentacles for the first time as a direct, offensive weapon, holding the monk in place while he garrotes him with his own coin-sword.
- This is the first clean, cold-blooded kill Li Huowang makes under the Office’s banner. He doesn’t flinch.
- The murder is immediately covered by a bribe. Hong Da shows up, sees the corpse, and is easily bought off with a pile of Lifespan Pills. The “colleague” relationship of the Office is laid bare: it’s a transactional network, not a brotherhood.
- The second Zuowandao is caught, but the stress of interrogation breaks them instantly.
- The Chief Recorder forces out the name “Zhuge Yuan.” His reaction is one of pure, mortal dread.
- Tuoba Danqing is left in the dark, and Li Huowang is beginning to suspect that the world of the Office is much larger and more dangerous than he thought.
- The chapter ends with the Chief Recorder fleeing to the Capital, the other handlers leaving, and Li Huowang deliberately turning back toward the Buddha Bone Temple, ready to investigate the source of the name on his own.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Let’s talk about the **Chief Recorder (记相)**. This isn't just a fancy title. A “Recorder” in the context of the Supervisory Heavenly Office heavily implies a eunuch official of the Ming dynasty court, an institution that wielded terrifying power. These were men who had been physically altered to serve the Emperor, and who often grew to command vast internal bureaus. The imagery here is perfect: the powdered face, the sweet perfume masking a stench of decay and urine, the abacus that is both a tool of bureaucracy and a weapon. The Chief Recorder represents the *state’s* use of the supernatural, a chilling blend of imperial authority and cosmic horror. He’s not a cultivator monk; he’s a corrupt accountant of fate, and his fear of Zhuge Yuan tells you everything you need to know about the scholar’s real weight.

Also, let’s appreciate the **bribe structure**. Hong Da’s willingness to look the other way for a pile of Lifespan Pills is a perfect example of the *jianghu* (rivers and lakes) code within an official body. The Office is not a place of justice or loyalty; it’s a marketplace. Every kill, every testimony, every piece of paperwork has a price. Li Huowang is learning this fast, and it’s making him a far more effective player than he was as a desperate, terrified victim. He’s learning the currency of this world.

## Reading Guide

Okay, so let’s chew on this. The reveal of **Zhuge Yuan** as a major player is a masterstroke. We met him briefly, a polite scholar with a fan, and we all had that spider-sense tingle, right? Well, now we know why. The name alone causes the Chief Recorder to break down, reciting it out loud with pure terror. He suspects Zhuge Yuan’s divine ability has something to do with making the absurd seem normal, a perfect cover for stealing the Heart-Murk from right under their noses.

This chapter also solidifies Li Huowang’s evolution. He is no longer a survivor reacting to threats. He is a predator making calculated moves. Killing Xin Chi wasn't a mistake; it was a statement. And using the bribe to instantly make it go away? That’s the move of a man who understands the system he’s in and is learning to exploit it. Keep a close eye on his choice at the end—turning back to the temple. This isn't retreat; it’s a deliberate search for answers. The world is much bigger than Danyangzi or even the Zuowandao. And Li Huowang is about to find out just how small he really is.
