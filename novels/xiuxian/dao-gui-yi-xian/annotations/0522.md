## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, folks—this chapter doesn't take prisoners. Li Huowang executes a high-cost, high-reward gambit by triggering the **Intercalating the Five Phases** ritual from the *Thousand Greats Record*, transforming himself into a temporary, horrifying juggernaut. The Ao-Jing Sect's burning discipline gets turned on its head as he single-handedly reverses the siege, and a key defection from within their ranks seals the Eldest Princess's fate. But the victory is as hollow as his final, emptied-out body—reminding us that *Dao Gui Yi Xian* never hands out power without demanding your pound of flesh first.

## Key Plot Points

- Li Huowang performs the **Intercalating the Five Phases** by tearing out his own organs and using the Heart-Element skin artifact to fuel a forbidden Ao-Jing rite, turning his body into a temporary flesh-worm incubator.
- The Eldest Princess's guards and Zu Yi are overwhelmed as Li Huowang becomes virtually unstoppable—his wounds instantly filled by writhing maggots.
- The **Grand Liang State Preceptor**, Huangfu Tiangang, makes a cryptic, fleeting appearance on his backward-riding green ox, offering neither help nor hindrance, only a weary observation.
- Zu Yi betrays the Eldest Princess at the critical moment, drowning her face in burning gold liquid and turning the tide.
- Li Huowang kills the Eldest Princess, her skull riddled with maggots to ensure death.
- As the Intercalating the Five Phases expires, Li Huowang collapses into a hollow shell; a piece of his own skin crawls free from his abdomen and swells, hinting at further consequences.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Intercalating the Five Phases (置闰五行)**: A concept borrowed from traditional Chinese alchemy and cosmology. The Five Phases (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) govern everything from organs to seasons. "Intercalating" refers to inserting an extra, out-of-cycle element—much like a leap month. In the *Thousand Greats Record*, this ritual *overwrites* the practitioner's natural Five Phase cycle with parasitic flesh, granting immense temporary power at the cost of catastrophic self-destruction. Think of it as a "heroic spirit possession" that your own body pays the bill for.

**The State Preceptor's Backward-Riding Ox**: The image of the old man riding backward on a green ox is a direct homage to **Laozi** (the founder of Daoism), who is famously said to have departed the world westward on a green ox, leaving behind only the *Dao De Jing*. Here, Huangfu Tiangang embodies that same aloof, world-weary Daoist detachment—he sees the chaos, recognizes the players, and chooses to let the "seas turn to mulberry fields" without intervention. It's a beautiful, melancholic Chinese literary trope of the transcendent who has seen too much to care about one more bloody fight.

**Ao-Jing Sect (袄景教)**: A fictional syncretic sect fusing elements of Zoroastrianism (袄教) and Nestorian Christianity (景教). The "Flaming Gold" (燃烧的黄金水) and the burning aesthetic of their ritual combat evoke the Zoroastrian reverence for fire and purification. Zu Yi's sudden defection might be read through their own internal theology—perhaps a "Great Elder" holds more doctrinal weight than a princess.

### Spirit-Nurse (Lifespan) (阳寿)

In the *Thousand Greats Record* system, lifespans can be harvested, traded, and spent like currency to power rituals or heal wounds, often with horrifying side effects.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a pure adrenaline rush with a twist of body horror and a dash of philosophical seasoning. Everything is paced for maximum impact: the ritual activation, the reversal, the betrayal, the hollow aftermath. But what makes it special is the State Preceptor's cameo—he doesn't say much, but his presence acts as a narrative mirror, reflecting Li Huowang's monstrous transformation against the cold, eternal gaze of true Daoist transcendence. If you're here for the visceral combat and the satisfying fall of a stuck-up princess, you won't be disappointed. If you're here for the creeping dread of "what did I just become," the last paragraph will haunt you.

## Additional Note: The True Cost of Power

The final image—Li Huowang as a hollow shell, a piece of his own skin crawling free from inside him—is the *Dao Gui Yi Xian* signature. Never let yourself think that any victory comes clean. The *Thousand Greats Record* gives, but it takes everything in return. That skin fragment is not a trophy; it's a reminder that the protagonist is now a walking, dying, borrowed vehicle for something else.
