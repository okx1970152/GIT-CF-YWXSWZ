## Chapter Overview

After the mountain’s horrors, this chapter feels like a slow, uneasy exhale—but don’t mistake the calm for safety. Li Huowang descends with a single-minded purpose, and his first act is to burn the group’s hard-won treasure. The quiet intimacy between Bai Lingmiao and Chun Xiaoman reveals raw emotional layers, while Xiaoman’s dawn sword practice plants a seed of agency. This is a chapter about love, loyalty, and the cold calculation of a man who has stopped caring about money because he’s thinking on a scale no one else can see. Get ready for a departure that feels less like travel and more like a slow-motion gamble.

## Key Plot Points

- A nighttime conversation between Bai Lingmiao and Xiaoman exposes the group’s unspoken worry about Li Huowang’s mental state. Bai Lingmiao defends him fiercely, revealing her deep gratitude and affection.
- Li Huowang bursts in at dawn, demanding the gold. He has been out all night.
- He announces a new plan: they will travel to a location to retrieve an object, after which he will see everyone home. No discussion, no negotiation.
- The gold is gone—all of it donated to Anci Nunnery. Gouwa’s despair is comic but real; Xiaoman’s outrage is futile.
- The group departs with heavy hearts, completely in the dark about Li Huowang’s intentions.
- Days later, near the destination, Xiaoman practices swordsmanship alone in the forest. She confesses her motivation: she wants to be strong enough to protect herself and others, haunted by the memory of being sold by her father.
- Li Huowang finds her, briefly examines the sword manual, and gives a single instruction: “Show this to the others.”
- The group halts in a small farmyard. The last line sees Gouwa wondering where Li Huowang has gone, emphasizing his increasing isolation and secrecy.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Anci Nunnery** is a perfect example of how the Dao-Twisted World corrupts religious institutions. Unlike the polished, ritualized evil of Zhengde Temple, Anci Nunnery is a picture of vulgar decay—its nuns are filthy, gluttonous, and indifferent to spiritual matters. Li Huowang’s decision to donate the gold there is puzzling at first, but it fits his pattern of rejecting material value in favor of something only he understands. This is the same man who traded a jade pendant for survival; now he trades a fortune for… what? Connection? Obligation? Or simply clearing his ledger before a dangerous move?

**Leaf Cards (叶子牌)**: These narrow paper cards were a popular pastime in Ming and Qing China, often associated with gambling. Seeing the group play them in a humble farmyard reinforces the gritty, historical texture of this world—a stark contrast to the cosmic horror simmering beneath.

**Human ingredient (药引)**: Xiaoman’s question—“why would that Bald-headed Li have tried to take him as a human ingredient?”—recalls the horrible alchemical practice where living people are used as raw materials for elixirs. This is not a metaphor; it is a concrete, visceral transaction in this world, one that defines Li Huowang’s value and his terror.

## Reading Guide

- **Bai Lingmiao’s devotion** is laid bare. She doesn’t care about his madness—she cares that he accepts her. This is a fragile but vital anchor for Li Huowang.
- **Xiaoman’s arc** takes a huge step. She is no longer just the silent, hirsute woman; she is actively seeking power. Her sword manual purchase is her first independent act of self-determination. Notice how she guards Bai Lingmiao even in sleep—her protectiveness runs deep.
- **Li Huowang’s opacity** grows. He doesn’t explain himself, doesn’t apologize, and doesn’t ask permission. He is moving toward a goal that makes gold irrelevant. That should worry us—what makes a man so certain that he can afford to give away the group’s only wealth?
- **The chapter’s final image**—the group stuck in a village, Li Huowang missing, everyone idle and playing cards—sets up a classic tension. Something is coming. The calm before the storm is never truly calm in this world.
