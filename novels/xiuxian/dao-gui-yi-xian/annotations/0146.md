## Chapter Overview

After the chaos and pressure of the previous ordeals, Chapter 146 gives us a quiet, hazy hangover chapter—and it’s *exactly* what this story needs right now. Li Huowang wakes up to a pounding head and the warmth of Bai Lingmiao’s bedside care, but don’t let the peaceful setting fool you: every seemingly mundane conversation is a loaded mine. Zhao Qin’s drunken ramble about the “Supervisory Heavenly Office” reopens a wound Li Huowang can’t even name, and Jiang Yingzi’s ghost is there to twist the knife. This is a chapter about the silence between screams, about how the worst thing a monster can do isn’t always kill—sometimes it’s just sit there, grinning, while you try to pretend you’re a normal person at breakfast.

## Key Plot Points

- Over drinks, Zhao Qin drops a bombshell piece of worldbuilding: the **Supervisory Heavenly Office**, the empire's supernatural special forces. He casually mentions a town of tens of thousands that was slaughtered overnight four years ago. The detail triggers a visceral reaction in Li Huowang, who immediately connects it to his own hidden actions.
- Jiang Yingzi's blood-soaked ghost appears again in Li Huowang's waking moments, accusing him directly: “See how they talk about you? Why don't you just die?” This is a rare moment where the narrative externalizes Li Huowang’s own guilt.
- Bai Lingmiao notices Li Huowang’s distress and smoothly covers for him, swapping seats and holding his trembling hand. Her quiet competence is on full display.
- The next morning, Li Huowang experiences his first brutal hangover—a nice grounding touch in the midst of all the supernatural horror. He notes that his enhanced senses might be making it worse.
- Li Huowang examines his new military sword but refrains from drawing it, feeling a deep unease similar to what he felt with the *Thousand Greats Record*. This foreshadows that the sword is far from a simple weapon.
- The chapter closes with a moment of fragile domesticity: Bai Lingmiao leaning into Li Huowang, and his confession that she's all he has left. The tenderness is undercut by Danyangzi's laughter from the ceiling.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **Supervisory Heavenly Office (监天司)** follows a classic pattern in Chinese supernatural bureaucracy: an imperial institution staffed by specialists (often Daoist masters or martial experts) tasked with handling cases that fall outside the jurisdiction of ordinary law enforcement. It's a staple in Chinese supernatural fiction, from *Investiture of the Gods* to modern web novels, giving the setting a sense of an organized state apparatus that coexists with—and sometimes corrupts—the mystical world. Its informal mention here is a significant piece of worldbuilding: it confirms that the Siqi court is not merely a political entity but an active player in managing supernatural threats.

### Bang Bing Jue (帮兵决)

Spirit-summoning verses from the northeastern Chinese chuma spirit-medium tradition, used to invoke and command spirit entities with a rapid, urgent rhythm.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a slow burn—this chapter is deliberately paced, letting the reader sit in Li Huowang’s physical and emotional hangover. Pay close attention to **why** he reacts so strongly to the mention of the town massacre. This isn't just guilt—it's a near-confession. The narrative is cleverly hiding a major reveal in plain sight, and anyone who’s been following Li Huowang’s darker turn should have a sinking feeling by now. Also, notice how Bai Lingmiao is being positioned not just as a love interest but as a quiet, stabilizing force who is increasingly capable of reading Li Huowang’s moods. Her offer to adjust their schedule is a sign of trust, but it’s also fragile: Danyangzi’s presence on the ceiling reminds us that Li Huowang’s leash has only been lengthened, not cut.
