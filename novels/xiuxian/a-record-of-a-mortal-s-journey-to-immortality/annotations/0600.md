## Chapter Overview

Alright, grab your spirit tea and settle in, Fellow Daoists, because Chapter 600 is a masterclass in how the Mortal Stream universe operates—a quiet, transactional chess game disguised as a polite conversation. Our man Han Li receives a thoroughly suspicious gift from a dying sect leader: the formula for the legendary “Descent Talisman.” But this isn’t an act of altruistic legacy preservation; it’s a calculated gamble from a man who has run out of options. The chapter also delivers a delicious slice-of-death-world vignette: a group of desperate mortals hunting ugly, edible toad-beasts in the blood-red hellscape of the Netherworld Domain. It’s a stark reminder that while Han Li is thinking about ancient treasures, the rest of the world is just trying to survive dinner.

## Key Plot Points

A red-faced, Foundation Establishment elder of the Heavenly Talisman Sect appears at Han Li’s doorstep and, after some polite preamble, hands over a bone box containing the refining method for the sect’s crown jewel: the “Descent Talisman.” The guy is basically saying, “I’m old and I’m dying, and I don’t want this to vanish forever. Keep it, learn it, and maybe, *maybe* if you ever drift through the Jin Kingdom, give it back to the sect. No pressure.”

Han Li, true to form, doesn’t just grab the box like a kid on Christmas morning. He probes, asks the obvious question (“You trust me? A stranger?”), and gets a surprisingly honest answer: the old man has already tacitly agreed to let Han Li learn it as an “advance payment,” and honestly, he doesn’t think anyone will *ever* manage to actually *make* the damn thing. The ingredients, he explains, are absurdly ridiculous.

And then the other shoe drops. The primary material for the Descent Talisman is the refined soul of a **Transformation-level demon beast**. That’s right, a creature so powerful it’s basically a walking natural disaster. No wonder the Heavenly Talisman Sect has treated this formula as a glorified heirloom for generations. It’s a chicken rib: valuable in theory, useless in practice.

Han Li, being Han Li, files this information away with a quiet, mysterious smile—a clear signal that he’s already running the numbers on how *he* might accomplish the impossible. The chapter then jumps ahead a month. We get a stark, brutal scene of mortal life in the Netherworld Domain: a group of villagers hunting giant, ugly toads for food. Their hunt fails, but just as the beasts escape, a blade of green light strikes them down from out of nowhere. The chapter ends on that chilling, unanswered note—suggesting our protagonist is still watching, still moving, still calculating.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Let’s break down the “Descent Talisman” (降灵符). The name itself is loaded. In Chinese folklore and Daoist mysticism, “降灵” (jiang ling) refers to the ritual of “summoning” or “calling down” a spirit or deity. It’s a practice often associated with mediums, spirit-writing, and shamanic rites, where a higher being is temporarily invited to inhabit a body or object. The novel’s Descent Talisman takes this concept and scales it to xianxia proportions: it doesn’t summon just any spirit, but the refined soul of a **Transformation-level** (化神期) demon beast—a being so powerful it’s essentially a demigod in the cultivation world.

The “Secret Talismans” (密符) mentioned here are a fantastic lore layer. Think of them as the “wild cards” of the talisman system—unique creations that don’t follow standard elemental logic and are virtually impossible to replicate. They are the intellectual property of individual sects, jealously guarded pieces of a sect’s institutional soul. Han Li’s own experience with the Doppelgänger Talisman from the Void Heaven Palace arc is a perfect example: it was a life-saving trump card he could use, but could never *make*.

Finally, the desperate mortal hunt at the end of the chapter gives us a brutally efficient look at the Netherworld Domain (阴冥之地). The “yin beasts” (阴兽) are not magical monsters; they’re fauna, adapted to a dead, mana-sealed wasteland. The fact that people are primarily concerned with which ones are *edible* reinforces the Mortal Stream’s core truth: even in a realm of ghosts and yin energy, the most basic struggle—finding lunch—still comes first. Han Li’s silent intervention, killing the escaped beasts with a casual flash of green light, is a chilling reminder of the power gap between the cultivator and the mortal world he’s left behind, even as he walks through hell.

## Reading Guide

This is a classic *RMJI* “calm before the storm” chapter. It’s a quiet victory—a logistics win, not a battle win. Han Li gains a piece of powerful, legendary knowledge for free, with no immediate strings attached. The negotiation is a textbook example of the Mortal Stream social contract: the old man gets peace of mind, and Han Li gets a ticket to a potential future power-up. There’s no screaming, no betrayal, no knife in the dark. Just two cultivators making a cold, practical transaction.

Keep your eyes on Han Li’s reaction. When he reads the formula, his face shows a “mysterious unusual expression.” That’s not just idle curiosity. Han Li’s mind is already scanning for a path. He knows a Transformation-level soul is a ludicrous requirement, but he also knows he’s only at the beginning of a very long road. This is a long-term investment—a treasure map he can keep in his back pocket for the next few hundred chapters.

The closing vignette is also structurally important. It grounds us in the mundane horror of the Netherworld Domain. Han Li has been dealing with Nascent Soul-level schemes, but the people in this land are still hunting giant toads for dinner. It’s a perfect tonal shift that resets the reader’s perspective, reminding us that the world of *RMJI* is vast, cruel, and built on different levels of survival.
