## Chapter Overview

Han Li has cleared the labyrinth and now faces the second great test: the Ultimate Illusory Mirage. But before stepping into that peril, he does what any sensible Mortal Stream veteran does—he stops, sits down, and tests his new toys. And *what* toys they are. Chapter 478 is a masterclass in equipment appraisal, Mortal Stream style: no shiny sparkles or chest-thumping, just cold, methodical field-testing. The five bronze rings reveal themselves as an assassination-grade nightmare—invisible, instant, and utterly terrifying—while the blood cloak turns out to be a devastatingly fast but *broken* escape tool that even Han Li can barely control. Afterward, he finally enters the mirage corridor, only to find that the famed “celestial music” and “fairy cranes” are about as subtle as a trapdoor. Get ready for a chapter that feels like a high-stakes science lab report, with a side of beautiful deception.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li concludes without any doubt that the square passage leads to the Ultimate Illusory Mirage, the trial everyone has been dreading. His confidence comes from two specific assets: the Great Derivative Art (for mental defense) and the Brahmaloka Pearl Bracelet (lent by Extreme Yin).
- Instead of rushing in, he sits down to test the two ancient treasures he just acquired. This decision itself is a core Mortal Stream move: *never enter a danger zone without knowing exactly what your tools can do*.
- **First test: the Five Bronze Rings.** Han Li discovers they can turn invisible, stack into a five-colored defensive barrier, and—most terrifyingly—teleport onto a target’s limbs and neck from anywhere. Even *he* barely sensed them in time. His conclusion: this is a perfect assassination weapon, capable of surprising even Nascent Soul elders if they are off guard.
- **Second test: the Blood Cloak.** The results are far more mixed. The cloak turns into a blood-feather shroud and grants instant, blistering speed—fast enough to outrun a Nascent Soul pursuer. But it is a *broken* top-grade treasure: the user cannot control their trajectory, and the mana drain is immense. Han Li files it as a “last resort escape tool.”
- After a full day of recovery, he enters the square passage, finds an elegant open-air corridor, and immediately recognizes the celestial music and white cranes for what they are: illusions designed to trap the unwary. He walks through with a mocking sneer, completely unimpressed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Testing Ritual” in Xianxia:** New equipment in *RMJI* is never just a stat boost. Han Li’s method of testing the bronze rings—invisibility, defensive formation, surprise-constriction—mirrors the genre’s obsession with *utility discovery*. A treasure’s hidden properties are as important as its primary function. This chapter also reinforces a key rule: even flawed top-grade treasures (like the blood cloak) can be game-changers if the user is desperate enough. But *desperation* is the key; Han Li does not celebrate the cloak as a win—he files it as a *have-to-use-only-if-I’m-about-to-die* option.

**The Ultimate Illusory Mirage (极妙幻境):** This trial is a classic xianxia “mental fortress.” The white cranes and divine music are not decoration; they are bait. In Chinese literary tradition, the image of a crane often symbolizes transcendence, immortality, and Daoist ascension. Here, that symbolism is weaponized. The beautiful crane dancing to celestial music is a *trap* designed to make the cultivator let down their guard, feel peaceful, and lose their edge. Han Li’s sneer is not just mockery—it is a hard-earned survival reflex that recognizes beauty as the first mask of danger.

## Reading Guide

Alright, fellow cultivators, time for a gear-check! This chapter is the calm before the storm, but do not mistake it for filler. Han Li’s meticulous testing of the bronze rings and the blood cloak is a *survival tutorial*. Pay close attention to the mechanics: the rings are his new *ace-in-the-hole* for sneak attacks, while the cloak is his *oh-crap button* for when everything goes wrong. The fact that he tests them *now*, before stepping into the mirage, says everything about his mindset. He is not looking for glory. He is looking for *options*.

Also, take a moment to appreciate the sheer *disdain* Han Li has for the illusion. Most cultivators would be tempted by the ethereal music and graceful cranes. Han Li? He clicks his tongue, smirks, and walks past like a grumpy inspector on a safety audit. That is the difference between a mortal who has scraped his way up through sheer paranoia and a sheltered genius who still believes in fairy tales.
