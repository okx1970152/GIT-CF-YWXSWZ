## Chapter Overview
*Gasp! Wheeze! Pant!* Folks, our boy Han Li is having the *worst* month of his life. After his clever scheme of using spirit liquid as both bait and bomb backfired spectacularly, he’s now on the run from a genuinely pissed-off grade-nine Rift Wind Beast. This isn't a fun chase scene; it’s a cold, month-long calculation of life and death. Han Li learns a brutal lesson about the power gap between Core Formation and Nascent Soul, but he also shows us why he survives: he can turn panic into methodical, long-term calculation. The Rift Wind Beast isn’t doing much better, trapped in a mirror-image struggle, and the whole chapter is a fascinating game of cat and mouse where *neither* side has a clean shot. Get ready for some serious, gritty cultivation-world introspection.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li’s flying swords prove completely useless against the physical defenses of the grade-eight flood dragon and grade-nine turtle demon’s *body*, a stark reminder of the power hierarchy.
- He rationally calculates the odds of using the uncontrollable Dry Blue Ice Flame against the Rift Wind Beast’s protective treasure and deems the risk of losing the treasure too high, choosing a clean retreat instead.
- After boosting the two demons with the spirit liquid, Han Li kicks the immobile beasts into the terrestrial fire pool, bags their storage pouches, and flees, initiating a protracted chase.
- The Rift Wind Beast breaks free from the spirit liquid’s effects but is now suffering from a similar “time-bomb” foreign substance inside *his* own body, creating a parallel crisis for the hunter and the hunted.
- The chase becomes a month-long game of “who can suppress their internal demon first.” Han Li’s Wind Spirit Restriction acts up, forcing him to change course, while the beast is forced to pause to suppress his own “time-bomb,” preventing him from closing the net.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Beast Hierarchy: Why Han Li Ran**
This chapter is a masterclass in the setting’s most important rule: the cultivation world is a rigid class system. Han Li, a late Core Formation cultivator, can effortlessly slay grade-seven beasts. But against a grade-nine Rift Wind Beast, he is utterly outclassed. The novel explicitly explains *why*: high-grade demon beasts have unimaginably long lifespans, which they spend cultivating their physical bodies. Their defense is so freakish that a mortal’s “high-grade” flying sword is like a needle pricking a boulder. This is the *Mortal Stream*’s philosophy—power isn’t just about having a high cultivation level; it’s about the horrifying gap between stages. A Nascent Soul cultivator with a properly nurtured treasure could kill these beasts, but for a Core Formation cultivator, it’s pure suicide.

**The Hollow Trade (空头交易)**
The spirit liquid trap is a perfect example of a “hollow trade.” Han Li wasn’t trying to poison the beasts to death; he was trading them a temporary power boost for a long-term control variable. He gave them a benefit (increased mana) in exchange for planting a “time-bomb” (the foreign substance) inside them. This is a classic Mortal Stream tactic: when you can’t win in a straight fight, you turn the conflict into a multi-stage logistics problem. The beast is now forced to fight his own body as much as he is fighting Han Li, and that internal fight buys Han Li precious time.

## Reading Guide
This is a “quiet desperation” chapter—no dramatic battles, no heroic last stands. Instead, it’s a chapter about *the misery of a prolonged, asymmetrical conflict*. Enjoy the psychological tension of two powerful beings, each unable to get what they want, locked in a stalemate. Pay close attention to Han Li’s internal monologue. He doesn’t despair; he doesn’t rage. He *calculates*. He thinks about his future Nascent Soul and how his swords will eventually be strong enough. This is the core of his survival: he accepts his current weakness as a temporary condition and plans around it. The parallel crisis of the Wind Spirit Restriction for Han Li and the foreign substance for the beast is a smart narrative device—it makes the hunter and hunted feel like equals in their misery, even though they are worlds apart in power.

## The “Wind Spirit” Symmetry
A beautiful bit of Mortal Stream storytelling is the “symmetry of crisis” here. Both Han Li and the Rift Wind Beast are fighting the same battle—a battle against an enemy *inside their own bodies*. Han Li has the Wind Spirit Restriction, a leash planted by the beast. The beast has the foreign substance from the spirit liquid, a time-bomb planted by Han Li. This turns the chase into a game of “Who can stabilize their internal condition first?” It’s not about pure speed or power; it’s about who can solve their internal logistics problem faster. This is the kind of clever, systemic conflict that makes *RMJI* so compelling—it’s not just about who has the bigger stick, but about who can manage their own flaws better.
