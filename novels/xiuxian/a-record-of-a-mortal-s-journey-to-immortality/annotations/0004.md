## Chapter Overview

This chapter throws Han Li headfirst into the Seven Mysteries Sect's brutal entrance test. The path is divided into three stages: a deceptive bamboo grove that saps stamina, a treacherous rock face that shreds skin, and a final vertical cliff scaled only by hemp ropes. Han Li starts with naive confidence but quickly discovers the physical toll. His competitors, especially the older Wu Yan, outpace him. Though he fails to reach the summit before noon, Han Li's stubborn refusal to quit even when completely spent reveals the core of his character: a quiet, unyielding determination that will define his journey.

## Key Plot Points

- Hall Master Yue outlines the test: three segments (bamboo grove, rocky wall, cliff) with a noon deadline. Those who fail but show promise earn Disciple on Probation status.
- Han Li enters the bamboo grove, immediately feeling the strain. A silent, cold senior brother follows him, a constant reminder of the sect's watchful eye.
- After resting, Han Li pushes through the grove and reaches the rocky wall, where sharp sedimentary rock cuts his hands and knees.
- Han Li fights discouragement; a stubborn streak ("I won't lose") keeps him moving despite pain and fatigue.
- He witnesses Wu Yan, the strongest candidate, reach the cliff and begin climbing the rope with ease, reinforcing the gap in ability.
- A moment of crisis: Han Li slips and nearly falls, saved only by his quick reflexes and the senior brother's ready posture.
- At the rope base, Wu Yan mocks the stragglers with a contemptuous hand gesture before disappearing over the top.
- Han Li struggles to climb even the first knot, utterly depleted. He finally sits on the lowest knot, unable to move.
- In a final act of will, he attempts to climb further despite knowing he can't make the deadline, frustrated by his own weakness.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

"Bone-Grinding Cliff" (炼骨崖) is a fitting name for this initiation. In martial arts and xianxia traditions, tests of physical endurance are common rites of passage that symbolize forging the body and will. The "twelve main meridians" and "eight extraordinary meridians" referenced in earlier chapters align with Chinese medical and martial arts concepts of energy channels, but the test here is purely physical—no cultivation methods are involved yet. The "Disciple on Probation" (记名弟子) status reflects a Confucian-Jianghu hierarchy: you are kept on the rolls but receive no formal training until you prove worthy. The senior brother's silent supervision also echoes the tradition of "protecting the disciple" during trials, a practice that fosters both discipline and group identity in Chinese sects.

## Reading Guide

This chapter establishes Han Li's baseline: physically unremarkable but mentally tough. Readers should note his mix of childish vanity (wanting a cool uniform) and adult stubbornness (refusing to quit even when failure is certain). His rivalry with Wu Yan introduces a class dynamic that will persist: the privileged, trained child vs. the determined outsider. The test's design—escalating difficulty, no shortcuts, a tight deadline—mirrors the sect's pragmatic philosophy: only the truly persistent deserve instruction. Watch for how Han Li's later cultivation journey echoes this first trial: he rarely wins through raw talent, but through preparation, caution, and the refusal to give up.
