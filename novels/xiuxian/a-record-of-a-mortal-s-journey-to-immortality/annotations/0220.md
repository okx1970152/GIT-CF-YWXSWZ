## Chapter Overview
After the high-octane madness of the Blood Forbidden Trial, we finally get a quiet victory chapter—and honestly? This is where *RMJI* flexes its real muscle. Han Li isn’t celebrating his survival with a feast; he’s doing what any rational cultivator in a resource-scarce world would do: securing a long-term strategic asset. This chapter is a masterclass in logistics, patience, and the art of letting a magic rat do the heavy lifting. It’s slow, it’s methodical, and it’s absolutely essential. If you came here expecting another fight, sit back. This is the other half of mortal-stream warfare: the accumulation phase.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li rents a Double-Pupil Rat from Spirit Beast Mountain for three low-grade spirit stones, showing the normal, transactional side of sect life.
- The narration provides a rare panoramic view of Spirit Beast Mountain’s organization, confirming the scale of the sect’s beast-taming industry.
- Han Li flies northwest for a full day to a desolate border zone between Yuanwu Country and Xizhou—a strategic choice for a low-profile cultivation base.
- The Double-Pupil Rat, after nearly two days of searching, suddenly goes into a frenzy and leads Han Li to a hidden natural cave behind a massive cliff face.
- Han Li discovers a genuine Spirit Eye Spring—a rare physical manifestation of concentrated spiritual qi that can accelerate cultivation by up to 20–30%.
- The chapter concludes with a lengthy, almost textbook-style exposition on the theory of spirit veins, spirit eyes, and how physical spirit-eye objects form over tens of thousands of years—delivered without apology.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Spirit Veins and Spirit Eyes (灵脉与灵眼):** This chapter drops a full-blown lecture on the cultivation world’s geography of power. A spirit vein (灵脉) is a concentrated vein of spiritual qi running through the land; think of it as a ley line, but Chinese. The “Spirit Eye” (灵眼) is the densest point on that vein—the best real estate for cultivation. Most spirit eyes are intangible, just a location label. But when qi accumulates over geological timescales (tens of thousands of years), it can crystalize into a physical object: a spring, a stone, a bead, or even a legendary tree that has been growing qi for millennia. In *RMJI* cosmology, this is not magic appearing out of nowhere—it is the natural world’s own slow, patient alchemy. Han Li didn’t find a treasure; he found a piece of geology that took longer to form than recorded human history.

**The Logistics Phase of the Mortal Stream:** If the Blood Forbidden Trial was the violent, zero-sum feast of the mortal stream, this chapter is the slow digestion. The genre is brutally honest about what power actually costs: time, patience, and low-stakes resource gathering. Han Li’s “victory” here is not over an enemy but over terrain—finding a hidden cave with a spirit spring is the equivalent of stumbling onto an oil reserve. The narrative rewards this not with a dramatic boss fight but with a dense info-dump on geological qi theory. This is peak *RMJI*: quiet, cold, and absolutely uninterested in flashy triumphs.

## Reading Guide
This is the chapter where *RMJI* shows its love for worldbuilding over action. If you’re here for the fights, you might find your attention drifting; but for readers who appreciate the *why* behind the power system, this is gold. The spirit-eye exposition is the kind of background that makes later cultivation breakthroughs feel earned—Han Li didn’t just level up because the plot demanded it; he found a spot with better qi density and exploited it. Also, note the Double-Pupil Rat: it’s not a combat pet. It’s a scouting tool with a specific magical property (seeing through obstacles and sensing qi density), reinforcing the mortal-stream principle that even a first-tier beast has value if used correctly. Watch how Han Li treats it: functionally, with a touch of warmth that doesn’t compromise his pragmatism.
