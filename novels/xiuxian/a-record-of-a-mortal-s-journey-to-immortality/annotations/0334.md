## Chapter Overview
Han Li has just scored big: the five legendary Blood Coagulation Five Elements Pills, a terrifying black bowl, a crimson drill, and a jade slip that turns out to be a full-blown demonic database. But what looks like the ultimate shortcut to Core Formation comes with strings attached—strings that could slowly rot your brain. In true Mortal Stream fashion, our favorite cautious cultivator has to weigh heaven-defying benefits against irreversible costs, and the choice he makes is a masterclass in cold, rational survival.

## Key Plot Points
1. **Inventory check**: Han Li retrieves the Five Elements Pills from the beast puppet, confirms their authenticity, then nervously eyes the remaining demonic loot—steering clear of any cursed items by wrapping his hands in azure light.
2. **The jade slip lore dump**: It contains the Yue Emperor’s demonic techniques, all of which trace back to something called the Mysterious Yin Scripture—but no sign of the actual scripture itself.
3. **The Baleful Pill Art**: The pills were designed to let cultivators stuck at Foundation Establishment form a “Baleful Pill,” a pseudo-golden core with a 1-in-3 success rate. Han Li’s heart nearly jumps out of his chest.
4. **The catch**: A Baleful Pill can never grow stronger (caps you at early Core Formation), and worse, it continuously leaks baleful energy that will slowly dull your mind over time. Han Li’s greatest weapon is his sharp wits—he cannot afford to lose them.
5. **The pivot**: Just as he’s about to write off the pills as a chicken rib, he reads about the External Incarnation art—a legendary demonic technique that lets you refine a second body. The Yue Emperor’s whole scheme suddenly clicks into place.
6. **Closing the inventory**: The black bowl is a Soul-Gathering Bowl (too yin for Han Li to touch safely), the red awl is a solidified Blood Spirit Drill that might work as a tool, and Han Li leaves the demonic gear behind with cold finality.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Baleful Pill (煞丹) vs. True Golden Core (金丹)**: In standard xianxia, a True Golden Core is the culmination of a cultivator’s own hard-won enlightenment—a living, growing engine of power that can keep expanding toward Nascent Soul and beyond. The Baleful Pill is its evil twin: a frozen, counterfeit core that will never grow. The art openly admits it’s a “man-made golden core,” which spells out the entire xianxia philosophy that shortcuts to power always leave a permanent scar.

**Chicken Rib (鸡肋)**: This idiom comes from the classic *Records of the Three Kingdoms*: a chicken rib has no meat, but it’s a pity to throw it away. In xianxia, it describes any resource that is valuable in theory but useless (or dangerous) in practice. Han Li’s entire internal struggle with the Five Elements Pills is a textbook chicken-rib dilemma—he can’t use them himself, but they’re too rare to discard without a plan.

**External Incarnation (身外化身)**: In Daoist and xianxia traditions, an External Incarnation is a second body cultivated separately from the main one—often used as a backup life or a vessel for a separate cultivation path. It’s an extremely advanced demonic secret art that most sects hoard as their highest treasure. The novel smartly uses it here to unlock a narrative door: if you can’t take a shortcut with your own body, you can always forge a new one that can.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a textbook example of why *RMJI* rewards patient readers. On the surface, it’s a simple loot-check scene, but look closer—the entire second half of Han Li’s journey is being quietly set up. The Blood Coagulation Five Elements Pills won’t go to waste; the External Incarnation technique reveals itself as the perfect solution. And that single line about the Mysterious Yin Scripture? Mark it. This is how the novel layers its worldbuilding—dropping a name, a rumor, a fragment of lore that only pays off chapters or even arcs later.

Also, pay attention to the moment Han Li *almost* takes the pill. The text deliberately lets him dangle on the edge of a decision before slamming the brakes with the mental erosion warning. That’s the Mortal Stream philosophy in action: the universe offers you candy with one hand and a brain-eating curse with the other. Only the paranoid survive.
