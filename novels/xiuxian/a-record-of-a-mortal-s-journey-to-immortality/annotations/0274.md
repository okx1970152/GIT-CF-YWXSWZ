## Chapter Overview

Han Li returns to his sect after the Blood Forbidden Land nightmare, only to find an even bigger one brewing overhead: the Seven Sects versus the Six Demonic Sects is escalating into a full-blown war. But our favorite paranoid mortal doesn’t panic. Instead, he sits down with a librarian’s patience and discovers that the shiny blue token he snatched from the underground cavern is no ordinary trinket. It’s a **Great Teleportation Token**—a lost artifact that could shoot him across the entire continent in one jump. His next step? Figure out if the broken teleportation array he found can be fixed, because if it can, he just found his one-way ticket out of an exploding war zone.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li safely returns to Yellow Maple Valley and is surprised to find no new mission waiting for him. The tension in the sect has eased slightly as the Seven Sects have gained an early upper hand in the war.
- He carefully examines the blue token taken from the cavern and discovers its bizarre material (wood-like, not metal) and its dangerous reaction to spiritual power—it nearly drains him dry.
- Finding the damaged underground teleportation array, he copies its markings onto a jade slip, hoping to find someone who might know how to repair it.
- He discovers and collects two white spider eggs behind a stone pillar, promptly stashing them for future cultivation.
- After sealing the cavern entrance and covering his tracks, Han Li spends time at the sect library and finally locates a description of the token in an ancient text: it is a **Great Teleportation Token**, essential for surviving ultra-long-distance teleportation. The knowledge to create them has been lost for millennia.
- Piecing the clues together, Han Li concludes the underground teleportation array is likely an **ancient teleportation array**, capable of jumping millions of *li*—potentially his escape route from the war-torn region.
- He searches the library exhaustively for information on repairing ancient arrays but finds nothing. Just as disappointment sets in, he recalls someone who might be able to help.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Great Teleportation Token & Ancient Arrays**  
The term “Great Teleportation Token” here evokes the classical xianxia concept of “Lost Inheritance” (失传)—a civilization’s peak knowledge that has been shattered by war and time. In Chinese fantasy, ancient cultivators are often portrayed as vastly superior to modern ones, making any surviving relic from their era a game-changing force. The token’s description as a soft, wood-like artifact that produces a crushing space-pressure is a narrative callback to the “spatial laws” that higher cultivation realms rely on. A token is thus not just a key—it’s an insurance policy against the universe physically tearing you apart.

**Foundation Establishment Mid-Stage Breakthrough**  
Han Li’s simultaneous breakthroughs in the Great Derivative Art and the Azure Origin Sword Art are no accident. In xianxia logic, mental cultivation (divine sense) and combat cultivation (sword arts) often reinforce each other—a “harmonious breakthrough” triggered by surviving extreme stress. His joy is not about power itself, but about the **security buffer** every extra layer of cultivation provides in a world where *every attack is lethal*.

**Returning to a Wartime Sect**  
Han Li’s calculation about why he *must* return—rather than fleeing—is a perfect illustration of the “Mortal Stream” logic: long-term survival sometimes demands short-term submission to a broken system. The Seven Sects’ execution squads are a real institutional enforcement mechanism, and Han Li knows that being tracked for life is a worse fate than being assigned a dangerous mission. Staying inside the beast’s mouth is his safest bet until he finds a way out entirely.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the **bridge between arc climax and logistics**. We’ve survived the Blood Forbidden Land, collected loot, and now watch Han Li do what he does best: sit down, open a book, and run the numbers. The Great Teleportation Token is a classic “Chekhov’s artifact” in xianxia—a tool that redefines the protagonist’s entire trajectory. If he can repair the array, the war in Yue Country becomes someone else’s problem. If he can’t, he’s trapped in the grind. The tension here is not in action, but in *information asymmetry*. Han Li is collecting intelligence that nobody else knows he has, and its value compound—we can feel the escape plan crystallizing in his mind even as he calmly tends his spider eggs. Also, keep an eye on that “someone who might be able to help” mention—it’s a narrative hook that will pull in a major supporting character soon enough.
