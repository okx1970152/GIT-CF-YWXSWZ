## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow readers! Our man Han Li has just had a brush with death from an immortal-level threat, and now he's back to his prime directive: survival. This chapter acts as a much-needed cooldown, shifting from the terror of high-stakes cultivation to the more familiar (for Han Li) world of mortal politics and scheming. He's got a target: the Warm Yang Jade, the key to curing his lethal Yin poison. But to get it, he'll have to navigate the treacherous social waters of the mighty Mo family, deal with a rival impostor, and not get distracted by the revelation that his target's daughter, Mo Yuzhu, is an absolute knockout. The chapter sets the stage perfectly for Han Li's next move: a direct confrontation under the cover of night.

## Key Plot Points
- From rooftop to restaurant, Han Li's internal state is one of survival euphoria. He's alive, and that’s all that matters, despite the disrespect shown by the blue-clothed cultivator.
- Han Li gathers intel on his target: Mo Yuzhu is not just a pretty face. She's a martial arts-trained, free-spirited huntress, the apple of her father's eye, and apparently in a romantic relationship with the hated Wu Jianming.
- Han Li gets his first look at Mo Yuzhu as she returns from a hunt with Wu Jianming. The narrative confirms her legendary beauty, which briefly throws even our pragmatic protagonist off his game.
- The chapter reinforces the strong bond between Wu Jianming and Mo Yuzhu, a complication Han Li must factor into his plan to poison the family's trust in him.
- Han Li makes his final tactical decision: a direct nighttime visit to Madam Yan, the de facto leader of the Startled Serpent Society. He will use Doctor Mo’s token, the ultimate card, to expose the impostor.
- This plan comes with a major risk: Han Li is openly acknowledging his connection to the wanted Doctor Mo, a gamble that could backfire spectacularly.
- The chapter ends with Han Li executing a flawless infiltration of the Mo estate, showcasing his skill and confirming that, among mortals, he is a ghost.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Art of the Purely Practical Bluff:** Han Li's entire plan hinges on a social gamble. In the Mortal Stream, information is a weapon, and his possession of Doctor Mo's tokens is a "trump card" that trumps any local intelligence. He cannot simply fight everyone; the smartest cultivators win the battle before the fighting starts.

**Falling in Love vs. Cultivation's Iron Rule:** Han Li's brief internal struggle upon seeing Mo Yuzhu’s beauty captures a core tension. In most cultivation stories, romance is a powerful motivator. Here, Han Li sees it as a potential weakness, a distraction from the supreme goal of longevity. His logic is chillingly cold: "Being attracted is normal. It doesn't mean it's love." He prioritizes his mission instantly.

**Mo Yuzhu: The Rebellious Spirit:** The description of Mo Yuzhu—a noblewoman rejecting needlework for martial arts and hunting—is a classic trope in Chinese fiction. It marks her as a strong, independent character, a "fiery rose" amidst a garden of wilting violets. This makes her more than just a prize, even if her primary role here is as a complication in Han Li's logistical equation.

## Reading Guide
This is a classic "preparation" chapter for Han Li. He's done the intelligence gathering (Sun Er Gou), he's assessed the target (the Mo family), he's identified the obstacles (Wu Jianming, his impostor status), and he's formulated a high-risk, high-reward plan. Watch how he internalizes the new variable of Mo Yuzhu's beauty—there is no teenage giddiness, only a logistical note that this could be a complication.

The narrative tone shifts back to a wuxia-style heist. The description of Han Li drifting over rooftops and entering the Mo estate "as a wisp of smoke" is a beautiful payoff for his training in the Seven Mysteries Sect. He is no longer just a village boy; he is a professional infiltrator. This chapter is a quiet lull before a storm of confrontation, proving that Han Li's most dangerous weapon is often not a spell, but a well-laid plan.
