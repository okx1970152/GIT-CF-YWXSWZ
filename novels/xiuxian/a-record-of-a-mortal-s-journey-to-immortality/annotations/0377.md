## Chapter Overview

Han Li skips the prestigious mountains and picks the runt of the litter—a tiny, qi-starved island with a few hundred fishermen for neighbors. The bureaucrat handling assignments practically sneers at the choice, warning him about thinner spiritual qi, crushing formation costs, and a long list of predecessors who crawled back tail-between-legs. But Han Li has a secret the ministry doesn’t know about: a certain little green bottle that turns “slow cultivation” into a private, undetectable assembly line. So he signs the jade slip, leaves a goodbye letter at his temporary lodging, and flies straight for his new, isolated real estate.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li studies the full map of Starfall Island, learning that only white-light locations are available for Qi Condensation cultivators; mountain peaks are blocked.
- He deliberately picks “Small Huan Island,” a location *outside* the main island, despite the middle-aged examiner’s transparently discouraging lecture about low qi density, high formation costs, and failed residents in the past.
- The examiner confirms that selecting this island means Han Li becomes its de facto “island lord,” responsible for the protective formation’s spirit stones and exempt from annual tribute to the main island.
- Han Li accepts the jade slip as proof of claim and exits the Ascension Pavilion without lingering or networking.
- The next morning, he leaves a letter and heads for the harbor, buying a sea chart along the way.
- He suppresses his curiosity about Starfall City’s “Heavenly Capital Street” due to his low cultivation level, acknowledging his own caution has grown with his awareness of the power gap.
- Han Li’s internal monologue reveals his true calculus: the thin qi is a feature, not a bug—it removes him from prying eyes and allows him to exploit the Heaven Vial in privacy.
- After a day-and-night flight on the Divine Wind Boat, he lands at Small Huan Island’s dock, introduces himself to the local fishermen as “Immortal Master Han,” and declares the island his cultivation site.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Chicken Rib (鸡肋)** — This chapter directly uses the classic Chinese idiom *jī lèi*. It describes something so mediocre that it’s “tasteless to eat, yet a pity to throw away.” In xianxia worldbuilding, it’s a favorite descriptor for resources or territories that aren’t worth fighting over but are just useful enough that no one wants to surrender them to an enemy. Small Huan Island is the textbook example: it has a spirit vein, so you can’t just abandon it, but it’s so weak that any ambitious cultivator would rather pay a fortune for better qi density. The bureaucrat’s lazy, mocking tone when he explains this tells you everything about how the cultivation world values “raw speed” over “strategic safety.”

**Island Lord Economy** — The deal Han Li signs reveals a fascinatingly practical side of the cultivation world’s real estate system. Becoming an “island lord” isn’t about feudal grandeur; it’s a service contract. You get autonomy and exemption from annual taxes, but you inherit the full operational cost of the island’s defensive formation—and you’re allowed to squeeze a token fee from the mortal residents, who clearly can’t cover the bill. This is a Dark Forest lease arrangement: the sect washes its hands of a marginal asset, and the tenant (Han Li) gambles that he can make the math work through private resource generation. The fact that no previous claimant lasted more than a few years tells you this is a trap that only a protagonist with a secret cheat engine could turn into a home.

### Green Bottle (Heaven Vial)

The mysterious artifact Han Li discovered early in the story; it absorbs celestial energy and produces a Green Liquid that dramatically accelerates the growth of spiritual herbs, making it his primary engine for alchemical cultivation.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in “the quiet victory” phase of a Mortal Stream arc. There’s no duel, no treasure hunt, no dramatic escape. The entire conflict is a bureaucratic negotiation where Han Li picks the option everyone else rejected, smiles politely, and accepts a deal that sounds terrible on paper. The reader who has been paying attention knows exactly why he’s doing it: the Heaven Vial makes spiritual qi concentration irrelevant because *his growth curve runs on accelerated herbs, not ambient energy absorption*. Every other cultivator saw a dead-end outpost; Han Li saw a private, no-surveillance greenhouse with a free title.

**Key insight for new readers**: Han Li’s caution here is not cowardice. He explicitly tells himself he’s skipping the big city market because *his cultivation is too low to risk exposure*. This is the same calculation that has kept him alive since Doctor Mo. The trade-off he makes—thin qi for total privacy—is a textbook “investment in long-term security over short-term speed.” And his moment of self-deprecating humor (“seems strength shrinks courage”) is a rare, contained crack in his wall of calculation, showing that he *knows* he’s being paranoid but trusts that paranoia more than he trusts a crowd of unfamiliar cultivators.
