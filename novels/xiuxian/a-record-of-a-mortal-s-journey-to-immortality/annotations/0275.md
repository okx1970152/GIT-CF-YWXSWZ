## Chapter Overview

Han Li has been stuck on the Jingu Plain for an entire year, serving as high-grade cannon fodder in the grinding war between the Seven Sects and the Six Demonic Clans. This chapter doesn't advance the plot with a single dramatic sword slash; instead, it builds world texture. We get a brutally vivid picture of how the war is actually fought—a cold, institutionalized meat grinder where Foundation Establishment cultivators are the prime casualty demographic, Core Formation cultivators are untouchable gods of the battlefield, and Qi Condensation disciples are treated as precious reserves. Between skirmishes, Han Li does what he always does: loot, trade, accumulate, and calculate. He secures the Dragon Sing Grass he's been saving up for, drops a disappointing but not surprising inquiry about rare pill formulas, and waits for his real opportunity—the chance to find Qi Yunxiao and hopefully access the ancient teleportation array.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li revisits his old plan to find Qi Yunxiao's formation-master friend, who might be able to repair the ancient teleportation array.
- Back in his cave, Han Li activates a Soul-Controlling Formation to bind the spider eggs, using his essence blood to secure ownership, then returns them to the Spirit Eye Spring to continue accelerated hatching.
- A summons to the meeting hall arrives; the long-awaited mission is finally here.
- One year later, Han Li is deep in the Jingu Plain battlefield. He kills a Ghost Spirit Sect Foundation Establishment cultivator, loots the pouch, and immediately withdraws before nightfall to avoid Core Formation-level battles.
- The chapter exposits the brutal rhythm of the war: massive monthly battles with low death tolls, daily small-scale hunter-killer skirmishes in the plain's center, and an unofficial curfew where day belongs to Foundation Establishment cultivators and night to Core Formation monsters.
- The toll is shown: hundreds of Foundation Establishment deaths on each side in one year, while Core Formation casualties remain precisely zero.
- Han Li returns to camp, sorts his loot, and sells the chaff at the camp exchange for nearly a hundred spirit stones, then trades the full sum for a pre-ordered Dragon Sing Grass.
- He asks about rare pill formulas and is told a polite but firm "impossible"; ancient formulas are hoarded, not sold.
- The chapter ends with a quiet epilogue: Han Li realizes the camp exchange is actually richer in rare items than any market town, and he has already acquired two ancient formulas for a song. He is playing the long game.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Cultivation War Economy** — The Jingu Plain battlefield is a perfect case study in *Mortal Stream* war mechanics. Notice the structural imbalance: Foundation Establishment cultivators bear the full brunt of death and attrition, Core Formation cultivators are effectively invincible (fast flight, no deaths in a year), and Qi Condensation cultivators are *preserved* as a reserve force. This isn't just realism; it's a cynical reinforcement of the cultivation world's class system. The higher your realm, the harder you are to kill and the more decisively you control the battlefield's tempo. Han Li's entire survival strategy—retreat before dark, never fight fair, treat every kill as a resource acquisition—is built on this grim cost-benefit reality. The war is not a war of good vs. evil; it's a resource-extraction operation disguised as armed conflict, and Han Li is optimizing his personal profit margin within it.

**The Phantom of Formulas** — Ancient pill formulas in *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality* are treated less like knowledge and more like lost relics of a golden age. The fact that Elder Chen calls them "trash formulas" that sat in his inventory for years, and that Han Li snapped them up anyway, tells you everything you need to know about the structural scarcity of the cultivation world. A single viable formula is a multiplying asset; it turns raw materials into experience for literally as long as you can produce the ingredients. In a closed economy where knowledge is actively hoarded, having *any* formula puts Han Li a step ahead of the competition.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a quiet victory lap that doesn't feel like one. Han Li has survived a year of asymmetric warfare without a scratch, accumulated a small fortune in spirit stones, locked down a rare herb, and acquired two ancient formulas at a steal. The tone is deliberately subdued—no dramatic revelations, no flashy breakthroughs. But for readers of *Mortal Stream* fiction, this *is* the victory state: surviving long enough to accumulate, trade, and prepare for the next move. The mention of the camp exchange being richer than a market town is a key piece of worldbuilding. The war is functionally a black-market economy in its own right. Pay attention to Han Li's quiet satisfaction at the end; he's not celebrating a triumph, but methodically noting that his position has improved. That's the highest note of optimism *Mortal Stream* ever allows.
