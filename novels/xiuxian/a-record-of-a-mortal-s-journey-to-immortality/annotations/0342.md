## Chapter Overview
Alright, buckle up fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a masterclass in psychological warfare as Han Li, freshly armed with a new body and a new trump card, decides to do a little interrogation of his own. The 'guest'? None other than the soul squatting inside our boy Qu Hun's head. And our friend is NOT having a good time. What starts as a polite-ish Q&A session quickly devolves into a terrifying display of information gathering through pure, cold intimidation. This isn't a fistfight; this is a dissection. Han Li uses a mistaken assumption about the Soul-Gathering Bowl to its maximum advantage, flipping the script on a would-be possessor. If you ever wondered how Han Li got so good at keeping secrets, just watch how he extracts them from others. It's efficient, it's chilling, and it's peak Mortal Stream.

## Key Plot Points
*   Han Li reveals his understanding of the situation: his Soul Summoning Bell is the 'lifebound artifact' for the body, giving him control over Qu Hun.
*   The possessed soul tries to negotiate, admitting to being an ex-Foundation Establishment cultivator and offering treasure in exchange for his freedom. Han Li is not tempted.
*   Han Li tests the claiming by asking about Han Yunzhi of Spirit Beast Mountain. The soul's obvious panic gives it away immediately.
*   Unwilling to spill his secrets, the soul clams up. Han Li escalates by immobilizing him with a Spirit-Fixing Talisman.
*   Han Li produces the Soul-Gathering Bowl. The soul, seeing its阴森 appearance, misidentifies it as a 'Soul Vessel' for the dreaded Soul Refining Technique.
*   Instead of correcting the misconception, Han Li weaponizes it. He uses the bowl's chilling aura to inflict unbearable pain on the soul, all while maintaining a terrifyingly calm expression.
*   Facing the threat of having his soul refined for an eternity, the soul breaks completely and agrees to talk. Han Li secures a complete psychological victory without ever casting a real soul-refining spell.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Soul Refining Technique (炼魂术):** This is the bogeyman of the cultivation world. While possession is about stealing a body, the Soul Refining Technique is about stealing *everything else*—a person's memories, their secrets, and their very ability to experience peace. In Chinese folklore and xianxia, the soul (元神) isn't just a ghost; it's the seat of consciousness and identity. To refine a soul is to trap a person's consciousness in an object and torture it indefinitely, potentially even erasing its ego and merging it with an artifact. This is considered far worse than simple death because it offers no finality. The common belief is that a Soul Vessel (魂器) must be personally attuned to the caster’s own soul to perform the technique, making the dark, ghost-wailing Soul-Gathering Bowl a perfect (and terrifying) match for that reputation. Han Li didn't have to cast a single spell to create this fear; the *myth* of the technique did all the work for him.

## Reading Guide
*   **Look for the Bluff:** Han Li is a master of the "fake it till you make it" philosophy. In this chapter, he doesn't just lie with his words; he lets the enemy's own fear do the lying for him. The narration never confirms he *can* use Soul Refining—he just doesn't deny it.
*   **Power Dynamics Through Pain:** This isn't about magical force; it's about controlled application of suffering. The chapter illustrates Han Li's understanding of a key Mortal Stream rule: fear is a resource, and pain is its most efficient currency. He lets the bowl's natural aura do the "talking."
*   **The Importance of a Poker Face:** Contrast Han Li's completely flat, emotionless demeanor with the soul's escalating panic. Han Li's deadpan responses are more terrifying than any shouted threat. It signals to the opponent that he is in complete, cold control.
*   **Forgetting the Human Element:** Don't feel sorry for the soul inside Qu Hun. Remember, this "guy" invaded and erased the consciousness of Han Li's childhood friend. This interrogation is a form of cold justice, and Han Li bringing the pain should feel satisfying, not cruel.
