## Chapter Overview

Our favorite paranoid alchemist is back in the mortal world, tying up loose ends. Chapter 338 isn't a cultivation showdown—it's a *cleanup mission*. Han Li first demonstrates the true value of a puppet network by effortlessly poisoning his own informant, then immediately learns that his strongest asset, Qu Hun, has suffered an *anomaly*. The walking corpse has developed self-awareness, spoken, and fled into the mountains. This isn't a mere plot complication; it's a terrifying sign: a creature born from forbidden alchemy and soul arts is undergoing an evolution the creator never intended. The chapter masterfully pivots from a mundane social check-in (with a perfect dose of Han Li's cold calculus) into a high-stakes mystery, reminding us that in the Mortal Stream, even a discarded tool can turn into a crisis.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Poison Parade:** Han Li demonstrates his refined methods of control. Rather than killing Sun Ergou, he reveals a slow-acting poison, cures him, and solidifies his loyalty once more. This is a textbook Mortal Stream lesson in management.
- **The Qu Hun Conundrum:** Sun Ergou reports the core crisis of the chapter. Six years prior, Qu Hun spoke, broke out of his confinement, and has been living as a feral entity in the surrounding mountains ever since. He actively avoids Sun Ergou (who carries the control artifact) but fights off anyone else.
- **The Bell is Returned:** Han Li reclaims the ‘Soul Summoning Bell,’ cutting Sun Ergou’s leash on the monster and officially taking personal responsibility for this problem.
- **The Warning Shot:** Han Li shifts gears, grilling Sun Ergou for intelligence on the Five Color Sect. He delivers a bone-chilling threat regarding loose lips, re-establishing his authority as a dangerous being who is not to be probed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Walking Corpse Anomaly:** In traditional Chinese folklore and Daoist mysticism, a corpse that has been reanimated via forbidden arts is not merely a puppet; it is a corrupted vessel. The ‘Three Hun and Seven Po’ (魂魄) philosophy dictates that while the physical ‘Po’ (corporeal soul) can be forced to move, the ‘Hun’ (spiritual soul) has already departed. For a soulless corpse to develop self-awareness and speech is an extreme anomaly, suggesting that perhaps some residual spiritual energy has latched onto the body, or the alchemical process has unintentionally birthed a new, twisted consciousness. This is not a zombie; it is an *abomination* in the xianxia sense, a being that defies the natural cycle of life and death, and its existence is a ticking time bomb.

## Reading Guide

This is a classic ‘lowering the sword before the storm’ chapter. The visit to Sun Ergou feels like a routine maintenance check, but it drops two massive hooks: one is the feral Qu Hun (a future combat asset or a serious threat?), and the other is Han Li’s deliberate probing of the Five Color Sect. He’s not just asking out of historical curiosity about the Mo family. He is laying the groundwork for a surgical strike, gathering intelligence on his enemies’ strengths and weaknesses. Pay attention to the ‘True Spirit Art’ used to reveal the poison; this is a reminder that Han Li’s skills are evolving, and he is now operating with a level of sophistication far beyond a simple martial artist. The calm before the storm is over; Han Li is loading his gun.
