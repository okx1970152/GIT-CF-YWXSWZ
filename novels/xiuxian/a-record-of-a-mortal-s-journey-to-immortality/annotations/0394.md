## Chapter Overview
The fangs finally come out! After years of lying low, Han Li and Qu Hun find themselves ambushed by a squad of Foundation Establishment Enforcement Cultivators from Kuixing Island, sent to capture them for the “murder” of some Tail Star Island disciples and the theft of the Drop Dust Pill. But these fools have gravely miscalculated just who they’re dealing with. What was supposed to be a routine capture turns into a one-sided slaughter when Qu Hun unleashes his true Core Formation power. This chapter is a brutal, satisfying payoff that showcases just how far Han Li’s trump card has come—and introduces a fascinating glimpse into the cynical politics of the high-level cultivation world.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li detects a hidden ambush early, forcing the eight Foundation Establishment cultivators to drop their stealth and confront him openly.
- The lead elder arrogantly demands Han Li’s surrender, citing Elder Miao of the Six Serenity Hall as a witness and ordering immediate cultivation crippling.
- Han Li, calmly assessing the situation, gives Qu Hun the order: kill them all, let no one escape.
- Qu Hun activates his full Core Formation power, casually absorbing all the attackers’ magical tools with a single blood-light technique and a “Seal” command.
- Two cultivators attempt to flee; Qu Hun incinerates them along with their defensive treasures with twin beams of blood light.
- Using a blood cloud that morphs into purple demonic flames, Qu Hun annihilates the entire squad in moments, reducing both men and treasures to ash.
- Han Li collects the Storage Pouches of the fallen, and the pair flee the scene.
- Two Core Formation Vice Island Masters (Yuan and Qi) arrive shortly after, but after a brief discussion, they decide not to pursue—citing the risk of fighting an unknown Core Formation peer over a mere disciple’s death.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter is a masterclass in **High-Level Cultivator Realpolitik**. The two Vice Island Masters who arrive at the scene—Fellow Daoist Yuan (the grieving master) and Fellow Daoist Qi (the cynical pragmatist)—embody a core theme of the Mortal Stream: the absolute devaluation of low-level life. Yuan’s initial rage is genuine, but Qi’s cold logic overrides it in seconds. A Foundation Establishment disciple, even a promising one, is ultimately a consumable asset. The calculus is brutally simple: a fight with an unknown Core Formation peer carries existential risk (it could be a hidden expert with allies, trump cards, or a vengeful sect behind them), and the reward (revenge for an ordinary disciple) is negligible. This is the “Dark Forest” logic at work even among nominally allied cultivators. The only “loss” is face, and Qi assures Yuan that silence can preserve his reputation perfectly. This scene is not about justice or vengeance; it’s about **resource management and risk avoidance**, where a disciple’s life is weighed against the chance of personal injury and found wanting.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for some serious catharsis, fellow Daoists! This chapter is a glorious showcase of **power disparity in action**. We’ve been watching Han Li and Qu Hun tiptoe around, disguising their strength, dealing with mortal warlords and petty sect politics for a long time. Now, when a group of Foundation Establishment toughs tries to throw their weight around, they get to see what a *real* Core Formation cultivator looks like. Qu Hun doesn’t even break a sweat. He absorbs their tools, incinerates their defenses, and burns them to ash with purple flames like he’s swatting flies. The real highlight, however, is the aftermath. The conversation between the two Vice Island Masters who *could* have pursued them is a textbook lesson in cultivation-world politics. It’s cynical, cold, and absolutely rational. The old man’s line, “a risk with insufficient reward is not worth taking,” is basically the motto of every smart cultivator who survives past middle age. Enjoy the power fantasy, but also savor the bitter realism beneath it. This is the world Han Li navigates—a place where even grief is calculated.
