## Chapter Overview

Our boy Han Li has been playing the long, cautious repairman game, and this chapter delivers the big payoff—and the biggest cliffhanger yet. After painstakingly restoring the ancient teleportation formation, a single moment of technical brilliance (guessing the low-grade spirit stones lack juice) triggers a beam of light that punches through a hidden mountain, alerting *every single demonic-path cultivator* within range. What follows is a frantic race against the clock: Han Li must decide whether to flee into the unknown or be captured by the Ghost Spirit Sect’s Young Master. The chapter closes with a cold, clean teleportation getaway and a grim epilogue for the Tian Nan world left behind.

## Key Plot Points

1. **Return & Completion:** Han Li cautiously returns to his underground lair and finishes all repairs on the ancient teleportation formation after six or seven days of intense work.
2. **The First Test Fails:** Installing low-grade spirit stones yields only a brief flicker before the formation goes silent, nearly dashing Han Li’s hopes.
3. **The Diagnosis:** Realizing the stones are depleted, Han Li swaps in medium-grade stones—and succeeds. A pillar of yellow light erupts, punching straight through the cavern roof and his own concealment formation.
4. **Exposure & Ambush:** The beam is noticed by a large force of Ghost Spirit Sect cultivators, including Wang Chan and the Li brothers (two Core Formation weirdos from Yanling Fort), who immediately recognize the formation’s value.
5. **The Siege:** The Li brothers order a full-scale assault on the Reversed Five Elements Formation, which holds just long enough—thanks to Han Li’s preparation—to give him the few critical seconds he needs.
6. **Teleportation:** With the Great Teleportation Token in hand, Han Li activates the array and vanishes with Qu Hun, uttering the word “Teleportation” just as the formation shatters.
7. **Epilogue / Closure (Time Skip):** The narrative jumps ahead. Wang Chan stations guards fruitlessly. The future of Tian Nan is sketched: the Six Sects join the Nine Nations Alliance, Han Li’s master Li Huayuan dies in battle a decade later, and a new generation of cultivators slowly rises.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Great Teleportation Token (大挪移令):** This isn’t just a fancy key. In the cultivation world’s spatial magic, crossing vast distances via an ancient formation requires more than just spirit stones. The “Great Teleportation Token” acts as a spatial anchor and a stabilizing core, preventing the traveler from being shredded by dimensional pressure during transit. The fact that Han Li had one (acquired in the Blood Forbidden Trial) proves his foresight is legendary. The Li brothers’ rage when they see it is not just frustration—it’s the realization that without such a token, they are completely locked out of this escape route, even if they captured the formation.

**The “Lizard Cuts Off Its Tail” Gambit:** Han Li destroys the teleportation platform on the other end immediately after arriving. This is a perfect example of the Mortal Stream survival philosophy. He trades any possibility of return for the absolute security of making the formation unusable for his pursuers. It’s a brutal, one-way ticket into the unknown, and he doesn’t hesitate.

**The Future of Tian Nan:** The concluding epilogue paints a picture of the cultivation world as a grinding war machine. The six sects from Yue are not heroes in exile; they are refugees fighting for resources and territory in the Nine Nations Alliance, suffering heavy losses against the Moulan “Dharma Warriors.” The death of Li Huayuan (Han Li’s master) is presented not as a dramatic tragedy but as a cold data point in a decade-long conflict—perfectly in line with the novel’s refusal to sentimentalize its characters.

## Reading Guide

So, Han Li is gone. Poof. Teleported to a *completely unknown* location, with no mana, a beaten-up corpse-puppet, and his Heaven Vial. This is the ultimate “clear the slate” moment in A Record of a Mortal’s Journey to Immortality, and arguably the end of the first grand story arc. The entire “Tian Nan” world—with its sect politics, demonic-path wars, and familiar characters like Wang Chan—is now officially in the rear-view mirror.

What makes this chapter so satisfying is the sheer, cold efficiency of Han Li’s escape. He doesn’t win a glorious battle. He doesn’t get revenge. He uses a clever technical insight, a hidden treasure, and a prepared escape route to simply *leave the board* when the game is no longer in his favor. That’s peak Mortal Stream philosophy.

Pay close attention to the timeskip at the end. The novel is telling us, very matter-of-factly, that the world doesn’t stop spinning for our protagonist. People he knew die. Wars are fought. A new generation arises. Han Li is not the center of the universe—and that’s exactly what makes him so compelling. Buckle up for the next continent; we’re going in completely blind.
