## Chapter Overview
Qi Yunxiao is dead. The source of Xin Ruyin’s haggard appearance and her widow’s dress is laid bare, and her grief is transformed into a cold, absolute demand for vengeance. In this installment, the idyllic bamboo cottage setting is replaced by a memorial tablet and an incense burner, and Han Li learns the full chain of events that led to Qi Yunxiao’s fatal encounter with the Fu Clan. More importantly, Xin Ruyin reveals that the teleportation array is nearly complete and makes an offer that turns her life’s work—and her husband’s—into a single bargaining chip: the extermination of an entire immortal clan. It is a chapter of closure, transaction, and the quiet, terrible resolve of a woman who has chosen to become a weapon of revenge through the hands of another.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Welcome**: Han Li arrives at the bamboo cottages to find Xin Ruyin and her maidservant clad in plain white mourning. Her haggard appearance signals tragedy before he even enters the room.
- **The Memorial**: Inside the cottage, a black spirit tablet bearing Qi Yunxiao's name confirms his death. Han Li pays his respects with incense.
- **The Backstory**: Xin Ruyin recounts how Qi Yunxiao, acting on her behalf to obtain a rare material, was identified by Fu Clan cultivators, fled back to his residence, and eventually died after a desperate fight that included trapping a Core Formation expert in an illusion formation.
- **The Offer**: Xin Ruyin reveals she has nearly finished repairing the ancient teleportation array and presents two jade boxes: one containing Qi Yunxiao's cultivation notes and classics, the other containing her own formation knowledge and texts.
- **The Price**: Her condition is stark: if Han Li ever reaches Core Formation, he must exterminate the entire Fu Clan from Yuanwu Country.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Memorial Tablets and Widowhood in Chinese Tradition**  
The act of marrying a deceased man’s spirit to become his widow (守寡) is a deeply traditional Chinese practice, rooted in the idea that a woman’s honor and social identity are tied to her husband. In pre-modern China, a woman could be encouraged or even forced to maintain chastity after her fiancé’s death. Here, Xin Ruyin chooses this path voluntarily, not out of social pressure but out of guilt and debt—a reimagining of the custom through the lens of mortal-stream tragedy.

**The Fu Clan’s Overreaction**  
Qi Yunxiao’s death arose from a cascade of small mistakes: a few nervous responses, a clan’s suspicion, and a deadly escalation. This is classic mortal-stream logic. The Fu Clan is not cartoonishly evil; they are a powerful clan defending their reputation, and their Core Formation elder was only deployed after heavy losses. The disaster is the product of systemic pressure, not deliberate malice—which makes Xin Ruyin’s demand for genocide all the more chilling.

**The Barter of Knowledge for Vengeance**  
Xin Ruyin’s gift of her life’s work is not generosity; it is an investment. In xianxia, knowledge is often the most durable currency, and by giving away the very tools that could help Han Li advance, she is buying a future probability: the chance that he will one day be powerful enough to fulfill her wish. This is a classic mortal-stream transaction.

## Reading Guide
This is a chapter that tests Han Li’s emotional distance. He was never a close friend of Qi Yunxiao, but the man’s death still weighs on him. More importantly, Xin Ruyin’s request forces him to consider a debt that may last decades. Accepting her knowledge binds him to a promise he may not be able to keep, and it reinforces the core theme of the Mortal Stream: every free meal comes with a receipt, and the receipt may call for blood. Pay close attention to Han Li’s wordless reactions—the slight changes in his tone, the moment of hesitation, the cold edge that creeps into his voice when he realizes the stakes. The real story here is not the request itself, but how he processes it and what his silence implies.
