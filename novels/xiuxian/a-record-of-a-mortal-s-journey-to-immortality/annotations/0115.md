## Chapter Overview

Alright, buckle up, fellow Daoists, because the gloves are officially off. Chapter 115 throws us straight into the ultimate face-off: Han Li versus the widows of the late Doctor Mo. And trust me, it’s not the tearful, dramatic confrontation you might expect. Our favorite pragmatic cultivator walks into the lion’s den, finds the ladies dressed in full mourning regalia, and immediately shifts from “respectful disciple” to “cold-eyed negotiator.” The chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s brand of psychological warfare. Forget flashy spells or flying swords—this is a battle fought with words, silences, and the sheer audacity to sit down in a room full of people who want you dead and stare them down.

## Key Plot Points

- Yan Shi dismisses Yan Ge, ordering Han Li to enter alone, a clear signal that a private confrontation is at hand.
- Han Li uses his spiritual sense to scan the room before entering, confirming no hidden assassins await him, a perfect example of his survival-first paranoia.
- He enters to find the women dressed in white mourning robes, confirming his suspicion that Doctor Mo’s letter revealed the truth of his death.
- Han Li’s momentary shock quickly gives way to cold calculation; he unceremoniously takes a seat and forces the women to make the first move.
- The chapter reveals the distinct personalities of the four wives through their reactions: Li’s righteous fury, Liu’s curious interest, Yan Shi’s chilling control, and Wang’s sudden, piercing question.
- Han Li refuses to be drawn into a war of words, bluntly stating he doesn’t want to hear “pleasantries.”
- Second Mistress Li directly accuses him of murder, but Han Li gives an ambiguous answer: “You could say he died by my hand, or you could say he committed suicide.”
- Fifth Mistress Wang, previously silent, interrupts with a request for a full account of Doctor Mo’s death, offering a seemingly fair middle ground.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Social Tribulation: A Different Kind of Danger** – This chapter is a textbook example of a “Social Tribulation.” In many xianxia stories, the protagonist’s primary challenge is fighting monsters or evil villains. In *RMJI*, the protagonist must navigate complex human relationships and political hierarchies with the same cold calculation as a physical battle. Han Li doesn’t draw a sword here; he uses information asymmetry, social bluffing, and an intimidating persona to control the pace of the interrogation. He knows that a war of words with four furious women is a losing battle, so he dictates the terms of engagement, forcing them into a defensive posture.

**The ‘Family’ as a Political Entity** – In ancient Chinese society, especially within powerful households or sects, the family was not merely a unit of sentiment; it was a political and economic entity. The wives of a dead patriarch like Doctor Mo are not just grieving widows; they are administrators of his remaining assets (the sect resources in Divine Hand Valley) and protectors of their own and their daughters’ futures. Their “interrogation” of Han Li is not just about justice; it’s about figuring out what this dangerous young man knows and what he wants, in order to secure their own position. Han Li recognizes this instantly, which is why he treats the confrontation as a negotiation, not a plea for forgiveness.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for one of the most tense and satisfying dialogue-driven scenes in the early novel. The key here is to watch Han Li’s *strategy*. He walks in expecting an ambush, finds a political trial, and instantly adapts. Note how he uses his body language—the uninvited seat, the direct stare—to assert dominance without saying a word. His answer to Li’s accusation (“died by my hand or committed suicide”) is a brilliant piece of social jiu-jitsu. It’s not a lie, but it’s deeply ambiguous, and it puts the burden of proof back on the women. Ask yourself: is Han Li trying to hide the truth, or is he trying to control *how* the truth is revealed? His final goal here isn’t to be proven innocent; it’s to become someone they need to bargain with rather than someone they can simply execute.

## Deep Dive: The Art of Social Bluffing

This chapter is a textbook demonstration of “Social Bluffing,” a core survival tactic in the Mortal Stream. It’s not about lying outright, but about using ambiguous information and an opponent’s own guilt to force a favorable outcome. Han Li has no army and no proof. His only weapon is the fear and uncertainty in the room. By acting unafraid and in control, he makes himself a problem that cannot be solved with a simple accusation. He forces his opponents to consider the possibility that a violent confrontation might be more costly than a negotiation. For new readers, this is a key shift: in this world, the smartest fighter isn’t the one with the biggest sword; it’s the one who controls the flow of information in the room.
