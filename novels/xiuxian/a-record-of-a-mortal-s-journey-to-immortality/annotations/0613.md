## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a classic Mortal Stream interlude — a quiet breather where the protagonist doesn’t fight or scheme against others, but methodically patches up the cracks in his own survival. Han Li has finally found a safe corner (a shabby herbal garden managed by the aloof Mu Peiling) to settle down. But safety, for Han Li, is a full-time job. He immediately carves out a hidden cave dwelling, layers on concealment formations, transplants his precious Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng, and begins a slow, agonizing process of expelling the Wind Spirit Restriction that has been lurking in his body since his clash with the ninth-grade Wind-Splitter Beast. At the same time, he starts refining the Crying Soul Pearl inside his beast — a ticking clock he can no longer ignore. The chapter is a masterclass in the quiet, obsessive maintenance that separates a long-lived cultivator from a dead one. No fireworks, no drama — just sweat, calculations, and the grim satisfaction of crossing items off an invisible to-do list.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li receives his assignment from Mu Peiling: a green jade slip for herb cultivation, a blue slip for the Profound Ice Art (a weak but easy-to-breakthrough technique), and a yellow token for the garden’s outer formation. He registers the token’s mediocrity without complaint — being underestimated is an asset.
- He immediately transforms the small rocky hill behind the garden into a concealed cave dwelling, laying down high-grade formation arrays. The process is executed with practiced efficiency.
- He transplants the Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng into a small garden within the new dwelling, surrounding it with strict prohibitions. The stakes: this herb is essential for his Nascent Soul breakthrough.
- He moves the slumbering Crying Soul Beast into a spirit beast chamber. The beast has been unconscious since absorbing massive amounts of Yin beast souls in the Netherworld Domain — it is undergoing an advancement. Han Li decides to take the opportunity to refine its Crying Soul Pearl for himself, fearing he may lose control of the beast otherwise.
- He begins the laborious process of forcing out the Wind Spirit Restriction, bit by bit. The method is crude and extremely painful — he sweats for two hours just to expel a few drops of gray liquid — but it works. He estimates six months to fully cleanse it.
- He divides his time: daily evil qi expulsion, studying the Spirit-Binding Formation, refining the Crying Soul Pearl, and using the Green Liquid to cultivate Rainbow Silk Grass for his Gold Devourer Beetles, which seem to be approaching a new evolutionary phase.
- The chapter ends with a time skip of several months. The routine continues. Mu Peiling visits only twice, sees the garden is fine, and leaves without conversation. Han Li has achieved a tenuous, temporary pause — but the work is far from over.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Wind Spirit Restriction (风灵劲) is a notorious xianxia concept: a lingering evil energy that slowly damages a cultivator’s meridians from within, functioning as a delayed-detonation attack. Han Li’s method of forcing it out via pure mana manipulation is crude but effective — it mirrors ancient Chinese medical practices of “discharging evil qi” through qi circulation and acupoint pressure. The Evil-Repelling Divine Lightning (辟邪神雷) he uses to contain it draws from Daoist thunder magic, where pure Yang lightning is considered the ultimate purifier of malevolent forces.

The Crying Soul Pearl (鸣魂珠) is a condensed energy core inside the Crying Soul Beast, itself a rare creature that feeds on ghostly yin energy. Refining the pearl gives the cultivator control over the beast’s growth, but comes with risks — severe headaches, possible backlash. In Daoist inner alchemy, such external substances must be “refined” and integrated into the cultivator’s own energy system, often at great cost. The Spirit-Binding Formation (拘灵阵) is a specialized formation for concealing spiritual energy, echoing folk traditions of geomancy and talismanic sealing.

## Reading Guide

This is a “maintenance” chapter, but don’t skip it — it’s a love letter to the Mortal Stream philosophy. Notice how Han Li never celebrates. Every solution creates new risks: the Crying Soul Beast might become uncontrollable, the Wind Spirit Restriction must be expelled slowly, the Spirit-Binding Formation needs to be learned. He treats his own body as a system with multiple vulnerabilities and works them one by one. The time skip is a signal that Mortal Stream progress comes from steady, unseen labor, not flashy breakthroughs. Pay attention to the cold efficiency: he doesn’t agonize over whether to refine the pearl — he weighs risks and decides. And he doesn’t stop to enjoy the quiet. He immediately moves to the next item on his list. That is the true face of cultivation in this world: not epiphany, but endless, patient toil.

### The Mortal Stream Workflow

If you’re new to the genre, this chapter demonstrates a core trope: the “gradual elimination of hidden debuffs.” Han Li treats his body like a combat vehicle that needs constant repairs. The Wind Spirit Restriction is a flat tire; the Crying Soul Pearl is a faulty engine sensor. No Mortal Stream mechanic fixes these things with a magic wave — he has to crawl under the hood, sweat, and swear. And he never takes a vacation. This is what sets the protagonist apart from a mortal: not destiny, but relentless, paranoid maintenance.
