## Chapter Overview

The demonic path cultivators have barely cleared the Ghost Mist when old tensions—and old greed—spill into the open. Nascent Soul Extreme Yin, the Confucian-robed Qingyi Hermit, and the brutish powerhouse Man Brutal Beard engage in a tense, layered negotiation over how to split a treasure none of them have even laid hands on yet. Han Li, stuck in the middle as ‘Extreme Yin’s disciple,’ is discussed like a chess piece whose only value is his utility, not his opinion. On the other side of the canyon, the righteous-path alliance led by Wan Tianming quietly strategizes its own approach, fully aware that the Void Heaven Cauldron must not fall to the demonic path. The chapter ends with the Ice and Fire Paths finally opening, setting the stage for the trial to come.

## Key Plot Points

- **Predatory generosity.** Man Brutal Beard, having noticed something unusual about Han Li’s cultivation technique, probes him openly. The confrontation is defused when Qingyi Hermit and Extreme Yin reveal—via voice transmission—a key secret about Han Li’s value to the inner-hall mission.
- **The share-splitting standoff.** Man Brutal Beard insists on taking half the treasure for handling Wan Tianming. Extreme Yin and Qingyi counter with a four-way split. After crisp verbal fencing and a show of united front, they settle on splitting it five ways with Man Brutal Beard taking two shares, Extreme Yin, Qingyi, *and* Han Li taking one each. The exchange drips with the cold transactional logic of the Mortal Stream.
- **Han Li’s suppressed calculation.** Throughout the entire negotiation, Han Li is treated as an asset, not a person. His internal reaction—resentment, stifled fury, but never panic—is a masterclass in enforced passivity. He does not protest; he files the moment away.
- **The righteous path’s parallel planning.** Wan Tianming reveals his trump card: the Golden Silk Silkworm fed with the rare Azure Heaven Herb, giving him a 30-40% chance of retrieving the cauldron. His primary concern is not the treasure itself, but preventing the demonic path from gaining it.
- **The Ice and Fire Paths open.** With a mountain-splitting tremor, two passages appear: Profound Ice Path and Magma Path. Each path teleports entrants randomly, scattering the cultivators. Han Li chooses the Magma Path, accepting a cold-defying jade pendant from a patronizing Extreme Yin.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Voice transmission (传音 / chuán yīn).** This is a staple communication technique in xianxia, allowing cultivators to project their voice directly into another person’s mind via spiritual sense. In a high-stakes negotiation, it is a weapon of information asymmetry: two parties can openly discuss secrets right in front of a third without them ever hearing a word. The protagonist, Han Li, is systematically excluded from the exchange, reinforcing his position as a junior whose consent is irrelevant.

**The “equal share for a junior” is never about fairness.** In mortal-stream politics, proposing that a Core Formation junior gets the same cut as Nascent Soul elders is not a gesture of equality—it is a tactical provocation. It forces the greedy party (Man Brutal Beard) to reject it, thereby allowing the more reasonable five-way split to be accepted as a “concession.” Han Li’s theoretical share is not about his worth; it is a bargaining chip in a negotiation between higher powers.

**The Ice and Fire Paths as a dispersal mechanism.** Large-scale trials in xianxia almost always include a “scattering” gate. The narrative purpose is twofold: first, to isolate the protagonist so they must overcome challenges alone, and second, to randomize encounters, preventing the powerful from simply herding their allies together. Han Li choosing the Magma Path over the Ice Path is a quiet, tactical decision that signals self-reliance over dependency on borrowed treasures.

## Reading Guide

If you came here for Han Li to show anger, bite back, or declare independence from his so-called “master”… well, welcome to the Mortal Stream, where that’s a luxury he cannot afford. What makes this chapter so uncomfortable and so rewarding is Han Li’s total silence. He is discussed, appraised, written into a settlement, and handed a token like a favored dependent—and he takes it all without a word. That is not weakness. That is discipline. In a world where words can get you killed and protest can cheapen your value, Han Li listens, files, and waits.

Also worth noting: the sheer confidence of these Nascent Soul cultivators. They are dividing a treasure they have not seen, in a trial they have not entered, as if the outcome is already certain. That hubris is not just arrogance—it is a dark mirror of the cultivation world’s fundamental rule: the higher you rise, the more you believe the universe bends to your will. And the fall, when it comes, is always from a terrifying height.
