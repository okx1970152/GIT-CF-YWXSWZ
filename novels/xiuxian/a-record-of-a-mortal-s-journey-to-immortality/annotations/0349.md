## Chapter Overview
The storm has finally broken. After a quiet interlude of domestic concerns, Han Li returns to Yellow Maple Valley to a ticking time bomb. He reconnects with his old acquaintance, the little old man of the Hundred Medicine Garden, only to deliver a piece of intelligence that shatters the man’s composure—and then the bells begin to toll. This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s “before the fall” cadence: the calm of a cultivator tending to his assets, the revelation of a world-breaking secret, and the immediate, chilling confirmation that the war has already reached their doorstep.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li returns to his cave dwelling and immediately checks on his two White Spider eggs, which have successfully hatched. The spiders imprint on him as their parent, demonstrating the effectiveness of his blood-restriction control.
- He replenishes his talisman stores and, leaving Qu Hun behind, heads to Yellow Maple Valley to assess the situation.
- He visits the Hundred Medicine Garden and informs the little old man (Brother Ma) of his encounter with the Imperial Spirit Sect cultivator, revealing the explosive suspicion that Spirit Beast Mountain may be a demonic path infiltrator.
- The little old man is visibly shaken but skeptical, noting that no bad news has reached the Valley from the front.
- Just as Han Li processes the possibility that he was fooled by the dead cultivator, the Dragon-Alarm Bell rings eighty-one times—an irreversible signal of sect-wide annihilation—confirming that the situation is, in fact, far more dire than anyone anticipated.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Dragon-Alarm Bell (惊龙钟 / Jing Long Zhong): This is not a casual warning gong. In xianxia sect lore, bells are tiered by the number of tolls, with specific counts signaling specific levels of threat. The eighty-one tolls referenced here carry the weight of a “sect-destroying calamity” (). For a sect as established as Yellow Maple Valley, this means the enemy has already won, and all that remains is to organize a final resistance or evacuation. The number eighty-one itself resonates with Daoist numerology—it is the product of nine times nine, representing the highest yang number, often used in rituals of ultimate finality. When a reader sees an eighty-one toll bell, they should understand that the game has already changed irreversibly.

**The Strange Insect Register (奇虫榜): This is a recurring world-building device in Mortal Stream xianxia. It is not a comprehensive biological catalog but a competitive ranking of rare spirit creatures, much like a “top 100” list for monster cores. A creature like the Blood Jade Spider, ranked around 100th, is already a rare gem, while the Golden-Back Demon Mantis, ranked 73rd, is even more formidable. The register serves as a quick shorthand for readers to gauge the value and threat level of an exotic beast without needing an extended digression.

**Essence Blood (精血) as Soul Tether: Using one’s Essence Blood is more than a simple magical contract. In Chinese occult traditions and xianxia alike, Essence Blood carries a fragment of the cultivator’s fundamental life force and spiritual essence. To use it as a control mechanism is to voluntarily sacrifice a part of oneself—permanently—to bind another being. The fact that the spiders recognize Han Li as “parent” rather than “master” hints at the intimate, irreversible nature of the bond: the cost is eternal, and the loyalty is absolute.

## Reading Guide
This is your classic “the calm before the storm” pivot point. For new readers: if you’ve been wondering where the war plot was going, this chapter is your answer. For returning fanatics: savor the contrast between Han Li’s moment of genuine fondness for the two spider hatchlings and the immediate, crushing weight of the bell. The Mortal Stream excels at making you care about a character’s small victories precisely so that the collapse of the larger world hits harder. Also, note how Han Li’s instinct to bypass the main camp and check his own base first was not paranoia—it was survival engineering. He didn’t just dodge a trap; he positioned himself to hear the news from a trusted source before the official messengers could even arrive. That’s textbook Mortal Stream thinking: never rely on the system to tell you the truth when you can get it from a friend faster.
