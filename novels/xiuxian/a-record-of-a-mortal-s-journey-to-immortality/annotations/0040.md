## Chapter Overview

Ever wondered how a sword technique that doesn't need true qi got locked up in Seven Peaks Hall for over a century? Chapter 40 finally spills the backstory—and it's a tragic tale of a crippled genius, an entire sect's golden age, and a manual so niche it stayed untouched for generations. Meanwhile, Han Li pulls an all-nighter, not cramming for a test, but mentally downloading a library of assassination techniques. This chapter is a masterclass in *A Record of a Mortal's Journey's* worldbuilding: the Blinking Sword Art isn't just a random power-up—it's a broken relic born from desperation, and Han Li's the first person desperate enough to actually want it.

## Key Plot Points

- The origin of the Blinking Sword Art is revealed: a Seven Mysteries Sect elder, crippled in his prime, secretly creates it over decades to compensate for his lost internal energy, elevating his forced adaptation into legendary legacy.
- This elder once single-handedly saved the collapsing Seven Mysteries Sect from annihilation during its decline, using the very art he created—an irony that gave the technique its fabled reputation despite never being cultivated afterward.
- Han Li, uninterested in the art's history or glory, only cares that it gives him a fighting chance against Doctor Mo—yet another character-defining moment of pure, cold pragmatism layered under a scrim of philosophical detachment.
- Rather than physically copying any manuals, Han Li memorizes every line using his photographic memory—a tactic that keeps Doctor Mo's surveillance none the wiser, preserving both his secrecy and his growing arsenal of hidden advantages.
- Han Li reads through the entire night, completely absorbed in the study of killing techniques; his realization that he has changed from a simple village boy into someone fascinated by murder methods is presented as an unflinching, neutral observation, without a shred of moral hand-wringing.
- At dawn, he wraps up by washing his face and running a single circulation of the Eternal Spring Art to eliminate fatigue—then grimly acknowledges that mastering the Blinking Sword Art will take years he simply does not have.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

A founding elder of the Seven Mysteries Sect who "lost his internal energy" in Jianghu combat represents a uniquely cruel reversal of fortune in Chinese martial fiction: strength is everything, and once it is gone, even the highest-ranked master must live in constant fear of exposure and replacement. The elder's decade-long secret project to build a "no-qi sword art" reflects a core xianxia fantasy: that a sufficiently driven genius can overcome even a broken cultivation foundation through strategy, obsession, and sheer unconventional thinking. His eventual one-man stand against the sect's besiegers became the stuff of legend, not because he was righteous, but because he was effective—a stark reminder that in the Jianghu, effectiveness always trumps intentions. Finally, Han Li's decision to memorize rather than copy mirrors a tactic found in countless Chinese martial and sect dramas: the safest place for dangerous knowledge is inside your own mind, where no physical evidence can be seized, no betrayal can expose it, and no master can steal it in a night raid.

### Photographic Memory

A side effect of reaching the fifth layer of the Eternal Spring Art, allowing Han Li to perfectly recall and mentally replay any martial art he reads or observes without writing it down.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a perfect example of why *A Record of a Mortal's Journey* feels so grounded even when it's telling a legendary origin story. The Blinking Sword Art isn't handed down by a martial god or discovered in a hidden tomb—it was cobbled together by a desperate, paranoid old man who lost everything and refused to accept it. The narrative treats the elder's tragedy with respect but no romanticism, and Han Li's complete indifference to the art's backstory is exactly right. He's not a historian, he's a survivalist. If you're here for the big revelations, don't worry—this chapter quietly lays the foundation for a technique that will define much of Han Li's early combat identity. But the real payoff is the mounting pressure: Han Li has a treasure trove of deadly knowledge, but no time to master it. The clock is ticking, and Doctor Mo isn't getting any less dangerous.
