## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a pure information-explosion milestone. Han Li finally deciphers both golden pages from Li Huayuan’s cave, and what he finds will define his entire cultivation path for the rest of the arc. First up: a complete, thirteen-layer Azure Origin Sword Art that reaches all the way to Spirit Transformation—no holes, no missing pieces. Then comes the real bombshell: an auxiliary “Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art” that claims to bypass the Core Formation bottleneck… by demanding the practitioner disperse their cultivation three times and start over. Han Li’s emotional rollercoaster—from ecstasy to horror to calculating acceptance—is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s trademark trade-off logic. Every shortcut has a price, and this one wants three decades of your life.

## Key Plot Points
1. Han Li uses sustained Azure Origin Swordlight to charge the first golden page, which absorbs it greedily until the page finally releases countless tiny golden characters.
2. The characters swarm into his body through his hand, then rush into his brain, causing excruciating pain. The transmission nearly kills him but completes just in time.
3. After three days of meditation to recover, Han Li deciphers the content: a complete Azure Origin Sword Art from Layer 1 to Layer 13, capable of reaching Spirit Transformation stage.
4. Attached to the sword art is the “Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art”—a bizarre auxiliary technique that promises to bypass the Core Formation bottleneck.
5. The catch: the practitioner must disperse their cultivation three times (at Layers 4, 7, and 10) and rebuild from zero, compressing their true essence to higher purity each time.
6. Even the creator admits the success rate is only 50%, but Han Li immediately accepts the gamble—it’s better than the near-zero odds of normal Core Formation.
7. Han Li tests the second golden page and receives the “Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords” refining manual—a set-type flying sword treasure requiring at least twelve swords.
8. Though useless before Core Formation, the manual cements Han Li’s decision to cultivate the Azure Origin Sword Art first, with the option to disperse later if needed.
9. The chapter ends with a clear decision tree: cultivate to peak Foundation Establishment, try normal Core Formation first, and only disperse if that fails.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Dispersing Cultivation (散功):** In xianxia logic, scattering your cultivation base is usually a suicidal act—you lose years or decades of progress in an instant. The Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art weaponizes this flaw into a feature: by intentionally destroying and rebuilding your foundation, you can compress your mana to a density that virtually guarantees Core Formation. It’s the cultivation equivalent of breaking a bone to reset it stronger. The risk, of course, is that you simply run out of lifespan before finishing the third rebuild.

**Set-Type Flying Swords:** Most magical treasures are single objects. The Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords are different—they form a synergistic set, like a swarm that attacks as one. The minimum is twelve swords, but the manual theoretically allows thirty-six or even seventy-two. This is a classic xianxia escalation: a single flying sword is deadly; a synchronized swarm is apocalyptic. The trade-off is material cost and the immense true essence required to control them all.

**The Fifty-Percent Gamble:** In Mortal Stream logic, a coin-flip odds is a bargain. Normal Core Formation for a cultivator with False Spiritual Roots is basically impossible without a miracle. Facing a 50% chance after immense effort versus a 0.001% chance after waiting forever—Han Li doesn’t hesitate. This is the core philosophy of the genre: take the active risk, even if it hurts, rather than sit back and hope fate smiles on you.

## Reading Guide
*Buckle up, cultivators—this chapter is where the long game gets real.* The Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art is *not* a feel-good power-up. It’s a grueling, multi-decade commitment that asks Han Li to destroy his own progress three times on the off-chance that the fourth rebuild sticks. But that’s precisely why it fits the Mortal Stream so perfectly: there are no free lunches, only expensive investments with uncertain returns. Pay close attention to the tone shift in Han Li’s internal monologue—he goes from despair to manic joy to white-faced panic to cold calculation. The man never stays emotional for long; he always pivots back to logistics. Also, keep an eye on the Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords—they’re a promise of future power that won’t pay off for a very, very long time. This is the kind of chapter that sets up payoffs hundreds of chapters later.
