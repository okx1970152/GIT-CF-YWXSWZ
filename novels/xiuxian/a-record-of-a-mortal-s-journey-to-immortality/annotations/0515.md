## Chapter Overview

After a deadly expedition that nearly cost him everything, Han Li returns to his Sacred Mountain cave dwelling—but there’s no time to catch his breath. The war between Star Palace and the Rebel Alliance is about to boil over, and our favorite cautious cultivator realizes the clock is ticking on two fronts: one, the condition Star Palace set for using the Outer Star Seas teleportation array (kill a fellow cultivator of equal rank), and two, the inevitable leak of the Void Heaven Cauldron’s whereabouts once those Nascent Soul monsters escape the pocket dimension. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream logistics: Han Li doesn’t scheme for power or glory—he schemes for *exit strategies*. With the market shut, his preparations validated, and a juicy bit of overheard gossip about a bribeable teleportation guard, the chapter sets up an entire new phase of the story with quiet, cold precision.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li enters Heaven Star City using the yellow ring given to him by the gatekeeper elder, who informs him of the half-day free movement period and that the ring will summon him when the battle begins.
- He returns to his cave on the 39th layer of Sacred Mountain, noting the terrifyingly tight security—spiritual sense sweeps from multiple Core Formation cultivators—but the yellow ring allows him to pass unhindered.
- After securing his Gold Devourer Beetles and harvesting his spirit herbs, Han Li lies down to formulate an escape plan. He mentally acknowledges that killing a Rebel Alliance cultivator of equal rank (Core Formation) is doable, but his real fear is that news of the Void Heaven Cauldron will leak before he can use the teleportation array.
- He explicitly decides not to wait passively for Star Palace’s summons. Instead, he plans to scout the teleportation array’s security and attempt to sneak through if possible.
- The next morning, he flies to the Star Void Hall on the 50th layer, feigning a casual pass. His spiritual sense probe detects a blue-yellow double restriction protecting the inner hall, which he cannot penetrate without setting off an alarm.
- While circling back, he overhears a sound transmission conversation between two Foundation Establishment rogue cultivators. One of them has a distant relative guarding the teleportation array, who demands 5000 spirit stones per person for unauthorized passage—an exorbitant fee the men are debating whether to pay or not.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Sacred Mountain (圣山) & Layer System**: Heaven Star City is built around the central Sacred Mountain, which is divided into multiple numbered layers. The higher the layer, the more restricted the access. Han Li’s cave on the 39th layer places him among mid-to-upper-tier Core Formation cultivators, while the 50th layer houses critical strategic assets like the Star Void Hall teleportation array. This layered geography is a recurring xianxia trope: the mountain is a physical manifestation of the sect’s hierarchy and resource distribution.

**Outer vs. Inner Star Seas (内海/外星海)**: The Chaotic Star Sea is split into the Inner Sea, dominated by Star Palace and the Rebel Alliance, and the Outer Sea, a frontier zone teeming with rogue cultivators, demon beasts, and ancient ruins. For Han Li, escaping to the Outer Sea is a tactical withdrawal—it trades the security of civilization for the freedom of the lawless frontier, a classic Mortal Stream gambit of accepting a lesser danger to avoid a greater one.

**Sound Transmission Eavesdropping**: In cultivation novels, private communication via sound transmission (传音) is considered secure unless the eavesdropper has a vastly superior spiritual sense or a rare top-tier eavesdropping art. Han Li’s ability to overhear two Foundation Establishment cultivators’ transmissions without their knowledge is a quiet flex of his Core Formation-level divine sense advantage—a classic example of how cultivation rank disparity can be exploited for information warfare.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a “breather” episode that feels anything but relaxed. It’s a transitional piece that bridges the explosive Void Heaven Palace arc and the coming Star Sea war arc, but Wang Yu delivers it with such tight, pragmatic tension that you barely notice the shift. What makes this chapter sing for Mortal Stream fans is Han Li’s *decision-making process*: he doesn’t wait for news; he immediately scouts, analyzes, and prepares a backup plan. That overheard conversation about the 5000-spirit-stone guard is a gift from the plot gods—a legitimate, in-world escape hatch that doesn’t feel like deus ex machina because the universe has already established that the Star Sea’s institutions are full of corruptible functionaries. Keep an eye on that little thread; in a Mortal Stream story, information overheard by accident often becomes the most reliable path forward.
