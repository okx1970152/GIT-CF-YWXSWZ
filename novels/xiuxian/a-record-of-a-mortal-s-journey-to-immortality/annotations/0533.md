## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because Han Li has officially graduated from “that weird guy with beetles” to full-blown boogeyman status. In this short but punchy chapter, we watch from the sidelines as three seasoned rogue cultivators stumble upon our favorite green-liquid hoarder while he’s calmly extracting a demon beast’s soul. The moment they suspect who he *really* is, their blood runs cold. And that’s before Han Li—without even breaking a sweat—sends his three-colored beetle-spears after them. The chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream world-building: showing how a reputation, once earned, becomes a weapon deadlier than any sword.

## Key Plot Points
A trio of veteran Outer Star Seas cultivators—a vulture-faced old man (Brother Qiu), the man surnamed Min, and a fierce-looking hothead named Xuan—discover Han Li alone on a coral island, fresh off killing a Glass Beast. They initially debate whether to ambush him, but the vulture-faced elder’s suspicion turns to dread when he connects the blue-clad youth’s appearance, green flying swords, and soul-extraction hobby to the rumored “Insect Devil.” The three immediately try to flee in different directions, but Han Li, having spotted them, summons his three-colored beetle swarm and condenses it into three giant spears. The final scene shows the trio flying meekly back toward Han Li, three-colored collars locked around their necks, looking like men who’ve already surrendered to the inevitable. Han Li, ever the polite predator, simply asks them why they ran.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Rumor Mills in the Cultivation World**  
This chapter is a goldmine for showing how intelligence and misinformation travel in the Immortal Realm of xianxia. The three cultivators don’t *know* Han Li; they only know the *stories* about him. The “Insect Devil” moniker has become a self-reinforcing legend: some say he’s a Nascent Soul monster in disguise, others claim his cultivation is weak but his beetles are an invincible trump card. In the Mortal Stream, a terrifying reputation is a force multiplier—it makes enemies hesitate, ensures witnesses panic, and often hands you the victory before a single blow is exchanged. The way the three debate the source of Han Li’s power (is he high-realm, or just the bugs?) perfectly illustrates how the Dark Forest works: fear of the unknown is the most efficient deterrent.

**The Power of Spear-Formation**  
Han Li’s trick of condensing his beetle swarm into giant projectiles is a classic xianxia tactical evolution. In earlier chapters, he releases the swarm as a chaotic tide; here, he’s refined their use into precision-guided weapons. This mirrors the broader cultivation principle: raw power gets refined into technique, and technique into art. The chapter doesn’t dwell on the mechanics, but sharp readers will note that Han Li’s command over the Gold Devourer Beetles has reached a new level of granularity—he can now shape them on the fly into flying spears that track targets across multiple trajectories.

## Reading Guide
This is a “reputation victory” chapter—one of those quiet, deeply satisfying moments in a Mortal Stream novel where the protagonist doesn’t need to throw a single punch (or, well, more than one) to win. The real drama is entirely in the three cultivators’ internal panic. Notice how Han Li’s final question—“Do you recognize me?”—is delivered with that trademark unsettling calm. He’s not angry; he’s curious. He wants to know exactly how much the rumor mill has distorted his profile. It’s a reminder that for Han Li, information gathering never stops, not even after dinner. Also, keep an eye on those three-colored collars—they’re a classic Han Li move: contain, neutralize, then question. He’s not planning to kill them right away, which means this meeting might be far more significant than a simple bug-snack encounter.
