## Chapter Overview
Roll up your sleeves, fellow Daoists—Han Li has just scored a top-tier flying artifact, and the chapter starts with pure, unadulterated *flex energy*. The Divine Wind Boat makes his old leaf-shaped junk feel like a donkey cart, and for a few glorious paragraphs, our cautious protagonist is actually *enjoying* the ride. But of course, the Mortal Stream never lets you coast. A random pillar of light nearly drills a hole through his new boat, and suddenly Han Li finds himself hovering over a full-blown war zone: a handful of desperate Foundation Establishment cultivators vs an endless tide of puppet beasts. This chapter is a masterclass in “situational awareness before heroism”—Han Li doesn’t charge in; he hides, observes, and calculates.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li entrusts the Ink Flood Dragon materials to Shopkeeper Xu, who identifies them instantly (and nearly has a heart attack). After half a month of refinement, Han Li receives the Divine Wind Boat—a speed-focused flying artifact that leaves his old junk in the dust.
- On the return trip, a massive yellow beam nearly obliterates him. He deploys an azure-red smoke cloud (a defensive trick from earlier chapters) and stabilizes to assess the situation.
- Below, five Foundation Establishment cultivators are besieged by an army of mechanical beasts and puppet soldiers. The puppets are endlessly replenished from the woods, grinding the cultivators through sheer attrition.
- One cultivator breaks formation and tries to flee, only to be instantly incinerated by a super-sized beam from the forest. The others’ morale plummets further.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Puppet Refinement (机关傀儡术):** In cultivation fiction, puppets are not mere dolls; they are complex constructs powered by spirit stones or formation cores. Higher-tier puppets can match human cultivators in combat, and the ability to mass-produce them makes a sect’s puppet army a terrifying strategic asset. The puppets here are clearly low-to-mid-tier—they lack intelligence but make up for it with relentless numbers.

**Accidental Stray Fire (误伤):** One of the unwritten rules of the cultivation world is that stray attacks are a common occupational hazard. Han Li’s near-miss is not a personal assassination attempt (yet)—it’s just bad luck. The fact that he immediately suspects an ambush shows how deeply ingrained his paranoia is, a direct product of the Dark Forest mindset.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a textbook example of the “quiet victory” phase wedged between conflicts. Han Li’s biggest win is securing the Divine Wind Boat—a mobility upgrade that will serve him for a long time. But instead of rushing home to gloat, he just *happens* to stumble into someone else’s nightmare. The tension here is purely observational: Han Li is a spectator, not a participant. That’s a deliberate narrative choice. The Mortal Stream rewards readers for enjoying the logistics—the careful planning and asset accumulation—as much as the explosive fights. Also, notice the shift in tone: a rare moment of genuine satisfaction (the boat) immediately followed by cold caution. That’s Han Li’s emotional range, and it’s perfect.
