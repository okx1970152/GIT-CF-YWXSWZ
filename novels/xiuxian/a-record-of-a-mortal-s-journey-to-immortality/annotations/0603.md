## Chapter Overview
Boom—Wen Tianren, the "Number One Core Formation Cultivator" and proud heir to the Six Paths Sect, just got deleted like a save file. Han Li doesn't give him a dramatic duel; he gives him two thrown daggers and zero dialogue. This is peak Mortal Stream efficiency: the moment the target becomes a loose end, the protagonist ties the knot without a second thought. Meanwhile, across the map, Yuan Yao makes a terrifyingly logical choice—to dual-cultivate a forbidden ghost-human art with the soul of her deceased senior sister, Yan Li, in a cave swimming with Netherworld energy. The chapter is lean, cold, and delivers exactly what the title promises: winds rising overseas, and the true beginning of the Yin-Yang Reincarnation Art. Get ready, folks—the game board just got a lot smaller.

## Key Plot Points
- Wen Tianren emerges from the mist and immediately recognizes Han Li. Han Li doesn’t waste a breath—first strike. The small sword is blocked by an inner armor, but Han Li adapts instantly, using the flexible spirit beast sinew to curve a second dagger behind Wen Tianren’s neck. Kill confirmed in two moves.
- Han Li loots the corpse with practiced, untroubled efficiency: storage pouch, Four Symbols Pan Dragon Belt, green inner armor, and several treasures. Zi Ling arrives and reveals she was traveling with Wen Tianren under duress, not by choice.
- Han Li accepts Zi Ling’s explanation and allows her to join his group with Mei Ning. No grudges, no drama—just cold, practical alliance logistics.
- At the same time, deep in a Netherworld cave, Yuan Yao and her senior sister Yan Li (now a ghost spirit) decide to begin cultivating the Yin-Yang Reincarnation Art. Yuan Yao’s Golden Core was shattered by the Soul Reunion Art, and this forbidden human-ghost technique is her only path back to power.
- The chapter ends with both pairs about to walk very different, equally irreversible paths.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The "Four Symbols Pan Dragon Belt"** — *Sixiang Panlong Dai* (四象蟠龙带). The term "Four Symbols" (*Sixiang*) comes from Chinese cosmology, originally representing the four directional guardians: Azure Dragon, Vermilion Bird, White Tiger, and Black Tortoise. In xianxia, any treasure carrying this name is expected to provide balanced, all-weather protection. Here, the belt’s four embedded pearls—Wind-Avoiding, Flame-Repelling, Water-Shedding, and Dust-Warding—are a literal interpretation of that idea, making it a perfect tool for navigating the chaotic elemental hazards of the Netherworld Domain.
**The Yin-Yang Reincarnation Art** — *Yin-Yang Lunhui Jue* (阴阳轮回决). This technique is the series’ infamous human-ghost dual cultivation art, and it’s the exact opposite of any warm, romantic "dual cultivation" trope. In Chinese folk religion, the boundary between the living and the dead is strictly policed; crossing it always demands a price. Here, the price is structural: the cultivator forfeits their own chance at reincarnation, and the ghost becomes permanently anchored to the mortal plane. Yuan Yao is betting everything on the hope that the dense Netherworld energy will let her rebuild a shattered Golden Core—but the rules don’t get relaxed just because you’re desperate.
**Why no one at the Qingyang Sect ever cultivated it** — The chapter explicitly mentions that the Qingyang Sect possessed this technique for ages but never used it. That’s the xianxia equivalent of a flashing neon skull-and-crossbones sign. The fact that Yuan Yao dives in anyway tells you exactly how cornered she feels—and how ruthless the cultivation world’s bottleneck economy really is.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s efficiency philosophy. Wen Tianren’s death should feel *fast*—because that’s all the narrative weight the world gives to a dead loose end. He was powerful, proud, and well-known, and he still got reduced to a footnote in two sword-throws. The real meat here is Yuan Yao’s parallel choice. Watch how the novel frames her decision: not as a tragic noble sacrifice, but as a cold, practical audit of her options. She’s not weeping over her shattered core—she’s calculating the odds. That’s the *true* xianxia survival instinct.
Also, pay attention to Zi Ling’s entrance. She’s one of the few characters in this novel whose "beauty" is described as a tactical asset rather than a romantic trait. When a cultivator’s reputation is built on something as intangible as appearance, you should be asking: *what did she trade to get that reputation?*
