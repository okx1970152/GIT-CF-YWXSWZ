## Chapter Overview
Alright, buckle up fellow Daoists—chapter 217 is what the Mortal Stream is all about: the calm, dangerous quiet after the storm. Han Li has just survived Foundation Establishment and emerged as an official high-tier disciple, but does he get to kick back and enjoy it? Absolutely not. Instead, we get a painfully detailed look at the cursed Azure Origin Sword Art he’s now forced to practice, his unexpected breakthrough to the fourth layer (by accident, thank you very much), and a heart-to-heart with old Steward Ma that reveals the sect’s dark little secret about where that sword art really came from. This is less “thrilling action” and more “anxious power analysis with a side of institutional horror,” and honestly, it’s even better for it.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li reviews the Split-Image Sword Technique, which promises increasingly powerful phantom images. But he’s deeply suspicious because no Foundation Establishment cultivator in the sect has ever trained it beyond a few layers.
- Despite his caution, the residual medicinal power from his Foundation Establishment pill forces an accidental breakthrough to the **fourth layer** of the Azure Origin Sword Art. He immediately panics about potential side effects.
- He tests the **Sword Shield**, a spiked protective barrier that can retaliate against attackers, but has no opportunity to try it in combat.
- Leaving the Earth Flame chamber, he runs into the Ugly Man, whose stunned expression is a private victory for Han Li.
- Returning to the Hundred Medicines Garden, Steward Ma (now Senior Martial Brother Ma) is shocked speechless by Han Li’s Foundation Establishment.
- Ma explains that since Han Li is now at the same cultivation level, they should address each other as equals. The old man then showers him with detailed advice about sect benefits—including the right to build his own cave.
- Finally, Han Li asks about the Azure Origin Sword Art. Ma drops a bombshell: it’s a **fragment** from a destroyed sect, only nine layers remain of an original thirteen, and the later stages are completely lost. It can only take a cultivator to Core Formation at best, and true masters of the art don’t exist.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The **“fragment technique”** is a recurring structural feature in Mortal Stream novels, and this chapter gives us a textbook version. The Azure Origin Sword Art isn’t just inferior because it’s harder to get materials for—it’s literally *missing half its content*. The original was thirteen layers, but Yellow Maple Valley only grabbed the first nine when they destroyed the Sword Origin Sect. This is a brilliant dark-forest detail: even the “cool inheritance” you get from a sect is usually spoil of war, incomplete, and deliberately kept just this side of useless to prevent any single disciple from becoming too powerful. It’s not just that Han Li chose badly—the system was rigged from the start.

The **Split-Image Sword Technique** also deserves special mention. It’s a low-upgrade, high-potential ability that scales with the user’s cultivation. But its fatal flaw is that each additional sword image costs more layers of dedication to the art—and since the art runs out of road at Core Formation, no one with real ambition would ever invest that deeply. It’s the perfect Mortal Stream trap: a skill tree that looks great on paper but leads to a dead end.

## Reading Guide
This is a “quiet victory” chapter, and readers who love logistics and slow-burn worldbuilding will eat this up. Pay close attention to Ma’s demeanor here—he’s one of the few characters in Han Li’s orbit who isn’t trying to extract something from him. His jealousy is openly stated, but he still gives Han Li genuine, useful advice. That’s a rare commodity in this world, and the narrative rewards us by letting him deliver a massive lore bomb about the sword art without any strings attached. Also, note how Han Li’s internal monologue shifts: he’s no longer a helpless Qi Condensation disciple worrying about survival. Now he’s calculating *institutional costs*—the risks of investing time in a dead-end technique, the political implications of announcing his breakthrough, and the quiet pleasure of seeing an old acquaintance’s stunned face. This is the new normal.

**Proverbs and Idioms**
When Han Li says he “gritted his teeth and attempted to train” in the sword art despite knowing it was problematic, that’s a classic Mortal Stream posture. This world doesn’t allow you to say “no” to a bad deal if it’s the only deal on the table. The art might be a trap, but the alternative (having no combat technique at all) is definitely death. Similarly, the phrase “silently memorizing it a few times” is pure Han Li—he never stops gathering information, even when he’s “celebrating” a breakthrough.
