## Chapter Overview

The title says it all—this chapter isn’t about intrigue or sect politics. It’s Han Li in pure predator mode, using formations, puppets, and the Heaven Vial to turn the Outer Star Seas into his own personal hunting ground. But the five-year time skip does something more important than just dispatching a kill count: it drills into the reader that **competence in the Mortal Stream is not talent—it is thousands of cold, methodical repetitions**. Han Li doesn’t get stronger because of a sudden breakthrough; he gets stronger because he spent half a decade doing the same dangerous thing over and over until the danger became routine. The chapter is a masterclass in **quiet victory logistics**—showing the cost (puppets destroyed, formation tools lost, close calls with higher-grade beasts) as vividly as the profit (hundreds of demon cores, a terrifyingly honed combat instinct). By the end, the reader isn’t just impressed by the haul—they *feel* the years of exhaustion and near-death that bought it.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li’s first major test: two fifth-grade demon beasts arrive at Red Coral Island simultaneously. He restrains himself, waiting for both to enter the formations before acting—showing that patience, not aggression, is his real weapon.
- The trapped beast, a Thousand-Eyed Beast, fights back with raw beams and a protective green barrier, but fails against the combined assault of Qu Hun’s Chaos Origin Bowl, a hundred puppet-beam volleys, and Han Li’s Red Thread Escape Light Needles.
- Over a month on Red Coral Island, he kills more than a dozen fifth-grade beasts using a single Rainbow Garment Grass seedling ripened to three hundred years, learning the dangerous upper limit of the grass’s scent.
- A near-disaster with a sixth-grade Cock’s Comb Flood Dragon forces him to recalibrate his bait strategy.
- When the local beast supply dries up, he moves deeper into the Outer Star Seas without hesitation—no sentiment, no memorial service for the old spot.
- Five years pass in a montage: hundreds of fifth-grade beasts killed, most puppets destroyed, the Reversed Five Elements Formation sacrificed to a seventh-grade beast, and Han Li’s combat skills forged into something lethal and instinctive.
- The chapter ends on a note of hard-won capability: Han Li can now face an ordinary fifth-grade beast alone without fear.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter illustrates a core dynamic of the Outer Star Seas economy: **demon beasts are not just monsters—they are walking resource deposits**. A fifth-grade demon core is the raw ingredient for high-grade pills, and demon beast materials (hides, bones, eyes, stingers) are valuable crafting materials. Han Li’s five-year hunt is essentially a mining operation—extracting value from a mobile, dangerous resource node. The efficiency of this extraction depends entirely on three things: formation trapping (which prevents escape), bait management (which controls the range and grade of attracted beasts), and overwhelming localized force (which minimizes combat risk). Also worth noting is the fatal flaw of the Rainbow Garment Grass: it does not discriminate by beast grade. Higher-grade bait automatically attracts higher-grade threats, forcing the hunter into a constant cost-benefit recalibration. This is the Mortal Stream’s way of saying that no shortcut is free—scale your bait too high, and the system punishes you by bringing something you can’t kill.

### Five-Year Time Skip

A narrative compression technique in xianxia where extended training or hunting periods are summarized to show accumulated power and resource costs without daily-by-daily detail.

## Reading Guide

If you’re the kind of reader who skips training montages, don’t skip this one. This isn’t a power fantasy; it’s a **maintenance log from hell**. Every puppet lost, every formation tool shattered, every ugly escape from a seventh-grade beast is *explicitly named* in the cost column. The Mortal Stream doesn’t let the protagonist win for free—it makes the reader do the math alongside the character. The five-year time skip works because the narration doesn’t wave it away; it says “here’s what it cost, and here’s what you got.” The demon core pile is satisfying precisely because you know how many formation breakdowns, close calls, and sleepless nights each one cost. Also pay attention to the tone shift in the final line: “Now, Han Li could face an ordinary fifth-grade beast completely alone, with no fear and full composure.” That’s one of the rarest emotional states in the Mortal Stream—not joy, not excitement, but **quiet, earned confidence**. Savor it.

## Deep Dive: The Thousand-Eyed Beast & Outer Star Seas Beast Taxonomy

The Thousand-Eyed Beast (*千目妖*) is one of the most common fifth-grade creatures introduced in the Outer Star Seas arc. With a pincushion-like body covered in dozens of mobile eyes, it is a textbook example of xianxia beast design: grotesque, functional, and built around a single combat gimmick. Its ability to focus multiple eye-beams into one powerful ray makes it threatening at range, but its low intelligence and predictable defensive strategy (green barrier + panic curl) make it a perfect target for a prepared hunter. The beast’s taxonomy hints at a larger pattern: the Outer Star Seas are full of creatures that evolved under high-pressure, resource-competitive conditions, and their biology reflects that—heavy defense, simple attack patterns, and no social cooperation. Han Li isn’t just killing beasts; he’s reading a textbook on the ecosystem.
