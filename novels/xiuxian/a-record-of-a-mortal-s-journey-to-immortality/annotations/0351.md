## Chapter Overview

The evacuation is on, but Han Li’s mind is running its own parallel calculation. Patriarch Linghu picks the "resurrection sparks," gives them half a day to pack, and the flight out begins. But the demonic path was one step ahead: a new message arrives, splitting the retreat into a sacrificial rearguard and a main column. Han Li is pulled into the blocking team—and this, for him, is the crack he was looking for. A moment of genuine uncertainty about whether staying with Yellow Maple Valley will cost him his Core Formation window turns into cold, concrete resolution: he’s not going all the way.

## Key Plot Points

- Patriarch Linghu selects the evacuation candidates—primarily those with high aptitude or rare skills—and declares them the sect’s “sparks” for future rebuilding. He warns that the outer line is already being bypassed and that anyone causing evacuation chaos will be executed personally.
- Han Li returns to his cave to secure assets: he retrieves hidden talisman pouches, collected rare herbs, then destroys the Spirit Eye Spring chamber to keep it from demonic path hands. He also dismantles one set of the Reversed Five Elements Formation, then brings down the entire peak to bury the cave.
- Back at the assembly hall, he finds several hundred Qi Condensation disciples—also part of the core evacuation group, including Xiao Cui’er. The short old man proudly notes she’s on the list for her aptitude.
- Just as the group clears the Taiyue Mountain Range, a flying-sword message arrives. The news: the demonic path has already split into two forces—one besieging the valley, the other chasing the evacuees.
- The leading cultivator, Martial Uncle Huang, forms a “blocking team” of ~20 Foundation Establishment cultivators (including Han Li, excluding the short old man) to draw pursuit and buy time for the main column. He hands out jade slips with meeting points for survivors.
- Han Li scans the jade slip without deep memorization—he already knows he won’t be rendezvousing. The chapter closes on his decision: this is his escape hatch from a doomed sect trajectory.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Retreat and Sacrifice Economy** – In cultivation-fiction sect warfare, evacuation is rarely clean. The sect’s logic here is cold: the outer disciples hold the line so the core “sparks” can flee. This isn’t noble sacrifice; it’s a logistical calculation. The sect treats its lower-tier members as consumable delay mechanisms, while the escapees are assets to be preserved at the cost of the rest. The “blocking team” is essentially a self-selecting death squad, but the protagonist sees it as an exit ticket, reinforcing the Mortal Stream rule that every crisis contains a window for the clever to pivot.

**Spirit Eye Spring and Resource Denial** – Han Li’s act of destroying his Spirit Eye Spring chamber is a textbook Mortal Stream decision: if he can’t take it, his enemies won’t get it. This isn’t vandalism born of spite—it’s a calculated act of strategic denial. In a world where every drop of concentrated qi is a weapon for the opponent, leaving a resource untended is the same as gifting it to your hunters.

**Flying-Sword Communication** – The “flying-sword message” (飞剑传书) is a classic xianxia communication device: a cultivator sends a spiritual-sword carrying a jade-slip message over long distances. It’s fast, secure against interception by low-level cultivators, and vanishes after delivery. The appearance of such a message mid-evacuation signals that the strategic situation has changed at the highest level in real time.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a quiet pivot point. Han Li doesn’t fight a dramatic battle or have a revelation under enemy fire. He simply *calculates* that staying with Yellow Maple Valley will cost him his chance at Core Formation—and that losing the sect in the chaos is a better bet than following its institutional rubble to a foreign land. Notice the way the chapter structures his decision: the fear of the Patriarch’s monitoring, the regret at not fleeing earlier, and the cold resolution born from scanning the jade slip he barely memorizes. That’s the Mortal Stream at work. Every mundane act—packing pouches, smashing a spring, ignoring a map—is a strategic move on a board only he can see.

If you’re new to the series, this chapter is also a masterclass in “show, don’t tell” for the protagonist’s mindset. Han Li never says “I’m leaving,” but the internal sequence he runs—the estimation of years lost, the irreversibility of the Core Formation window, the weight of sect duties in a new country—is louder than any desertion speech. Watch the friction between his visible compliance and his silent audit of escape routes. That gap is where the real story lives.

Also: the short old man saving himself and his apprentice by staying off the blocking list? That’s the Dark Forest law in miniature. Everyone who survives this chapter did so by letting someone else be named first.
