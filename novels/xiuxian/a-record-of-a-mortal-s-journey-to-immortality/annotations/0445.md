## Chapter Overview
Fresh from the uneasy alliance forged with the mysterious black-robed figure, Han Li, Fairy Ziling, and the old man Ge Li plunge into the Ghost Mist. Instead of a heroic charge, our protagonist coolly notes that someone else has cleared the path—so he might as well take advantage. The chapter unfolds as a slow, tense march through a living mist, where the only company is death and decay. Han Li’s mind is not on camaraderie; it is on risk calculations, hidden threats, and the bitter lesson inscribed in every discarded skeleton. When they stumble upon the remains of a once-famous cultivator, the vision of a glorious path to immortality is violently shattered. This is the Mortal Stream at its most visceral: the value of a life is weighed by its utility, and every corpse is a data point.

## Key Plot Points
- The black-robed figure enters the Ghost Mist with the Crying Soul Beast, leaving a visible trail.
- Han Li decides to follow that trail, openly acknowledging he is exploiting another’s labor—no false pride here.
- Ge Li hesitates to admit he is riding coattails, but Han Li’s bluntness forces him to follow anyway.
- The three cultivators each deploy unique protective measures: Ge Li’s red umbrella, Fairy Ziling’s orb barrier, and Han Li’s subtle Gold Lightning Bamboo sword qi.
- Han Li notes the Ghost Mist’s intelligence and hostility, reinforcing that this is a living environment, not a passive fog.
- A sudden crunch underfoot reveals a pile of old bones wrapped in a blue robe—the remains of a Core-formation candidate named Jade True Person.
- Ge Li identifies the bones and reflects on Jade True Person’s superior cultivation and treasures, yet here he lies, defeated.
- Han Li uses this discovery to warn his companions that a powerful entity must be nearby, because the path has been too clean.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Ghost Mist (鬼雾)** – In Chinese folklore, miasmatic fogs often serve as barriers between the living and the dead. In *RMJI*, this mist is not a passive weather event but an animate entity that drains life force and traps souls, preventing reincarnation. The “soul-trapping” aspect echoes Buddhist and Daoist ideas of hungry ghosts and lingering resentment: a soul that dissolves into the mist is cut off from the Wheel of Rebirth, a fate worse than death for cultivators who spend their lives pursuing transcendence.

**Void Heaven Palace (虚天殿)** – The mysterious ancient site mentioned in passing. “Void Heaven” suggests a structure that exists in a *liminal space* between worlds, much like the Buddhist concept of *shunyata* (emptiness) or the Daoist “cavern-heavens” (洞天). Experienced readers will note that any mention of the Void Heaven Palace is *never casual* in this novel—it always signals a major upcoming arc with extreme risk and reward.

**Jade True Person (玉真人)** – The title “真 人” (True Person) is a Daoist honorific for a perfected being. In a Mortal Stream context, it is almost *sarcastic*: this “true person” could not even pass the first trial. The name reinforces the novel’s cynical theme that titles and fame mean nothing against the world’s brutal mechanics.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s “silent tension” writing style. There are no flashy fights, but every paragraph is heavy with dread. Pay attention to how Han Li’s mind never stops processing: he calculates the value of the path, factors in the possibility of a stronger enemy, and treats a dead man’s bones as tactical intelligence rather than occasion for pity. His speech to Ge Li is not a philosophical debate—it is a warning dressed as a question. The Ghost Mist behaves like a predator, and the human reaction to it is a litmus test for each character’s survival instincts. Ge Li loses face; Fairy Ziling loses color; Han Li loses none of his focus. This is the kind of quiet but relentless world-building that defines *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality*. Enjoy the slow burn—it is setting up a storm.

## Expedition Dynamics in the Mortal Stream
This chapter is a textbook example of how expeditions work in Han Li’s world. There is no fellowship; there is only temporary alignment of interests. The black-robed figure does not help them out of kindness—he is simply not bothered by their presence. Han Li does not thank him—he notes the practical advantage and takes it. Ge Li chafes at the asymmetry but has no choice. Every interaction is a silent negotiation of leverage and face. When a cultivator falls, he is not mourned—he is analyzed. This is the true essence of the Mortal Stream’s “adventure”: a high-risk resource-gathering mission where trust is a currency spent only when absolutely necessary.
