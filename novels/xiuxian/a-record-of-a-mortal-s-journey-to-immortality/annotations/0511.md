## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow Daoists—this chapter is all about the quiet, tense storm that builds before the real thunderclap. Our favorite mortal-stream survivor, Han Li, finally slips out of the Rebel Star Alliance’s port, only to find that the Alliance’s hospitality was a thin veil over a much more cautious surveillance. A Nascent Soul old monster’s spiritual sense sweeps over him; a green-robed elder dissects his killing intent like a butcher appraising a prize cut of meat. And just when Han Li thinks he’s home free, two Core Formation cultivators appear with a not-so-polite request: *please come with us.* The chapter isn’t a fight scene—it’s a masterclass in tension, paranoia, and the ever-present calculus of who will strike first, and when.

## Key Plot Points
- A green-robed elder and a burly bodyguard discuss Han Li’s identity as Elder Han of the Sublime Sound Sect. The elder reveals a stored portrait jade slip, confirming Han Li’s match.
- The elder analyzes Han Li’s abnormally dense killing intent, deducing it comes from slaying many same-realm cultivators. He notes the killing intent is permanent and overwhelming, capable of disrupting opponents’ wills mid-combat.
- The elder concocts a two-bird scheme: leverage the upcoming war to pressure the Sublime Sound Sect, force Zi Ling the Fairy to be presented to their Young Master, and in the same stroke, pressure Elder Han into revealing his secret for gathering killing intent.
- Han Li is swept by a Nascent Soul cultivator’s spiritual sense at port. He remains perfectly still and calm, gambling on his genuine identity to avoid suspicion.
- Half a day later, two Core Formation First Layer cultivators intercept Han Li under the pretext of hunting Star Palace remnants. Their polite words carry a clear ultimatum: cooperate or be forced.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Power of Killing Intent (煞气, Shà Qì):** In many Chinese cultivation stories, “killing intent” is more than just a mood or a menacing glare—it’s a tangible, quasi-spiritual force that accumulates on a cultivator’s body through the act of taking lives. The green-robed elder’s analysis here is textbook xianxia logic: weak killing intent dissipates over time, but strong, concentrated killing intent from slaying same-rank opponents becomes a permanent aura. This isn’t just flavor text—it has real combat implications: it can weaken illusions, suppress mind-control spells, and even seize an opponent’s will at a critical moment. It’s the cultivation world’s version of a bloodstained veteran effect—you carry the gravity of every life you’ve taken, and those with the right arts can *see* it.

**Spiritual Sense Probes (神识探测):** An experienced reader will recognize this scene as a classic “security checkpoint” in cultivation politics. When a major power lets you through a gate with a token, you’re not truly free—you’re just past the first layer of inspection. The Nascent Soul cultivator’s probe from afar is the *second, real* screening. Han Li’s decision not to use a disguised identity is rooted in a hard truth of the xianxia system: most disguise arts cannot fool high-level spiritual sense. Feigning transparency is often safer than playing hide-and-seek with a monster who can see through the curtains.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in the kind of tension that *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey* does so well: not the clash of spells and sword lights, but the cold, calculated weight of observation and suspicion. Pay close attention to how the green-robed elder uses killing intent as an *information weapon*—he’s reading Han Li’s combat history from a single aura reading, and he’s already building a plan around it. This is classic mortal-stream villainy: the enemy isn’t just strong; he’s a schemer who treats every piece of intelligence as a lever to press later. Meanwhile, Han Li’s silent endurance of the Nascent Soul probe, followed by his methodical flight, shows that his survival instinct remains razor-sharp: he doesn’t panic, doesn’t deviate, and doesn’t let a single intercept change his course. The stage is being set for a confrontation that hasn’t happened yet, but every word of this chapter sharpens the knife.
