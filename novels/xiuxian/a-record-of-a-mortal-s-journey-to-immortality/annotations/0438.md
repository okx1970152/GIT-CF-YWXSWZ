## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists, because Han Li has just walked into a hornet’s nest made of jade. After a brief, frosty social call with an old acquaintance, our favorite herb-gardener-turned-Core-Formation-recluse finally sets his sights on the grand prize: the legendary Void Heaven Palace. But this isn’t a sightseeing tour. The buildup is pure Mortal Stream gold—Han Li doesn’t just bumble in; he spends over a week lurking in the shadows, analyzing traffic patterns, and observing the competition like a paranoid survivalist casing a casino. And when he finally steps inside? The palace itself immediately starts flexing on him with soul-crushing architecture and forbidden zones that make his eyes bleed. The tension cranks up not with explosions, but with dreadful silence and the weight of power that even a Core Formation cultivator can barely comprehend.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Debt is Settled**: Han Li gives Wen Qiang and Wen Siyue a curt, polite exit. He’ll put in a good word with Zi Ling, but makes zero promises. It’s not coldness—it’s calculated distance. He’s already done the math on how much emotional investment an old surface-level acquaintance is worth.
- **One Month Later, The Jurisdiction Changes**: The narrative skips smoothly to the open sea where the Void Heaven Palace hangs in the sky. Han Li arrives, checks his map, and finds… nothing. Classic Han Li—he distrusts empty space before he trusts a treasure signal.
- **The Stakeout**: After spotting the palace hidden in the clouds, Han Li doesn’t charge in. He vanishes, observes a red-haired elder enter with the same map, and waits a week to confirm the pattern. This is a textbook Mortal Stream expedition protocol: confirm the entry conditions before burning your own chance.
- **Entering the Golden Barrier**: Using his map as an entry token, Han Li passes through the golden light barrier without incident. The mechanism is confirmed: the map *is* the key.
- **The Name That Hurts to Look At**: Han Li reads the three silver ancient characters “Void Heaven Palace” and his eyes literally ache. The calligraphy is so sharp it attacks his divine sense—a chilling statement that this palace operates on a completely different power scale.
- **The Oppressive Passage**: The entry corridor is narrow, tall, and completely enchanted. Spiritual sense is blocked. Han Li has no choice but to walk blind—and he does so without panic, but with extreme caution.
- **The Grand Hall Revealed**: Han Li emerges into a massive hall filled with carved jade pillars. Dozens of cultivators are perched atop them. And among them, he recognizes several faces. The chapter ends on a classic cliffhanger of looming entanglement.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**“Void Heaven Palace” (虚天殿 / Xu Tian Dian)**: “Void Heaven” implies a transcendent, empty firmament—a space that exists outside normal worldly geography. In xianxia cosmography, such a name signals that this palace is not built, but *manifested*; it may be a remnant of a forgotten era, where the laws of physics and cultivation operate differently.
**Ancient Gold Script vs Silver Ancient Script**: Han Li is stunned by the silver ancient text above the door. In xianxia lore, ancient scripts (especially those from pre-human epochs) often carry residual Dao intent. Just *looking* at them can harm a cultivator if their cultivation realm or soul strength is insufficient. This is not a simple signboard—it’s a power filter.
**One Zhang**: Approximately 3.33 meters. Han Li notes the palace is 100 zhang tall (~330m). The passage is 2–3 zhang wide (~7–10m) but 30–40 zhang high (~100–130m). This extreme aspect ratio is deliberately disorienting, meant to crush a cultivator’s spatial confidence before they even reach the main hall.
**The Map as Token**: Han Li uses the brocade map as a key to pass through the golden barrier. In xianxia, such map-tokens are not just navigation aids; they are metaphysically tied to the original architect’s will. Anyone without the map cannot enter—this is systemic gatekeeping, not a puzzle to be solved.
**Beasts on Jade Pillars**: The hall is filled with engraved mythical creatures. In Chinese tradition, such carvings are never merely decorative—they often anchor formations or function as dormant guardians. The fact that Han Li doesn’t know some of them suggests these are primordial beings, now extinct or exclusive to forgotten realms.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in *atmospheric dread through architecture*. Forget the monsters; here, the *room* is the threat. Pay close attention to Han Li’s reconnaissance phase—the week-long stakeout isn’t filler; it’s character establishment. He treats a legendary treasure hunt like a military operation, because in the Mortal Stream world, it *is*. The palace’s oppressive geometry (narrow, tall corridors), its active rejection of spiritual sense, and its name that damages the eyes all tell you: this is not a place you conquer. It is a place you survive through—if you’re lucky. Also, note the familiar faces at the end. If you remember any past grudges or debts from the earlier arcs, get ready for them to come back and bite.
