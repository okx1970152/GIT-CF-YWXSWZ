## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, can you feel it? The unmistakable, deeply satisfying sensation of a *quiet victory phase*. After all the scrambling in the Blood Forbidden Trial and the cold cruelties of the mortal realm, our boy Han Li has finally had a solid twenty-year streak of doing exactly what he does best: logistics, profits, and slowly, *very* slowly, building up his power base. This chapter is the calm before the storm—the whirring of a machine being assembled in secret. We get to see Han Li as a small-town shopkeeper, dealing with the delightful chaos of mortal customers and the satisfying click-clack of a successful business model. But underneath the rustic facade of "Little Bamboo Green," a Core Formation cultivator is grinding through a twenty-year training arc. He’s not just whittling wood; he’s mastering mid-grade talisman crafting, delving into formations, and preparing the materials for his ultimate trump card—the Green Bamboo Flying Cloud Swords. It’s a montage of patience, and if you're new to the Mortal Stream, this is the kind of carefully paced, deeply satisfying setup that makes the later explosions of power feel so, so good.

## Key Plot Points

- **A Twenty-Year Time Skip:** The narrative leaps forward two decades, establishing a long period of stability and quiet cultivation for Han Li in the market town.
- **The "Little Bamboo Green" Economy:** Han Li opens a small shop to experience the mundane world. Over time, it gains a reputation for selling cheap but effective magical tools and, most importantly, occasional mid-grade talismans.
- **Mid-Grade Talisman Grind:** We get a brutally honest look at a Core Formation cultivator's perspective—even with his high realm, the success rate for making mid-grade talismans is a pitiful 1 in 20 or 30, a testament to the systemic difficulty of high-level crafting.
- **Mastering a New Domain:** Han Li doesn't just rest on his laurels. Using Qi Yunxiao and Xin Ruyin’s insights, he begins learning the art of refining formation flags and plates.
- **The Vial’s Final Preparations:** The “sixth stalk of Gold Devourer Divine Thunder Bamboo” reaches its ten-thousand-year maturation point, signaling the end of Han Li’s worldly experiment.
- **Return to the Cave & The Refining Begins:** Han Li closes his shop and returns to his secluded cave. After three days of mental preparation, he begins the painstaking process of refining the Divine Thunder Bamboo, successfully purifying it into a concentrated emerald liquid.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Quiet Victory” Phase in Mortal Stream Storytelling**
In many fantasy novels, a protagonist’s power increases are often treated as happenstance: they stumble into a treasure, get a sudden epiphany, or receive a cheat. In *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey to Immortality*, however, power is engineered. A “quiet victory” phase like this one is a hallmark of the Mortal Stream. It’s where the protagonist spends years *earning* his strength through methodical research, tedious resource accumulation, and mastering new skills. This chapter is a perfect example: Han Li doesn’t just magically learn talisman crafting. We are explicitly told about his material stockpile, the practice runs, the abysmal success rates, and the patience required. This section is a love letter to the virtue of patience, and it makes every future spell he casts and every treasure he forges feel earned, not gifted.

**The Economics of Mortal-Cultivator Boundary**
Han Li’s twenty-year detour is not just a training arc; it’s a psychological and spiritual recalibration. He purposefully re-immerses himself in mortal life. This is a subtle nod to a core xianxia tension: the danger of losing one's humanity. By observing the “partings and reunions, joy and sorrow” of mortals, Han Li acquires a unique kind of worldly wisdom (*renqing shigu*). This experience enhances his state of mind (*xinjing*) and cultivates his Dao heart, preventing him from becoming a cold, detached machine. In the Mortal Stream, the strongest cultivators are often those who understand the frailties of the world they want to transcend.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the eye of the hurricane. After an intense story arc, this is a long, satisfying exhale. Don’t skim it just because there’s no combat. This is where Han Li’s power for the next arc is being *built*. Pay close attention to the details: the fact that he sets aside specific rare materials from his earlier loot for talisman-making. This isn't just flavor text—it's a perfect example of his long-term planning. He thinks about what he’ll need years in the future before he even needs it.

Also, note the quiet character moment at the end. He feels a “trace of reluctance” leaving the shop after twenty years. This is a rare, extremely contained moment of emotional vulnerability in our otherwise calculating protagonist. It doesn't slow him down, but it acknowledges that even Han Li has roots forming. It makes his cold, calculated decisions more impactful because we know he made them not from ignorance, but from a place of experience and deliberate choice. Get ready—the workshop phase is ending. The hammer is about to fall on the raw materials.

## Behind the Prod: A Note from the Author

And here we have a small, lovely piece of Chinese web novel history embedded in the text! The author, Wang Yu, has included a production note at the end of this chapter: a note from the author apologizing for the late chapter, explaining that the weather is getting cold and he can no longer stay up late, and advising the readers to go to bed early. This is a direct window into the grueling work-cycle of a Chinese web novelist, who often writes two 3,000+ character chapters a day. It’s a small moment of humanity from the person behind the story, and it directly connects the reader to the real-world pressures of serialized storytelling. It’s also a reminder that this masterwork of worldbuilding and cold calculation was forged in the late-night, cold-weather hustle of a single, very dedicated author.
