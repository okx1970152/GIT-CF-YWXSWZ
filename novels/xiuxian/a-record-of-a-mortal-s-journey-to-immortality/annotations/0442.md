## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists! Here we are, inside the Void Heaven Palace, where the old monsters of the Nascent Soul realm are playing their favorite game: chess with each other’s lives—and yours. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream politics. The demonic big shots (Extreme Yin, Man Brutal Beard, Green Ease Hermit) are huddling over voice transmissions, trying to rope Madam Wen into their scheme to steal the Void Heaven Cauldron. Meanwhile, the orthodox faction (Wan Tianming) sits in meditative silence, and the Star Palace’s Law Enforcement Elders show up to drop the rules: “We’ll stop you from killing each other in the outer hall, but once you step inside the inner hall, we won’t lift a finger.” Han Li, our quiet observer, files every glare and whisper as tactical data, hoping the old monsters get so tangled up that they forget about him. The atmosphere is thick with information asymmetry, hidden trump cards, and zero-sum calculus.

## Key Plot Points
- Extreme Yin Patriarch reveals he knows Wan Tianming may have obtained a Golden Silk Silkworm, a rare creature capable of neutralizing the cauldron’s defenses.
- Man Brutal Beard openly mocks Extreme Yin’s plan, pointing out the absurdly high death rate of Nascent Soul cultivators in the inner hall across previous expeditions.
- Extreme Yin offers to split all remaining treasures (except the cauldron + one Heaven-Mending Pill) between Man Brutal Beard and Green Ease Hermit in exchange for cooperation.
- Madam Wen refuses flatly, citing good judgment; she’s only there for spirit herbs and won’t risk the inner hall.
- The Star Palace Law Enforcement Elders arrive: one kindly (but sharp), one cold. They outline the supervision rules—outer hall only—and hint that they will not intervene in inner hall deaths, no matter how brutal.
- Profound Bone Saint warns Han Li via voice transmission that the Star Palace deliberately balances orthodox and demonic power, often eliminating participants from both sides in murky incidents.
- Han Li’s strategic position is reinforced: he wants the old monsters to focus on each other so he can navigate the inner hall without becoming a target.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Void Heaven Palace & the Inner Hall Divide**: This dungeon is split into two tiers. The outer hall is a “safe zone” where the Star Palace enforces a no-kill rule (to preserve cultivator stock?), but the inner hall is a free-for-all deathtrap. In *RMJI*, this is not a game mechanic—it’s a social filter: only the truly desperate, confident, or reckless dare enter the inner hall. The Star Palace’s refusal to intervene there is a cold structural choice that maximizes attrition among high-level cultivators, preventing any single faction from becoming dominant.

**Heaven-Mending Pill (补天丹)**: A legendary pill said to *compensate for incomplete Five Elements spiritual roots*. In a cultivation world where Spiritual Roots determine your glass ceiling, a pill that can patch your elemental deficits is arguably the most valuable single resource for anyone stuck at a bottleneck. This is why Extreme Yin is willing to sacrifice all other treasures just to get it. The name “补天” echoes the Chinese myth of Nüwa mending the sky—a grand metaphor for repairing an inherent flaw in one’s foundation.

**Primordial Ancient Treasures (洪荒古宝)**: These are not your usual magic tools. The term “洪荒” refers to a mythical primordial era before the current heavens and earth stabilized—a time of pure, wild power. Ancient treasures from that age are often cruder in form (no absorption into the body, clunky activation) but far more potent than refined modern alternatives. Holding one is like carrying a quantum cannon in a crossbow arms race.

## Reading Guide
Keep your eyes on the *information warfare* here. Extreme Yin’s attempt to recruit allies is a transparent bid to counterbalance Wan Tianming’s rumored Golden Silk Silkworm. But Man Brutal Beard is no fool—he smells weakness and demands heavy guarantees. The Star Palace Elders are the true wildcards: they are not friends, they are “ecosystem wardens” who trim the grass on both sides. Han Li’s takeaway is perfect: let the Nascent Soul seniors tangle themselves in their schemes, and he’ll slip through the cracks. The real battle hasn’t even started—it’s happening in whispers. Also, note the chapter-ending author note: Wang Yu’s plea for recommendation tickets is a classic web serial moment—he’s directly asking readers to support the story, blending the line between narrative and creator. It’s wholesome, and we keep it in the translation as a nod to that culture.
