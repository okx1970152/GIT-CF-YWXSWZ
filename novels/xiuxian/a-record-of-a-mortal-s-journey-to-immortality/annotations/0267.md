## Chapter Overview

Han Li is putting down roots at the spirit mine, and his dual cultivation projects—the *Great Derivative Art* and puppet-making—are both hitting fascinating inflection points. But the war is never far away. This chapter delivers a brutal, high-resolution update on the state of the conflict between the Six Demonic Sects and the Seven Great Sects, revealing a war of attrition so savage that even Core Formation cultivators are dying in the double digits. The quiet before the storm at the mine finally breaks with a shrill alarm: the demonic path has arrived.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li reaches a critical milestone in the *Great Derivative Art*: the first layer is nearly perfected, and he notes with quiet bewilderment that this technique, unlike his other cultivation methods, comes to him with extraordinary ease.
- He successfully refines his first Grade Two puppet beast after over a dozen failed attempts, though its quality and power still lag behind the Thousand Bamboo School’s craftsmanship.
- A major info-dump on the war reveals its shocking scale: over ten thousand cultivator casualties, seven or eight Core Formation deaths, and the revelation that the Spirit Beast Mountain Sect was originally a sleeper agent planted by the demonic path thousands of years ago—but chose betrayal over obedience.
- The intervention of cultivators from Yuanwu and Zijin Countries prevents the Seven Sects’ collapse, turning the war into a grinding stalemate.
- The spirit mine receives a second wave of reinforcements, including the ugly man Zhong Wu from the Blood Trial, who can barely hide his envy when he’s forced to address Han Li as “Senior.”
- The chapter ends with the arrival of the demonic path at the mine, a force of roughly twenty to thirty Foundation Establishment cultivators.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Sleeper Agent” Trope in Xianxia:** The Spirit Beast Mountain Sect’s backstory as a millennia-old plant of the Imperial Spirit Sect draws on a recurring theme in Chinese cultivation fiction: the long con. Unlike the quick betrayals of Western intrigue, xianxia sleeper agents can span entire dynasties, maintained through generations of cultivation inheritance. The twist here—that the “agent” chose its own path after thousands of years—adds a layer of realpolitik: loyalty, even planted loyalty, has an expiration date when power and identity are involved.

**The War of Attrition Economy:** The shift from decisive battles to small-scale raids on resource nodes (like this spirit mine) is a classic xianxia scale-down. When direct conquest fails, the war becomes a supply-chain war: cripple the enemy’s spirit stone production, starve their formations, and wait for their Core Formation elders to collapse from spiritual exhaustion. It’s not epic; it’s logistical.

**Han Li’s Cultivation Asymmetry:** One of the most quietly fascinating details in this chapter is Han Li’s remark that the *Great Derivative Art* comes to him effortlessly while his other techniques (Azure Origin Sword Art, Five Elemental Dao) are a struggle. This isn’t an inconsistency; it’s a character clue. The *Great Derivative Art* relies on mental agility and divine sense, which align perfectly with Han Li’s core strengths: caution, calculation, and extreme situational awareness. His talent isn’t in raw qi manipulation—it’s in *processing power*.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the calm before a storm, and the storm has finally arrived. The war update is essential not just for worldbuilding but for raising the stakes: Han Li is no longer in a secluded valley practicing alchemy; he’s now a fixed asset on a board where every move matters. The reappearance of Zhong Wu is a satisfying callback to the Blood Trial arc, and his barely concealed envy is a subtle reminder of how far Han Li has come—and how far he still has to go before he can truly stand above the Core Formation carnage being described. Brace yourselves: the fight for the mine is about to begin.
