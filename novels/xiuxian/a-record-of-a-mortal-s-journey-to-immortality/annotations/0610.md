## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to the moment of formal entry! After practically *scamming* his way through the Blood Forbidden Trial and escaping the utter chaos of the Huanglong Mountain siege, Han Li is now standing at the doorstep of a *real*, established mountain sect: the Luo Yun Sect. But this isn't your typical welcome wagon. This chapter serves as the ultimate Xianxia gatekeeping montage, reminding us that even "getting in" is a multi-layered social and psychological obstacle course. We meet a spectrum of sect folk—from the openly predatory Yellow-Robed cultivator who wants to use the rookies as slave labor, to the mysterious, coughing Senior Brother Miao who’s far more perceptive than he looks. It's a masterclass in how the "Dark Forest" shifts from the battlefield to the bureaucracy. Buckle up for a test of character (literally) and a vivid demonstration that in the cultivation world, everyone has a price, and every smooth surface conceals a sharp edge.

## Key Plot Points

- The rookies are personally escorted by a surnamed Yu Core Formation cultivator toward the Luo Yun Sect’s Heavenly Spring Peak, encountering the sect’s internal power dynamics along the way.
- A sleazy yellow-robed Foundation Establishment cultivator named “Senior Brother Yan” tries to snatch two of the new arrivals for his talisman workshop, revealing the predatory labor market inside established sects.
- Surnamed Yu deftly deflects the demand by invoking “Senior Brother Miao,” whose name alone freezes Yan in his tracks—showing the status hierarchy.
- At the Pine-Greeting Pavilion, the gaunt, sickly late-Foundation Establishment “Senior Brother Miao” greets the candidates, his sharp eyes scanning them with unsettling thoroughness; he notably hesitates when looking at the bearded man.
- Miao produces a set of silver Heart-Questioning Talismans and administers a ten-question truth test; everyone must pass or else be rejected.
- Han Li internally scoffs at the talisman’s ability to detect *his* lies, setting up a tactical mental contrast.
- All seven candidates, including Han Li, are ultimately cleared; the chapter ends with them being sent to the Sect Leader for final registration and formalization.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Heart-Questioning Talisman / Mind-Reading (问心符)** – This is a classic Xianxia method for screening sect entrants. It usually functions as an enhanced lie detector, but its effectiveness is often a matter of author convenience. In *RMJI*, it's treated as “90% accurate,” which is enough to weed out obviously hostile spies but leaves room for protagonists with exceptional mental discipline or soul-protection artifacts to slip through. In Daoist internal-alchemy traditions, the “heart-mind” (心) is the seat of intention; these talismans essentially read the cultivator’s spiritual intent, not just surface words.
- **Sect Entry Protocols** – Real-world Chinese martial arts schools (and their fictional counterparts) historically used elaborate initiation rites to test a student's character—sometimes involving vigil fasting, endurance trials, or questioning. In Xianxia, this becomes a formalized, bureaucratic filter designed to protect the sect’s resources from spies or people who might waste them.
- **Bottleneck Economy** – This chapter brilliantly illustrates the *Bottleneck Economy* concept. Established sects hoard talent and resources. The yellow-robed cultivator isn’t just being mean; he’s desperate for labor because *everyone* in a resource-tight system is trying to maximize their leverage. This is not an idyllic school—it’s a corporation where each department head is fighting for a bigger share of the “human resources” budget.

### Bottleneck Economy

A systemic reality in the Mortal Stream where progress is gated by specific, scarce resources, forcing constant strategic shifts and preventing linear growth.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in **reading the room** from the protagonist’s perspective. Han Li isn't just being processed; he is *auditing* the entire organization for weaknesses and opportunities. Pay close attention to:
- **The Yellow-Robed Yan vs Senior Brother Miao**: A classic Mortal Stream contrast. Yan is a *visible* predator—greedy, impatient, and easy to read. Miao is the *invisible* one—polite, thorough, coughing in the shadows, but possessing a depth that makes even a Core Formation elder pause. Which one is more dangerous to Han Li’s long-term goals?
- **The Hesitation at the Bearded Man**: Miao’s unusually quick glance at the bearded rogue is a subtle but important narrative breadcrumb. Is this man special? Or is it a test designed to see how the other candidates react? This is the hallmark of Han Li’s mindset—he files away the anomaly, even if he can’t solve it yet.
- **Han Li’s Internal Calm** – Despite the formalities, Han Li is completely unflappable internally. He knows his secrets are deep. This isn’t arrogance; it’s the result of years of forcing himself into a state where even a potentially magic lie detector finds nothing to latch onto because his true intentions are buried so deep. Watch how his inner coldness contrasts with his placid outer demeanor.
