## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a gut punch, fellow readers. After the tense rescue and getaway of the last chapter, Han Li’s luck takes a catastrophic nosedive. In a moment of panic and flawed information, he makes an utterly human mistake—and pays for it with everything. This chapter, titled “An Awkward Mistake,” is a brutal showcase of the Mortal Stream’s mantra: the universe doesn’t reward good intentions. It punishes them. We see Han Li outmaneuver a pursuer, only to fall into a trap far more insidious than any magical formation. The consequences are devastating, stripping him of over a decade of progress in a single, horrifying moment, and leaving him to face a terrifyingly powerful stranger who knows his name—and has every reason to hate him.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Bluff and the Escape:** Han Li initiates a full-power attack on Dong Xuan’er, unleashing multiple magical tools and puppet beasts. However, it’s all a feint. While she braces for impact, he simply flies past her on his Divine Wind Boat and escapes, leaving her shields to absorb a meaningless volley. This displays his pragmatic, non-confrontational combat doctrine, prioritizing escape over honor.
*   **The Fatal Error of Kindness:** After shaking off Dong Xuan’er, Han Li finds a hiding spot. Believing the unconscious woman he rescued is a critically injured Nan Gong Wan, he makes the fatal mistake of injecting his own spiritual power into her body to check her injuries.
*   **Forced Energy Transfer:** His spiritual power is violently sucked into the woman’s body against his will. He is helplessly paralyzed as his cultivation base drains away: from mid-Foundation Establishment, to early-Foundation Establishment, and all the way down to the Qi Condensation third or fourth layer. He ultimately loses consciousness.
*   **The Reveal of the Stranger:** Han Li awakens to find the woman is not Nan Gong Wan, but a look-alike with a similar, slightly huskier voice. She identifies herself as Nan Gong Wan’s younger cousin and reveals she knows all about him.
*   **A Death Threat Delivered Coldly:** The chapter ends on a razor’s edge. The woman confirms his identity, reveals her knowledge of his relationship with her cousin, and states that her first instinct was to kill him. The future of their interaction hangs in the balance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **The Bluff and Feint (虚晃一枪):** This is a classic strategy in wuxia and xianxia, often translated as “making a feigned attack.” It’s the art of appearing to commit to a direct confrontation only to use that commitment as a screen for a completely different action. Han Li executes this perfectly against Dong Xuan’er, using a show of overwhelming force as cover for a simple bypass. This reinforces the Mortal Stream principle that deception and efficient resource management are often superior to brute strength.
*   **The Trap of Kindness (好心办坏事):** This Chinese idiom translates directly to “doing a good deed but causing harm.” It perfectly encapsulates Han Li’s predicament. His first instinct, born from his residual humanity and concern for Nan Gong Wan, is to immediately check on his “ally.” This very act is what leads to his downfall. In the Mortal Stream, even compassion is a liability that must be ruthlessly managed.
*   **Utter Devastation (五雷轰顶 / Wǔ Léi Hōng Dǐng):** The text says Han Li felt like “five thunderbolts had struck him at once.” This is a distinctively Chinese expression for total, shocking devastation. It emphasizes the complete and sudden nature of his ruin, conveying a spiritual and emotional collapse that goes beyond simple anger or frustration.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in the “Mortal Stream” philosophy: your best intentions are your enemy. Han Li’s single, incorrect assumption—that the woman’s voice was just Nan Gong Wan being weak—leads to the most catastrophic loss of his cultivation journey so far. The fall is brutal, swift, and pits his survival instinct against his connection to Nan Gong Wan. The woman’s cold delivery of her murderous intent creates an incredible cliffhanger. But notice Han Li’s survival instinct hasn't died with his cultivation. Despite the shock, he restrains his terror. He notices she doesn’t seem to want to kill him... yet. His mind even immediately latches onto the “Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art” as a potential solution. This is the core of the Mortal Stream: the protagonist doesn’t break when his power is taken; he simply recalculates his odds.
