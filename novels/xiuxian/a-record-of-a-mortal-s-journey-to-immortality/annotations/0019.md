## Chapter Overview

Oh boy. Han Li finally drags himself out of his cave and into the real world of the Seven Mysteries Sect — and what he finds is a sect bleeding from years of street-level war. This is not the serene cultivation paradise he thought he signed up for. It’s a Jianghu meat grinder, where older disciples are sent down the mountain to die in skirmishes over territory, and the mountain feels empty because half of them never came back. Meanwhile, Han Li meets a star senior brother, witnesses a flashy duel, and awkwardly befriends a walking gossip network. This chapter is a lore dump of epic proportions, delivered by the talkative human encyclopedia known as Little Abacus. Get ready for world-building, folks.

## Key Plot Points

- The Seven Mysteries Sect is locked in a long-running violent conflict with the Wild Wolf Gang over several wealthy towns. What started as a territorial dispute has spiraled into years of open warfare, with heavy losses on both sides.
- The Wild Wolf Gang’s members are trained like bandit cavalry and fight with suicidal recklessness. The Seven Mysteries Sect disciples, though individually more skilled, lack that edge, and have been losing more men as a result.
- In response, the sect leadership has changed tactics: inner disciples now must descend the mountain for practical experience before being assigned to frontline combat. This has reduced casualties, but the mountain is now nearly empty of older senior brothers — either dead, fighting, or out on training missions.
- Zhao Ziling challenges Senior Brother Li to a duel and is utterly overwhelmed by his raw power. Senior Brother Li’s blade technique disarms Zhao Ziling effortlessly, drawing admiration from the watching disciples.
- Li Feiyu shows off his Qinggong and leaves abruptly, revealing hints of an urgent responsibility. He’s cool, he’s competent, and he knows it.
- Han Li reflects that his own mental age has aged faster than his peers due to his obsessive cultivation, but remains pragmatic. He doesn’t get starstruck by Li Feiyu’s display; he just notes it as data.
- Han Li befriends Little Abacus (Jin Dongbao), identifies himself as a doctor, and offers free medical treatment. This is less kindness and more tactical networking — he now has an informant on the payroll.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter gives us our first real window into how a Jianghu sect operates in wartime. The conflict between the Seven Mysteries Sect and the Wild Wolf Gang is not a clash of ideals or righteous justice; it’s purely territorial. The towns are “wealthy,” and ownership is “disputed.” That’s all the cause either side needs. This is *Jianghu*: a lawless, violent ecosystem where power decides ownership, not contracts. The sect’s strategy — sending disciples down the mountain to “gain experience” — is a pragmatic adaptation to a brutal reality. They are not training heroes; they are hardening soldiers.

The term **Jianghu** (江湖) literally means “rivers and lakes.” In martial arts fiction, it refers to an alternative society of wanderers, sects, and outlaws, with its own code and hierarchy, entirely outside official government control. In the *Mortal Stream* iteration, it’s a dark forest of survival, where the weak are fed to the strong and experience is measured in blood.

## Reading Guide

- **Watch Han Li’s instincts**: Even while listening to a simple story, his mind is working. He’s not feeling pity or fear for the fallen disciples. He’s collecting intelligence on the sect’s resource allocation, its vulnerabilities, and where he fits into this machine.
- **Why the free medical offer?** Han Li is a boy in a market. He’s betting that in a sect about to go to war, a healer is valuable. Tying a talkative, well-connected disciple to himself with a promise of free treatment is not generosity — it’s an investment in information security.
- **Li Feiyu as faction leader**: His abrupt departure after the duel hints that he has real responsibilities. In the Jianghu, rank comes with obligation. If you’re the strongest, you get sent to the front.
- **Han Li’s age anxiety**: He worries his mind has aged from cultivation. This is a subtle hint that his worldview is already diverging from his peers. He sees performance as vanity; he values survival over glory. That’s the *Mortal Stream* soul waking up.
