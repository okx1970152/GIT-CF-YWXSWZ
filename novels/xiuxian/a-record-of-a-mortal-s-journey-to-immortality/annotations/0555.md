## Chapter Overview
Our favorite mortal, Han Li, is now stuck playing mana battery for a trio of demonic overlords who call the shots in this part of the ocean. In this chapter, we finally get the real reason why the Deep Sea Beast Tide was unleashed on the human islands, and it’s not just because some human cultivators were getting cocky. No, the true motive is way more personal for the demon race—a sacred relic got snagged by the humans, and they want it back. Han Li, of course, just got his hands on a mysterious copper slab that sounds an awful lot like that “Sacred Sanctum Fragment” everyone is fighting over. Classic Han Li—always in the wrong place at the right time to collect the world's hottest potatoes.

## Key Plot Points
* The venom flood dragon drops the truth bomb: the demon race’s massive assault on human islands was triggered by a human killing a Grade 7 Suanni Beast of the Thousand-Fathom Sea Royal Clan and, more importantly, stealing the clan’s sacred heirloom—the “Sacred Sanctum Fragment.”
* The three demon lords reveal their internal hierarchy: the Flood Dragon clan only got involved to reclaim the relic, not out of loyalty to the Suanni clan. They even hint at possible ambitions to take over the region entirely.
* Feng Xi warns Han Li to stay put or else, and our boy complies without a fuss. He even sets up an anti-snooping formation, which is basically him flipping the bird at his captors in a technically polite way.
* Han Li learns that the “Chaos Evil Qi” shot into him is actually more dangerous “Chaos Demon Qi,” harvested from a dead devil-path elder. Even the demon lords find it hard to expel. That’s a big red flag.
* After two tense days, the refining of the Wind and Thunder Wings begins. Han Li is led to a massive geothermal flame chamber with a complex formation designed to drain his wood spiritual energy for the ritual. Welcome to the battery station.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Suanni (狻猊) – A Mythological Heavyweight:** The Suanni is one of the nine sons of the Dragon King in Chinese mythology, often depicted as a lion-like beast that loves fire and smoke. You’ll see its image on incense burners everywhere. In *RMJI*, the “Royal Clan” owning a Suanni stud is a clever way of signaling that these are the baddest demons in the neighborhood—a bloodline with mythical dragon cred. When a Grade 7 Suanni gets ganked by a human, it’s not just a battle loss; it’s a dynastic insult.

**The Ten-Thousand-Demon Command (万妖令) – A Cataclysmic Button:** The “Demon Emperor Token” is a classic cultivation trope: a one-time-use artifact that demands high-level essence blood to activate but allows a ruler to command every demon beast in their domain. It’s the ultimate “I’m using my nuclear option” move. In this chapter, it’s explicitly tied to the legitimate anger of the Suanni clan’s patriarch, who burned his own vitality to issue it. This reinforces the rule that high-level demon politics is just as cutthroat and emotional as human sect politics.

**Sacred Sanctum Fragments (梵圣真片) – Ancient Knowledge, Modern Headaches:** The concept of “lost ancient techniques” scribbled on indestructible tablets is a staple of xianxia worldbuilding. It conveniently creates McGuffins that powerful factions will kill for, and it gets the protagonist into trouble without him having to lift a finger. The fact that it’s a demon race technique and Han Li just happens to have it? That’s peak Mortal Stream narrative coincidence—except here, coincidence is just another type of tactical liability he has to calculate around.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in **information asymmetry**—the key tension of the Mortal Stream. Han Li, the prisoner, overhears the villains expose their entire motivation for a war, and simultaneously realizes he might be carrying the trophy everyone is fighting over. The agony here is that he can’t do a damn thing about it. He’s not heroic; he’s just storing data.

Notice how Feng Xi handles him. The demon doesn't scream or threaten. He just says, “Stay in your room, or I’ll misunderstand.” That’s the voice of a predator who knows he’s already won. The real conflict in this chapter isn't swords and spells—it’s Han Li mentally cataloging his options while playing the role of the docile captive. If you like slow-burn tension and watching a protagonist do cold math on his survival odds, this is the kind of chapter that delivers.

Also, keep an eye on that **Chaos Demon Qi**. The fact that it’s sourced from a dead devil practicioner and even the demon trio admit it’s nasty stuff means this isn’t just a “pop a pill and you’re fine” problem. This is a long-term cultivation crisis. Han Li’s secret trump card—his mysterious vial—might not be enough to fix this kind of systemic damage. That’s a proper hook.

## The Cold Calculus of a Captive
Han Li’s behavior here is textbook Mortal Stream survival: don’t panic, establish a perimeter (the alarm array), conserve information (the copper slab), and keep analyzing the threat environment (the Chaos Evil Qi). He doesn’t waste breath on rage or pleading. He just starts running the numbers. That’s the core of his character, and this chapter is pure Han Li fuel.
