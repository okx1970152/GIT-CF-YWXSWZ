## Chapter Overview
Han Li is shopping for a sect—and in the Mortal Stream, that means kicking the tires on three very different power structures before signing anything. The Brilliant Artifact Institute is a closed family cartel (cross it off the list), the Ancient Sword Sect demands top-tier Spiritual Roots (a non-starter for our four-attribute protagonist), and the Setting Cloud Sect is the desperate, scrappy underdog that will take almost anyone with cultivation. Why? Because they need bodies to fuel their alchemy, talisman, and formation assembly lines. Get ready for a chapter that’s part economic analysis, part patience test, and all Han Li. Fellow Daoists, the assembly line awaits.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li systematically evaluates the three major sects of the Yunmeng Mountains. He dismisses the Brilliant Artifact Institute (closed family dynasty) and the Ancient Sword Sect (elite talent barrier), zeroing in on the Setting Cloud Sect (low threshold, massive need for cheap labor, alchemy fame).
- The Setting Cloud Sect’s two-track acceptance policy is revealed: take high-talent prodigies, or take anyone who has demonstrated sustained cultivation discipline regardless of talent. This is explicitly tied to the sect’s need for a large low-ranked workforce to support its alchemy and miscellaneous industries.
- Han Li arrives at the sect’s base and endures a roughly 1.5-hour climb on a path laced with crude illusions—a patience test for prospective disciples.
- Upon reaching the platform, Han Li sees through the illusionary formation, identifies the three Foundation Establishment examiners, and eavesdrops on their conversation using his high-level spiritual sense without their knowledge.
- The examiners’ internal debate reveals changing recruitment standards: a recent policy shift from "quantity over quality" back to "quality over quantity" after complaints from higher-ups.
- Han Li settles in, projecting a Qi Condensation layer 10 profile—confident this level will pass muster.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The "Sect Economics" of the Setting Cloud Sect is a perfect illustration of the Bottleneck Economy principle. Unlike western fantasy apprenticeship where a master takes one or two students, the Setting Cloud Sect needs a *standing army* of low-rank cultivators (炼气期) to operate its alchemy and talisman production lines. This isn't mystical fellowship—it's institutional division of labor. The sect provides a "big tree" (好乘凉) for those with limited prospects; in exchange, those disciples become reliable, standardized labor. This dynamic reveals that in the Mortal Stream, even "lenient" sects are not charities—they are resource-extraction engines that harvest human attention and time as surely as any demonic sect harvests blood. And the legend of the Settling Spirit Pill (定灵丹) acts as a strategic "brand asset": even a second-rate sect can attract talent if it holds the key to a rare bottleneck-breaking resource. This is dark, systemic, and entirely logical.

## Reading Guide
This is a classic "protagonist-research-phase" chapter—which in less skilled hands would be deadly boring. But watch how *RMJI* weaves real economic reasoning into every paragraph. Han Li doesn’t *feel* his way into a choice; he runs a strict multi-factor audit: family control (bad), talent gate (bad), labor demand + resource access (good). Even the illusion path is framed not as a magical test but as a simple patience filter—a psychological barrier to weed out the least disciplined. The chapter also serves up a hearty portion of foreshadowing: the Settling Spirit Pill, the sect’s desperate need for cheap hands, and the internal debate about "quality vs quantity" all set the stage for Han Li’s long-term strategy. New readers: pay attention to the phrase "宁缺毋滥" (better to go without than accept mediocrity)—it’s a rule of thumb that will get broken. Probably by Han Li himself.
