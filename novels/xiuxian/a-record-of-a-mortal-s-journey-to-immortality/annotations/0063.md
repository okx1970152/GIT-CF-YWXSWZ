## Chapter Overview

Chapter 63 delivers the long-awaited payoff to one of the earliest mysteries in the novel: the fate of Zhang Tie. After months of dread and speculation, Han Li finally confirms that the silent, iron-skinned giant is built from the body of his childhood friend. For a rare moment, Han Li’s iron composure softens—but only long enough to process a horror he cannot reverse. The chapter is a masterclass in mixing triumph with tragedy: the joy of gaining an invulnerable servant is instantly poisoned by the cost of obtaining it.

## Key Plot Points

- After prolonged hesitation, Han Li decides to test the control method described in Doctor Mo’s letter, using a brass “Soul Summoning Bell” to command the giant.
- The bell proves effective—the giant collapses unconscious when struck, and Han Li uses his own blood to complete the binding ritual.
- The blood is absorbed completely into the giant’s skin, and a strange mental link forms between them, granting Han Li total control over the giant’s body.
- Han Li tests the link with a command, and the giant obediently demolishes the stone door.
- Elated by his new asset, Han Li examines the giant’s features more closely and realizes the swollen face, when mentally reconstructed, is unmistakably Zhang Tie’s.
- The chapter ends with Han Li silently processing this truth—his friend is gone, reduced to a soulless, transformed shell.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Soul Summoning Bell (引魂钟):** A ritual artifact used in cultivation fiction to summon or bind an entity’s spirit. Unlike ordinary bells, these are typically inscribed with blood or spiritual symbols to establish a resonance with a soul without a body. The bell’s plain appearance in this chapter highlights a recurring theme: the most terrifying tools often look the most ordinary.

**Blood-binding control:** The act of using blood to control a soulless body draws on the Chinese folk concept that blood carries a person’s life-force (精血, essence-blood). By smearing his blood onto the giant’s face, Han Li effectively grafts his own spiritual signature onto the shell, establishing a master-servant connection. This mechanic sidesteps the need for complex formations or high-level cultivation—it’s brute-force soul engineering at its most pragmatic.

**Body transformation (躯体改造):** The giant’s enlarged, swollen features are not random mutations but signs of extreme alchemical or external-force tampering. The idea that a human body could be “expanded” into a giant form without breaking suggests that Doctor Mo used marrow-altering medicines or forbidden bone-growth techniques, reinforcing the novel’s recurring theme that the physical body is just another material to be refined.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the emotional payoff for readers who have been waiting since Chapter 26 for Zhang Tie’s fate. Don’t brush past the last paragraph—Han Li’s silent recognition of his friend is one of the few moments in the entire novel where his mask cracks. It’s not dramatic; it’s quiet, because that’s how Han Li processes grief.

Pay attention to the progression of Han Li’s emotional state: curiosity, caution, surprise, elation, satisfaction, dawning horror, then silence. The chapter’s power lies in the final pivot—the discovery turns a victory into a loss. This is the Mortal Stream’s core philosophy laid bare: every advantage comes with a wound.

Also note that Han Li does not name the giant yet. That will come in a later chapter. For now, the body is simply “the giant,” and the tragedy is that it no longer deserves a name.
