## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow Daoists, because we just witnessed a classic Mortal Stream sequence—and Han Li didn’t throw a single punch. This chapter is a clinic in knowing when to stay small, when to run, and when to come back to sweep up the leftovers. We open with the sudden appearance of a Grade 8 Poison Flood Dragon, a beast so terrifying that even Nascent Soul cultivators would need allies to take it on. Han Li, a mere early Core Formation cultivator, witnesses the battle between the dragon, an elderly Nascent Soul Daoist, and two Core Formation brothers armed with Divine Sand. His role? Hide, observe, and then *flee like his life depends on it*. Because in the Mortal Stream, the smartest move is often the one that keeps you out of the way while your enemies exhaust themselves.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li identifies a massive crimson Poison Flood Dragon—a Grade 8 beast that can fight above its rank and rival Grade 9 demons.
- The Poison Flood Dragon transforms into a humanoid form, a clear sign of its high cultivation and intelligence.
- Three arrival cultivators appear: the Nascent Soul old Daoist Jinxia, and the Huo brothers (late Core Formation) with their Divine Sand of Creation.
- Jinxia proposes an alliance with the Huo brothers to take down the flood dragon, showing his pragmatism despite his senior rank.
- Han Li remains hidden, preparing his blood-colored cape for a rapid escape as the battle begins.
- When the fighting fully engages, Han Li uses the blood cape to flee at top speed, covering thousands of miles without pursuit.
- After several days, Han Li cautiously returns to the Mist Sea to find the area completely calm—the battle is over, all parties vanished.
- Han Li detects something with his spiritual sense, dives into the sea, and emerges holding two items.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Flood Dragons (蛟, Jiao) occupy a special place in Chinese mythology. They are not quite dragons (龙, Long), but are seen as powerful serpentine beings on the path to becoming true dragons. In xianxia, pure-blood Flood Dragons cultivate faster than ordinary demon beasts and can punch above their weight class, making them apex predators even among high-grade monsters. The Poison Flood Dragon here is a rare and particularly vicious subspecies. Its ability to take humanoid form marks it as a creature of substantial intelligence and age; in the cultivation world, the capacity to shapeshift is a clear threat indicator—it means the beast understands tactics, not just raw instinct.

Also noteworthy is the Huo brothers’ Divine Sand of Creation (破天神砂, Poshen Shas). These kinds of named, legendary treasures often appear as trump cards for old families or sect elites, hinting at a deeper background the chapter doesn’t fully explore.

## Reading Guide
This is the kind of chapter that makes *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey* stand out from standard power-fantasy. Han Li doesn’t charge in, doesn’t try to steal the spotlight, and doesn’t pull off a miraculous intervention. He hides, he calculates, he flees. And then he *comes back later when it’s safe*. That third step is the real lesson: the victory in a Mortal Stream story isn’t always in the clash—it’s in the patient return to the scene of the storm after everyone else has either killed each other or moved on. The chapter ends on a quiet but meaningful note: Han Li dives into the sea and retrieves two items. What did he find? The answer will have to wait, but the payoff is clearly coming. For now, enjoy the tension, the worldbuilding, and the quiet satisfaction of a survivor’s patience.
