## Chapter Overview

Well, folks, our boy Han Li has officially landed in the Blood Forbidden Land! And let me tell you, the welcome package is… not great. Rotting soil, stinking air, and a terrain that screams “I’m going to kill you in three different ways.” But this is the Mortal Stream, baby. No time for panic. Han Li does what Han Li does best: he runs a checklist. Location? Check. Enemies nearby? Check. Memorized his homework on the area’s flora and fauna? Double check. He zeroes in on the Black Dragon Pool, a spot famous for the mildly valuable Cold Smoke Grass. It’s a low-risk side quest, just a nice little prelude… until a Heaven’s Fortress disciple shows up and gets turned into a colander by a pack of frost toads. The kicker? It was a trap set by two Spirit Beast Mountain thugs who used the docile beasts like trained attack dogs. This chapter is a perfect showcase of two core Mortal Stream principles: first, that even the lowliest beasts can be weaponized with a little creativity, and second, that Han Li’s greatest asset is knowing when to walk away from a bad bet.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter dives right into the Forbidden Land’s chaotic teleportation mechanic. Han Li gets dumped alone in the northern fringe near the Black Dragon Pool, a stroke of decent luck. He immediately drops into survival mode: concealment spell active, talisman in one hand, magic weapon in the other. The narration is a masterclass in procedural caution. He recalls the dossier’s tip about the Cold Smoke Grass and decides to check it out on his way to the richer central zone. The tension builds silently as he spots a Heaven’s Fortress cultivator making his way to the same patch of herbs. Han Li watches, calculating the odds of a backstab, but decides the risk isn’t worth the mediocre reward. He turns to leave—and that’s when the scream rings out. Returning to the scene, he finds the corpse of the Heaven’s Fortress man and two Spirit Beast Mountain disciples giggling over his loot, surrounded by a pack of Frost Toads. The reveal is pure Mortal Stream genius: they didn’t fight the man; they used beast-repelling powder to coerce the local amphibians into an ambush. The younger thug even suggests turning the spot into a permanent kill zone using the herb patch as bait. The chapter closes with Han Li watching this unfold, a silent observer who has learned a new, cheap tactic to file away for later use.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Cold Smoke Grass (寒烟草):** This isn’t one of the legendary ingredients for the Foundation Establishment Pill, so to our profit-driven protagonist, it’s barely worth the dirt on his shoes. But in the cosmic economy of the cultivation world, even “useless” herbs can serve a purpose: as bait. The scene perfectly illustrates a core tenet of the Mortal Stream genre: nothing has inherent value, only situational value. A herb that can’t help you breakthrough is just a lure for another fool trying to breakthrough. The way the Spirit Beast Mountain disciples treat it as a “鱼饵” (fishing bait) rather than a treasure is a brutally honest assessment of its worth.

**The Docile Beast as a Weapon (驯兽为刃):** The tactic employed here is chillingly clever. The Spirit Beast Mountain disciples don’t fight. They simply manipulate the environment. Using “驱兽粉” (beast-repelling powder) to agitate the Frost Toads, they redirect the natural defensive instincts of a docile creature into a lethal, targeted volley. This is a far cry from high-level monster taming; this is cheap, local, and devastatingly effective. It reinforces the idea that in this world, intelligence, preparation, and a willingness to exploit any tool—including the very ecosystem—are more valuable than raw power. The Frost Toads’ breath attack, the “冰锥术” (Ice Spike Technique), is a basic skill, but a dozen of them fired at an unsuspecting back is a death sentence for anyone at the Qi Condensation stage.

**The Bluff (诈语) and the Observer:** The Heaven’s Fortress disciple attempts to flush out a potential ambusher by shouting a bluff (“Come out! I see you!”). It’s a common Jianghu trick, and Han Li, the ultimate misdirection expert himself, sees right through it. He watches the man’s shoddy performance with a mix of amusement and disgust. This small scene underscores the massive gap in tactical awareness between a paranoid survivor like Han Li and an average sect disciple. It’s a reminder that the protagonist’s survival isn’t just about luck; it’s a hard-won skill set built on constant vigilance and a deep understanding of human (and inhuman) cunning.

## Reading Guide

This is a low-stakes, high-drama chapter that functions as a perfect “welcome to the jungle” appetizer for the Blood Forbidden Land arc. The central tension isn’t a massive boss fight; it’s a quiet, predatory social game. Watch how the narrative prioritizes Han Li’s *internal* decision-making over external action. He draws no glory, hoards no treasure, and risks nothing. He simply observes, calculates, and then… leaves. The real hero of the chapter is the Spirit Beast Mountain duo’s plan. It’s a beautiful example of “work smarter, not harder.” They don’t out-muscle their opponent; they out-think the terrain. Pay attention to the phrase “照方抓药” (follow the prescription/do it again) from the younger disciple. He’s suggesting they turn this spot into a business model, a recurring kill box. This is the unromantic, brutal reality of the Mortal Stream: violence is just a method of resource extraction, and strategy is the only real currency.
