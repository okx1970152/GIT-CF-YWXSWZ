## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow Daoists, have your tissues ready—no, really. Chapter 542, “A Wind Rises Beyond the Seas—A Gift of Treasures,” is one of those quiet, emotional payout chapters that *RMJI* does so devastatingly well. After the intensity of the last arc, we get a breather, but don’t mistake “quiet” for “boring.” This is Han Li doing something he almost *never* does: being generous without a calculated return. We see Gongsun Xing’s awe as she discovers a trove of ancient techniques and even legendary magic treasures—and then her confusion when the terrifyingly powerful “senior” who claimed her as a furnace turns out to be a gentle young man who simply leaves her a fortune and disappears. It’s a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s unique form of sentiment: cold, calculated, and yet achingly human.

## Key Plot Points

Gongsun Xing explores the stone rooms of the cave dwelling. In the first room, she discovers a jade slip containing the top-tier “Golden True Art,” followed by numerous other advanced techniques and Daoist texts. She is particularly drawn to the “Jade Entwining Art,” which is perfectly suited for a female cultivator. In a second hall, she is stunned to find a massive formation housing over a dozen sentient magic treasures, surrounded by racks of high-grade and top-grade magic tools. Despite the overwhelming wealth before her, her joy is tempered by the memory of her own status as a “furnace.” Two days later, Han Li appears—not as the ancient, fearsome master she imagined, but as a calm, youthful-looking man. He gently reassures her that he allows her to choose any technique. After a brief conversation about a nearby market, he tells her to come to his room that night. When she arrives, she finds only a silk handkerchief with a message explaining that he has left the cave’s remaining treasures as a gift for her filial piety, warning her to keep them hidden. Han Li, already thousands of *li* away, reflects that he was moved not by lust but by respect for her devotion to her father, and by a faint, lingering ache for his own family and younger sister. The items he left were merely a “chicken rib” to him—useless but too valuable to discard.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“Chicken Rib” (鸡肋, Jī Lèi):** This is a classic idiom from the *Records of the Three Kingdoms*, where the strategist Yang Xiu compares a seemingly valuable but ultimately useless region to a chicken rib—“tasteless to eat, but a pity to throw away.” In *RMJI*, it perfectly describes the Mortal Stream economy of resource management: Han Li doesn’t give the treasures away out of pure kindness; they were literally “dead weight” in his inventory, offering no strategic value to him. The gift is a clinically efficient way to settle a debt of respect without carrying sentimental baggage.

**“Furnace” (炉鼎, Lú Dǐng):** This term originally comes from Daoist alchemy, referring to the physical vessel used for refining pills. In the cruel lexicon of xianxia, it is twisted into a horrifying institution: a living person who is “used” as a cultivation vessel, often with their vitality and foundation being forcibly drained. Han Li uses the term as a deliberate, shocking jest to create a pretext for keeping Gongsun Xing at his dwelling without arousing suspicion or requiring explanation. This chapter subverts its dark meaning by turning it into a cover story for a completely benevolent act.

**“Filial Piety” (孝, Xiào):** This is a foundational virtue in Confucian-influenced Chinese culture. Gongsun Xing’s willingness to sacrifice herself for her father is exactly the kind of moral weight that can briefly override Han Li’s otherwise iron-clad “never lift a finger without benefit” rule. It triggers a rare emotional chord, connecting her to his own unresolved feelings of filial failure.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a beautiful, melancholic breather that shows Han Li not as a cold machine, but as a man shaped by his own past losses. Pay close attention to the prose: look at how the awe of the treasure hall is framed not as wonder, but as a tragic reminder for Gongsun Xing of her own powerlessness. The shift from the “dreaded furnace master” to the gentle, teasing young man is a classic Mortal Stream subversion of expectations—the scary senior turns out to be the only one who gives her a way out without asking for anything in return. The chapter’s final line, where Han Li reflects that he had *already* decided to abandon the cave before they arrived, is the real kicker. It wasn’t a heroic last-minute choice; it was a logistical cleanup that happened to benefit someone worthy. That’s *RMJI* writing at its finest.
