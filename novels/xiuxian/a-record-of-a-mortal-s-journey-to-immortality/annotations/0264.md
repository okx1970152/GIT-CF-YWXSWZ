## Chapter Overview
Wang Chan thinks he has Han Li cornered—again—and decides to sacrifice a bit of essence blood to finish the job. But when he finally cuts through the fog, he finds Han Li has already activated a Talisman Treasure, shatters the blood-cloud, and bolts. The chase that follows is short-lived because Wang Chan suddenly realizes he’s been poisoned by the very blood-cloud he used to hunt. What should have been a clean kill turns into a facepalm-inducing escape, leaving both sides fuming with regret. Meanwhile, the Ghost Spirit Sect’s larger operation hits an unexpected snag when the Joyous Union Sect swoops in and snatches two prisoners. And poor Han Li? He gets conscripted by another sect before he can even make it home.

## Key Plot Points
- Wang Chan pushes his Blood Spirit Grand Art to the limit, absorbing the poisoned mist Han Li had deliberately refined into the blood-cloud.
- Han Li reveals he had been secretly activating a Talisman Treasure (the short dagger from earlier) while the mist covered his actions, using the delay to launch a surprise escape.
- The yellow streak Talisman Treasure punches through the blood-cloud, and Han Li flees on the Divine Wind Boat.
- The chase begins, but Wang Chan’s body suddenly shows signs of poisoning (green-black face) because the blood-cloud he used to pursue had absorbed the ink flood dragon’s residual cinnabar poison from the Greenfire Miasma.
- Wang Chan abandons the golden skull Talisman Treasure and retreats in panic.
- Han Li collects the golden skull (now a talisman) but realizes too late that he missed a golden opportunity to kill his enemy and seize more loot—his regret is immediate and bitter.
- Wang Chan runs into the two Core Formation Li brothers, who help him expel the poison. He then learns that Tian Buque (Joyous Union Sect) stole the two female cultivators Wang Chan had captured, adding fuel to his grudge.
- Wang Chan coldly orders the Yan Clan ancestor to begin evacuation, then puts Han Li and Tian Buque on his must-kill list.
- Han Li, trying to return to Yellow Maple Valley, is intercepted and conscripted by a mixed group from Carving Knife Dock and Moon-Masking Sect.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Talisman Treasure (符宝)** – A Talisman Treasure is a compressed portion of a real magical treasure’s power, sealed into a talisman. It can be used by cultivators too weak to wield the original artifact. However, each use drains it permanently. Wang Chan’s golden skull is a Talisman Treasure he has already used several times; he estimates it’s nearly empty of power, which is why he doesn’t mind losing it. For Han Li, even a nearly-drained Talisman Treasure is still a significant asset—a perfect example of the Mortal Stream’s “resource reclamation” logic.

**Blood Escape (血遁)** – A high-speed escape technique used by blood-path cultivators. It trades raw vitality (essence blood) for explosive speed. Wang Chan uses it both to chase Han Li and to retreat. The cost is real: losing essence blood weakens the cultivator and can leave them vulnerable to poison, as we see here.

**Ink Flood Dragon’s Residual Cinnabar Poison (墨蛟残余的丹毒)** – Back in the Blood Forbidden Land, Han Li defeated an ink flood dragon and harvested its remains. He then refined the monster’s poison (cinnabar-type) into the Greenfire Miasma. This chapter pays off that setup: the poison in the miasma is now circulating through Wang Chan’s blood-cloud, and when he absorbs the cloud back into his body during the chase, he becomes poisoned. Classic xianxia cause-and-effect: the environment is often the true weapon.

**Tian Buque (田不缺)** – Second son of the Joyous Union Sect’s sect leader, famous among the younger generation for being a ruthless “flower-picking” expert. His name literally means “Tian Not Lacking,” but the real bite is in his reputation for stealing beautiful female cultivators from other sects. His sudden intervention here is a delightful monkey-wrench in Wang Chan’s plans.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream ironies. Wang Chan thinks he’s the predator, but he’s been poisoned by his own chase. Han Li thinks he’s escaped cleanly, but he immediately regrets not finishing the job. And right when Wang Chan is fuming over his losses, his own prisoners get stolen by a rival. The universe just loves to twist the knife. Han Li’s escape is clever—using the mist to buy time for a Talisman Treasure activation—but he’s not invincible; he lets a perfect killing chance slip by. That regret is what makes him feel real. Also, the author’s note at the end is a great window into web novel culture: real schedules, real apologies, and the grind of daily serialization. Don’t skip it.
