## Chapter Overview

The Void Heaven Palace opens at last—but not without a final dose of political theater. The Star Palace's white‑robed elders seal the hall, reveal a hidden teleportation array, and vanish without a backward glance, leaving the assembled cultivators to scramble for position. Among the chaos, Han Li catches Extreme Yin Patriarch's predatory smile and realizes his danger hasn't diminished one bit. Then the teleportation scatters everyone randomly, and Han Li finds himself on a barren hill with three strangers—one of whom is Fairy Zi Ling, looking both delighted and desperate. The chapter closes on a note of reluctant obligation: Han Li agrees to watch over her, but his face warms as he looks away, hinting at emotions he rarely lets surface.

## Key Plot Points

- The sealed hall and hidden teleportation confirm that the Star Palace has long orchestrated this trial; the two white‑robed elders inspect and activate the array without a word to the crowd.
- Wan Tianming and his allies depart first, followed by the Profound Bone Saint; Han Li hesitates, but Extreme Yin's lingering gaze forces him to move.
- Extreme Yin refuses to explain his interest in Han Li to Wu Chou, deepening the mystery and Han Li's sense of threat.
- The teleportation scatters cultivators randomly; Han Li ends up alone except for three others: a friendly gray‑clothed old man, a hostile demonic cultivator, and Fairy Zi Ling.
- Zi Ling explains she is at the Half‑Foundation stage and desperate for spirit herbs to assist Core Formation, having purchased a fragment map at great cost.
- Han Li's internal calculation: he recognizes a shared plight, remembers the Divine Thunder Bamboo debt, and reluctantly agrees to protect her during the first trial—but not without a clear escape clause.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Void Heaven Palace (虚天殿)**  
One of the Chaotic Star Sea's most legendary ruins, a floating jade palace that appears only periodically. It is famous for its sealed inner treasure (the Void Heaven Cauldron) and its tiered trials. The fact that cultivators enter through a teleportation array that appears suddenly and vanishes for a month underscores the "dungeon‑crawl" logic of Xianxia adventures: once you enter, there is no easy exit.

**Half‑Foundation (假丹)**  
A transitional state for peak‑Foundation Establishment cultivators who have compressed their qi but not yet fully formed a Golden Core. Unlike true Foundation Establishment, Half‑Foundation means the core is "fake"—unstable and unable to sustain breakthrough without external aid. Zi Ling's desperation mirrors Han Li's own past, making this a rare moment of emotional resonance.

**Favors and Debts in the Mortal Stream**  
Han Li's decision to protect Zi Ling is not born of chivalry but of cold ledger‑keeping: the Divine Thunder Bamboo and yearly spirit stones constitute a debt he now chooses to repay. This transactional approach to relationships is the bedrock of *RMJI*'s social logic—no gift is free, and every favor is a future obligation.

## Reading Guide

Watch Han Li's guard snap up the moment Extreme Yin smiles at him—even without a direct threat, his paranoia is immediate and practical. His later interaction with Zi Ling is a masterclass in Mortal Stream negotiation: he never says "I'll protect you," only "I'll take some care of you, but if I'm doomed, you're on your own." The closing blush is a rare crack in his armor—proof that shared hardship and a grateful smile can still reach him, however fleetingly. Also note the Profound Bone Saint's quiet exit: a thousand‑year ghost slipping away without drama. That's how the truly dangerous move in this world.

**Why Zi Ling Matters**  
She is not just a plot device for a temporary party. She represents the "mortal struggle" that every non‑genius faces: scraping together fragment maps, borrowing from seniors, and gambling one's life on a single trial. Han Li sees a reflection of his own past self, and that weakens his usual cold calculation just enough to say yes.

## Deep Dive: The Thousand‑Year Ghost

The Profound Bone Saint (Xiao Chan) is introduced as a self‑proclaimed "former master of Extreme Yin's founder." His existence as a Profound Soul Body—a dispersed Nascent Soul that has sacrificed reincarnation for prolonged undead survival—is one of the novel's most chilling concepts. In Daoist‑flavored Xianxia, the soul normally seeks rebirth; to choose eternal ghosthood for the sake of vengeance is a measure of obsession that few cultivators can match. His quiet, calculating nature makes him more dangerous than any roaring demon.
