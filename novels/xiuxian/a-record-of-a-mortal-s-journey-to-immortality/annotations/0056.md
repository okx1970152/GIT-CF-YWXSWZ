## Chapter Overview

Welcome to *the* chapter where the possession arc hits its psychedelic, metaphysical climax. Forget martial arts and swordplay—this round is fought entirely inside the mind. If you’ve been wondering what “soul battle” actually looks like in Mortal Stream logic, here it is: two glowing orbs of consciousness, biting and tearing at each other in a dark void. There are no chants, no clever formations, no last-minute saving throws. It’s pure, primal consumption. Doctor Mo has finally committed to the ritual, Han Li is helpless in his own body, and the fate of his consciousness—and his flesh—hangs in the balance of a dream that is anything but a dream. Prepare for a chapter that redefines the word “uncomfortable.”

## Key Plot Points

- Doctor Mo hesitates at the last moment, trying to extract final guarantees from Yu Zitong about the possession technique’s integrity. This reveals that even after all his scheming, he is still afraid of a hidden trap.
- Yu Zitong swears an absolute, family-annihilating poisonous oath to reassure him, then logically points out that Doctor Mo only has one shot with the Seven Ghost Soul Devouring Art—if he hesitates now, he won’t have the vital essence to try again.
- Doctor Mo makes his decision. He steels himself and officially begins the possession ritual, inserting golden needles into his own secret acupoints to boost his energy.
- Han Li, trapped and helpless, is subjected to a powerful hypnotic chant. He tries to resist but is overwhelmed by the spell, losing consciousness.
- In his dream state, Han Li becomes a green light orb. A smaller yellow orb attacks him and is easily devoured. A larger but weaker green orb then enters, fights a losing battle, and escapes after losing a third of its volume by tearing off pieces of itself.
- The dream ends with Han Li’s orb alone, drifting forever in the void.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Let’s talk about **Possession (夺舍 / Duo She)**. In the RMJI universe, this isn’t just a magical takeover—it’s a full-on metaphysical invasion. The “Battle of the Light Orbs” is a brilliant literalization of what’s really happening: two consciousnesses, stripped of all physical form, fighting over one body in the most primitive way possible. The orb’s size represents the strength of the Primordial Soul; its brightness represents vitality. Doctor Mo’s orb, though bigger, is described as dim and weak—a perfect visual translation of someone who has burned through his life force with forbidden drugs and desperate cultivation hacks. The protagonist’s orb, while smaller, is bright and full. This isn’t just cool imagery; it’s a Death Note-level tactical diagram of who actually stands a chance. Also, note that the dream state is not a metaphor in the xianxia sense—it is a real, tangible layer of the soul dimension. What Han Li experiences *is* the possession ritual.

## Reading Guide

Okay, real talk: this chapter is where the scales could tip in either direction. Doctor Mo is executing his plan, but the “light orb dream” sequence already plants the seed that Han Li’s soul—despite his lower cultivation and lack of magical power—is unusually robust. The subtext here is massive: Han Li’s bright, energetic soul orb versus the enemy’s dim, weakened one. The lore has already established the three iron laws of possession: no mortals, higher magic power required, and only one attempt per lifetime. But none of those rules say anything about the *quality* of the soul being possessed. If you’re the kind of reader who loves it when a system’s strict rules create loopholes the protagonist can exploit, keep your eyes wide open. This chapter is laying the final brick in a very, very dangerous wall for Doctor Mo—and Han Li might just be the one who walks away from the rubble.

## Daoist & Folkloric Correlates

For readers unfamiliar with the religious roots: the idea of a Primordial Soul (元神 / Yuan Shen) battling in an inner realm borrows heavily from Daoist inner alchemy (Neidan) and Chinese folk-Buddhist dream-state mysticism. In Daoist practice, the adept learns to “see” the gods and energies within the body as luminous forms—not physically, but spiritually. This chapter weaponizes that meditative vision into a horrifyingly real battlefield. The “light orbs” are a direct visualization of the soul-body in its raw, unadorned state, stripped of all flesh and technique, which is why the fight is so purely about mass and brightness.
