## Chapter Overview

All hell breaks loose when the Heaven-Mending Pill emerges from the inner hall. The uneasy ceasefire that had held the Nascent Soul cultivators together shatters instantly, and the sky above the altar becomes a free-for-all of light beams, ghost snakes, and golden palms. In the chaos, Han Li makes his first serious attempt to flee, only to be publicly called back by the Profound Bone Saint—a single line that pins him in place under the eyes of half a dozen old monsters. Meanwhile, Wan Tianming, cornered by both greed and a ticking life-clock, escalates to a move he never intended: Nascent Soul Ejection. The pressure in this chapter is claustrophobic, the threat landscape total.

## Key Plot Points

- The Heaven-Mending Pill appears, triggering an immediate and intensified three-sided melee among the Nascent Soul cultivators; the earlier “truce” is revealed as a paper-thin convenience.
- Wu Chou moves to seize the pill under Extreme Yin’s telepathic order, but the Profound Bone Saint intercepts him with green ghost-snakes, openly sabotaging Extreme Yin’s plan.
- Man Brutal Beard declares the demonic path alliance dissolved in a single hearty laugh, codifying what everyone already knew: the pill will be taken for oneself, not shared.
- Star Palace elder’s white rainbow strike nearly kills Han Li; he survives only because the Imperial Scale Armor Man Brutal Beard lent him and the jade ruyi’s hidden wolf-head effect drastically reduced the blow.
- Han Li attempts a silent retreat toward the first-floor stone steps, but the Profound Bone Saint’s cold question freezes him in place and draws the attention of all Nascent Soul cultivators—a bitter reminder that he has no exit.
- Wan Tianming, blocked repeatedly by Man Brutal Beard, threatens to use the Heaven-Entwining True Thunder; when his bluff is called, he actually ejects his Nascent Soul with thunder pearls in hand, pushing the standoff to the brink of mutual destruction.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Heaven-Mending Pill (补天丹):** A legendary elixir said to be able to repair a cultivator’s Five Elements spiritual roots, even granting rootless cultivators a chance at advancement. Its name references the ancient Chinese myth of Nüwa mending the sky (女娲补天)—a metaphor for fixing what is fundamentally broken. In the Mortal Stream economy, a pill that can bypass innate talent barriers is worth the life of any senior cultivator whose lifespan is running out.

**Nascent Soul Ejection (元婴出窍):** For a Nascent Soul stage cultivator, the soul-infant is both their ultimate survival asset and their most vulnerable point. Ejecting it from the body is a high-risk move: the soul-infant is physically fragile and extremely difficult to sustain outside the body for long. Once out, the original body becomes an empty shell. Wan Tianming’s decision to do so here signals genuine desperation—he would rather gamble on instant suicide than lose the pill.

**Heaven-Entwining True Thunder (天罗真雷):** A secret thunder-type treasure known to be feared even by other Nascent Soul cultivators. The specific mechanism is not detailed here, but in xianxia lore, “true thunder” (真雷) often refers to refined lightning that has been condensed to near-elemental purity, capable of bypassing ordinary magical defenses.

**Imperial Scale Armor (皇鳞甲):** Borrowed from Man Brutal Beard earlier, this scale armor is strong enough to survive a hit from a Nascent Soul cultivator’s magical treasure—but only because most of the power was absorbed by the jade ruyi’s hidden wolf head. Even top-grade defensive gear has limits; the escape margin was razor-thin.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in **pressure without escape**. Han Li’s every option is systematically closed: his protector is dead, his attempt to flee is exposed, and he is now a visible target with no value left to trade. The text does not linger on his fear; instead it shows him recalculating, suppressing pain, memorizing exits, and weighing whether to trust the Profound Bone Saint’s game. Watch how the Profound Bone Saint, despite clearly being strong enough to bypass Wu Chou, chooses instead to merely block him and call out Han Li—his motives are deliberately unclear, and that ambiguity is the real trap. Wan Tianming’s soul-ejection is a textbook escalation of a hostage game: when you have nothing left, you make the other side believe you will take everything down with you. The chapter ends on a author’s note apologizing for a potential delay—a touch that grounds the epic chaos in the very real rhythm of serialization.
