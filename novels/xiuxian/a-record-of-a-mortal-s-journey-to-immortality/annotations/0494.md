## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, it’s time for a major shake-up! Just when the tug-of-war over the Void Heaven Cauldron reaches its peak, the Star Palace makes a stunning entrance—not with their true bodies, but with high-cost Incarnation Talismans that let them act from a safe distance. In a single, perfectly timed strike, they shatter the Bloodjade Spider, send Han Li flying, and kill the momentum of the cauldron’s extraction. The righteous and demonic factions are left fuming, and the real power players are revealed to have been playing a completely different game all along. Oh, and as if that weren’t enough, a five-colored Heaven-Mending Pill shoots out of the cauldron like a last-minute middle finger to everyone’s plans. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream scheming: the third party that everyone forgot about shows up, executes a perfect disruption, and vanishes without a trace—leaving only chaos and a new McGuffin in their wake.

## Key Plot Points

1. The Star Palace Law Enforcement Elder is exposed: while hiding nearby to observe, he is detected by Man Brutal Beard but remains utterly calm, revealing that even a single elite elder is unfazed by the gathered Nascent Soul bigwigs.
2. The second elder’s ambush: while everyone’s attention is fixed on the exposed first elder, the second elder launches a lightning-fast strike with his “Piercing Sun Sword” at Han Li and the Bloodjade Spider; the spider is sliced in half, and Han Li is saved only by his jade ruyi.
3. The complete withdrawal: both elders dissolve their bodies into starlight, leaving behind golden Incarnation Talismans that self-destruct on the ground—proving they were never truly present, and that all losses are purely the Star Palace’s monetary expense.
4. The post-strike fallout: the righteous and demonic sects are left helpless and furious, but quickly realize that retaliating against the Star Palace is impossible due to the nature of the spell.
5. The Void Heaven Cauldron’s descent resumes, but an unexpected blast sends a five-colored “Heaven-Mending Pill” shooting into the air, instantly rekindling greed and conflict among the watching Nascent Soul cultivators.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Incarnation Talisman (化身符 / Huàshēn Fú):** This is not your everyday magic scroll. In *RMJI*, the Incarnation Talisman is a high-grade artifact that allows a cultivator to project an avatar of themselves—complete with combat capability—while their true body remains far away. It’s the ultimate “I’m-not-even-here” flex. The catch? It consumes immense resources to create and is permanently destroyed upon use. The Star Palace deploying not one, but two of these signals both their deep pockets and their absolute commitment to sabotaging the cauldron extraction without risking a single real life. In the Mortal Stream, this is a classic “deniable asset” play: you take the win, but leave no corpse to trace back.

**The Heaven-Mending Pill (补天丹 / Bǔ Tiān Dān):** The name literally means “Pill to Repair Heaven,” which is the ultimate marketing slogan in the cultivation world. In Chinese mythology, *Nüwa* (女娲) famously repaired the sky after a cosmic catastrophe. Taking that name for a pill implies it can fix what is broken—in this case, the cultivator’s innate spiritual roots. The novel’s lore is careful to debunk the wilder rumors (no, it doesn’t extend lifespan or bypass Nascent Soul bottlenecks), but confirms one real effect: it can help Core Formation cultivators condense a Nascent Soul by purifying their spiritual roots. Still, even that is an almost impossible task, and most people chasing it are just desperate old monsters grasping at straws. The irony is thick, and the Mortal Stream loves that.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is all about *scale* and *perspective*. For the first time in this arc, a faction that has been a background player—the Star Palace—steps onto the stage and instantly demonstrates why they are the dominant power. They don’t fight fair; they don’t even show up in person. They simply calculate the perfect moment, execute a surgical strike, and walk (or rather, dissipate) away. For Han Li, this is a brutal lesson in how the truly powerful operate: not with raw force, but with intelligence, resources, and pre-paid insurance policies. Notice his reaction—no panic, no rage, just a cold assessment of his own survival and a quick check on his treasure. That’s a Mortal Stream protagonist in a nutshell. The chapter also sets up a perfect new crisis: the Heaven-Mending Pill has now been thrown into the mix, ensuring that the already tense standoff will descend into chaos. This is the kind of chapter that makes you want to immediately read the next one.

## Behind the Scenes: The Author’s Note

You might have noticed the author’s note at the bottom—this is a classic *Qingjia Tiao* (请假条), or “leave request note,” from the era of web novel serialization. In the early days of online Chinese literature, authors often posted daily chapters and occasionally had to take a break due to real-life commitments. These notes were a direct, transparent communication with the readership, explaining the delay and apologizing. They are a charming artifact of the genre’s history, and a reminder that every chapter we read was once a fresh, urgently produced installment in a long-running, community-driven narrative. Respect to the author for still being transparent with his readers even at this stage of the story!
