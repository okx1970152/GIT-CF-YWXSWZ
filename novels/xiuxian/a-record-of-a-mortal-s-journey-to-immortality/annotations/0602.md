## Chapter Overview

We’ve seen Han Li grind through underworld ghost-beasts, collect loot, and push his Crying Soul Beast through a bizarre evolution. Now the real climb begins. This chapter isn’t a battle scene—it’s an environmental survival sequence, the kind of “the world itself is the enemy” design that makes the Mortal Stream so relentlessly physical. Stormwind Mountain is a pyramid of Yin wind, black ice, and man-eating mist. No shortcuts. No flying up with a magic treasure and skipping the ordeal. Han Li and Mei Ning earn every inch the hard way. And just as they think they’ve dealt with every hazard… footsteps in the fog.

## Key Plot Points

- The last of the local alpha Yin beasts is eliminated by the evolved Crying Soul Beast, clearing the mountain’s immediate perimeter.
- Han Li takes inventory: over a hundred Yin Beast Crystals gathered, but he consciously decides to stop hunting to meet the rift-opening deadline.
- The Crying Soul Beast’s unique transformation is explained: it can now assume a giant silver ape form—neither a true Crying Soul Beast evolution nor a standard mutation, but a product of consuming Netherworld Domain souls.
- The climb begins. Below 1000 zhang, it’s merely steep. Above that, the Netherworld Wind forces Han Li to dig out the rare Wind-Avoiding Pearl.
- As they push higher, the terrain becomes a glacier of black ice—treacherous footing, suffocating cold, and exhausting physical strain.
- Han Li reads Mei Ning’s exhaustion without her having to say a word and calls a rest under a sheltering rock, showing his careful management of his companion’s condition.
- Two days later, they’re in a thick, corrosive gray fog—easily the most dangerous segment. Han Li’s Brahmaloka Pearls become the only thing keeping them alive.
- The chapter ends on a classic cliffhanger: Han Li detects other people moving in the mist, closing in on their position.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Stormwind Mountain as a Classical “Nether Pass”**: Chinese folk and Daoist cosmology often imagines the underworld as a region with an impassable barrier—a frozen mountain, a river of blood, or a wall of fire—that the dead must cross or that living intruders must overcome. Stormwind Mountain is a xianxia version of that motif: not a literal afterlife barrier but a natural-physical filter that only those prepared to endure extreme Yin conditions can survive. The Wind-Avoiding Pearl (避风珠), a classic “element-negating treasure” in the genre, allows Han Li to skip one layer of the trial but not all of it; he still must bear the cold, the ice, and the mist, which keeps the challenge weighty.

**The Crying Soul Beast’s “Synthetic Evolution”**: A true Crying Soul Beast (啼魂兽) is supposed to advance to a standard form with black fur. Han Li’s half-refined beast, fed on Yin beast souls from a completely alien realm, developed the ability to turn into a giant silver ape—a form with no canonical precedent. This is the Mortal Stream’s way of showing that talent and resources can sometimes produce better results than orthodox methods if one is lucky enough to land in the right environment. It’s not a miracle; it’s a calculated risk that paid off.

**Brahmaloka Pearls (婆罗珠)**: The term “Brahmaloka” is borrowed from Buddhist cosmology, where it refers to the heavens of Brahma, the highest pure realms. In a xianxia context, the name is used to denote objects with supreme, pure defensive properties—here, specifically against Yin-aligned miasma. The trick is that they consume spiritual energy to maintain their barrier; Han Li can’t hold them forever, and if he runs out of fuel, the mist will consume them.

### Netherwind

A Yin-type wind infused with Netherworld energy that can freeze ordinary flesh and spirit alike in moments; cultivators require special flame-attribute defenses or elemental-negating treasures to survive prolonged exposure.

## Reading Guide

This is one of those chapters where *A Record* reminds you that cultivation isn’t just about flashy battles. Surviving the environment is just as important as surviving the enemy. Han Li’s systematic approach—clearing the local threats, assessing the gear he needs, managing his companion’s stamina, and never letting his guard down even when the scenery looks empty—is the core of what makes him a protagonist you can believe in. Also pay attention to how he handles Mei Ning: he doesn’t offer speeches about courage. He reads her body language, makes the call to rest, and moves on. That’s Mortal Stream leadership: quiet, observational, and ruthlessly practical.

The final line—footsteps in the mist—sets up the next challenge. Who else would be climbing this nightmare of a mountain, and are they friend or threat? In the Netherworld Domain, the answer is usually “threat until proven otherwise.”
