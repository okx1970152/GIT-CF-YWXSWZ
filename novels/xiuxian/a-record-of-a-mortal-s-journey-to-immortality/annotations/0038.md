## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow travelers of the mortal path, because Chapter 38 is a masterclass in what makes *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality* so addictively tense. Our boy Han Li is just trying to get back from a late-night trip, but the universe—ever hostile—throws a classic Dark Forest scenario in his path. The chapter is short, but it's a dense little bomb of paranoia, tactical cunning, and the kind of world-building that makes you realize this cultivation world has teeth. Han Li isn't fighting a monster or a rival cultivator here; he's fighting information, risk, and the terrifying possibility of stumbling into the wrong place at the wrong time. By the end, you'll see that the most dangerous weapon in a cultivator's arsenal isn't a flying sword—it's knowing when to shut up, hide, and listen.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li is walking back through a dark forest at night, relying on a deliberately cultivated habit of extreme vigilance. This isn't innate talent; it's a survival skill he consciously developed after his escape from Doctor Mo, a practice of keeping his senses sharp to avoid fatal surprises.
- He hears faint footsteps approaching. Without hesitation, he slips into the forest and hides behind a tree, his movements as silent and fluid as a cat's.
- The chapter explicitly justifies his caution: it's a remote area, at a dark hour, so anyone coming here is almost certainly up to something shady. He's not being paranoid; he's being smart.
- As the two figures pass, he overhears fragments of their conversation: “chieftain,” “down the mountain,” “list,” “strike.” Given the local geography, the only “chieftain” is Jia Tianlong of the Wild Wolf Gang, the Seven Mysteries Sect's mortal enemy.
- Han Li recalls the terrifying rumors about Jia Tianlong, but also Li Feiyu's more grounded description: not a hulking demon, but a slender, handsome man with an iron, murderous personality. This reinforces the novel's habit of contrasting fearful legend with grim reality.
- He curls his body tighter, barely daring to breathe, fully aware that discovery means a one-way trip to the afterlife.
- After they pass, he waits for a long time, using his cultivation technique to scan the area for any other hidden presences before daring to move.
- From the fragmented words, he deduces that the Wild Wolf Gang is planning a major action against the Seven Mysteries Sect, tied to a mysterious “list.”
- The final, chilling discovery: he recognizes one of the voices. It belongs to an unassuming steward from the sect's main kitchen—a man who once sold him rabbits, a petty, greedy guy with a handlebar mustache. The man he dismissed as a nobody is a deep-cover spy.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Art of Listening (偷听术):** In the Mortal Stream genre, eavesdropping is a high-stakes skill. It's not a quirky personality trait; it's a calculated risk. Han Li decides to listen *only* after ensuring his own safety is guaranteed. He is not driven by curiosity or morality, but by the cold calculation that information is a form of currency. The moment the risk outweighs the potential reward, he would have silenced his own ears.

**Jianghu vs. Cultivation World:** This chapter sits firmly in a Jianghu (martial arts world) sub-setting. The Wild Wolf Gang is a mortal-world organization, but their tactics—violent, political, espionage-based—are the exact same behaviors that high-level cultivators use at a cosmic scale. This chapter is a microcosm of the entire novel's philosophy: the rules of survival don't change, only the scale of the power involved. The paranoia Han Li uses against a gang spy is the same paranoia he will use against immortal emperors.

**The Unremarkable Mask (不起眼的面具):** The kitchen steward is a perfect example of a classic xianxia trope: the deadly inconspicuous character. In a world where status and power are everything, hiding in plain sight as a lowly nobody is the most effective camouflage. This steward's humble role is his greatest weapon. This reinforces the novel's dark logic that everyone is a potential threat, and the most dangerous enemy is often the one you've already dismissed.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the Character Growth:** Han Li doesn't panic. He doesn't hope for the best. His first instinct is to hide, calculate, and survive. This isn't a hero's journey; it's a survivor's manual. Notice how his past trauma (Doctor Mo) has directly shaped his present behavior.
- **The Fragmented Conversation Trap:** A weaker story would have had the bad guys conveniently lay out their entire plan in clear, loud voices. *RMJI* doesn't do that. The wind snatches away most of the words, leaving Han Li (and the reader) with tantalizing, terrifying fragments. This is brilliant—it mirrors the real-world difficulty of gathering intelligence and raises the stakes for the coming chapters.
- **The Final Twist:** The chapter's real punch is the identity reveal. That guy selling rabbits? A spy. This is a cornerstone of the Mortal Stream worldview. No one is safe. No one is what they seem. Han Li's world just got a whole lot more dangerous, and his paranoia just got a whole lot more justified. Get ready for the consequences.
