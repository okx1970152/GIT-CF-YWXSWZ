## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks a crucial transition in Han Li's survival marathon. Fresh off his desperate gamble to obtain the *Drop Dust Pills*, he now faces the most daunting logistical challenge yet: finding a safe, spiritually-charged hole to disappear into for three to five years of Core Formation seclusion. The entire chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream's quiet, logistical tension—there are no explosions, no duels, no betrayals. Just a man running the numbers on his own immortality, meticulously eliminating every variable that could get him killed while his Core is half-formed. Get ready for a deep dive into the slow, brutal grind of advancement.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Drop Dust Prize**: Han Li examines his newly acquired *Drop Dust Pills*, confirming that his desperate flight was worth it. He and Qu Hun now have a slightly better chance at Core Formation.
- **The Cost-Benefit of Core Formation**: The narration lays out the brutal economics of breaking through. *Snow Spirit Water* and *Skyfire Liquid* are practically mandatory, and most cultivators can only afford one or two auxiliary medicines. Han Li's decade of preparation with the Heaven Vial allows him to stockpile over a dozen, a luxury that would make any other cultivator green with envy.
- **The Looming Clock**: Core Formation is not a weekend project. It takes three to five years of absolute isolation, emotional stability, and zero combat. Any disturbance could cripple his cultivation forever. This frames his need for a perfect hideout as a life-or-death necessity.
- **Finding the Hole**: Han Li methodically charts his course, performing multiple late-direction changes to shake any potential pursuit before committing to his final destination.
- **Sea Ape Island Introduced**: Han Li selects an anomalously peaceful, low-stakes island known for its mediocre spiritual vein and lack of high-level competition. It is a "free city" for low-level cultivators and utterly beneath the notice of any major power—perfect for a man who needs to be invisible for half a decade.
- **The Quiet Cost of Departure**: Han Li reflects on the fate of his old cave on Small Ring Island, especially the exotic insects he had to abandon. He accepts the loss with cold pragmatism, calculating the odds of his decoy cave holding up and leaving the rest to fate. He and Qu Hun enter their separate chambers.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter provides a rare, non-combat look at the **structure of a cultivation bottleneck**. In xianxia fiction, breakthroughs are not just about accumulating power; they are distinct, formulaic processes that often involve specific ingredients, long periods of seclusion, and a high risk of failure. The "medicinal cocktail" approach Han Li uses—consuming multiple auxiliary pills alongside the core ingredients—is a well-known trope. However, the novel grounds it in a brutal economic reality: most cultivators are too poor to do it properly. Han Li's advantage, born from the Heaven Vial, is that he can sidestep the world's scarcity logic, turning a *four-leaf clover* advantage into a life-saving edge.

## Reading Guide
This is a **Logistics Chapter**, and in the Mortal Stream, logistics are their own kind of battlefield. Note the quiet paranoia: Han Li changes directions multiple times not because he saw a tail, but because the *risk of a tail* was unacceptable. The decision to abandon his insect collection—a significant asset—for the sake of a clean getaway is a classic "must-win-retreat" gambit. Pay attention to how the *Sea Ape Island* is described: it's chosen precisely because it's unremarkable, poor, and full of low-level cultivators. In a world where everyone is hunting for treasure, the safest place to hide is often the one no one thinks is worth looking at. This chapter sets the stage for the longest and most vulnerable period of Han Li's journey so far.
