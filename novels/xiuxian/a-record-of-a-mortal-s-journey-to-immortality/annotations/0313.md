## Chapter Overview

So the victory lap was a little premature. Just when Han Li was feeling good about bagging the young prince and his steward, the chapter throws a proper curveball. The bald giant from the previous chapter has finished cooking. And by *finished cooking*, we mean he has turned into a two-zhang-tall, horned, fanged, scaled nightmare with zero human left in his eyes. This is not a simple power-up. This is a full demonic-transformation nope button. Han Li’s top brass magic tool, the Sky-Covering Bell, gets torn apart from the inside like paper. His beast puppets keep the thing at bay, but nothing sticks. The chapter transitions from a clean capture to a desperate holding action, forcing Han Li to reach for his final trump card. It is the classic Mortal Stream pivot: the moment you start celebrating a win, the system reminds you that survival is a sliding scale, not a destination.

## Key Plot Points

*   **Quick Cleanup**: Returning to the battlefield, Han Li finds a moment of calm. The surviving black-robed cultivators flee without much of a fight.
*   **The Bell Breaks**: The Sky-Covering Bell, Han Li’s reliable trapping tool, starts bulging outward under relentless internal blows. The monster inside is coming out.
*   **Emergence of the Demon**: The bald giant explodes out of the bell, completely transformed: two *zhang* tall, horns, fangs, a scaled iron tail, and a body covered in dark red demonic patterns. His eyes are pure green slaughter-light.
*   **Ineffective Barrage**: The Meng Mountain Four launch a full volley of their combined magic tools at the demon. They do not leave a single scratch. The demon swats them aside with contemptuous ease.
*   **A Desperate Shield**: The demon nearly tears through the White Phosphorus Shield. The dark-faced old man can barely take a single blow alone. The four are forced to link their mana together just to hold the line.
*   **Han Li Resets**: Recognizing that ordinary top-grade magic tools are utterly useless, Han Li makes the call. He orders the Meng Mountain Four and the beast puppets to hold the front while he sits down to fully activate his Talisman Treasure.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Demonic Transformation / 妖化**: This is not a random monster mutation. In the world of RMJI, certain secret techniques, bloodlines, or forbidden pill overdoses can trigger a state where the cultivator’s body is overtaken by a bestial or demonic nature. The key horror here is that the transformation is *total*—the man is gone, and only the killing instinct remains. This is a recurring trope in xianxia: the fear that the pursuit of power without the proper foundation can lead to the loss of the self. It’s not just a power-up; it’s a tragedy of identity, and a reminder that the system punishes shortcuts even as it tempts you with them.

**Talisman Treasure Fatigue**: The chapter doubles down on the heavy cost of using a Talisman Treasure. Earlier, Han Li used one in the Blood Forbidden Trial and nearly exhausted himself. Here, he needs a dedicated, uninterrupted casting window while his allies hold the line. This reinforces a key RMJI rule: trump cards are not “I win” buttons; they are high-risk, high-cost investments that require the right conditions to use. The strategic layer is not in who has the biggest stick, but in who can afford the time and safety to swing it.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a textbook demonstration of how the Mortal Stream manages pacing. We just had a victory—Han Li captured his targets—but the genre does not allow clean exits. The bald giant’s transformation is not a random power-up; it is a narrative consequence of the desperation and extreme measures characters in this world resort to. Notice how Han Li’s reaction is not panic, but immediate tactical recalibration. He does not ask *why* or *how* at this point; he just identifies the threat level (beyond top-grade tools) and deploys his next asset (Talisman Treasure). Also pay attention to the Meng Mountain Four. They are terrified, but they are also veterans. They link their mana without a second thought. In a world where betrayal is the norm, this moment of desperate cooperation is a rare, fragile thing—and we all know how long *that* usually lasts in RMJI.
