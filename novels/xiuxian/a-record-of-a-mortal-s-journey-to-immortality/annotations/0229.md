## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s core philosophy: **never be the main character in someone else’s drama**. Han Li spends the entire chapter as a silent observer, tucked inside a cloud, watching a brutal, one-sided slaughter unfold below. And honestly? He loves every second of it—not because he’s bloodthirsty, but because he gets to analyze the fight, assess the new top-tier toys (hello, Divine Wind Boat!), and calculate his own odds without lifting a finger. The chapter delivers a visceral lesson in power disparity: when a wealthy sect disciple starts burning mid-grade spirit stones to fuel a giant puppet tiger, there’s no "plucky underdog" comeback—only a messy, desperate rout. The quiet victory isn't in the battle; it's in staying hidden and walking away smarter.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Aftermath of a Failed Escape**: The chapter opens with the surviving cultivators in a state of cold shock after witnessing the first runner get obliterated by a single, monstrous light beam from the forest. Their earlier confidence evaporates.
- **Han Li’s Stealth Game**: From his high-altitude cloud perch, Han Li uses the Divine Wind Boat and the Azure Haze magic tool for concealment. He cleverly moves into a low-hanging gray cloud to fully mask his presence, refusing to intervene or reveal himself.
- **Psychological Collapse**: The besieged cultivators realize their unity is crumbling. Each is secretly hoping someone else will be the next target. An older cultivator attempts a bluff, threatening retribution from the Yuanwu Nation's sects.
- **The Villain’s Unmasking**: The hidden opponent reveals his identity as the burly man from the auction, the Thousand Bamboo School disciple who bought the puppet beast. He openly mocks their threats, stating he’s not even from their country and that a vow means nothing to him.
- **Enter the Giant Puppet Tiger**: A colossal puppet tiger, five to six *zhang* tall, emerges from the forest with the burly man riding on its head. It’s the source of the devastating light beams.
- **The Breaking Point**: The cultivators manage to hold off one super beam through teamwork, but their faces go pale from the spiritual power drain. Their hope shatters when the burly man casually tosses a mid-grade spirit stone into the tiger to fuel a second shot.
- **Rout and Massacre**: Facing certain death and depleted mana, the remaining cultivators abandon all pretense of unity and scatter in a desperate flight for survival. The giant tiger picks them off one by one with its beam.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**"Puppet" (傀儡 / Kuilei)** in Chinese xianxia is far more than just a walking statue. It descends from a long tradition of ancient automaton lore, from the legendary mechanical birds of the *Mozi* to the terrifying wooden figures of the *Records of the Grand Historian*. In the Mortal Stream, puppets are the ultimate expression of "wealth is power"—they represent a permanent, expendable labor force and army that doesn't eat, sleep, or betray you (unless your control talisman wears off). The burly man controlling the crowd from within the forest is a classic "Puppet Cultivator" archetype.

**Spirit Stones (灵石)** aren't just money—they are the universal fuel of the cultivation world. A mid-grade spirit stone is a significant asset for any Qi Condensation or even Foundation Establishment cultivator. Using one as a single-use battery for a single attack is the equivalent of burning a luxury sports car's engine to boil a pot of water. It’s a statement of overwhelming resource disparity, and it signals that the Thousand Bamboo School operates on an entirely different economic scale than these local Yuanwu Nation disciples.

**Scattering (一哄而散)** is a very real survival tactic in the Mortal Stream. Unlike western fantasy where a party fights to the last breath for friendship and glory, in Han Li’s world, a group under certain death will almost always dissolve into a "every man for himself" rout. The smartest calculation for any survivor at that point is to be *slightly* faster than the slowest comrade.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a perfect example of why Mortal Stream fiction feels so "real" to its readers. There are no dramatic last stands, no hidden aces up the heroes' sleeves, no "power of friendship" speeches. Just cold, hard resource calculus. The cultivators aren't cowardly—they're rational. They know they're outgunned, and staying to fight is a waste of a precious lifespan.

Watch how carefully Han Li positions himself. He’s not a spectator for fun; he’s gathering intelligence. He notes the puppet tiger’s attack speed, its spirit stone consumption rate, and the psychological state of everyone involved. He’s not hoping for a dramatic intervention—he’s running a silent simulation: *"If I were the target, when would I run?"* That’s the kind of high-level, quiet victory that defines the protagonist of this story. He’s not the strongest, but he’s always the one who does the math.
