## Chapter Overview

And *breathe*. After the chaos of enemy puppets, a poisoned corpse, and a near-possession, we finally get a chance for our favorite mortal to simply… sit on a stool and wait. But of course, the Thousand Bamboo School isn't done yet, and neither is the Mortal Stream's love for complications. Just when the formation starts to show real stress, a **thunderous** savior arrives: a corpulent, lightning-wielding Core Formation cultivator named Lei Wanhe. This chapter is a classic "big gun arrives to clean house" beat, but the real meat is in the aftermath: the quiet negotiation, the careful omission of truth, and Han Li's razor-sharp calculation about how much to reveal. Get ready for a fat man with a terrifyingly sharp mind and a corpse with a very inconvenient secret.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li reflects on Senior Brother Lin's death, marking this as the second cultivator whose Primordial Soul he has personally extinguished. The narration emphasizes the rarity of using Primordial Soul Departure in a fight—a desperate gamble that only pays off for Nascent Soul cultivators or the terminally unlucky.
- The Thousand Bamboo School continues its relentless puppet assault on the Reversed Five Elements Formation for over an hour. The formation finally begins to flicker with azure light, giving Huang Long hope that it can be breached.
- Just as the attackers prepare a stronger push, a thunderous roar shakes the heavens. A Core Formation cultivator has arrived, and he is *not* happy.
- Lei Wanhe, a grotesquely fat man wielding lightning-element silver light, effortlessly annihilates all the Thousand Bamboo School pursuers in a single, devastating attack. Huang Long doesn't even get to finish his plea for mercy.
- Han Li opens the formation and greets his savior with formal respect. Lei Wanhe introduces himself and immediately cuts to the chase, asking why the Thousand Bamboo School attacked.
- Han Li delivers a carefully edited version of events, omitting the trip to the Tianxing Sect market and his role in extinguishing Senior Brother Lin's soul. Lei Wanhe seems to accept the story, even expressing some sympathy for the long-dead "problem child."
- The chapter ends on a tense note: Lei Wanhe asks Han Li if he saw Senior Brother Lin's Primordial Soul escape before his body died. The question hangs in the air, and Han Li's internal alarm bells are surely ringing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Primordial Soul Departure (元神出体):** This is a high-risk, high-reward desperation technique. The text explicitly states that it's not something used lightly in battle because of the long casting time and the caster's immobility. This is a crucial rule in the Mortal Stream's combat logic: if you have time to let your soul flee, you're not in a real fight; you're in a siege or a slow-death scenario. Only Nascent Soul cultivators have the "instant," cheat-code version. This reinforces why Han Li was able to extinguish Senior Brother Lin's soul—the man was already trapped and dying, not in the middle of a duel.

**The Reversed Five Elements Formation (颠倒五行阵):** The fact that an hour-long bombardment by numerous puppets only *starts* to affect the formation speaks volumes about its quality. A lesser formation would have shattered in minutes. The novel uses this to demonstrate that Han Li's investment (and his strategic foresight) is paying dividends. It is not just a defensive wall; it's a statement of preparedness that earns him the respect of a Core Formation cultivator.

**The "Thank-You-For-Teaching" Gift (赏赐):** While not explicitly used here, Lei Wanhe's arrival and behavior perfectly set up the classic "Uncle-Master" dynamic. He is not a kindly old grandpa; he is a Core Formation elder who has just extended a massive favor. In the Dark Forest of cultivation, such a favor never comes for free. Han Li's careful omission of the market trip and the soul's fate is not just paranoia; it's survival. He is preparing the ground for an expected future negotiation, where he will need to "thank" Uncle-Master Lei properly. This is a textbook example of the Mortal Stream's social logic: every rescue is an investment, and every debt is a trap.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in the "quiet aftermath" of a crisis. There are no more explosions or sword fights, but the tension is perfectly preserved. Pay close attention to how Han Li manages the flow of information to Lei Wanhe. What he says is polite and respectful, but what he *doesn't* say—the trip to the market, the destruction of the soul—is where the real story is. The chapter ends with a perfectly sharp question from Lei Wanhe. Our hero is a cunning survivor, but now he has to navigate a conversation with a man who can kill him with a flick of his finger. How will he spin this? The answer is coming, and the wait is deliciously agonizing.
