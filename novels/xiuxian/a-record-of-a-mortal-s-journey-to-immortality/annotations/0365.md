## Chapter Overview

We open with a life-or-death standoff in the sky: the masked woman (our familiar Nascent Soul cultivator, Nan Gong Wan) has used a tactical feint—pretending to be exhausted—to trap Wang Chan and the flamboyant man in a relentless sword-light barrage. But her ploy is a double-edged sword: her magic power is truly depleted, her vital treasure is shattered, and her secret technique is burning through her life force. Just as the demonic duo break free and bear down on her, a streak of white light shoots up from the forest below. Someone snatches her and bolts. That someone is Han Li. And he has prepared a nasty surprise for the pursuers.

## Key Plot Points

- Nan Gong Wan lures Wang Chan’s group into a close-range range by feigning weakness; her sword-light cage engulfs them, but they survive thanks to the flamboyant man’s powerful multicolored light and Dong Xuan’er’s contributions.
- Wan’s true state is revealed: she fought a Core Formation cultivator to a near-draw, exhausted her mana, damaged her lifebound treasure, and has been propped up only by a life-draining secret technique. She deliberately descended near a mid-Foundation Establishment cultivator (Han Li) hoping to drag him into the fight as a wild card.
- Han Li initially hides, unwilling to be involved. When he sees Wan is truly about to fall, he breaks his own rule: he acts, not for profit, but because he cannot abandon her.
- Han Li seizes Wan mid-air and flees on his Divine Wind Boat. Wang Chan and the flamboyant man chase, but Dong Xuan’er hesitates—she recognizes Han Li.
- Just as the demonic cultivators close in, the scene shifts: the forest is gone, replaced by towering stone pillars. Han Li has laid an Illusion Formation. His earlier “panic” was a performance; his real plan was to bait them into the trap.
- Wang Chan and the flamboyant man are trapped but furious. They realize the formation is weak and they can break it quickly—then the flamboyant man reveals a backup: Dong Xuan’er is still outside. She should intercept Han Li.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Stage Names (Core Formation vs. Nascent Soul)** – In this chapter’s context, “Core Formation cultivator (结丹期修士)” refers to a cultivator who has condensed a Golden Core 金丹. This is the stage *after* Foundation Establishment and *before* Nascent Soul. Although the masked woman is Nan Gong Wan (a Nascent Soul cultivator from earlier arcs), her power is heavily restricted here, so she is described by her *current* functional tier. In *RMJI*, a cultivator’s stage name tracks the refined energy body they have achieved: a Core is solid; a Nascent Soul is a “living second self” that can leave the body, flee, or possess another. The distinction matters for combat logic, but in the thick of battle, “Core Formation” is used as a general indicator of oppressive power.
- **"Dong Xuan’er" and the Disguise Continuity** – Readers may notice that Dong Xuan’er, formerly a Yellow Maple Valley disciple, is now aligned with the Blissful Union Sect. Her hesitation when she recognizes Han Li is a quiet echo of their shared past: she was once a beautiful, proud senior sister of the same sect. In the cruel world of cultivation, sects and loyalties shift, but a moment of recognition can still break a combat rhythm.
- **Illusion Formation (幻阵)** – A “phantom array” or illusion formation is one of the most common defensive tools in xianxia. It does not harm directly but confuses the senses, misdirects movement, and buys time. Han Li’s use of it here is a textbook application: it is not meant to kill, but to delay a superior force long enough for an escape. The fact that he set it up *in advance* (while observing from below) shows his core combat doctrine: layer your exits before you engage.

### Lizard Cuts Off Its Tail (壁虎断尾)

A Chinese idiom describing a strategic sacrifice where one gives up a valuable asset to survive a greater threat. In *RMJI*, it reflects the principle that no victory is clean; every escape costs something.

## Reading Guide

- This is a textbook demonstration of the Mortal Stream’s “layered retreat” strategy: Han Li doesn’t just flee—he sets up the terrain *before* committing to the rescue. Every panicked expression is a poker face; every delay is a planned window.
- Nan Gong Wan’s plan to “drag a passerby into the fight” is a classic Dark Forest move: in a world without law, any neutral actor is either prey or a potential emergency resource. It backfires only because she didn’t know the “passerby” already owed her.
- Dong Xuan’er’s hesitation is a rare human crack in the ruthless machine. The chapter ends on a question: will she help the demonic path, or let the past weigh the scales?
- Pay attention to the “Lizard Cuts Off Its Tail” logic at play: everyone here is burning their reserves (life-force, secret arts, foresight) to survive. No one wins cleanly in a Mortal Stream fight.
