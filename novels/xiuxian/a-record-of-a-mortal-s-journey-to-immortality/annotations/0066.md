## Chapter Overview
Han Li consolidates his position as the sole master of Divine Hand Valley, establishing a bizarre rule that turns him into a sect legend. But beneath the surface of this new authority, old debts linger. The chapter masterfully balances Han Li’s growing power with the invisible chain that still binds him—a hidden threat planted by a dead man.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li negotiates for exclusive use of Divine Hand Valley; Sect Leader Ma eagerly agrees and even offers a maid, which Han Li reluctantly declines due to the risk of exposing his secrets.
- Han Li installs a bell at the valley entrance, mandating that anyone—even high-ranking members—must ring it to see him. Initial backlash from higher-ups dissolves after he saves a critically injured Protector.
- Through his medical exploits, Han Li becomes a revered figure, surpassing Doctor Mo’s former status. The Sect Leaders now address him as “Physician Han.”
- Li Feiyu remains the one person who treats Han Li exactly as before—calling him by name without deference—which Han Li secretly appreciates.
- Han Li encounters Wu Yan, now a Seven Peaks Hall core disciple, suffering from a stubborn illness. Han Li cures him in two days but deliberately increases the medicine’s potency, causing extra pain—a small revenge for past rudeness.
- Han Li continues his daily routine: using the vial’s green liquid to accelerate herbs, refining medicines (most for his own cultivation), and sitting in Doctor Mo’s old armchair.
- The chapter ends with a grim reminder: Han Li has examined his body and found a trace of yin-cold energy lurking in his dantian, confirming that Doctor Mo’s posthumous constraint remains active. The journey set for a year later is inevitable.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **鸣钟而见 (Bell-Ringing Protocol)**: In traditional Chinese society, a bell or gong was used to signal important announcements or summon attendants. By establishing this rule, Han Li signals that his time is valuable and that access to him is a privilege, not a right—a classic “divine physician” trope in wuxia/xianxia, where rare skill earns eccentric treatment.
- **太师椅 (Taishi Armchair)**: A high-backed wooden armchair associated with authority and prestige. Sitting in it signals that Han Li has inherited not only Doctor Mo’s physical objects but his symbolic role as the sect’s go-to healer.
- **清灵散 (Clear Spirit Powder)**: A common antidote in cultivation fiction, often used as a catch-all detoxification agent. Its failure here underscores that the yin-cold energy is not ordinary poison but something more sinister—perhaps linked to a cultivator’s soul or forbidden technique.
- **余毒未清 (Residual Poison)**: The concept of a hidden curse or poison that remains dormant for a fixed period is a staple of xianxia tension. It forces the protagonist into a high-stakes deadline, creating urgency beneath the surface of daily life.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a chapter that feels like a warm bath—until you realize the water is slowly getting colder. Han Li has made it. He’s the undisputed medical authority of the sect, respected by high and low, with a private valley and a quirky rule that only enhances his mystique. Even his old bully Wu Yan comes crawling for help. But the real genius of this chapter is how it lulls you into comfort before yanking the leash. That lingering yin-cold thing in his dantian? That’s Doctor Mo’s final gift—a countdown Han Li can’t stop. So enjoy the scene of him lounging in the dead man’s chair, because the clock is ticking. Also, Li Feiyu continues to be the best.
