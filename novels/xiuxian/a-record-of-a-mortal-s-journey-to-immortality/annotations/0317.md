## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream logistics: the war council before the storm. The scene is quiet—a room, tea, greetings—but every word is a piece on a game board. Han Li’s four senior siblings come to hear his report on the Black Fiend Cult, and what follows is a classic xianxia debate: Do we charge in, or do we call for backup? More importantly, *who* is willing to risk everything? Get ready for a chapter where the real battle isn’t swords and spells, but loyalty, principle, and the cold calculation of survival.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Power Shift:** Wu Xuan is visibly shaken when he realizes Han Li has broken into mid Foundation Establishment, while he himself remains stuck at early—a stark reminder of the social and hierarchical tensions within even a single master’s lineage.
- **The War Council:** Han Li gives a detailed account of the Black Fiend Cult’s strength, revealing multiple Foundation Establishment Blood Servants and powerful Cult Masters, crushing any hope of an easy fight.
- **The Prohibition Problem:** The Black Fiend Cult’s lair is inside the Yue Imperial Palace—a location strictly forbidden to the Seven Sects disciples. This creates a high-stakes political and moral dilemma.
- **The Fracture:** The group splits along ideological lines. Liu Jing, driven by a need for justice, resolves to attack. Song Meng and Zhong Weiniang pledge their support. Wu Xuan, ever cautious, refuses to break the rules without explicit permission from their Master.
- **The Final Word:** Cornered by Zhong Weiniang’s clumsy taunt and his own reasoning, Han Li agrees to join the raid, citing both his sense of responsibility and a cold curiosity about the Black Fiend Cult Leader.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Blood Sacrifice (血祭) in Cultivation:** The term is used to describe a forbidden technique where a cultivator literally drains the life essence and blood of others to boost their own power. This isn’t just a magical spell; it’s a socio-economic crime in the cultivation world. It’s the ultimate taboo because it bypasses the slow, sustainable path of cultivation, turning other cultivators into disposable resources. In orthodox sects, practitioners of such arts are hunted down with extreme prejudice.

**The Seven Sects’ Prohibition of the Palace:** Why can’t cultivators just waltz into the mortal emperor’s palace? In a world of superhuman power, a clear separation between the secular nation-state and the cultivating sects is a fundamental political treaty. Breaking it is not a matter of physical danger, but of breaking a ‘sacred’ agreement that keeps the peace between the mortal world and the world of immortals. It’s a high-risk political act, not just a simple trespass.

**The ‘Blood Hand Reaper’ Archetype:** Liu Jing is characterized as someone who is "naturally intolerant of evil." This is a classic xianxia archetype—the righteous pathwalker who believes action is a moral imperative, even if it means bending the rules. His willingness to bear the blame alone marks him as a leader who is trusted, even by a hothead like Song Meng.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a **force layout** in its most elegant form. Pay attention to the dynamics within Li Huayuan’s disciples:
1.  **Liu Jing** is the Commander. He is principled, authoritative, and willing to take personal responsibility. He is the moral core of this squad.
2.  **Song Meng** is the Enforcer. A battle junkie who needs no more motivation than a fight. His loyalty to Liu Jing is shown by his immediate silence after a rebuke.
3.  **Zhong Weiniang** is the Lieutenant. Her infatuation with Liu Jing makes her fiercely loyal and eager to recruit more power (Han Li) for his cause.
4.  **Wu Xuan** is the Dissenter. His caution is rational, but his reasoning is framed by jealousy and personal animosity toward Han Li. He is a weak link in the chain, concerned more with rules than with the mission.

Han Li’s decision is perfectly in character. He doesn’t join out of a sense of heroic camaraderie. He weighs the risks (a dangerous raid vs. political fallout) and the personal stake (he *did* start this mess). His final line—admitting curiosity about the Sect Leader—is his true motivation: a calculated need to understand a high-value target and potential threat.
