## Chapter Overview
The demonic path patriarchs, having secured a precarious alliance with Han Li at its center, finally trigger the next phase of the Void Heaven Palace expedition. The inner hall’s passages are opened, but before anyone steps inside, the old freaks open their vaults. Han Li, the early-Core Formation pawn, is lavishly equipped with a spirit-calming bracelet, a Nascent Soul-grade Talisman Treasure, and an almost comically heavy scale armor—not out of kindness, but to ensure a disposable asset survives long enough to serve the plan. Meanwhile, two white-robed elders of the Star Palace watch from the shadows, their cold analysis cutting far closer to the truth than the demonic cultivators would like. As Han Li finally crosses a suspended jade bridge toward a floating celestial pavilion, the chapter closes on a vertigo-inducing image of an abyss beneath him and an ancient, jade-carved treasure vault ahead. Welcome to the gate of the inner trial, where every gift is a leash and every alliance is a countdown to betrayal.

## Key Plot Points
- The demonic and orthodox cultivators disperse into the passages of the inner hall; Wan Tianming, Extreme Yin, Man Brutal Beard, and the Confucian-robed elder each choose their own path, while Han Li is silently pressured to enter first.
- Extreme Yin Patriarch gifts Han Li a bracelet of four **Bodhi Beads**, a rare spirit-calming treasure that guards against inner demons and illusory effects—a significant asset for the Extreme Illusory Mirage Realm ahead.
- The Confucian-robed elder, seemingly a representative of Wan Tianming’s faction, produces a **Verdant Nether Needle Talisman Treasure**, confirming that even the orthodox side is invested in Han Li’s survival past the first gate.
- Man Brutal Beard loans Han Li the **“Imperial Scale Armor,”** a scale-covered inner armor, with a chilling proviso—it is a loan, not a gift, and his smirk carries an implicit threat: *do not disappoint me*.
- Han Li evaluates each gift with cold, transactional clarity: the beads may be reclaimed, the talisman treasure is a consumable, and the armor is a loan. He accepts them all, knowing he is a pawn but also calculating how to survive long enough to flip the board.
- The two Star Palace Law Enforcement Elders observe the scene remotely. Their dialogue reveals deep institutional knowledge: they suspect the demonic cultivators have obtained an ancient remnant beast, and they discuss the legendary **Heaven-Mending Pill** that was once nearly retrieved from the Void Heaven Cauldron. They decide to follow secretly—not to stop the attempt, but to intercept the pill if it surfaces.
- Han Li, after a long climb, emerges into a cylindrical void space dominated by a floating jade pavilion—the **Precious Light Pavilion**—visible only through a gap in the mist wall and connected by an exquisite jade bridge.
- The chapter closes on Han Li’s brief, chilling glimpse into the abyss beneath the bridge—a darkness that cannot be measured, only felt.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Bodhi Beads (婆罗珠 / Suo Luo Zhu):** In real-world Buddhist traditions, *bodhi* beads (often made from *boshi* wood) are used for counting recitations during meditation, serving as a focus tool to calm the mind and suppress distracting thoughts. The novel transmutes this into a spiritual artifact that actively resists illusion and inner demons. The four-bead bracelet is astronomically rare—one such bead alone would be a prize for most cultivators. Extreme Yin’s willingness to part with four at once signals the extreme danger of what lies ahead.

**The “Loan” Culture of Cultivation Treasures:** In xianxia’s dark forest economics, powerful cultivators rarely *give* high-grade treasures to subordinates. They *lend* them—creating a loan relationship that can be revoked at will. This chapter is a perfect textbook case: the Imperial Scale Armor is a loan, the Bodhi Beads will likely be reclaimed, and only the single-use Talisman Treasure is an outright gift. Every item is a leash, and the receiver knows it. There is no gratitude in the Mortal Stream—only temporary resource allocation.

**Talisman Treasure (符宝):** A consumable artifact created by sealing a portion of a matured magic treasure’s power into talisman form. Han Li’s earlier battles have shown that even a “trash” Talisman Treasure can turn a fight. A Nascent Soul-grade one like the Verdant Nether Needle is an entirely different order of power—a trump card that, once used, is gone forever. This makes it a perfect fit for the Mortal Stream’s logic: the most powerful items are also the most temporary.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, fellow Daoists, because the payoff chapter is here, and it *delivers*. If you’ve been following the long, grinding arc of Han Li being cornered into this expedition, this chapter is the moment when the demonic patriarchs finally *spend* their chips—and what chips they are! A Nascent Soul-level Talisman Treasure, an armor that once served a late-stage Nascent Soul cultivator, and spirit-calming beads that can resist illusions? Han Li has just been given the best equipment package of his entire cultivation life… and it’s all borrowed, leased, or destined to be reclaimed. The tension is exquisite: every gift comes with a silent price tag, and Han Li knows it.

But the real treat here is the **Star Palace elders**. Their cold, offhand conversation in the back half of the chapter is a masterwork of dark forest exposition. With just a few lines, the author confirms that: (1) the elders know about the ancient remnant beast theory, (2) they know about the previous failed attempt to steal the Heaven-Mending Pill from the Void Heaven Cauldron, and (3) they’ve already decided to intercept the pill if it surfaces. They are not watching to stop the theft; they are watching to *take the prize from the thieves*. It’s a perfect reminder that in the Void Heaven Palace, everyone—including the silent, meditating white-robed figures—is a predator.

And then there’s that final image: a jade bridge to a floating celestial pavilion, and an abyss below that you cannot see the bottom of. This is xianxia world-building at its finest: a threshold that looks like paradise, yet is built over nothing but darkness.
