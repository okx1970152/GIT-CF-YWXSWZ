## Chapter Overview

*Record scratch. Freeze frame.* Yep, that’s our guy Han Li, buried under tens of thousands of ravenous beetles, turning himself into a living, walking anti-teleportation ninja suit. Welcome to the most gloriously ridiculous heist of the Void Heaven Palace arc. In this chapter, our ever-cautious protagonist faces the ultimate choose-your-own-adventure dilemma: one tower, three ancient treasures, and a system that kicks you out the moment you touch one. But Han Li, being Han Li, doesn’t just pick the shiniest toy. He pulls a trick straight from his own forgotten lab notes—using the Gold Devourer Beetles to create a temporary “spiritual blackout” armor—so he can walk the catwalk, grab TWO prizes, and flip the bird to the ancient restriction. The result? A hauntingly beautiful scene of a blue-and-black energy tug-of-war, a shattered armor, and a very satisfied Core Formation cultivator teleported into an empty stone chamber. Get ready for some serious beetle-based strategy.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li zeroes in on three ancient treasures in the tower: a mysterious dark red cloak (feathers flickering with blood-light), a golden mirror, and a five-colored copper ring set. Each one represents a distinct tactical appeal—defense, raw power, and an unknown set-piece surprise.
- Han Li’s internal debate lays bare his survival logic: the cloak offers *immediate* utility (defense/concealment) in the life-or-death inner hall; the mirror is a safe but unexciting power boost; the copper ring is a gamble on a hidden surprise. For a moment, even his legendary composure cracks under the pressure of such tempting loot.
- He recalls a forgotten experiment from his cave dwelling days: using the Gold Devourer Beetles’ spiritual-devouring ability to create a temporary “spiritual disconnection” from the environment—a trick born from Xin Ruyin’s theoretical conjecture on countering spatial restrictions.
- Han Li executes the plan: unleashes tens of thousands of beetles to cover his body, commands them to synchronize their devouring hum, and creates a pitch-black “armor” of pure energy absorption that severs his connection to the surrounding spiritual energy.
- He seizes the cloak first, triggering the spatial restriction’s teleportation. The blue light of the restriction surges but is absorbed by the beetle-armor, buying him precious seconds.
- The armor begins to buckle under the blue light’s relentless pressure, but Han Li forces his way to the copper ring and grabs it.
- The black armor shatters, and the restriction sweeps him away. He lands in a quiet, empty stone chamber, shaking, with two treasures in hand and a cloud of confused beetles hovering overhead.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Spatial Restrictions & the “Anti-Teleportation” Trick:** In xianxia, high-level restrictions that forcibly teleport a person are usually absolute—once triggered, there’s no negotiation. The system doesn’t care about your feelings, your power level, or how close you are to a second treasure. It just *relocates* you. Breaking one requires either prodigious formation mastery (years of study) or brute force (Nascent Soul-level output minimum). Xin Ruyin’s theoretical conjecture—severing the cultivator’s connection to all ambient spiritual energy—is brilliant precisely because it’s *impractical* for normal cultivators. No cultivator can function without at least *some* link to Heaven and Earth qi. Han Li’s ability to execute this trick is a direct consequence of owning the single most broken spiritual parasite in the cultivation world: the Gold Devourer Beetles. They don’t just *store* energy; they *consume it*—a perfect counter to a system that relies on continuous energy interaction.

**The Outer’s Record of Formation-Breaking:** This is our first real glimpse into Xin Ruyin’s depth as a formation genius. The fact that she not only described a theoretical countermeasure but also *admitted in writing* that it was probably impossible shows a rare intellectual honesty. In the Mortal Stream, knowledge is often hoarded or distorted; Ruyin’s willingness to document a “failed” hypothesis is a subtle testament to her commitment to the craft, even if she didn’t live to see it proven right.

**Beetle Synergy & Collective Behavior:** The synchronization of the Gold Devourer Beetles’ hum is a critical detail. Han Li doesn’t just command them; he *harmonizes* their activity. This mirrors ancient Chinese military and Daoist thought: when a collective finds a common rhythm, its power transcends the sum of its parts. For the beetles, that rhythm is the “devouring resonance”—a unified eating pattern that creates a stable field of energy absorption rather than chaotic consumption. It’s the difference between a crowd and an army.

### Beetle-Armor (黑甲)

A temporary armor formed by tens of thousands of Gold Devourer Beetles synchronizing their energy-devouring hum, creating a field that severs the wearer’s connection to ambient spiritual energy.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in how the Mortal Stream turns “loot rooms” into high-stakes tactical puzzles. Han Li doesn’t just pick the best item; he performs a full cost-benefit analysis on three distinct profiles, evaluates the risk of a novel plan, and executes a heist that relies on a synergy between his own forgotten experiment and his rarest asset. The beetle-armor scene is visually stunning—imagine a man slowly encased in living, breathing, buzzing darkness, moving like a terrifying oil slick toward his prize. The blue light’s desperate surge against the black armor creates a beautiful tension between order (the system’s automatic defense) and chaos (the devouring swarm). The payoff is a two-for-one treasure haul that feels earned, not lucky. Pay attention to Han Li’s internal restraint: even as his heart races, his movements remain slow and deliberate—a lesson in the discipline required to pull off a high-risk high-reward play. And the final image of him in an empty chamber, clutching his new prizes while the beetles hover above? Perfectly sets up his next move: assessment, cataloging, and planning. Because in this world, grabbing the loot is only half the battle.
