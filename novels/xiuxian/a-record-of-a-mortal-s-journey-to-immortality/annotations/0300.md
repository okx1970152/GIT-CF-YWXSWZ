## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a quiet, space-clearing breather after the intense events at the market town. Han Li effortlessly concludes the transaction with the mortal merchant and his granddaughter, obtaining the priceless hidden technique manual without bloodshed—a masterclass in the Mortal Stream's non-violent resolution. However, just as he thinks the ordeal is over, the old man springs his real trap: not a physical attack, but a social one—a plea for his granddaughter to become Han Li's disciple. This sudden pivot from survival negotiation to generational scheming catches our hero off guard, offering a rare glimpse of his flustered humanity before he slams the door shut with icy pragmatism. Yet, in a surprising twist typical of this novel’s nuanced kindness, Han Li doesn’t just walk away; he offers a lifeline, setting up future possibilities that feel earned rather than forced.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li examines the ancient beast-hide book and confirms it contains a valuable passive qi convergence technique, written in an ancient script he can only partially read. He finds the key oral formula on the last pages.
- He places the book in a jade box and tells the merchant to name his price, fulfilling his end of the bargain and defusing the tension of the earlier eavesdropping.
- The terrified merchant and his granddaughter, realizing Han Li is not going to kill them, ask for time to discuss what they want. Han Li grants this, showing his capacity for measured, non-vicious pragmatism.
- After their discussion, they return with a shocking request: for Han Li to take the girl as his personal disciple.
- Han Li is visibly stunned by the request, labeling it an absurd notion given his own precarious position in the cultivation world.
- He checks the girl’s spiritual roots, confirming she is a highly desirable dual-spiritual-root cultivator—a testament to her potential and the old man’s desperation to secure her future.
- Han Li refuses the request bluntly but, after a moment’s calculation, proposes an alternative: to recommend her to his Senior Martial Brother Ma, a Foundation Establishment elder who lacks a disciple.
- The chapter ends with the grandfather and granddaughter stunned and disappointed, but with a proverbial door left slightly ajar.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The social act of 'taking a master' (拜师) is a cornerstone of the cultivation world, carrying far more weight than a simple student-teacher relationship. In Chinese culture, this is a formal bond akin to a second filial relationship; the disciple is expected to serve, obey, and protect the master’s legacy, while the master is obligated to provide guidance, resources, and lifelong shelter. This is why the old man is so desperate to secure one for his granddaughter—it’s the only real social security in a world of predators. Han Li’s instant refusal is not just personal caution; it’s a systemic rejection of the immense responsibility that comes with true mastership. His alternative—a recommendation—is a culturally clever dodge: he offers the benefit of connection without the crushing weight of the full master-disciple contract.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a perfect palate cleanser that highlights the *other* half of survival in the Mortal Stream: not the blade, but the social contract. You can practically feel the old man's relief turn into scheming ambition the moment he realizes Han Li isn't a murder-hungry villain. The request for Han Li to take a disciple is a brilliant ‘social tribulation’—a conflict not solved with qi and talismans, but with social nuance and risk calculation. Watch how Han Li doesn't just feel annoyed; he instantly runs a cost-benefit analysis on her aptitude, his own schedule, and the political capital of making an introduction to Senior Ma. This is the mark of a true veteran—solving a problem without spending a single drop of mana. And that sly wink at the end, leaving the door open? It's a reminder that even in this cold, dark forest, the little seeds of human connection, planted for purely pragmatic reasons, might just grow into something more useful down the line.
