## Chapter Overview

When the house you’re holed up in has a terrified coward waving a borrowed authority token, your best scout is stuck playing babysitter, and your only attack plan is "wait to die"—it’s time for a protagonist to arrive. And true to form, Han Li and Li Feiyu don’t just show up; they come crashing into the chapter with a blade-happy, combat-ready vengeance. This is not a chapter of subtle maneuvering or careful negotiation. It’s a release valve. After chapters of being hunted, pinned down, and forced into hiding, Li Feiyu finally gets to cut loose—and the result is a short, sharp, brutal engagement that sets the stage for the bigger storm to come.

## Key Plot Points

1. **The paralyzed rear base**: Ma Rong has already secured the non-combatants and is desperate to send scouts when the fat messenger—wielding Sect Leader Wang’s personal token—shows up and freezes all movement. Anyone who disobeys the token risks execution, so Ma Rong is trapped, pacing like "an ant on a hot pan."

2. **Han Li and Li Feiyu arrive**: Unaware of the internal deadlock, the duo runs straight into a Duan Shui Sect patrol less than two li from Li the Elder’s residence. There's no time to hide, no backup to call—just a standoff.

3. **Li Feiyu’s first real fight**: Offering to take point, Li Feiyu seizes a blade, blitzes the nearest enemy, and sends him headless in a single, fluid motion. His speed shocks the Duan Shui Sect leader into ordering an immediate retreat and signal for help.

4. **The ghost kills**: The chapter’s most chilling sequence follows a fleeing Duan Shui Sect disciple who thinks he’s safe, only to feel a sword tip burst from his throat. In his dying moments, he watches a flickering black shadow systematically execute every retreating enemy—one by one, leaving none alive.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Command tokens in Chinese sects**: The token Sect Leader Wang gave to the fat messenger is a classic trope in Jianghu fiction. A *lingpai* (令牌) is not just a piece of wood or metal—it’s a portable fragment of the leader’s authority. Whoever holds it can command subordinates as if the leader himself were speaking. This creates immense narrative tension when the bearer is incompetent or cowardly, because protocol and loyalty to the token override common sense. Ma Rong’s paralysis is not cowardice—it’s the price of rigid hierarchy.

**The "ghost step" evasion technique revisited**: The black shadow that kills the fleeing enemies is clearly Han Li in Smoke Step mode—a technique we saw earlier as a mobility and evasion tool. Now, combined with his bladework and his near-supernatural speed after crossing the fourth layer barrier, Smoke Step has evolved into an assassination-grade technique. The novel emphasizes "忽隐忽现" (flickering in and out of visibility), which in xianxia terms signals that the user’s speed and energy control have reached a point where the eye cannot track them.

**Silent kills and the philosophy of clean execution**: Notice that the Duan Shui Sect disciple dies with a "smile." In many Chinese martial narratives, an opponent’s death is described with either horror or dignity. The fact that this dying man smiles—knowing that the shadow will soon send company—echoes a kind of grim acceptance that is common in ruthless xianxia: in this world, death in battle is so normalized that the only comfort is knowing you won’t die alone.

### Smoke Step (罗烟步)

A high-speed mobility technique that blurs the user's form, allowing evasion at unnatural angles. In this chapter, Han Li uses it for ghost-like assassination.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a palette cleanser. After the dense, tactical setup of the previous chapters—the politics, the token abuse, the siege—we finally get a pure, unfiltered strike. Li Feiyu’s roar is a release of pent-up frustration: he was chased, he was underground, he was useless. Now, with a blade in hand and an enemy in his sights, he reminds us why he earned the nickname "Fierce Tiger." But the true star here is Han Li’s ghost-kill sequence. The description is deliberately clinical: we don’t see his face, only the results. This serves a dual purpose—it keeps his trump card hidden from potential watchers, and it reinforces his transformation into a cold, efficient killer when the situation demands it. The chapter ends on a brutal high note, leaving the reader hungry for more action and the inevitable escalation that the Duan Shui Sect signal will bring.
