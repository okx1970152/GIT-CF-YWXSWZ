## Chapter Overview

The feast is on, the wine is flowing, and the mortal nobles are busy kissing up to the new Immortal Master—but our favorite cautious cultivator is sitting at a table full of disappointed families, quietly observing. This chapter solidifies the "social tribulation" as the primary conflict type. Han Li doesn't swing a blade, but he navigates a minefield of class dynamics, royal scrutiny, and family politics. The chapter’s real punch is in its final beat: a polite, handsome young prince steps up, and Han Li’s danger instincts scream the same warning they did for the terrifying Palace Steward. In the Mortal Stream, a smile can be more chilling than a sword.

## Key Plot Points

*   Immortal Master Wu tests the remaining guests for Spiritual Roots, finding none among the Qin and Hua clans, confirming the rarity of the "tong" fat youth’s talent.
*   Prince Xin, aware of the rumors about Han Li, studies him with a knowing, ambiguous look, creating an awkward moment for Qin Yan.
*   The feast begins with Prince Xin mingling freely, displaying the charismatic, approachable reputation that precedes him.
*   The Young Prince, a refined and elegant white-robed youth, is called over to greet the guests, showcasing his perfect princely demeanor.
*   When the Young Prince personally greets Han Li, Han Li senses the same type of **dangerous**, oppressive aura he felt from the Palace Steward, plunging him into shock and confusion.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter provides a fascinating piece of cultivation-world sociology. It explicitly explains *why* cultivators vanished from mortal high society: the emergence of the Ascension Assembly. Before this event, low-level cultivators with no hope of Foundation Establishment often gave up, becoming retainers for wealthy clans and enjoying mortal luxuries. But the Assembly gave everyone a fighting chance for Foundation Establishment, prompting cultivators to retreat into seclusion to train. This pivot transformed "Immortal Masters" from semi-common figures into rare legends.

### Social Tribulation

A type of high-stakes conflict in the Mortal Stream where the protagonist navigates complex human relationships and political hierarchies with the same cold calculation as a physical battle.

## Reading Guide

Get ready, fellow Daoist readers, for a classic Mortal Stream twist. This chapter is a masterclass in building tension through social cues. We see Han Li doing what he does best—blending in, playing the fool, and silently gathering data. The arrival of the handsome, polite Young Prince is a perfect trap for the reader's expectations; we're lulled into a sense of seasonal festivity. Then, WHAM. Han Li's survival instincts, honed by years of paranoia, flare up, directly linking this charming noble to the single most dangerous person he's sensed so far. The cliffhanger isn't a fight or a chase; it's a quiet, internal alarm bell that changes everything.

## The Social Tribulation

The interaction between Han Li, Prince Xin, and the Qin elder is a perfect performance of the "Social Tribulation." Han Li plays the bumbling, shy youth, while Prince Xin plays the benevolent, observant noble. Neither is what they appear to be. For Han Li, every smile, every bow, every introduction is a tactical calculation. He must project weakness to avoid drawing attention, all while assessing the hidden danger levels of everyone in the room. In the Mortal Stream, a successful dinner party is an espionage operation.
