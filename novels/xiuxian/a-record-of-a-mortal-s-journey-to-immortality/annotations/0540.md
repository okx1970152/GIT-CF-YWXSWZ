## Chapter Overview
Well, fellow Daoists, if you thought the last chapter was a wild ride, buckle up. This is one of those *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey to Immortality* chapters that reminds you why this isn’t a happy-go-lucky adventure story. We get a massive lore dump about the beast tide that shattered the Outer Star Seas, turning the entire region into a survival horror zone. Then, just when Han Li has saved a group of stranded cultivators, we pivot to one of the most emotionally charged scenes in the entire novel so far: a desperate daughter, Gongsun Xing, kneeling and begging for her father’s life. Han Li’s response? Pure, cold, calculating Mortal Stream gold. This chapter is a masterclass in contrasting the systemic horror of the cultivation world with a personal plea, and watching Han Li process *that* plea is a treat for any veteran reader.

## Key Plot Points
- Through a middle-aged cultivator’s account, Han Li learns the full, devastating history of the Outer Star Seas. Twenty years ago, a catastrophic beast tide from the Abyssal Sea wiped out all human settlements on Abyssal Island, including Black Rock City.
- The demon beasts didn’t stop there. They split into packs and systematically exterminated the human population across the entire sea region. Only a few survivors, like this group, now live in hiding, hunted like animals.
- The survivors harbor deep resentment toward the Inner Star Seas, who cut off all contact and sent no help. The region has been effectively abandoned.
- After the information exchange, the yellow-robed girl, Gongsun Xing, bursts forward to beg Han Li for help. Her father is bedridden with a cultivation injury (true-qi reversal and meridian disorder).
- She offers herself as a lifelong servant, even willing to be bound by a restrictive technique first, in exchange for her father’s cure.
- The middle-aged cultivator scolds her, but his tone reveals a flicker of hope. He knows Han Li could possibly help.
- The chapter ends on a tense silence as Gongsun Xing waits for Han Li’s decision. The reader is left hanging on the edge.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Outer Star Seas & Abyssal Sea (外星海 & 深渊海域)**: This chapter officially expands the map of the cultivation world. The “Outer Star Seas” are a frontier zone, far from the more established “Inner Star Seas.” The “Abyssal Sea” is a deep-sea trench that serves as a demon beast nest. This reinforces a classic xianxia world-building pattern: the wilderness outside the safe zones is always exponentially more dangerous. A “beast tide” (兽潮) is a xianxia staple—a mass exodus of demonic beasts from their habitat, often caused by a resource shortage, a territorial dispute among higher-level beasts, or the awakening of an ancient overlord. It functions as a natural (if catastrophic) disaster that reshapes political boundaries and decimates the mortal population. The complete abandonment of the Outer Star Seas by the Inner Star Seas is a brutal reminder of the cultivation world’s cold, transactional logic: if a territory is too expensive to hold, you simply let it burn.

**True-Qi Reversal & Meridian Disorder (真元逆转经脉错乱)**: This is a classic cultivation injury, a direct consequence of the novel’s core philosophy: shortcuts always have a price. Trying to force a higher-tier technique before reaching the required cultivation base is like trying to shove a watermelon through a straw. The result is a catastrophic internal energy reversal that cripples the body. It’s not a disease; it’s a *mechanism failure*. The fact that multiple “fellow Daoists and seniors” couldn’t fix it underscores how specialized and rare high-level healing knowledge is in this world. For Han Li, with his deep understanding of alchemy and energy systems, it *is* a minor problem—which tells you exactly how far above the common rabble he has risen.

**Filial Piety (孝) in Xianxia**: Gongsun Xing’s act of kneeling and offering herself as a servant to save her father is a powerful expression of *xiao* (孝), or filial piety, a core Confucian virtue that remains hugely influential in Chinese culture. But don’t mistake this for simple sentimentality. In the relentless logic of *RMJI*, this is a character trying to make a transaction using the only currency she has: her own freedom. It’s a desperate trade, and the elegant translation of a human bond into an economic contract is a defining feature of the Mortal Stream. The drama comes from the clash between the “selfless” virtue of filial piety and the “selfish” reality of the cultivation world.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch Han Li’s Silence.** The chapter ends with “A stretch of absolute silence.” That silence is deafening. Don’t read it as Han Li being touched. Read it as Han Li running a silent, high-speed cost-benefit analysis: *What is the risk of exposure? What is the value of this girl as an asset? How much would it reveal about me to heal this man? Is the debt worth the potential entanglement?* That thirty-second pause is doing more work than a page of internal monologue.
- **The Daughter’s Emotional Gambit.** Gongsun Xing’s plea is perfectly crafted for maximum emotional impact. She doesn’t just ask; she offers unconditional servitude and a pre-emptive submission to a restrictive technique, which is the cultivator-world equivalent of handing over the keys to your soul. From her perspective, it’s a winning move. From Han Li’s perspective, it’s a highly suspicious proposal. A slave you don’t ask for is often a spy you don’t see coming.
- **The Real Horror is the Setting.** The beast tide story is the true horror of this chapter. It’s not a monster attack; it’s a complete ecosystem collapse. The fact that no reinforcements came from the Inner Star Seas is a sentence of death for the entire region. It reveals that the cultivation world has no central authority, no safety net. You are on your own, and if you live in the wrong neighborhood, the whole world can just decide to let you die.
- **A Fork in the Road.** This chapter sets up a classic moral fork for Han Li, but “moral” isn’t the right word. It’s a *strategic* fork. He can walk away and preserve absolute anonymity and zero risk, or he can intervene, gain a loyal asset and a cure for a cheap price, but accept the risk of exposure and future obligation. The pause tells you he knows exactly how much each choice costs.
