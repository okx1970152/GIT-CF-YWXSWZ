## Chapter Overview

The second round of the Void Heaven Palace trials reveals its true, murderous face. While Han Li navigates his fragile alliance with Yuan Yao through the Lava Path’s black desert—a zone never seen before—the chapter’s real payload is a cold, calculated exposé of the Star Palace’s power games. Hidden figures admit they deliberately unleashed nightmare-level swarms (Iron Fire Ants, Silver Light Rats) to cull a generation of Core Formation cultivators, all to bluff the orthodox and demonic sects into backing down during their rulers’ secret seclusion. The Ice Fire Path is no longer a trial; it is a premeditated slaughterhouse designed to send a political message written in blood.

## Key Plot Points

- Yuan Yao hands over the Soul-Collecting Bead to Han Li, claiming it allows control of the Crying Soul Beast without refinement. Han Li accepts it but notes her evasiveness about the refinement side effects, filing it as a future investigation.
- Yuan Yao complains about the black desert and the mysterious flying ants (Iron Fire Ants), revealing that neither the terrain nor the ant swarms have ever appeared in previous Lava Path trials.
- Han Li picks up on the anomaly and begins to suspect that the entire trial is being manipulated from outside.
- On the Profound Ice Path, the Azure Robe Scholar effortlessly destroys a swarm of hundreds of Silver Light Rats, then ominously notes that the abnormal trials will spell doom for “that brat surnamed Han.”
- The big twist arrives in a dark-corner conversation between two hidden Star Palace elders: they openly admit to deploying the Iron Fire Ants and Silver Light Rats as a calculated political act.
- Their logic is chilling: the two Saint Masters are in unreachable seclusion, leaving the Star Palace vulnerable. By engineering a massacre of Core Formation elites from both orthodox and demonic paths, the elders hope to project strength, buy time for their rulers, and force the enemy factions into caution. They bet that Wan Tianming and Man Brutal Beard won’t make a fuss over losing other people’s disciples.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### The Star Palace’s Problem: Two Saint Masters in Critical Seclusion

The Star Palace has ruled the Starfall Sea for generations through overwhelming personal power—specifically, the two Saint Masters’ unique Primordial Magnetic Light (元磁神光), a technique so terrifying that even a top-tier Nascent Soul cultivator like the “madwoman of Ten Thousand Methods Sect” or Six Paths of Sacred Devil Island would avoid a direct confrontation. The current crisis is that both Saint Masters are in deep, unreachable seclusion at a “critical moment,” leaving the palace in the hands of lesser elders. This is a classic mortal-stream political nightmare: the institution’s invincibility rests on two individuals who are temporarily unreachable, and the whole structure could collapse if someone calls their bluff. The elders’ solution—massacre as messaging—is brutal, pragmatic, and perfectly in line with the Dark Forest logic of the cultivation world.

### The Iron Fire Ants (铁火蚁) and Silver Light Rats (银光鼠)

These are not random monsters; they are high-tier restrictions that the Star Palace elders have spent generations learning to control within the Void Heaven Palace’s inherited array system. The Iron Fire Ants can ignore ordinary magical treasure attacks, spit black fire, and combine into offensive formations—making them a perfect anti-Core Formation weapon. The Silver Light Rats, with their thunderous silver-horn charges en masse, serve the same culling function on the Profound Ice Path side. The fact that these creatures are described as “restrictions” (禁制) rather than naturally occurring beasts reinforces a key point: the entire Void Heaven Palace is a pre-built killing matrix under the Star Palace’s limited administrative control, not a wild ruin.

### The Elders’ Cold Calculus

The unnamed Star Palace elders are a textbook example of mortal-stream institutional thinking: they do not act out of malice or bloodlust, but from a cold, systems-level reading of their own vulnerability. Their dialogue reveals an intricate game of bluff and counter-bluff—the orthodox and demonic sects have known about the Star Palace’s “small manipulations” for years but tolerated them because the power balance held. The moment that balance shifts (two Saint Masters unavailable), the elders escalate to extreme violence to project the illusion that nothing has changed. They also know that the enemy factions are there for the Void Heaven Cauldron, not for justice; as long as their own core disciples survive, the sects won’t go to war over expendable rank-and-file. This is Dark Forest politics at its most elegant and savage.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in the *mortal stream* narrative structure. The “plot” (Han Li surviving the Lava Path) is almost secondary to the real action: a backroom confession that recontextualizes every previous death in the trial as a deliberate culling operation. Pay close attention to the tone shift between the two celestial conversations. The Azure Robe Scholar’s worry about “that brat surnamed Han” reveals he is invested in Han Li’s survival, while the Star Palace elders’ cold discussion treats the massacre as an acceptable price for institutional stability. This layering—where audiences know more than the protagonist about the game being played above his head—creates the signature tension of the arc. Also, note Yuan Yao’s continued manipulation: her claim of discomfort-free refinement is almost certainly a lie, and Han Li knows it, but he files the bead as a future project rather than forcing a confrontation. That is pure Han Li: table the unknown, survive the present, analyze later.

### Bonus: The Return of the Soul-Collecting Bead & Crying Soul Beast Thread

Yuan Yao’s delivery of the Soul-Collecting Bead ties back to her earlier promise. The Crying Soul Beast (啼魂兽) is a rare spirit beast with an innate ability to suppress evil spirits and soul-based attacks—exactly the kind of countermeasure that could prove decisive in the ghost-heavy second trial. But the bead’s refinement side effects (which Yuan Yao downplays as mere “discomfort”) are a classic mortal-stream red flag: nothing powerful comes without a hidden cost. Watch for whether Han Li ever refines it, or whether he finds a way to use the bead without paying the price.
