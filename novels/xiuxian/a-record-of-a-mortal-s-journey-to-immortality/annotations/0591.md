## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the Netherworld Domain, fellow Daoists—a pocket of sheer desperation where mana is dead, food is a luxury, and getting devoured by a yin-born beast is just another Tuesday. Chapter 591 punches the new-world reveal into high gear: Han Li gets a front-row seat to a life-or-death village defense against a Jiaozheng, a hulking Netherworld beast that belches black ice and bristles with razor needles. But the real treasure isn’t the green crystal they dig out of its skull—it’s the stark, brutal ecology the old elder lays out after the fight. This chapter isn’t just a monster hunt; it’s the Mortal Stream’s definitive guide to surviving a lawless, magic-stripped frontier.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens mid-battle: the village’s defenders are exchanging volleys with a Jiaozheng, a giant four-eyed beast whose yin wind neutralizes arrows and whose neck bristles turn into lethal projectiles. After a few rounds of attrition, the village’s heavy-spear team pins the beast to the ground with a coordinated volley—a moment of celebration that almost ends in disaster when the dying beast hurls its club at the crowd of women and children. The pale-faced middle-aged man intervenes with a brilliant mid-air strike, redirecting the club to safety. Han Li notices the man’s cold glance in his direction, filing away the potential hostility as a data point.

After the kill, someone splits the skull and retrieves a green crystal—a Netherworld Beast Crystal. Han Li recognizes it: exactly the same type of crystal he saw in the puppet remnants from the Void Heaven Palace’s inner hall, sparking a connection between this domain and the ancient cultivators’ artifacts.

Back in the hall, the elder explains the local rules: the Netherworld Domain is a hundred-li pocket where demon beasts, Netherworld beasts, and humans coexist under a universal mana seal (Spirit Void Eruption). Netherworld beasts are born spontaneously from yin energy, inexhaustible, and predatory toward humans. Demon beasts are too powerful to challenge but usually stay in their territories. Human villages are few, food is desperately scarce, and only the useful survive—those who cannot contribute are expelled to die. It’s a chilling, Darwinian onboarding speech that sets the tone for Han Li’s new environment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Netherworld Domain (阴冥之地)** is a staple locale in xianxia—a “dead zone” where normal spiritual energy is absent, and practitioners are forced to rely on physical strength, mundane tactics, and mental endurance. This concept taps into the Daoist idea of differentiated realms beyond the mortal world, each governed by its own natural laws. In *RMJI*, it’s not a mystical training ground but a prison-colony-level survival game.

**Spirit Void Eruption (绝灵之气喷发)** is a recurring phenomenon in chaotic spaces. It disables all magical power, reducing cultivators to mortals with perhaps enhanced bodies and memories. It’s the ultimate equalizer—and a perfect arena for the Mortal Stream’s “no cheats” philosophy.

**Netherworld Beasts** are born from condensed yin energy, not through reproduction. This echoes the classical Chinese concept of *qi* spontaneously generating life forms (like demons and ghostly creatures) when environmental energy accumulates to a critical point. Killing one only makes room for another to be born—there’s no permanent victory, only temporary safety.

The green crystal connection to Void Heaven Palace puppets is a classic *RMJI* lore thread: it subtly hints that this domain and the ancient palace share a common origin or resource economy, rewarding attentive readers who remember details from hundreds of chapters ago.

## Reading Guide

Let’s be real—after a whole arc of treasure-hunting and backstabbing in the Void Heaven Palace, dropping Han Li into a mana-dead village with a shotgun-slinging militia feels like a genre switch. But this is peak Mortal Stream: the protagonist isn’t fighting with spells or treasures now; he’s watching, cataloging, and figuring out how to game a system where the currency is brute strength and the cost of failure is being eaten. The elder’s speech about “only the useful survive” isn’t just worldbuilding—it’s a mission brief. Han Li’s mind is already running the numbers on how to make himself indispensable, and we get to see him puzzle out the crystal mystery on top of it.

The cold glance from the pale-faced rescuer is a classic *RMJI* social thread: someone important has already taken a measure of Han Li, and it’s probably not in his favor. Keep an eye on that.

Finally, if you’ve ever wondered what happens when you throw a cultivator into a zombie apocalypse without mana, this chapter is your answer. The result: more analytical silence, less panic. Just how we like it.

## Deep Dive: The Yin-Born Ecosystem

The Netherworld Domain operates on a hard energy scarcity model that’s brutally honest with the reader. Yin energy = hostile to human life. Demon beasts = untouchable apex predators that avoid humans only because they’d rather eat yin beasts. Humans = a fragile parasitic population living off a few arable patches and the occasional non-toxic Netherworld beast carcass. The “useful or die” rule isn’t cruelty; it’s logistics. In xianxia terms, this is a “force cage” version of the Mortal Stream’s core thesis: there is no free lunch, no hidden spirit vein, no plot armor—only what you can physically secure with your own hands.
