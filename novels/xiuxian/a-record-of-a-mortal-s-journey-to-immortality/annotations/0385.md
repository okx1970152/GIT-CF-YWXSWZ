## Chapter Overview

What if your entire journey was a setup, and you only found out the destination when it was too late to turn back? This chapter is the Mortal Stream’s version of a heist crew assembling for a job—except everyone knows the target is terrifyingly dangerous, and nobody is sharing the full brief. Han Li, still operating under the cover identity of Qu Hun, has spent the past month practicing the “Six Avoidance Water Wave Formation” with his five fellow mercenaries. They arrive at a barren island, meet a Core Formation elder who acknowledges the plan is set, and are told the beast will be baited into their trap by morning. The tension here isn’t in the monster’s roar—it’s in the silence. No one has been told what they’re up against. And that’s exactly how the Six Pillar Hall wants it.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li (as Qu Hun) arrives with Feng Sanniang and the other hired cultivators at the unnamed island where the trap has been pre-set.
- The team consists of a middle-aged Confucian scholar (arrogant, secluded all voyage), a young matron pair, an old wood-element specialist called Green Calc, a Darkflame youth, the burly Dou, and the formations master Feng Sanniang.
- The Confucian scholar openly antagonizes Qu Hun on first meeting, but Han Li’s puppet returns the jab calmly, establishing that this is a group of high-strung contractors, not comrades.
- Feng Sanniang gradually reveals her role as an expert formations master, teaching the group the “Six Avoidance Water Wave Formation” intricacies over several weeks.
- The Core Formation Elder Miao appears, confirms the plan, and reveals the formation is already laid—this wasn’t a scouting trip; it was a deployment.
- Elder Miao and Elder Gu will chase the beast into the net tomorrow morning. The hired cultivators’ job is simply to hold the formation and keep the beast trapped.
- Green Calc finally pushes for information: *What exactly are we hunting?* Feng Sanniang, however, remains evasive—sealing the chapter on an unanswered, ominous question.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Contract Cultivators & The Six Pillar Hall Economy**: This chapter showcases a recurring social layer in the Mortal Stream: the non-sect, independent Qi Condensation / Foundation Establishment cultivator who hires out for high-risk jobs. The Six Pillar Hall doesn’t draft these contractors like soldiers—it pays them in spirit stones, rare pills, and promises. But the risk asymmetry is baked into the deal: the Six Pillar Hall knows exactly what the beast is; the contractors have been fed a vague “troublesome sixth-grade beast.” This is not a partnership. It’s a transaction where the employer holds all the dangerous data. In xianxia, “contract cultivator” is a class marker: you are strong enough to be useful, but not strong enough to dictate terms or demand the full briefing.

**The Six Avoidance Water Wave Formation**: The name “Six Avoidance” (六遁) likely references the Six Directions or Six Void principles, combined with Water Wave (water-wave) nature, suggesting a fluid, trapping-type formation that uses water qi to ensnare and immobilize its target without directly damaging it. It is not an attack formation. Its purpose is containment so that a high-damage killing team (the elders) can finish the beast off at their leisure. In formation theory, each overseer is responsible for maintaining one axis of the trap, and if any one node collapses, the entire field weakens. This gives the chapter’s team-building a brutal subtext: every single contractor is a single-point failure for a grade-6 beast encounter.

**Social Tribulation: The First Meeting**: The Confucian scholar’s immediate hostility toward Qu Hun is a classic Mortal Stream social tribulation. He is not angry at anything Qu Hun said; he is testing rank. By challenging Qu Hun’s credentials at the very first greeting, he attempts to establish dominance over a rival whose background he doesn’t recognize. Han Li’s response—calm, non-escalating deflection—is textbook Mortal Stream survival. He refuses to waste capital on a social duel that provides no tactical or material gain.

### Contract Cultivator

A freelance or independent Qi Condensation/Foundation Establishment cultivator hired for a specific high-risk task, often with incomplete intelligence, treated as a disposable asset by the employer.

### 六连殿

The name translates literally as “Six Connections Hall,” rendered as Six Pillar Hall; a major cultivation organization in the sea region.

### 筑基期

Foundation Establishment (Jīdàn) denotes the second major cultivation stage after Qi Condensation; cultivators at this stage can fly, use magical tools offensively, and preside over formations.

### 结丹期

Core Formation (Jīdàn) is the third major cultivation stage; Core Formation cultivators command far greater power, lifespan, and status, often serving as the strategic pillars of sects and halls.

## Reading Guide

This is a slow-burn setup chapter, but don’t skim it. Every detail here matters: the dynamics among the six overseers, Feng Sanniang’s concealed expertise, the Confucian scholar’s desperate need for validation, and the deafening silence around *what is actually in the water*. The core tension is dramatic irony with a Mortal Stream twist—we know Han Li knows nothing, and everyone else knows just as little. That lack of shared intelligence is a ticking bomb. If you are new to xianxia formation mechanics, treat this chapter as a tactical table-setter: the next chapter’s payoff will land much harder if you track who is positioned where and who has a grudge. Also, note the geography: an island **dozens of li** across—this is not a fight on a narrow beach, but a large-scale sea arena. The beast will be powerful enough to need a mile-wide cage.
