## Chapter Overview
Finally, some open sea! After three days of tense, uneventful flying—punctuated only by a wide berth around a rank-five beast too big to ignore—Han Li reaches the Outer Star Seas in earnest. This chapter is a masterclass in *worldbuilding through logistics*: the moment he steps onto Condensing Jade Island, he has to recalibrate everything he thought he knew about the local ecosystem. No more safe harbors or crowded markets. The shops are owned by major alliances, the Core Formation cultivators are aggressively recruiting partners, and the very geography of the island screams "deadly frontier." Han Li spends his time buying a chart, dodging a price-gouging merchant, politely declining a hunting invite, and then immediately putting the island behind him. It’s a quiet chapter on the surface, but beneath it hums all the cold, calculating machinery of the Mortal Stream: information is currency, trust is scarce, and the sea outside that island is a predator’s kingdom.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li arrives on Condensing Jade Island, a small Outer Star Seas outpost, and immediately notices the near-empty streets—a stark contrast to the bustling Inner Star Seas.
- He correctly deduces that the island’s cultivators are mostly out hunting, as only the brave or the desperate operate this far out.
- A blatant price-gouging attempt by a Fengle Merchant Alliance shopkeeper (100 spirit stones for a chart) tempers Han Li’s expectations of frontier hospitality; he pays without further haggling, mentally filing the Alliance as a competent, ruthless operator.
- The arrival of a Core Formation cultivator named Hao Yuantian, loudly selling a demon beast corpse to a rival depot, paints a vivid picture of the local economy and the casual, competitive atmosphere among high-level cultivators.
- Hao Yuantian’s friendly but transparently strategic recruitment offer confirms the chapter’s core social rule: on these islands, solitary high-level cultivators are rare and valuable assets to be poached.
- Han Li declines the offer and departs immediately, trusting his own plans over an unknown partner’s goodwill.
- After a tense, uneventful three-day flight, he spots a striking red coral island in the distance—his target, apparently. The chapter ends on a note of cautious optimism, underscoring that his luck has held so far.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Demon Beast Island Economy** – Condensing Jade Island isn’t just any trading post; it’s a frontier settlement in a zero-sum arena. The entire ecosystem is built around extracting value from demon beasts before the beasts extract value from you. The fact that only two major merchant alliances (Fengle and others) operate competing depots here reinforces the Dark Forest logic: even trade is a weapon. A high-level cultivator selling a freshly killed carcass is simultaneously showing off his power (deterrence) and advertising his services (recruitment). The price-gouging on charts isn't simple greed—it's a tax on information asymmetry. The powerful profit from keeping the less-connected ignorant. This is the same principle we've seen since the Seven Mysteries Sect: knowledge is a commodity, and those who control its flow control the game.

**The Outer Star Seas Death Code** – The chapter’s implicit rule—"do not fly too low, but do not fly too high"—is classic xianxia *negative space* worldbuilding. The danger is defined by what the author *doesn't* describe: beasts that can suck you from the seabed through hundreds of feet of water; aerial predators that patrol the sky. The protagonist’s survival depends not on fighting these threats, but on correctly navigating the narrow safe band between them. This is a core Mortal Stream aesthetic: the world is not a combat arena where you can win; it is an eco-system you must negotiate. The disaster of the "all-island hunt" that attracted a swarm of rank-seven beasts also echoes a deep genre meme: over-ambition in the cultivation world is punished not by a villain but by the system itself correcting an imbalance. It's a cap on cooperative power, a reminder that even unity has limits under Heaven.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is the calm before the storm—the "quiet victory" phase of Han Li’s preparation arc. If you're a fan of exposition-dense worldbuilding that treats a boring island market as a source of tactical intelligence, you'll love this. Pay close attention to the three seconds of hesitation Han Li shows when Hao Yuantian makes his offer; that's not social awkwardness, it's a full risk-assessment subroutine running in real-time. Also, note the contrast between the Fengle Alliance's transparent greed and the rival depot's "sending a quick-witted youth to butter up a big customer" strategy—two different approaches to the same hostile market. Finally, the coral island at the end isn't just a beautiful sight. In the Mortal Stream, a place this striking usually means something important is there, or something deadly. Get ready.

## Deep Dive: The Fengle Alliance's Monopoly Logic
For readers unfamiliar with Chinese web novel business models, the Fengle Merchant Alliance isn't just a shop; it's a shadow state. In many cultivation stories, the "four great merchant alliances" function as corporate empires that operate beyond sect politics—they're the economic subsystem that keeps the whole bloody machine running. A shopkeeper on a remote island can charge 100 spirit stones for a chart because the Alliance has purchased (or extorted) the exclusive rights to sell updated sea charts in the region. The price is a reflection of their market power, not the chart's intrinsic value. Han Li paying without protest is a sign of experience: he knows a losing battle when he sees one, and he's conserving energy for the real fight ahead.
