## Chapter Overview

Han Li has wrapped up his time at the Qin residence, but his social tribulations for the day are far from over. After the banquet at Prince Xin’s estate, he deliberately separates from the Qin family convoy and lies in wait near the back gate. His target: the elusive old man and his cross-dressing granddaughter who caught his eye earlier. What follows is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s favorite sport—psychological warfare. No flying swords, no explosive spells, just two wary cultivators engaged in a cold, high-stakes game of mutual assessment and verbal fencing. Old master Xiao tries to surrender with dramatic, self-sacrificial flair, while his sharp-eyed granddaughter attempts to bluff her way through. But Han Li, with his honed Great Derivative Art-enhanced perception, sees through every act, leaving the pair pinned under his silent, calculating gaze. The chapter is a pure “social tribulation” that tests not cultivation base, but nerve, deception, and the art of saying nothing while controlling everything.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li spots an old man and a young man at the banquet that pique his interest; old master Qin identifies them as the little-known owner of a local restaurant and his grandson.
- Instead of returning to the Qin estate, Han Li silently detours to the Prince Xin residence’s back gate, setting up an intercept.
- The old man and the young woman (revealed to be in disguise) arrive, and the old man—named Xiao Zhen—immediately launches into a groveling apology, claiming he feared offending Han Li earlier.
- When questioned, Xiao Zhen admits to being from the “Xiao clan of Fenghejian,” a small immortal clan, and says he has no hope of Foundation Establishment, hence his mortal-world finance duty.
- Han Li tests the waters: “Aren’t you afraid I’ll lose all inhibition and immediately turn hostile?” The old man responds with a pitiful, self-sacrificial monologue, begging for his granddaughter’s life—but Han Li coldly reads his eyes and finds no genuine defeat.
- The granddaughter tries a show of defiance but her sly, darting eyes betray her bluff, which Han Li’s superior divine sense easily catches.
- The chapter ends on a tense stalemate: HanLi just stares at them with a knowing smile, saying nothing, as the duo’s composure begins to crack under the silence.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a textbook example of a “Social Tribulation” (社会试炼). In the Mortal Stream, a fight isn’t always a battle of spells; sometimes it’s a conversational duel where every sigh, bow, and theatrical confession is a move. Old master Xiao’s surrender speech is a classic feint: he performs the role of a pitiful, resigned old man to trigger a sense of moral superiority or boredom in his opponent, hoping to be dismissed as worthless. However, Han Li’s training with the “Great Derivative Art” (大衍决) gives him a sharpened divine sense, letting him see the genuine mana still coiled and ready beneath the old man’s groveling surface. The young lady’s “hand on the storage pouch” act is another Jianghu classic—the false readiness bluff, used to make an opponent think you’re a hair-trigger away from pulling a trump card when you’re actually hoping they’ll back down.

Another key element is the concept of “nurturing clan foundations” through mortal-world businesses. In this universe, cultivation requires vast resources (spirit stones, herbs, pills), and these come from spirit-rich mountains or from mortal economic activity. Small clans often assign their outdated members to manage mortal businesses (like the Xianyun Restaurant) to generate funding, forming the lowest tier of the xianxia supply chain. This reinforces the Mortal Stream’s brutal economic realism: even a washed-up old cultivator near the end of his potential is still a useful cog in a larger machine.

### Yue Kingdom (越国)

A small nation within the cultivation world, home to the Seven Great Sects; its mortal capital is the setting of this chapter.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a different kind of thrill: the pleasure of watching a masterfully silent protagonist dismantle a practiced actor with nothing but a gaze. This is Han Li at his most unnerving. He doesn’t need to threaten or draw a weapon; he simply refuses to react to the script the old man is reading from. The old man’s performance is actually quite good—practiced, tearful, full of self-deprecation—but Han Li’s icy patience exposes the gaps. The chapter’s true tension lies not in who will throw the first spell, but in who will break the silence first. Pay attention to the young woman’s “shrewd little glances” she thinks she’s hiding, and how one of Han Li’s new techniques (the Great Derivative Art) gives him the perception to see through her act. The game is afoot, and it’s all being played with facial expressions and pregnant pauses. Who will blink first?
