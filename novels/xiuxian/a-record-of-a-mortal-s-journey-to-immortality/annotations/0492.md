## Chapter Overview

Alright, folks, buckle up—because the *Void Heaven Palace* arc is hitting its most chaotic crescendo yet. While the Nascent Soul big-shots are duking it out in the sky for the motherlode (the Void Heaven Cauldron itself), a very interesting side-show is unfolding down on the altar. A random fire-wolf-turned-basket-treasure-turned-jade-ruyi has sparked a three-way tug-of-war between Wu Chou, Profound Bone, and—surprise, surprise—our boy Han Li. And *how* it falls into his hands is the kind of delicious irony that makes Mortal Stream fans cackle. But the *real* drama? Han Li nearly has a heart attack when he realizes the basket he’s using to grab the treasure might just be a giant, blinking neon sign that says “I freed Profound Bone!” Classic panic, followed by a sigh of relief—only for Extreme Yin to be left utterly baffled by the whole outcome.

## Key Plot Points

1.  **The Altar Treasure War:** The twin-headed fire wolf transforms into an ancient treasure. Wu Chou and Profound Bone immediately clash over it, using their respective techniques (black python vs. green net) to try and seize the glowing wolf-beast. The stalemate is broken when Han Li, using the basket treasure he acquired from Profound Bone’s slain guard, casually scoops up the ruyi.
2.  **Top-Tier Standoff:** Up top, the six Nascent Soul cultivators are ready to spring into action as the Void Heaven Cauldron rises. Wan Tianming pulls a brilliant (and cheap) trick with a *fake* Heavenly Luo True Thunder orb to claim a square ancient pendant, leaving Extreme Yin and the Confucian-robed elder furious and humiliated.
3.  **Han Li’s Heart Attack:** The moment Han Li whips out the basket treasure, he has a flashback: *this was the treasure from that dead guard!* If Extreme Yin recognizes it, the jig is up. He mentally prepares for a world of hurt, only to realize—to his shock and relief—that Extreme Yin doesn’t bat an eye. The treasure was never the Patriarch’s property.
4.  **Lucky Break:** The chapter confirms that the guard who imprisoned Profound Bone had actually *gone rogue* to snatch this basket treasure for himself, giving Han Li a completely clean (albeit terrifyingly close) escape. Profound Bone gets a mental “you owe me one” note from Han Li.
5.  **The First Batch Escapes:** As the six Nascent Soul elders refocus on the cauldron, two more beams of light (yellow and white) shoot out. Wan Tianming fakes a real thunder orb to claim a jade pendant, while Man Brutal Beard powers through a combined attack to snag a strange coin.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### The Art of the Bluff (Heavenly Luo True Thunder)
Wan Tianming’s trick is a classic xianxia staple: the *fake trump card*. In a world where cultivators are constantly assessing each other’s “win buttons” (ultimate techniques, divine treasures, self-destructive moves), being able to fake one—even for a split second—is a valid and powerful tactical asset. The key is that Extreme Yin *doesn’t* know for sure if it’s a fake. In a fight between peak-realm cultivators, the cost of being wrong about a potential one-shot kill is too high. It’s a beautiful demonstration that in the Mortal Stream, deception and psychological warfare are often more effective than raw power.

### The Social Hierarchy of Treasure Acquisition
Note how the dynamic shift works here. The Nascent Soul cultivators are fighting over the big cauldron, while the Core Formation-level players (Wu Chou, Profound Bone, Han Li) are left to scrap over the “leftovers” (the ancient treasures that *escaped* the cauldron). This perfectly illustrates the **tiered economy** of the cultivation world. The top shelf takes their pick, and the bottom rung fights for scraps—but sometimes, as Han Li proves, a well-timed move on the lower level can snag a prize that leaves the higher-ups scratching their heads.

### Three-Way Standoff

A classic xianxia battlefield macro-state where three or more forces reach a deadlock because no single party can secure the prize without exposing themselves to a fatal attack from the others.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterful example of the Mortal Stream’s unique tension: **the protagonist’s greatest enemy isn’t a beast or a rival, but the information he himself carries.** Han Li’s entire world nearly collapses because he forgot where he got a single item. That moment of internal panic—the cold sweat, the frantic calculation of backup plans—is pure *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey*. It’s not about fighting stronger people; it’s about not making a slip that turns a powerful neutral into a mortal enemy. Also, savor the beautiful irony of the chapter’s end: Extreme Yin, the plotting mastermind, is left utterly speechless by the outcome. He planned for Wu Chou or Profound Bone to get the treasure. He did *not* plan for the quiet, seemingly passive rogue cultivator to be the one holding the ruyi. The game is changing.
