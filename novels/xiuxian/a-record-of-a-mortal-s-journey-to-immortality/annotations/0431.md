## Chapter Overview

Get ready for one of those classic *RMJI* chapters where a quiet investigation turns into a bloody trap (and then a *second* trap). Our crew of treasure hunters finally reaches the jade pool, only to find that the fabled Seven Iris Flower is a total lie—a lure crafted from a demon snake’s crest. The fight that follows is quick, brutal, and a brutal reminder that in this world, a beautiful rainbow is just as likely to be venomous gas. Han Li stays in the background, striking with surgical precision when the moment is right, and the aftermath reveals deeper layers to an already suspicious setup. Oh, and the chapter ends with a classic Mortal Stream *jumpscare*—a dead man walking, and a backstab nobody saw coming. Buckle up, fellow Daoists. This one is all about the payoff for patient observation.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Seven Iris Flower is a fake.** The rainbow is just colored demonic qi spat out by a Crested Snake, whose fleshy horn has been deliberately shaped into a flower bud—a meticulously crafted deception.
- **Coordinated fight against the demon snake.** Shi Die opens with aggression but is outmatched; it takes the combined efforts of Jin Qing’s seal, Hu Yue’s blades, and a perfectly timed Blood Drill from Qu Hun to wound the beast.
- **Han Li pins the snake with the Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Sword.** He deliberately supresses the Evil-Cleansing Divine Lightning to hide the sword’s origin material, showing his constant calculus about maintaining secrecy even in battle.
- **The party deduces the trap.** The Seven Iris Flower was not a natural treasure but a lure, likely set for the skeleton lying beside the pool, who tried to pick it and died from poison and a follow-up arrow.
- **The skeleton’s arrow is noted.** Its green color and faint black energy are similar to but distinct from Han Li’s swords—raised as a potential clue for later.
- **Team morale collapses.** Hu Yue is crushed by the failed expedition, having lost both money and an ally; Shi Die vents her fury on the snake, beheading it with visible bitterness.
- **The dead man walks.** Jian’s corpse suddenly crawls out of the pool and advances on the party; Hu Yue destroys it easily.
- **The real attack comes from behind.** Just as the group relaxes, a bone hand pierces Hu Yue’s abdomen from behind, clutching a blue pill—the final chill of the chapter, executed with cold narrative efficiency.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Crested Snake (妖冠蛇):** This is a classic poisonous spirit serpent in Chinese xianxia lore, identifiable by the fleshy crown or “crest” on its head—a feature often tied to its mutation, poison sac, or hidden treasure. The fact that someone carved it to look like a flower turns a biological trait into an ambush artist’s prop.
- **Primordial Ancient Treasure (洪荒古宝):** Mentioned implicitly through the chapter’s treasure-hunting logic. In *RMJI*, “primordial ancient treasures” are a separate category from modern magical weapons: they cannot be absorbed into the body and are often one-trick wonders (but devastatingly good at that one trick). They explain why seasoned cultivators would risk a pool of venomous water for a strange artifact.
- **Dead man walking as a narrative trope:** In xianxia, a corpse that moves after death is not always a zombie (尸变). It could be a secret art, a remnant soul clinging to the flesh, or—most terrifyingly—a puppet being controlled from outside. The ambiguity is deliberate: it makes the reader feel the same cold uncertainty as the characters on the page.

### Sealing Spirit Pillar (封灵柱)

A composite artifact formed from rare materials and engraved with archaic characters; used to seal great formations or entities under ancient ruins.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in *Mortal Stream payoff*. The entire pool sequence, from the fake rainbow to the pinning of the snake, is a single seamless loop of “setup → reveal → resolution.” Notice how Han Li never commits more than he needs to: he lets Shi Die take the first hit, waits for Jin Qing and Hu Yue to join, commits Qu Hun’s Blood Drill only when the timing is perfect, and then ends the threat with a single surgical sword-stroke. No fanfare. No heroics. Just clean execution.

And then the real punch comes at the very end. The chapter could have ended safely with the party regrouping and analyzing the trap, but instead, we get the dead man walking followed by a backstab that none of them saw coming. That contrast—the false sense of security followed by sudden, visceral horror—is *peak RMJI*. It tells you that in this world, you never relax until you’ve burned every piece of evidence, and sometimes not even then.

Also, pay attention to the skeleton. The arrow in its body is deliberately set up as a plot hook. Han Li is studying it with unusual care, noting its green color and faint black taint. That’s a clue that will almost certainly echo later—either as a technique match, a sect signature, or a foreshadowing of the real mastermind behind this cave.
