## Chapter Overview

Get ready, because the chess match is about to begin! After half a year of tense avoidance, Han Li finally walks right into the lion’s den—Doctor Mo’s personal quarters. The two old acquaintances sit down for a talk that is anything but a cozy reunion. Every smile is a feint, every compliment carries a hidden blade. Han Li secures the antidote to the Corpse Worm Pill, but he knows full well that this is not the end of his leash—just a longer one. Doctor Mo plays the doting elder expertly, but the chapter opens with him pacing like a trapped beast, revealing just how desperate he really is. This is a masterclass in two-faced negotiation, where the stakes are measured in freedom, trust, and survival.

## Key Plot Points

- The chapter opens with Doctor Mo pacing anxiously in his room, a rare display of vulnerability from the usually composed schemer. He is worried about whether Han Li will show up—a sign that his grip on the situation is not as ironclad as he pretends.
- Han Li arrives punctually, forcing Doctor Mo to suppress his excitement and put on a grandfatherly mask. The dynamic is immediately set: Han Li holds more power than the old man wants to admit.
- Han Li refuses to let Doctor Mo close the door, calling out the classic “catching a turtle in a jar” trap tactic. This small victory establishes the rules of engagement for the entire conversation: no hidden setups, no unfair advantages.
- Both men size each other up. Han Li notes Doctor Mo’s dramatic aging (now indistinguishable from a seventy-year-old), which fuels his suspicion that the man’s story about needing vitality restoration might actually be true.
- Doctor Mo tries the warm, paternal approach, reminiscing about Han Li’s childhood. Han Li plays along with respectful deference, but internally brands him an “old fox.”
- The core negotiation: Han Li demands the Corpse Worm Pill antidote *before* letting Doctor Mo check his cultivation progress. He successfully forces the exchange on his own terms—a significant tactical win.
- Doctor Mo hands over the antidote. Han Li hesitates, suspecting a trick, but his life is on the line. He swallows the pill, and it works: the Corpse Worm dissolves completely. The burden is lifted.
- With the poison gone, Doctor Mo returns to his armchair, taking his time with idle chatter. He is no longer in a rush, subtly reminding Han Li that one hostage situation has simply been replaced by another.
- The chapter ends on the same tense note as it began: Doctor Mo asks to take Han Li’s pulse, and the reader knows this is a request that could easily turn into a trap. The game is not over—it has only just begun.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Catching a Turtle in a Jar (瓮中捉鳖):** This is a classic Chinese idiom describing a situation where an enemy is trapped with no escape route. Han Li calls out the tactic before it can be used, forcing Doctor Mo to keep the door open and maintain a degree of fairness. This is not just paranoia—in a world built on poison, hidden blades, and secret arts, anything that limits your mobility is an instant death sentence.
- **Corpse Worm Pill (尸虫丸):** A parasitic pill introduced earlier in the novel. It lays dormant for a year before hatching and devouring the host from within. The antidote is a calculated risk: Doctor Mo gives it up now, but he does so because he believes he still has leverage over Han Li through other means (likely his family or the threat of the mysterious Iron Servant). In the Mortal Stream, an antidote is never a gift—it is a reinvestment.
- **The Goading Technique:** Doctor Mo uses a subtle psychological ploy when he says, “It’s not a dragon’s pool or a tiger’s den.” This is a veiled challenge to Han Li’s courage. If Han Li had acted timid, he would have lost face and ceded psychological ground. Han Li counters with a dismissive “Hmph!” and walks in confidently, preserving his dignity and control.

### Divine Sense Check / Pulse Diagnosis (把脉)

Allowing an elder cultivator to take your pulse is a high-risk act of trust—they can detect lies, hidden injuries, and even lock your cultivation. Han Li’s refusal to submit immediately is a calculated victory.

### Pseudo True Qi (伪真气)

The non-combative energy stream generated by the Eternal Spring Art. It sharpens the senses and mind but is useless for destructive work in combat.

## Reading Guide

- **Pay close attention to Doctor Mo’s pacing in the opening.** This is one of the very few times we see him truly unsettled. It tells you that Han Li has become a variable he cannot fully control—and that terrifies him more than any physical threat.
- **Han Li’s internal monologue versus his spoken words.** Notice how polite and submissive he sounds on the surface, while his inner voice is a constant stream of calculation and insults (“Old fox!”). This is the essence of the Mortal Stream protagonist: the mask never drops, even when victory seems assured.
- **The pulse-taking request.** It seems like a minor procedural step, but in a world of hidden cultivation, letting someone take your pulse is like handing them the keys to your soul. They can detect lies, hidden injuries, and even trigger a lock on your energy. Han Li’s refusal to comply immediately, and his success in delaying it, is a major tactical victory that many first-time readers might miss.
- **The chapter title matters.** “Antidote” lulls you into thinking this is a resolution chapter. But the last line leaves you hanging on a knife’s edge. Don’t let the successful pill dissolve fool you—this is an armistice, not a surrender. The war between master and disciple is far from over.
