## Chapter Overview
This chapter takes a sharp turn from the raw survival pressures of the Black Desert into an unexpected reunion, all while the desert’s ecological nightmare rages on. Our favorite cautious cultivator finds himself in a logistical nightmare—his Gold Devourer Beetles are racking up casualties faster than he can replenish them against endless waves of Iron Fire Ants. But just when the reader might settle into another routine desert slaughter, a familiar voice cuts through the chaos, dragging Han Li—and the story—into a wholly new direction. It’s a masterclass in pacing: the relentless grind of cultivation-world travel, the cold calculus of resource management, and the sudden, charged introduction of a past acquaintance.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li engages a massive swarm of Iron Fire Ants with his Gold Devourer Beetles, noting with satisfaction that his swarm’s numbers give them an overwhelming advantage, even though the ants are formidable opponents.
- The ants attempt to retreat by forming an arrow formation, but Han Li intercepts them with a sword-light, allowing the beetles to finish them off.
- Han Li calculates the cost of his journey so far: nearly ten thousand beetles lost in a single day, a rate of attrition that visibly pains him due to the time required to breed replacements.
- He encounters a cultivator fighting for their life against another ant swarm, and initially decides to walk away, having no interest in wasting resources on a stranger.
- The desperate cultivator calls out by name, revealing herself to be **Yuan Yao**, a female cultivator Han Li has met before.
- Han Li’s intrinsic calculation shifts from cold indifference to guarded practicality; he saves her, seeing it as a convenient opportunity to settle a potential future debt and gather information.
- Yuan Yao survives thanks to a mysterious green pellet that creates a powerful flame, destroying a part of the ant swarm.
- The chapter concludes with Yuan Yao expressing her gratitude, her alluring appearance deliberately highlighted, and an author’s note apologizing for the day’s delay.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The “Insect War” we witness here is a classic trope found throughout Chinese web novels, known as “驱虫 (qū chóng)” or insect-driving combat. It’s a logistical arms race: a cultivator’s power is not just their own cultivation level, but the quality, quantity, and breed of the spirit insects they command. The Gold Devourer Beetle and the Iron Fire Ant represent two different philosophies: the former is a generalist devourer that eats everything, including magical flames, while the latter is a specialist that uses fire as a primary weapon. This chapter reinforces the idea that in the Mortal Stream, even a basic survival resource (crossing a desert) is a battle of attrition, not a single epic fight. Han Li’s painstaking calculation of beetle losses vs. territory covered is a perfect example of the “resource management” core of the genre. The mysterious green pellet used by Yuan Yao is also a great example of the “hidden trump card” trope—in the cruel world of cultivation, no one shows their full hand until their life truly depends on it.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a pivotal shift in the story’s social landscape! The Black Desert is more than just a dangerous environment; it’s a pressure cooker that forces encounters no one planned. Han Li’s meeting with Yuan Yao is not just a random rescue; it’s a strategically valuable coincidence. Pay attention to Han Li’s internal monologue here—he is not simply being kind; he is making a calculated investment. The fact that he was “thinking of seeking her out anyway” is a huge red flag (in a good way!) that he has a long-term plan brewing. Yuan Yao, for her part, is not a damsel in distress. Her survival against a swarm of ants, thanks to a rare treasure, shows she has resources and wits of her own. This is the beginning of a classic Mortal Stream alliance—a temporary, interest-based partnership where both parties are aware the other might be useful, but neither fully trusts the other. The stage is set for some serious backroom dealing and information exchange, which is often far more dangerous than any monster battle.
