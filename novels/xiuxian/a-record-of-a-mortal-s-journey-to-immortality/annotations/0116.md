## Chapter Overview

The gloves come off. Han Li decides that sweet-talking the Mo widows for the Warm Yang Jade is a dead end, so he flips the negotiation table with a little fire and brimstone. But just as he’s about to terrify them into submission, the ladies reveal they know exactly what a cultivator is—and they’ve prepared a welcome committee in the form of a fancy paralysis incense. This chapter is a masterclass in Han Li’s signature “I’m just going to start threatening people now” strategy, followed by a shocking discovery that his opponents are playing the same game. It’s all about escalations and the terrifying realization that mortals can be annoyingly clever.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li decides to abandon his “naive doctor” act and go straight for the jugular. He declares he could massacre the entire Mo Estate with one hand, establishing a very clear threat.
- To prove his point, he creates a fireball with his finger, instantly raising the room’s temperature. When the women still look doubtful, he flicks the fireball at a table, turning it to ash in a flash.
- The women reveal they already know about “Immortal Masters” (修仙者/Xianxiu). Li Shi cries it out in fear, and Yan Shi confesses that many in Jiayuan City have seen cultivators fighting, even mentioning their god-like abilities.
- Han Li questions them about Doctor Mo, confirming that Mo Juren’s obsession with cultivation originated from witnessing a real cultivator’s power firsthand.
- Acting on a hunch, Han Li notices a pair of half-burnt candles and smells a faint, suspicious scent.
- Yan Shi admits the candles are infused with a paralysis incense called “Drunken Stupor,” claiming it can weaken even a martial artist or cultivator. She tries to bluff, hoping it’s already too late for him.
- Han Li smugly reveals he has his own personal countermeasure against poisons and incenses, a secret method he developed after his past experiences with Doctor Mo, and refuses to explain it.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter beautifully illustrates the concept of **Social Bluffing**. Han Li has no idea if his crude method of resisting incense actually works against a refined alchemical agent like “Drunken Stupor,” but he ***acts*** as if it does. This is a core tactic in the Mortal Stream: when you’re in a tight spot, the best weapon isn’t a spell, but a confident expression and a vague threat. The power of a bluff lies in the opponent’s inability to verify your claims. By revealing he “has a method” and then grinning with utter confidence, Han Li forces the women to confront the terrifying possibility that their only trap has failed, psychologically breaking their advantage. He turns a defensive position into an offensive one using pure information control.

### Social Bluffing (诈胡/信息战)

A core survival tactic where a protagonist uses ambiguous information and an opponent’s own uncertainty to force a favorable outcome without physical violence.

## Reading Guide

Notice how the dynamics shift the moment the women reveal they know about cultivators. For a split second, Han Li is outmaneuvered—his trump card (“I’m a powerful mage!”) has already been played by the scenery. They weren’t terrified; they were “interested.” This forces him to adapt instantly, turning his attention from a brute force display to a sharper, more paranoid investigation of the room. Also, keep an eye on his final line: “I don’t make a habit of revealing my secrets to my enemies.” This isn’t just a funny quip. It’s a hard-learned lesson from the Doctor Mo incident, and it perfectly encapsulates his core philosophy of operational security.
