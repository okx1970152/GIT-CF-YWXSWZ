## Chapter Overview

The hunt for the Black Fiend Cult has turned into a slaughterhouse. Just as Liu Jing’s team breathes a sigh of relief after their True Treasure incinerates the Blood Servants, their victory lap becomes a death march. The Cult Leader, a calm, smiling man who looks like everyone’s favorite uncle, strolls out and casually executes a Foundation Establishment cultivator for pocketing a treasure. Then, in a flash of cold calculation, someone *inside* the team turns on them, killing Liu Jing and another disciple before anyone can react. This chapter is a brutal lesson in what happens when you assume a cornered predator is already beaten.

## Key Plot Points

- The Firebird True Treasure, wielded by Liu Jing, utterly annihilates the transformed Blood Servants and Qing Wen with contemptuous ease. Its power confirms the terrifying gap between Qi Condensation, Foundation Establishment, and Core Formation.
- Senior Brother Wang is caught pocketing a green bead from the battlefield. The Black Fiend Cult Leader appears, kills him with a single strike, and takes the bead back. Wang’s greedy mistake cost him everything.
- The Cult Leader introduces himself with a grandfatherly smile—clean-shaven, crow’s feet, utterly disarming—then demonstrates that his mercy is nonexistent.
- Liu Jing, emboldened by his success, delivers a heroic speech and prepares to lead the final charge. He doesn’t get the chance.
- Before the team can attack, an unseen traitor—someone they trusted—runs a hand through Liu Jing’s chest from behind. Liu Jing dies mid-thought, realizing only that Zhong Weiniang is still crying for him.
- The traitor reveals himself, holding two bodies impaled on his arms, and grins at the remaining survivors. The team is shattered.
- Zhong Weiniang’s reaction is pure, visceral trauma: her screams fade into a catatonic shock. Chen Qiaoqian pulls her behind her, shielding her, her own face a storm of grief and fury.
- Han Li watches it all unfold in cold silence. He registers the attack but couldn’t have stopped it. The chapter ends with the team in disarray, facing an enemy who has already dismantled their command structure.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“Passing into Liberation” (兵解, Bing Jie)**—When Chen Qiaoqian asks about Senior Sister Xuehong, she’s told the disciple has “passed into liberation.” This isn’t flowery euphemism; it’s a specific term in Chinese martial and cultivation fiction. Bing Jie refers to a cultivator’s soul being freed from the body through violent death (often a fatal wound). It implies the soul escaped before the body perished, leaving a chance—however slim—for reincarnation or survival. It’s the cultivation world’s version of “died with her boots on,” but with metaphysical consequences.

**The Cult Leader’s Casual Evil**—The Black Fiend Cult Leader is a masterclass in the “smiling villain” archetype in xianxia. He wears the face of a kindly elder, speaks gently, laughs easily, and kills without a flicker of hesitation. In Chinese web novel tradition, this contrast is more terrifying than any snarling monster: it signals that violence is not an emotion for him, but a tool. When a character like this appears, the reader knows the protagonist is not facing rage—they’re facing clinical indifference.

### Passing into Liberation / 兵解

A term in Chinese cultivation fiction for a cultivator dying by violence, releasing their soul from the body while still intact, granting a chance at survival beyond death.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a wake-up call for anyone who thought the Blood Servant fight was the climax. The structure mirrors classic Dark Forest logic: just when the hunters think they’ve won, a new predator reveals himself, and their own ranks turn out to have been compromised all along. Liu Jing’s death is especially poignant because he dies in the middle of a heroic fantasy—imagining his reputation soaring, his hand on his weapons, his mouth full of righteous words—only to have his heart punched out from behind. The lesson is ugly but consistent: in this world, standing up to give a speech is often the last thing you do.

Han Li’s behavior here is instructive. He doesn’t scream, doesn’t charge, doesn’t avenge anyone. He watches. He assesses. Even his internal reaction is clinical: he notes the Cult Leader’s power, identifies the traitor’s position, and files the information away. This is not cowardice—it’s survival programming. The Mortal Stream protagonist knows that a battle is lost the moment you waste energy on rage. For readers, this chapter is a test: can you still enjoy a story where the hero doesn’t avenge his comrades with a dramatic roar? If yes, you’re in for one hell of a ride.
