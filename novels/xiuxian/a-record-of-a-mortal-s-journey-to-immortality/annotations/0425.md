## Chapter Overview

After three long months of painstakingly dismantling a multi-layered ancient formation, the team is finally face-to-face with the last restriction, a fiery barrier swarming with tiny flame spirits called Fiend Wings. The chapter is a slow-burn tactical breakdown: watch the main duo—Han Li and Fairy Shi Die—shift from theoretical bickering to grudging mutual respect, and see how the entire team is deployed not for glory, but as a water-attribute fire brigade. It’s a textbook xianxia logistics session: no heroics, just cold, methodical problem-solving under a blazing sun.

## Key Plot Points

- The group finally gets a clear look at the forbidden area’s core: a multi-layered, murky yellow light barrier with a stone pillar at its center. The barrier repels spiritual sense, and the seven-colored light ribbons are revealed to be alive.
- Han Li feels suspicious about the formation’s sinister aura, but defers to Shi Die’s superior formation knowledge. He is briefly self-deprecating about his own judgment.
- Hu Yue suggests resting for the night before tackling the formation, and everyone agrees.
- A three-month montage follows: Shi Die handles the theoretical deduction, Han Li applies destructive techniques from Xin Ruyin’s playbook. They slowly form a tacit, efficient partnership.
- The last restriction transforms into a blazing red barrier, now inhabited by winged fire serpents—Fiend Wings—which devour souls and spit fires comparable to cultivator Nascent Flames.
- Shi Die explains the Fiend Wings’ origin and danger, then assigns water-attributed magic tools to Jin Qing, Qu Hun, Brother Jian, and Hu Yue. They take their positions.
- Han Li spreads out dozens of blue water-attributed formation implements, ready to strike.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Fiend Wings (翅恶 / Chi E):** These aren’t just magical fire snakes—they’re “fire element spirits” born from an extreme concentration of Yang energy. In xianxia logic, such spirits form only in ‘Three Yang’ (三阳) terrain, a rare and often man-made configuration designed to boost fire-elemental power while making containment a nightmare. Their short lifespan (mere hours!) means they’d disappear on their own *if* they weren’t instantly reborn by the formation, which turns them into an endless, disposable army. The classic counter is “water overcomes fire” in the Five Elements cycle (五行相克), hence Han Li pulling out every blue, cold-attribute tool he owns.

### Five Elements (五行)

The classical Chinese system of five phases (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) in a cycle of mutual generation and overcoming. Here, “Water overcomes Fire,” which is why Han Li deploys only water-attribute tools.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is all about *process*—the real meat of Mortal Stream expeditions isn’t the big boom, but the endless grind of research, resource management, and delegation. Notice how the narrative skips the boring days but lingers on the *dynamic*: Han Li finds the formation suspicious, yet he keeps his mouth shut and lets the ‘expert’ take the lead. He’s not trying to be a hero or prove he’s smart; he’s just trying to survive an unknown danger with minimal personal risk. Also, the author gives us a rare bit of insight into Han Li’s mind: “Why are all the formation experts I know women?” It’s a small, humanizing joke—and then he moves on without making it a big deal. Perfect Mortal Stream tone: acknowledge the oddity, file it away, and keep working.

## Legacy of Xin Ruyin

Though absent in body, Xin Ruyin’s technical legacy looms large over this entire operation. Every destructive technique Han Li uses to crack the formation is pulled straight from her notes. This isn’t a ‘power of friendship’ moment—it’s cold intellectual inheritance. Han Li found a superior asset (her knowledge), stored it, and now deploys it when his own skills hit a wall. The chapter underscores that in a dark cultivation world, the most dangerous advantage isn’t a high-grade weapon; it’s a complete set of theoretical tools that allow you to *unmake* what others have built.
