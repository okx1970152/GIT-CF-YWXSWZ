## Chapter Overview
Book 3 kicks off with a massive map reveal, and boy, does it deliver. This chapter is basically the "Prologue to the Starfall Sea" — a concentrated, high-density lore dump that reshapes everything we thought we knew about the world. Han Li, fresh off nearly getting caught by Extreme Yin Island’s net, plays it smart and heads straight for the neutral, free-est city in the entire sea. We get power structures, teleportation economics, and the tantalizing promise of demonic core hunting. But beneath all the information, there’s a quieter, more dangerous layer: Han Li is now moving from "survive in a local sect" to "survive in an entire ocean’s geopolitical sandbox." He’s no longer a low-level target. He’s a player deciding where to sit at the table. Get ready, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is the door opening to a much bigger, much bloodier playground.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li escapes the immediate threat of being hunted by Extreme Yin Island, but remains cautious. He modifies his appearance with a secret technique from Qu Hun and changes direction toward the northeast.
- He stops at a small border island market, stocking up on raw materials and buying every text and sea chart he can find about the Starfall Sea — a major investment in information warfare.
- Reading the texts reveals the Starfall Sea’s core world structure: the known "Inner Starfall Sea" vs. the beast-infested "Outer Starfall Sea" (nicknamed the "Sea of Demons"), where demonic beasts can rival Nascent Soul cultivators.
- The overarching power is the "Star Palace," ruling from the super-city "Heaven Star City," governed by the mysterious "Heaven Star Twin Saints" — believed to be Nascent Soul late-stage or even Deity Transformation level beings.
- The Star Palace’s administrative control is fascinating: Twenty-Four Outer Star Islands (selected every century via the "Star Plucking Assembly") and Twelve Inner Star Islands (directly appointed by the Twin Saints).
- Heaven Star City is presented as a truly neutral, free market zone, notorious for its laissez-faire policy — even wanted criminals can stroll the streets as long as they don't cause trouble.
- The city’s greatest draw is its teleportation arrays to dozens of "Demonic Beast Islands" in the Outer Sea, enabling a lucrative demonic core hunting economy — a single 6th-grade core can fund a Foundation Establishment cultivator for life.
- Han Li coldly weighs his options and instantly commits to heading for Heaven Star City, prioritizing safe neutrality and financial opportunity over local power struggles.
- On the long journey, he practices dual-mindedness — flying his artifact while cultivating the Great Derivative Art — and gradually masters the skill through sheer repetition (the "practice makes perfect" principle in full effect).

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The "Star Plucking Assembly" and Its Worldbuilding Function**

The "Star Plucking Assembly" (*摘星会*, zhāi xīng huì) is not just a cool name. In Chinese internet novel culture, the term "plucking stars" is often used to describe grasping something seemingly unreachable — a metaphor for ambition that defies one’s innate status. The very name of the competition signals that the Star Palace has designed a systemic pressure valve: instead of letting frustrated cultivators fester into rebellion, they give the ambitious a controlled, titanic arena to bleed off their energy. It’s a masterstroke of institutional survival. The outer island lords, won through open combat, become loyal because they *earned* their seat. No bloodline, no bureaucracy, just a blade and a dream.

**"Demonic Core" vs. Golden Core: A Dangerous Distinction**

The chapter introduces **Demonic Cores** (煞丹, shā dān) as a legitimate, valuable resource harvested from demonic beasts. In standard cultivation logic, a human cultivator’s Golden Core (金丹) is refined through years of meditation and pill consumption — pure, Yang-aligned stability. A Demonic Core, however, is raw, untamed, and Yin-tempered — extracted directly from a beast’s violent life force. Refining it into usable energy requires special techniques or high-temperature alchemy. This is where the "kill the beast, take its core, sell it, buy better pills" economic loop comes from. It also sets up a wonderful tension: is a cultivator who relies *too heavily* on demonic cores risking their own spiritual purity? The novel will explore this later.

## Reading Guide
- **Pay close attention to the "Outer Starfall Sea" lore.** The mention of demonic beasts rivaling Nascent Soul cultivators is not just flavor text — it’s a direct promise that the difficulty curve of the story is about to spike. Han Li isn’t fighting Qi Condensation juniors anymore. The sea is full of monsters that could eat him alive.
- **Han Li’s instant decision to go to Heaven Star City is a masterclass in Mortal Stream logic.** He doesn't waste time on emotions or revenge fantasies. He sees a free, safe, resource-rich environment with a clear route to advancement (demonic core hunting, assembly access) and commits without hesitation.
- **The "dual-mindedness" practice is a quiet character moment worth savoring.** It shows Han Li’s relentless self-improvement instinct: even while riding a flying artifact for months, he tries to bend the rules of mental focus. This is a much more realistic "training montage" than any flashy breakthrough.
- **Watch for the "Star Palace’s neutrality" line — it’s a setup.** A city that refuses to arrest its own wanted criminals is a powder keg. When the big storm comes, that "freedom" will turn into the opening for a bloodbath.
- **This chapter is a map.** If you love the thrill of a new game world opening up, you’ll love this. If you’re impatient for action, know this: the “fishing” metaphor is about to pay off big.

## Deep Dive: The Economics of Demonic Core Hunting
The chapter casually mentions that a sixth-grade demonic core can fund a Foundation Establishment cultivator for life (~a thousand spirit stones for a fifth-grade, even more for a sixth). But the *cost* is hidden in the rest of the text. You need to pay for teleportation (not cheap), risk your life against beasts that can destroy Foundation Establishment cultivators, and then have the skill to actually *extract* the core from a rotting beast corpse. This creates a brutally efficient "wealth separation" mechanism: the poor stay poor because they can’t afford the entry fee, while the rich get richer by having the gear to survive multiple hunts. Heaven Star City is a meritocracy only if you define "merit" as "being able to pay the starting cost."
