## Chapter Overview

Whoa, fellow Daoists! Put down your spirit tea—our boy Han Li just pulled off one of the most cold-blooded, elegantly petty assassinations in the mortal world! And it wasn’t even against a cultivator; it was against a mortal sect leader who had the sheer audacity to *think about* snitching. We also get the long-awaited reunion with Qu Hun, and surprise—the soulless puppet has sprouted a mind of his own! This chapter is a masterclass in Han Li’s signature brand of paranoid logistics, building a terrifyingly beautiful trap that spans a *year* just for a little insurance. We’re talking about flesh-burrowing heart worms, a spectral reunion, and the stomach-churning realization that Han Li’s “kindness” to a beautiful woman is delivered through a guaranteed, untraceable death sentence for her abductor. Get ready to see the true cost of Han Li’s peace of mind.

## Key Plot Points

- **The “Goodbye” Slap:** Before leaving the Five Colors Sect, Han Li gives the old leader a friendly pat on the shoulder. However, this is a delivery method for the Heart-Boring Worm, a parasitic insect as fine as a hair that will slowly crawl to the man’s heart over a year, causing a death that perfectly mimics a heart attack.
- **The Long Con:** Han Li’s logic is chillingly pragmatic. The leader showed signs of being duplicitous (口不对心), and since Han Li can’t trust a mortal who *might* inform on him, he sets up a failsafe that will only trigger a year later, creating a perfect, untraceable alibi.
- **Tracking the Ghost:** Han Li uses the Soul Summoning Bell, which contains a strand of Qu Hun’s Essence Blood, to track the mutated puppet deep into a desolate mountain range west of Jia Yuan City.
- **The Awakened Puppet:** Han Li finds Qu Hun alive and well, and is shocked to sense the energy of a Qi Condensation Layer 5 or 6 cultivator emanating from the body. The puppet is no longer a lifeless shell; it has regained consciousness and is actively cultivating.
- **A Bitter Realization:** Han Li realizes the truth about Zhang Tie (Qu Hun’s original body). Zhang Tie wasn’t talentless; he simply lacked a wood-attribute Spiritual Root. He had a different root all along, making his fate a tragic case of wrong-place-wrong-time.
- **The Confrontation:** The awakened Qu Hun, fearing nothing, monologues about needing a few more months to counter his only weakness. This is the cue for Han Li to step out of the shadows and reveal himself, holding the very artifact that the stranger fears.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Mortal Stream’s “Assassin’s Patience”:** This chapter perfectly illustrates the “one-to-two-year” kill window in a Mortal Stream narrative. It’s not about immediate revenge; it’s about absolute, institutional deniability. The Heart-Boring Worm’s delayed activation is a political weapon, not just a personal one. In the world of wuxia and xianxia, the most terrifying techniques are often the ones that don't even *look* like an attack.
- **Essence Blood (精血) as a Soul Anchor:** The fact that the Soul Summoning Bell was refined with a strand of Qu Hun’s Essence Blood (精血) is a cornerstone of xianxia tracking magic. A cultivator’s essence blood contains a fragment of their spiritual imprint and soul energy. Using it to create a tracking tool implies a deep, almost ownership-level connection—reinforcing how Qu Hun is initially a bound asset, not an ally.
- **The Creeping Horror of Gu:** The Heart-Boring Worm belongs to the “Gu” (蛊) tradition of Chinese folklore, albeit a highly refined, specialized version. Gu are usually poisonous insects cultivated for control or assassination. The fact that this worm is nearly invisible and takes a *year* to kill is a terrifying upgrade from the standard flesh-eating bugs, showcasing a cold, scientific approach to assassination.

## Reading Guide

Okay, let’s talk about Han Li’s mental state here. He’s not just satisfied; he’s practically smug. He thinks he’s being clever for using the worm, and he even mentally pats himself on the back for “fulfilling his promise to Mo Fengwu” (墨凤舞). This is a chilling moment of self-deception. He wasn't protecting Mo Yuzhu from a bad father-in-law; he was securing his own anonymity with a biological time bomb. Notice the wording: he says he doesn’t have to play the “villain” (恶人) in front of her. What he’s really doing is using one promise (to kill or stop the old man) as a cover for a separate, much crueler act of prevention. It shows how his own internal narrative is adjusting to justify his ruthlessness. When you read his final, satisfied thought about "fulfilling the promise," ask yourself: is this righteousness, or a man justifying a murder he finds convenient? The narrative leaves the answer chillingly ambiguous, which is the hallmark of the Mortal Stream.

- **Essence Blood (精血):** The single most vital and concentrated form of a cultivator’s life force. Unlike ordinary blood, it can be permanently lost and is a source of immense power for artifacts, tracking spells, and forbidden arts; losing it causes irreversible damage to one’s vitality and cultivation potential.
- **Heart-Boring Worm (钻心虫):** A sinister parasitic insect nearly invisible to the naked eye, used for slow, perfectly disguised assassinations; it crawls from the skin to the heart over a period of years, mimicking a natural heart attack with no detectable magical traces.
