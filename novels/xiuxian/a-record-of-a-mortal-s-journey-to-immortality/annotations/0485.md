## Chapter Overview

Hoo boy, fellow Daoists! Just when you thought things were settling into a predictable “three Nascent Soul monsters stroll through an ancient ruin” routine, Han Li does *that thing* he does best—stumbles over a secret that everyone else missed. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream storytelling: while the heavy hitters posture and calculate, Han Li uses his grubby mortal-world street smarts to find a hidden cavity in a wall where no right-thinking cultivator would ever look. It’s a classic reminder that in this world, professional paranoia and low-brow skills pay better than raw power.

## Key Plot Points

- The three Nascent Soul cultivators (Man Brutal Beard, Extreme Yin, the Confucian-robed elder) finally reach the fifth floor, where the Cold Pearl Platform awaits. They confirm that Wan Tianming’s righteous-path group entered hours ago and haven’t emerged.
- Extreme Yin’s fleeting glance at the bronze halberds Han Li picked up earlier tells Han Li (and us) that those weapons are valuable—and that another post-treasure “settlement” is being added to his mental ledger.
- Han Li realizes Profound Bone Saint is now aligned with Man Brutal Beard, reducing his own leverage. The window for renegotiation with the undead schemer has effectively closed.
- While the elders rest and scheme, Han Li circles the platform alone. He’s not hiding—he’s *hunting for data*. And he finds it: a hollow section in an otherwise heavily restricted stone wall, hidden behind a carved demon-beast eye.
- The chapter closes on that discovery: Han Li confirms the cavity is hollow and contains *something*. The nature of that secret is left for the next chapter.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Heavenly Barrier” (天罡罩)** mentioned here is an ancient restriction type that xianxia often uses as a plot-wall: it’s explicitly designed to be unbreachable except through a single, pre-set entrance. In real-world Chinese folklore, the term *Tiangang* originally referred to the Big Dipper stars and, by extension, celestial arrays used in Daoist ritual magic to lock, seal, or suppress. Here, it’s the author saying “no sneaky exits, kids.”

**Hollow Cavities in Ancient Architecture** — The idea that an ancient cultivator’s abode might have a mundane hidden compartment (rather than a magical one) is a wonderfully subversive twist on the genre. Normally, xianxia readers expect secrets to be protected by divine formations and spirit-sensing traps. Han Li’s discovery here relies not on divine sense or magic, but on *Jianghu survival training*—specifically, the kind of manual door-tapping practiced by mortal burglars and martial artists in wuxia stories. It’s a sharp reminder that in the Mortal Stream, the best skills are often the lowest-tech ones.

## Reading Guide

This chapter reads like the calm before a storm. Everyone’s waiting, the pressure is building, and Han Li is quietly turning over a stone no one else bothered to check. The real tension here isn’t action—it’s the *information asymmetry* Han Li is about to generate. Get ready—something’s about to crack open, and it probably won’t be friendly.

Also, pay attention to how Han Li’s paranoia is framed: he’s not brave, he’s not desperate, he’s just *systematically curious*. That slap on the wall wasn’t anger; it was a reflex honed by a lifetime of scanning every environment for escape routes. That’s the Mortal Stream difference.

**Deep Lore Corner: The Blood-Eyed Winged Beast Carving** — The description of this mural is brief but loaded. In xianxia aesthetics, monster carvings are rarely random decoration; they often function as warning markers, seal indicators, or key-switches. The fact that this one covers a hollow cavity suggests someone (an ancient architect? a later escapee?) deliberately chose the image of a *狂暴, bloodthirsty beast* to distract intruders. It’s a classic misdirection tactic—hide your secret behind the ugliest thing you can find. Han Li’s ability to see past the ugly and focus on the functional is exactly what keeps him alive.
