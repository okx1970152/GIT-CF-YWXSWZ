## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists! After months of silent waiting on a barren reef, our favorite paranoid herb-gatherer finally makes his move. This chapter is a classic Mortal Stream setup: Han Li acquires a mysterious treasure that turns out to be a total dud (hello, ancient demonic beast yoga manual), sighs philosophically, and pivots to his real mission—sneaking onto the island of an eighth-grade demon beast to steal its precious Companion Herb. The tension is built not through explosions, but through footsteps, silence, and the slow dread of a pool that refuses to give up its secrets. It’s slow-burn suspense, not firework action, and it shows exactly why this novel rewards the patient reader.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li examines the copper plate acquired from the dark-skinned cultivator, discovering it records an ancient beast-cultivation technique with thirty-six postures, completely unusable by a human cultivator.
- After a moment of frustration (and a wry “I’ve been had” feeling), Han Li tucks it away and refocuses on the real priority: the Companion Herb hunt.
- He reflects on his improving cultivation aptitude thanks to the Heaven-Mending Pill, noting he is now on par with a three-spiritual-root cultivator—a small but meaningful boost to his Nascent Soul hopes.
- Han Li leaves Wen Siyue to her cultivation, deposits tools and formation secrets for her, and departs alone for the Profound Abyss Sea.
- Upon arrival at the target island, he spends months in patient observation from a distant reef, never once daring to scan the island with his spiritual sense.
- Finally committing to a stealth infiltration at dawn, he finds the cave, navigates its depths undetected, and confirms the presence of the black Companion Herb—exactly what he’s been seeking.
- The chapter ends on a cliffhanger: Han Li’s spiritual sense brushed only the surface of the impossibly deep pool, and the water hides unknown dangers. Is the Gale Beast lurking below?

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Three Heads and Six Arms (三头六臂)** – In Chinese mythology, this form is famously associated with Nezha (哪吒), the child-god who could manifest multiple heads and arms in battle. Xianxia often borrows this imagery for ancient demonic or divine entities. Here, the copper plate’s beast-cultivation technique uses this posture sequence, hinting at both ancient power and profound incompatibility with human cultivation.

**Companion Herb (伴妖草)** – A classic xianxia trope: certain rare herbs only grow near high-grade demon beasts, fed by their residual demonic qi. The herb’s color indicates the beast’s grade (black for eighth-grade and above). Harvesting it means risking direct proximity to the beast—a classic *high-risk, high-value* scenario that defines the genre’s resource economy. It’s not a quest; it’s a calculated theft.

**Heaven-Mending Pill (补天丹)** – The name evokes the ancient Chinese myth of *Nuwa mending the sky* (女娲补天), implying a remedy for a fundamental flaw. In xianxia, such pills repair degraded or defective spiritual roots, making them nearly priceless. Han Li’s gradual improvement from “false spiritual roots” to “three-root competence” is a slow but meaningful character progression—no flashy power-ups, only patient biological recalibration.

**Profound Abyss Sea / 奇渊海域** – The name “奇渊” (Strange Abyss) signals a place of lawless, dangerous waters where high-grade demon beasts dominate and human cultivators tread with extreme caution. In the Mortal Stream universe, such zones are less adventure destinations and more **high-stakes foraging zones** where a single wrong move equals death.

### Magical Backlash (反噬, Fangshi)

The common term for a forbidden or unstable technique turning on its caster, often with fatal results.

### Chicken Rib (鸡肋)

A classical Chinese idiom for something too valuable to discard but useless to its owner—like a demonic cultivation technique in human hands.

## Reading Guide

Han Li’s detour with the copper plate is a subtle but important echo of the novel’s core lesson: **not every treasure is a prize.** Some are traps, some are junk, and some are simply tools for another species. The Mortal Stream protagonist wastes no energy on dramatic disappointment—he sighs, files the loss, and moves on. Pair this with the long, patient island vigil, and you see why Han Li is who he is: he operates on months-long timelines, not adrenaline spikes.

The real tension here is the **unknown variable in the pool**. Han Li has confirmation that the Companion Herb exists, but the water hides a potential eighth-grade apex predator. The chapter ends with him reaching out—his hand literally extended toward both the herb and the mystery—leaving the reader suspended in that moment of uncertainty. That’s peak Mortal Stream storytelling: the reward is right there, but the price tag is invisible.

Get ready. The next chapter might be very short—or very, very bloody.
