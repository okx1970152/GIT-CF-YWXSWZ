## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow travelers of the Starfall Sea! Here we are—Han Li eyeball-deep in yet another life-or-death calculation, and this time the odds are stacked with freshly forged black fire. Chapter 412 cranks the tension to eleven. Revered Ancestor Extreme Yin has arrived in a possessed body, and he’s not here to negotiate. He masterfully demonstrates his newly perfected Celestial Corpse Flame by vaporizing a Core Formation escapee like he’s swatting a fly. Our boy Han Li is stuck making the worst kind of choice: submit to a soul-branding leash that essentially hands over the keys to his existence, or try to fight his way out against a Nascent Soul monster who can erase you from spacetime. But here’s the kicker—our favorite cautious cultivator senses something is wrong. The old fiend seems *too* confident, *too* alone. Call it the ‘Han Li sixth sense.’ Get ready for a standoff built on razor-sharp calculation, crumbling alliances, and that signature Mortal Stream tension where survival is a math problem with no clean answers.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Demonstrated Terror:** The chapter opens with the confirmation that the old man (Extreme Yin's possessed vessel) has indeed mastered the Celestial Corpse Flame—a black fire so fast it reduces a Core Formation cultivator to ash before he can finish a sentence. The speed and power are deliberately highlighted: Han Li calculates he could not dodge it within 200–300 feet, and ordinary treasures would be useless.
- **The Soul-Branding Ultimatum:** Extreme Yin produces Soul-Branding Tablets and demands one-third of everyone’s primordial soul. This is not a threat he makes lightly; it’s an institutional leash of the cultivation world, a technique only Nascent Soul cultivators can wield. Refusal means death; acceptance means permanent servitude. Han Li flatly refuses internally—this is a line he will not cross.
- **The Brief Alliance:** A loose coalition forms under duress. The Hidden Kill Sect’s middle-aged man, Elder Chi Huo (still hidden in his cloud), and the Sublime Sound Sect reluctantly agree to a temporary battle plan: Chi Huo and the middle-aged man take Extreme Yin; everyone else cleans up the traitors. It’s a shaky arrangement held together by mutual terror.
- **The Political Bombshell:** Zileng drops a critical piece of intel: the Star Palace has prohibited Soul-Branding Art among Nascent Soul experts. Extreme Yin’s chilling reply—that the Heavenly Twin Saints dare not leave Heavenly Star City—changes the entire power calculus of the region. The old guard is no longer the deterrent it once was.
- **Han Li’s Unease:** Despite the temporary alliance forming, Han Li is unsettled. He senses something is wrong. Why would Extreme Yin come alone? He quietly instructs Qu Hun and prepares his options. That paranoid calculative instinct is screaming, and it’s never wrong.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Soul-Branding (禁神术):** This is a classic 'institutional leash' technique in xianxia, and one of the most terrifying. The victim voluntarily gives up a fragment of their primordial soul, which the caster seals onto a tablet. If the tablet is destroyed, that fragment is gone—causing permanent mental damage or death. The translation 'Soul-Branding Tablet' is locked; think of it less as a magical gadget and more as a loaded gun pointed at your very existence. Only Nascent Soul cultivators can perform it, which underscores the absolute power gap in the cultivation world: the strong can literally own a piece of your soul.

**Possession Art (附身大法) vs. True Body:** The chapter makes a subtle point about Extreme Yin possessing a vessel rather than being present in person. A possessed body has limitations: techniques like the Celestial Corpse Flame can only be used a few times before the vessel destroys itself—it cannot handle the strain of full Nascent Soul power. This is the logical loophole the allies will rely on. In xianxia, a possessed body is always a weaker, compromised vector.

**Twin Saints of the Heavenly Star (天星双圣):** The ultimate authorities of the Star Palace, the nominal enforcers of order in the Chaotic Star Seas. Extreme Yin’s remark that they ‘dare not leave the city’ is a political earthquake. It implies either they are trapped, weakened, or the entire region is on the verge of a power vacuum. For readers, this is a worldbuilding signal: the training wheels are coming off, and the Dark Forest is about to get even darker.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in tension-building without a single sword clash. The entire conflict is a *standoff of calculation*: everyone is running threat assessments and exit strategies in real time. Pay close attention to Han Li’s unease—it’s not generic anxiety; it’s a specific pattern-match that tells you something is off about Extreme Yin’s plan. The black flame demonstration is not just action porn; it’s the book establishing the stakes so the reader feels the weight of every character’s decision.

Also, note the failed escape by the hawk-nosed cultivator. In the Mortal Stream, running alone almost never works. The world punishes solo opportunism. The only viable survival path is either a coordinated stand or a *smarter* escape—and Han Li is already thinking several moves ahead.
