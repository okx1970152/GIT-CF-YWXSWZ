## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to another chapter where the *Mortal Stream* philosophy slices through any lingering heroic delusions. This isn’t an adventure; it’s a brutal audit of survival. We find our expedition team deep in the ancient ruins, and the tension that has been simmering finally boils over. The master of the ghost fog shows their hand, and the cost of this treasure hunt becomes terrifyingly clear. Get ready for a sharp, unforgiving lesson in what happens when your “allies” are driven by desperation and your enemies are ancient, calculating, and far beyond your weight class. The winds of the海外 (overseas) are blowing, and they carry the stench of death.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Price of Overconfidence:** Hu Yue, despite being a Core Formation cultivator with a hidden trump card (a spiritually-aware flying blade), falls to a surprise attack from the white skeleton. His blade’s auto-defense never triggers because the ambusher is not a living enemy he can perceive. This is a classic *Mortal Stream* execution: the strongest in the party is the first to fall, because he relied on a system of power that didn’t account for true, ancient stealth.
*   **The Zhì Yáng Bird Makes Its Debut:** Cornered by the ghost fog, Han Li activates the scroll he acquired earlier, unleashing clones of the legendary Zhì Yáng Bird. This reveals his strategic preparation—while others are panicking, he’s calmly deploying counters. The birds are a temporary solution, forcing the ghost fog back but not eliminating it, buying him time to assess the real threat.
*   **Shi Die’s Fatal Greed:** The demonic maiden, whose sharp tongue and entitlement were on full display earlier, makes a desperate break for the exit. She’s immediately killed by a ghost claw from the wall, turned into a dry husk, and tossed aside. Her death is shockingly fast and ignominious, a brutal lesson that cowardice and selfishness are not survival traits in this deep a darkness.
*   **The Master of the Ruins Revealed:** A strange, green-glowing entity emerges from a stone pillar. It possesses intelligence and speaks with chilling clarity. The immense pressure it exerts on both Jin Qing and Han Li suggests a power level far beyond theirs—perhaps even Nascent Soul. The chapter ends with the revelation that the ruins’ true guardian is no mindless beast, but a calculating predator.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

*   **僵尸 vs. 尸傀 (Jiangshi vs. Corpse Puppet):** The white skeleton that kills Hu Yue is not a standard 僵尸 (hopping vampire) from folklore. It is a 尸傀 (corpse puppet), a refined tool controlled by a master. In Chinese mythology, powerful necromancers or demonic cultivators can inhabit and manipulate the dead, turning them into nearly indestructible, weaponized vessels. The skeleton’s resilience against Jin Qing’s magical treasure is a testament to the master’s refinement skill and the puppet’s ancient origin.
*   **Zhì Yáng or 脂阳鸟 (Fat-Sun Bird):** The name of this fire-element spirit bird is deceptive. The character 脂 (fat/oil) suggests it was a fragile, oil-rich creature that was easy to burn, yet it specialized in consuming Yin spirits. In *Xianxia* world-building, this is a classic example of a “restrained element” system: the most fragile-looking bird made of fire is the ultimate predator of ghostly entities. Its use also hints at the massive amount of accumulated wealth needed to survive in this world—having a scroll with even a fragment of its spirit is a significant asset.

## Reading Guide

*   **Watch the Power Dynamics:** Pay close attention to how the ghost fog’s master categorizes the remaining party. It dismisses Jin Qing as a mere tool, but its tone shifts when addressing Han Li. The entity is assessing *value*, not just strength. Han Li’s ability to summon the Zhì Yáng Bird and his calm demeanor mark him as the most dangerous variable in the room.
*   **The “Asset” Mentality:** The narrative treats Shi Die’s death not with tragedy, but with cold, clinical efficiency. She was a resource that tried to flee and was disposed of. This reinforces the core *Mortal Stream* principle: in a life-or-death dungeon, your allies are as much a liability as an asset. Friendship and debt are currencies that can be spent, but they don’t buy loyalty in the face of absolute terror.
*   **The Unseen Master’s Gamble:** The master of the ruins lets its servants do the initial work, then emerges to claim the spoils. This is a classic strategic opening. It has bought time to assess the newcomers, and its offer to find a “vessel” for itself reveals its core motivation—it wants to escape the ruins, not just guard the treasure. This makes it a far more dangerous and unpredictable opponent than a simple guardian beast.
