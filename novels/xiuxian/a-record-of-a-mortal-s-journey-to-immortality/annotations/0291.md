## Chapter Overview

Prince Xin’s celebration party for his concubine’s recovery turns into an unexpected recruitment fair for an immortal master—a white-haired, baby-faced cultivator at the grand height of Qi Condensation Layer 5 or 6. Han Li watches from the sidelines with cold amusement while mortal nobles trip over themselves to shove their children forward. But the key question quietly lands in Qin Yan’s lap: is this the demonic infiltrator he was hired to guard against? And even if he’s not, is a low-tier cultivator worth selling your grandchildren into apprenticeship for? The answer, Han Li makes clear with a few chilling chuckles, depends entirely on what you think you’re buying.

## Key Plot Points

- Prince Xin announces that his concubine’s mysterious illness was cured by a “living immortal” staying at his residence—a true cultivator who can summon wind and rain, swallow fire and spit ice.
- The prince further reveals that the same immortal recognized a “celestial root” in his youngest son and accepted him as a disciple, now planning to take him away for immortal cultivation.
- Prince Xin offers the same opportunity to all his guests’ children, inviting them to meet the immortal master—who turns out to be a white-haired Daoist with a baby-like complexion.
- The mortal guests are ecstatic, swarming the old cultivator with flattery and requests for their sons and daughters to be taken as disciples.
- Qin Yan hesitates, tempted but uneasy, until Han Li speaks into his ear with a sound transmission: the man is *not* a demonic cultivator, so whether to send his grandchildren is up to Qin Yan—but Han Li’s cold “heh heh” hints that this “immortal master” is no real prize.
- The chapter ends with the author’s note urging readers to save monthly votes for the double period and apologizing for a single chapter due to health.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**“Immortal Master” (仙师)**  
A common mortal honorific for any cultivator, regardless of actual power. In the novel’s world, even a Qi Condensation disciple who can toss a single fireball is seen as a living god by ordinary people—cultivation’s power gap is so absolute that the lowest-tier cultivator is still a miracle-worker to mortal eyes. Han Li’s contempt is entirely deserved: a Foundation Establishment cultivator could crush this “immortal master” without using a single spell.

**Sound Transmission (传音)**  
A basic cultivation technique that sends one’s voice directly to a specific person’s ear without anyone else hearing. It is a hallmark of cultivator-to-mortal communication in xianxia—often used to dispense cold, tactical advice that the recipient cannot acknowledge aloud without breaking social protocol.

**Monthly Tickets and Author’s Notes**  
The author’s closing note is a standard feature of Chinese web novel serialization. Writers often use these personal addendums to explain delays, ask for votes, or share health updates. In translation, it’s important to preserve this tone of transparent, urgent honesty, as it maintains the bond between author and reader that is part of the genre’s culture.

### Social Tribulation

A type of conflict in the Mortal Stream where a protagonist must navigate complex human relationships and political hierarchies with the same cold calculation as a physical battle.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in the *social tribulation*—a type of conflict where the battlefield is not mana or swords, but social pressure, status games, and the fear of missing out. Han Li’s silent observation while every mortal noble scrambles to throw their children at a mediocre cultivator is a beautiful demonstration of what happens when you outrank the entire scene: you don’t need to say a word to own the room. His sound transmission to Qin Yan is the perfect Mortal Stream move—reveal key information, leave the choice to the client, and let your cold laughter do the rest. Also worth noting: supply-chain warfare isn’t always literal. By showing that even an obvious fake “immortal master” attracts desperate families, the narrative reminds us that in a system where resources are hoarded from the top, the middle class will leap at any chance to climb—even if the ladder is made of bamboo and wobbling.
