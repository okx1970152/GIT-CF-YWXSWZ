## Chapter Overview

The survivors of the Ice Flame Path are finally counted—and the list is grim. Only a handful of the original expedition remain in the stone hall, and the atmosphere is thick with suspicion and barely restrained fury. The three Nascent Soul titans—Extreme Yin, the Azure Garment Elder, and Wan Tianming—are all silently blaming Star Palace for the deadly upheaval that cut their forces down. But no one dares to speak openly. When Star Palace’s two elders arrive with a transparently insincere apology, the hall simmers with hatred that nobody is willing to act upon. Then, in a flash of white light, Han Li appears—much to the relief of the demonic old monsters who still need his Blood Jade Spider. The chapter closes with the four treasure passages opening, setting the stage for the next deadly gamble.

## Key Plot Points

- The Ice Flame Path aftermath is revealed: only a dozen or so cultivators made it through alive, confirming that the weak have been systematically eliminated.
- Extreme Yin’s and the Azure Garment Elder’s anxiety over Han Li’s absence reveals just how dependent they are on his Blood Jade Spider for the inner chamber’s treasure.
- Wan Tianming, though more composed, is equally frustrated—many of his righteous-path plans relied on Core Formation pawns who did not survive the altered trial.
- The two Star Palace elders arrive and deliver a carefully worded, shameless apology that shifts all blame onto an anonymous “careless” cultivator; no one dares to confront them, reinforcing Star Palace’s absolute authority.
- Han Li and Yuan Yao are the last to teleport in; Extreme Yin’s delighted reaction and immediate summoning confirms Han Li’s elevated status as a key asset.
- Ugly Wu’s hostility toward Han Li is openly addressed by Extreme Yin, who uses a voice transmission to rein his disciple in—but the tension remains.
- The four passages open simultaneously, and the Star Palace elder explains the rules: three paths lead to treasure pavilions (ancient treasures, elixirs, techniques), while the fourth is a dead end that leads directly to the next trial without any prize.
- Han Li notes the Profound Bone Saint’s presence and begins to consider coordination, knowing that the real confrontation is still ahead.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Master-Disciple” Transactional Relationship**  
Extreme Yin’s display of warmth toward Han Li in this chapter is a textbook example of how *master-disciple* bonds function in the Mortal Stream. It is not affection; it is *asset management*. Extreme Yin places a high value on the Blood Jade Spider and sees Han Li only as its carrier. The moment Han Li’s utility expires, so will the “kindness.” New readers should note: in *RMJI*, a master’s smile is often more dangerous than a stranger’s blade.

**Star Palace’s Tactical Bluff**  
The two elders’ public apology is a masterstroke of power projection. They admit fault—but in terms so vague that no one can pin the blame on them. Then they confidently wait to see who dares to act. No one does. This is the cultivation world’s version of *credible deterrence*: Star Palace’s reputation is so overwhelming that even the most powerful cultivators in the hall swallow their rage. The lesson: in the Dark Forest, the strongest faction does not need to win every argument; it just needs to make the cost of arguing prohibitively high.

**The Four-Is-Not-Optional Rule**  
The fourth passage’s description is a classic xianxia trap: it taunts the greedy by offering a direct path with no treasure, daring them to take it. The risk of regretting a missed opportunity is often stronger than the fear of danger. Han Li’s calculation will likely factor in the hidden cost of *opportunity loss*—a concept the Mortal Stream treats with deadly seriousness.

## Reading Guide

Get ready, because the gauntlet just got sharper. The trash has been sorted, and only the real players are left on the board. What makes this chapter so satisfying is the way the power dynamics shift in real time: Han Li walks in as a survivor, but Extreme Yin, the Azure Garment Elder, and Man Brutal Beard all immediately treat him as a *prize asset*. That is both protection and a leash—and Han Li knows it. The real game begins with the four treasure passages, and you can bet he is already running the numbers on which path gives him the best escape vector. Yuan Yao’s silent wariness and Ugly Wu’s suppressed hostility are the extra seasoning. The stage is set for the first real confrontation—and the first real choice—of the inner trial. Fasten your seatbelt, Fellow Daoist.
