## Chapter Overview
Get ready, fellow Daoists—chapter 137 marks a rare breather in Han Li’s relentless survival grind, but don’t be fooled: even a shopping trip in the cultivation world is a razor-sharp negotiation where every glance, sniff, and stammer is logged in his mental ledger. Our stoic protagonist stumbles into the stall of a sweet, soft-spoken young woman peddling copies of the Eternal Spring Art. After bartering with Yellow Dragon Pills, he finds himself drawn—against his better judgment—to a genuine treasure: the Golden Bamboo Brush, a top-tier talisman tool made from demon beast hair. But the transaction comes wrapped in layers of calculation, sudden vulnerability, and a reminder that even in the mundane act of trading, the Mortal Stream’s logic of scarcity and leverage never sleeps.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li successfully trades two Yellow Dragon Pills for the full thirteen-layer Eternal Spring Art manual, confirming that his stockpile of pills is a powerful currency in the low-level cultivation market.
- The stall owner—a gentle, pretty young woman—becomes visibly flustered when she identifies the pills as cultivation-enhancing medicine, revealing how scarce and valuable such pills are among cultivators stuck at bottlenecks.
- Her older brother, a giant of a man with Qu Hun’s stature, crashes into the scene with zero subtlety, displaying the kind of reckless, brute-force personality that contrasts with Han Li’s cautious precision.
- The young woman offers the Golden Bamboo Brush—a family heirloom connected to a master talisman maker—as a last resort to fund her brother’s breakthrough for the Ascension Assembly.
- Han Li’s mind briefly wanders, moved by the young woman’s resemblance to his younger sister, a rare flash of sentiment that almost leads him to accept a less favorable deal before his survival instincts pull him back.
- The chapter ends with a crucial worldbuilding revelation: most cultivators entering the Ascension Assembly arena are stuck at bottlenecks precisely because those with growth potential still avoid open competition—creating a panic-buying market for pills.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Ascension Assembly Bottleneck Economy**  
This chapter delivers a sharp, almost cynical insight into the cultivation world’s social structure: the Ascension Assembly (升仙会) is not a venue for rising stars to compete fairly, but a last resort for the desperate. Those who still have room to grow hide in seclusion and hoard their potential, leaving only the “stuck” to fight it out in the arena. This creates a brutal supply-demand spiral where any pill that can push a cultivator over a bottleneck becomes priceless—and sellers like Han Li hold immense, quiet leverage. It’s a textbook example of the Mortal Stream’s resource-scarcity realism: power isn’t just a matter of talent, but of timing, information, and knowing who’s buying.

**The Golden Bamboo Brush**  
The brush (金竺笔) is a classic xianxia crafting item. Its materials—Gold Essence, Black Iron, and the neck hair of a second-tier Golden-Eyed Ape—place it firmly in the domain of Foundation Establishment-level artifact refinement. The process (three days and three nights of tempering with both *civil fire* and *martial fire*, 文武火) evokes traditional Chinese alchemical craft, where the balance between gentle, sustained heat and fierce, intense flame determines the final quality of a tool or pill.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in Han Li’s social engineering. Watch how he:
- **Tests** the seller’s credibility before revealing his full hand.
- **Reads** her micro-expressions—the change in tone, the sudden grip on the bottle—to assess the true value of his pills.
- **Hesitates** not from incompetence, but from a flicker of empathy triggered by a memory of his sister.
- **Recovers** almost instantly, proving that sentiment in the Mortal Stream is a luxury he can only afford in the smallest, most controlled doses.

Also, pay close attention to the brother: his brutish entrance and complete disregard for other cultivators’ irritation mirrors Han Li’s own Qu Hun, suggesting that in this world, raw physical advantage is a blunt instrument that often invites long-term grudges. Han Li’s caution is the smarter play.
