## Chapter Overview

Our favorite paranoid pill-refiner is almost home free—but the Mortal Stream never makes anything that easy. After an exhausting journey to the Heaven Star City region, Han Li touches down on the last transit island, Nanming Island, hoping to restock supplies and slip into the city unnoticed. Naturally, the universe has other plans. The Rebel Alliance picks this exact moment to launch a massive assault on the harbor. Han Li, ever the survivalist, takes one look at the thousand-cultivator dogfight unfolding before him and decides the smartest move is to vanish into thin air and watch from the sidelines. It's a textbook Mortal Stream chapter: minimal action from the protagonist, maximum tension, and a heavy reminder that the cultivation world's machinery grinds on with or without him.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li swaps his flashy blood cloak for ordinary flying light, deliberately keeping a low profile in the increasingly chaotic waters near Heaven Star City.
- He arrives at Nanming Island, the last transit island before the city, and senses an immediate escalation in security—three Core Formation cultivators scan him the moment he enters harbor range.
- He visits the market to stockpile supplies, checking the Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng formula from the Profound Bone Saint, but discovers he's missing two critical ingredients: Thousand Leaves Dew and an Agate Horn.
- A knowledgeable shopkeeper suggests that 'Thousand Leaves Dew' might simply be an ancient name for a modern herb that has since been renamed under a different term. Han Li files this away as a lead for later research.
- After a successful supply run (he loads up on enough materials to keep his alchemy going for a long time), Han Li heads for the harbor at dusk—only to witness a massive battle erupt as the Rebel Alliance launches a full-scale assault on the island with over a thousand cultivators.
- Han Li immediately hides himself, refusing to get involved in the clash between major powers. He estimates at least one Nascent Soul cultivator is directing the attack and decides the only smart move is to wait out the chaos.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Art of the Waiting Game.** One of the most underrated skills in *RMJI* is patience. Han Li has powerful trump cards, but he almost never uses them to force an outcome. Instead, he lets his enemies overextend, exhaust themselves, or make fatal mistakes. The Rebel Alliance attack on Nanming Island is a perfect example: the protagonist's entire contribution this chapter is to *hide and watch*. In Western fantasy, this might feel like a lack of agency. In the Mortal Stream, it's peak strategic intelligence. Knowing when *not* to act is just as important as knowing when to strike.

**Supply Chain Anxiety.** Han Li's obsessive stockpiling isn't just paranoia—it's a core survival mechanic in the xianxia world. If you run out of a common ingredient, your entire cultivation plan can grind to a halt for years. The novel treats resource logistics with the same gravity as combat. Han Li doesn't just want to win battles; he wants to ensure his alchemy pipeline never stops. This chapter's market scene is a quiet reminder that the most important fight a cultivator fights is often against scarcity, not monsters.

## Reading Guide

Fellow Daoist, this chapter is a masterclass in what makes *RMJI* different from your typical power fantasy. There's no dramatic one-man-stand against a thousand enemies. No hot-blooded declaration of war. Han Li sees a massive battle brewing, calculates the risk, and chooses the option that maximizes his survival odds: *disappear*. It's a quiet chapter, but it reinforces the soul of the novel. Read it as a tension-building interlude. The storm is coming, but Han Li isn't going to be the one dancing in the lightning. He's going to be the one hiding behind a rock, waiting for everyone else to tire themselves out.

Also, the shopkeeper's tip about 'Thousand Leaves Dew' being an ancient name for a modern herb is a fantastic piece of xianxia worldbuilding. Knowledge itself is a bottleneck. Just having a rare pill formula isn't enough—you also need to decipher the language it was written in. This is the kind of logistical friction that makes the world feel real.

**Parenthetical author's note:** This chapter concludes with a classic web novel sign-off—the author acknowledging a new month and asking readers to stick with the story. It's a small window into the serialization culture that birthed *RMJI*.
