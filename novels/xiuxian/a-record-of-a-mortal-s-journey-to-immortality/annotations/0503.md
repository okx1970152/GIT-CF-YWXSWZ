## Chapter Overview

It's negotiation time—Mortal Stream style. The Profound Bone Saint, reduced to a ghostly wisp clinging to a treasure arrow, throws everything he has at Han Li: secret arts, hidden caves, the complete Profound Yin Art, even the knowledge to break Extreme Yin's tracking mark. It's a desperate, high-stakes sales pitch, delivered as he's literally being dragged into the Crying Soul Beast's nose. But Han Li, with the cold calculation of a veteran, knows a thousand-year-old ghost makes for a terrible business partner. The real prize here isn't a new master—it's a clean escape. And when the dust settles, he finds something even better: a worn-out scroll with a red line leading to an ancient teleportation array, a map to a way out that was hidden right under everyone's noses.

## Key Plot Points

- The Crying Soul Beast effortlessly overpowers the ghost face's arrow treasure, dragging out the Profound Bone Saint's remnant soul.
- The ghost face offers a desperate, escalating bribe: becoming a ghost slave, revealing the full Profound Yin Art, giving up secret caves, and even offering to remove Han Li's tracking mark.
- Han Li appears to hesitate, "agreeing" to the deal, but it's a feint; the Crying Soul Beast inhales the ghost face completely.
- Han Li explains his choice: a thousand-year-old ghost is far too cunning to keep as a servant. Killing him is the only safe option.
- Using the Gold Devourer Beetles, Han Li locates and neutralizes the tracking mark on his calf.
- He escapes the barrier and, while retreating, examines the ancient scroll he found earlier.
- The scroll is a floor plan of the fifth level, with a red line marking a route to a hidden teleportation formation.
- Faced with the high risk of encountering old monsters on the way back, Han Li decides to follow the scroll's red-line route, deploying ape puppets as scouts.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**"Fellow Daoist" as a Desperate Bargaining Chip:** The ghost face calling Han Li "Fellow Daoist" while offering to become his "ghost slave" is a classic example of the transactional desperation of the Mortal Stream. The term "Fellow Daoist" is a formal, polite fiction of equality. The fact that he uses it while simultaneously offering total, life-long subservience shows just how low he has fallen and how far he is from any real "Daoist fellowship." It's the politeness of the cornered predator, not the peer.

**Magical Backlash (反噬, Fǎnshì):** This is a core xianxia mechanic that serves as the universe's ultimate check on power without wisdom. In *A Record of a Mortal's Journey*, the world punishes overreach. When a cultivator uses a technique or power they haven't truly mastered—like the Profound Bone Saint with the Asura Holy Flame—it can turn on them with fatal consequences. A true Mortal Stream victory is often less about the protagonist's overwhelming strength and more about surviving long enough for the opponent's own overconfidence and reckless use of power to cause their magical backlash and self-destruction.

### Magical Backlash

The common term for a forbidden or unstable technique turning on its caster, often with fatal results.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a textbook example of the Mortal Stream's "quiet victory" phase. The most dangerous part of the fight is over, but the most important decisions are just beginning. Pay close attention to Han Li's internal logic. The ghost face offers him *everything*—knowledge, power, and the solution to his most pressing problem (the tracking mark). A less cautious protagonist would have fallen for it. But Han Li's core principle shines through: "How could I dare scheme with a wolf?" He calculates the risk of deception from a being a thousand years his senior as absolute, and opts for the clean exit over the risky reward. This is a perfect demonstration that in this world, survival is often about knowing when *not* to take a deal. The chapter then pivots from a masterclass in refusal to a masterclass in problem-solving, as he uses his trump card (the Gold Devourer Beetles) to solve the tracking mark problem himself.

## The Tao of the Ghost Servant

In Chinese folklore, the idea of a powerful ghost or demon being bound into servitude is a recurring trope. It’s a feature in popular stories like *A Chinese Ghost Story* or the *Investiture of the Gods*, where powerful entities are forced to serve a mortal master. However, the Mortal Stream applies a cold, Darwinian logic to this fantasy: the power dynamic is never static. A "servant" who is a thousand-year-old ghost is always, always looking for a weak moment to reverse the relationship. Han Li's refusal isn't just caution; it's an understanding of this deep, systemic truth of his world. Binding a ghost is a short-term power boost that trades a guaranteed future betrayal for an uncertain immediate gain. His choice to kill and be done with it is the only decision a true survivor would make.

## The Weight of a Thousand Years

The Profound Bone Saint’s desperate sales pitch, listing every secret technique and treasure he knows, is a powerful narrative device. It’s not just filler; it’s a lore dump delivered under extreme duress. This is how the Mortal Stream world-builds. We learn the name of the full technique (Profound Yin Art) was never given to Extreme Yin, establishing that the master-disciple relationship in demonic sects is built on mutual distrust and strategic information-hoarding. Every sentence out of the ghost’s mouth is a potential plot hook for future treasures (secret caves) or solutions (the tracking mark). The reader, just like Han Li, has to parse this data under pressure, wondering which offer is genuine and which is a trap. It reinforces that in xianxia, knowledge is a loaded gun, and learning something new often comes with a price tag of being a target.
