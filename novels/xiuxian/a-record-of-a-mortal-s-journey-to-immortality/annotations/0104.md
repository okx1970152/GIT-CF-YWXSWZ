## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow explorers of the mortal gutter! This chapter is a full-on **information warfare** installment. There's no blazing swordplay or desperate escape. Instead, we get the coldest, most practical currency in the cultivation world: *intel*. Han Li, now a mortal man with a ticking clock of poison in his gut, is doing what he does best—sitting still, reading the board, and letting the pawns come to him. The chapter is a masterclass in how a cultivator's mind works when it's not throwing fireballs: treat everyone as a variable, every piece of information as a resource, and every plan as provisional until the map is filled in. Get ready for a deep dive into gang politics, dead men's family trees, and the slow, patient art of gaining leverage.

## Key Plot Points

This chapter is structured as a two-act intelligence dump. First, we get the full contents of Doctor Mo's letter. This is not a tearful farewell; it's a **corporate succession document**. It lists the Soaring Serpent Society's manpower (64,000 foot soldiers, 7,000 elite core members), its status as one of Lan Province's "three hegemons," and the complete psych profile of Mo's family. The letter is a toolkit, not a confession. It details his five wives—from the trustworthy and steady Fourth Wife Yan to the "ambitious" and "must be watched" Third Wife Liu—and his two apprentices, noting their ages and the specific ultimate techniques they were taught. The letter ends with his sworn brother Ma Kongtian, the Chief Protector. It's a complete data set for a hostile takeover or a cold reading.

The second act is the arrival of Sun Ergou, the local dock boss Han Li press-ganged with a Corroding Heart Pill last chapter. Sun Ergou's report paints a clear picture of Jiayuan City's underworld: over forty small gangs, eight medium-sized ones (two-three thousand men), and three major ones with tens of thousands. He also provides the background of the man who attacked Han Li (Black Bear of the Iron Fist Society) and admits his own gang, the Four Harmonies, is a "small" operation (under a thousand men). Han Li absorbs this data silently before ordering the report to continue. The chapter ends with the setup for a full geopolitical brief on the city's power structure.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a perfect example of a core trope in Chinese web novels: **the Deathbed Letter** or the **Posthumous Testament**. In Western fiction, a dying man's letter is usually an emotional apology or a confession of love. In the *Mortal Stream*, it's a **strategic asset**. Doctor Mo's letter is a brutal, clinical list of assets, liabilities, and threat assessments. He names his wives not by how much he loved them, but by their political connections (Third Wife's brother leads a sect), their administrative competence (Fourth Wife holds the power), and their hidden potential (Fifth Wife has a "secret power base"). This transforms the concept of a dying wish into a final, coldly logical data transfer from a master to his inheritor.

This chapter also firmly grounds the story in the **Wuxia and Xianxia underworld hierarchy**. Sun Ergou's explanation of the "thirty-three small gangs," "eight medium ones," and "three major ones" is a textbook description of the Jianghu's social structure. In this world, mortal power is not about armies or kings; it's about intricate, nested webs of influence and violence at the street level. For a cultivator like Han Li, entering this world is like an adult entering a sandbox: the power dynamic is so skewed that these mortal conflicts become a logistical puzzle to be solved rather than a genuine threat. Understanding the hierarchy is the first step to exploiting it.

### Jianghu (江湖)

The martial world; the underground society of gangs, sects, and wanderers that exists parallel to official law.

## Reading Guide

- **Note the "Pysch Profiles"**: Doctor Mo's descriptions of his wives are not sentimental. They are threat assessments. Pay close attention to who he labels as "trustworthy" (Fourth Wife, Fifth Wife) and who he flags as requiring caution (Third Wife). Han Li is reading this as a guide to who to approach and who to avoid.
- **Han Li's Internal Clock**: The entire chapter is framed by Han Li's internal medical examination. He has two months. This gives every subsequent action a stark, ticking deadline. The detective work is not idle curiosity; it's a desperate race against his own body.
- **The Sun Ergou Dynamic**: Sun Ergou is a perfect example of the "hierarchical tool." He is terrified, obedient, and pragmatic. He betrays his entire organization without a second thought because his survival is at stake. He is not a friend or an ally; he is a piece of software that Han Li has installed to mine city data.
- **De-Moralization of Betrayal**: Notice how Sun Ergou feels no shame in revealing his gang's secrets. In the Mortal Stream, under duress, loyalty is just a transaction that hasn't found its final price. This is not villainy; it's realism.
