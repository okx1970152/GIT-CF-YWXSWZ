## Chapter Overview

Chapter 5 marks a significant turning point in Han Li's early journey. After barely surviving the entrance test, he and Zhang Tie are designated as disciples on probation—a precarious status that could lead either to full inner discipleship or to being cast out as outer sect laborers. More importantly, they are singled out by Doctor Mo, a frail but deeply respected physician whose cough belies a hidden power. The chapter introduces the secluded Divine Hand Valley, a self-contained medicinal compound that will become Han Li's first real training ground in the Seven Mysteries Sect. The narrative also offers a glimpse into the sect's internal politics, showing how connections and nepotism (as with Wu Yan) can override merit, a theme that will resonate throughout the story.

## Key Plot Points

- The entrance results are announced: six children become inner disciples assigned to Hundred Forging Hall, while Wu Yan is fast-tracked to the elite Seven Peaks Hall due to his family connections with the vice sect master.
- Han Li and Zhang Tie are named disciples on probation, given a six-month trial before their final status is decided.
- The cold-faced apprentice Zhang Jun speaks bitterly about Wu Yan's backer, revealing the sect's internal resentment toward nepotism.
- Doctor Mo appears from the forest, selecting Han Li and Zhang Tie as his medicine-refining and herb-gathering assistants, overruling the other apprentices without challenge.
- The two boys are led to Divine Hand Valley, a secluded valley with medicinal fields and living quarters, effectively cut off from the rest of the sect.
- Doctor Mo orders them to rest and report to the main hall at night, hinting at future instructions and tests.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of "disciples on probation" (记名弟子) is a staple in Chinese martial and cultivation fiction. It represents a conditional entry into a sect—a probationary period during which the disciple must prove their worth. Failure means being relegated to the outer sect, a lower tier where resources and instruction are scarce. This tiered system mirrors the rigid social hierarchies common in ancient Chinese institutions, from imperial examinations to clan structures. Doctor Mo's selection of the two weakest candidates is not random; it suggests he has his own agenda, perhaps valuing obedience and a blank slate over already-formed habits. His coughing, frail appearance is a classic wuxia trope—the seemingly weak master who hides immense skill.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is deceptively quiet but lays crucial groundwork. Pay attention to Doctor Mo's effortless authority: even Zhang Jun, who openly criticizes the vice sect master, treats Doctor Mo with deep respect. That signals that Doctor Mo's status in the sect is unusual—he stands outside the normal power structure, yet commands deference. The Divine Hand Valley's isolation is also important: Han Li has been effectively removed from the main disciple hierarchy, for better or worse. This setup will test his ability to survive under an enigmatic master whose true intentions remain hidden. The night meeting foreshadows the first real test of Han Li's adaptability and caution.
