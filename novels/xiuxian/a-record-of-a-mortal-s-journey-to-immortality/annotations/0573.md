## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up! Our favorite paranoid pill-refiner just pulled off a textbook “fake surrender and ruthless counterattack” on the open sea. This chapter is a brutal, satisfying masterclass in why you *never* trust a mortals-stream cultivator who suddenly looks weak. Han Li, having been trapped and drained, finally flips the script with surgical precision. But it’s not just a slaughter—it’s a reunion. The mysterious woman from the black mist? It’s Yuan Yao, and she’s not just pretty; she’s sharp, calculating, and quick to switch from ‘potential victim’ to ‘social engineer’. The chapter ends with a tense, delightfully layered tea chat where old bargains and legendary treasures (yes, *that* cauldron) are laid bare on the table, all for the price of a tiny bottle of spirit liquid.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li reveals his true intentions: a bloody purge of the demonic cultivators who imprisoned him. He beheads the old man to shock the other two, then deploys his **24 flying swords** with the **Split-Image Sword Technique**, transforming them into 96 sword rays that instantly butcher the fleeing Skull Head and the ugly man. This is a cold, calculated display of overwhelming force.
- After the massacre, Han Li dismisses his disguise to reveal his true identity to Yuan Yao, who had already seen through his feint.
- Yuan Yao’s survival instincts are on full display: she curtsies, thanks him, and immediately tries to build rapport, knowing that the power differential makes running useless.
- The two return to Yuan Yao’s elegant cave on the green mountain. The setting is deliberately calm and refined, contrasting with the bloody violence just moments before.
- Over tea, Yuan Yao demonstrates her sharp intelligence by openly bringing up the Vast Heaven Cauldron and the Heaven-Mending Pill, effectively disarming any suspicion of hidden greed.
- Han Li admits the cauldron is in his possession but that he cannot open it yet—a “chicken rib”. He pivots the conversation to a direct trade request for the remaining Ten-Thousand-Year Spirit Liquid.
- To Han Li’s surprise, Yuan Yao does not bargain. She simply slides the full bottle across the table and declares it a gift, her eyes completely clear.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Chicken Rib (鸡肋 / Ji Lei)**: This classic Chinese idiom originates from a famous story in the *Records of the Three Kingdoms*. A strategist used it to describe a piece of territory that was “too tasteless to eat, but a pity to throw away.” In xianxia, it perfectly describes a legendary treasure you cannot use—it invites danger but offers no immediate power. Han Li’s Vast Heaven Cauldron is the ultimate chicken rib.
- **The Gift as a Social Weapon**: In the cutthroat economy of the Mortal Stream, a gift is never just a gift. When Yuan Yao hands over the spirit liquid for free, she is making a **strategic investment**. She attaches herself to a powerful later-stage cultivator, creating a debt of goodwill that is far more valuable than any spirit stone price she could have named. It’s a masterful move from a smart survivor.
- **Ten-Thousand-Year Spirit Liquid**: In xianxia alchemy, “spirit liquid” is a condensed form of natural energy. The “ten-thousand-year” prefix isn’t just flavor text; it implies an incredibly rare and potent substance that has taken millennia to form. It is often used to instantly replenish a cultivator’s mana, making it the ultimate “reset button” in a life-or-death fight, just as Han Li has experienced.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a fantastic showcase of post-Core Formation Han Li operating at peak efficiency. Watch how quickly he transitions from being a captive energy source to an executioner. The exchange with Yuan Yao is a masterclass in how smart characters interact in the *RMJI* world: no drama, no shouting, just subtle reads, open admissions, and calculated offers masked as kindness. Pay close attention to Yuan Yao’s phrasing and her choice to bring up the cauldron first. She is not a damsel in distress; she is a political animal. The ominous line at the end about “early to bed, early to rise” also serves as a classic web novel author’s tired apology, but in the story’s context, it perfectly underscores the mundane, human weariness that even cultivators must endure between bouts of incredible violence.
