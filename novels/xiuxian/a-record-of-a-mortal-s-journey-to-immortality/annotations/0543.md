## Chapter Overview
Get ready for a classic Mortal Stream transitional chapter that proves even a “simple” journey can deliver worldbuilding depth, character contrast, and a neat little secret-market hook. Han Li emerges from twenty years of seclusion with a bottleneck on his hands—and a brand new disguise art to play with. The Shape-Shifting Art makes its debut, giving our favorite paranoid cultivator a sleek tool for sneaking around without being recognized. But no rest for the wicked: almost immediately, he’s dragged into a rescue scene that showcases the uglier side of cultivator survival instincts. The rescued rogue is a crafty, self-serving old bird who’s perfectly willing to drag a stranger into danger without a second thought. And the chapter ends with a classic pivot: the original market is destroyed by beasts, so the old man dangles a mysterious “secret market” as bait. Cue the next arc.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li emerges from Core Formation mid-stage seclusion after twenty-plus years. He still has a small stockpile of unused pills.
- He hits a cultivation bottleneck near late-stage Core Formation; no amount of pills will push him further without a breakthrough.
- Deciding to search for the legendary Companion Demon Grass, he abandons his island cave dwelling and sets off for the market suggested by Gongsun Xing.
- Debut of the **Shape-Shifting Art** (from the Profound Yin Scripture): a disguise technique that alters height, muscle, and skin color at the cost of limiting mana use to 70%.
- On the second day of travel, Han Li spots a red streak fleeing from demonic beasts. The fleeing cultivator (Huang Mingli) heads straight for him, hoping to use him as a shield.
- Han Li lazily helps, using Azure Origin Sword Lights to trap and then slowly kill the beasts, masking his true cultivation level.
- Huang Mingli, a Half-Foundation stage rogue cultivator, thanks Han Li and reveals that the Nanli Island market was destroyed by fifth-rank beasts. He offers to lead Han Li to a secret market instead.
- Han Li’s internal observation: human nature is inconsistent—contrasting Gongsun Xing’s filial piety with this man’s selfishness.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Secret Markets (秘市):** In the cultivation world, a “market” is never just a place to shop. Regular markets are often public, fixed-location trading grounds under sect protection—but they get discovered and destroyed by demonic beasts all the time. A secret market, by contrast, is invitation-only, moves frequently, and deals in higher-end goods only accessible to Foundation Establishment cultivators and above. Think of it as the underground speakeasy of the immortal world: same danger, better inventory. Also, the requirement of personal introduction (熟人引荐) is a classic xianxia social gate—trust is scarce, so you need a guarantor to even get in.

**Cultivation Bottlenecks (瓶颈):** In xianxia, a bottleneck is not just a “difficult level-up.” It’s a narrative checkpoint where the author forces the protagonist to leave seclusion and engage with the world. Pills alone stop working; only personal insight, adventure, or life-or-death combat can break the barrier. This is a core mechanism of the Mortal Stream: progression is never a straight line of consuming resources—it must be *earned* through risk and experience.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a textbook **setup piece**—and in the Mortal Stream, setups can be just as satisfying as showdowns. Pay close attention to the Shape-Shifting Art; it’s going to be Han Li’s go-to for incognito operations for a long while. The 70% mana cap is a clever trade-off that forces him to fight smarter rather than harder when he’s undercover. Also, take note of how Han Li handles the rescue: he’s not heroic, he’s *tolerating* the hassle. He doesn’t jump in, doesn’t show off, and even lets the old man collect the beast corpses. That’s classic Han Li—he knows the real prize is the secret market lead, not the petty loot. Also, the contrast between Gongsun Xing and Huang Mingli is the novel’s quiet reminder that the cultivation world runs on a spectrum from genuine kindness to ruthless self-interest. Han Li files that observation away without getting sentimental. Because sentiment is a luxury he can’t afford.
