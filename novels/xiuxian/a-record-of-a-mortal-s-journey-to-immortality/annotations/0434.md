## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a masterclass in Mortal Stream tension, but not through a straight-up fight. The core dynamic is a **standoff between two calculators**. Qu Hun’s body has been seized by a mysterious green shadow—an ancient spirit who immediately recognizes the External Incarnation technique and claims to be the original master of the demonic path. Han Li, bleeding from a near-fatal sneak attack, has a single second to decide: does he flee or test the old monster’s current strength? The answer, of course, is *test*. The chapter is a breathtaking display of cold tactical assessment layered over psychological warfare, culminating in a sword strike so powerful it forces the enemy to reconsider his assumed superiority. This isn’t a slugfest; it’s a duel of engineering and information asymmetry.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li is wounded by a Blood Essence Drill from the possessed Qu Hun, confirming the green shadow’s control is absolute and his technique is lethally refined.
- The green shadow explicitly identifies the External Incarnation method as something he taught to “those two unfilial disciples” (Extreme Yin and Extreme Xuan), establishing him as their former master or creator.
- The enemy performs the Mystical Soul Bone Condensation Art, using a pile of bones to physically reshape Qu Hun’s body into a smaller, more agile form—a clear indicator of his centuries of experience.
- Han Li reveals his full arsenal: Divine Thunder Bamboo flying swords, thousands of Gold Devourer Beetles, and over thirty giant ape puppets. The visual alone is a statement of overwhelming material preparation.
- The enemy’s reaction is the chapter’s turning point: upon seeing the power of the golden lightning on the flying sword, he drops his aggressive posture and proposes negotiation, acknowledging Han Li might not be a disciple of his old foes.
- Han Li’s final move is a direct test—he launches the flying sword not to land a decisive blow, but to gauge the enemy’s true combat power after the body-shape transformation.
- The enemy’s first defense (five crescent black blades) is effortlessly annihilated by the golden lightning, confirming the sword’s superiority and setting up a deeper confrontation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The **External Incarnation (身外化身)** technique is one of the most prestigious secret arts in the *xianxia* pantheon. In orthodox Daoist internal alchemy, “incarnation” refers to the ability of a highly advanced practitioner to project their consciousness or even generate a separate, autonomous body. *RMJI* weaponizes this concept into a core tactical asset: a cultivator can refine a puppet or a second body to act as a proxy, scout, or combat unit. The danger, as this chapter vividly demonstrates, is that any creature capable of soul possession can hijack that connection. The green shadow’s claim to have *invented* the technique used by Extreme Yin and Extreme Xuan is a terrifying power play—it implies that every principle Han Li relies on is already fully understood by his enemy.

The **Mystical Soul Refining Great Skill (玄魂炼妖大法)** is a forbidden art that subverts the normal rules of soul seizure (夺舍). In standard cultivation lore, a cultivator can only possess a body once per lifetime, and only if the target has a lower cultivation level. This skill claims to bypass that limit entirely, allowing the user to freely attach their “Mystical Soul” form to any host. It’s a narrative tool that raises the stakes: possession is no longer a one-time gamble but a repeatable, strategic weapon for this ancient enemy.

## Reading Guide
This is the kind of chapter that reminds you why *RMJI* isn’t just a power-leveling story. The fight hasn’t even properly started yet, and already we have a full-blown intelligence war. The green shadow reads Han Li’s External Incarnation correctly in seconds; Han Li immediately deduces the enemy’s non-human nature from the *method of possession* alone. Every action is layered with subtext. The corpse beside the ancient pool, the casual mention of a master-student betrayal, the effortless reshaping of a body with a pile of old bones—none of this is decoration. It’s all data. And Han Li? He processes it, files it, and then fires off a Divine Thunder Bamboo sword just to see what happens. That’s the Mortal Stream spirit: never assume you’re stronger; always assume you haven’t tested enough. If you love chapters where every line is a chess move, this one rewards rereading.
