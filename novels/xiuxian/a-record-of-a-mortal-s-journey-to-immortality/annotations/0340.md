## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers a gut-wrenching reunion that forces Han Li to confront the limits of his “never lift a finger without benefit” principle. He comes to the Five Colors Sect ready to settle the Mo Estate’s blood debt, only to find his old ally Mo Yuzhu now married into the very family he’s hunting. The calculated vengeance he planned collapses when she shields her husband—the son of his target—and demands that he kill her and her child, too. For a rare moment, Han Li’s machinery of cold logic grinds to a halt. He doesn’t feel moral outrage; he feels annoyed that the situation has become complex and “profitless.” What follows is a masterclass in his calibrated pragmatism: he renegotiates the terms of revenge on the spot, extracting a promise of good treatment for Mo Yuzhu before laying down the sword. The chapter closes with a small, telling act of grace—a jade pendant for her daughter—that feels more like closing a ledger than a declaration of kindness.

## Key Plot Points

- Mo Yuzhu unexpectedly blocks Han Li’s attack, revealing she is now the wife of the Five Colors Sect Leader’s son, placing her directly in the path of his revenge.
- The Five Colors Sect Leader and his son first try to intimidate Han Li but freeze in terror the moment he manifests fireballs, confirming they are mortals facing a cultivator.
- Mo Yuzhu gives a layered defense: the Five Colors Sect only relayed orders from a higher power (Spirit Beast Mountain), and the true mastermind is beyond Han Li’s reach.
- Han Li internally concedes the logic—he was never going to take on a Spirit Beast Mountain cultivator over a grudge that isn’t even his own—but masks the retreat as a concession to her plea.
- He transmits the location of Mo Feng Wu and Mo Cai Huan to Mo Yuzhu, formally stepping out of the revenge equation.
- The little girl “Ying Ning” is introduced in a lullaby-like domestic scene that disarms Han Li’s killing intent momentarily, marking a rare emotional override.
- Han Li gifts a Spirit-Connecting Jade pendant to the child as a “memento,” an act that reads simultaneously as a farewell gesture and a small investment in a possible future karmic link.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of “结个善缘” (jie ge shan yuan), or “forming a good karmic connection,” surfaces here when Mo Yuzhu asks Han Li to hold her daughter. In Chinese religious cosmology, this is a practical spiritual investment: by making contact with a powerful figure, the child inherits a trace of that person’s “immortal fortune” (仙气). It’s not superstition in this world—in xianxia, causality can be a measurable force, and a small gesture by a cultivator can indeed influence a mortal’s fate down the line. This is why Han Li doesn’t refuse; he understands the transaction. The Spirit-Connecting Jade he leaves is both a parting gift and a material anchor for that connection. For Mo Yuzhu, this is the highest form of insurance she can secure for her daughter in a world where cultivators decide life and death with a single spell.

## Reading Guide

This is a quiet, emotionally complex chapter that tests one of the series’ core rules: Han Li’s “no profit, no effort” principle. When Mo Yuzhu blocks him, his first reaction is not pity but irritation—the revenge has become “profitless” (出力不讨好). That’s pure mortal stream logic: he didn’t come for justice, he came for an objective. When the objective becomes messy, he recalculates. The lullaby scene with Ying Ning is the only crack in his armor, but even that crack is measured; he doesn’t stay softened, he just buys time to think. For longtime readers, this is a crucial balance point: Han Li is not a robot, and a domestic scene can slow his killing intent, but he never lets that vulnerability linger. The chapter ends with a transaction—a jade pendant for a future possibility—not an emotional reconciliation. If you feel a pang of sympathy for Mo Yuzhu, you’re reading correctly; the world is designed to make that pang feel hollow.
