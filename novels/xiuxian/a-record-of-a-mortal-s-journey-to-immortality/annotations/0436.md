## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists, because the chessboard of the Chaotic Star Sea just got a whole lot bigger! This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s “crisis equals opportunity” economy. Han Li escapes from the underground ruins, but he doesn’t leave empty-handed—he comes out with a brand new, shiny mystery in the form of a golden sword map that has the Profound Bone Saint behaving like a giddy child. The old ghost runs off with a clue of his own, while Han Li, ever the pragmatist, picks through the wreckage and adds a new objective to his list: follow the map and see what’s at the end of the red line. It’s not an adventure; it’s a meticulously calculated resource-gathering expedition with a ticking clock. The chapter closes with a classic Mortal Stream hook: a cry for help from a familiar face, reminding us that in this world, debts, favors, and sect politics are just another form of currency.

## Key Plot Points

*   The Profound Bone Saint’s hidden magical beast (a cricket-like insect) chirps at the sound from a sealed bone, revealing a white silk handkerchief—a fragment map identical to Han Li’s own.
*   The Saint immediately severs the conversation, claiming a prior engagement, and blasts out of the underground temple, leaving Han Li to process the new intel.
*   Han Li loots the fallen cultivators’ storage pouches and incinerates their bodies in a matter-of-fact, business-as-usual manner, before checking his own fragment map.
*   Han Li discovers his map has transformed into a golden compass: a small sword that always points northwest, with a red line marking a destination. He deduces the map has a time limit and that a race is on to claim the prize.
*   A furious middle-aged cultivator (the Extreme Yin Island Master) arrives at the ruins, finds the seal broken, and swears to track down the “old fiend” after finishing his business at the Heavenvoid Hall.
*   After days of travel, Han Li stumbles upon a fight between a male-female duo from the Sublime Sound Sect and three Venom Dragon Society cultivators, triggering a recognition scene and a plea for help.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Race for the Map” Trope**
In Chinese web novels, a treasure map is rarely a leisurely invitation; it’s a countdown timer. The moment a piece of the puzzle is revealed, a race begins between factions—each holding a different fragment. The urgency is not just about the treasure itself, but about *access*: whoever arrives first gets the pick of the loot, and in a Dark Forest world, being second means getting an ambush instead of a grimoire. The Profound Bone Saint’s frantic exit isn’t bad manners; it’s a survival calculation.

**Fragments and Communication**
The use of a bone-sealed insect that chirps only upon detecting a matching fragment is a brilliant bit of xianxia technology. It bypasses the need for spiritual sense clues or cryptic riddles. It’s a biological key, an organic homing beacon that is nearly impossible to jam or forge. This reinforces the classic trope that ancient cultivators left behind not just traps, but also ingenious solutions for those who know where to look.

### Primordial Ancient Treasure (洪荒古宝)

A magic weapon from an ancient era, distinct from modern treasures; they cannot be absorbed into the body, but their singular power is far greater than modern equivalents.

### Expedition (探险)

In the context of *RMJI*, expeditions to ancient ruins are high-risk resource-gathering missions, not heroic quests.

## Reading Guide

Here’s a perfect example of the Mortal Stream operating procedure. Han Li doesn’t waste a single moment feeling sorry for himself over Qu Hun. He processes the loss, notes the power gap, and then immediately pivots to the new variable: the fragment map. His internal debate (“Do I follow the map? Is it a trap? Do I have time?”) is a pure logistical assessment, not a heroic dilemma. This is how you get things done in a world where hesitation is death.

Also, pay close attention to the Sublime Sound Sect cameo at the end. “Elder Han” is a title that carries weight. The fact that a disciple recognizes him and *expects* him to help is a fascinating glimpse into how Han Li’s reputation is growing, whether he likes it or not. Every favor is a debt in the making, and in the Mortal Stream, debts are just another kind of leash.
