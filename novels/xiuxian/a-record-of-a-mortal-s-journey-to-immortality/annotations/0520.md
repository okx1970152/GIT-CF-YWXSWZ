## Chapter Overview

Welcome to Black Stone City, fellow Daoists—a place where the usual rules of the cultivation world are bent, broken, and then reassembled into something far more pragmatic. In this chapter, our cautious protagonist Han Li steps off the teleportation array and lands in a bizarre sanctuary that operates on its own brutal logic. Scar-faced Xu Yun drops the bombshell that everything he and the other stranded cultivators thought they knew about Qiyuan Island is a lie—fabricated by a mysterious force even the Star Palace avoids antagonizing. But it's not all bad news. As it turns out, this is less a prison and more a lawless paradise… if you have the spirit stones and the guts to enjoy it. Buckle up; the Outer Star Seas just got a whole lot more interesting.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the stranded cultivators stepping out of the teleportation hut, only to find themselves not on a desolate beast island, but inside a fully functioning city built of black stone, protected by a formidable white light barrier. Han Li immediately notes the strange mix of mortals and cultivators who live and work side-by-side, with a surprisingly high number of beautiful women in colorful attire. Scar-faced Xu Yun, having seemingly accepted his fate, volunteers to give the group a tour. As they walk, Xu Yun reveals the first major twist: all prior intelligence on Qiyuan Island has been deliberately falsified by its ruling power—an organization even the Star Palace treats with kid gloves. To hammer it home, he warns them that the city is under the direct supervision of a Nascent Soul elder, and breaking the peace carries a non-negotiable sentence of total bodily and spiritual annihilation. The group then enters the city’s largest building—an establishment fronted by a magical screen painting, staffed by an alluring female cultivator of middling looks but overwhelming charm—raising the obvious question: exactly what kind of “goods and services” are on offer here?

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces a key narrative cheat code for the Mortal Stream: the controlled information zone. In many Chinese web novels, island cities like Black Stone are hubs where the normal rules of sect politics are temporarily suspended. The "fabricated intel" trope is a classic power move: a strong local faction sacrifices outsiders' freedom of information to stabilize its own resource monopoly. Cultivators who know the truth sell it for a premium—or, as Xu Yun demonstrates, use it as currency to form temporary alliances. The mention that even the Star Palace avoids this island’s rulers is a classic xianxia writing technique known as "worldbuilding via absent force." We don't see the city's true masters, but learning that an existing top-tier power (Star Palace) fears them creates immediate narrative weight. It also raises the stakes for Han Li: if even Star Palace gives this place a wide berth, breaking the local rules means facing an enemy that scares the scariest faction we've met so far.

### Stand-in cauldron (双修炉鼎)

A euphemism for a human being used as a cultivation resource. In the Mortal Stream, this term strips away all romance—it means a living person whose vitality or spiritual energy is drained by another cultivator for power, often against their will.

## Reading Guide

Slow down and take a good look, because Black Stone City is one of those chapter-length worldbuilding scenes that plant seeds for the entire upcoming arc. Notice how Han Li does exactly three things here: he observes, he stays silent, and he follows. He doesn't probe for information, doesn't volunteer an opinion, and barely reacts to the bombshells Xu Yun drops. That’s classic Han Li survival 101: when every variable is unknown, the safest move is to remain a blank slate. The chapter also serves as a subtle trap demonstration: Xu Yun, though friendly and helpful now, is clearly reeling from his own misfortune. He’s presenting the "paradise" angle to soften the group up. The bigger question is why he was stuck here in the first place—and what kind of "cooperation" he actually needs. Meanwhile, that female cultivator at the establishment entrance? She’s a walking red flag. A building with this much traffic, guarded by a magnetic hostess and a magical artifact painting, is almost certainly not a tea house. The Mortal Stream world is about to give Han Li a very different kind of test.

## Beyond the Four Walls: The Economy of the Forbidden City

Black Stone City operates on a brutal but elegant economic paradox: total safety purchased through total surveillance. The prohibition on dueling, backed by the ever-present threat of a Nascent Soul elder, creates a rare commodity in the Outer Star Seas—trust. But trust here is not born from morality; it's a side effect of overwhelming force. The city openly advertises spirit beasts, rare materials, and top-quality cultivation cauldrons. In xianxia economics, "cauldron" (*lu ding*) is a code word for humans used as cultivation resources. This place isn't just a trading post; it's a meat market for the soul, wrapped in rules so strict that the only thing anyone *can* do is trade. It's the purest expression yet of the Mortal Stream's core truth: stability isn't law; it's the biggest bully's business plan.
