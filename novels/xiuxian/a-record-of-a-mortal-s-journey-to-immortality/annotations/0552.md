## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow cultivators, to the frontier grind! This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream negotiation, served with a side of scalding-hot spirit wine. Our favorite paranoid alchemist, Han Li, has stumbled (or rather, strategically flown) into the territory of a Riftwind Beast named Feng Xi, a demonic cultivator who is terrifyingly powerful, surprisingly chatty, and has a very specific reason for not crushing Han Li like a bug. The entire chapter is a tense, high-stakes conversation where every word is a probe, every gesture is a threat, and the central question is simple: will Han Li be drinking a miracle cure, or a poisoned chalice? If you’ve ever wondered how a mid-Core Formation mortal survives a conversation with a being that can kill him with a snap of his fingers, this is your textbook example. Get ready for a bottle of booze that could change Han Li’s fate, or end it.

## Key Plot Points
*   **A Strange Hospitality:** Han Li is led to a meeting with Feng Xi, a humanoid Riftwind Beast. Instead of an immediate attack, the demonic cultivator offers him a seat and asks his name. This immediately puts Han Li on high alert, as any deviation from the expected predator-prey dynamic is a massive red flag.
*   **The Reason for Mercy:** Feng Xi reveals he spared Han Li for two reasons. First, Han Li’s Qi Concealment Art reminds him of an old, deceased friend. Second, and more importantly, Han Li cultivates an exceptionally pure Wood-attributed technique, which is exactly what Feng Xi needs for a secret plan.
*   **The Emerald Flame Wine:** Feng Xi produces a bottle of Emerald Flame Wine, a century-brewed spirit wine made from the demon core of a Transformation-level beast. He claims it has a high chance of breaking Han Li’s cultivation bottleneck, but he admits he needs Han Li to be at a higher level for his task to be useful.
*   **A Transparent Ultimatum:** Feng Xi is brutally honest about the stakes. If Han Li drinks the wine and breaks through, he becomes a useful tool. If he doesn’t, he is worthless, and his life will be taken to compensate for the wasted wine. This is peak Mortal Stream honesty: a favor is a leash, and a gift is a transaction.
*   **A Calculated Gamble:** After a tense standoff, where Feng Xi’s aura becomes chilling, he finally gives a full explanation. Faced with a non-negotiable choice, Han Li drinks the wine. He is then led to a sealed coral chamber to spend months refining the wine’s power with no clear exit strategy.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces the **Demon Core (煞丹)** as a brewing ingredient, highlighting the brutal resource loop of the xianxia world. A “Transformation-level demon core” isn’t just a rare material; it represents a slain being of immense power. The process of refining such a core into a spirit wine is a form of alchemical consumption, where the life essence of one being is transformed into a tool for another’s advancement. This starkly illustrates the “Dark Forest” logic: every powerful item in a cultivator’s pocket is a trophy from a life-or-death struggle, often against a being just as sentient and ambitious as themselves. The fact that Feng Xi’s clan holds a monopoly on this brewing method is a classic “magic formula” trope, where unique racial or clan talents create artificial scarcity and immense political power in the cultivation world.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a perfect example of how to build tension without a single punch being thrown. Pay close attention to Han Li’s internal calculation. His suspicion of the wine isn’t just paranoia; it’s a logical survival instinct honed over hundreds of chapters. The brilliance of Feng Xi’s character is that he is not a cackling villain but a pragmatic predator who uses honesty as a weapon. By laying out the brutal calculus of the deal so plainly, he removes all ambiguity and forces Han Li’s hand. The ultimate lesson here is the cost of weakness in this world. Han Li is not being targeted because he has a treasure, but because his *potential* is not yet high enough to be worth the risk of killing. His survival depends on becoming more valuable alive than dead. This is the core economic principle of the Mortal Stream, where your life is a stock that must constantly appreciate in value to avoid being liquidated.
