## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, strap in. This is the chapter where the mask comes off for good. Doctor Mo—now fully restored to his prime, handsome and charismatic—finally reveals the true purpose of his decade-long investment in Han Li. And it’s not a pleasant one. After a brief, chilling exchange, he produces an object that completely changes the game: a talisman that can rob a living man of all control over his own body. Han Li, the pragmatic survivor who has outmaneuvered everyone so far, finds himself utterly paralyzed, his consciousness a prisoner in his own flesh, while the old monster prepares to take everything from him. This is possession, Mortal Stream style, and it’s every bit as horrifying as it sounds. Get ready for a masterclass in powerlessness.

## Key Plot Points

*   Doctor Mo, now in his rejuvenated, powerful body, drops all pretense. He directly addresses Han Li by name for the first time, signaling that the game is over.
*   He produces a silk-wrapped yellow talisman covered in silver symbols. The symbols themselves have a tangible effect on Han Li’s cultivation technique, making it pulse uncontrollably—a strong hint that this is no ordinary martial world trick.
*   Despite Han Li’s desperate struggle, the Iron Servant’s overwhelming strength renders him completely immobile. The Mortal Stream principle is brutally reinforced: clever plans don’t matter if you can’t execute them.
*   Doctor Mo chants an incantation, and the silver symbols on the paper begin to glow one by one. The moment the last symbol lights up, he slaps the talisman onto Han Li’s forehead.
*   The effect is immediate and terrifying. Han Li is completely stripped of all bodily control—he can’t blink, twitch, or move a single muscle. His consciousness is intact and aware, but he is a helpless, passive observer in his own body, awaiting an unknown fate.
*   The chapter ends with Doctor Mo’s cold, almost apologetic words: “May you reincarnate soon. I am taking this body.” The nature of the threat is no longer ambiguous.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **Spirit-Fixing Talisman** (定神符) introduced here is a classic weapon in the arsenal of soul-based combat. Its name literally translates to ‘Fix Spirit Talisman,’ which perfectly describes its function: it pins the victim’s spirit (or consciousness) in place while simultaneously locking down the physical body. In Chinese folk and Daoist traditions, talismans are often drawn on yellow paper (黄纸) with cinnabar or, in this case, silver ink. The yellow paper represents the earth or the material plane, while the ink channels the spiritual power of the practitioner. The silver gleam here suggests a higher grade or more sinister origin than mundane talismans.

The ‘incantation’ (咒语) Doctor Mo chants is not just random noise. In cultivation lore, spoken words have power—they are the sonic bridge between the cultivator’s will and the laws of the world. The final exclamation of ‘Fix!’ (定) is a command word, essentially a one-word veto over the victim’s autonomy. This scene also reinforces a key horror of the cultivation world: your own body is not a sanctuary, but merely the most important and vulnerable asset you own, and one that can be legally seized by a higher power. The victim’s preserved awareness is the cruelest part; they are forced to witness their own violation.

### Incantation (咒语)

A ritual spoken formula that channels a cultivator's power into the physical world. The words and sounds are not arbitrary; they are keys that unlock spiritual effects, often culminating in a single, powerful command word like ‘Fix!’ (定).

### Possession (夺舍)

A forbidden technique where a more powerful soul forcibly overwrites and expels the original consciousness of a living body. It is the ultimate violation of personal sovereignty in the cultivation world, driven by desperation for a new lifespan or body.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a turning point where all of Han Li’s earlier survival advantages—his caution, intelligence, and layered deceptions—are rendered completely useless. The power imbalance is no longer about skill or wit; it’s about raw, metaphysical force. Pay close attention to the description of the talisman and the chanting. This is the first real-time demonstration of a formal magical ritual in the novel, and it’s not a flashy explosion of light—it’s a quiet, terrifying transfer of control.

The chapter also reveals the final form of Doctor Mo’s character. He is no longer a manipulative old man or a scheming rival. He is a predator who has finally caught his prey, and the moment is almost intimate and regretful. This sets up the ultimate stakes for Han Li: he is about to lose everything, including his very identity. The tension isn’t in the fight—there is no fight. The tension is in the helpless waiting, the countdown to the moment when Han Li’s consciousness is erased. Enjoy the dread; it’s the Mortal Stream at its most visceral.
