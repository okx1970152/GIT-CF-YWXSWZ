## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the sea, fellow Daoists! Chapter 369 marks a monumental geographic shift in *A Record of a Mortal's Journey*. After surviving the teleportation chaos of the Blood Forbidden Land and a grueling repair marathon, Han Li and Qu Hun are hurled into the great unknown. He awakens groggy from a long-distance teleport, destroys the ancient array behind him, and crawls out of a dusty cave to find himself… on a tiny island in the middle of a vast, endless ocean. It’s the classic cultivator’s worst nightmare: lost, isolated, and sitting on a spiritual qi desert. The chapter is a masterclass in *Mortal Stream* problem-solving: no panic, no dramatic mourning over his lost geography—just a cold, immediate triage of his situation, a pragmatic nap, and a calculated directional gamble that leads him to a mysterious ship. Get ready for the open sea, because the world just got a whole lot bigger.

## Key Plot Points

- **Teleportation Aftermath:** The chapter opens with Han Li waking from the teleportation, suffering the disorienting effects of a long-distance jump. His first critical action is to order Qu Hun to destroy the teleportation array behind them, a classic Han Li move to sever any potential trail.
- **Exploration of a Strange Cave:** He finds himself in an abandoned, dusty stone room on a cliffside. After a brief rest, he pushes open an easy-to-open stone door and climbs a long staircase, leading to an exit blocked by a boulder.
- **The Revelation of the Sea:** Qu Hun effortlessly cleaves the boulder, revealing a dazzling, unfamiliar landscape. Han Li steps out onto a cliff and is genuinely stunned to find himself looking at a vast, azure ocean, a sight he has only read about in books.
- **Assessment and Acceptance:** Realizing he is on a small, isolated island with thin spiritual energy, Han Li does not despair. He methodically circles the island on the Divine Wind Boat, finds nothing but rocks and snakes, and then makes the pragmatic decision to get a full day’s sleep before tackling the problem.
- **The Voyage Begins:** After his rest, Han Li picks the direction of the setting sun as his heading and flies for half a day. By pure luck, he spots a massive, bizarre ship pulled by enormous, non-magical fish. This encounter provides his first potential lead for resettlement.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **The Great Teleportation Token (大挪移令):** This isn’t just a fancy travel pass. In RMJI, long-distance teleportation is a horrifying ordeal for low-level cultivators. The GTT is a critical, high-value artifact that protects the user from the crushing spatial pressure. Han Li’s severe reaction (dizziness, near-vomiting) highlights this danger and reinforces that even with the token, his low cultivation makes him vulnerable to the journey’s physical toll. The token also consumes his spiritual power, making the post-teleport vulnerability a legitimate threat.
- **The Sea as a New World:** For a cultivator from the Heavenly South, a continent-focused region, the ocean is a terrifying blank slate. It’s a space without established power structures, sects, or safe havens. In the Mortal Stream, this is a high-risk, high-reward zone. The sea represents freedom from persecution but also a terrifying isolation where supplies, spiritual qi, and information are scarce. Han Li’s methodical approach to finding a path is a direct contrast to a hero who would charge off with blind determination.
- **Mortal Ships and Towing Fish:** The chapter introduces a wonderful piece of world-building: a large, mastless ship pulled by trained, non-magical giant fish. This grounds the cultivation world’s technology. Even without cultivators, this society has developed advanced maritime solutions. It subtly hints that Han Li has arrived in a region with a sophisticated mortal culture, which is a much safer first contact for a cautious survivor than a cultivator's outpost.

### Great Teleportation Token

### Divine Wind Boat

A speed-focused flying artifact refined from Ink Flood Dragon fin and tail; extremely fast but lacks defensive capabilities and passenger capacity.

### Moonlight Stone

A common illumination stone used by cultivators in dark places; it emits a soft, steady light.

### Mortal Stream

A subgenre of xianxia defined by a realistic, cruel, and resource-scarce cultivation world where the protagonist survives through caution, calculation, and pragmatism rather than raw talent or luck.

### Li (里)

### Towing Fish

Extremely large, non-magical fish with sharp teeth, used by this region's mortals to pull ships instead of sails or oars, indicating a unique maritime culture.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the quiet before the storm, and it’s brilliant. Don’t expect a fight or a treasure hunt. The true action here is *logistics*. Watch how Han Li’s brain works: survive the teleport, destroy the evidence, check for danger, rest, assess resources, and make a calculated, low-information gamble to find land. The author is deliberately slowing down the pace after the intense Blood Forbidden Land arc. This is a “breather” chapter that feels anything but relaxing. The sense of scale is immense. The solitary island, the crushing emptiness of the ocean, the lack of qi—this is the Mortal Stream working on a macro level. The final shot of the giant ship is a perfect cliffhanger, promising a brand-new civilization to navigate. Get ready for a fresh cast and a whole new set of problems, because Han Li is officially off the map.
