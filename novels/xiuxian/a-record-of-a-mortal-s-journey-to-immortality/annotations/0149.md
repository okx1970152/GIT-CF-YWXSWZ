## Chapter Overview

Fresh from Uncle Wang’s orientation tour, Han Li wastes no time settling into Yellow Maple Valley—but he’s not about to lounge around a stone hut waiting for his cultivation to climb. He grabs the green leaf flying tool for the first time, wobbles through the skies like a fledgling bird, then promptly heads to the Hundred Mechanisms Hall to snag a work assignment. But here’s the twist: he doesn’t just take any task. He wants a specific niche—plant cultivation duty—the perfect cover for a certain miraculous vial in his possession. And when the steward tries to enforce the “no picking and choosing” rule, surprise backup arrives in the form of Elder Ye himself, whose inexplicable generosity lets Han Li cherry-pick his ideal gig. It’s a short, tight chapter that quietly redefines what “strategic career planning” looks like in the cutthroat world of immortal cultivation.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li moves into his temporary stone hut, learning that reaching the tenth layer of the basic technique earns a disciple a move to the much freer Xuan Kun Mountain with unrestricted housing.
- He test-files the green leaf flying tool; after some clumsy wobbling, he gets the hang of it and enjoys a rare moment of carefree aerial play.
- Passing disciples size him up, sneer at his ninth-layer cultivation, and fly away—confirming that sect snobbery is alive and well even among cultivators.
- Han Li heads to Hundred Mechanisms Hall. The steward, Steward Yu, initially refuses to let him pick his own task, citing standard allocation policy.
- Elder Ye appears conveniently behind Han Li, overriding the policy in a single sentence: “Let Nephew Han pick whichever cultivation-type work he pleases.”
- The steward, stunned by the favoritism, fetches the full list of planting duties.
- Han Li scans the tasks, bypassing every entry until he finds one that perfectly matches his unspoken needs—a position with maximum access to herbs and minimum oversight.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Miscellaneous Duties as a Sect Survival Tactic**: In orthodox sect culture, new disciples are assigned labor tasks (often menial or dangerous) without a say. But for a protagonist with a secret growth accelerator like the Heaven Vial, “volunteering” for the right duty is a quiet power move: it provides legal access to rare herbs, privacy for accelerated cultivation, and a mundane cover story that deflects suspicion. Han Li’s choice here is less about earning contribution points and more about building a low-risk operational base.

**The Green Leaf Flying Tool**: These mass-produced basic flying tools are the immortal sect’s equivalent of a training bicycle. They’re slow, easy to break, and every disciple gets one—which means they also serve as a crude social marker. A ninth-layer cultivator on a standard leaf is nobody special; one glance tells any passerby exactly where you stand in the pecking order. The hierarchy is literally visible at a glance.

**Elder Ye’s Overriding Privilege**: Yellow Maple Valley, like most cultivation sects, is not a democracy. Elders and hall masters hold near-absolute authority within their domains of oversight. When Elder Ye says “let him pick,” policy dissolves. This is a classic Mortal Stream power dynamic: the rules apply to the weak; the strong rewrite them at will. Han Li’s ability to cultivate a patron relationship early is itself a survival skill.

### Foundation Establishment Cultivators

The highest tier of a sect’s active disciples; true cultivators exempt from ordinary duties, with private caves and yearly resource stipends, contrasted with the exploited lower classes.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in setup without fireworks. Han Li does not fight, scheme, or ambush anyone. Instead, he plays a quiet social game: he tests the system (by asking for a duty pick), accepts the blockage (the steward’s refusal), and lets his carefully cultivated relationship with Elder Ye do the heavy lifting. The flying scene is the only moment of unfiltered joy—a rare beat in a world of constant calculation.

Notice how Han Li’s reaction to the sneering disciples is internal cold laughter, not wounded pride. He doesn’t need their respect; he needs their invisibility. The lower they think he is, the less they’ll watch him.

And that final scene—thumbing through jade slips until he finds the right task—is the quietest, most satisfying kind of suspense. We don’t know exactly which duty he’s chosen yet, but we know it’s the one that will let him sprint toward Foundation Establishment while the rest of the sect sees only a diligent herb-tender. Fellow Daoist Han is already playing three moves ahead.

## Deep Dive: The Social Geometry of Sect Politics

This chapter also quietly lays out a critical rule of Mortal Stream social navigation: **institutional favors are more valuable than institutional rules**. Han Li’s “arranged” access to Elder Ye’s goodwill is not a cheat—it’s a specific investment of social capital that pays dividends precisely because it bypasses the normal system. The steward’s stunned reaction is the reader’s cue: Han Li has just demonstrated that his network inside Yellow Maple Valley is deeper than his cultivation level suggests. In a sect where most disciples are interchangeable numbers, being *the one the hall master favors* is a silent threat and a hidden shield. This is not heroism; it’s career management in a cutthroat bureaucracy.
