## Chapter Overview

This chapter settles Han Li into Green Ox Town, where his third uncle runs the Spring Fragrance Tavern. After two quiet days of rest, a representative from the Seven Mysteries Sect arrives: Protector Wang, a proud and impatient man who softens only after receiving a bribe. The narrative pauses to outline the history of the Seven Mysteries Sect and its conflict with the Wild Wolf Gang, establishing the power dynamics of this corner of the world.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li arrives in Green Ox Town with his third uncle, Fatty Han, and is left to rest in a quiet courtyard.
- He eats, sleeps, and gradually warms to his uncle's stories over two days.
- On the third evening, a black carriage bearing the "Xuan" flag of the Seven Mysteries Sect arrives.
- The reader learns the sect was founded two hundred years ago by the Seven Ultimates Sage, once dominated Jingzhou, then declined and moved to the remote Xianxia Mountains.
- The Wild Wolf Gang is introduced as the rival local power—former bandits who inherited brutal aggression and now eye the sect's prosperous towns.
- Protector Wang, sent personally to fetch the candidate, shows arrogance but accepts a bribe from Fatty Han.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter presents a classic wuxia setting rather than a cultivation one. The "Xuan" flag functions as a territorial marker; any experienced wanderer would recognize it, showing that reputation and symbols carry weight in the martial world. The Seven Mysteries Sect is a secular Jianghu organization, not an immortal sect—no qi, no magic, only martial arts and gang politics. The saying "a skinny camel is still bigger than a horse" reflects a Chinese idiom about old power retaining hidden strength. The Wild Wolf Gang's origin from bandits emphasizes the harsh survival ethics of the world: brutality is inherited and respected.

## Reading Guide

New readers should note Han Li's early behavior: he is not timid, sleeps soundly in a strange place, and shows a quick adaptability that will become a core trait. Fatty Han is shrewd—he pays off Protector Wang to secure some protection for his nephew. The chapter also establishes that the Seven Mysteries Sect is recruiting heavily due to pressure from the Wild Wolf Gang, setting the stage for future conflict. The foundation of the world is being laid: the sect is a declining power, the gang is rising, and Han Li is about to enter a violent arena where money, connections, and raw survival skill matter more than justice.

## Seven Mysteries Sect {#seven-mysteries-sect}

The Seven Mysteries Sect (七玄门), originally called the Seven Ultimates Sect (七绝门), was founded two centuries ago by the Seven Ultimates Sage. It once ruled Jingzhou and cast influence across several provinces before losing its founder and being forced out of the capital. Today it controls about a dozen towns in the remote Xianxia Mountains. The sect is organized into inner and outer divisions; the inner halls train core disciples in advanced techniques, while the outer halls handle business and entry-level recruits. Its rivalry with the Wild Wolf Gang is the main source of current instability.

## Wild Wolf Gang {#wild-wolf-gang}

The Wild Wolf Gang (野狼帮) began as mounted bandits within Jingzhou. After government crackdowns and partial pacification, the remaining bandits reformed into a formal gang, but kept their raiding ethos. They control more territory than the Seven Mysteries Sect but lack economic skill, so their towns are poorer. Frustrated by this wealth gap, they frequently provoke clashes—especially over the richer settlements held by the sect. Their strength lies in ferocity and willingness to inflict and absorb losses.
