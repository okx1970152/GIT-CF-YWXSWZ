## Chapter Overview

This chapter depicts the long-awaited test of Han Li and Zhang Tie's cultivation progress after nearly half a year of dedicated practice. Doctor Mo's cold exterior cracks when he discovers the faint energy flowing within Han Li, revealing his underlying obsession with the nameless formula. Zhang Tie, who has failed to produce any result, is on the verge of dismissal until Doctor Mo unexpectedly offers him a different path based on his bone structure. The chapter solidifies the hierarchical nature of the master-disciple relationship and introduces Doctor Mo's true motivations—his excitement over Han Li's energy signals that this is far more than a routine assessment.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li and Zhang Tie undergo their cultivation test; Zhang Tie has achieved nothing, while Han Li's energy flow has grown from a hair to a thread.
- Doctor Mo shows no anger toward Zhang Tie's failure, only faint disappointment, then turns to examine Han Li.
- Upon detecting Han Li's internal energy, Doctor Mo becomes visibly excited, laughing loudly and staring at Han Li with a fanatical gleam.
- Doctor Mo declares Han Li his personal disciple (亲传弟子) and urges continued effort.
- Just as Zhang Tie expects to be expelled, Doctor Mo offers him a new cultivation method based on his bone structure, allowing him to stay.
- The chapter ends with both disciples feeling relieved and rewarded for their efforts.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

In xianxia and wuxia settings, the assessment of a disciple's progress often involves direct contact with a master's energy (qi) to gauge internal cultivation. Doctor Mo's method—grasping the wrist to feel the pulse and placing a hand on the lower abdomen (dantian)—reflects traditional Chinese medical diagnostic techniques adapted for cultivation. The "Eight Extraordinary Meridians" (奇经八脉) are a key concept in both medicine and internal alchemy; their spontaneous activation in Han Li indicates a natural aptitude that Doctor Mo covets. The transition from "disciple on probation" (记名弟子) to "personal disciple" (亲传弟子) signifies a deeper, more committed—and more dangerous—relationship in the master-disciple hierarchy of the Jianghu.

### The Eight Extraordinary Meridians

In traditional Chinese medicine and internal alchemy, the Eight Extraordinary Meridians (奇经八脉) are a system of eight vessels that store and regulate qi, distinct from the twelve main meridians which circulate qi in a daily rhythm. They are often described as "reservoirs" that fill when the main channels overflow. Spontaneous activation of these meridians is considered a sign of advanced or innate talent in cultivation fiction, granting practitioners superior control and faster circulation. Doctor Mo's excitement upon detecting this indicates that Han Li's potential greatly exceeds expectations.

### Personal Disciple vs. Disciple on Probation

In sect-based stories, a "disciple on probation" (记名弟子) is a provisional member who receives only basic instruction and can be dismissed at any time. A "personal disciple" (亲传弟子) is formally accepted by a master, receiving one-on-one guidance and protection, but also inevitably becomes entangled in the master's personal ambitions, debts, and secrets. The upgrade is both an honor and a sentence—the disciple's life is no longer their own.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a turning point in Han Li's journey: he moves from a passive apprentice to a targeted asset of Doctor Mo. Readers should note the doctor's extreme emotional shift—from cold indifference to manic joy—as a warning sign. His obsession with the energy rather than the person is a hallmark of the mortal stream's transactional worldview. Zhang Tie's reprieve is not mercy; it hints at Doctor Mo's broader plans. Pay attention to the phrase "this is exactly what I wanted"—it reveals that the nameless formula is a specific prize, not an incidental teaching. The chapter also subtly reinforces the theme of aptitude discrimination: Zhan Tie's failure is attributed to lack of compatibility, not effort.
