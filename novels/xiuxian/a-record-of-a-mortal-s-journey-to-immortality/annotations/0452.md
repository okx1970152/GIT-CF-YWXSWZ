## Chapter Overview

This chapter serves up a delicious tension cocktail: Han Li reunites with a beautiful woman from his past, only to watch her nearly jump out of her skin at being recognized. His calm mention of “Miss Yuan” sends her into a full-blown denial mode, with a hand hovering over her storage pouch. Meanwhile, Ziling the Immortalette, ever the survivalist, subtly shifts her alliance closer to Han Li. In a sharp scene cut, we then check in at the ghost-mist-surrounded garden “safe zone,” where the green-robed stranger arrives—disgusting the local Nascent Souls with his spotless robes—and immediately starts probing for an ally who is conspicuously absent.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li casually greets the black-robed woman as “Miss Yuan,” instantly transforming her from a proud beauty into a panicked denier who claims her surname is “Ruan.”
- The woman’s hand darts toward her storage pouch; the atmosphere tightens until Han Li calmly provides a meeting date and place: over a century ago at Kuixing Island’s Celestial Capital Street market.
- Recognition clicks, and the tension dissolves as the woman (Yuan Yao) admits the familiarity and apologizes for forgetting him, revealing she had mistaken him for someone more dangerous.
- Yuan Yao’s surprise at Han Li’s Core Formation status is genuine—she recalls him as a mere Qi Condensation cultivator, unaware of his Foundation Establishment past or his self-crippling cultivation art.
- Ziling makes a quiet but decisive political move: she steps half a pace toward Han Li without a word, declaring her allegiance based on power and prior trust.
- Scene shifts: the ghost-mist garden sanctuary, where Extreme Yin, Wan Tianming, and other Nascent Soul cultivators rest. A green-robed younger man arrives, completely unblemished by the ghost-fighting that has scarred everyone else.
- The green-robed man looks around, disappointed by Han Li’s absence, and internally argues with a mysterious voice living inside his body, who mocks his missing “helper.”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Power of a Name in the Cultivation World:**
In Chinese folklore and xianxia, a name is not neutral. Addressing someone by an unknown or abandoned name can feel like a threat, implying surveillance, prior vulnerability, or a connection the speaker thought was buried. Yuan Yao’s instantaneous panic at “Miss Yuan” is not just embarrassment—it signals that her true identity, her doppelganger, or the name she left behind at Kuixing Island is tied to a deadly secret. The cultivation world runs on secrets; no one recognizes a forgotten acquaintance without first weighing whether that person is a liability.

**Ghost Yaksha and Undead Politics:**
This chapter casually drops the Ghost Yaksha (鬼夜叉), a zombie variant so terrifying it moves and fights in broad daylight. For Western readers unfamiliar with the concept, think of it as a high-tier undead that breaks the “vampire burns in sunlight” rule—except it is far less romantic and far more relentless. Yaksha-class monsters in xianxia lore are often guardians of ancient tombs or byproducts of failed reincarnation; their green fur and bone-fork projectiles are dead giveaways for a creature powered by deep resentment and trapped souls.

### One-Hundred-Year Gap (百年之隔)

In xianxia, a century is a standard measure for a significant absence; two cultivators who last met at Qi Condensation or Foundation Establishment may find the other has crossed realms, forcing a total reassessment of the power dynamic.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a case study in “never trust a calm recognition scene.” Han Li’s polite, “Oh, it’s you” moment pivots from a potential knife fight into a tea-party reunion faster than you can say Ghost Yaksha. Yuan Yao’s terror at being identified—then her rapid-cooling relief once she realizes Han Li is not her stalker—is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s social high-wire act: every hello is a risk assessment. And off to the side, Ziling’s silent half-step is the most eloquent character note in the chapter. She doesn’t debate, doesn’t argue; she just *moves*. That’s the kind of loyalty you buy with proven power, not sweet talk.

Meanwhile, the green-robed man’s internal argument with his body’s passenger hints that the “safe zone” of the ghost-mist garden is a powder keg. If someone is already disappointed that Han Li didn’t show, the reader should brace for the inevitable accounting.
