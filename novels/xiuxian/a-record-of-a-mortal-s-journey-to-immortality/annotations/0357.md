## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s central currency: the promise. Xin Ruyin, knowing she has only two years left to live, offers Han Li her inheritance—rare formation manuals and a restored teleportation array—in exchange for a single, open-ended promise to one day avenge her husband against the Fu Clan. Han Li, ever the pragmatist, first evaluates the costs, then is moved by her condition to give a conditional promise: he will act only when he is strong enough. This is not a heroic oath but a calculated admission of a future possibility, hedged with escape clauses. The scene then cuts to Han Li visiting a tool-smithing shop, where he effortlessly quells a hostile peer with a glance—a quiet demonstration that his reputation now precedes him. The chapter balances emotional gravity with logistical preparation, reminding readers that in this world, even kindness is weighed on a scale.

## Key Plot Points

1. Xin Ruyin reveals she has at most two years to live due to her “Dragon’s Roar Constitution” (a male soul in a female body), and she is willing to die once vengeance is arranged.
2. Han Li examines her and confirms the diagnosis; her meridians are completely withered, and he cannot cure her.
3. Xin Ruyin explains that Qi Yunxiao was killed by the Fu Clan because of a dispute involving her, and the Qi Clan refused to act.
4. Han Li, after deep deliberation, agrees to give a promise: he will act only when he is sufficiently capable, with no time limit.
5. Han Li leaves and goes to the Starfall Sect market, noticing an unusually crowded atmosphere with many Foundation Establishment cultivators.
6. He visits the same tool-refining shop as before, run by Shopkeeper Xu.
7. A fierce-looking early Foundation Establishment cultivator snorts at Han Li’s preferential treatment. Han Li subdues him with a single cold glance carrying killing intent.
8. Shopkeeper Xu eagerly invites Han Li to the back courtyard to discuss new refining.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Promise” as a Binding Force**  
In Chinese xianxia cultivation lore, a promise (承诺, *chéngnuò*) is not just a verbal agreement; it can carry karmic weight. Breaking a sworn oath, especially one made before a witness or tied to a life-and-death matter, can generate Inner Demons (心魔) that obstruct future breakthroughs. Han Li’s careful wording—“I will act only when I am sufficiently capable”—is a classic Mortal Stream hedge: he offers hope without signing a suicide pact. This mirrors the real-world Daoist caution about incurring *yin guo* (karmic debt), a recurring theme in the genre.

**Dragon’s Roar Constitution (龙吟之质)**  
This is a rare, tragic constitution in cultivation fiction: a male soul trapped in a female body. Cultivating as a woman causes the meridians to prematurely wither, drastically shortening lifespan. It is a poetic inversion of the “female-in-male-body” trope seen in some other works, and in *RMJI* it serves as a stark reminder that the cultivation world is not fair even to the talented—some fates are structural, not earned.

**Market Crowds and Bottleneck Economy**  
The sudden influx of Foundation Establishment cultivators in the market hints at the upcoming bottleneck economy effect (see earlier chapters). When a major bottleneck-breaking event (like an Ascension Assembly) approaches, cultivators flood markets to buy pills, tools, and materials, driving prices up and competition fierce. Han Li’s observation of this phenomenon is a subtle clue that the world’s rhythm is about to accelerate.

### Bottleneck Economy

A market phenomenon where cultivators stuck at cultivation bottlenecks flock to trade before an Ascension Assembly, driving up prices and competition.

## Reading Guide

If you’ve been following Han Li’s journey since the Blood Forbidden Trial, this chapter feels like a quiet exhale—a moment to take stock. Xin Ruyin is one of the few characters who interacts with Han Li on a nearly equal footing of intellect and tragedy, and her fate is a poignant echo of the cost of cultivation. This promise will likely come due many volumes later, if at all: that’s the Mortal Stream way. Pay attention to Han Li’s negotiation tactics: he never says “yes” without first calculating the price, and even his “yes” is a guarded vault door. The shop scene, meanwhile, showcases his growing intimidation factor—a subtle marker that he is no longer the frightened village boy, but a predator whose reputation walks ahead of him. Enjoy the relative calm before the next storm.

## Additional Deep-Dive: The Fu Clan and Immortal Clan Politics

Fu Clan (付家) appears as the antagonist in Xin Ruyin’s backstory. In the cultivation world, a clan with a Foundation Establishment ancestor is considered a small-to-medium immortal clan; those with Core Formation elders are major regional powers. The Fu Clan’s ability to kill a rogue cultivator without consequence and intimidate even the Qi Clan into silence suggests they hold at least a Core Formation cultivator, or strong political ties to a major sect. This chapter quietly expands the world’s power map: not all enemies are lone wolves—some are institutions.
