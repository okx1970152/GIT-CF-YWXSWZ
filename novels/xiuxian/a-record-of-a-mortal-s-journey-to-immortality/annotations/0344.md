## Chapter Overview

Han Li’s interrogation of ‘Qu Hun’ hits a painful fork in the road. The trapped Yuling Sect elder spills the grand strategy behind the Spirit Beast Mountain betrayal and the impending Devil Dao sneak attack—a bluff that sounds chillingly plausible. But Han Li isn’t buying the story without proof. When he follows a lead to a hidden cave, the classic bait-and-switch springs: no jade slip, just a mountain of gnawed bones and a shadowy predator that bisects his prized defensive shield with a single blow. The trap has teeth, and Han Li just walked right into it.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li presses ‘Qu Hun’ on the unbelievable detail of two Core Formation cultivators dying in the staged attack. The elder reveals a secret Devil Dao technique: forcing a Foundation Establishment cultivator into a temporary, fake Core Formation state at the cost of near-immediate death—enough to fool allied observers during the short “ambush.”
- ‘Qu Hun’ argues that the Devil Dao will launch their decisive assault within days, not according to the treaty timeline, catching the Seven Sects at their most relaxed and undermanned. This suggests Han Li’s window to warn his superiors has effectively closed, even if he believed the story.
- Han Li demands physical proof, refusing to trust a sheer confession. The elder admits to hiding a jade slip of incriminating letters between his sect and the Spirit Beast Mountain in a nearby cave.
- After a tense pause, Han Li asks about the mechanics of ‘Qu Hun’ inhabiting a zombie shell. The elder insists it was a desperate accident—his soul found the soulless body while searching for any vessel and entered it successfully by pure chance.
- Han Li leaves, but not before sticking two more Spirit-Fixing Talismans onto ‘Qu Hun’ as a final layer of paranoia.
- Arriving at the cave, Han Li discovers not a storage pouch but a rotting pile of beast bones. Before he can retreat, a shadowy creature strikes, smashing him into the wall and shattering his White Phosphorus Shield clean in half with a second blow, sending him into a state of shock and alarm.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Forced Cultivation Boost (激发秘法).** The ability to temporarily elevate a cultivator’s realm is a recurring forbidden art in xianxia. It often carries irreversible side effects—meridian damage, soul collapse, or assured death within a short time. In this chapter, the Devil Dao exploits it for a tactical lie: sacrificing expendable cultivators to produce fake Core Formation deaths that fool allied intelligence. The classic “burn a pawn to win the game” logic.

**Essence Blood (精血).** Described in file 6 as a cultivator’s most vital life force, permanently lost upon use. Essence Blood is not just “powerful blood”; it is a foundational resource that irreversibly damages one’s lifespan and cultivation base when expended. Any blood ritual, forced boost, or life-saving technique that draws on it is a permanent transaction with one's own longevity. This concept underpins the irreversible cost of forbidden arts in this chapter and throughout the Mortal Stream.

**Spirit-Fixing Talisman (定神符).** A classic talisman from earlier chapters that completely immobilizes a target’s body while preserving their full consciousness. Han Li applying two extra talismans onto ‘Qu Hun’ before leaving is a textbook demonstration of his paranoid discipline: he never enters a back alley without locking the suspect’s ankles first.

**The Trap Construction (设伏·白骨诱饵).** The cave scene follows a classic mortal-stream ambush structure: a convenient lead → a plausible location → a scene that looks normal at first glance → a hidden predator waiting to spring the moment the target’s guard drops. The pile of gnawed bones is a brutal tell, and Han Li spots it—but half a second too late. Stacking psychological pressure with sensory detail (blood stench, bone teeth marks, black void) is standard horror technique, but in the Mortal Stream, it signals: the hunter you thought you had cornered has been running the game all along.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a shift in the interrogation arc: the information flow has peaked, and now the tension pivots from words to action. Pay close attention to the structure of Han Li’s paranoia here—he demands proof, searches the body, double-tags the prisoner, yet still walks into a classic trap. The chapter is a test of how far his caution can go before it fails. The monster that shattered the White Phosphorus Shield is deliberately kept invisible in the narration; Han Li doesn't get a clear look before the scene cuts. That visual gap is intentional—it leaves the reader with only the impact, the sound, and the clean bisection of a familiar defensive tool to measure the threat. If the shield that held against elders and talisman treasures can be cut in half, Han Li is suddenly no longer the predator in this fight.
