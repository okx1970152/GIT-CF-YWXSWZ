## Chapter Overview

Our expedition into the Ghost Grievance Ground goes deeper—and so does the chaos. Fairy Zi Ling nearly falls to a high-grade ghost sound art, saved only by Han Li’s timely thunder-shout. Up ahead, the black-robed demon cultivator is being systematically dismantled by a shadowy ghost with a nasty green pearl, while his prized Crying Soul Beast is held at bay by two green-haired Ghost Yakshas. Han Li watches—coldly, deliberately—before choosing his moment to intervene. And just when the black-robed man thinks he’s been rescued, a third, near-invisible ghost lunges from the mist. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream combat doctrine: never commit until you’ve seen every card on the table, and never assume the visible threat is the only one.

## Key Plot Points

- Fairy Zi Ling is ensnared by the “Soul-Reaving Ghost Sound,” a high-grade innate ghost art that forces victims into uncontrollable dancing. Han Li’s sharp shout breaks the spell, and she regains control. This establishes his tactical prioritization: he saves the nearest ally first.
- The black-robed man is locked in a losing battle with a black shadow ghost that uses a green pearl to release Xuan Yin cold energy, overwhelming the black-robed man’s crimson demon flame hammer.
- The Crying Soul Beast is pinned down by two green-haired Ghost Yakshas—a rare zombie variant that can operate in daylight. They attack from range with ghost-fire, wary of the beast’s soul-sucking yellow light. Their physical bodies allow them to resist the beast’s pressure as long as they keep their distance.
- Han Li observes the entire fight without intervening, to the black-robed man’s fury. This is textbook Mortal Stream: he gathers intelligence on the enemy’s abilities, positioning, and hidden reserves before committing.
- When the shadow ghost closes in for the kill, Han Li strikes with precise azure sword beams, forcing the ghost to retreat rather than exchange injuries. The black-robed man recovers his hammer and flees toward Han Li.
- Han Li suddenly warns of additional ghosts. A near-invisible gray humanoid form lunges from the mist at the fleeing black-robed man, who can no longer dodge. The chapter ends on this cliffhanger—a confirmation that the battlefield holds more than meets the eye.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Ghost Sound Arts (鬼音术) & Soul-Reaving Ghost Sound**
In Chinese folk and xianxia traditions, ghostly sound attacks are often tied to resentful spirits who lured victims into danger using cries that mimic human voices. Here, the “Soul-Reaving Ghost Sound” is a high-grade innate art that physically overrides the victim’s motor control. The countermeasure—guarding the heart-mind—is a classic xianxia principle: as long as your internal spirit is fortified, external psychic attacks can be resisted. Han Li’s shouted “呔” (tai/tè) is itself a traditional Daoist exorcism syllable, used to shock wandering spirits and reassert one’s own soul’s authority.

**Ghost Yaksha (鬼夜叉)**
“Yaksha” originally comes from Hindu/Buddhist mythology as a class of nature spirits. In Chinese xianxia, Ghost Yakshas are a specific subspecies of zombie—not mindless rotting corpses, but cunning, physically intact undead that retain enough intelligence to fight tactically and can even move under sunlight. The fact that they have flesh bodies makes them harder to banish than pure ghostly entities. Their green fur and bone forks are signature visual markers.

**Xuan Yin Cold Energy (玄阴寒气)**
This is a classic yin-aligned energy property. “Xuan Yin” (玄阴) literally means “mysterious/dark yin” and is often associated with ghostly or death-attribute techniques. The green pearl the shadow ghost uses is clearly a high-grade artifact that generates this cold, able to smother demonic flame by sheer qualitative superiority. The black-robed man’s demon flame hammer, while powerful, is simply outclassed in elemental alignment.

**Crying Soul Beast (啼魂兽)**
Introduced in earlier chapters, this rare beast is nature’s counter to ghostly entities. Its nose emits a “soul-sucking light” that devours ghost-fire and spirit energy alike. But as Zi Ling notes, this beast is still young and has only this one ability—meaning it can be neutralized by opponents who know how to keep their distance. The yakshas’ strategy (ranged attacks + physical bodies) is a textbook example of how cunning undead exploit a beast’s limitations.

## Reading Guide

Buckle up, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a perfect demonstration of why you never rush into a fight in the Mortal Stream. Han Li does absolutely nothing while the black-robed man is getting his demon flame snuffed out. Is he being petty? Sure, there’s some of that. But more importantly, he’s running a full battlefield analysis: he’s identified the shadow ghost’s pearl as the main threat, clocked the yakshas’ ability to neutralize the Crying Soul Beast, and he’s scanning for hidden actors. The moment he finally acts is a surgical strike, not a heroic charge—and then he immediately calls out a threat nobody else saw coming. That’s Mortal Stream combat doctrine at its finest: patience, pattern recognition, and knowing that the real danger is often the one you don’t see.

Also, take a moment to appreciate Fairy Zi Ling’s role as the lore-dispenser here. Her explanation of the yakshas and the Crying Soul Beast’s limitations is classic xianxia exposition, but it also highlights Han Li’s biggest weakness as a rogue cultivator: he lacks systematic sect knowledge. This is going to become a recurring theme—his hunger for archives and ancient texts isn’t just plot convenience, it’s a genuine survival need in a world where knowing your enemy’s weakness can mean the difference between life and death.
