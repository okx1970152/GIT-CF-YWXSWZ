## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks the calm before the storm as the Void Heaven Palace’s Inner Hall finally opens. Han Li navigates a cruel illusion that weaponizes his deepest memories—his long-lost little sister—while the real battlefield is a high-stakes political standoff among Nascent Soul old monsters who treat him as a pawn and a key. The chapter brilliantly contrasts a haunting, personal ghost with the cold, transactional negotiations of immortals who view each other not as enemies but as temporary partners in a game of mutual extraction.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li encounters an illusion that takes the form of his deceased little sister, but he sees through its ruse immediately: she would not recognize him after so many years. The Brahma Pearls confirm his suspicion, and he destroys it without hesitation.
- A day later, the surviving trial participants assemble before the massive five-tier Inner Hall pagoda, a structure of staggering scale and covered by a white light barrier.
- Extreme Yin Patriarch is visibly relieved when Han Li finally appears in the teleportation formation alongside Yuan Yao, marking the assembly of all major players.
- Neither Extreme Yin nor Man Brutal Beard asks for their lent treasures (the Brahma Pearls and the armor) to be returned, signaling an unspoken understanding that the real prize lies ahead.
- The Profound Bone Saint sends Han Li a secret voice transmission, warning him not to reveal their collusion and hinting that his plan only becomes viable *after* the treasure is retrieved—a statement that deeply unsettles Han Li.
- As the Inner Hall gate rises, the teleportation formation dies, confirming the Star Palace will not intervene.
- Wan Tianming proposes a duel to decide who enters first, but Man Brutal Beard goads him. Unexpectedly, Wan Tianming refuses and instead suggests a “turn-based retrieval” pact to avoid a costly preemptive fight.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Illusions of Loved Ones (幻境亲友)**: The trope of an enemy or environment manifesting a deceased loved one to disarm a cultivator is a staple of xianxia psychological warfare. It tests a crucial tenet of Daoist cultivation: the ability to sever attachment without losing humanity. Han Li’s recognition that the illusion could not possibly know him after decades of separation shows his emotional clarity—he grieves, but he does not suspend his logic for grief. The moment his hand trembles slightly after the kill is the story’s quiet, bitter acknowledgment that even a cold calculator still bleeds inside.

**The “First Dibs” Politics (轮流取宝)**: Wan Tianming’s proposal to avoid a pre-duel and simply take turns is a classic example of “applied face politics” in the cultivation world. In a setting where pride and territory are currency, offering a compromise before a fight is actually a power play: it forces the other side to accept a deal on your terms or be seen as unreasonable. The demonic path’s hesitation shows they know any agreement is a temporary leash—both sides expect to betray the deal the moment the treasure is in someone’s hands. The entire exchange is a formality wrapped in a trap.

**Brahma Pearls as Sentry (婆罗珠预警)**: The Brahma Pearls are Han Li’s early-warning system against spiritual and illusion-based threats. Their heat response to the illusion-like white shadow is a reminder that in the Mortal Stream, even an emotional weakness must be backed by a fail-safe mechanism. Han Li doesn’t trust his own heart; he trusts the artifact to tell him when his heart is being exploited.

### Turn-based retrieval pact (轮流取宝)

A diplomatic arrangement where opposing factions agree to take turns attempting to claim a treasure, to avoid a preemptive duel; widely understood as a temporary truce that will be broken as soon as the prize is secured.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in tonal contrast: the deeply personal, solitary horror of the little-sister illusion sits in sharp opposition to the highly theatrical, political confrontation in the second half. Pay attention to how Han Li processes the two modes—he is stone-faced and silent among the elders, but carries a flicker of raw hurt from the illusion into his silence. The Profound Bone Saint’s warning that the plan only works “after the treasure is taken” is the chapter’s most chilling line: it confirms that Han Li is walking into a trap-shaped victory, and the moment he succeeds is also the moment all bets are off. The “turn-based retrieval” pact is a classic xianxia diplomatic farce—everyone knows it’s a setup, but no one is willing to fire the first shot.
