## Chapter Overview

After a tense standoff, Han Li’s transaction with the stocky young Qi Yunxiao takes an unexpected turn. What begins as a straightforward material trade morphs into a long-term strategic investment. Han Li receives a complete set of formation tools for the legendary Reversed Five Elements Formation and a rare volume of artifact refinement notes. But instead of hoarding the knowledge, he seals the notes with a time-lock spell and sets a five-year challenge: improve the formation tools, and the pristine notes will be returned. Then, in classic Mortal Stream style, Han Li does the boring-but-brilliant thing: he visits the artifact shop to transact his centipede shells, reminding us that logistics always follow a hard-won victory.

## Key Plot Points

- Qi Yunxiao reveals his identity as the descendant of a Divine Soldier Sect Artifact Refinement Elder and offers his personal refinement notes in exchange for the spiritual herb.
- Han Li agrees, but instead of just taking the notes and walking away, he makes a counter-offer that reeks of long-term calculation: he seals the book and promises to return it unread if Qi can improve the formation tools within five years.
- This move isn’t charity—it’s a social engineering play. Han Li plants the seeds of a future alliance with a gifted refiner and his formation master friend.
- Han Li returns to the shop and completes the material transaction, selling the centipede shells to the white-haired shop owner who nearly runs off with them like an excited child.
- The chapter closes with Han Li’s inner acknowledgment: connections with skilled craftsmen are worth more than any dead book.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Reversed Five Elements Formation (颠倒五行阵)** is a standout piece of worldbuilding. In xianxia, formations are often divided into killing formations (that destroy enemies), trapping formations (that seal areas), and—rarest of all—illusion formations. The Reversed Five Elements Formation is a “Minor Forbidden Array” that specializes in misdirection and concealment, making it a prized defensive asset for any solo cultivator. Portable versions (using pre-made array flags and plates) are considered precious because they don’t require the user to master the complex art of formation-setting; you just place them in the right spots and activate them.

**The “thank-you-for-teaching” gift** that Li Huayuan earlier leveraged is a classic institutional trick in the cultivation world. In a system where masters hold all the cards, giving something “for free” (like guidance to a disciple-on-probation) and then demanding an “etiquette offering” in return is a form of soft exploitation. Han Li recognizing this pattern is part of his education in navigating sect politics—he learns not to trust generosity without a price tag.

**Memory Sealing (封印之术)** is a neat technique that appears across xianxia. It allows a cultivator to bind a book or jade slip to a specific time condition or a spiritual fingerprint. The silver runes Han Li uses here are a low-grade variant—enough to deter casual tampering, but not an immortal-level absolute lock. It’s a symbol of trust that also doubles as a tracking mechanism; if the seal is broken, Qi Yunxiao will know.

### Memory Seal

A cultivation technique that binds a book or jade slip with silver runes, preventing it from being opened without breaking the seal. The seal can be keyed to a spiritual fingerprint for verification.

### Silence Barrier

A sound-dampening technique used by cultivators to create a private space for negotiation, preventing eavesdropping.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is what we in the biz call a **“quiet victory”** —no explosions, no deaths, no dramatic duel. But for readers of the Mortal Stream, these are the chapters that age like fine wine. Han Li isn’t just buying a set of array tools; he’s hedging his friendship bets. Qi Yunxiao is clearly a sheltered heir from a fallen lineage—he’s honest, desperate, and has a friend who is a formation genius. Instead of extracting maximum short-term value (reading the notes now), Han Li invests in long-term credibility. Return the notes unimproved and keep the seal intact, and Qi will trust him for life. That’s the kind of play that pays dividends ten chapters down the line.

Also, let’s appreciate the shopkeeper scene: the old man practically vibrating at the sight of rare centipede shells is a hilarious counterpoint to Han Li’s icy pragmatism. It’s the kind of grounded, human moment that makes the cultivation world feel lived in—not every interaction is a life-or-death ambush. Sometimes, it’s just a cranky craftsman trying to scam a good deal.

## The Art of the Long Con

This chapter is a masterclass in **deferred value**—a concept that separates the ordinary rogue cultivator from someone who actually has a shot at Foundation Establishment. Qi Yunxiao’s *Art of Soaring Cloud* notes might contain genuine sect-level secrets, but Han Li knows that a book without context or collaboration is just a useless trophy. The real treasure? The relationship with Qi and his friend. So he turns a one-time trade into a five-year probationary alliance, and he lets Qi *keep* the spiritual herb to prove his goodwill. If Qi improves the formation tools, Han Li gets a better set and still has the option to read the notes. If Qi fails, Han Li can still open the seal—at no cost. This is the strategic patience of someone who plays the game board, not just the current turn.

## Final Thoughts

For the journey-reader, this chapter is a palate cleanser—a chance to watch Han Li operate outside of combat. His ability to pivot from suspicious trade partner to long-term networking is just as important as his ability to dodge a flying sword. And the centipede shell transaction adds a nice touch of “boring-but-profitable” reality to the cultivation economy. Not bad for a chapter where almost nothing “happens.” Get ready, fellow Daoists; the quiet moments are where the real wars are won.
