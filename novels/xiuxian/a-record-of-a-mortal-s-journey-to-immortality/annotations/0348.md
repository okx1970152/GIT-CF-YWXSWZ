## Chapter Overview

Han Li finally puts the giant mantis out of its misery, but the real reward isn’t the kill—it’s the discovery of a Core Formation corpse and its peculiar storage pouch. For a mortal-stream protagonist, looting is the true boss fight, and this chapter delivers a bittersweet haul: dead eggs, a mountain of insect feed, and one priceless jade slip. It’s a classic RMJI beat—victory does not mean treasure, and treasure does not mean immediate power.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li sets up a full Reversed Five Elements Formation to trap the mantis, using puppets to exhaust it over two days before delivering the killing blow with his Talisman Treasure.
- He harvests the mantis’s forelimbs and wings, then finds over a dozen demon beast eggs inside—all dead, which frustrates him.
- Inside the cave, he discovers the Imperial Spirit Sect cultivator’s skeleton with its storage pouch intact.
- The pouch is packed with insect feed and live bug samples, but no talismans, tools, or treasures—a letdown that nearly makes Han Li lose his composure.
- The saving grace: a green jade slip containing detailed notes on strange insects, including breeding techniques and power rankings, with the mantis placed at #73.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Dead Eggs and Demon Biology**: In xianxia, high-grade demon beasts reproduce rarely, and their eggs often come out stillborn. This isn’t just biological realism—it reinforces that powerful life is never cheap. Han Li’s decision to keep the dead eggs anyway is a classic hoarder’s instinct; in this world, even trash can become leverage with the right formula.

**The Jade Slip Economy**: The jade slip is the true prize. In cultivation fiction, scrolls and slips are the primary vehicles for lost knowledge, and one good manual can be worth more than a pile of magical tools. Han Li’s disappointment at the pouch’s contents is genuine, but the slip’s insect-ranking list opens a new strategic door—puppetry, insect mastery, and future pet-cultivation subplots.

**Golden-Back Demon Mantis (金背妖螂)**: The name is deceptive—the mantis is gray, not gold. This mismatch may hint at a mutation, a translation quirk, or simply a naming convention that stresses potential over appearance. Its #73 ranking means there are 72 deadlier bugs out there, a subtle worldbuilding flex that tells readers the insect-path runs deep.

### Essence Blood (精血)

A cultivator's most vital life force, permanently consumed upon use; irreversible damage to lifespan and cultivation base.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a textbook “quiet victory”—the battle is over before it begins; the real tension is in the loot roll. Pay attention to Han Li’s near-anger at the storage pouch; it’s one of the few times he lets genuine frustration show, and it makes his character more human without breaking his pragmatism. Also, note the ranking system for strange insects. In later arcs, knowing which bugs dominate the top 10 may become an unexpected advantage for our cautious protagonist. Don’t sleep on the jade slip.
