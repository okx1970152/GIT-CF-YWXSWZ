## Chapter Overview
Han Li thought he’d dodged a bullet by surviving the Blood Forbidden Trial, only to walk straight into a brand-new trap: a Core Formation Ancestral Master wants him as a disciple. And not because he’s talented. This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s favorite pastime—social survival under smiling threat. Han Li has to perform joy while calculating cost, accept a “privilege” that feels more like a leash, and then sit through an elder’s “helpful” explanation of why his reward just got cut in half. The real gut punch? He’s actually *relieved* to figure out his new master is just after his spirit herbs. Because in this world, a transparently greedy master is still safer than one with hidden motives.

## Key Plot Points
- Li Huayuan announces he’s accepting Han Li as a disciple-on-probation, expecting ecstatic gratitude. Han Li freezes, visibly uneasy—which nearly costs him.
- Cornered by the unspoken threat of refusal, Han Li forces a flush of excitement, bows, and performs the full ceremony, accepting a top-grade magic tool as a “welcome gift” that he can’t refuse.
- The onlooking disciples burn with envy, completely unaware that Han Li would gladly trade places with any of them.
- Back at the Hundred Herbs Garden, Steward Ma is stunned to see Han Li alive—and even more stunned by his “promotion.”
- Ma reveals the sect’s unwritten rule: a master can claim half of a disciple’s first tribute to the sect. Li Huayuan’s “generous” offer of discipleship was, in fact, a tax grab on Han Li’s twenty-odd spirit herbs.
- Han Li’s reward is effectively halved: one Foundation Establishment Pill instead of two.
- Instead of anger, Han Li experiences deep relief. A master who wants his herbs is predictable. A master with unknown motives would be far worse.
- Steward Ma offers genuine advice: a protective Core Formation backing is worth more than a second pill to someone with Han Li’s poor spiritual roots.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Disciple-on-Probation (记名弟子)** is a loaded term in Chinese cultivation fiction. It’s not a full, permanent discipleship—it’s a provisional, record-only status where the master acknowledges the relationship but isn’t obligated to pass on their core techniques or secrets. It’s often used for exactly what Li Huayuan is doing: a low-cost way to claim the “master’s share” of a disciple’s tribute without investing real teaching. It’s the cultivation world’s version of a tax loophole.

**The “Thank-You-for-Teaching Gift” (谢师礼)** is drawn from real-world Chinese Confucian tradition, where students would present gifts to their master as a token of gratitude. In the brutal reimagining of *RMJI*, what was once a voluntary gesture of respect has been weaponized into a mandatory one-time tax, reinforcing the novel’s central thesis: even the most benign-looking institutional traditions are systems of extraction.

**Core Formation Cultivators** as sect-level deterrents. Li Huayuan isn’t just a powerful fighter; he’s a strategic asset whose name alone justifies the unequal exchange. The sect allows the “tax” because it reinforces the power structure: a Core Formation elder gets richer, the sect gets stronger, and the low-level disciple gets… the privilege of not being eaten by bigger fish.

## Reading Guide
This is a quiet, high-stakes chapter that rewards close reading. Han Li’s *inner* reaction to the discipleship offer is pure dread—his first thought is Doctor Mo. But the *external* performance is flawless: eager, grateful, obedient. The gap between the two is the entire point. Pay attention to how quickly he recalibrates once Ma explains the “real reason.” That *relief* is terrifying in its own way—it means Han Li has internalized the logic of the Dark Forest so deeply that a greedy, exploitative master is *comforting.*

Also note Steward Ma’s character development. He reads as a gruff, cynical old man, but he gives Han Li genuinely useful advice and warns him out of concern, not malice. He’s one of the few characters in this novel who consistently acts without a hidden knife. Keep an eye on him.

Finally, this chapter plants a seed: Li Huayuan is willing to take a loss (betting on Han Li’s potential) but recoup it via institutional rules. That says a lot about his personality—he’s not a brutal aggressor, but he’s also not generous. He’s a *calculator*, and Han Li is now a number on his ledger.

## Hidden Depth: The “Fool’s Mask” Strategy
Han Li has been wearing social masks since Volume 1, but this chapter is a textbook example of the *Fool’s Mask*: pretending to be dazed by good fortune while rapidly scheming beneath the surface. The “flush of excitement” he manufactures isn’t just acting—it’s weaponized subservience. He knows that Li Huayuan expects a certain performance, and he delivers it *exactly*, buying himself time to figure out the man’s true angle. In the Mortal Stream, sometimes the best defense is looking exactly as harmless and grateful as your predator expects you to be.
