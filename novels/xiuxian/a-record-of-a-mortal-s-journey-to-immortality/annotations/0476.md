## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to another lesson in why patience, preparation, and an army of adorable-yet-terrifying beetles are the true keys to cultivation success! In this chapter, our ever-cautious junk-rate-root cultivator Han Li steps into the legendary "Treasure Light Pavilion." But instead of diving headfirst into a greedy grab, he does what he does best: looks, calculates, frowns at the cheap stuff, and strategizes his way to the big league. The first floor is a bit of a letdown—middle-shelf antiques at best—but the second floor? Oh, that’s where the real game begins. Get ready for a looting sequence that’s less "unbridled greed" and more "a systematic, high-stakes asset acquisition."

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li recovers his composure after earlier battles and enters the towering Treasure Light Pavilion through a yellow light barrier that offers surprisingly little resistance.
- The first floor is filled with rows of jade platforms, each capping a semi-circular light shield containing a single ancient treasure. Several platforms are already empty, indicating that earlier arrivals have already made their selections or moved on.
- After scanning the hall, Han Li fails to find a staircase to the second floor but identifies a small, custom teleportation array instead.
- Browsing the first-floor treasures, Han Li grows deeply underwhelmed; the items are all standard-issue ancient halberds and lances, no better than common magic tools. He has zero interest in such middling wares.
- Han Li activates the teleportation array and ascends to the second floor, where a single massive spherical light barrier floats at the center, containing a dazzling collection of dozens of unique ancient treasures—scrolls, jade tablets, mirrors, banners, and more.
- Han Li tests the barrier’s defenses by striking it with a single Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Sword; the blade is effortlessly repelled. He then merges nine swords into one giant blade for a more powerful strike; it manages to rend a gap in the barrier, but the wound heals almost instantly.
- Han Li deploys his Gold Devourer Beetles, which gnaw through the barrier faster than it can recover, creating a temporary opening. He transforms into a streak of light and dashes inside just before the barrier reseals.
- Once inside, Han Li finds that his divine sense is completely suppressed. He cannot probe the treasures' spiritual power, so he must rely entirely on instinct and visual appraisal to make his choice.
- He shortlists three candidates: a golden oval mirror, a set of five linked multicolored copper rings, and a dark-red wide cloak. He leans toward the mirror and the rings, but the chapter closes with his gaze fixed on the cloak.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a masterclass in the *Treasure Snatching Sequence*, a staple trope of xianxia fiction. The setup is classic: the protagonist enters a sealed, time-locked room that earlier competitors have already picked over, yet finds the truly valuable goods still unclaimed because the later-tier defense mechanisms are proportionally more absurd. The logic is pure *RMJI*: the higher the barrier’s difficulty, the more precious the reward inside—and the more likely a "commoner" like Han Li, with his unconventional tools, can bypass a test that "genius" cultivators with brute force couldn’t crack.

Also noteworthy: the first floor’s treasure selection is deliberately underwhelming. This is the xianxia equivalent of a low-tier loot box—things that look ancient and prestigious but are actually just re-skinned ordinary weapons. This is a classic subversion of the "old = good" assumption. Han Li, with his battle-proven arsenal, can see through the hype. This reinforces the Mortal Stream’s core lesson: knowing what *not* to take is a survival skill.

The second-floor barrier’s self-healing property will be familiar to fans of hard magic systems. It’s not a static shield that can be overwhelmed with a single powerful blow; it’s an active, regenerative defense that punishes excessive mana expenditure and rewards sustained, consistent attack power. Enter the Gold Devourer Beetles: a classic “cheat code” that sidesteps the intended test by exploiting a loophole (the barrier cannot regenerate faster than the bugs can eat). This is the xianxia equivalent of hacking a high-security vault by bringing a swarm of nano-bots to the heist.

### Self-healing barrier

A defensive formation that automatically repairs any breach in its structure; countering it requires sustained output or a loophole exploit rather than a single overpowering strike.

## Reading Guide

Okay, let’s be real for a second. If you were expecting Han Li to walk in, see a dozen legendary treasures, and start hoarding them all like a dragon, you’ve forgotten who you’re reading about. Our man Han Li does not "collect." He *audits*.

What I love about this chapter is the quiet, systematic ruthlessness of Han Li’s treasure selection. He doesn’t get dazzled by the pretty lights. He looks at a halberd from the ancient era—a thing that any other cultivator would drool over—and basically says, "Pfft, that’s a junk weapon with a fancy coating." He has a clear standard: *Is this better than what I already own?* And for 99% of the stuff on the first floor, the answer is a firm no.

Then comes the second floor: the real deal. But even here, his instinct is not "grab everything" but "pick the one that fits my build." He shortlists the golden mirror (classic OP item in xianxia—mirrors are never weak), the five-element copper rings (potential for insane combo power), and the mysterious cloak. The chapter ends with him pondering the cloak. Cloaks in Chinese web novels are rarely just for camouflage. They often signal a strategic, defensive, or concealment-type artifact that perfectly suits Han Li’s "don't-be-seen, don't-be-hit" philosophy. Watch this space.

Also, let’s talk about the Gold Devourer Beetles. Remember when Han Li spent all that time feeding and nurturing these ugly little bugs? This is the payoff. They just turned a high-difficulty, self-regenerating barrier into a non-issue. This is the beauty of the Mortal Stream: your wins don’t come from "leveling up your sword," but from "my weird bug army can eat through your super-barrier." It’s petty, practical, and perfect.
