## Chapter Overview

Alright, fellow cultivators, buckle up. You know those auction scenes in every cultivation novel where the protagonist sits through endless junk before buying something overpowered? *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality* flips that script hard. This chapter is pure *Mortal Stream* gold: Han Li enters a new city, endures a trash-tier auction, makes a cold, clean break from his convenient traveling companions, and then heads straight to the real prize—not a magic sword, but *information*. Specifically, a specialist in ancient herb names and pill formulas. This is the quiet, logistical warfare that defines Han Li's journey: he doesn't need another flashy weapon; he needs a *clue* to break his cultivation bottleneck. The chapter also showcases the brutal, systemic horror of the *Human Furnace* trade and the quiet power of a Core Formation cultivator's aura. It's a masterclass in efficient, atmospheric worldbuilding through cynical eyes.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Failed Auction:** The Pearl Pavilion tries to auction off two female cultivators as Human Furnaces. They fail to attract any bidders because their cultivation base is too shallow to be of practical use to higher-level cultivators. Xu Yun coldly explains this logic to the group.
- **Han Li's Strategic Withdrawal:** Bored with the auction, Han Li decides to part ways with the group. He uses a simple statement of intent, backed by a chilling flash of killing intent in his eyes, to freeze Xu Yun and the brocade-clad man into silence. The scar-faced Xu Yun then *sound-transmits* a warning to the brocade-clad man, identifying Han Li as an inscrutable force not to be tangled with.
- **The Inch Gold Pavilion:** Han Li enters the city's premier material shop and immediately requests to see the manager, leveraging his Core Formation aura to jump the queue. He meets Li Xun, a manager who is also a Core Formation initial-stage cultivator.
- **The Specialist is Summoned:** Li Xun, reading Han Li's mood and eager to assist, summons Gu Yu—a Qi Condensation scholar with specialized knowledge in ancient pill formulas and spirit herbs. This is the true prize of Han Li's visit.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a brutal, systemic look at the institutionalized horror of the cultivation world. The auction of two female cultivators as "Human Furnaces" (炉鼎) is not portrayed as a crime, but as a standard market transaction. The term "furnace" comes from Daoist alchemy, where a literal cauldron refines pills. In the *Mortal Stream*, this metaphor is twisted into a ghastly reality: weaker cultivators, overwhelmingly women, are treated as consumable biological resources to boost a more powerful cultivator's foundation. Xu Yun's explanation that they were "too shallow in cultivation to be useful" is the real punchline—they weren't rejected out of morality, but out of poor market value. This is a foundational *Mortal Stream* principle: morality doesn't exist; only the cold calculus of resource utility.

Also note the power of social presence. Han Li doesn't threaten or argue with Xu Yun. He simply *feels* like withdrawing, and a flash of cold intent in his eyes is enough to silence two Foundation Establishment cultivators. His Core Formation aura, even suppressed and disguised, carries an instinctual weight that higher-tier beings simply project. This isn't arrogance; it's the efficient transmission of a threat assessment.

## Reading Guide

Pay close attention to the chapter's efficiency. Han Li doesn't waste energy on adventure or sightseeing. He immediately identifies the local information hub (the Inch Gold Pavilion), deduces the authority structure (ask for the manager), and secures access to a specialist (Gu Yu). This is the *Mortal Stream* protagonist at his finest: he doesn't fight the system; he exploits its own rules to get what he needs. Also, watch how Han Li handles the departure. He doesn't explain or apologize; he gives a final statement and a silent, visceral threat. In a world where words are cheap and knives are sharper, the most effective communication is often a quiet flash of killing intent. The chapter ends on a perfect setup—not a battle, but the promise of a key piece of *information*. That is the currency that matters most here.

## The Human Furnace Economy in Detail

For those of you new to *xianxia*, the "Human Furnace" trope can be stomach-churning. It's a direct, brutal extrapolation of Daoist "dual cultivation" (阴阳双修) concepts, where partners supposedly help each other balance their internal energies. In the *Mortal Stream*, this ideal is systematically perverted. Wealthy or powerful cultivators purchase "furnaces" to extract their vital energy (usually Yin energy from female cultivators). The victim's cultivation is sacrificed to accelerate the buyer's. The fact that these two women were *volunteering* for this (according to Xu Yun) highlights the desperation of the low-level cultivation economy. They are trading their future for survival. This isn't a fantasy; it's a grim, systemic predatory logic that defines the world Han Li is trying to navigate.
