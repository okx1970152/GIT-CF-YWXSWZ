## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to the high-stakes world of high-end trading! This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream's negotiation dynamics, where the battlefield shifts from sword and spell to the subtle dance of appraisal, bluffing, and calculated reveals. Han Li steps into the back room of the Ten Thousand Treasures Pavilion, and the weapons he wields are not magical tools, but a sharp understanding of economics and human greed. If you've ever wanted to see a protagonist use packaging and strategic silence to punch above his weight class, this is your chapter. Get ready for a seminar on how to turn two rare herbs into a life-saving arsenal—and a tantalizing glimpse at an item so valuable it's kept under wraps for a whole other layer of intrigue. The game is afoot, and Han Li is playing it perfectly.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Arsenal Unveiled:** Tian Zhanggui begins his sales pitch, introducing three high-value magical tools from the brocade boxes.
- **Golden Fleece Mother-Son Blades:** A set of one mother blade controlling eight sub-blades; a classic "overwhelming numbers" attack tool made by a high-level craftsman.
- **Mysterious Iron Floating Shield:** An automatic defensive artifact that orbits the user, blocking vital points. Han Li tests it by infusing his spiritual power and is highly satisfied with its flexibility.
- **The Heaven Thunder Seed:** A legendary, one-use thunder pearl of immense power, said to be able to vaporize a Foundation Establishment cultivator. Its rarity and price are hinted to be astronomical.
- **The Art of the Deal:** After the thunder seed, Tian Zhanggui stops talking and sips his tea, signaling it's time for Han Li to show his financial worth before the final, sealed box is revealed.
- **The Bait is Cast:** Han Li, relying on his two thousand-year spirit herbs, presents one in an exquisite box, understanding the principle that "fine packaging increases value."
- **The Appraiser's Shock:** Tian Zhanggui, initially dismissive of the *Huangjingzhi*, realizes it might be a thousand-year-old herb after a closer look, causing him to lose his composure.
- **Calling in the Expert:** Unable to verify the herb's age himself, the manager orders his servant to fetch the venerable Elder Ding, a mortal but highly respected appraiser, to make the final call.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Economics of Longevity:** In the Mortal Stream, a thousand-year spirit herb is not just a rare plant; it is a cornerstone of the cultivation economy. Its value is derived from its scarcity and its irreplaceable role in high-grade pill refinement. The fact that Han Li, a mere Qi Condensation cultivator, possesses such an item marks him as a client of serious (and potentially dangerous) worth. Tian Zhanggui's excitement is not mere merchant greed; it's the recognition of an asset that can secure his pavilion's reputation and profits for years to come.

**The "Clothes Make the Man" Principle:** Han Li's choice to present the herb in an "exquisite-looking small wooden box" is a deliberate, strategic move rooted in worldly wisdom. This principle, often translated as "fine feathers make fine birds," acknowledges that presentation is part of the product's value. In a cultivation world where first impressions and perceived status can determine the outcome of a transaction or even a life-or-death encounter, this tactic is a sign of Han Li's practical intelligence.

**Why a Mortal Appraiser?:** The involvement of Elder Ding, a mortal with no cultivation, is a fascinating detail. It underscores that while cultivators possess incredible power, specialized, non-magical knowledge is still highly valued. A mortal who has dedicated his life to appraising herbs may have a trained eye and instinct that a cultivator, even a high-level one, lacks. In this niche, mortal expertise is the ultimate authority.

## Reading Guide

- **Watch the Micro-Negotiation:** Pay close attention to the subtle power plays. Tian Zhanggui's silence after the Heaven Thunder Seed is a classic negotiation tactic to force the buyer to show their hand. Han Li's response—sipping tea and saying nothing—is a perfect counter-bluff, mimicking the seller's own earlier behavior.
- **Appreciate the Foreshadowing:** The three items introduced—the offensive blades, the defensive shield, and the ultimate trump card (the Thunder Seed)—each serve a specific tactical role. Notice how Han Li's mind immediately categorizes them for use in the upcoming Blood Forbidden Trial. This is not a shopping spree; it's a tactical supply mission.
- **Han Li's Calculated Greed:** Observe that Han Li does not immediately jump at any of the items. He tests the shield, stays silent about the blades, and only shows a flicker of *inner* calculation for the Thunder Seed. His external calm is a weapon, preventing the seller from knowing what he truly values.
- **The Mystery of the Final Box:** The sealed box that Tian Zhanggui *didn't* introduce is a major hook. What kind of treasure requires not just a show of wealth, but a full appraisal of a thousand-year herb to even be shown? This narrative beat builds suspense for the next chapter.
- **Key Concept:** **Information Asymmetry.** The entire negotiation hinges on who knows the true value of the *Huangjingzhi*. Han Li knows its supposed age, but the merchant has the market knowledge to set its price. The tension comes from their mutual struggle to control this imbalance.
