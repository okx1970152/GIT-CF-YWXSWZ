## Chapter Overview

Our dear, perpetually unlucky Han Li kicks off this chapter with a genuinely peaceful stroll—no explosions, no ambushes, no sudden ghost attacks. He actually follows a map and walks right to a wall. And guess what? He finds a teleportation array that’s ugly as sin but functional. It’s practically a vacation, by xianxia standards. Naturally, this serene golden moment lasts exactly as long as it takes for the white light to fade. Because Han Li materializes in a hidden stone chamber with a big glowing pool, a whiff of perfume, and a very naked, very startled Yuan Yao staring back at him. The Mortal Stream has its own special way of saying “plot twist”—and it’s usually awkward, wet, and accompanied by a few hastily thrown spells. Get ready, fellow Daoists, because this chapter serves up an unexpected bath scene, a high-value resource reveal, and the kind of tense negotiation you only get when two people know each other’s secrets and neither has a clear upper hand.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li reaches a wall marked on his map and cuts an opening with his sword lights, confirming a hidden chamber exists.
- He finds a crude teleportation formation inside. After inspecting it, he determines it’s functional and short-range.
- Before teleporting, he repairs the wall with restoration spells to erase any trace of his passage.
- Han Li teleports and arrives in a large stone chamber containing a milky-white, fragrant pool.
- A naked woman is standing in the pool, playing with the water. It’s Yuan Yao.
- After a tense moment of mutual shock, Han Li recognizes her and calmly remarks on the “coincidence,” showing no intention of looking away.
- Yuan Yao is furious and embarrassed, but quickly recovers her composure and begins a playful, seductive act.
- Han Li tests the pool water and identifies it as a Spirit Eye Spring—a rare and extremely valuable resource for cultivation.
- He notes another detail: a green jade bottle beneath a dragon-head carving on the far wall, which Yuan Yao reclaims at the first chance behind a water-curtain distraction.
- Yuan Yao uses a water curtain to buy a moment of cover, dresses herself with black energy, and reclaims the jade bottle without further comment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### Spirit Eye Springs (灵眼之泉)

In the cultivation world, there are a few supernatural “Spirit Eye” formations. A Spirit Eye Spring is exactly what it sounds like—a physical concentration of pure spiritual qi that manifests as a pool of liquid energy. Meditating near one can boost one’s cultivation speed by a significant margin (20–30%, by some accounts). They’re incredibly rare and almost never found on the open market except as a rumor or a founding sect’s greatest treasure. In this chapter, the Heavenvoid Palace’s master relocated an entire Spirit Eye Spring into a sealed chamber—a display of wealth and power that changes everything about how dangerous this place really is. Remember, in the Mortal Stream, any resource worth killing over has already been found by someone else, which means Han Li is standing in the middle of a triage zone without realizing it.

### The Face-Off in a Nude Pool: Xianxia’s Awkward Tactic

You might be wondering why Yuan Yao doesn’t scream, attack, or call for help. In xianxia logic, a sudden compromise like this signals a delicate power balance. Yuan Yao is clearly strong enough to resist, but Han Li just walked in through a teleportation array she apparently thought was broken. That means he knows more than she expected. He’s also a known quantity: he saved her life before. Even in her naked humiliation, she gauges him, tests his intentions, and decides that a seductive distraction is a better play than a defensive fight. This is not a bedroom comedy—it’s a cold piece of social calculation dressed up as a blushing scene. Yuan Yao uses her beauty as a tactical asset, buying time to secure her own prize (the jade bottle) without revealing its value. Classic Mortal Stream: even the most intimate moments are resource negotiations.

## Reading Guide

This is a masterclass in how the Mortal Stream handles the intersection of high-value loot, old debts, and personal embarrassment. Han Li is not a romantic lead who simpers and looks away. He’s a survivor who takes in every detail—the pool, the bottle, the woman’s real cultivation level, and the exits. Yuan Yao, for her part, is a fox wrapped in a silk dress: she recovers from her indignity with impressive speed and turns the situation into a bargaining position. Keep an eye on that green jade bottle. Its reappearance near the dragon-head carving is not a coincidence. And watch how both of them use polite conversation as a screen for mental audits of the other’s intentions. In a few chapters, this encounter might make more sense as a setup rather than a punchline.
