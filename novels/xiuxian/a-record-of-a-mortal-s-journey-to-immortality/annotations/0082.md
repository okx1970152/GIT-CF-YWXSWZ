## Chapter Overview

This chapter pivots from personal cultivation struggles into large-scale sect warfare—and drops a bombshell that redefines the entire power balance. Jia Tianlong, the Wolf Gang chief, has secured an unexpected trump card: a rude, gold-obsessed dwarf cultivator who calls himself the Brilliant Gold Elder. This being can fly swords and boasts an indestructible body—legends made flesh in a world that, up to now, has only hinted at such powers. While the Wolf Gang masses advance on the Seven Ultimates Hall, the story ends on a cold, shocking note: the Seven Mysteries Sect leader, Wang Juechu, emerges alone from the main hall and calmly declares he has come to discuss surrender. But is a man with eyes like unsheathed swords the kind who gives up without a fight? The battlefield is set, and the reader is left holding their breath.

## Key Plot Points

- The Brilliant Gold Elder is physically described in meticulous, almost absurdist detail: a three-chi dwarf in a gold-trimmed red robe, loaded with jewelry and gold teeth. His vulgar, nouveau-riche appearance contrasts sharply with his terrifying power.
- He openly criticizes Jia Tianlong’s hesitation and speaks with unchecked arrogance, revealing that he is not a subordinate but a hired immortal master.
- Jia Tianlong’s backstory with the elder is revealed: they met at a Daoist temple near the barbarian borderlands. The dwarf demonstrated Flying Sword Art and an Indestructible Body technique, convincing Jia Tianlong he is a true cultivator.
- Jia Tianlong lavished gold on him (the elder’s obsession) and secured one promise of aid, now being called in. He even increases the promised payment to five thousand taels total.
- The elder confirms he will handle the three hidden uncles of the Seven Mysteries Sect—the real power behind the sect—dismissing them as mere mortals.
- The Wolf Gang and allied gangs surround the seven stone halls on Sunset Peak. Jia Tianlong is already planning to move his headquarters there after victory.
- As Jia Tianlong raises his hand to give the signal to attack, a cold voice stops him. The Sect Leader, Wang Juechu, emerges alone from the darkness.
- Wang Juechu’s presence is chilling: pale face, sharp sword-like gaze, no visible fear. He comes out carrying only a single sword.
- The chapter ends on a shocking note: when Jia Tianlong mockingly asks if he has come to surrender, Wang Juechu replies with cold precision: “You are correct.”

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Brilliant Gold Elder introduces a crucial boundary in the *Mortal Stream* world: **the line between martial arts and immortal cultivation**. Up to this point, the Seven Mysteries Sect was presented as a peak *Jianghu* power, with masters at the "transcendent" stage of martial inner energy. Now a character has appeared who can fly swords and claim invulnerability—proper *xianxia* (immortal hero) abilities. In Chinese cultivation fiction, this marks a major power escalation: Jianghu experts fight with speed and skill; cultivators rewrite the rules of physics.

The elder’s name, **Brilliant Gold Elder (金光上人)**, plays on the common trope of using *Gold (Jin)* and *Light (Guang)* to evoke alchemical purity and divine radiance. Yet his appearance—crammed with gold jewelry, gold teeth, and gaudy accessories—is deliberately vulgar. This contrast is a favorite trick of xianxia authors: true cultivation transcendence is supposed to be free of worldly attachments, so a cultivator who wallows in mortal wealth signals either a heretical path, a profound flaw in their Dao, or both. Readers familiar with the genre will immediately read this as a red flag: this is not a wise immortal; this is a dangerous, unstable asset.

The **Flying Sword (飞剑)** is one of the most iconic weapons in Chinese fantasy. Unlike a sword wielded by hand, a Flying Sword is controlled by a cultivator’s spiritual sense (divine consciousness), enabling ranged attacks at terrifying speeds and unpredictable angles. It is the hallmark of a true cultivator and represents a qualitative leap beyond any martial art. When the elder says “no mortal skill can withstand my flying sword,” he is stating a genre law, not making a boast.

## Reading Guide

Get ready, fellow readers—this is where the *real* power scale enters the story, and it’s wearing gold chains and missing a foot of height. The Brilliant Gold Elder is exactly the kind of character that makes the Mortal Stream universe so dangerous: he’s not a wise mentor or a heroic ally; he’s a hired gun with a god complex and a shopping addiction. Jia Tianlong treats him like royalty, and you can see the calculation in every line: he knows this dwarf could destroy his entire gang if offended, so the only language he speaks is gold.

Pay attention to Wang Juechu’s entrance—it’s masterfully staged. He comes out of pitch darkness, his footsteps the only sound in total silence, and his first two words stop an army of thousands. His conversation with Jia Tianlong is a verbal duel: Jia uses casual, mocking language to assert dominance (“Sect Leader Wang has come alone?”), but Wang matches him with ice-cold precision. And then the surrender line hits. Is it a trap? A feint? A last-resort negotiation? The chapter doesn’t tell us—it just drops the hook and lets it hang.

One more thing: note how Jia Tianlong thinks about the seven stone halls. He’s already mentally moving in. That kind of overconfidence—celebrating victory before the battle is fought—has a way of punishing itself in cultivation fiction. Something tells us this siege isn’t going to be as simple as a bribe and a dwarf.
