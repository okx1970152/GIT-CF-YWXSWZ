## Chapter Overview
Oh, you thought the fight was over, fellow Daoist? Think again! Chapter 170 is the **victory lap that refuses to be clean**. Han Li stands over the cooling corpse of Senior Brother Lu, counting his spoils with a smile—but the bill for this brutal fight has come due. His body is wrecked from devouring raw spirit herbs, his Talisman Treasure turns to ash in his hand, and just when he thinks he can breathe, a very *different* kind of battle finds him in the form of a naked, Harmony Elixir-addled Junior Sister Chen. This chapter is a masterclass in "nothing is ever free in the Mortal Stream"—not even victory.

## Key Plot Points
1. **The Bill Comes Due:** Han Li's Talisman Treasure, pushed beyond its limit, self-destructs into ash. He feels the sting of loss, but his immediate assessment of the Cyan Flood Dragon Flag and the two Foundation Establishment Pills quickly recalibrates his cost-benefit analysis. The trade, in his mind, was worth it.
2. **The Hidden Cost of Desperation:** The chapter reveals the damaging aftermath of Han Li's gambit—swallowing raw spirit herbs. His dantian burns with accumulated impurities, and he is forced into a painful, slow recovery.
3. **Tactical Debriefing:** Han Li mentally replays the fight, breaking down why he won. The key lessons: his mid-grade spirit stone's superior recharge rate, and his crucial decision to cancel his Water Shield to conserve mana—a subtle tactical advantage his opponent never understood.
4. **The Unexpected Ambush:** With his guard lowered after the fight, Han Li is tackled from behind by a fully nude, drug-crazed Junior Sister Chen, who has just been freed from her binding spell. The scene is awkward, tense, and charged.
5. **Discipline Reasserted:** Despite a brief moment of human weakness, Han Li swiftly reasserts control. He uses a Spirit-Fixing Talisman to immobilize her, ending the encounter with characteristic cold pragmatism.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Burden of the Talisman Treasure (符宝):** This is a crucial chapter for understanding the *real price* of using these compressed artifacts. Talisman Treasures are not infinite weapons; they are dying batteries. The fact that Han Li's treasure turned to ash mid-descent is not just a plot device—it's a grim reminder that even high-level tools in the Mortal Stream have finite lifespans, and using one up in a single battle is a devastating loss of a strategic asset. It reinforces the "scarcity" principle at the heart of the cultivation world's economy.

**The Hidden Cost of "Mana-Draining Auras":** Han Li's realization about talismans constantly draining mana to maintain contact is a classic example of *xianxia* logic that rewards keen, observational readers. Most cultivators would never notice this tiny, almost imperceptible drain. But in a protracted war of attrition, that "negligible" cost becomes a fatal wound. This is not a glitch in the system; it's a hidden rule that only the paranoid and the meticulous survive long enough to exploit.

**The Fallout of Forced Alchemy:** Han Li's raw-herb consumption is a desperate, last-resort tactic. In *xianxia*, consuming unrefined ingredients is a classic shortcut, but it always carries the consequence of "miscellaneous energy" and "poison impurities" (杂质). This is why alchemy is a sacred art—it separates the useful from the harmful. Han Li's current agony is the price he pays for not having time to brew a proper pill.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a fantastic example of what makes *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality* so different from standard power fantasy. The victory is not applauded; it is audited. Han Li spends more time mentally calculating the cost of his win than celebrating it. The Talisman Treasure's destruction, the physical backlash from his tactics, and even the awkward post-battle "reward" are all treated as logistics problems. The final scene with Junior Sister Chen is a brilliant conclusion—even a moment of potential "triumph" is immediately turned into a threat and neutralized. This is the Mortal Stream in a nutshell: enjoy the spoils while you can, but know that every single one has a string attached.

## The Harmony Elixir: More Than Just a Plot Device
The Harmony Elixir (合huan丹) in *xianxia* is rarely a simple love potion. It's a tool of violation, a weapon of control that strips a cultivator of their mental discipline and reduces them to pure, helpless instinct. In a world where mental fortitude (心境) is a core component of cultivation, such an elixir is a horrifyingly effective humiliation tactic. Han Li's decision to freeze the scene with a Spirit-Fixing Talisman rather than take advantage is not born of morality alone—it's a cold calculation that taking that path would create its own set of unpredictable consequences. In the Mortal Stream, even a fleeting moment of pleasure is a liability.
