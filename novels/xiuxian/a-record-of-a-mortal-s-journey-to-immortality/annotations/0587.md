## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the first chapter of a brand new arc, fellow Daoists! The wreckage of the ghostly mist battle is still settling, but Han Li has no time to count his losses. After a brutal teleportation strips him of his mana and divine sense, he finds himself a helpless mortal in a dark, fishy-smelling cavern—with an unfamiliar woman literally on top of him. From the high-stakes, life-or-death pressure of a Core Formation cultivator’s duel, we pivot to an absurd, tense, and unexpectedly intimate survival scene that feels like a wild cosmic joke. This chapter is a masterclass in “adapt or die,” though for once, the immediate danger is a bit more… fragrant.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li survives the fall from the dissipating golden barrier by using the mortal-level Smoke Step technique, confirming that old skills remain viable when high cultivation is stripped away.
- His prized treasures—the Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords, golden mirrors, ancient blue basket, and silver bell—fall lifelessly to the ground, having lost all spiritual energy.
- A terrified Han Li discovers that both his mana and divine sense have been rendered completely inert; he cannot even open his storage or spirit beast pouches.
- The pursing black ghostly mist engulfs him instantly, and a black lightning teleportation rips him away from the island.
- He awakens in a pitch-black underground cavern, lying in a pile of slippery, wriggling fish and shrimp, his body too weak to move from the forced teleportation.
- A second black lightning strike deposits a young woman directly on top of him, and in their mutual helplessness, they are forced into a close, awkward, and deeply embarrassing physical embrace.
- The woman’s suspicion is cut short by the arrival of distant torchlight, revealing four or five tall, green-clad strangers wielding crude weapons and carrying large leather pouches, who are approaching fast.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Forced Teleportation & Mana Loss:** Being “teleported raw” without the protection of one’s cultivation base is a known hazard in the Mortal Stream. Han Li’s physical weakness and dizziness are not just dramatic flavor—they are the logical consequence of a mortal body being squeezed through a spatial tunnel designed for energy-wrapped beings. It reinforces that even at Core Formation, the *body* is still a fragile container without active mana reinforcement.

**The ‘Mortal State’ as a Narrative Reset:** This chapter introduces the “zero-state” pressure cooker: Han Li is reduced to a baseline human with no access to his usual trump cards. In Xianxia, this is a classic device used to test a protagonist’s *core competence*—his wits, reflexes, and martial instincts—apart from his cultivation level. It is not a setback; it is a tactical audit.

**Darkness and Physical Contact in Chinese Fiction:** The trope of strangers forced into close physical proximity in complete darkness is a staple of martial arts romance (*wuxia/xianxia*). Unlike Western writing, which may dwell on explicit sensation, the Chinese tradition often uses it as a device to reveal character psychology: Han Li’s wry, clinical self-awareness versus the woman’s shame and suspicion. The absurdity (a pile of fish beneath them) is a deliberate tonal contrast to the serious deadly world.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a tonal whiplash, because after a high-octane magical duel, we are now in a dark, stinky fish cave with a woman who is *furious* at being stuck on top of a strange man. The tension here is not from a beast or an enemy cultivator—it is from an exquisite, awkward social standoff. Han Li’s response here is perfect: he does not panic, does not flirt, and does not apologize profusely. He just calmly explains the biological inevitability of his situation and waits. It is a cold, logical read of a hot, messy moment. Also, keep an eye on those approaching torch-wielders. In a world without mana, a meaty club and a sharp knife are just as threatening as a flying sword.
