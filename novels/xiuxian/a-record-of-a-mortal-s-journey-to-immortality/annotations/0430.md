## Chapter Overview

Fellow Daoists, take a deep breath—we’ve officially left the calm waters behind. Chapter 430 kicks off the overseas arc with a textbook demonstration of why you never, ever touch a shiny object in a xianxia ruin without running a full risk assessment first. What starts as a promising treasure hunt devolves into a gruesome, almost surgical trap that reminds everyone: in *RMJI*, “legendary immortal herb” usually means “bioweapon in disguise.” Han Li stands at the center of it all, watching the other cultivators dig their own graves with nothing but greed—and the payoff is as satisfying as it is brutal.

## Key Plot Points

- The group discovers a Seven-Iris Flower growing from a pool of Thousand-Year Stone Milk, triggering excitement and an immediate shift in group dynamics. Han Li’s earlier display of the Gold-Devouring Beetles has made the others wary, and they begin to tacitly align against him.
- Han Li calmly questions the flower’s lore, displays subtle self-mockery at their suspicion, and quickly pivots to inspecting the nearby skeleton—already treating the scene as a forensic puzzle rather than a payday.
- Jian attempts to pick the flower after being cleared by Shi Die’s formation check, but the stem is inexplicably tough. Ignoring Han Li’s warning, he tries to snap it off and is yanked into the pool.
- The pool water is revealed to be lethally toxic: Shi Die’s face is corroded by mere splashes, and Jian’s corpse floats up as a half-decayed mess.
- Han Li calmly reveals the skeleton’s blackened right hand with a missing finger—evidence that the trap was designed to lure victims in, not protect the treasure.
- He tests the suspicious flower with a fireball volley, provoking a counterattack: a dark shadow shoots out of the pool straight at him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Seven-Iris Flower (七霞莲)**: This is a classic “legendary herb” in Chinese folklore—never found in formal alchemical texts, but appearing constantly in oral tales about immortals or mountain gods. The name “Seven Iris” evokes a rainbow-like spectrum, and each additional color of petal signifies greater maturity. The fact that it’s described here as “unrecorded in any herb manual” is a deliberate signal: if a treasure can’t be found in the academic record, it’s because it’s either mythical or a trap. Spoiler: it’s the latter.

**Thousand-Year Stone Milk (千年石乳)**: In real Daoist alchemy, stone milk (石乳) refers to a milky, mineral-rich stalactite secretion believed to have life-extending and purifying properties when refined. In xianxia, it’s often used as a precious ingredient for high-grade pills or as a cultivation aid. Its presence here makes the poison trap doubly sinister: the cultivators’ greed was so blinding that they assumed the pool’s beautiful contents were naturally beneficial—a perfect ambush.

**Gold-Devouring Beetles (噬金虫)**: These insects are Han Li’s current ultimate trump card, and their terror is already well-established among the other cultivators. They function as a psychological weapon as much as a combat one: the moment the group shifts from cooperation to wariness, it’s because they know those little bugs can annihilate them all. In the Mortal Stream, a single high-value asset can completely warp social dynamics.

### Expedition (探险)

In *RMJI*, ancient ruin expeditions are high-risk resource-gathering missions, not heroic quests; trust is a currency spent with extreme caution.

### Primordial Ancient Treasure (洪荒古宝)

A magic weapon from an ancient era, distinct from modern treasures; they cannot be absorbed into the body but possess singular power far greater than modern equivalents.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in “show, don’t tell” for the Mortal Stream ethos. Han Li doesn’t preach caution—he simply lets the others’ greed do their work for them. Notice his tactical patience: he inspects the skeleton while the rest are mesmerized by the flower, calls out a single warning without insisting, and watches Jian make the fatal choice on his own. That’s not callousness; it’s survival calculus. In a world where every party member is a potential rival and every treasure might be a trap, the person who hesitates is the one who lives. Also, keep an eye on that final counterattack—Han Li’s provocations are never accidental.
