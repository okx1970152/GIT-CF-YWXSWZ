## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to another tense episode of marketplace economics! Chapter 140 is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream's core principle: *Social Bluffing and calculated negotiations are just as deadly as any blade*. Forget the epic battles for a moment. This chapter is a high-stakes game of chicken played out in a crowded bazaar, where the currency isn't spirit stones alone, but wits, patience, and the willingness to exploit an opponent's desperation.

We find our protagonist, Han Li, observing a standard low-level transaction: a stubborn seller, a keenly interested buyer, and a growing crowd of onlookers. The prize is a seemingly small "magic treasure fragment" with a unique property. As tempers flare and a hapless bystander digs himself into a hole, Han Li sees not a conflict, but an opportunity. He steps in not as a peacemaker, but as a strategic player, ready to snatch the prize from right under everyone's noses with a single, well-timed move.

## Key Plot Points

- A dark-skinned, tenth-layer cultivator desperately wants a "Wind-Returning Bowl" from a stall, but the owner stubbornly insists on a trade for his "magic treasure fragment."
- The fragment has a unique property: it can become invisible and suppress spiritual energy, making it perfect for hiding other items.
- A series of bids from the surrounding cultivators push the price of the fragment up to twenty spirit stones, offered by an eager but naive round-faced youth.
- It is dramatically revealed that the youth does not have the spirit stones he bid, having only shouted out of impulsive fun, which enrages the powerful dark-skinned man.
- Han Li, who has been silently observing, steps in at the critical moment, not with spirit stones, but with a high-grade primary Flight Talisman, completely changing the nature of the negotiation.
- The chapter ends with the crowd stunned by the value of the talisman, implying Han Li has secured a decisive advantage in the trade.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is a perfect primer on the "Bottleneck Economy" and the value of "Chicken Rib" items in the cultivation world. The "Wind-Returning Bowl" is a valuable, specialized item that is useless to most but a massive power-up for the right person. The “magic treasure fragment” is a classic example of a “Chicken Rib” (鸡肋, jī lè): something that is tempting to have but, for most, impossible to use effectively. Its true value is hidden, known only to someone with Han Li’s vision.

Furthermore, the reaction of the crowd highlights a key difference between low-level and high-level cultivators. The average cultivator is scraping by on a handful of spirit stones, while family disciples have deeper pockets. Han Li, however, plays a different game. He doesn't measure value in mere currency; he measures it in *strategic utility*. A high-grade talisman, which is a scarce and powerful resource for most, is just the right tool for Han Li to acquire a prize he deems far more valuable. This reinforces the Mortal Stream principle: a cultivator’s true wealth is not their spending power, but their ability to see and exploit opportunities others miss.

### Bottleneck Economy (瓶颈经济)

A market phenomenon in the cultivation world where cultivators stuck at a bottleneck engage in desperate trade, valuing potential breakthrough items above all else.

## Reading Guide

Pay close attention to Han Li's non-verbal actions in this chapter. He doesn't jump into the negotiation immediately. Instead, he watches the entire drama unfold, letting the seller's frustration and the buyer's desperation reach a boiling point. He only steps in when the situation is at its most fraught, making his intervention seem like a savior’s hand rather than a greedy grab. This is the hallmark of a master of Social Tribulation (社会试炼, shèhuì shìliàn). He is not buying an item; he is manipulating a social dynamic to present an offer that the seller will find irresistible.

Notice also how Han Li frames his proposal. He doesn't say, "I will pay you for it." He says, "I don't plan to buy it. I’ll trade this item for it." This subtle linguistic shift positions him not as a struggling bidder, but as an equal offering a rare and valuable treasure. It is a powerful psychological play that instantly changes his status in the seller’s eyes from a nuisance to a potential patron. This is the translation of cold calculation into action.
