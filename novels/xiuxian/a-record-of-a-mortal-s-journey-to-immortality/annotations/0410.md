## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow Daoists—this chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s favorite trick: the double trap. Just when our posse of righteous(ish) cultivators thinks they’ve cornered the villainous bandits, everything flips sideways. What looks like a straightforward extermination raid turns into a tense standoff, with both sides realizing they’ve been played by a hidden third party. Han Li, naturally, spends most of the chapter sliding backwards like a paranoid crab—and we love him for it.

## Key Plot Points

- The Sublime Sound Sect tracks the heretics to a tiny island fortress and blasts through a pathetic illusion array.
- Battle erupts immediately. Han Li and Qu Hun engage several Foundation- Establishment-tier enemies, who unleash a Demonic Corpse transformation that feels awfully familiar—but isn’t quite Black Fiend Sect tech.
- Chi Huo the Ancient Monster traps the three Core Formation leaders inside his black cloud; outnumbered, the heretics are slaughtered almost to the last man.
- A late-Core Formation middle-aged man storms out, furious, and demands to know why his sect is being massacred. He has *zero* guilt-ridden vibes.
- Madam Fan and Zi Ling piece it together: both sides were set up. The real killers framed the Hidden Fiend Sect and sent the Sublime Sound Sect on a revenge-fueled rampage.
- Elder Zhao’s suspiciously eager attack and two elders defying Zi Ling’s halting order confirm there’s a mole (or multiple) in their midst.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Demonic Corpse vs. Black Fiend Sect Devouring Corpses:** This chapter introduces a subtle mechanical distinction—these heretics can transform instantly, without the blood-cocoon ritual or the condensed Blood Coagulation Five Elements Pill that marked the Black Fiend Sect’s devouring corpses. In xianxia, such differences are rarely accidental; they hint at a shared origin but divergent techniques, or perhaps a simplified battlefield adaptation. The “transformation without incantation” is a classic sign of a mass-production combat art, optimized for expendable troops rather than elite assassins.

**The “You’ve Been Framed” Trope:** The framing setup here is pure wuxia/xianxia tradition: a hidden enemy engineers a bloody conflict between two powers by having one group’s elites impersonate the other. The beauty of the trick is that it doesn’t need airtight logic—just enough rage to make the first punch land before anyone asks questions. Once blood is spilled, the trap snaps shut, and even discovery of the truth can’t undo the massacre.

### Enforcement Cultivators (执法修士)

## Reading Guide

This is the kind of chapter where an attentive reader should feel the creep of paranoia along with Han Li. Notice how the battle starts efficiently but gets weird fast—the heretics fight like cornered animals, *not* like bandits expecting to be hunted. Pay attention to Elder Zhao’s too-quick trigger finger; the best traitors aren’t the ones sneaking in back doors, but the ones shouting “Charge!” before anyone can ask questions. Also, enjoy Han Li’s silent crab-walk backwards—it’s a beautiful reminder that in the Mortal Stream, the only wrong move is not having an exit strategy.
