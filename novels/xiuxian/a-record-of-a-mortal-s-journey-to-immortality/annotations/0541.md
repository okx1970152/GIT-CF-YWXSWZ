## Chapter Overview
Alright, *fellow daoists*, buckle up. This chapter is a short but *heavy* one. We’ve just surfaced from the chaotic Void Heaven Palace arc, and the narrative takes a sharp, intimate turn. We are transported to a misty, nameless sea, where we witness the desperate transaction of a young girl’s future. Forget ancient treasures and Nascent Soul battles for a moment. This is a classic Mortal Stream chapter that focuses not on combat, but on the quiet, systemic horror of the cultivation world's resource economy. We watch a girl named Gongsun Xing make a choice that will define the rest of her life, not with a bang, but with a quiet, tearful sentence of sacrifice. It’s a masterclass in showing, not telling, the cruel logic that underpins this entire universe. Get ready for a gut punch of pure, distilled *Mortal Stream* atmosphere.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens on a tiny, isolated island shrouded in mist. A group of cultivators, led by a middle-aged man, have brought the young Gongsun Xing to a mysterious elder who claims he can cure her dying father. The elder’s method is chilling: he doesn't offer his help out of kindness. He proposes a straight trade. His ‘Ultimate Origin Pills’ for her body. He needs a ‘furnace’ to break through his cultivation bottleneck. Gongsun Xing, after a brief moment of stunned hesitation, accepts without bargaining, her main concern being the veracity of the medicine. She tosses the pill bottle to her uncle-master for authentication before sealing her own fate. Despite the desperate protests of her companions, especially a young man who clearly cares for her, she walks into the light formation without a backward glance. She is teleported away to the elder’s hidden cave dwelling, a cold, quiet prison. The second half of the chapter follows her in this new, silent space. She is led to a bare stone room by a giant ape puppet. Left alone, her initial fear slowly gives way to a wounded curiosity. She ventures out, tests the limits of her cage, and eventually discovers a room filled with jade slips—ancient texts on formations and artifact refining. The chapter ends with her browsing these slips, a quiet moment of intellectual escape that does nothing to change her grim future. It also ends with a brief author’s note, apologizing for the short chapter.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Crucible of the Furnace (炉鼎 - Luding)**. We’ve talked about this term before, but this chapter gives us its most visceral, unromanticized demonstration. Remember, in the *Mortal Stream*, the term is stripped of any Daoist poeticism about spiritual alchemy or balancing yin and yang. It is redefined as a brutal institution of objectification. A "furnace" here is a living, breathing person—often a female cultivator with a special constitution—whose vitality is meant to be forcibly extracted to boost the user's cultivation. This isn't a marriage or a partnership; it’s a parasitic transaction. The horror is amplified by its sheer *normality*. The elder offers the trade as a cold, business-like proposal. The girl accepts it as a logical, albeit devastating, solution. There is no dramatic villainy, just the cold, grinding gears of a system where a person’s life and future have a calculable market price.

**Dual Cultivation (双修 - Shuangxiu)**. In many romantic or adventure stories, this is a mystical path to power that emphasizes harmony and mutual benefit. In the *Mortal Stream*, it rarely is. This chapter shows the predatory side: a powerful cultivator at a bottleneck seeing a weak, vulnerable girl as a consumable resource, a biological tool to force his own advancement. The term is used here not as an invitation to partnership, but as a threat and a sentence.

**The ‘Never Act Without Benefit’ (无利不起早) Protocol.** This is Han Li’s personal mantra, but in this chapter, we see it expressed by a complete stranger. The elder’s opening line—“I never act without benefit”—is a mirror held up to the entire world’s operating system. It’s a direct, explicit statement of the Dark Forest principle that governs all interactions in this universe. This chapter brilliantly uses a different character to reinforce the protagonist’s own core philosophy, showing that Han Li is not a unique outlier, but a product of his environment.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a palate cleanser and a world-building feast, all rolled into one. After the high-octane, multi-climax action of the Void Heaven Palace, the story takes a deep, quiet breath to remind us what this world is really like for 99% of the people living in it. Pay close attention to Gongsun Xing’s demeanor. She’s not a powerless victim weeping and begging. She’s composed, clear-headed, and makes her choice with a chilling, pragmatic resignation. She is a **Mortal Stream hero in miniature**—not choosing the best option, but choosing the *least-bad* option available to her. The chapter also introduces a seed for the future. Why is this mysterious elder surrounded by formations, artifacts, and ancient jade slips? Why does his life feel so isolated? This is not just a random side-story. This is the cultivation world in microcosm. The final scene of the girl reading the jade slips is a powerful image of escape—not a physical escape from her cage, but a mental one into forbidden knowledge. For a character about to be reduced to a biological resource, that flicker of curiosity and independence is a quiet act of defiance, and a classic *Mortal Stream* setup for a future twist.
