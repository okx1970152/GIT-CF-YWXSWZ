## Chapter Overview

This chapter, despite its relatively short length, operates on multiple levels that reward careful reading. On the surface, it follows Han Li as he sneaks back into the Yue Kingdom to repair the ancient teleportation array—a purely logistical sequence of material procurement, spatial concealment, and skill-check frustration. But the chapter’s real payload arrives in its second half, when Han Li’s cautious night-travel leads him to a chance encounter near the spirit mine. Flying overhead in a three-way chase, Han Li recognizes not just the Ghost Spirit Sect Young Master and the effeminate man from Yanling Fort, but also a veiled woman in white whose voice makes his heart skip a beat: Nan Gong Wan. Worse, Dong Xuan’er—Han Li’s former travel companion who disappeared into demonic hands—reappears standing shoulder-to-shoulder with the enemy, her cultivation improved and her old bewitching manner replaced by an eerie, cold dignity. It is a classic RMJI reunion trap: no warm embraces, only layers of new complications, unanswered questions, and a hostile sky full of old faces.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li decides to return to the Yue Kingdom via a remote border crossing, avoiding the well-patrolled main routes; his caution proves justified as the demonic path is heavily guarding the entry points.
- He arrives safely at the ancient teleportation array location, seals the cave with an Illusory Formation, and fortifies it with the Reversed Five Elements Formation before beginning repairs.
- The repair process is slow and painful: seven days in, he has completed barely half the work and runs out of materials due to a high failure rate in his own craftsmanship. This is a practical bottleneck—not a combat one, but a skill-check forcing him to leave the safety of the cave.
- Han Li travels east at night toward a small cultivation market, planning to buy more materials; his night-travel habit is a direct extension of the paranoid survival discipline he developed throughout earlier arcs.
- While surveying a rest spot at dawn, he detects three rapidly approaching figures: a white streak in front, a bloody mist in the middle, and a pink cloud behind.
- The figures stop and reveal themselves mid-air. The dialogue confirms the white-light figure is the veiled woman whose voice matches Nan Gong Wan’s; the blood mist carries Wang Chan (Ghost Spirit Sect Young Master); the pink cloud contains the effeminate man and Dong Xuan’er.
- Dong Xuan’er, last seen in Chapter 315 (someone check it—she was captured or disappeared amidst the chaos of the demonic path invasion), is now alive, standing with the effeminate man, and her cultivation has risen from early to mid Foundation Establishment. Her former bewitching aura is gone, replaced by cold dignity.
- The veiled woman is revealed to be a Core Formation cultivator who fought hard in the Moon-Masking Sect battle; she is currently weakened after mutual destruction with a Ghost Spirit Sect Core Formation expert.
- The effeminate man and Wang Chan exchange verbal jabs, each revealing the other’s agenda: Wang Chan identifies the “Moon-Devouring Yin Absorption Technique” as the man’s real goal, aiming to drain the veiled woman’s yin for a breakthrough.
- The chapter ends with the veiled woman attacking both pursuers in fury, and the two retreating in caution.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Moon-Devouring Yin Absorption Technique (玄月吸阴功)** — This is not a generic cultivation method, but a specific dual-cultivation art from the Pleasure Union Sect. In Chinese xianxia tradition, “yin absorption” techniques represent the most predatory wing of dual cultivation, where one partner (typically the male) drains the other’s foundational yin essence for personal advancement, leaving the victim weakened or crippled. The fact that the effeminate man is pursuing a high-level Core Formation practitioner for this purpose reveals both his ambition and his ruthlessness: forcing a higher-realm cultivator into submission is an extreme act.

**The Veiled Woman’s Voice and Han Li’s Recognition** — The text mentions that her voice sounds like Nan Gong Wan’s but is hoarser. The original Chinese uses “声音清寒无比” (voice clear and bitingly cold) followed by Han Li’s astonishment. The comparison matters: Nan Gong Wan is not just Han Li’s first woman, but a figure associated with the Maiden Rebirth Art (索女轮回功), which can alter the practitioner’s age and voice upon certain disruptions. The hoarseness could be a clue—perhaps this is indeed her, post-disruption after the battle.

**Master and Disciple Reversal: Dong Xuan’er** — Dong Xuan’er’s transformation from a mischievous, bewitching girl to a cold, dignified woman standing with a demonic-path partner is a classic “fallen disciple” trope in xianxia. The fact that her cultivation has advanced while her personality has flattened into something almost mechanical strongly suggests she has been subjected to a mental or spiritual reprogramming technique. The effeminate man from Yanling Fort, who has his own unsettling charm, is likely her handler. Her presence here is a deliberate narrative twist: she is not a prisoner, but an ally—at least for now.

### Maiden Rebirth Art (索女轮回功)

A cycle-based cultivation technique practiced by Nan Gong Wan; initiates a periodic age regression and cultivation reset, making its practitioner vulnerable during certain phases.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in RMJI’s “antagonist chessboard”: Dong Xuan’er’s reappearance is not a happy reunion, but a deeply unsettling transformation. Han Li’s observation is clinical—he notes her cultivation increase and her change in temperament, but he does not immediately rush out to save her or resolve the situation. This is consistent with the Mortal Stream law: do not act without sufficient information and a favorable risk-reward ratio. The fact that the veiled woman might be Nan Gong Wan (but might not be) is the other major puzzle piece. The chapter ends mid-confrontation, leaving readers with three burning questions: Who is the veiled woman? What happened to Dong Xuan’er? And will Han Li intervene? Answers are likely deferred until the next chapter, which promises a high-stakes standoff over the mine.
