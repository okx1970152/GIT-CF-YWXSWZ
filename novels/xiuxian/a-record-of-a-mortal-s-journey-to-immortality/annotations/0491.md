## Chapter Overview

The treasure vault erupts into chaos as the Void Heaven Cauldron spits out a fully transformed magical beast—a double-headed fire wolf with a mind of its own. The Nascent Soul old monsters, who moments ago were locked in a theatrical sparring match, instantly drop their pretenses and converge on the prize with murderous intent. But this is no ordinary artifact; the wolf deflects a Nascent Soul's full-power grab and takes flight, headed straight for the crowd of Core Formation cultivators below. As Wu Chou makes his move and the Profound Bone Saint intervenes, the stage is set for a brutal scramble where status means nothing and opportunity means everything.

## Key Plot Points

- The double-headed fire wolf emerges from the Void Heaven Cauldron, displaying full transformation and independent action—a sign of a top-tier treasure.
- Green Bamboo Hermit’s azure hand strike is repelled by the wolf’s red-yellow light shield, confirming the treasure’s defensive capability.
- Wan Tianming clones himself to intercept, but the real battle is over who can claim the prize first, not who can kill it.
- The Nascent Soul cultivators escalate from sparring to genuine conflict, unleashing secret arts and reckless attacks.
- The fire wolf escapes downward, fleeing toward Han Li, the Profound Bone Saint, and the other Core Formation cultivators.
- Wu Chou acts first, binding the wolf with his Profound Yin Art, but fails to seize it.
- The Profound Bone Saint makes his move, spitting out a green silk net that covers both Wu Chou’s pythons and the fire wolf, signaling his intention to claim the prize.
- An author’s note (as Qingjia Tiao) explains the holiday schedule disruption and apologizes for the single update.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Transformed Treasures (化形之宝)** — This is a major tier-up in artifact logic. Most treasures in *RMJI* are tools that obey a cultivator’s will. But a *transformed treasure* can take on a living form, act independently, and even defend itself. This places it in a class far above ordinary magic tools or even most Talisman Treasures. The fact that it withstood a Nascent Soul’s direct grab is the system confirming: this is not a weapon you wield—it’s a near-sentient asset you negotiate with.

**Nascent Soul Sparring vs. Real Fighting** — Han Li notices that the earlier battle felt like a contest of skill. That’s because, at the Nascent Soul level, full-commitment combat carries existential risk. These old monsters are too valuable to kill each other over a single treasure—until a transformed treasure appears. The moment the fire wolf emerges, the gloves come off, and the sparring becomes a real scramble. This is the Mortal Stream’s economic logic in action: risk scales with reward.

**The Qingjia Tiao (请假条)** — The author’s note at the end is a standard fixture of web novel serialization, especially around holidays. It’s not narrative content, but it reveals the real-world constraints behind the story. In the translation, it preserves the author-reader relationship and the transparent, apologetic tone of the original.

### Qingjia Tiao (请假条)

An author’s note explaining a delay or disruption in a web novel’s update schedule, preserving the reader-author relationship.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in escalation. The first half lulls you into thinking the Nascent Souls are just showing off—then the fire wolf appears, and the entire tone shifts. Han Li’s observation that their fighting ‘didn’t feel like a life-or-death struggle’ is the bait. The switch is immediate and brutal when the treasure appears. Pay attention to Han Li’s hesitation: he nearly acts but pulls back, weighing the risk of Extreme Yin’s wrath. That moment of calculation—the pause between opportunity and consequence—is pure Mortal Stream. Also, the Profound Bone Saint’s intervention is a quiet reminder that he’s not just a spectator; he’s been waiting for his moment. Watch him.
