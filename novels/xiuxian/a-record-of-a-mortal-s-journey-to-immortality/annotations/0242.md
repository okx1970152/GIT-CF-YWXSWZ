## Chapter Overview
After four long years of seclusion, Han Li's cave finally gets a visitor—but not before we get a front-row seat to his meticulous, resource-hoarding planning. Fresh off breaking through to Foundation Establishment and getting the Azure Origin Sword Art, Han Li immediately identifies his core bottleneck: spending spirit stones burns his secret advantage (accelerated spirit herbs), while fighting without good gear gets him killed. His solution? Become a talisman crafter. Not out of artistic passion, but cold, hard logistics. Blank talisman paper is made from spirit herbs—and that’s exactly what the Heaven Vial excels at. The chapter closes with a mysterious blue-clad scholar arriving at his formation with a message. The quiet storm is about to get a visitor.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li analyzes the Heaven Vial’s green liquid one final time: no matter how diluted, it still causes explosive death in test animals. He shelves the mystery.
- He identifies his primary combat issue: mid-grade talismans are lifesavers in Foundation Establishment fights, but buying them costs a fortune (dozens of spirit stones each).
- Han Li devises a production loop: grow spirit herbs with the Heaven Vial → make blank talisman paper → craft mid-grade talismans. This bypasses the material bottleneck that normal talisman masters face.
- The only remaining constraint: he must *learn* mid-grade spells before he can seal them into talismans. He acknowledges his terrible talent at elemental magic but believes he can master at least a few.
- He also plans for his worst-case scenario: if he has to dissolve his cultivation to restart the Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art, a stockpile of talismans will keep him alive during that vulnerable period.
- Over several days, he gathers ingredients for one ancient formula (Qi Refining Powder) and stockpiles talisman paper materials.
- He seals his cave dwelling and begins four years of closed-door cultivation.
- A blue-robed Confucian scholar arrives at his formation, identifies himself as a yellow maple valley disciple sent by “Uncle-Master Lei,” and tosses in a sound transmission talisman before leaving.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Why talisman crafting is a logistics hack:** In the *Mortal Stream* economy, most talisman masters are limited by material supply—spirit herbs with sufficient spiritual qi take years or decades to mature naturally. Han Li’s Heaven Vial shortcuts this completely. But the novel is careful not to hand him everything: he still has to *learn* the mid-grade spells, and his talent for elemental cultivation is canonically awful. This creates a realistic cost for his clever workaround.

**Spirit herb → talisman paper chain:** Low and mid-grade blank talisman paper is made from aged spirit herbs. High-grade paper requires demon beast hide. Han Li can produce low-to-mid-grade paper infinitely with the vial, but high-grade remains out of reach—a classic *Mortal Stream* resource ceiling that prevents unlimited power growth.

**The four-year skip:** Time skips are a signature *Mortal Stream* technique. Unlike Western fantasy where every training montage is shown blow-by-blow, xianxia often isolates the protagonist for years, then cuts to the results. This preserves the feeling of immense effort without bogging down the pacing. The results speak for themselves: the room is littered with talismans of every low-grade type.

## Reading Guide
- **Pay close attention to Han Li’s escape-plan mindset**—he’s already preparing for the possibility that his cultivation reset might leave him crippled mid-transition. This isn’t paranoia; it’s *Mortal Stream* survival doctrine. In a world where enemies can knock on your door at any moment, preparation is the only armor that never rusts.
- The blue-robed scholar’s reaction to the formation is a subtle flex: even a fellow Foundation Establishment disciple considers attacking it for fun, then backs down. This tells us Han Li’s defensive array is already famous enough to discourage casual probing.
- **This chapter sets up Han Li’s biggest long-term weakness**—his terrible talent at elemental spells will haunt him for the entire Foundation Establishment stage. Don’t expect him to suddenly become a spell-slinging genius; his strength lies in preparation, resource conversion, and that little green bottle.
- The mention of the “Gold Bamboo Brush” and Han Yunzhi is a rare moment of softness. It passes quickly, but it’s a marker that Han Li hasn’t completely suppressed his humanity—he simply files it away and moves on.
