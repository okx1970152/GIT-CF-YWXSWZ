## Chapter Overview

The calm before the storm is over. The Six Demonic Sects have arrived at the mine, and the Seven Sects’ defensive line is about to be put to the test. With the Four Fiends Formation as their anchor, Xuan Yue’s squad tries to hold off a two-pronged assault from the Heavenly Fiend Sect and the Devil Flame Sect. At first, it looks manageable—just a standard siege. Then the white specters appear, and Lü Tianmeng’s face goes pale. The real nightmare is only beginning.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the invaders finally showing their faces. The Heavenly Fiend Sect, led by a late Foundation Establishment old man in yellow, charges straight into the formation’s perimeter. The Devil Flame Sect, under a plain-looking young woman in red, hangs back. These two factions have very different tactics. The Heavenly Fiend Sect goes for brute engagement, while the Devil Flame Sect takes its time, setting up a formation of their own.

Han Li joins the counterattack, using his Golden Cicada Mother-Child Blades to lock down a mid-level opponent. The fight feels almost routine—Han Li even finds time to glance around and assess the overall situation. But then Lü Tianmeng spots the red-robed cultivators pulling out large crimson banners with sun-and-golden-crow motifs. He lets out a warning cry that shakes the entire line.

A quick attempt to stop them with two flying centipedes fails spectacularly. The centipedes are sliced into pieces by invisible silver threads, and the following magic tools get entangled and neutralized. The culprit? A squadron of white, translucent figures standing in front of the Devil Flame Sect’s formation—spectral assassins that are nearly immune to normal attacks. They move like ghosts and cut like reapers.

The chapter ends with those red banners already flickering with the first traces of blue flame. The Azure Sun Demon Flame is about to be unleashed, and there’s nothing the Seven Sects can do to stop it.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Formations as Force Multipliers**

The Four Fiends Formation is a classic example of the “formation” (阵法) trope in xianxia warfare. Unlike individual combat, sect-scale conflict relies heavily on pre-prepared defensive formations that multiply the power of lower-level cultivators. The formation doesn’t just block attacks—it lets Qi Condensation disciples survive blows that would instantly kill them in open field combat. This is why sieges in cultivation novels are so often about formations: breaking the formation is the first real victory.

**The Blazing Flame Devils (狂焰修士)**

These are not a separate sect but a specialized unit within the Devil Flame Sect. The term 狂焰 (kuang yan) means “wild flame” or “blazing flame,” and these cultivators are trained to synchronize their spiritual power through ritual banners to release a massive, coordinated flame attack. In Chinese martial fiction, the “ritual banner formation” (旗阵) is a recurring tactical device: a group of weaker cultivators, acting in perfect alignment, can punch far above their weight class. The golden crow on the banners is a deliberate solar symbol—link the flame to the yang principle, making it both destructive and hard to counter with standard water-based defenses.

**The White Specters (白色人影)**

These translucent guardians are almost certainly refined spirits or ghostly puppets, a staple of demonic-path sects in the xianxia tradition. They are immune to most magic and physical damage, but their offensive repertoire is limited to silver-thread entanglement and sword slashes. This makes them excellent stationary defenders but poor pursuers. In storytelling terms, they are a “speed bump with teeth”—they buy enough time for the real threat (the flame ritual) to finish casting.

### Cloud-Wing Bird (云翅鸟)

A swift messenger bird trained for aerial surveillance; appears in earlier chapters as Doctor Mo’s spy.

## Reading Guide

You can almost feel Han Li’s brain running the numbers in this chapter. He’s not panicking. He’s scanning, assessing, and filing away new data points. The white specters? Noted. The Azure Sun Demon Flame? Filed under “new threat, must avoid.” This is vintage Mortal Stream combat logic: don’t get emotional, don’t try to be a hero, just keep calculating.

But the structure here is also a beautiful example of how *RMJI* escalates tension through information asymmetry. The Seven Sects don’t know what’s coming until it’s too late. Lü Tianmeng, the only one with prior experience against the Six Sects, has to shout a warning that no one else fully understands until the blue flames start flickering. The reader is right there with Han Li—seeing the pieces but not yet the full picture.

Also, pay attention to the way Han Li’s fight is described. He’s holding back. Using a familiar tool, controlling the engagement at low effort, leaving mental bandwidth for surveillance. If you’ve been reading closely, you know this is his standard operating procedure: never show all your cards. The question is whether he has a counter for the demon flame.

This is siege warfare in the cultivation world. Formations, ritual spells, ghostly guardians, and lots of disposable Qi Condensation troops. The real battle hasn’t even started yet.
