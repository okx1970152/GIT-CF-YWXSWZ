## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the calm before the storm, fellow Daoists. Chapter 214 is the ultimate “preparation montage” we’ve been waiting for—but in true Mortal Stream fashion, it’s anything but boring. Han Li has finally reached the Earth Fire room inside Yellow Maple Valley, the most secure and isolated workspace a low-level cultivator could dream of. With his stash of spirit herbs and the mysterious Heaven Vial’s accelerant at his disposal, he stands on the verge of attempting something that would make any Qi Condensation disciple’s eyes bulge: refining the Foundation Establishment Pill. But this is not a chapter about glory—it’s about sweat, patience, and a healthy dose of self-aware realism. Our favorite paranoid herb farmer knows he’s a rookie alchemist, and he’s planned accordingly. The stage is set, the tools are checked, and the Earth Fire roars to life. Get ready for the quiet, methodical grind that separates the haves from the have-nots in this brutal world.

## Key Plot Points

- The ugly man, delighted by Han Li’s generous tip of a mid-grade spirit stone, leads him through the wu-shi-constructed passage into the vast circular hall containing thirty-six Earth Fire rooms.
- Han Li is assigned Room Number 19—the second-best pill-refining room in the facility, as Number 18 is already occupied by a senior.
- The room’s layout is detailed: a wu-shi round platform with eight lifelike fire-dragon heads for spraying Earth Fire, four wall-mounted gourds containing Mars Fire Sand to amplify flame power, and a sealed jade token that cuts off all external contact when the door is shut.
- Han Li learns that only several Core Formation cultivators working together could breach the sealed door—perfect for his secretive pill-refining plan.
- He practices controlling the Earth Fire, adjusting the flame thickness and height until he achieves mastery, then rests half a day to reach peak condition.
- Han Li acknowledges his rookie status as an alchemist: he knows the success rate for even grandmasters is only around 50%, so he has prepared over a hundred batches of ingredients to develop his skills through repeated attempts.
- He retrieves the silver-threaded tripod, suspends it over the platform with a floating spell, ignites the Earth Fire, and methodically adds all the herb powders. The first step is completed without error.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

### Earth Fire: The Cultivator’s Industrial Furnace

Unlike Western fantasy where magic is often fueled by personal mana alone, xianxia introduces *infrastructure*: cultivated resources, spirit veins, and specialized sites that provide high-grade energy for specific tasks. Earth Fire (地火) is a perfect example. It is a naturally occurring subterranean flame, far more powerful and stable than a cultivator’s personal true fire. But harnessing it requires advanced engineering—here, rooms made of *wu-shi* (坞石), a rare heat-resistant material imported from distant western kingdoms. This is the cultivation world’s version of an industrial furnace, and it underscores a key theme: civilization and institutionalized power matter even for immortal aspirants. No lone-wolf cultivator, no matter how talented, can replicate the output of a sect’s Earth Fire facility without years of preparation.

### The Economics of Spirit Stones

The ugly man’s delight at receiving a *mid-grade* spirit stone touches on a subtle but critical economic reality. Yes, the official exchange rate is 100 low-grade to 1 mid-grade, but in practice, mid-grade stones are hoarded because they allow faster qi absorption. This creates a premium market where holders of mid-grade stones can wield disproportionate influence. Han Li’s choice to tip with a mid-grade stone is not just generosity—it’s a calculated display of wealth that buys immediate, preferential service.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream pacing. There are no explosions, no ambushes, no betrayal—just a quiet afternoon of preparation. And yet, the tension is palpable. Han Li is about to attempt something that could change his trajectory forever, and he knows the stakes. His decision to refine pills one by one, accepting early failures as tuition fees, is the kind of cold, rational planning that defines the genre. The chapter also reinforces a crucial distinction: Han Li is not a genius alchemist. He is a scrounger, a pragmatist, and a man who uses his insurance policy (over a hundred batches of ingredients) to brute-force his way through a skill wall. For readers, this is the calm before the breakthrough—or the crash. Either way, the foundation is being laid, and it’s built on Earth Fire, jade tokens, and a paranoia that would make a squirrel proud.
