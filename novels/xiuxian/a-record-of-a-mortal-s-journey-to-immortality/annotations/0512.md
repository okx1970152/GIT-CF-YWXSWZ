## Chapter Overview

Han Li, fresh off his conversation with two Star Palace Core Formation cultivators, decides to accompany them—then immediately attacks. What follows is a surgical, two-versus-one slaughter that showcases the raw power differential a well-prepared cultivator with *genuine* ancient treasures can wield against opponents stuck with self-refined or borderline equipment. The chapter moves fast, the kills are clinical, and the reveal of the Five Elements Ring’s containment ability is a game-changer. Oh, and someone’s watching from the water the whole time. This is Mortal Stream combat at its most transactional: don’t negotiate unless you’re ready to execute.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li agrees to follow the Star Palace cultivators, then immediately launches a preemptive strike.
- The Confucian scholar belatedly identifies Han Li’s Five Elements Ring as an ancient treasure—too late.
- Han Li deploys the Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords in a split-pincer formation: six beams merge against the burly man’s seal, six more attack from the flank.
- The burly man’s runic barrier holds against the early-stage swords, confirming Han Li’s swords are still underdeveloped without the Evil-Slaying Divine Lightning.
- The Confucian scholar’s Illusion Inkstone generates black mist-birds, but Han Li counters instantly with the white jade basket, absorbing them utterly.
- The Five Elements Ring appears silently on the Confucian scholar’s body, suppressing his mana completely—he dies without using a single offensive technique.
- The burly man tries to flee, abandoning his natal treasure, but the Five Elements Ring pursues and binds him; Han Li bisects him with a sword beam.
- Han Li loots both corpses and claims the seal and inkstone as trophies.
- He reveals a hidden observer in the sea below and calls them out.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Power Differential of Ancient Treasures (古宝).** In the RMJI cultivation world, most Core Formation cultivators will never own a true ancient treasure. Those that do are either backed by a superpower, exceptionally lucky, or—in Han Li’s case—raiding ancient ruins like the Blood Forbidden Land and the Void Heaven Palace. The gap between a “self-refined magic tool” and an “ancient treasure” is not incremental; it is often absolute. A binding-type treasure like the Five Elements Ring can trivialize a fight against an opponent of equal or even slightly higher cultivation, as seen here when the Confucian scholar is sealed without ever deploying his full power. This reinforces the Mortal Stream’s core truth: *equipment is a multiplier, and the right equipment is a win condition*.

**The Five Elements Ring (五行环) — Invisibility & Mana Lockdown.** Han Li’s new acquisition from the Void Heaven Palace arc demonstrates two devastating properties: true spatial invisibility (it reappears on the target without being tracked by spiritual sense), and full mana suppression (the victim’s mana disperses immediately upon binding). These are not common properties for binding tools—most can be sensed or resisted. The ring’s performance here establishes it as a top-tier Core Formation trump card that can bypass standard defensive measures.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *palate cleanser of pure efficiency*. After the chaos of the Void Heaven Palace, Han Li is back in the open sea, and he’s operating at a new gear. Notice how the fight is framed: he doesn’t out-cultivate his opponents; he *out-equips* them. The Confucian scholar dies without casting a single spell. The burly man abandons his natal treasure just to flee, and still dies. That’s not brute force—that’s a setup executed with cold precision. Pay attention to the tease at the end: who’s in the water? In the Mortal Stream, no observation is ever purely coincidental, and no reveal is thrown away.

## Worldview & Analysis

**The Rise of the Aftermath Economy.** Han Li’s looting pattern here—two storage pouches, a seal, an inkstone—is a reminder that in the Mortal Stream, victory is incomplete without asset extraction. Every Core Formation corpse is a loot box, and Han Li has just won a mini-jackpot. The seal and inkstone are both usable assets (or trade capital), and the storage pouches likely contain maps, materials, or information. A reader should track Han Li’s inventory growth as a secondary plot line: *every kill funds the next breakthrough*.

**The Cost of Underdeveloped Techniques.** Han Li’s Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords, without the Evil-Slaying Divine Lightning, fail to penetrate the burly man’s barrier. This is a subtle but important worldbuilding beat: even a superior cultivation technique takes time to mature. Han Li is not invincible—he is *prepared*, and his win condition depends on his ancient treasures covering his swords’ current weakness. That is the Mortal Stream’s tactical signature: know your gap, and carry something that fills it.
