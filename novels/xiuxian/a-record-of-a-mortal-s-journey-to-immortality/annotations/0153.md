## Chapter Overview
Welcome, fellow Daoists, to a brand-new arc of logistics and bureaucracy! After the bloody, high-octane survival of the Wild Wolf Gang arc, our boy Han Li is back where real growth happens: standing in a bureaucratic queue, trying to get his hands on some sweet, sweet knowledge. This chapter is a masterclass in “how to survive in an immortal sect without being a genius.” We get our first real look at a massive sect infrastructure—the Yue Lu Hall, the sect’s central archive and crafting hub—and the *layer cake* of security that protects it. But more importantly, we watch Han Li deploy his most powerful technique: strategic negotiation and the art of the half-truth. He’s traded a year of free labor for a single entry pass, and he’s about to learn that even getting past the front door of this place requires navigating a social minefield of Foundation Establishment gatekeepers. The tone shifts from martial brutality to cool-headed institutional maneuvering, but the stakes are just as real: access to knowledge is access to power.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li arrives at the hidden cave-gate of Yue Lu Hall on Wu Jun Shan, immediately sensing multiple hidden sentries who are likely above his cultivation layer.
- He triggers the outer formation, revealing two masked Foundation Establishment-level Red-Clad Disciples who serve as gatekeepers.
- The disciples are curt and dismissive until Han Li presents his guarantor token from Senior Martial Uncle Ma, an eccentric alchemist who rarely sponsors anyone.
- Han Li explains he earned the token by agreeing to tend the old alchemist’s garden for a full year without pay—a classic “deferred payment” strategy in the Mortal Stream.
- The disciples, amused by the story, grant him entry, teleporting him via a formation into the main hall, leaving him awestruck by the scale of the sect’s inner facilities.
- The chapter establishes the strict hierarchical rules of the sect: non-Foundation disciples need guarantors, and every trip to the archives costs a spirit stone in teleportation fees after the first freebie.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Teleportation Formations (传送阵).** In xianxia, teleportation formations are a staple of high-level infrastructure, but in *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey*, they are treated with the same mundane practicality as a subway turnstile. The process is described with technical detail (spirit stones powering the array, a brief disorienting sensation), and the cost is openly discussed: one low-grade spirit stone per trip. This is not a mystical experience; it’s a fee-based transit system, reinforcing the world’s obsession with resource economy. The fact that Han Li, a mere Qi Condensation disciple, gets one free ride as a “first-time customer” is a subtle touch of *face* (give face to a junior on their first visit), a deeply ingrained Chinese social custom. Also, note the *Red-Clad Disciples*—at Foundation Establishment, they are the sect’s “middle management,” powerful enough to be condescending but not so powerful that they can afford to ignore a token from a senior alchemist. The entire interaction is a delicate dance of status and utility.

## Reading Guide
This is a **setup chapter**, and in the Mortal Stream, setup chapters are treasure troves of tactical information. Don’t skim it! Watch how Han Li handles being talked down to. He doesn’t bristle, doesn’t assert his pride. He stays polite, delivers a self-deprecating (and partially true) explanation, and lets the gatekeepers feel superior. This is a core survival skill: making the powerful feel good about helping you. Also, note the price he’s paying: one year of free labor. That’s a massive investment for what is essentially a library card. It tells you how desperate Han Li is for knowledge and how much he values the Heaven Vial’s potential synergy with alchemy. This chapter sets the stage for a slower, more calculating section of the story, where Han Li’s war is fought with paperwork, bribes, and careful reading. Get ready for the birth of Han Li, the master pharmacist.

## Deep Dive: The Favor Economy of an Immortal Sect
One of the most fascinating mechanics introduced here is the “guarantor system.” A low-level disciple cannot simply walk into a restricted area; they need a higher-status member to vouch for them. This turns sect relationships into a currency. Why did Senior Martial Uncle Ma agree to guarantee Han Li? Not out of kindness—the old alchemist explicitly didn’t want to. Han Li had to buy that favor with a full year of unpaid labor. This is the *guanxi* (关系, social network) economy of a xianxia sect in action. Every favor is a debt, every debt has a price, and the price is usually paid in time, loyalty, or resources. Han Li understands this instinctively; he doesn’t resent the transaction, he simply accounts for it as another cost on his path to immortality.
