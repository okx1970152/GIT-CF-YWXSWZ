## Chapter Overview
Welcome back to the forge, fellow Daoists. This chapter is pure, concentrated artifact-crafting porn: Han Li, our favorite paranoid accountant of cultivation, finally moves from gathering materials to the *actual* furnace work. After months of refining Divine Thunder Bamboo down to its pure liquid essence and setting up a nightmarishly complex spell formation, he begins to shape the famous Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords—**seventy-two** of them, no less. But before we dive into that, the chapter throws in a quiet, surprising detour into the Gold Devourer Beetle situation, and Han Li confronts a grim logistics problem that will haunt his long-term plans. This is a “quiet victory” phase in the truest Mortal Stream style: no bloodshed, no dramatic showdowns, just methodical, high-stakes technical work with a terrifying margin for error. Get your spirit stones ready; this is where the real investment begins.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li finishes refining the last five stalks of Divine Thunder Bamboo into pure liquid after three months of grueling effort with his natal flame.
- He spends half a month setting up a hyper-complex spell formation inside his secluded chamber, following the golden page’s instructions to the letter. A single misdrawn rune could ruin everything.
- After testing the formation with mid-grade spirit stones, he leaves the chamber to rest and restore his mental state for the final phase.
- Outside, he discovers the Gold Devourer Beetles have re-entered their cannibalistic evolution phase—a pleasant surprise.
- But the joy is short-lived: Han Li realizes that with his limited Green Liquid supply for force-growing Rainbow Skirt Grass, evolving tens of thousands of beetles to their full golden form could take decades. He decides to focus on cultivating a smaller elite core instead.
- After a month of rest and two days of incense-fueled mental purification, Han Li begins the real forging.
- He activates the formation, uses spirit threads to guide the bamboo liquid into the fire pillars, and spends a full day and night shaping all seventy-two liquid sword blanks with his spiritual sense—a near-crippling strain.
- He adds essence blood, silver powder, black powder, and other auxiliary materials to each sword blank.
- The chapter ends with Han Li beginning to inscribe miniature spell formations onto each sword one by one, following the golden page’s diagrams with painstaking precision.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Let’s talk about the **Crafting Process** in xianxia—because this chapter is a masterclass in how the genre treats artifact forging not as a quick magic moment, but as a serious **engineering discipline** with real costs. Han Li isn’t just waving a wand; he’s burning months of his natal flame (丹火), consuming rare materials, draining his mental energy, and risking a total loss if any rune is off by a hair. This mirrors the actual Daoist alchemical tradition (外丹术 / *waidan*), where refining elixirs required strict timing, pure ingredients, and a profound understanding of elemental interactions—except here, the alchemical vessel is the cultivator’s own body and the furnace is a spell formation. The “golden page” (金页) is a classic xianxia McGuffin: an inherited, near-scriptural knowledge source that the protagonist must follow blindly because he doesn’t fully understand the underlying theory. It’s a great narrative device that keeps the forging feeling both authentic and perilous.

Also, the **seventy-two** number is not random. Seventy-two (七十二) is an auspicious number in Chinese folk tradition, frequently associated with transformations, earthly powers, and the *Seventy-Two Earthly Sha* in Daoist lore. Han Li forging exactly this many swords hints at a deeper numerological significance in his ultimate sword formation.

## Reading Guide
This is the kind of chapter that divides Mortal Stream readers. If you came here for non-stop brawls and narrow escapes, the forge scenes might feel slow—but if you’re in it for the *long game*, this is where the magic happens. Pay attention to Han Li’s **resource allocation mindset**: the moment he realizes the beetle evolution is logistically impossible at scale, he immediately calculates a smaller, more efficient path rather than lamenting his luck. That’s pure Mortal Stream problem-solving in action. Also note the **physical toll**: shaping seventy-two swords with spiritual sense gives him a headache. This is a subtle but important reality check—even a Core Formation cultivator has limits, and pushing them carries costs. Finally, savor the contrast between the soaring ambition (seventy-two flying swords!) and the grinding, mundane reality (weeks of meditation, rune-by-rune inscription). That tension is the heart of *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey to Immortality*: grand dreams built one painful, methodical step at a time.
