## Chapter Overview

Welcome back to the Ghost Mist, fellow Daoists, where the survival calculus just got a whole lot sharper. This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s true battlefield: the cold, unspoken negotiation of risk, trust, and walking away. Han Li doesn’t just push forward—he forces his temporary allies to stare down their own mortality and choose their path. And when one of them folds under the pressure, there’s no drama, no betrayal speech, just a quiet acknowledgment that the world is what it is. The chapter then shifts into a lore dump about the legendary Void Heaven Palace, delivered with the grim pragmatism of a man who knows that knowledge is the only armor that costs nothing to carry.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li, Zi Ling, and Ge Li encounter a skeleton of a near-Core Formation mid-stage cultivator, armed with a cold-attribute claw and a flame-repelling garment—confirming the “ghost” they face is a top-tier threat.
- Han Li issues a blunt warning: the ghost is likely close to the Ghost King realm, and if they want to turn back, now is the time. Ge Li, after visible hesitation, decides to retreat, citing his aim to collect Longevity Fruits rather than fight for his life.
- Zi Ling, in contrast, declares she will press forward even if it means eternal damnation—she has no path to her Core without this risk.
- After Ge Li leaves, Han Li buries the skeleton, explaining he hopes someone would do the same for him; he takes the Flame Repelling Garment as his “burial fee.”
- As they walk, Han Li probes Zi Ling’s knowledge of the Void Heaven Palace. She explains it descends once every 300 years, is filled with ancient treasures, and has a three-stage trial system to reach the inner halls—where the true valuables are kept.
- The chapter ends with Han Li detecting the black-robed cultivator already locked in battle with the ghost. Zi Ling struggles to resist the ghost’s soul-stirring cry as she catches up, her control nearly broken.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **Void Heaven Palace (虚天殿)** is a classic xianxia dungeon—a floating jade palace on a fixed 300-year cycle. Its trial structure (three major “gates” to the inner hall) mirrors a common Chinese narrative rhythm: a series of escalating tests that filter out the unworthy. The term **“Ghost King” (鬼王)** refers to a high-tier spectral entity in Chinese folklore, often a ruler of the undead; here, its proximity signals a challenge that can kill even Core Formation cultivators. The **Flame Repelling Garment** is a sign that the dead cultivator came prepared for elemental hazards, yet still fell—underscoring that no amount of gear guarantees survival in *RMJI*’s Dark Forest.

### Burial Fee

A cold, transactional logic: taking a dead cultivator’s belongings in exchange for giving them a proper burial.

## Reading Guide

This is a quiet chapter, but don’t let the lack of explosions fool you—it’s doing heavy narrative lifting. The departure of Ge Li is a clear signal that the Mortal Stream rewards self-preservation over loyalty. Han Li’s decision to bury the skeleton and take the garment isn’t morality; it’s a cold, transactional gesture that acknowledges the unspoken contract between all cultivators: we are all walking corpses waiting for an empty grave, so help a brother out while you can. The lore dump on the Void Heaven Palace is essential—memorize the three-trial structure, because Han Li is already planning his own route. Finally, note how Han Li’s calm questioning of Zi Ling is as much a probe as any spirit sense sweep: he’s testing her resolve and reliability, not just satisfying curiosity.
