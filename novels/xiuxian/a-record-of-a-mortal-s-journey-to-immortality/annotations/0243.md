## Chapter Overview

Get ready, fellow cultivators, because Han Li’s carefully laid cultivation highway has hit a terrifying dead end! In this chapter, our favorite cautious alchemist emerges from a four-year seclusion with a mountain of talismans and a nasty surprise: his body has built up a resistance to the miracle drug that was supposed to carry him through Foundation Establishment. It’s a classic ‘Mortal Stream’ punch to the gut—no shortcut lasts forever. And just to top off his already lousy mood, his master barges in with a mysterious summons. A strange, murderous female cultivator is waiting, and she seems to be giving Han Li the "buyer's inspection." Who is she? And why does she look so satisfied? The external world is re-entering Han Li’s life, and it never brings good news.

## Key Plot Points

- A time skip of four years establishes Han Li’s disciplined life in seclusion. It’s a quiet victory phase: he has nearly mastered high-grade elementary talismans, built a secret inner garden for accelerated herb farming, and managed a third pass of the Azure Origin Sword Art.
- The chapter ruthlessly introduces the hard limit of ‘pill resistance.’ His overpowered Qi Condensation Powder becomes completely ineffective by the fourth year, leaving him desperately searching for new formulas and methods.
- Han Li’s internal analysis of the pill failure is crucial: he links it to the unique intensity of his medicinal behavior, acknowledging that no cultivator in history has ever consumed such potent pills this frequently.
- Li Huayuan’s summoning with a transmission talisman ends Han Li’s seclusion. The context is ominous: a new powerful character, the cold and murderous ‘Red Feather’ cultivator, is sizing him up in the master’s cave.
- Han Li, the ever-pragmatic observer, immediately detects the tension: his master seems to be *respecting* this new woman, which hints at a higher cultivation level or extreme political power.
- A secondary social note: the meeting is not mediated by the familiar Fourth Senior Brother, but by a new face—the rude and handsome Sixth Disciple Wu Xuan.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces a potent concept in the 'Mortal Stream' cultivation system: **Drug Resistance (抗药性)**. In many cultivation novels, magical pills are treated as unending power sources, limited only by the user's ability to refine them. *RMJI* brutally undercuts this fantasy. The logic here is simple: a mortal body, even a cultivator's, has a limit to how much foreign energy it can process. Han Li's body has built up a tolerance to the specific medicine, and the solution is not "train harder," but "shop for a different drug dealer." This forces innovation and resourcefulness over brute-force leveling.

Another milestone in this chapter is the mention of the **Beauty-Preserving Pill (定颜丹)**. Han Li refined and consumed one. In the Xianxia world, these are not just cosmetic vanity items. They signal a character's utter commitment to the long game and a deep awareness of cultivation’s ultimate cost: time. For a character whose entire drive is *survival*, not romance, taking such a pill proves he wants to avoid the weakening effects of old age itself. It's a powerful, quiet statement about his character.

### Drug Resistance (抗药性)

A concept in the Mortal Stream where a cultivator’s body builds tolerance to a specific pill, causing it to lose all effect over time.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the perfect example of the 'Mortal Stream' philosophy of logistics. There is no grand battle, no life-or-death escape, and no heavenly treasure. It’s a pure *maintenance* chapter, and it's absolutely riveting. Pay close attention to how Han Li plans his next move: he's already thinking of *multiple* ancient pill formulas to rotate in case the next one also fails. This shows a deep, cold understanding that the universe is designed to break your tools, so you must always have spares. Also, keep an eye on the Red Feather cultivator. Her "checking him out" is not about looks; it’s about assessment. In this world, a senior's sudden interest in a low-level cultivator is rarely a good sign.
