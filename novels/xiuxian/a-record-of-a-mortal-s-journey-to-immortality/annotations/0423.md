## Chapter Overview
Okay, buckle up, fellow Daoists, because after all that hustle in Heaven Star City, Han Li is finally leaving the nest! This chapter is the calm before the storm—a classic Mortal Stream transition where our boy weighs risk against reward, reluctantly says yes to a favor, and then sets off on a two-month voyage to a mysterious, tree-killing island. The vibe shifts from city politics to expedition logistics, and we meet a new batch of expedition allies, each with their own agenda and attitude. Think of it as the “assembling the party” phase, but with zero heroic speeches and plenty of quiet calculation.

## Key Plot Points
- Jin Qing proposes a trip to an ancient cultivator ruin. Han Li, ever the cautious cucumber, asks for two days to think it over instead of jumping in headfirst.
- Han Li’s internal conflict is laid bare: he doesn’t want to go (risky, his swords aren't ready), but he owes Jin Qing a favor, and curiosity nibbles at him. This is classic Mortal Stream decision-making—spreadsheets, not emotions.
- He accepts. The expedition party of three (Han Li, Jin Qing, Qu Hun) departs immediately.
- After two months of travel, they reach a massive, barren island. No spirit veins, no trees—just endless yellow earth. The very abnormality of the place is treated as a *good* sign for finding a hidden ruin.
- Jin Qing leads them to a towering earthen mountain. A sudden, localized sandstorm greets them as they approach, which is conveniently ignored by the cultivators’ protective barriers.
- At the mountain’s foot, they meet the rest of the team: Hu Yue (the polite organizer), Shi Die (the arrogant formation expert), and Brother Jian (the silent powerhouse). Shi Die immediately lays down her terms—she picks first loot and won’t fight beyond the formation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter gives us a peek into the “expedition meta” of the cultivation world. Ancient ruins (古修士遗址) are a staple trope—think of them as procedurally generated dungeons, but with better loot tables and no respawn. They’re leftovers from ancient civilizations or powerful cultivators who kicked the bucket, and their value is a total gamble. The Chaos Star Sea is littered with them, which tells you how old and bloody this setting is.

Also, note the power dynamic in team assembly. You have Core Formation cultivators pooling resources to solve a problem (a sealed formation). They don’t trust random people, so they recruit through favors and personal connections. This is the *guarxi* network in action—Han Li’s social capital (a favor owed by Jin Qing) is as much an asset as his formation knowledge. And Shi Die’s behavior? She’s a classic “nepo-baby” cultivator, leveraging her father’s reputation to demand premium treatment. It’s all very pragmatic and cold-blooded—nobody is here for friendship.

## Reading Guide
This is a breather chapter, but don't sleep on it. Pay attention to Han Li's hesitation—it's not cowardice, it's a calculated risk assessment. He weighs the unknown danger of the ruin against the social debt he owes. His final decision to go is a mark of his growth: he’s learning when to bend his “never lift a finger without benefit” rule. Also, get ready to meet Shi Die. Her arrogance is a red flag, and in a world where pride gets people killed, she’s either got serious backing or a very short lifespan. The stage is set for a classic expedition arc, and the real danger is still lurking behind that formation.
