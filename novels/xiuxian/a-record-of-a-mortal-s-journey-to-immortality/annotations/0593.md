## Chapter Overview
New environment, same old Han Li! Our favorite paranoid cultivator has barely landed in the mysterious Netherworld Domain village before he’s poking at the local magic infrastructure like a kid with a new toy. But surprise—no spiritual sense, no mana, and suddenly the world feels a lot bigger and a lot deadlier. Enter Bao Huanzi, a chatty old cultivator from the Five Dragons Sea who’s basically the village’s unofficial welcome committee and lore dispenser. What starts as a polite chat about stone talismans quickly turns into a data dump about Yin Beast cores, local politics, and the ghost of a Nascent Soul cultivator who once called this place home. If you came here expecting action, hold your horses—this chapter is all about atmosphere, worldbuilding, and Han Li quietly hoarding information like a digital packrat.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li examines a strange circular stone disc at the center of the village, which generates the protective purple mist. Despite losing his spiritual sense and mana, his formation expertise kicks in as he studies it.
- Bao Huanzi, a cultivator from the Five Dragons Sea, approaches Han Li, noting his interest in the “stone talisman” and launching into a detailed explanation of the village’s unique magic system.
- We learn that stone talismans and jade talismans are ancient techniques largely lost outside the Five Dragons Sea, modified to work with Netherworld force instead of standard spiritual energy.
- The core economy of the village is revealed: Yin Beast cores are rare but essential fuel for defensive spells, collected through dangerous hunts, and tightly controlled by the village elders.
- Bao Huanzi subtly hints at internal power struggles over the distribution of beast cores, but Han Li masterfully sidesteps the bait—he has no intention of getting tangled in local politics.
- The chapter ends with a tantalizing lore bomb: a Nascent Soul cultivator once died in this very village, leaving behind his cultivation notes and relics in a rundown storehouse.
- Han Li, suppressing his excitement, heads straight for the storehouse to claim this unexpected treasure trove of knowledge.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Netherworld Domain (阴冥之地) & Netherworld Force (阴冥之力):** This is the RMJI take on a classic xianxia setting concept—a sealed, cursed realm where normal cultivation rules break down. The “Yin” here refers to the dark, ghostly, or death-aligned aspect of Chinese cosmology, opposed to “Yang” (life/light). In real Daoist thought, Yin and Yang are complementary forces; here, they’ve been twisted into a hostile environment that literally drains your mana. Think of it as the cultivation world’s version of a dead zone or a mana-silence field in RPGs—except the locals have jury-rigged a workaround using beast cores and hybrid talisman-formations.

**Stone Talismans (石符) & Jade Talismans (玉符):** While regular talismans (符箓) in xianxia are typically paper or silk inscribed with mystical script, the idea of carving magic into stone or jade has deep roots in Chinese occult history. Jade was considered a material that could preserve spiritual energy and communicate with the heavens, used in everything from burial suits to ritual discs (bi). Stone steles with Buddhist or Daoist inscriptions were believed to hold protective power. The novel twists this into a lost art form kept alive only in isolated regions—a nice bit of worldbuilding that makes the Five Dragons Sea feel like a relic-rich backwater.

**The Bitter Lesson of a Nascent Soul:** The mention of a Nascent Soul cultivator dying here of old age is a brutal Mortal Stream reminder: even the quasi-immortal can be trapped and starved into extinction by a bad environment. It reinforces the core theme that in this world, strategic positioning and resource access matter more than raw cultivation level. Nascent Soul doesn’t mean invincible—it means you can die *slower*.

## Reading Guide
Welcome back, fellow survivors! If you were worried the Netherworld Arc was going to be all sad desperate villagers and mana-less panic—relax. This chapter is a classic *RMJI* setup: drop the protagonist into a strange new ecosystem, let him sniff out the local rules, then dangle a shiny lore carrot (Nascent Soul notes!) in front of him. Bao Huanzi is a perfect NPC for this kind of info-dump scenario: knowledgeable, slightly bitter, and just self-interested enough to be realistic without immediately stabbing Han Li in the back (for now). Enjoy the quiet before the storm. This is Han Li doing what he does best: being a silent, calculating sponge. Watch how he reads the village’s resource economy, its social tension, and its strategic weak points—all within a single conversation. And by the end? He doesn’t even smile. He just walks toward the loot. Peak Han Li behavior.
