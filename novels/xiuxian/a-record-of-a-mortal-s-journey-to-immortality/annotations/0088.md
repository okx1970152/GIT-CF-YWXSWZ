## Chapter Overview
The duel between the Seven Mysteries Sect and the Wild Wolf Gang kicks off with a shocking display of supernatural power. The opposing side doesn't field a Jianghu master—they field a cultivator. The Brilliant Gold Elder, a vulgar dwarf obsessed with gold, steps forward alone and uses a golden talisman to create an impenetrable barrier around himself, completely neutralizing a powerful saber strike. While the rest of the peak is stunned by this mystical sight, Han Li recognizes it for what it truly is: a spell. Using his Celestial Eye Technique, he peels back the layers of golden light to see the cultivator beneath, and what he finds is both a relief and a cause for concern.

## Key Plot Points
- The Brilliant Gold Elder enters the battlefield alone, having upped his fee from 5,000 to 8,000 ounces of gold for single-handedly handling the duel.
- Li Feiyu and Zhang Xiuer are absorbed in their intimate moment, leaving a slightly bitter Han Li to observe on his own.
- A Seven Mysteries Sect protector goes for a probing strike; his full-powered blow is blocked by a golden barrier conjured from a talisman.
- The protector is forced to retreat after the strike, and the crowd is left stunned and muttering about the impossible sight.
- Han Li, unnoticed, activates the Celestial Eye Technique and discovers the dwarf is a cultivator with weaker magical power than his own.
- The chapter ends with Han Li weighing the risks: the dwarf is a lower-tier cultivator, but his spell arsenal is unknown, making the outcome of a potential fight uncertain.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Talisman Magic (符咒)**: In xianxia, talismans are pre-inked spell scrolls that allow a cultivator to invoke a magical effect instantly, without needing to perform a full hand-seal or incantation sequence. They are a classic low-to-mid-level cultivation tool: powerful enough to dominate a mortal battlefield but crude in the eyes of any real sect cultivator. The Brilliant Gold Elder’s golden barrier is a classic example of a defensive talisman—cheap, loud, and terrifying to mortals, but a transparent gimmick to anyone with even basic Celestial Eye cultivation.

**“Golden Bell Cover” Logic (金钟罩)**: The chapter’s visual of an impenetrable golden halo surrounding the user is a direct xianxia upgrade of the classic martial arts “Golden Bell Cover” technique—an invulnerability skill from wuxia that makes the body resistant to blades and blunt force. In the cultivation world, that concept is upgraded into a glowing outer-armor made of pure magical energy, raising the question: is it a skill anyone can learn, or just a flashy one-use talisman trick?

## Reading Guide
**Han the Observer**: This chapter is a showcase of Han Li’s most dangerous asset: reconnaissance. While everyone else is gasping at the golden spectacle, he’s quietly running a **power-level check** and a **risk assessment** on the enemy. The gap in magical power he detects (his deeper than the dwarf’s) tells him he could win—but the unknown factors (how many spells does the dwarf know?) keep him cautious. This is classic Han Li: he doesn’t rush to fight; he gathers intel, processes it, and waits for the right angle to strike or slip away.

**No Cultivator Respect**: Notice how the Brilliant Gold Elder acts: flashy, arrogant, and thoroughly transactional. He’s not a mysterious sage; he’s a hired thug with magical tools who treats the duel as a business transaction. This reinforces the novel’s core setting—cultivators are not all enlightened hermits. Many are just powerful, greedy people who’ve found a way to weaponize the supernatural.

**Li Feiyu’s Detachment**: The brief moment showing Li Feiyu absorbed in romance while a cultivator battle is about to erupt is a small but telling character beat. It shows his priorities have shifted, even if only temporarily. Han Li, meanwhile, is alone in the crowd—observing, calculating, preparing. The leader and the strategist are drifting apart in focus, which is a quiet setup for future divergences.
