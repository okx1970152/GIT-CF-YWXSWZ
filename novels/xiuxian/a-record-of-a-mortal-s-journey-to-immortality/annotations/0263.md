## Chapter Overview
Get ready, because Chapter 263 is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream's core doctrine: **turning a desperate escape into a resource-management war**. Our protagonist Han Li is boxed in by the Ghost Spirit Sect's mad young master, Wang Chan, and the blood-red tide of his Blood Spirit Grand Art. This isn't just a chase; it's a tactical nightmare where the enemy's mana is a nearly limitless pool, and every one of Han Li's counters has a built-in, grinding counter-counter. The chapter is a high-tension, multi-stage attrition duel that showcases exactly why "fight smarter, not harder" is the only rule that matters when you're the underdog.

## Key Plot Points
- The chase begins to fail: Wang Chan's Blood Spirit Grand Art is simply faster, forcing Han Li to realize flight alone won't save him.
- Han Li attempts to use Continuous Fireball Talismans to disrupt the blood cloud, but Wang Chan's flying fork artifact forces him to redirect his defense.
- Wang Chan unleashes the full power of his technique, creating a sky-covering blood dome that completely traps Han Li's Divine Wind Boat.
- Han Li deploys his top-grade Black Flood Dragon Claws to counter Wang Chan's sinister green fork, successfully breaking the fork's black qi tendrils.
- The real horror show begins: Wang Chan summons endless "blood ghosts" from the mist—they're killable, but every one you slay is instantly replaced, creating a perfect attrition trap.
- Han Li tests a direct artifact strike into the blood cloud, only to find his Claws become sluggish, nearly losing control to the corrupting blood aura.
- Shifting tactics, Han Li hurls a massive volley of 70-80 mid-to-low-grade talismans to try and blast a path open, but Wang Chan counters by summoning black-skull artifacts that tank the bombardment.
- The chapter ends with Han Li, now desperately using a medium-grade spirit stone to replenish his mana, shrouding himself in an opaque green-red mist to hide his next move from Wang Chan's view, leaving his countermeasure a mystery.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Blood Spirit Grand Art (血灵大法) is a classic example of a "Demonic Path" technique. In xianxia, demonic arts are often defined not by "evil" intentions but by their method of operation. They typically rely on sacrificing vitality, blood, or souls for power, granting terrifying effects (like speed, regeneration, or area control) but at a great cost to the user's longevity or sanity. Wang Chan's manic laughter and the self-sustaining nature of his blood cloud perfectly illustrate this trade-off: immense battlefield control for a slowly fraying mind.**

**The "Blood Ghost" tactic is a textbook application of the *Wave Defense* principle in cultivation fiction. A single, powerful opponent can project a field that generates endless, weaker minions. The strategy for the defender is a brutal calculation: can you, with your finite mana pool, kill enough of them faster than the caster's mana pool is drained to maintain the field? If the answer is 'no,' you are slowly starved of energy and overwhelmed. This is a war of attrition, not a clash of single blows.**

**The chapter brilliantly demonstrates the "Rock-Paper-Scissors" logic of magical engagements. Talismans (firepower) are weak against Black Skulls (pure defense); Artifacts (precision and power) are weak against the Blood Cloud (corruption/attrition); the Blood Cloud is weak against raw talisman firepower. Han Li, at a lower cultivation, is forced to cycle through his 'hand' of tools, trying to find the one trump card his opponent cannot immediately counter. This creates the high-stakes puzzle-solving tension unique to Mortal Stream combat.**

## Reading Guide
**This is a textbook example of *Resource Management Pacing*. The author doesn't just tell you that Wang Chan is strong; he shows it by methodically taking away every one of Han Li's options. First, the escape route is blocked. Then, the talisman volley is neutralized. Then, even the high-quality artifacts are nearly corrupted. Each failed attempt raises the stakes, but notice how Han Li never panics—he just runs the next calculation. The story's rhythm is a deliberate, accelerating heartbeat of failed plans until the final, mysterious counter-preparation.** Pay attention to the **"madman"** label Han Li assigns to Wang Chan. In xianxia, this isn't just an insult; it's a tactical assessment. A madman is unpredictable and willing to pay costs a rational agent wouldn't, making him a terrifying opponent to fight against but also potentially susceptible to psychological gambits. Keep an eye on that green-red smoke. It's completely passive, but it's the first move Han Li makes that Wang Chan cannot immediately see through.
