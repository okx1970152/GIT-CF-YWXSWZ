## Chapter Overview
The quiet, cautious standoff between Han Li and Feng Yue finally erupts—but not in a clean duel. This chapter delivers a brutal, tactical lesson in the Mortal Stream’s psychology of fear. Feng Yue’s infamous reputation isn’t just backstory; it’s his primary weapon, a psychological edge that cripples opponents before a single spell is cast. And when the yellow-robed woman breaks the alliance and tries to flee, she learns the hard way that desertion in a deadly arena is a one-way ticket to a bloody end. Han Li, meanwhile, is forced to confront a grim reality: his Golden Light Brick, for all its raw power, may be useless against an enemy who can move like smoke. The chapter sets up a tense, high-stakes calculation: how do you kill a man who won’t stay still long enough to be hit?

## Key Plot Points
- Feng Yue’s infamous backstory is laid bare: a sadistic killer who tortures his victims for days, yet survives by knowing exactly whom not to provoke and retreating to Tianque Fortress when hunted by stronger cultivators.
- Feng Yue is enraged by Han Li’s earlier verbal taunt and immediately attacks with his Talisman Treasure (a small knife), expecting a one-shot kill.
- Han Li successfully blocks the attack with his Flying Heaven Shield, a top-grade defensive tool, confirming his earlier calculation that it can withstand a Talisman Treasure for a short time.
- The yellow-robed woman, terrified of Feng Yue’s reputation, attempts to flee the battlefield, breaking the alliance.
- Feng Yue intercepts her in a flash, killing her with a single bloody hand strike through the chest—a display of brutal efficiency.
- He then deliberately licks the blood from his fingers, performing a psychological intimidation tactic aimed at Han Li.
- Han Li’s grim expression is not from fear of the gore, but from recognizing a critical tactical problem: Feng Yue’s movement speed rivals his own Smoke Step, making the slow, powerful Golden Light Brick nearly useless in a direct fight.
- Han Li is left calculating how to trap an opponent who can disengage from any entanglement at will, confronting a genuine tactical deadlock.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Psychology of “Infamy” (恶名)**  
Feng Yue’s power is as much social as it is martial. In the cultivation world, reputation is a force multiplier. A name that precedes you can make enemies hesitate, lose focus, and make fatal mistakes. This is not merely a personality quirk; it is a deliberate strategy. Feng Yue has weaponized terror, and his ability to know exactly when to flee and when to strike is a survival instinct honed over years. The trope of the “famous villain” who is both savage and cunning is a staple of xianxia, reflecting a world where psychological warfare is just as critical as cultivation realm.

**The “Talisman Treasure” Limitation**  
This chapter deepens the reader’s understanding of Talisman Treasures. Not all are created equal. Han Li’s Golden Light Brick is a “one-blow” weapon: devastating if it lands, but slow and inflexible. Feng Yue’s small knife, by contrast, is a “persistent” type, better suited for sustained combat. This reinforces a key Mortal Stream principle: having a powerful trump card is not enough. You must also have the tactical means to *deliver* it. A slow, unstoppable hammer is useless if you cannot pin down your target.

## Reading Guide
Get ready for a shift in the fight’s tempo. This chapter isn’t about a clash of magical power; it’s about a collision of two different forms of cunning. Feng Yue is a psychological predator who uses fear as his opening move. Han Li is a cold calculator who must now solve a genuine tactical puzzle. The dead woman’s fate serves as a stark reminder: in this world, panicking and running *is* a death sentence. The real battle hasn’t even started yet—Han Li is currently in the war room, running the numbers on an opponent who refuses to stand still. How do you corner a ghost? That’s the question he has to answer before he can make his next move. And don’t underestimate the value of Feng Yue’s backstory here—knowing *why* he’s still alive tells you exactly how dangerous he really is.

## Deep Dive: The Limits of Power (The Golden Light Brick’s Design Flaw)
Han Li’s internal analysis of the Golden Light Brick is a masterclass in xianxia worldbuilding logic. It’s not a weapon for a fair fight; it’s an executioner’s tool. It requires the enemy to be *already* immobilized. This reveals a fundamental truth about the cultivation world: most powerful techniques come with a corresponding weakness. The flying-sword Talisman Treasure is flexible but low-damage; the Golden Light Brick is high-damage but inflexible. There is no “perfect” trump card, only the right tool for the right trap. Han Li’s current dilemma—how to deliver a powerful but slow attack—is a textbook example of the strategic depth that defines *A Record of a Mortal’s Journey to Immortality*.
