## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists! After the high-octane chaos of the Blood Forbidden Trial, Han Li steps into a whole new kind of nightmare: a black desert of iron ore dust, baked by hellfire heat and infested with a type of insect even *he* treats with deadly respect. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream worldbuilding—what looks like barren terrain is actually a perfectly designed killing field. From the shocking instant death of a Foundation Establishment cultivator to Han Li’s cold dissection of the Iron Fire Ants’ biology, we watch a survival expert methodically convert fear into data and data into a tactical gamble. The desert hasn’t even been crossed yet, and the tension is already suffocating.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the brutal aftermath of the sallow-faced cultivator’s foolish assault: a hurricane-like ant swarm reforms into a black spear, punches a hole through his chest, then devours him down to the bone in seconds. There is no dramatic duel—just an instant, clinical erasure.

From his vantage point, Han Li identifies the killer as **Iron Fire Ants**, ranking 37th on the Strange Insect List. This triggers a dense internal lore dump: they are immune to most spells, resistant to magical tools, can spit black flames, and—crucially—cannot be tamed. Any attempt to bind them causes them to self-destruct. They require scorching lava zones with iron and copper ore to survive.

Han Li then analyzes the desert itself, correctly deducing that it is not sand but **iron ore dust**, a deliberate construction by the Void Heaven Palace’s master to create the perfect habitat for these ants. Walking through it without preparation is suicide.

Faced with an impassable barrier if he relies solely on his mid-grade ice talismans, Han Li’s thoughts turn to his **Gold Devourer Beetles**. Though in a semi-mature state, they are ranked higher than the Iron Fire Ants on the Strange Insect List. He formulates a plan to test one swarm, using the beetles as a potential counter. The chapter ends with him advancing into the desert, Ice Pearl and Fire-Repelling Jade Pendant fully active, heading directly for the ant swarm he witnessed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Strange Insect List (奇虫榜)** is a classic xianxia device that acts as a tiered hierarchy of spirit insects, similar to a bounty board for bugs. In the Mortal Stream, such a list is not just a curiosity—it is a crucial piece of survival data. Han Li’s instant recall of the Iron Fire Ant’s rank (37th) and the Gold Devourer Beetle’s higher standing is the equivalent of a wildlife expert knowing exactly how fast a predator can run and what it eats. This is knowledge repurposed as a weapon.

The Iron Fire Ant’s fatal limitation—**inability to be bound**—is a recurring motif in the Mortal Stream. The universe is filled with tempting tools that are deliberately *unusable*. By making the ants self-destruct upon any binding attempt, the author prevents them from becoming a permanent power-up for Han Li. Instead, they remain a pure environmental threat, forcing him to solve the problem with tactics and existing assets rather than collecting another broken pet. This is the genre’s way of saying: *the best loot isn’t always the one you can take home.*

**Ore Dust Terrain**: The black desert being iron ore dust rather than sand is a clever piece of environmental storytelling. It explains why the heat is so intense (metal conducts heat) and why the ants can survive there. It also foreshadows that Han Li’s scientific, analytical mind will beat a brute-force approach every time.

## Reading Guide

- **Watch Han Li’s processing speed.** He witnesses a brutal death, identifies the insect, recalls its entire biological profile, notes its rank on a fictional list, and formulates a test plan—all within a few paragraphs of internal monologue. This is the Mortal Stream ideal of ‘cold calculation under pressure’ in action. Don’t skip the lore dump; it *is* the action.
- **The Gold Devourer Beetle gambit is a high-stakes pivot.** Han Li is about to pit two semi-mature insect hordes against each other with no guarantee his beetles won’t just get eaten. The tension comes not from the battle itself but from the *information asymmetry*—he knows the ants’ stats, but not how they will interact with his own creatures.
- **Pay attention to the environmental pressure.** The black desert is not just a backdrop—it is an active debuff that drains mana and inflicts constant damage. Han Li is already burning mana just to *stand* in this place. Every second he spends testing will be a calculated cost.
- **This is the quiet before the storm.** The chapter is entirely setup and analysis, but the stakes are clear: if his beetle test fails, he will have to cross an insect-infested hellscape with only a dozen ice talismans. That is the Mortal Stream’s signature move—making you watch the protagonist *calculate* his way into a corner before he can fight his way out.

Get ready, fellow Daoists. The next chapter is going to be a bug war.
