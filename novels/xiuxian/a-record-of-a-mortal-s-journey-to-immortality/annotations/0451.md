## Chapter Overview
The chapter opens with a bang: the three-headed tiger-demon, newly unleashed from its bizarre spirit fusion, is caught in the web of Han Li’s deadly lightning. The battle is swift and decisive. The *Divine Evil-Repelling Lightning* from his Golden Lightning Bamboo flying swords proves to be the absolute bane of demonic existence. In a flash of white light and a puff of ash, the monster is erased. But the fight is just the prelude. The real drama unfolds in the quiet aftermath: a tense, knowing exchange between the black-robed figure and our protagonist. A mask is discarded, a face is revealed, and Han Li’s eyes linger just a moment too long. Is it admiration? Calculation? Or something else entirely? This chapter delivers both high-octane destruction and a deliciously awkward social standoff.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li unleashes the full power of his “Divine Evil-Repelling Lightning” (辟邪神雷) from the Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords, instantly incinerating the three-headed tiger demon. The massive monster is reduced to ashes, leaving only a tri-colored orb floating in the air.
- He destroys the orb with a single sword slash, releasing three black streams of qi—the demon’s main souls attempting a last-resort possession trap. Han Li, ever cautious, burns them with fireballs.
- The chapter confirms that the combined tiger demon was at least as strong as a late Core Formation cultivator, but the lightning’s inherent counter to demonic/ghostly arts made it a one-sided battle.
- The surrounding ghosts and Ghost Yakshas, sensing the wave of pure Yang energy, flee in all directions, breaking off their attack on the black-robed figure and Zi Ling.
- Zi Ling recovers her composure quickly, but the black-robed figure is visibly shaken, mentally recalculating Han Li’s threat level and placing him far above a typical early Core Formation cultivator.
- The black-robed figure finally drops her hoarse disguise and speaks in a soft, feminine voice. She removes her hood, revealing a stunning, coldly beautiful face.
- Han Li stares at her with a strange, intense expression that goes beyond simple surprise or admiration, piquing both Zi Ling’s discomfort and the black-robed woman’s irritation.
- The chapter ends with a subtle layer of social tension: Han Li is not just staring—he is staring *with recognition*.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
### The “Divine Evil-Repelling Lightning” — A Perfect Counter
This chapter pulls the trigger on a concept set up much earlier: the Golden Lightning Bamboo’s (金雷竹) inherent property. In xianxia cosmology, certain materials and techniques possess innate *affinities* that go beyond raw power. Think of it less as a “super-effective” Pokémon type match, and more like a blade forged from the concept of “purity” itself. Evil spirits, ghostly qi, and demonic transformation are fundamentally Yin-aligned. The “Divine Evil-Repelling Lightning” is pure, unbending Yang. It doesn’t just hurt the tiger demon—it unmakes it. This is why Han Li, despite having this trump card, tried not to use it. Against a mortal opponent, it might still be strong. Against a ghost-demon, it was decisive. This is also why the surrounding ghosts fled in terror: that flash of white light was a tiny, localized apocalypse for any Yin creature nearby.

### The Three-Headed Beast and the Tri-Colored Orb
The three-headed beast is a classic xianxia trope, often symbolizing an unnatural conglomerate of power or will. The tri-colored orb it left behind, and the three black qi streams that flew from it, are a direct hint at its nature: three separate souls forcibly merged into one monster. The ‘three souls, seven spirits’ (三魂七魄) concept is deeply rooted in Chinese folk religion. When a creature has multiple heads, it often means multiple consciousnesses are fighting for control. The orb being a treasure that housed the main souls is a clever, if desperate, trick. Even in death, the beast tried one last possession gambit, hoping Han Li’s greed would override his caution.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream action economy. The fight itself is almost perfunctory once the correct tool is deployed. It’s not a slugfest; it’s a surgical strike. The real pleasure here is watching everyone around the table recalculate their hand. The black-robed figure goes from feeling superior and mysterious, to terrified, to calculating, and finally to embarrassed. It’s a complete emotional arc, all told through her eyes and her frozen expression. Then there’s Zi Ling, who watches Han Li’s reaction to the revealed beauty with a mix of professional jealousy and personal curiosity. Is she just a fellow traveler feeling left out, or is she developing a proprietary interest in her “senior”? And Han Li himself—he’s not only a tactical genius, but he’s also a social reader. The way he calls her out, watches her squirm, and then lets his gaze linger is a deliberate power play. He’s establishing that he sees *everything*. This isn’t just a fight chapter; it’s a poker match where the stakes are information and status.

### The “Frozen Smile” Moment
Pay close attention to Han Li’s expression when the hood comes off. The text says he wore a “strange, indescribable expression” and then a “mysterious smile.” This is a rare tell from our stoic protagonist. It suggests he *recognizes* this face. Is this an old acquaintance? An enemy? A woman from a wanted list? The Western reader, new to xianxia, might read this as simple attraction. But the veteran reader knows: Han Li doesn’t get distracted by a pretty face. If he’s staring, it’s because he’s running a calculation, a memory. This moment plants a seed: there’s a history here we don’t know about yet. The chapter is titled “The Black-Robed One,” but the real mystery is who is *behind* the black robe.
