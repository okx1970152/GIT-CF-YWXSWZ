## Chapter Overview

Buckle up, fellow Daoists, because the real ride just began. In *Winds Rising Beyond the Seas – The Small Stone Mountain*, our favorite paranoid herb-grower finally gets the promised payoff: the Profound Bone Saint actually leads him to the legendary Nine-Turn Spirit Ginseng. But of course, *nothing* in the Mortal Stream is ever that simple. The chapter opens with a crisp lore dump on the second trial’s killbox (the Crystal Path vs. the Lava Road), before shifting to an all-star lineup of Nascent Soul monsters playing a polite game of hide-and-seek. While Extreme Yin, Man Brutal Beard, and the orthodox big shots each peel off on side quests with suspiciously motivated excuses, Han Li follows the thousand-year-old ghost across a landscape that gets stranger with every mile—until they stop at a barren, qi-poor pimple of a mountain that screams “wrong location.” But the Profound Bone’s smirk says otherwise. Prepare for tension, trust issues, and a ginseng that might be smarter than both of them.

## Key Plot Points

- The Profound Bone Saint lays out the second trial’s structure: a long, forbidden canyon with only two paths—the freezing Crystal Path or the scorching Lava Road—leading to an exit teleportation array. He also confirms the one-day grace period for herb gathering, a classic Mortal Stream window of calculated opportunity.
- The Nascent Soul cultivators arrive and scatter with barely concealed agendas: Grand Matron Wen departs coldly, refusing all alliances; Wan Tianming and the righteous faction merge into a three-colored escape signal; and Extreme Yin vanishes with a theatrical burst of black smoke, claiming his own herb hunt. Man Brutal Beard’s attempt to recruit Qing Yi the Layman is politely but firmly rejected, leaving him muttering revenge against everyone.
- Han Li follows the Profound Bone for over two hours of straight, purposeful flight, passing mountain after mountain thick with spiritual energy—each one promising rare herbs. Han Li’s internal radar lights up with suspicion, as he notes that the old ghost ignored every single one on his supposed earlier visit.
- The Profound Bone belatedly explains his improbable discovery: on his first trip millennia ago, he was chasing a spirit bird so far that he accidentally stumbled upon the Nine-Turn Spirit Ginseng. He frames it as a freak cosmic accident, not something any rational cultivator would have found deliberately.
- Their destination turns out to be an utterly unremarkable, barren little stone mountain with negligible spiritual energy. The Profound Bone confirms the ginseng is there, and his eyes suddenly emit an inch of blood-red light—a chilling reminder of his undead nature.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Nine-Turn Spirit Ginseng (九曲灵参):** In Chinese mythology, ginseng is not just a medicinal root—it is a semi-sentient life form capable of moving through the earth, often depicted as a glowing infant-like creature (参娃) that flees at the slightest disturbance. The “Nine-Turn” prefix signals its supreme age and potency: each mythical “turn” represents a stage of transformation, so a nine-turn ginseng is nearly immortal-grade. In xianxia, such a ginseng can directly boost cultivation, extend lifespan, or serve as the central ingredient for legendary pills. The fact that it chose a barren, qi-poor mountain to hide in is a survival tactic straight out of folklore: treasures hide in the most unassuming places to avoid greedy cultivators.

**Crystal Path vs. Lava Road—The Ice and Fire Trial:** The binary “freeze or burn” trial is a recurring motif in Chinese fantasy literature, derived from the classical Daoist principle of Yin-Yang opposition as the fundamental structure of reality. Ancient cultivation arrays often force practitioners to endure pure Yin or pure Yang extremes to test their elemental balance. The “one day grace period” before the trial is a subtle but critical piece of Mortal Stream design: even a paradise-like herb-hunting zone is only a prelude to a mandatory lethal challenge, ensuring that no resource comes without cost.

**The Profound Bone Saint’s Blood-Red Gaze:** The blood light shooting from his eyes is not just a visual effect—it is a classic sign of a “ghostly” or demonic cultivator who has abandoned the human condition. In Chinese folk religion, red eyes are associated with demons, corpse mutilation, and vengeful spirits. Combined with his “Profound Soul Body” (玄魂之体), this visual confirms that the Profound Bone is no longer a living cultivator but a walking grudge held together by pure obsession.

### Grand Matron Wen

A beautiful Nascent Soul matron who refuses all alliances and acts independently; her cold tone reveals a high-status cultivator who trusts no one.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream logictics disguised as exploration. The first half is all politics: watch how the top-tier cultivators each use the flimsiest excuse to detach from the group, leaving the reader with a clear understanding that *everyone* has a secret agenda and zero intention of sharing loot. Han Li’s internal calculation, meanwhile, is the real show—he’s not just following the old ghost; he’s mentally marking every mountain they pass as a missed opportunity, and filing every suspicious detail for later use. The reveal of the barren little mountain is a perfect bait-and-switch: the reader, like Han Li, expects the ginseng to be hidden on a majestic peak. The fact that it’s not tells you everything about the Mortal Stream’s philosophy: treasures don’t go where it’s obvious; they go where no one bothers to look. And that closing shot of the Profound Bone’s blood-red eyes? That’s not a visual effect—it’s a warning label.

## The Politics of the Herb Hunt

This chapter is also a silent masterpiece in showing how high-level cultivators navigate social friction. Extreme Yin’s theatrical exit, Man Brutal Beard’s failed partnership pitch, and Grand Matron Wen’s cold dismissal—every interaction is a calculated move in a game of face and projection. Extreme Yin deliberately looks for Han Li and hides his disappointment. Man Brutal Beard tries to secure an ally and is publicly snubbed; his muttered threat is not just pride but a statement of intent for the inner hall. None of these are idle conversations; they are threads being pulled for future conflicts.
