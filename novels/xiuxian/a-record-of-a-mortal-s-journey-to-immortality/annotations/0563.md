## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists! Our favorite eternally-on-edge cultivator is back in business, and business is *rough*. Han Li has escaped the clutches of the Spirit Wind Beast for now, but his ticket out—the ancient teleportation array—is locked behind the Sublime Sound Sect's doors. This chapter is a classic Mortal Stream "social engineering" mission: Han Li needs to negotiate a passage, but the people he has to deal with are the same ones he terrified and strong-armed the last time he was here. Get ready for a masterclass in using reputation, cold silence, and a well-timed sneer to get what you want.

## Key Plot Points
*   Han Li arrives at the abandoned island where the Sublime Sound Sect’s market used to be, finding it empty. He needs a lead.
*   He spots a group of low-level cultivators—including two familiar faces from his beast tide teleportation run (the burly man named Yi and the scar-faced Xu Yun)—fighting demon beasts.
*   Han Li casually kills the beasts from a distance, revealing a vastly superior cultivation level, but he has changed his face using the Shape-Shifting Art.
*   The burly man, unaware of Han Li’s true identity, nervously provides the location of the next secret market: Twin Peaks Island, opening in a month.
*   Han Li takes the sea chart and leaves without a word of acknowledgment, firmly establishing his “no friends, only utility” policy.
*   Calculating the time left, Han Li decides a month is too long. His Spirit Wind Beast pursuer is closing in. He must negotiate a *private* teleportation passage now.
*   He changes his face to the “Li” identity he used at the previous market—the one with a reputation for being either a Core Formation or a Nascent Soul cultivator—and heads to Twin Peaks Island.
*   He finds the entrance and intimidates the same old gatekeeper from last time into immediately reporting his arrival to Madam Fan.
*   The old man, terrified, sends a message talisman and reveals that Yun Tianxiao is *not* present; Madam Fan is running the show alone.
*   Madam Fan invites him in, and Han Li is led into her private chambers, ready for a high-stakes negotiation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Power of Reputation as a Weapon:** This chapter is a perfect example of why you cultivate a terrifying reputation. Han Li does absolutely nothing except stand still, speak coldly, and imply a threat. He killed *one* person last time to make an example, and now the entire Sublime Sound Sect believes he might be an early-stage Nascent Soul cultivator. In the Mortal Stream, a well-cultivated reputation for being unpredictable and powerful is a form of social currency that can open doors (or make people open them for you) without spending a single spirit stone. The gatekeeper’s fear is the real fuel here.

**The “Shape-Shifting Art” (换形决):** This is a handy utility technique Han Li picked up from the Profound Yin Scripture. It lets him modify his bone structure, skin tone, and features, but it costs him 30% of his mana to maintain. This is why he doesn't just walk around as a normal guy; he switches between masks depending on who he expects to meet. His “Li” mask is the aggressive one, while his default “swarthy middle-aged man” mask is for casual information gathering. This multi-layered approach to identity is a hallmark of the paranoid but highly competent cultivator.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a slow burn setup, but it's a *tense* one. Pay close attention to the subtext of Han Li’s conversation with the old gatekeeper. The old man’s hesitation and stuttering reveal a key piece of information: Yun Tianxiao isn't there. This changes the entire power dynamic. Madam Fan is the one he needs to convince, and she is a far more complex and cunning opponent than the brutish Yun Tianxiao.

Also, note the fate of the two familiar cultivators. They are still stuck at the Foundation Establishment level after over a decade. Han Li, by contrast, has moved from late Foundation Establishment to mid-Core Formation. This is a perfect illustration of the Mortal Stream’s brutal class divide: those without a “golden finger” or extreme luck get stuck, while those with the right assets (and paranoia) skyrocket past them. Han Li’s lack of interest in catching up with them is a cold, hard statement of fact: they belong to a different world now. The psychological pressure is building beautifully.
