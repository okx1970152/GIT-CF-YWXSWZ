## Chapter Overview

Chapter 15 marks a quiet but ominous turning point in Han Li's early journey. After years of intensive cultivation and heavy reliance on medicinal herbs, Han Li hits a wall—his progress on the nameless oral formula stalls completely. Worse, Doctor Mo's stockpile of rare medicines is exhausted, and without them, Han Li cannot advance. Doctor Mo departs alone to search for new herbs, leaving Han Li alone in Divine Hand Valley for the first time since Zhang Tie's mysterious disappearance two years earlier. Restless and frustrated, Han Li wanders the Cai Xia Mountains, only to stumble upon a large gathering of teenage disciples locked in a tense standoff. The chapter deepens the atmosphere of hidden danger and unspoken secrets, while showing Han Li's growing wariness of Doctor Mo.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li has spent four years in Divine Hand Valley, devoting himself entirely to cultivation under Doctor Mo's watchful eye.
- Doctor Mo's health continues to decline, making him more anxious about Han Li's progress and increasingly desperate.
- Despite outwardly caring for Han Li, Doctor Mo's gaze occasionally reveals a greedy, covetous quality that makes Han Li feel like an object rather than a person.
- Han Li hits a cultivation bottleneck: his nameless oral formula has shown no improvement for an entire year, and all of Doctor Mo's precious herbs are gone.
- Feeling deeply ashamed, Han Li confesses his stagnation to Doctor Mo, who reacts with grim silence but no blame.
- Doctor Mo leaves the sect alone, claiming he will search for medicinal ingredients, leaving Han Li alone in the valley.
- Flashback: Two years earlier, Zhang Tie—Han Li's fellow disciple and friend—mysteriously vanished right after mastering the third layer of the Elephant Hide Art. He left only a farewell letter, and the sect eventually dropped the matter thanks to Doctor Mo's intervention. Han Li suspects something is off.
- Restless and lonely, Han Li leaves the valley for the first time in years and wanders the scenic mountain paths, encountering suspicious patrol disciples who question him.
- He hears the sounds of a fight and follows them to a hidden cliff, where over a hundred teenage disciples are gathered, watching two groups face off.
- Han Li climbs a tree and recognizes many old faces from his entrance test years ago, noting how they have changed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Cultivation bottlenecks are a cornerstone of xianxia fiction. They represent not just a plateau in power, but a crisis of resources and opportunity. In a world where external aids like medicinal herbs can drastically accelerate progress, running out of such resources is a devastating setback—especially for a cultivator with false spiritual roots like Han Li. The Elephant Hide Art, which Zhang Tie was practicing, is known to be extremely painful and dangerous at higher levels, which may explain his sudden flight—or perhaps there is a darker reason. Doctor Mo's peculiar, objectifying gaze hints at a common trope in dark cultivation stories: the master who nurtures a disciple not out of kindness, but because he intends to use the disciple's body or cultivation as a vessel, a furnace, or a sacrifice. The contrast between the seemingly caring surface and the underlying greed sets the stage for future betrayal.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in building slow-burn tension. Pay close attention to Han Li's internal doubts about Doctor Mo's true intentions—the narrative is signaling that his wariness is justified. The disappearance of Zhang Tie is a major red flag: Han Li's suspicion that Zhang Tie fled in fear may be half right, but the intervention by Doctor Mo to shield his recommender suggests the master had a hand in the matter. The brief glimpse of the teenage crowd at the end serves as a reminder that the wider sect world continues to turn, but also foreshadows that Han Li's isolation is about to be broken. Readers should note how Han Li's emotional state oscillates between shame, unease, and fleeting nostalgia—human touches that make his later ruthlessness all the more striking.
