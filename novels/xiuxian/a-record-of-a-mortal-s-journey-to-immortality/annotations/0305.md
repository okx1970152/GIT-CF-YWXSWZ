## Chapter Overview

Chapter 305 is a masterclass in Mortal Stream negotiation—a tense, layered conversation where Han Li tests the limits of his leverage over the captured siblings of Meng Mountain. After dismantling the old man’s blood seal, he reveals that he has no intention of removing their Blood Curse for free; instead, he forces them to renegotiate from a position of weakness, all while casually dropping his Yellow Maple Valley affiliation to anchor their loyalty. The chapter also introduces a hidden secondary crisis: a slow-acting poison planted by their masters as a failsafe, which Han Li neutralizes with practiced ease.

## Key Plot Points

- The second brother volunteers to be the test subject for a Blood Curse removal attempt, treating the act as atonement for his earlier failure.
- Han Li coldly shuts down the group’s assumption that he will remove their curse just because he studied it, forcing them to ask what he wants.
- The dark-faced old man recognizes the unspoken leverage: the information they have is not valuable enough to buy their freedom.
- Han Li presents his terms: temporary obedience, aid against the Blood Curse faction’s impending counterattack, and a promise of Yellow Maple Valley reinforcements.
- The group accepts, but reveals they have a fourth member still hiding in the capital whom they want rescued.
- Han Li reveals that in addition to the Blood Curse, the eldest brother is also afflicted with a fast-acting poison, planted by their master as a backup kill mechanism.
- He distributes antidote pills; the group expels the poison, clearing the way for the real Blood Curse removal attempt.
- For security, Han Li deploys soldier puppets to guard the perimeter, making it clear he still does not trust his new “allies” completely.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Blood Curse (血咒 / Xue Zhou)**: In many Chinese fantasy settings, a *blood curse* is not just a magical binding—it is a *spoken pledge reinforced by a supernatural contract*. Breaking it often triggers retaliatory effects that are pre-encoded in the curse’s structure, such as spontaneous death, cultivation base collapse, or soul damage. In *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality*, it is not a simple debuff; it’s an institutional leash, designed to let one master control many puppets at once, and its removal requires not just magical skill but a deep understanding of how the curse was structured in the first place.

**Poison as a failsafe (双重保险)**: The chapter introduces a common trope in dark cultivation fiction—the “backup kill mechanism.” Even if a subordinate is trustworthy enough to be given a mission, their handler will often implant a secondary, hidden death condition (poison, delayed trigger, soul lock) in case the primary binding (the Blood Curse) is broken. This illustrates the absolute paranoia of the cultivation world: no one ever trusts anyone fully, and every relationship is built on stacked layers of control. Han Li’s detection of the poison is a mark of his superior medical and perceptive skills.

### Social Tribulation (社会试炼)

A type of conflict in the Mortal Stream where the protagonist must navigate complex human relationships and political hierarchies with the same cold calculation as a physical battle.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a textbook case of *Social Tribulation* (社会试炼): a high-stakes conversation where the protagonist’s survival depends on precise negotiation, not combat. Han Li does not coerce with violence—he lets the siblings *realize* that their information is insufficient, then *generously* fills in the gap with a revised offer. The bait-and-switch from “I’ll study your curse” to “I’m not removing it for free” is a critical power move that forces the group to commit before they have any real alternative.

Also noteworthy is the moment Han Li deploys soldier puppets for perimeter security. This is not because he expects an immediate attack—it is a *calculated trust signal*. By making his distrust explicit (and even slightly theatrical), he subtly reminds the siblings that the power asymmetry remains, even as he helps them. This cold practicality ensures that gratitude does not evolve into entitlement, a classic Han Li maneuver.

Finally, the fourth sibling’s introduction sets up a rescue side-quest that will test Han Li’s commitment to his new assets. Readers can expect this to be a strategic checkup—not a heroic rescue, but a cost-benefit decision on whether the fifth sibling is worth retrieving.
