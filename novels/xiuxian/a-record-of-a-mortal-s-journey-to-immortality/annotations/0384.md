## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks a significant gear-shift in Han Li’s journey, transitioning from quiet consolidation to active risk-taking in the vast, lawless sea of the Chaotic Star Sea. We see Han Li in a familiar posture—cold calculation, methodical research, and a single, decisive pivot—as he digests his options and commits to a dangerous gambit for the sake of a legendary pill. The narrative structure is classic Mortal Stream: action is preceded by exhaustive analysis, and emotional weight is carried by the logic of survival rather than dramatic flourish. The chapter also provides a poignant, quiet coda to Han Li’s ties with the mortal Gu family, a reminder of the world he has left behind and the transience of ordinary life against a cultivator’s long timeline. Finally, we get our first view of the high-stakes expedition itself, with a tense introduction to the other cultivators on board.

## Key Plot Points
- After a period of contemplation, Han Li purchases a book titled *A Critique of Dao Alchemy* to research the Dustfall Pill, a legendary core-forming elixir. This act signals his intent to pursue the pill, despite his earlier caution.
- He visits Gu Family Village, reflecting on the passage of time at his old wooden hut, which has been meticulously maintained by the family. The elderly Lord Gu presents him with a bag of spirit stones and introduces his young son, a transparent attempt to secure Han Li’s future patronage. Han Li offers a vague promise based on their old connection, a cold yet measured act of grace.
- After reading the *Critique of Dao Alchemy* overnight, Han Li learns that the Dustfall Pill can indeed increase the chance of forming a core, albeit only slightly and usable only once. Its extreme scarcity and value solidify his determination.
- Han Li weighs the obvious dangers of the Six Connected Halls’ mission against the irreplaceable value of the pill. He decides to take the risk, rationalizing that his and Qu Hun’s combined strength as two late-Building Foundation cultivators should provide adequate self-preservation, and that the connection with a powerful organization like the Six Connected Halls is strategically important for his future in the region.
- Half a month later, Han Li and Qu Hun arrive at a massive sea vessel to begin their mission. As the ship sets sail, they enter the main cabin to find several other cultivators already present. Qu Hun steps forward to represent them, shielding Han Li from their intimidating scrutiny and establishing their front as the powerful partners.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Dustfall Pill (降尘丹, *Jiang Chen Dan*) is a classic example of a key resource in the Mortal Stream economy. It is not a miracle cure but a probabilistic booster—a precious, scarce commodity that only tilts the odds slightly in the user’s favor. In the cutthroat world of cultivation, even a small percentage point increase in the chance of forming a core is enough to justify extreme risk. This is the fundamental calculus of the genre: every step forward is a gamble paid for with blood, spirit stones, and a willingness to face the unknown.

Han Li’s decision to let Qu Hun take the lead and absorb the social pressure is also a staple tactic. In the Mortal Stream, information is a weapon, and a protagonist often benefits from being underestimated. By having his more formidable-looking puppet—a peak late-Foundation Establishment proxy—take center stage, Han Li can observe the other cultivators from a position of relative anonymity. He is a “shadow captain,” evaluating his new “crewmates” without revealing his own capabilities. This is a textbook application of the principle that in a dark forest, being the unknown variable is safer than being the most obvious threat.

## Reading Guide
Pay close attention to the layered cost-benefit analysis Han Li performs before committing to the mission. He doesn't just want the Dustfall Pill; he calculates that the risk of death is mitigated by his combined power with Qu Hun, and that the potential political gain from allying with the Six Connected Halls is a long-term strategic asset. This isn't an emotional leap of faith—it's a spreadsheet decision, and that is the beating heart of the Mortal Stream.

Also, note the one human moment: Han Li’s quiet visit to his old hut and his faint bemusement at the Gu family’s upkeep of it. He feels the passage of time, but he doesn’t wallow. He acknowledges the debt of his past, pays it with a nominal promise, and moves on. This is Han Li’s relationship with the mortal world: a clean, rational transaction. The chapter’s final scene, with the unknown cultivators eyeing Qu Hun, perfectly sets the stage for the social battlefield that awaits them on the open sea.
