## Chapter Overview
Han Li drops a full-blown lore bomb on blood sacrifice—and it’s not just random trivia; it’s a cold, clinical risk assessment that instantly defuses the tension in the room. Meanwhile, the bald-headed survivor makes it back to the Black Fiend Cult’s stronghold, and we get a peek into the cult’s internal dynamics via a frosty welcome from “Ice Fiend.” Two narratives, one goal: both sides are counting the clock until the next clash. Get ready—this is a chapter where information is the deadliest weapon.

## Key Plot Points
- The dark-faced old man advises retreat and awaiting reinforcements, but Han Li cuts him off with absolute confidence: the cult leader is at most late Foundation Establishment. His basis? The cult leader is still using Foundation Establishment cultivators for blood sacrifices—an outdated method that permanently locks the user out of Core Formation.
- Han Li delivers a comprehensive history lesson on the two types of blood sacrifice: the original, which devours essence and blood (fast but fatal to future advancement), and the later “soul sacrifice,” which targets souls and origins (safer but still restricted to high-level demonic sect insiders). This knowledge cements his authority in the group.
- The Meng Mountain Friends, having lost their fear of an unknown powerhouse, now shift their worry to the cult possibly fleeing. Han Li reveals that the cult leader is in a critical seclusion inside the imperial palace’s yin cave and won’t abandon his years of preparation—so they wait.
- The scene cuts to the imperial palace. The bald-headed man, seriously weakened from his fiend transformation, passes through heavy security using a gold token and enters a cold hall to meet “Ice Fiend.”
- A tense exchange reveals that the cult’s core team has a special symbiotic relationship with the leader—they are “of one body” and unlikely to face severe punishment. Ice Fiend casually mentions that Qingwen and Yeshe are training at the Blood Prison, and Yeshe is on the verge of a mid-Foundation breakthrough.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Blood Sacrifice and Soul Sacrifice**: The two-tier blood sacrifice system is a classic xianxia device for showing how forbidden knowledge evolves under systemic pressure. The first version is a raw, zero-sum transfer of essence—think of it as alchemical battery theft, except the battery is a living cultivator. The later “soul sacrifice” version is more refined, avoiding the core-formation bottleneck but remaining under absolute secrecy. This mirrors real-world Daoist alchemy’s distinction between external alchemy (外丹) and internal alchemy (内丹)—the former was often criticized as dangerous and eventually suppressed, while the latter became the orthodox path. In *RMJI*, the moral isn’t “demonic arts are evil”; it’s that every shortcut has a strict, uncrossable price tag, and the cultivation world is engineered to cut off the greedy where they sit.

**Fiend Transformation (煞妖化身)**: The bald-headed man used a “fiend transformation” to escape. In the Mortal Stream logic, such a transformation is a forced, temporary demonic state that burns the user’s origin energy for survival—a panic button that leaves lasting damage. It’s not a power-up; it’s a self-mutilation tool. The narrative treats it with cold pragmatism (“you’ll need half a month to recover”) rather than awe or horror.

**The “Yin Cave”**: The cult leader is using a yin cave (阴穴) in the imperial palace—a place of concentrated yin energy, often found in geomantically significant spots. In Daoist geomancy (fengshui), such caves can be natural wells of power, but they are also dangerous to the untrained. Here, it’s simply a strategic asset that locks the leader into a fixed location, making him vulnerable to a siege.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in turning exposition into tension. Han Li’s quiet speech about blood sacrifice isn’t just worldbuilding—it’s a power move. He casually drops enough forbidden knowledge to put the entire Meng Mountain Friends in his debt, while also making it crystal clear that he’s operating on a completely different level of information gathering. Notice how he never says “I’m strong enough”—he lets the data speak for itself. Meanwhile, the parallel scene in the palace grounds the emotional weight: the enemy isn’t a faceless horde; they’re a tight-knit unit with their own grudges (Ice Fiend’s sour envy), their own training regimens (the Blood Prison), and their own calculus. The “symbiotic body” relationship between the core members and the cult leader is the kind of organizational detail that makes the villain faction feel real—and dangerous. We’re officially in the waiting game, and both sides know it. Buckle up.
