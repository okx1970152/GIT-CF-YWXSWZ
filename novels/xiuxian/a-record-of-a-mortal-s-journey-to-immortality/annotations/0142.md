## Chapter Overview
Fresh off his shocking acquisition of an Ascension Token, Han Li is riding a rare high—but this is the Mortal Stream, and joy is always a trap. The Southern Minor Assembly concludes, and immediately the predators begin to stir. The “righteous” Azure-Robed Daoist, rebuffed in his recruitment attempt, reveals his true colors by planting a tracking mark on Han Li. Our hero’s legendary caution (leaving at dawn, running over a hundred li without stopping) throws the hired assassins’ ambush plans into chaos, but a brutal two-on-one fight still catches up with him. Han Li gets to demonstrate a terrifying new level of combat intelligence—using a contortionist’s flexibility and a tactical feint to slip an immobilization spell—but it costs him two deep wounds. The chase is on, and the hunter has just become the hunted.

## Key Plot Points
- **The Assembly Ends:** The Southern Minor Assembly formally concludes. Several older, prestigious figures appear to offer closing remarks, solidifying the event’s structure as a recognized cultivator gathering.
- **The Cursed Invitation:** The Azure-Robed Daoist makes one final push to persuade Han Li to travel with his group. Han Li’s refusal earns him the open hostility of Wu Jiuzhi and the Mo Brothers, making his choice to go solo a very clear survival calculation—he knows loners are vulnerable, but he also knows these “allies” are a greater threat.
- **The Seed of Betrayal:** The Azure-Robed Daoist, after patting Han Li’s shoulder in a show of reluctant friendship, secretly dusts him with a colorless, odorless tracking powder. This is a textbook Mortal Stream betrayal: smiling to your face, planting a knife in your back, and never missing a beat.
- **Cautious Parry:** Han Li doesn’t leave immediately. He waits until the next morning at dawn, then slips out silently, casting the Wind Riding Technique and bolting over a hundred li without a single rest. His paranoia ruins the assassins’ planned ambush, turning a clean kill into a desperate chase on their terms.
- **The Two-Man Ambush:** A yellow-clad conjurer and an underground earth-attribute cultivator spring a coordinated attack. The earth cultivator uses a giant-hand technique to immobilize Han Li, while the yellow-clad man prepares a massive volley of Ice Crystal Art.
- **Brutal Escape:** Trapped by the foot-binding spell, Han Li manages to dodge 18 out of 20 ice crystals through pure core-body manipulation. He then uses a formless dislocating technique to shrink his feet and wrench free. It is a display of extreme flexibility, tactical creativity, and high pain tolerance. The chapter ends with the burly earth cultivator emerging from the ground, furious and ready for round two.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Tracking Powder: Simple but Effective:** The Azure-Robed Daoist uses a “colorless, odorless powder” as a tracking agent. This is a common but extremely effective trope in xianxia noir. It subverts the idea that magical energy is always visible—mundane tricks invented by paranoid alchemists can be just as devastating as high-level spells.
- **Mighty Strength Art vs. Bone Softening Art:** The two cultivators use opposing techniques: one strengthens the body (Mighty Strength Art), the other makes it flexible (Bone Softening Art). This creates a tactical rock-paper-scissors dynamic that rewards preparation over raw power. Han Li came prepared; the assassins did not account for his flexibility, and that single oversight cost them their clean kill.
- **The Wind Riding Technique (御风决) as a Survival Tool:** Han Li’s near-constant use of the Wind Riding Technique to cover long distances at dawn is not magic—it’s a logistics decision. In the Mortal Stream, the best way to survive an ambush is to never be in the right place at the right time. He moves faster, earlier, and longer than a normal pursuer expects.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in how the Mortal Stream handles “post-boss celebration.” Most novels would let the hero relax after getting a legendary item. Here, Han Li’s first reaction to the Ascension Token is pacing, then planning, then *paranoia*. He knows that any gift in this world comes with a hidden price tag. The chapter subverts the “ally gathering” trope—instead of helping him move forward, the Azure-Robed Daoist immediately tries to have him killed for refusing to join. The highlight is the ambush survival: watching Han Li twist his body like an eel to dodge twenty ice crystals while literally trapped by the feet is both terrifying and exhilarating. Pay attention to the little details—the tracking powder, the dawn start, the hundred-li bolt. These aren’t just quirks; they are the survival algorithms of a man who has learned that in this world, hesitation is death, and trust is a suicide pill.
