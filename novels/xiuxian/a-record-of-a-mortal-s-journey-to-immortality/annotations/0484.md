## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow cultivators! Chapter 484 is a pure Dungeon-Crawl Deluxe—our favorite loot goblin Han Li follows three Nascent Soul monsters deeper into the Void Heaven Palace’s second floor, picking up broken puppets and being treated like a glorified janitor. Don’t expect any dramatic one-liners or heroic speeches; what you get instead is a masterclass in how the Mortal Stream uses silent observation, economic scavenging, and layered power dynamics to build tension even in a “transition” chapter. Plus, we finally meet the Serpent Guard Puppet, a two-headed, four-armed monstrosity that makes even Man Brutal Beard pause. Let’s dig in.

## Key Plot Points

- The group encounters more Wolf-Headed Puppets, which are effortlessly dismantled by Man Brutal Beard’s Heavenly Devil Arts; Han Li collects all the wreckage, earning exasperated looks from Wu Chou and the Profound Bone Saint.
- They arrive at a small teleportation formation to reach the second floor; the Confucian-robed elder is annoyed that Wan Tianming’s righteous faction is ahead of them.
- Man Brutal Beard dismisses their lead, claiming the Void Heaven Cauldron is not easy to take.
- Extreme Yin and Azure Yi Hermit exchange a meaningful glance before sending Han Li into the formation first—a silent power play.

- The Confucian-robed elder suggests letting the righteous group take the risk of retrieving the cauldron before ambushing them.
- The second floor features fewer intersections, more restrictions and traps, and the introduction of the Serpent Guard Puppet—a dual-headed, four-armed snake-based construct.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Let’s talk about the **Loot Goblin Economy** of the Mortal Stream. Han Li’s obsessive collection of puppet wreckage and tossed-away gems is not a random eccentricity—it is a core survival tactic. In a world where every scrap of ancient material could be a clue, a bargaining chip, or a hidden resource, the protagonist who treats even “trash” as capital is the one who survives. Western fantasy readers might see this as hoarding behavior; in RMJI, it is cold, rational resource acquisition. Han Li has no idea what these indestructible stones are, but he files them away because information + material = future leverage. That’s the entire xianxia economic philosophy in one action.

Another interesting point: the Serpent Guard Puppet’s azure halberds resist Man Brutal Beard’s Heaven Devil Arts but only *deform*. This reinforces a recurring rule in the cultivation world: ancient treasures often outclass modern ones in raw durability, even if their power systems are lost. It is not just about being stronger; it is about material science from a lost age. The indestructible stones that nobody can refine or melt are a perfect example of “chicken rib” resources—frustratingly impossible to use, but impossible to forget.

### Loot Audit

A distinctive RMJI trope where the protagonist systematically collects all wreckage, discarded items, and seemingly worthless materials from combat scenes, treating every scrap as a potential future resource.

## Reading Guide

Hold onto your storage pouches—this chapter is a setup piece, but it is doing crucial work under the hood. The tracking game between the righteous and demonic factions is heating up, and Han Li is being treated as a silent asset by three Nascent Soul monsters who keep exchanging “meaningful glances” at him. If you are a new reader: pay attention to every time a high-level cultivator looks at Han Li without explanation. In the Mortal Stream, those glances are never friendly—they are assessments of utility, threat, or potential hostage value. Also, note how Man Brutal Beard’s casual arrogance is tested by a single resistant gem; his split-second embarrassment shows that even the most boastful cultivator can be humbled by ancient junk. The chapter may not have a big fight, but it is building a quiet tension that will snap later.

## The Art of the Post-Battle Loot Audit

One of RMJI’s most distinctive features is the post-combat loot audit. Han Li does not celebrate the destruction of the Serpent Guard Puppet—he immediately sweeps the scene for salvage. This is not just greed; it is the cultivation world’s version of an after-action report. Every broken puppet, every discarded stone, every deactivated formation part is a data point. In a system where resources are finite and every scrap can be repurposed, the protagonist who treats a dead puppet like a potential blueprint is the one who never runs out of options. Western readers used to seeing heroes walk past “worthless” treasure should watch Han Li: he never scoffs at junk—he picks it up, stores it, and waits for the moment it becomes relevant.
