## Chapter Overview
The brooding silence breaks as Wind Xi finally unveils his grand ambition: to forge a pair of Wind-Lightning Wings from the fossilized bones of an ancient Thunder Roc. To bring this mad scheme to life, he’s assembled a bizarre party—a cynical Poison Flood Dragon, a worrywart Turtle Demon, and one very nervous human Core Formation cultivator: our boy Han Li. But just as the alliances feel nailed down, Wind Xi drops a poison bomb with a casual smirk. The Green Flame Wine wasn’t just a toast; it was a death sentence cleverly disguised as a hospitality drink. Han Li, trapped at the negotiating table, quickly realizes that even if the wings are forged successfully, his exit strategy is far from guaranteed. The true battle of the chapter isn’t fought with magic—it’s fought with silence, calculation, and the crushing weight of a power imbalance.

## Key Plot Points
- Wind Xi introduces the Poison Flood Dragon (Wu) and the Turtle Demon (Gui) as the key demonic cultivators for his secret plan.
- The two demon cultivators are initially skeptical of Wind Xi’s desire to refine an external magical treasure, arguing that a demon’s own body is supreme.
- Wind Xi explains his discovery of a Thunder Roc skeleton’s lightning-infused bone wings, which he plans to integrate with his own Wind-Fire Wing cultivation art to create Wind-Lightning Wings.
- Wind Xi explicitly states the team’s elemental composition: the Flood Dragon provides water, the Turtle Demon provides earth, Wind Xi provides wind, and Han Li’s wood-attributed technique is required to balance the final product.
- After reaching an agreement with his demon peers, Wind Xi reveals to Han Li that the Green Flame Wine contains a latent, incurable Chaotic Evil Qi that will kill Han Li in about a year if unaddressed.
- Han Li, under immense duress, correctly deduces the terms: survival is contingent on the successful refinement of the Wind-Lightning Wings.
- The Poison Flood Dragon casually mentions that his clan participated in the destruction of a human city, which resulted in the deaths of two Nascent Soul cultivators, underscoring the raw power of the demonic world Han Li is trapped inside.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Demon Cultivators and the Body as a Vessel:** The debate between Wind Xi and his peers isn’t just idle talk—it touches on a core divide between human and demonic cultivation philosophies in xianxia. Most demonic beasts (yao) believe their physical form is the ultimate weapon. Every scale, claw, and hide is a vessel for their natal power. This “body-refining” school of thought is a massive sub-genre trope. Wind Xi’s plan to integrate an external spiritual tool is considered borderline heretical among them. His obsession with hybridizing a human artifact with a spirit-beast skeleton shows a rare, cross-species ambition.

**The Thunder Roc (雷鹏):** This creature is a staple of Chinese mythological bestiaries, often described as a divine bird of immense size capable of generating hurricanes and thunderclaps. In the context of RMJI, it sits on the tier of “Heavenly Spirit Beasts,” a classification that places it in the same league as the Vermilion Bird or Azure Dragon in terms of raw cosmic potential. Finding even a fossilized bone-wing of such a creature is a legendary stroke of luck—and a target that any other Nascent Soul cultivator would kill for. Han Li is, once again, sitting in a room full of people who would trade his life for a profit without blinking.

**Chaotic Evil Qi (混沌邪气):** This is a classic xianxia “time-lock” poison. It doesn’t kill you instantly; it waits and festers. In many cultivation settings, “Chaos” Qi is the primordial energy before Heaven and Earth split—alien to a human body. Wind Xi’s claim that even a Nascent Soul cultivator can’t remove it isn’t necessarily a lie; mixing mortal true essence with chaos energy is like trying to pour water into fire. The only hope is the antidote (the host’s original formula), or reaching a stage where your own power is high enough to expel it—but Han Li is nowhere near that level.

## Reading Guide
**Han Li’s Forced Hand:** This chapter is a masterclass in the “Mortal Stream’s” most brutal negotiation tactic: the no-win scenario. Han Li isn’t being offered a choice; he’s being offered a delay. The real stakes are immediately clear to the reader: even if the wings are forged, Wind Xi has no reason to keep a witness alive who knows the secret of his treasure. Han Li’s silence isn’t defeat—it’s a tactical freeze. He’s buying time to think of an escape. Watch how he files every piece of information (a hybrid bird, a chaotic poison, a political murder) into his mental ledger. Every word from these demon cultivators is a data point he’s storing for his inevitable breakout.

**The Icy Subtext:** The Flood Dragon’s offhand mention of a battle that killed two Nascent Soul cultivators is a deliberate power display. He’s not just bragging. He’s telling Han Li, “Your Core Formation ass is nothing to us.” Keep that cloud of fear in your mind as the story progresses. Han Li has always worked from the bottom up; here, he’s further down the power pyramid than he has been in a long time.
