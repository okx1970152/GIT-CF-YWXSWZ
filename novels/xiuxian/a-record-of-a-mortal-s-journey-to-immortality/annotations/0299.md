## Chapter Overview

Han Li is not in the business of fair trades—he’s in the business of *tilted tables*. In this chapter, he moves a piece on the board: the old Taoist attached to the prince’s household, who will soon become the young prince’s formal master. Han Li drops the news that his future “disciple” and Head Steward Wang may be demonic path agents, then watches the old man’s fear calcify into reluctant obedience. The bait is a high-grade defensive magic tool; the hook is a spiritual mark lodged in the Taoist’s body. But when Han Li arrives at the Xiao residence for his scheduled exchange, the birds have already flown—straight to the opposite end of the city. A quiet social tribulation unfolds: the Xiao elder tests whether Han Li’s tracking claim is real, and discovers that in the Mortal Stream, a Foundation Establishment cultivator’s reach is longer than any mortal can bluff their way out of.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li delivers his warning to the old Taoist: the young prince and Head Steward Wang may be connected to the demonic path. The Taoist is terrified and nearly refuses the surveillance mission.
- Han Li sweetens the pot with a high-grade defensive magic tool (Violet Light Bead) and a future bottle of Yellow Dragon Pills, turning fear into greed-based compliance.
- He plants a spiritual tracking mark in the Taoist’s body, both for safety and control—a move that costs the Taoist nothing verbally but binds him completely.
- Han Li spends the rest of the day in a teahouse, reflecting on recent events—a habit that underscores his cautious, methodical nature.
- He detects the Xiao pair’s spiritual marks in the **western** district instead of the expected eastern one, confirming they have tried to evade him.
- Using the Divine Wind Boat, Han Li arrives at their new hiding spot before the appointed hour, catching the grandfather and granddaughter mid-conversation.
- Their dialogue reveals the elder’s calculation: he gambled that a Foundation Establishment cultivator *couldn’t* track them across such a distance, and prepared an excuse for the western district relocation.
- The granddaughter’s more optimistic read (fair trade, possible benefits) is overruled by the elder’s darker experience of the world.
- Han Li enters silently, sits in the main seat, and gazes at them without speaking—a cold, wordless power play that leaves them trapped in their own failed evasion.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Social Tribulation** (社会试炼): This chapter introduces a conflict type unique to the Mortal Stream—a high-stakes conversational game where the protagonist’s weapon is not a flying sword but *silence*. Han Li doesn’t shout, threaten, or interrogate. He simply appears, sits, and watches. The psychological pressure of the unfilled pause is what breaks the opponent’s prepared script. In the Mortal Stream, navigating these sharp human interactions requires the same cold calculation as a physical battle; a misplaced word or a too-eager reaction can cost as much as a missed block.

**The Chicken Rib (鸡肋 / Ji Lei)**: The old Taoist’s situation is a perfect chicken-rib scenario. A high-grade defensive magic tool is staggeringly valuable, but accepting it means binding himself to a Foundation Establishment cultivator’s secret mission. He can’t afford to refuse, and he can’t afford to fully trust. This idiom originates from a Three Kingdoms anecdote and is widely used in xianxia to describe something that is “tasteless to eat, yet a pity to discard”—a resource or opportunity that carries hidden costs that may outweigh its apparent value.

**Spiritual Tracking Marks**: Han Li plants a spiritual mark in the old Taoist’s body using the same technique he used on the Xiao pair. This is a standard Foundation Establishment tool, but Han Li’s range (over 100 li) is exceptional due to the Great Derivative Art. The mark is not just a location beacon—it’s a *psychological leash*. The marked person knows they can be found, and that knowledge alone is often more controlling than any physical restraint.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in quiet power projection. Han Li doesn’t draw a weapon, doesn’t raise his voice, and doesn’t even need to be present to apply pressure. The entire social tribulation with the Xiao family is resolved before a single word is exchanged inside that room. Watch how the elder’s careful plan—move to the opposite district, prepare an excuse, gamble on Han Li’s tracking being a bluff—unravels in a single, silent entrance. The *presence* of Han Li in their doorway is a louder statement than any spell.

Also note the contrast between Han Li’s handling of the old Taoist (carrot-and-stick, tangible reward, clear threat) and his handling of the Xiao pair (pure psychological pressure, no offer, no explanation). He calibrates his approach to the target: the Taoist is useful but replaceable, so he invests resources; the Xiao family holds a specific item he wants, so he employs silence as leverage. Every interaction is a transaction, and every transaction is optimized for maximum return with minimum exposure.

**Warning to new readers**: The “fair trade” the granddaughter suggests is a naive ideal that the Mortal Stream actively punishes. The elder’s cynicism is not paranoia—it’s survival experience. Han Li is not a villain, but he is a product of a world where trust is a liability and leverage is the only real currency.
