## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow cultivators, to the grand tour of the Void Heaven Palace—where the walls are thick, the doors are many, and the Nascent Soul monsters are using your favorite dungeon for their personal power walk. This chapter is a short, atmospheric breather between battles. Han Li and his ‘escorts’ traverse a labyrinth of sealed stone doors, each one a potential treasure trove or a one-way ticket out of the palace. But with Extreme Yin Patriarch, Man Brutal Beard, and the Confucian-robed elder running the show, Han Li might as well be window-shopping while handcuffed to a parade float. The only real action comes from a surprise summon: a wolf-headed puppet mechanism that dares to scuffle with a mid-Nascent Soul cultivator. It doesn’t last long, but its shattered remains give Han Li something shiny to collect, study, and add to his hoard of ‘maybe-useful-later’ junk. It’s a quiet chapter that sets the stage for the chaos to come, proving that even in a lull, Han Li’s mind is always grinding on the next angle.

## Key Plot Points

- The group enters a maze-like section of the first floor, filled with identical stone doors, each bearing shimmering restrictions. Han Li notes that successfully entering and looting one would teleport him out of the palace—a fact he stores as a potential emergency exit.
- Wu Chou awkwardly calls Han Li “Junior Brother” (韩师弟). Han Li is visibly uncomfortable but hides it behind a polite smile. Wu Chou explains his ancestor ordered him to stay close, revealing the leash he’s on.
- A heavy, rumbling sound approaches from the darkness. Man Brutal Beard activates the Heavenly Devil Arts, covering himself in golden scales and grinning ferociously at Han Li, who musters a weak, forced smile in return.
- Man Brutal Beard charges ahead alone and dismantles a golden wolf-headed puppet mechanism in a brief, violent scuffle. The old monsters note that their three-century-ago selves would have struggled, but now it’s a warm-up.
- Han Li collects the shattered puppet parts (silver alloy, wolf-head fragments, a green gem) after the fight, ignoring the Profound Bone Saint’s dismissive remark about their uselessness. He reasons he can study ancient puppet refinement techniques.
- The chapter ends with Han Li’s cold, self-aware conclusion: he is under constant surveillance by the three Nascent Soul cultivators and has zero chance of sneaking away to loot any stone doors. The broken puppet is his sole consolation prize for the treasure run.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Puppet Mechanisms (机关傀儡)**: These aren't your average golems or fantasy automatons. In Chinese xianxia, jīguān (机关) refers to a trap, mechanism, or cleverly engineered device—often spring-loaded or powered by spiritual energy. Kuǐlěi (傀儡) is the Chinese word for puppet or marionette. So a “puppet mechanism” is a hybrid: a construct that is both a battle doll and a mechanical trap, usually running on spirit stones or internal formations. The wolf-headed puppet here is at least early-Core Formation level, meaning it’s a formidable piece of ancient tech.

**The Power Walk**: This chapter showcases a classic trope: the low-level protagonist being dragged through a high-level zone by vastly superior cultivators who treat the danger as a light workout. Han Li’s frustration at being unable to loot a single stone door is a perfect example of the Mortal Stream’s systemic powerlessness—he’s not free; he’s just a guest in a cage the size of a palace.

**One-Way Ticket**: The rule that entering a stone door teleports you out of the palace reinforces the Mortal Stream’s aversion to irreversible choices. Han Li files this away not as an opportunity for treasure, but as a potential escape hatch. That’s the difference between a hero and a survivor: one dreams of reward, the other looks for exits.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is all about positioning and information asymmetry. Han Li is physically present but has no agency; he’s being watched, herded, and politely ignored. Every line of his internal monologue is a cold calculation of how much he can’t do. The wolf-headed puppet fight is less about the fight itself and more about what Man Brutal Beard’s casual domination reveals: the gap between Nascent Soul cultivators and everyone else is a canyon, not a gap. The only bright spot is Han Li’s decision to scavenge the broken puppet parts—a perfect example of his “never leave empty-handed” philosophy. Even if the materials are useless, the knowledge of ancient design patterns is a seed he can water later. Fans of Han Li’s quiet, methodical nature will appreciate his silent defiance: he can’t break the rules, but he can bend the crumbs into something useful.
