## Chapter Overview

Alright, folks, settle in. Han Li’s first day as a proper Yellow Maple Valley disciple is officially in the books, and it wasn’t exactly a red-carpet welcome. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream positioning: Han Li is thrown into a hostile political inheritance, meets the most wonderfully grumpy old man in the sect, and begins to lay the quiet, long-term foundation for his true cultivation plan. He’s learned his lesson from Doctor Mo—never walk into a trap without setting up your own escape tunnel first. And by the end, he's done exactly that, turning a raw deal into a hidden advantage that will define his entire early sect life.

## Key Plot Points

Han Li stews in his own frustration after the meeting with Ye and Wu, but that anger doesn’t last. It quickly crystallizes into cold resolve: he *will* make Ye pay for swallowing his due, and he's already plotting how. His immediate focus, however, shifts to the Hundred Herbs Garden.

He meets Senior Martial Uncle Ma, a scrawny, ill-tempered old man with a waspish tongue and a face like a weasel that chewed on a lemon. Ma immediately tries to turn him away, dismissing him as too weak and too young. Han Li, cool as a cucumber, accepts the challenge: he proves his competence by flawlessly identifying rare herbs, shocking the old man into silence. Impressed despite himself, Ma hands over the authority of the garden.

Ma gives a quick, brutally honest speech: succeed, and you'll be treated well; fail, and you're out. Han Li respects the old man’s lack of hypocrisy and accepts. Ma flies off, and Han Li claims a hut, sleeping off his first exhausting day.

The real meat of the chapter comes next: Han Li's nocturnal operations. He secretly buries the mysterious vial in a quiet corner of the garden, covering it with the celestial mecha fragment to mask its energy absorption. His long-term cultivation base is physically planted in the soil.

Days later, Ye returns with the pitiful leftovers of Han Li's promised payment—less than a fifth of what he was owed. Han Li says nothing, pretending the pill never existed. He accepts the haul: a couple of mid-grade spirit stones, dozens of low-grade stones, three decent magical tools, and some talismans. He's instantly rich by his own standards, but it's still a bitter reminder of how the system works.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces a classic "sect occupation" scenario: the Herb Garden Duty. In xianxia, these are rarely just jobs. They are either punishment assignments for weak disciples or—in the hands of a protagonist with a secret advantage—the single most valuable strategic asset a low-level cultivator can obtain. Han Li's choice is a direct application of his "never lift a finger without benefit" rule, but applied to long-term resource management: he gives up immediate power (a Foundation Establishment Pill) for the means to produce his own. The mysterious vial is now a full-fledged part of his cultivation strategy, not just a curious artifact.

The chapter also confirms a core Mortal Stream dynamic: power within a sect is relational, not absolute. Ye the Hall Master can openly cheat Han Li, but he can't kill him or completely alienate him because the deal is an institutional obligation, not a personal favor. The system works against the weak, but it also offers them the faintest, most cynical thread of protection. The spirit stones and magical tools Ye gives are a minimal compliance with the rules, a bribe to keep Han Li quiet and cooperative rather than a genuine exchange of value.

Han Li's decision to feign ignorance about the missing Foundation Establishment Pill is a classic Mortal Stream survival tactic: never force a confrontation you can't win. He absorbs the loss, categorizes his new assets, and waits. The chapter ends not in defeat, but in a moment of profound strategic patience.

## Reading Guide

Watch Han Li's emotional calculus here. He's furious, but he doesn't act on it. He hums and haws, but pours himself into a grumpy old man's task. He's cheated, but he accepts the scraps. This isn't a powerless phase—it's a *positioning* phase. The chapter telegraphs a key contradiction in Han Li's character: he can feel the sting of being taken advantage of, but he never lets that impulse override cold calculation. The true war isn't being fought with swords and spells yet; it's being fought in the soil of a herb garden and the ledgers of a debt he will eventually call due.

### The Vial's First True Use

Also worth noting: this is the first time the mysterious vial is used proactively, not just as a curious discovery. Han Li is no longer a passive owner—he is an active *user* of the artifact, integrating it into his cultivation strategy. This marks a subtle but crucial shift: he is learning to leverage his greatest asset.
