## Chapter Overview

Holy ribs, fellow Daoists—Han Li’s loot analysis phase is in full swing! The mysterious bone from Xuan Gu turns out to be a jade slip in disguise, holding the **Grand Yin Scripture** in all its demonic glory. For a moment, Han Li is disappointed—he already has his Azure Origin Sword Art—but the second half reveals a treasure trove of secret arts that can be cultivated at Core Formation. Cue the cautious excitement. Then comes the inevitable reality check: the Void Heaven Cauldron remains sealed tighter than a crab’s shell, mocking every trick Han Li throws at it. But he doesn’t sulk—he pivots. The Refining Crystal from the lava path is finally getting processed into his flying swords. Let the ultra-marathon forging begin.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li deduces the rib is a specially made jade slip and feeds it yin energy from the Soul-Gathering Bowl.
- The rib activates, revealing the **Grand Yin Scripture**, confirming it as the source technique for his clone’s Blood Essence Divine Light.
- Han Li reads the second half and discovers several demonic secret arts applicable at Core Formation, including the weakness of Extreme Yin’s **Celestial Corpse Fire**.
- He memorizes two or three techniques for future training but accepts he cannot fully practice the Grand Yin Art itself.
- Han Li then attempts to open the **Void Heaven Cauldron** with every method available—physical force, lightning, fire, water, ancient treasures—all without a single scratch.
- After two weeks of failure, he admits the cauldron is beyond his current reach and puts it away.
- The Golden Silk Silkworms still show no reaction to the five-colored pills.
- The Gold Devourer Beetles begin their cannibalistic phase, signaling new eggs soon.
- Han Li resolves to refine the **Refining Crystal** into his Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords, a process estimated to take over half a year, now feasible thanks to his Ten-Thousand-Year Spirit Milk stock.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **Grand Yin Scripture (玄阴经)** is a classic demonic-path cultivation manual in the *xianxia* tradition. Its name literally means “Grand Yin,” referencing the pure yin/dark force that governs ghosts, night, and the underworld in Chinese cosmology. In the novel’s world, such a scripture is often treated as forbidden knowledge, but for a pragmatic protagonist like Han Li, it is purely a resource—he mines it for applicable secret arts while remaining loyal to his existing orthodox swordsmanship.

The **Void Heaven Cauldron** is treated as the ultimate sealed treasure, requiring either a specific key or impossible cultivation threshold. That Han Li can leave it untouched for now and pivot without drama is a hallmark of the Mortal Stream mindset: the treasure is not a destiny hook, just a problem to be solved later, if at all. His cold acceptance of failure is a lesson in resource allocation under uncertainty.

**Refining Crystal (炼晶)** is an ultra-hard metal that can only be processed through sustained, massive mana output over months. The requirement of continuous mana supply makes it the ultimate bottleneck: only cultivators with access to rare mana-restoring items (like Ten-Thousand-Year Spirit Milk) can even attempt it. This reinforces the “preparation is everything” philosophy of the Mortal Stream.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the definition of a *Mortal Stream interlude*—no fights, no betrayals, just pure logistics, development, and frustration management. Han Li’s emotional arc here is a masterclass in *calibrated hope*: he lets himself feel excitement about the Grand Yin techniques, but only after verifying they are usable and safe. When the cauldron defeat comes, he does not rage or lament—he simply *files it* under “try again later.” Then he immediately pivots to the most productive use of his time: reinforcing his weapons. The real tension is not “will Han Li win?” but “how efficiently can he turn setbacks into setups?” Read this as the quiet engine room of the long journey ahead.

## Depth Dive: The Psychology of the Mortal Stream Staging Point

This chapter perfectly exemplifies what makes *RMJI* different from standard power fantasy. Han Li just got one of the most powerful demonic scriptures in the world, and his reaction is: disappointment (because he can’t practice the main body), then clinical cataloging (extracting applicable spells), then filing (for future use). No dramatic declaration of switching paths, no “with this power I will conquer.” Then he fails to open a legendary treasure—and he just… moves on. The Mortal Stream does not reward obsession; it rewards adaptability. Han Li never falls in love with any single path. That is his true longevity insurance.
