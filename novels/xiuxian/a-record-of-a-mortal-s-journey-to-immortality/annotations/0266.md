## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a deep, satisfying systems-check for all you puppet-heads out there. Forget flashy fights—we’re diving into the nuts and bolts of Han Li’s latest side hustle. After the near-death encounter with the Ghost Spirit Sect Young Lord, Han Li is doing what he does best: methodically building a countermeasure. We get a full, unflinching breakdown of the Great Derivative Art, the real-world logistics of crafting mechanical puppets (animal souls, anyone?), and the hard limitations that keep this power-up grounded. It’s a masterclass in Mortal Stream resource management, and a peek at a whole new dimension of combat power that feels earned, not handed out.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li is in the middle of a desolate plain, using the Soul Tethering Technique (牵魂术) to extract a lizard’s soul—a task with a brutally low success rate for an early Foundation Establishment cultivator like him.
- We learn the secret behind his puppet obsession: the Puppet Daoist Canon requires using real animal (or better) souls to give puppets spiritual nature; without them, they’re just empty dolls.
- The chapter provides a full primer on the three essential demonic-path techniques from the Canon: Soul Tethering (extraction), Soul Condensation (compounding weak souls into stronger ones), and Soul Refining (processing for fusion).
- Han Li’s first successful puppet is a crude lizard-beast made from yellow weasel souls, but it comes with a clear picture of its limitations: a 3-li range limit and a combat delay between command and action.
- The payoff for mastering the first layer of the Great Derivative Art is huge—being able to command a team of puppet-beasts could negate the threat of a massed enemy like the Ghost Spirit Sect’s blood ghosts.
- Han Li decides to skip Grade One puppets and aim straight for Grade Two, a level on par with upper-grade magic tools, requiring expensive ironwood and—now—lizard souls.
- The chapter ends with a technical comparison: the Great Derivative Art is vastly superior to common spirit-division arts because it actively *strengthens* the primordial spirit over time, making it the true core of the Thousand Bamboo Sect’s power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The “Soul Economy” in Xianxia**  
The word “soul” here isn’t religious poetry—it’s a raw material. In Chinese folk tradition and xianxia alike, animals and humans possess a *hun* (魂), an animating spirit that lingers briefly after death. The Puppet Daoist Canon weaponizes this belief, treating souls as a scarce, consumable resource for powering artificial life. This is a classic Mortal Stream twist: even spiritual creation is ultimately about extraction logistics.

**The Great Derivative Art (大衍决)**  
The name “Da Yan” is a profound reference to the *I Ching* (Book of Changes) philosophy of “Great Expansion” or supreme numerical logic. But here, it’s turned into a cognitive superpower—a technique for splitting your consciousness into multiple parallel streams, like having a personal cloud-computing network for your soul. The text is explicit: common spirit-division arts just chop off a piece of your mind; this one actually *grows* your capacity, letting you run a dozen puppets at once without going insane. This is a major power system unlock.

## Reading Guide
Alright, fellow cultivators, this is a “quiet victory” chapter—don’t expect an epic duel. Instead, grab a cup of tea and appreciate the *process*. Watch how Han Li turns a humiliating escape into a systematic, multi-point plan. He identifies the problem (outnumbered by ghosts), researches a solution (puppetry), acquires the skills (Great Derivative Art), and then **grinds** until he has a viable prototype. That little lizard puppet in the first paragraph? That’s a monster built from failed attempts and sheer obsessive calculation. Pay attention to the limitations: the 3-li range and the delay. The Mortal Stream never gives you a cheat code—it gives you a tool with built-in drawbacks, and it’s up to the protagonist to figure out how to exploit the strengths without dying because of the weaknesses. This is the foundation for a whole new playstyle, and it’s going to pay off big later.
