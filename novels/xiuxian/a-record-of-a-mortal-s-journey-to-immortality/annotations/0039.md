## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow traveler of the mortal path! This chapter is a deep dive into the ultimate heartbreaker of the Seven Mysteries Sect: the Blinking Sword Art. Forget the cool name—Han Li uncovers why this legendary technique has been a dusty paperweight for over a century. It’s not just hard; it’s a *brutal, soul-crushing puzzle box* of conditions that would make most martial artists run screaming. We get a front-row seat to Han Li’s brain as he dissects the manual, revealing the cold, hard logic of why *he* might be the only weirdo who can actually pull it off. Plus, a little light spy work and a plan to use Senior Brother Li as a political pawn—classic Han Li efficiency in action.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li overhears a spy discussing infiltration plans, but wisely avoids even looking at them to prevent detection, reinforcing his extreme caution.
- After returning home, the normally cold Doctor Mo shows complete indifference, revealing their relationship is now purely transactional.
- Han Li decides to gift the "credit" of exposing the spies to Li Feiyu, seeing it as repayment for the Blinking Sword manual, and a way to avoid personal entanglement.
- The bulk of the chapter is Han Li’s analysis of the Blinking Sword Art. He defines it not as a martial art, but as an **assassination technique** focused on perfect, instant kills using terrain, light, and psychology.
- Han Li identifies the three killer prohibitions that make the art inaccessible to almost everyone:
  1. Requires superhuman senses (vision and hearing).
  2. **The deal-breaker**: The practitioner must have *no pure internal energy (true qi)*, as it conflicts with the technique's subtle force mechanics.
  3. Insane complexity: Each "move" has hundreds of sub-moves, each with dozens of situational variations.
- Han Li realizes that his own lack of powerful true qi (a weakness in normal terms) might be his greatest strength for this art, yet he still finds the sheer volume of information daunting.
- The chapter ends with the sect's official verdict on the art: a legendary failure, likely a dying elder's fantasy, now gathering dust.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The "Pigeon Tower"**: The spy's line about infiltrating through the waterway by using a "pigeon tower" as an observation post refers to a type of military or sect outpost. In a Jianghu context, a "pigeon tower" could be a small, secret fort used by a specific gang (like the Wild Wolf Gang) or simply a watchtower. It highlights the feudal, territorial nature of these sects, where land, resources, and information are guarded jealously.
**The Sword and the Self**: The Blinking Sword Art's requirement of having "no pure internal energy" is a fascinating inversion of standard xianxia martial logic. In the world of wuxia, internal energy (*neili* or *zhenqi*) is the cornerstone of power. Han Li's reflection on the idiom "Practice the fist without cultivating the skill, and all your efforts will be in vain" shows how radical this prohibition is. For a normal martial artist, abandoning qi cultivation is like a general throwing away his command—it's a non-starter. This makes the art a "chicken rib" (something tasteless to eat but a pity to discard) for the vast majority.
**Comprehensive Killing Technique**: The chapter’s description of the art perfectly illustrates the difference between a "martial art" and a "killing technique" (*sharen ji*). A martial art implies a path, a philosophy, a system. A killing technique is purely practical, stripped of all fluff. The Blinking Sword Art is a toolkit for assassination, a technical manual on how to end a life as quickly and efficiently as possible in any given scenario. This brutal pragmatism fits perfectly within the Mortal Stream's dark forest ethos.

### Chicken Rib (鸡肋)

A classical Chinese idiom for something that is 'tasteless to eat, yet a pity to discard'; in cultivation fiction, it refers to a resource that is valuable in theory but useless to the possessor.

### Three Prohibitions (三不练)

The three disqualifying conditions for practicing the Blinking Sword Art: having some true qi accomplishment, lacking great perseverance, or lacking natural talent.

## Reading Guide

**The Inevitability of the "Dead End"**: This chapter is a masterclass in constructing a "perfectly imperfect" treasure. The Blinking Sword Art is absurdly powerful, but its conditions are so insane that it’s essentially a dead end for 99.9% of people. This isn't bad writing; it's a conscious narrative choice. It tells you that *if* someone could master it, they would be a terrifying force. The author is setting up a trap—will Han Li, the 'fake root' disciple, walk into it? This setup is a cornerstone of the Mortal Stream's "intelligence over brute force" philosophy.
**Han Li's Calculated Indifference**: Did you notice how Han Li thought about exposing the spies? He doesn't feel patriotic loyalty. He feels a mild duty as a "half-inner disciple" and immediately thinks of how to weaponize this information as a political favor to Li Feiyu. This is the 'Mortal Stream' mindset: everything is a resource. A secret is a resource. A friend's ambition is a resource.
**The "Mountain of Information" Hurdle**: The final condition—the sheer, mind-boggling volume of information—is a real-world test of Han Li's character. He has a photographic memory, but he still finds the amount daunting. This highlights a key theme: even with a cheat, the path is still a grind. The xianxia genre loves to create obstacles that aren't just about raw power, but about endurance, patience, and the sheer will to not give up.
