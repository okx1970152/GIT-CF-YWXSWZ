## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a masterclass in the "quiet victory" phase of a Mortal Stream arc. Han Li has won, but the real battle is extracting every ounce of tactical intelligence from a helpless enemy before coldly disposing of him. The chapter is essentially a tense, one-sided interrogation between Han Li and Senior Brother Lin’s trapped primordial soul. Lin, desperate, tries to bargain with secrets and promises—chief among them the legendary "Great Derivative Art" and a rumor that it can boost one’s chances of forming a Golden Core. But Han Li, ever the pragmatist, isn’t buying the hype. He systematically deconstructs the offer, identifies the flaws in Lin’s logic, and decides that the risk far outweighs the reward. The result is a brutal, logical execution that feels less like a fight and more like a surgical removal of a loose end. Get ready for a heavy dose of exposition, cold-blooded calculation, and a reminder that in this world, mercy is a luxury only fools can afford.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li holds Senior Brother Lin’s primordial soul captive, demanding full information. Lin tries to bargain, but Han Li shuts down any conditions with immediate, painful force.
- Lin reveals the origins of the Great Derivative Art: it was created by the founder of the Thousand Bamboo School, is the core technique for controlling numerous puppets via soul division, and is essential for high-level puppet mastery.
- The "half-volume" mystery is solved: Lin, the former young master, only possesses the techniques for the first four layers, which he compiled into an "upper volume" after fleeing. The final three layers are held by the usurper, Jin Nantian.
- Lin's big temptation: a secret rumor that mastering the Great Derivative Art to a high level makes Core Formation significantly easier. He claims a "fifty to sixty percent" chance, but the truth (as his father told him) was only thirty percent.
- Han Li’s cold analysis: the art doesn’t enhance magical power, takes enormous time and focus, has a dubious track record, requires a hopeless war against an entire sect, and Lin is an untrustworthy survivor. The calculus fails on every front.
- Han Li kills Lin’s soul without hesitation, justifying the act with a clear, logical internal monologue that ties back to his trauma with Doctor Mo and his fundamental survival principles.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Great Derivative Art (大衍决)** & The Art of Soul Division: The name "Great Derivative" comes from the *I Ching* (Yijing), where "Da Yan" (大衍) refers to the grand process of divination and cosmic generation. In xianxia, it’s co-opted to mean a technique that "derives" or "extends" one's consciousness. The "Soul Divide" (分神) technique is a classic high-level secret art that allows a cultivator to split their consciousness into multiple independent strands. This is the foundational tech for controlling a puppet army; without it, you can only control one puppet at a time, making your collection of hundreds utterly useless. It’s a high-cost, high-reward skill—extremely taxing on the mind and requiring immense focus, which is why Lin’s cultivation was so slow.

**Core Formation (结丹) & The Odds Game:** Core Formation is the single most terrifying bottleneck in the human realm. It’s a one-way door—success means a five-hundred-year lifespan and massive power; failure often means death or crippling injury. The rumor that the Great Derivative Art can increase the success rate is therefore a *huge* deal, even if it’s only a thirty percent rumor. This chapter perfectly illustrates the Mortal Stream's economic and logical structure: would you invest a century of life into a skill that doesn't make you stronger now, just for a *chance* at an easier bottleneck later? Han Li’s answer is a resounding "no." He treats the offer like a bad investment, not a magical opportunity.

## Reading Guide
- **Watch the Tone Shift**: Watch how Han Li’s voice changes over the chapter. He starts cold and interrogative, then allows a sliver of genuine interest when the Core Formation rumor is mentioned. But that interest lasts only a few seconds before his logical mind crushes the fantasy. Notice how his final, whispered monologue—basically him justifying the kill to himself—is the most emotionally honest he gets all chapter.
- **The Failure of the "Big Secret" Tactic**: Lin tries the classic xianxia escape: dangling a powerful secret to buy time. But Han Li doesn't work that way. He immediately performs a risk/reward analysis and finds the secret worthless because the *cost* (fighting a sect, trusting an enemy, investing in a non-primary art) is too high. This is a core lesson in Mortal Stream logic: a secret is only valuable if the path to using it doesn't kill you first.
- **Han Li’s Trauma Flag**: His final statement—"I detest nothing more than others trying to possess me. It brings back unpleasant memories"—is a rare, direct nod to his past with Doctor Mo. This isn't just clinical pragmatism; Han Li has a deep, personal revulsion to soul theft and possession. When he kills Lin, part of his motivation is genuine disgust, but he never lets that emotion rule his logic. It's the perfect mix of cold reason and buried human feeling.
