## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a devastatingly effective piece of social and character worldbuilding that operates on multiple levels. First, it shatters the reader’s (and the characters’) preconceptions about Dong Xuan’er, revealing that the scandalous reputation she carries is built on a foundation of half-truths, magical side effects, and malicious gossip. The *Spring Transformation Art* she cultivates gives her an alluring, seductive appearance that has nothing to do with her actual conduct, and Hongfu’s Draconian threat—crippling her cultivation if she ever loses her chastity—has kept her physically pure despite her flirtations and provocations. But this revelation arrives too late; her reputation is already ruined beyond repair, a classic Mortal Stream tragedy where the truth is powerless against the momentum of social judgment.

Simultaneously, we watch Han Li and Dong Xuan’er on the road, a painful mismatch in terms of personality and power dynamics. Han Li’s unshakeable immunity to her charm is a direct consequence of his experience with the near-fatal trap of Junior Sister Chen; he has built a psychological firewall against this exact archetype. Dong Xuan’er, for her part, is revealed not as a villain but as a deeply frustrating, spoiled, and ultimately tragic figure—a product of her technique and her environment, whose weapons of manipulation are completely useless against an opponent who refuses to play the game. The chapter masterfully sets up the political stakes of the upcoming Treasure-Seizing Assembly while giving us a richly layered character study that recontextualizes everything we thought we knew.

## Key Plot Points

- **The truth about Dong Xuan’er**: Hongfu explains to Li Huayuan’s wife that Dong Xuan’er is still a virgin. The *Spring Transformation Art*, a top-tier technique for preserving youth, gives its cultivator an alluring aura that is purely superficial.
- **A ruined reputation**: Despite her physical purity, Dong Xuan’er’s habit of flirting and causing jealousy among male disciples led to rumors so entrenched that her reputation is now permanently damaged. No reputable cultivator will risk forming a partnership with her.
- **A critical bottleneck**: The *Spring Transformation Art* now requires Dong Xuan’er to engage in dual cultivation to continue her progress. Without it, her cultivation will stagnate or regress.
- **Hongfu’s Draconian safeguard**: A key condition of learning the art was that Dong Xuan’er must not lose her chastity; if she does, Hongfu will cripple her cultivation.
- **Li Huayuan’s dilemma**: Although the misunderstanding is cleared, Li Huayuan refuses to sacrifice his own reputation by letting his disciple marry her, choosing instead to feel guilty toward Hongfu and helping Han Li’s trip to the assembly.
- **Wu Xuan’s infatuation**: Li Huayuan’s seventh disciple has become obsessed with Dong Xuan’er and begged his master to propose again. He is reprimanded and sent away.
- **The road to Yanliang Mountain**: Han Li and Dong Xuan’er travel together, constantly clashing. Her seductive charms have no effect on him, and he keeps her behavior in check by threatening to report her to Hongfu.
- **An unexpected delay**: Arriving at the designated meeting point, they find no one from the Yan Clan to receive them, raising Han Li’s caution.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The *Spring Transformation Art* (化春决)**: This is a classic xianxia trope where a cultivation technique has external side effects that mislead social perception. It is not a charm or a curse but a natural consequence of channeling the body’s energy in a certain way. The technique preserves youth but also magnifies the practitioner's natural allure, making her appear sexually experienced even when she is not. This is a distinct concept from traditional Daoist dual cultivation arts and leans more into the literary tradition of “beautiful women with hidden secrets.”

**The Cinnabar Mole (守宫砂)**: In ancient Chinese culture, a cinnabar mole was a red spot placed on a woman’s arm to mark her as a virgin. The legend claimed it would fade or disappear if she had sexual relations. Historically, it is associated with palace maidens and was used as a practical (if unreliable) test of chastity. In xianxia novels, its presence or absence often becomes a plot point that either confirms or subverts expectations about a female character’s history.

**Reputation as a cultivator’s currency**: In a cultivation world where social standing and family “face” (面子, *mianzi*) directly affect one’s access to resources, alliances, and marriages, a ruinous reputation is a genuine curse. Even the truth is often irrelevant; once a social judgment has been made, it is nearly impossible to reverse. This chapter is a textbook example of how the Mortal Stream treats gossip and reputation as a real, tangible battlefield.

## Reading Guide

Oh, Dong Xuan’er. You poor, tragic, infuriating thing.

This chapter is a masterclass in the kind of tragic worldbuilding that makes *RMJI* so addictive. You want to hate Dong Xuan’er for being a spoiled brat; you want to blame her for the situation she’s in. And then the story calmly reveals that, no, actually, she’s a virgin whose only crime was being too beautiful *by accident* and flirting a bit too much. Her reputation was destroyed by a technique she was allowed to learn, under the condition that she *not* use it the way it was intended.

This is peak Mortal Stream misery: the system itself sets you up for failure, and even when the truth comes out, no one cares. The gossip has already done its damage.

Also, pay close attention to Han Li here. He’s not just being cautious; he’s running a psychological script. The moment a woman appears with seductive allure, his brain immediately flashes back to the Lu incident and slams the door shut. He’s not immune because he’s above such things; he’s immune because he’s been traumatized into building an unbreakable firewall. It’s a dark, brilliant bit of character consistency.

And that cliffhanger of a delay? Classic Han Li: the moment something doesn’t go according to schedule, he’s already calculating worst-case scenarios. Get ready, fellow travelers—a trip to the Yan Clan is never just a trip.
