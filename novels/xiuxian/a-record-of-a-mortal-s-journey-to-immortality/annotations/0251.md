## Chapter Overview

The battle royale of flirting, fighting, and face-saving continues! This chapter delivers a one-two punch: first, a brutal magical duel showcasing the Ghost Spirit Sect’s sinister power against the Yan Clan’s prized artifact, then a cringe-worthy, three-way tug-of-war over Dong Xuan’er’s fleeting attention. Yan Yu’s top-grade Bone-Melting Treasure Fan gets utterly owned—more like devoured—by the enemy’s ghostly skulls, confirming that the Ghost Spirit Sect is playing on a different level. Meanwhile, Han Li, the designated dad of the group, observes the whole spectacle with the tired indifference of someone who’s already seen it all. The chapter ends with a formal standoff: Ghost Spirit Sect’s masked Junior Sect Leader wants to see the Yan Clan’s legendary Blood Cultivators. The tension is officially escalated.

## Key Plot Points

- The Yan Clan’s cousin unleashes the Bone-Melting Treasure Fan, a top-grade magical tool that spears a deadly purple mist, completely enveloping the green-robed Ghost Spirit Sect attacker.
- The green-robed man reveals his trump card: a set of gruesome skulls that absorb the black qi, swell to carriage-wheel size, and devour the green mist, permanently crippling the fan’s power.
- The skulls then fire a combined black beam that pierces the Yan Clan member’s defensive yellow wind barrier, knocking him unconscious and ending the match.
- The Ghost Spirit Sect has now won four out of five matches.
- An elder from the Yan Clan proposes to continue, but the mysterious Ghost Spirit Sect Junior Sect Leader—speaking in a cultured, youthful voice—suggests waiting for the Yan Clan’s Life-and-Death Hall disciples, explicitly wanting to face their infamous Blood Cultivators.
- Off to the side, Yan Yu and Senior Brother Feng are locked in a petty contest over Dong Xuan’er’s flirtatious remarks, ready to come to blows.
- Han Li silently notes that Dong Xuan’er’s bewitching charm seems to have no effect on him at all, while it obviously clouds the judgment of other male cultivators—a fact that puzzles and disturbs him.
- Dong Xuan’er defuses the brewing fight by asking Yan Yu to take her to rest, successfully dividing the two rivals and ending the scene.
- Han Li, mission complete, wanders off alone with no interest in the social games.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Spiritual Root Gatekeeping of Magical Tools.**  
The fan debate in this chapter introduces a brutal, practical reality of the cultivation world: many powerful artifacts have hyper-specific activation requirements. The Wind-Thunder Fan is god-tier *in theory*, but requires both Wind and Thunder spiritual roots to use. The Bone-Melting Fan is weaker but accessible to anyone with a common Wood Spiritual Root. This makes ‘strong’ artifacts paradoxically *less* valuable in a real-world exchange economy—only a tiny fraction of cultivators can even use them. It’s a grim systemic reminder that your birth-given talent (spiritual root) dictates not only your cultivation path but also which weapons you can even *pick up*.

**Bewitching Charms (狐媚之功) and the Cultivator’s Mind.**  
Dong Xuan’er’s technique is explicitly noted to be influencing even Foundation Establishment cultivators, causing them to behave irrationally. This raises a fascinating world-building point: enchanting or mind-influencing arts are not just tricks for mortals; they can scale to affect higher-realm cultivators if the skill gap is wide enough. Han Li’s immunity is treated as an anomaly—a sign of his unusual mental fortitude or a quirk of his cultivation method—rather than the expected norm.

## Reading Guide

This is a classic ‘calm before the storm’ chapter that sets two major threads in motion. First, the Ghost Spirit Sect has thoroughly flexed on the Yan Clan in open combat, and the Junior Sect Leader’s explicit request to face the Blood Cultivators means the real fireworks are yet to come. Second, Han Li’s passive observation of Dong Xuan’er’s social warfare gives us a perfect character moment: he is completely immune to her charms, and he sees her as a ‘trouble magnet’ to be managed, not a person to befriend. This reinforces his core pragmatism. Also, keep an eye on that Yan Yu–Feng rivalry; the way Xuan’er plays them shows she’s not just a pretty face but a skilled social manipulator. The chapter is short on action for Han Li but long on world-building and character dynamics.
