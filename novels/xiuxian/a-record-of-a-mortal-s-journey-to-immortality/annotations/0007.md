## Chapter Overview

This chapter shifts focus from external action to internal struggle. Han Li confronts the harsh reality of cultivation: his progress with the nameless oral formula is agonizingly slow, especially compared to peers practicing the sect's standard Zheng Yang Jin. The contrast deepens his frustration, but a chance discovery—that Zhang Tie has achieved nothing at all—renews his determination. The chapter also expands the sect's social hierarchy, showing how inner vs. outer disciple status determines a cultivator's quality of life and access to resources.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li learns that the formal test for Disciples on Probation involved a grueling physical trial; he and Zhang Tie were exempted due to Doctor Mo's arrangement.
- Han Li realizes the vast gap in treatment between inner and outer disciples, which motivates him to abandon thoughts of going home and to pursue inner disciple status for the sake of his family's financial relief.
- After three months of solitary cultivation, Han Li produces only a faint, cool stream of true energy, far inferior to the warm, powerful Zheng Yang Jin of other disciples.
- Zhang Tie reveals he has produced no true energy at all, which reignites Han Li's flagging confidence and drives him to cultivate even more intensely.
- Doctor Mo remains completely aloof, absorbed in studying a book called the 'Longevity Classic,' showing no interest in his apprentices' progress.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**True Energy (真气):** In Chinese martial arts and cultivation fiction, true energy (zhenqi) is the internal power generated through breathing and meditation techniques. Different arts produce energy with different qualities—warm (yang) or cool (yin)—and these qualities affect their combat applications. The warm Zheng Yang Jin grants immediate physical power, while Han Li's cool energy seems to enhance spirit and appetite but offers no martial benefit, hinting at its more esoteric nature.

**Inner vs. Outer Disciples:** This hierarchical split is a staple of xianxia and wuxia sects. Inner disciples receive direct instruction from masters, better resources, and higher status; outer disciples are treated as labor or cannon fodder. The Mortal Stream genre emphasizes this class divide as a source of ruthless competition and resource hoarding.

**Longevity Classic (长生经):** Books on longevity and immortality have a long history in Daoist practice, where physical cultivation and alchemical regimens aim to extend life. In the novel, Doctor Mo's obsession with this text marks him as a man driven by the fear of death—a common trait among powerful but aging cultivators.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a textbook example of Han Li's core pragmatism. His motivation to cultivate is not driven by ambition or a thirst for power, but by the concrete need to support his impoverished family. When he discovers his progress is slow, he considers giving up—until he learns that Zhang Tie is even worse off. This is not compassion; it is a comparative calculation that restores his will. The chapter also subtly reinforces the world's cruelty: even the chance to become a mere disciple is a zero-sum game where most fail and are relegated to menial roles. Han Li's decision to intensify his efforts, rather than rely on talent or luck, reflects the Mortal Stream theme that survival depends on relentless effort and cold-eyed self-assessment.
