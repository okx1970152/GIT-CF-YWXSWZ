## Chapter Overview

Holy smokes, folks. The power scale just tilted so hard it nearly cracked the axis of the cultivation world. This chapter is a masterclass in what happens when a cultivator stops playing by mortal rules. Han Li, having just concluded the Shadow Valley showdown, finally drops the act and shows the Wild Wolf Gang exactly what a Qi Condensation cultivator can do to a Jianghu army. This isn't a fight; it's a systematic, one-sided extermination performed with the casual air of a man clearing out a pest nest. Jia Tianlong, a brilliant warlord, is reduced from smug victor to a trembling victim in the span of a few breaths. Get ready for pure, unadulterated, asymmetric violence.

## Key Plot Points

- After collecting his spoils, Han Li faces the cornered Wild Wolf Gang and Jia Tianlong, offering them only the choice of how they wish to die: suicide by severing their own meridians, or death by his hand.
- Jia Tianlong, mentally cracking, manages to rally his men with a roar, calling on his personal Iron Guards and the smaller gang experts for a desperate last stand.
- As the crossbows fire, Han Li demonstrates a terrifying new level of mastery over the *Smoke Step*, allowing his body to become intangible and letting the bolts pass through him harmlessly before he vanishes from sight completely.
- Han Li then unleashes his true power, using the Fireball Technique in an innovative, melee-oriented way. He blinks in and out of the crowd, and every touch of his hand incinerates a man to ashes instantly.
- In less than a minute, Han Li systematically exterminates the entire combined fighting force of Jia Tianlong's alliance. He saves Jia Tianlong for last, executing him with a full-powered fireball.
- The chapter concludes with Han Li casually musing on how easy killing has become, cementing his psychological shift away from the mortal world.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Welcome to the Immortal-Mortal power gap, xianxia style. This chapter is the textbook definition of "courting death." Jia Tianlong, a mortal warlord with a general's mind for logistics and politics, makes the fatal mistake of assuming his army of trained killers and advanced steel crossbows can threaten a being who has begun to touch the fringes of the Dao. He is wrong.

The *Fireball Technique (火弹术)* is classified in the novel as a "basic spell," one of the five fundamental techniques a Qi Condensation cultivator learns. However, Han Li's display here breaks the mold. He doesn't just hurl a fireball from a distance; his "partial casting" melee application—holding a small, concentrated fireball in his palm and using physical touch as the delivery mechanism—is a tactical innovation born from his martial arts background. This shows that even a basic spell can be turned into a terrifying weapon of mass destruction with the right mindset. Forget the grand, incinerating pillars of fire; the scariest weapon is the one that fits in your palm and can touch a thousand throats in an hour.

Also, note the *Smoke Step (罗烟步)* evolution. While originally a mobility technique, Han Li now uses it to achieve a near-physical intangibility, a side effect of pushing it to its absolute limits. This is a classic xianxia trope: a low-tier technique, when pushed to perfection by a genius, can temporarily mimic the effects of much higher-tier arts.

### Qi Condensation (炼气期)

The first and lowest realm of cultivation, where a practitioner refines ambient qi into their own energy system. Even this low level is enough to make a mortal army helpless.

### Dissolving Cultivation (散功)

The voluntary destruction of one's own cultivation base to revert to a mortal state, often done to save one's life from the side effects of dangerous techniques or poisons.

### Smoke Step (罗烟步)

A high-speed mobility technique from the Seven Mysteries Sect. Han Li has pushed it to a level where his body can briefly become intangible, allowing him to pass through physical attacks.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a major payoff moment that re-contextualizes Han Li's entire journey. It answers the question: "Why is Han Li so paranoid and obsessed with power?" Because this is the kind of world where a single superior being can erase a coalition army without breaking a sweat. His paranoia is not a flaw; it's the only rational response to the world he lives in.

Jia Tianlong's portrayal here is crucial. The incremental rules remind us he is a *calculating, pragmatic warlord with a general's mind for logistics and politics*, not a brute. His plan to use hostages and bribery was sound by mortal rules. His mistake was failing to grasp the qualitative difference a single cultivator represents. His stunned, terrified silence after watching his elite forces be turned to ash serves as the reader's proxy—this is how a competent mortal is supposed to react when facing a being from a higher realm. Han Li’s victory is not earned by a clever trick, but by simple, overwhelming, vertical power.

Pay attention to Han Li’s closing monologue. He doesn't gloat; he lectures. He offers a perfectly logical, if terrifying, business proposal for a cleaner solution. The *Dissolving Cultivation* option he'd discussed earlier with Li Feiyu would have been the *kind* way out. He chose the efficient, illustrative way. This chapter solidifies that in the A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality, power isn't the goal—it's the only viable currency for survival.
