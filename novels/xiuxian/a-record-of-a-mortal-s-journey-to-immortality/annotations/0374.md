## Chapter Overview
Welcome to the Mortal Stream’s favorite sport: the corporate challenge match! Han Li has finally arrived at Eaststone City on Starfall Island, a bustling market hub that makes everything he’s seen so far look like a sleepy village. The squad—Shopkeeper Gu, Wang Changqing, and Han Li—navigates a sea of humanity (literally tens of thousands packed into one square) to reach the challenge grounds. But this isn’t a free-for-all brawl; it’s a structured tournament where Han Li and other low-level cultivators compete for the right to replace established merchant representatives. After an awkward moment where Han Li realizes he can’t read the local script on his lot, he ends up in the ring against a confidently smirking opponent who’s two layers above him. Our boy’s quiet chuckle at the end says everything: this is going to be fun.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li observes the mortal guards’ Spirit Disc, a device that lets ordinary humans identify cultivators by spiritual power. Wang Changqing explains its purpose, marking the first clear example of mortals actively monitoring magical activity in this new region.
- The cart ride through Eaststone City reveals a rigid social hierarchy: pale clothing as the norm, bright colors forbidden, and clear master-servant divisions among pedestrians.
- The island’s once-in-three-months market day has turned the central square into a chaotic, swarming bazaar of tens of thousands, with shops, temporary stalls, and heated negotiations everywhere.
- The challenge bout is a structured tournament: single-elimination, no killing, winner takes the opponent’s lot. The supervising Foundation Establishment elder doesn’t care *how* you win, only that you do.
- Han Li misreads his lot number (a silver symbol he doesn’t recognize) and almost misses his call. He bluffs it off by claiming he thought he was number nine.
- Paired against a confident Qi Condensation Layer 7 cultivator—two full layers above Han Li’s own—who suggests an immediate concession. Han Li’s quiet laugh closes the chapter.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Spirit Discs and Mortal Technology**: The Spirit Disc is a brilliant Mortal Stream worldbuilding device. It means that even without cultivation, mortals have developed methods to police the supernatural. In Chinese xianxia folklore, mortals are often at the mercy of cultivators, but here they’ve built tools to level the playing field—at least in administrative settings. This reflects a practical, "Dark Forest" logic: if cultivators are walking weapons, you build a detector, not a welcome committee.
- **Dress Codes and Social Hierarchy**: The muted clothing palette (white, pale yellow, light green) isn’t just aesthetics. In classical Chinese society, color was a rigid status marker. Bright, vibrant dyes were expensive and reserved for the elite or for formal occasions. Here, the rule seems universal: if you’re inside the city, you dress down. Flashy clothes might draw unwanted attention from cultivators or mark you as a target for exploitation.
- **The Challenge System**: The structured tournament isn’t just a plot device—it’s a window into the island’s political economy. Merchants don’t just hold their positions by wealth; they must defend them through proxy fights. This transforms a commercial territory into a combat arena, reinforcing the Mortal Stream’s central irony: everything, even commerce, is ultimately backed by violence. The “only injure, no killing” rule acknowledges that these are asset disputes, not blood vendettas.

## Reading Guide
Han Li’s quiet, amused chuckle at the end is *chef’s kiss*. He’s looking at a guy who’s two layers above him, who hasn’t even bothered to raise a shield, and who expects a walkover. Han Li knows exactly how many layers *he’s* suppressed, how many trump cards he’s holding, and how quickly he can end this. That laugh is the sound of a predator who’s just been told to play nice with lunch. Also, watch how Han Li handles the local language and script—it’s a pragmatic, low-key struggle that he solves by bluffing. He doesn’t panic over not knowing the characters, he just fakes it and moves on. That’s peak Mortal Stream problem-solving: no drama, just execution.
