## Chapter Overview

So. The universe has finally dropped the other shoe.

Han Li’s naive hope that there might be a quiet, peaceful way to gather the three legendary ingredients for the Foundation Establishment Pill? Obliterated. Wu Feng, the first genuinely helpful senior brother Han Li has met in the Yellow Maple Valley, lays out the brutal truth: the only place these herbs still grow is a sealed ancient forbidden land, and the only way to get them is the "Blood Forbidden Trial"—a quadrennial deathmatch where seven sects throw their Qi Condensation disciples into a magical arena and let them fight over scraps.

And the mortality rate? Let's just say the odds are not in our mortal boy’s favor. Fewer than a quarter come back alive. The rest become fertilizer.

This chapter is a cold, hard dose of systemic reality. The cultivation world isn't just about talent and hard work. It's about being willing to survive a meat grinder for a *chance* at the resources you need to advance. For the first time, Han Li is staring down a choice that has no clever third option, no backup plan, no hidden advantage to reach for. Just calculation, risk, and the terrifying weight of a decision that will determine whether he lives or dies.

Get ready. The stakes just got very, very real.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Source Revealed:** Wu Feng explains that the Jade Marrow Mushroom, Purple Ape Fruit, and Celestial Spirit Fruit are extinct in the wild. They only grow inside a specific forbidden land—a place sealed by an ancient wind-attribute spell.
*   **The "Blood Forbidden Trial":** For centuries, the seven great Yue Nation sects have sent disciples into this place every five years during a brief window when the seal weakens. The mission is codenamed the "Blood Forbidden Trial."
*   **Escalating Violence:** Initially, the disciples just gathered herbs. Now, over a hundred years of escalating conflict, it has become a brutal zero-sum game where disciples from different sects murder each other for the best herbs. The survival rate has dropped to below 25%.
*   **The Incentive Crisis:** The sects tried using heavy rewards (spirit stones, elixirs, even a Foundation Establishment Pill) to attract volunteers, but the horrific mortality rate made it so unpopular that disciples actively dodge the mission, forcing sects to incentivize it rather than mandate it.
*   **Han Li’s Calculative Despair:** Han Li processes this information with cold pragmatism, not panic. He immediately calculates his own odds, acknowledging he is an average fighter with good tools and a sharp mind—but he knows that's not enough to guarantee a spot in the surviving quarter.
*   **The Trap of the System:** Wu Feng clarifies the root cause: the pills are shared according to the *amount* of ingredients each sect provides, making inter-sect cooperation structurally impossible. The system is designed for cannibalism.
*   **No Easy Outs:** Han Li realizes that searching for the herbs outside of Yue Nation is a dead end, and the opportunity is only six months away. He is left with the single, terrifying option of participating in the Trial.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The "Blood Forbidden Trial" as a Systemic Prisoner's Dilemma:** The "Blood Forbidden Trial" in *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality* is a perfect model of what game theorists call a "prisoner's dilemma" applied to a xianxia context. The sects could all cooperate, share the herbs, and produce pills for everyone. But because the reward (the pill) is distributed according to the *proportion* of herbs each sect brings back, the rational choice for *each* sect is to maximize their take, even at the cost of bloodshed. This turns a resource-gathering mission into a zero-sum death game. This is a microcosm of the entire "Mortal Stream" philosophy: the world's systemic structure itself is designed to create conflict, not cooperation. It’s not that the cultivators are all evil; it’s that the rules of the game force them to be ruthless in order to survive.

**The Forbidden Land as a Xianxia Trope (and its Deconstruction):** In many conventional xianxia, a "secret realm" or "forbidden land" is a trove of lucky chances, a place where plucky protagonists find secret inheritances and defy heaven. *RMJI* takes that trope and drags it into the mud. The "trial" isn't about uncovering ancient secrets; it's a grim, industrial-scale harvest operation that has devolved into a meat market. The "lucky chance" is being the one who survives a low-odds fight. This deconstruction is a key part of the "Mortal Stream" appeal—it takes the familiar, romanticized elements of the genre and shows you their bloody, cynical skeleton.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in escalating the stakes without any action. Forget fight scenes—this is a piece of compelling, oppressive worldbuilding delivered through dialogue. Pay attention to how Han Li processes the information. He doesn't rage, he doesn't whine, he doesn't declare he'll "defy heaven." He goes quiet. He runs the numbers. He feels the trap closing in.

This is the first time Han Li’s core strength—preparation and cleverness—seems insufficient. The problem isn't a single enemy he can outsmart; it's a systemic death sentence with bad odds. How he reacts to this feeling of being cornered will define his character. Will the solution be more cleverness, or will he have to change something fundamental about himself?

Also, note the complete absence of any "righteous" solution. Han Li doesn't think about fighting for justice or protecting the weak. The only question is survival and advancement. That’s the Mortal Stream difference. This chapter sets the stage for a major turning point, and the tension is almost unbearable. Buckle up.
