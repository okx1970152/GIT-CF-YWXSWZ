## Chapter Overview
Alright, fellow Daoists, buckle up. This chapter is a masterclass in applied paranoia. Our boy Han Li has spent days poking and prodding his shiny new toy—the stolen talisman—and he’s come to a chilling conclusion: all treasures have a shelf life. He’s not just hoarding power; he’s counting the seconds until his ultimate trump card turns to dust. Meanwhile, the mortal leaders of the Seven Mysteries Sect are getting *very* nervous about the demonic physician in their midst. They send their cat’s-paw, our old friend Li Feiyu, to probe Han Li’s intentions. But they’re playing chess against a grandmaster. Han Li’s response is a brilliant two-step: promise a meeting at noon, then show up at dawn to rattle the cage and establish dominance before negotiations even begin. It’s a short but dense chapter that shifts the power dynamic in the valley completely.

## Key Plot Points
*   **The Talisman’s Hidden Cost:** Through systematic analysis, Han Li identifies three harsh limitations to his gray-light talisman: (1) It drains his magic power rapidly, lasting only a quarter of an hour. (2) It’s ineffective beyond a twenty-zhang range. (3) Most critically, its spiritual light is fading with each use, indicating a finite lifespan. This forces him to treat it as a consumable trump card, not a reliable main weapon.
*   **Li Feiyu’s Errand:** Li Feiyu arrives to deliver a message from the sect leadership, who are deeply unsettled by Han Li’s recent display of power. They want to gauge his intentions and have promised to reward Li Feiyu for his service, a clear attempt to use a friend as a lever.
*   **The Strategic Commitment:** Han Li agrees to a meeting, setting it for noon the next day. This seems like a concession, a sign that he’s willing to play ball with the sect’s hierarchy.
*   **The Preemptive Strike:** Breaking his own promise, Han Li infiltrates Wang Juechu’s bedroom at dawn. The chapter ends with him using the Celestial Eye Technique, which he’s weaponized into a form of “Soul-Enticing Art,” to psychically intimidate the sect leader, flipping the script from being the one who is judged to being the one who passes judgment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
### Talismans in Xianxia
This chapter offers a fantastic, grounded look at one of xianxia’s most iconic items: the talisman. Forget the fancy spells; a talisman is essentially a pre-cast ‘magic in a bottle’. A powerful cultivator can inscribe a spell onto a piece of paper, infusing it with their own energy. A lesser cultivator (or even a mortal with enough qi) can then activate it, unleashing a power far beyond their own capability. Han Li’s analysis highlights a crucial trope: **talismans are not infinite.** They are consumables, a finite resource that must be used wisely. This reinforces the Dark Forest logic of the ‘Mortal Stream’—even a god-tier treasure is just another resource to be managed and hoarded with paranoia. The concept of a talisman's "spirit" fading aligns with the Daoist idea that all things, even man-made objects, possess a form of life force or spiritual nature that can be depleted.

### The Celestial Eye Technique
Han Li’s repurposing of the Celestial Eye Technique (天眼术) is a brilliant bit of tactical innovation. In its basic form, this is a perception spell, allowing a cultivator to see the flow of spiritual energy. But as Han Li demonstrates, a skilled practitioner can weaponize even a harmless technique. By channeling his spiritual sense through it, he can project his presence with such intensity that it becomes a physical and mental assault on a mortal. This is a classic xianxia trope—the “aura of power” or “spiritual pressure” used by high-level cultivators to dominate lower beings. It’s not a real technique; it’s just the sheer weight of one’s existence being forced through a narrow lens, a chilling indication of the absolute gap between the mortal and the transcendent.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a pure delight for those who love the strategic, paranoid heart of the ‘Mortal Stream’. Pay close attention to how Han Li’s mind works. He doesn’t just use a treasure; he dispassionately evaluates its cost, range, and lifespan. He is a master of **asset management**. Also, watch his social strategy. Promising a noon meeting and arriving at dawn is a classic power move. He expects the sect to be prepared and on guard; by arriving early, he catches them off-balance, in their most vulnerable state. The Celestial Eye Technique trick is the final brushstroke—he doesn’t negotiate; he *intimidates*. He forces the conversation to start from a position of his own strength. This chapter highlights that in the cultivation world, the mind is often a more potent weapon than any glowing sword. Get ready to see how that intimidation pays off.
