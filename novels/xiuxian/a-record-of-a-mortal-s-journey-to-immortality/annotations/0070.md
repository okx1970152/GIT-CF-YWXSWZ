## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow travelers of the mortal path! Chapter 70 is a masterclass in something this novel does better than almost any other xianxia on the market: **the silent, high-stakes politics of small sects**, where the poison is often applied with words before it ever touches a needle. If you thought the last few chapters were all about chemical compounds and combat, think again—this time, the battlefield is a sickroom, and the weapons are compliments.

Han Li finds himself caught in a quiet tug-of-war between two of the Seven Mysteries Sect’s higher-ups. On one side, the ever-smiling, knife-hiding Sect Leader Ma. On the other, the hotheaded Elder Zhao. Their verbal sparring is not just petty squabbling—it’s a display of resource warfare, where a talented young doctor is the prize. And through it all, Han Li plays his favorite role: the stone-faced, unreadable outsider who would rather be anywhere else. The chapter’s real treat, however, is the final reveal of Elder Li’s horrifying condition—a moment that reframes the entire rescue mission as a near-certain death sentence.

## Key Plot Points

- **The Setup**: As Ma Rong begins introducing Han Li to Elder Li’s family, the tension between Sect Leader Ma and Elder Zhao is immediately palpable. Their exchange is brittle and layered with double meanings.
- **Ma’s Scheme**: Sect Leader Ma openly praises Han Li’s skill, knowing it will provoke Elder Zhao’s skepticism. His goal is not to help Han Li, but to isolate him from other factions by poisoning their perception of him. His tactic is effective: Elder Zhao blurts out his distrust the moment Ma presses.
- **Han Li’s Internal Calculus**: Our protagonist sees through the game instantly. He recognizes that Ma has been trying to recruit him for months, and that these “breakdowns” of other relationships serve only to keep Han Li available. Han Li’s private disdain for the sect’s petty power games is a key character moment: he is not tempted by local authority.
- **The Introductions**: The scene moves at a deliberately slow, almost bureaucratic pace, as Ma Rong introduces each family member. This pacing serves to heighten the tension of what’s to come. The highlight is Li Feiyu’s deliberate cold-shoulder, which is undercut by a hidden, childish prank.
- **The Reveal**: All pretense drops when Han Li sees Elder Li. The description is brutal: copper-coin-sized, multicolored poison spots covering every inch of skin; blue lips; a face shrouded in black qi. The chapter ends on this stark note, making it clear that this is not a routine medical call—it is a desperate, likely impossible, rescue.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Factional Politics in Sects (派系斗争)**: In the Mortal Stream universe, sect politics are not an abstract backdrop—they are the primary vector for resource conflict. The Seven Mysteries Sect, despite its small scale, is a microcosm of the entire cultivation world. Every senior member is a faction leader hoarding talent, secrets, and loyalty. Sect Leader Ma’s tactic of “breaking bridges” between Han Li and other elders is not childish; it is textbook resource denial in a closed, zero-sum environment.
- **The Meaning of Han Li’s Disdain**: Han Li’s contempt for these local power struggles is a direct consequence of his worldview shift after escaping Doctor Mo. He has seen the true ladder of the cultivation world, and it makes a mortal sect’s internal turf wars look like ants fighting over a grain of rice. This is a crucial narrative anchor: even as a low-tier cultivator, Han Li thinks in terms of *realms and lifespan*, not titles and land.
- **The Poison’s Description (毒斑症状)**: The multicolored spots and black facial qi are classic descriptors of extreme Yin-type toxins in Chinese medical fiction. The color itself is a diagnostic clue: the brighter and more varied the spots, the more the poison has invaded the body’s energy system. Black qi on the face is a universal death flag.
- **Face and Flattery**: The entire first half of the chapter is a performance of *face*—Ma’s public praise, Zhao’s blunt retort, Ma’s serene absorption of the insult. These are not incidental social faux pas; they are deliberate maneuvers within a rigid hierarchy where public perception determines negotiable power.

### Knife-hiding smile (笑里藏刀)

A classical Chinese idiom describing someone who is outwardly friendly but inwardly ruthless, a staple of sect intrigue.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a slow burn, and that’s exactly the point. Readers used to faster-paced action scenes should pause and appreciate the craftsmanship: the entire first half is a verbal standoff where nothing is said directly, yet every sentence is a chess move. Ma is not just an annoying bureaucrat—he is a perfectly drawn example of a “knife-hiding smile” (笑里藏刀), a predator who uses politeness as his primary weapon.

The contrast between the quiet, almost theatrical tension of the introductions and the sudden, visceral horror of Elder Li’s condition is the chapter’s structural genius. Han Li’s detached, clinical observation of the poison is a return to form after his recent emotional breakthrough. He does not panic or recoil—he simply calculates the odds. And those odds, as the text makes clear, are stacked heavily against success.

Also, note the Li Feiyu moment. It’s tiny, almost throwaway, but it underscores a rare space in this grim universe: friendship that survives without calculation. It’s a sliver of warmth in a world that has been, up to now, relentlessly cold.

Enjoy the calm before the next storm, because if Elder Li’s symptoms are any indication, this rescue mission will demand everything Han Li has—and even that may not be enough.
