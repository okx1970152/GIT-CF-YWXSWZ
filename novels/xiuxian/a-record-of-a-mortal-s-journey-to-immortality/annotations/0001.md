## Chapter Overview

This opening chapter establishes Han Li, a ten-year-old boy from a poor mountain village, known by the nickname 'Er Lengzi' (Second Fool) despite being one of the cleverest children in the village. Through a visit from his third uncle—an outer disciple of the Seven Mysteries Sect—Han Li is offered the chance to take the sect's inner disciple examination. The chapter paints a vivid picture of rural poverty, family dynamics, and the simple hopes that drive Han Li: to earn money, become a respectable craftsman, and one day return to support his family. It quietly sets the stage for his departure from his home and his unwitting entry into a far larger and more dangerous world.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li is introduced as a precocious and observant child, already dreaming of leaving the village to seek a better life.
- His family's poverty is clearly depicted: seven members, barely enough to eat, and reliance on an eldest brother's apprenticeship for income.
- Third Uncle, a successful relative in the nearby city, arranges for Han Li to take the Seven Mysteries Sect's inner disciple test—a rare opportunity for a poor family.
- Han Li's father hesitates at the mention of 'Jianghu' and 'sects' but agrees upon learning of the potential monthly income and social standing.
- Han Li feels sadness and anxiety as he leaves home, but resolves to earn money and return quickly—unaware that his path will lead far beyond ordinary wealth.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- 'Jianghu' (江湖): Originally meaning 'rivers and lakes,' this term denotes the martial arts underworld—a loose society of fighters, sects, wanderers, and outlaws operating outside official governance. In xianxia and wuxia fiction, the Jianghu is both a setting and a code of conduct, often characterized by honor, violence, and personal loyalty.
- The Seven Mysteries Sect (七玄门) follows the classic Chinese sect structure: an outer division handling business and recruitment (where Third Uncle belongs) and an inner division where true martial arts are taught. This dual structure reflects the practical nature of such organizations—wealth and influence are as important as combat skill.
- The test for inner disciples is a common trope in cultivation fiction, often involving examinations of bone structure, willpower, or innate talent. Passing such a test can lift a peasant family into a new social class.

## Reading Guide

Pay attention to Han Li's early characterization: he is called 'Er Lengzi' ironically—he is not stupid but quietly intelligent, observant, and cautious. His motivation is purely practical: to earn money and improve his family's circumstances. This sets the tone for the entire novel, which consistently prioritizes survival and pragmatism over heroic ideals. The chapter also introduces the Seven Mysteries Sect as an entity of both opportunity and danger—a theme that will deepen as the story progresses. Readers familiar with xianxia should note that this is not a 'chosen one' start; Han Li is an ordinary boy from an ordinary family, and his journey will be one of hard-earned progress.
