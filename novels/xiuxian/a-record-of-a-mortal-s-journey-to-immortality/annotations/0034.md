## Chapter Overview

If you've been waiting for Han Li to finally catch a break, this chapter is *that* moment—sort of. Our boy is on the hunt for a real combat edge, and the Blinking Sword Art might just be the wild card he needs. But as always in the Mortal Stream, nothing comes without a cost, and the tension ratchets up when he returns home to find Doctor Mo's silent enforcer waiting for him like a faithful, terrifying watchdog. Get ready for a quieter, more introspective chapter where the real battles are fought inside Han Li's head.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li learns the full backstory of the Blinking Sword Art: a technique that requires *no true qi*, has never been successfully practiced by anyone, and only exists in the Seven Peaks Hall collection because of its creator's dying wish.
- Li Feiyu, showing his signature reckless loyalty, offers to steal the original manual from the Seven Peaks Hall rather than just copying it—a much riskier move.
- Han Li's suspicion of Li Feiyu's carelessness leads him to agree to the original manual plan, showing his preference for control and accuracy over convenience.
- Li Feiyu leaves; Han Li's smile fades immediately, revealing the weight of his situation.
- Han Li observes the mysterious Iron Servant standing motionless outside Doctor Mo's door, analyzing him as a perfect, emotionless puppet with no apparent weaknesses.
- A subtle detail: Han Li feels a faint sense of familiarity when looking at the Iron Servant from behind, hinting at Zhang Tie's fate.
- Han Li uses his new photographic memory ability (from reaching the fifth layer of the Eternal Spring Art) to mentally simulate and refine the techniques he just learned.
- The chapter ends with Han Li calculating his remaining resources: barely enough pills for the sixth layer, and less than half a year until Doctor Mo's deadline. His last hope is the Blinking Sword Art.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Blinking Sword Art is a classic "chicken rib" (鸡肋) technique**—valuable in theory (requires no qi, so anyone can use it), but useless in practice (no one has ever mastered it). This idiom comes from a famous Three Kingdoms story where a strategist describes a useless possession as "tasteless to eat, yet a pity to throw away." Han Li's gamble on this technique reflects the entire *Mortal Stream* philosophy: when you're weak, you take risks that the strong would never consider.

**The fifth-layer breakthrough granting photographic memory** is a common xianxia trope that marks a qualitative shift in mental processing. It's not just "getting smarter"—it's the first hint of what will later become *Spiritual Sense* (神识), a cultivator's non-physical perception. For now, it gives Han Li a massive training advantage: he can now mentally replay and refine techniques without physical practice, massively accelerating his learning curve.

**The Iron Servant's behavior—standing motionless for entire days without speaking or showing fatigue**—echoes themes from Chinese martial arts fiction where loyal bodyguards are trained or conditioned to be perfect, unthinking weapons. The growing sense of familiarity Han Li feels from behind strongly implies a dark fate for Zhang Tie.

### Chicken Rib (鸡肋 / Ji Lei)

A classical Chinese idiom describing something that is "tasteless to eat, yet a pity to discard"; refers to knowledge or resources that are valuable in theory but useless without the means to apply them.

### Iron Servant (铁奴)

The tall, silent man serving Doctor Mo; shows no emotion, never speaks, has inhuman stamina, and obeys orders without question. Han Li suspects he is trained to be a perfect puppet or weapon.

## Reading Guide

- **Watch Han Li's mask slip**: His smile vanishes the moment Li Feiyu leaves, showing that even his rare moments of levity are calculated shields.
- **The Blinking Sword Art is NOT a joke**: In most cultivation stories, a technique "no one has ever mastered" is either a trap or a hidden gem. Han Li's intuition is screaming "hidden gem," and given his track record, you should trust it.
- **The Iron Servant's familiarity is a Chekhov's gun**: If you remember Zhang Tie's disappearance, this detail is *very* ominous. Pay attention.
- **The deadline pressure is real**: Han Li has less than half a year. The pills are running out. This is the calm before a storm.
- **Enjoy the rare "training montage" feel**: This chapter is quieter than the action-packed ones, but it's setting up a critical pivot point in Han Li's combat capability. The calm is deceptive.
