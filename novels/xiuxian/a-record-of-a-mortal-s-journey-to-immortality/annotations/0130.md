## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the Tainan Small Exchange, fellow Daoists—a bazaar where independent cultivators rub shoulders with family elites, and where a loner’s survival depends on finding the right cover. In this chapter, Han Li runs into a group of scattered cultivators who are actively recruiting. They offer him protection in numbers, and he offers them a respectable level of power. But Han Li being Han Li, he doesn’t jump in blind. He asks the right questions, calibrates his odds, and only then agrees to ‘join forces’—on a temporary basis, of course. This isn’t a tug-of-war of friendship; it’s a cold, functional merger of assets. And as always, Han Li’s mask of a curious newcomer hides a machine of perpetual calculation.

## Key Plot Points

- A Daoist calling himself Songwen approaches Han Li, correctly guessing he is an unattached independent cultivator. He invites Han Li to join a small group of scattered cultivators for mutual protection at the fair.
- Han Li does not accept immediately. He demands introductions and clarification on what membership entails—a sign of his ingrained caution even when offered a seemingly harmless alliance.
- The group is introduced: Hei Mu and Hei Jin, brothers from Canglang Ridge; the Red Lotus Ascetic from Feilian Cave; Master Kusang of Pulu Mountain; and the married couple Hu Pinggu and Xiong Dali from Tianshui Village.
- Daoist Songwen explains that he is only the de facto leader for external disputes; otherwise, everyone is free to act independently.
- Han Li agrees to join temporarily, having calculated that the arrangement is beneficial with no apparent downside.
- Two more members are mentioned but absent: one sleeping, one wandering. Songwen admits they are “a bit special.”
- Songwen provides Han Li with a talisman key to the group’s base building and a set of etiquette guidelines for the fair.
- After Songwen’s group leaves, Han Li examines the talisman, tucks it away, and heads into the plaza to browse the stalls.
- The trading methods are briefly explained: barter is the dominant mode, and it is not uncommon for sellers to wait days for a single exchange.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- **Independent Cultivators (散修, sanxiu) vs. Family Clans:** In the cultivation world, a ‘family’ isn’t just a bloodline—it’s a political-economic bloc with generations of accumulated resources, techniques, and protection. Independent cultivators have none of that safety net. Organizing into temporary alliances is a survival tactic born from raw necessity, not camaraderie. Songwen’s group is the equivalent of a medieval trade guild for the powerless.
- **Talisman Keys (符箓禁制):** The talisman Songwen gives Han Li is a miniature version of a classic formation-breaking token. In Xianxia, barriers are often keyed to a specific talisman or technique, turning mundane-looking pieces of paper into functional access cards. This reinforces the idea that even something as simple as a door lock in the cultivation world is layered with mystical security.
- **Transient Cooperation in Dark Forest Logic:** Han Li’s immediate mental calculation—“this is beneficial with no downside”—perfectly encapsulates the Mortal Stream survival principle. He does not trust Songwen; he trusts the configuration of power and information. The group’s existence is a resource to be used, not a bond to be cherished.

## Reading Guide

- Notice how Han Li does not volunteer a single unnecessary piece of information about himself. Even his admission of being a sanxiu is a calculated disclosure—the group already guessed it, so denying it would only draw suspicion.
- The scarred woman, Hu Pinggu, shows immediate disdain for the absent members, hinting at internal tensions within this “mutual aid” group. Keep an eye on those two missing members—they are described as “special,” which in this genre often means either incredibly useful or incredibly dangerous.
- The fair’s two-trade system (barter vs. spirit stone) will become relevant later. Barter is how protagonists usually stumble into once-in-a-lifetime deals—or once-in-a-lifetime traps.
- Songwen’s demeanor is polite and open, but remember: in the cultivation world, a friendly face often hides a long memory and a longer knife. Han Li is not wrong to be cautious; the question is whether Songwen’s kindness will hold.
