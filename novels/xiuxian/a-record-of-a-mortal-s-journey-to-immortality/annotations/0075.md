## Chapter Overview

Picture this: you've been grinding for weeks, chanting and waving your hands at a piece of yellow paper, expecting it to glow. Nothing. You try it on a sword, hoping to make it float. Still nothing. For any aspiring immortal, this is the moment the dream crashes into a wall of cold, hard reality. In Chapter 75, our favorite pragmatic cultivator, Han Li, does exactly that—he slams into the limits of mortal attempts at immortal arts, only to pull a brilliant, resourceful pivot that screams "Mortal Stream protagonist." This chapter is a masterclass in Han Li's philosophy: if the rules don't let you win, change the game. He officially gives up on two spells he can't cast due to lacking specialized materials and instead does what he does best—he MacGyvers a fusion of his martial techniques and spells into deadly new combat arts. And just as he's feeling confident enough to finally head to Lan Province for a cure, the plot throws a curveball: the Wild Wolf Gang wants peace talks. Cue the internal sect drama and a very unhappy Li Feiyu.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter begins with Han Li's frustrating dead-end in his spell research. He discovers that the Spirit-Fixing Talisman and the Telekinetic Art are impossible for him to perform, not because he is inept, but because they require specific tools and materials used by cultivators. His self-made talismans, drawn with silver powder on yellow paper, look the part but are utterly inert. Similarly, his attempts to move objects with the Telekinetic Art fail because he needs "cultivator-specific items," and even the artifacts he looted from Doctor Mo—the Soul Summoning Bell and the ritual silver blades—prove to be the wrong type of object.

The true highlight of the chapter is Han Li's pragmatic and creative response to this setback. Instead of wasting more time on a lost cause, he pivots to combining his already mastered techniques. His first success is the integration of the Wind Riding Technique and the Smoke Step. This isn't a true fusion, but a perfectly timed sequence that compensates for each technique's weakness: the Wind Riding Technique lacks agility, and the Smoke Step is stamina-intensive. The result is a movement technique of terrifying speed and elusiveness.

His second innovation is even more ingenious. Finding the standard Fireball Technique too slow to hit a martial arts master, Han Li forgoes launching the projectile entirely. Instead, he holds the partially cast fireball in his hand, using its lore-breaking destructive power as a short-range, melee weapon. This, combined with his new movement speed, creates a potent assassination tool. Following this breakthrough, Han Li's cultivation of the Eternal Spring Art also quietly advances to the eighth layer, significantly boosting his magic power.

Just as Han Li plans his trip to Lan Province, the chapter cuts to the sect-wide shock of the Wild Wolf Gang's sudden proposal for peace talks, a move that divides the Seven Mysteries Sect's leadership.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Chapter 75 is a brilliant demonstration of the "hardware constraints" placed on low-level cultivators. In the xianxia genre, spells are often assumed to be learnable by anyone who has the manual and enough qi. *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality* defies this trope by grounding its magic system in material requirements. Cultivator talismans (法符) are not just paper with special ink; they are crafted from unique, often rare materials that are part of a completely separate economy from the mortal world. This reinforces the harsh class divide: a cultivator's power is not just a matter of personal talent, but also of access to a closed, monopolistic resource network.

The chapter also subtly introduces the concept of "spell-casting focus." Han Li failed to move objects with the Telekinetic Art because he used "ordinary items." In xianxia, magical instruments and weapons are often imbued with spiritual properties that allow them to resonate with a cultivator's qi. The technique itself is useless without a proper focus, a concept reminiscent of wands in Western fantasy but rooted in the idea that materials can be "refined" to conduct spiritual energy.

Furthermore, Han Li's "Fusion of Martial Arts and Spells" (法武并用) is a classic trope in many xianxia stories where the protagonist, lacking pure magical talent or resources, uses martial arts (武学) as a base structure and enhances it with spells. This is a form of magical engineering, not raw power, and it perfectly encapsulates the "Mortal Stream" ethos of overcoming systemic disadvantage through cleverness.

### Dissolving Cultivation (散功)

The voluntary destruction of one's cultivation base to revert to a mortal state, often done to save one's life from the effects of dangerous techniques.

## Reading Guide

This is a "tools and tactics" chapter, showing Han Li's growth not as a sudden power-up, but as a series of smart, incremental upgrades. Pay attention to how Han Li's frustration at his failure does not lead to despair or a quest for a lucky break. Instead, he calmly accepts the external limitation—he lacks the right materials—and redesigns his combat approach around what he *does* have. This is the core of his character.

The chapter also sets up a fascinating juxtaposition. Han Li, having solved his immediate combat issues, is now more confident than ever. However, the sudden proposal of peace talks from an enemy that is winning (the Wild Wolf Gang) introduces a new, large-scale external threat. The question for readers is: will Han Li be able to slip away unnoticed, or will this sect-wide political storm drag him into another conflict before he can seek his cure?
