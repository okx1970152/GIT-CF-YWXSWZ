## Chapter Overview

Han Li has his hands full against the transparent Ice Demon, forced to fight a grueling close-quarters battle while seething with frustration. The rest of the group, paralyzed by fear of the partially transformed Blood Servants, hesitates to press their advantage. Liu Jing faces a painful decision: retreat and protect his life but lose his hard-earned reputation as the righteous, axe-wielding nemesis of heretic cultivators, or burn his ultimate trump card — a legendary True Treasure that costs more than just spirit stones. This chapter is less about monster fighting and more about the psychology of face, reputation, and the terrifying price of taking a stand in the cultivation world.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li saves Senior Brother Wang but is locked in combat with the Ice Demon, using his Smoke Step and silver sword to keep the demonic man grounded and away from the others.
- Han Li internally criticizes the other disciples for not attacking the remaining three transformation cocoons, understanding their fear but knowing it will only make the battle worse.
- Liu Jing is revealed to be motivated by a deep-seated need for admiration and a fear of losing his righteous reputation, stemming from his childhood trauma of being captured by heretic cultivators.
- Liu Jing decides not to retreat, knowing it would destroy his image, and instead chooses to activate a secret weapon: a True Treasure.
- The True Treasure is introduced as a rare, one-time consumable artifact vastly more powerful than a normal Talisman Treasure, requiring a specific bloodline to use.
- The chapter ends with the reveal of the fiery talisman, leaving the upcoming confrontation hanging in tense anticipation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The “True Treasure” (真宝, Zhen Bao) is a fascinating piece of worldbuilding that reinforces the Mortal Stream’s obsession with cost. Unlike Western magical items, a True Treasure is a family heirloom that literally drains the permanent power of the original magic treasure. This means a founding ancestor’s decision to leave one descendant a single, powerful disposable trump card means their own signature weapon is permanently crippled for everyone else. It’s a cruel economic choice — a one-time asset for the now versus a permanent asset for the lineage. The fact that such treasures exist but are rare enough to be legendary perfectly captures the novel’s logic: even the ultimate inheritance has a hidden tax.

## Reading Guide

Watch how Liu Jing’s motivations unfold in this chapter. He’s not just a hero; he’s a man whose trauma has shaped him into a reputation addict. His decision to use the True Treasure isn’t just about killing the enemy — it’s about not losing face. The moment of hesitation, the burning need for approval, the cold calculus of saving his image even at the cost of a priceless heirloom — this is the Dark Forest logic applied to social currency. It’s also a stark reminder that in the Mortal Stream, even a righteous act carries a hidden receipt. Liu Jing is about to pay a price he might regret.
