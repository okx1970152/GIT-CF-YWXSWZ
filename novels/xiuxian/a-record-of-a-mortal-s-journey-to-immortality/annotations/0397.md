## Chapter Overview

Welcome to the settlement arc! After months of bumpy travel across the Starfall Sea, our favorite stone-faced survivalist finally lands in Heaven Star City—the cultivation world’s equivalent of a glittering, ultra-expensive metropolis. But Han Li doesn’t come to sightsee. He comes to *park*. And chapter 397 is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s quieter virtues: logistics, investment, forward planning, and the sheer *sticker shock* of renting a top-tier cave dwelling on the Sacred Mountain. The chapter is a slow, deliberate exhale after the chaos of open water, and it’s packed with worldbuilding that reveals the brutal economics of the Starfall Sea’s power center. Get ready for spirit stone accounts, formation-layering, jade-plating, and beetle-releasing—all rendered with Han Li’s signature cold, methodical precision.

## Key Plot Points

- Lu Er explains the Sacred Mountain’s layered rental system: 81 layers total, the top 30 reserved for Star Palace elites, the peak for the Saintly Masters themselves. Han Li learns the market.
- Han Li decides to rent a cave dwelling rather than a house, prioritizing privacy and security over cost.
- Qu Hun is deployed as the face-man during the rental negotiation, leveraging his Core Formation aura to command respect without Han Li revealing his own strength.
- The rental fee is a jaw-dropping 2,700 spirit stones for a century on the 39th layer—a sum that visibly pains Han Li but is accepted as a worthy long-term investment.
- After touring the city, settling accounts with Lu Er, and moving in, Han Li crashes into a deep, exhausted sleep—a rare moment of physical vulnerability after months of travel.
- The next morning, Han Li layers three additional great formations outside the cave dwelling, reinforcing security beyond the original restrictions.
- He stocks the empty medicinal garden with spirit herbs for future accelerated ripening.
- Finally, he buys a large quantity of fine jade, plates an entire secret chamber with it, and deploys the Gold Devourer Beetles inside—an investment in long-term asset cultivation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Sacred Mountain Layer Economy** — The 81-layer system of Heaven Star City’s Sacred Mountain is a perfect microcosm of the cultivation world’s structural hierarchy. It’s not just about altitude; it’s about *access*. The first 50 layers are the “free market” for outsiders with spirit stones—the middle class of the cultivation world. The next 30 layers are reserved for Star Palace insiders, a gated community of institutional loyalty. And the top layer, the Sacred Palace, is the seat of absolute power, accessible only to the two Saintly Masters. In the Mortal Stream, geography *is* social class, and the price of a cave dwelling is the price of proximity to power.

**Spirit Vein Rent Logic** — The white-robed cultivator’s pitch is a brutal sales pitch: the Sacred Mountain’s spirit vein is among the top five in the Starfall Sea, and cultivation speed increases by more than a quarter on layers above the 30th. This isn’t flavor text—it’s a statement of *return on investment*. In the cultivation world, time is the only non-renewable resource. Paying 2,700 stones for a century is a calculation: will the accelerated cultivation progress earn back more than that amount in opportunity cost? Han Li’s grudging acceptance confirms that he has run the numbers and found the deal acceptable—a classic Mortal Stream transaction.

**Jade-Lined Chambers** — Jade is not a luxury material in the cultivation world; it is a *functional* one. In Chinese folk tradition and Daoist alchemical practice, jade is believed to contain pure qi and to ward off impure energies. In *RMJI*, lining a chamber with it creates an environment that preserves the purity of spirit beasts and elixirs. Han Li’s decision to plate an entire room with high-quality fine jade is not aesthetics—it’s an optimal environment investment for the Gold Devourer Beetles, ensuring they develop under ideal conditions. That’s real cultivation-world cost accounting.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the calm before the storm—a logistical interlude that the Mortal Stream does so well. **Don’t expect fireworks.** Expect receipts, spirit stone ledgers, and the quiet satisfaction of a man building his foundation. Watch how Han Li delegates through Qu Hun, preserving his own anonymity. Feel his jaw clench at the rental fee—then immediately pivot to acceptance because the math works out. This is *how you survive as a monster*: you don’t just fight well; you *set up your base* better than anyone else. The three formation layers are a statement of intent. The jade chamber for the beetles is a long-term bet. This is Han Li playing the long game, and it is *immensely satisfying* to see him finally have a place to call his own—even if it costs 2,700 stones a century.
