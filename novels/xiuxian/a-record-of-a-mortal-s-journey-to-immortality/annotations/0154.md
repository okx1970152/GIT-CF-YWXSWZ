## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to the world's least rewarding library trip! This chapter is a masterclass in **Mortal Stream disappointment**: Han Li finally gets access to the sect's alchemy archive—and finds a pile of mundane medical textbooks, folk remedy pamphlets, and a poison manual that would make a Jianghu physician shrug. The legendary Yuelu Hall, repository of immortal secrets, is staffed by a smirking old miser who charges extortionate spirit-stone rates for the privilege of sifting through trash. It's a bitter, funny, and deeply *real* moment that reinforces the novel's core truth: nothing is free, nothing is easy, and even the grandest institutions run on greed and bureaucracy.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li enters Yuelu Hall, a converted limestone cavern with three passages marked "Artifacts," "Elixirs," and a bare third passage.
- He chooses the Elixir passage, where he meets Old Xu, a Foundation Establishment cultivator with a ruddy face, a cheerful grin, and an immediate, shameless love of money.
- Old Xu sets the price: one low-grade spirit stone per hour for browsing, and ten stones per document for copying—extortionate rates that would bankrupt most low-level disciples.
- Han Li pays and ascends; the second floor reveals only two shabby bookshelves, a grimy table, a broken chair, and a depressing collection of mundane medical books and odd treatises.
- Flipping through the entire collection, Han Li finds not a single alchemy text: only Hua's Secret Gold-Needle Art (already memorized), folk prescriptions, and a poison master's memoir.
- The bamboo slips on the table do contain a few elixir recipes, but they are all low-grade formulas on par with his current pills—useless for an eleventh-layer cultivator.
- His final hope rests on two worn jade slips, the last unread items in the room.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Price of Knowledge (知识的代价):** Old Xu's pricing scheme is not just narrative comedy—it reflects a structural reality of the cultivation world. In many xianxia, access to knowledge is gated not by merit but by **spirit stones** (the universal currency). A low-grade disciple earning 20–30 stones per year faces a cruel choice: spend them on cultivation resources, or spend them on information that might someday yield a breakthrough. The system is designed to keep the poor poor. Old Xu is not a uniquely evil merchant; he is a symptom of an economy where even "free" sect resources are monetized by the powerful.

**Jade Slips (玉筒):** These are the standard storage medium for cultivation texts. Made from processed jade, they can hold far more information than paper or bamboo, and they are often inscribed with spiritual imprints that only cultivators can read. Their faded, unidentifiable appearance in this chapter hints at extreme age or neglect, making them either priceless artifacts or worthless junk—a gamble Han Li must take.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a **bait-and-switch** in the best Mortal Stream tradition: it builds the expectation of a grand library reveal, then undercuts it with shabby shelves, a miserly librarian, and books that would be more at home in a mortal clinic than an immortal sect. The humor comes from the *tedium*—Han Li's frustration is palpable, and Old Xu's transparent greed (plus the image of him polishing a single spirit stone like a dragon with a coin) is a delightful bit of character work. Pay close attention to the third, unmarked passage; its presence in the hall's layout is a quiet narrative hook that may pay off later. And note that Han Li does not complain or storm out—he coldly invests another spirit stone to check the jade slips. That is the protagonist's core discipline: he does not waste energy on outrage; he pivots to his next tactical option.

## Deeper Lore: Laojun and the Alchemical Tradition
The "Elixir" passage and the entire library setup echo a trope rooted in Daoist alchemy, which has two major branches: *Outer Alchemy* (wai dan), the physical refinement of elixirs in a cauldron, and *Inner Alchemy* (nei dan), the cultivation of energy within the body. The sect's storage of mundane medical texts alongside elixir recipes reflects a deeper truth: in a world built on *qi* manipulation, even a poison manual or a needle therapy book can become a valuable resource for someone clever enough to repurpose it. Han Li's disappointment is our cue to remember that in a Dark Forest, knowledge is never wasted—only the price tag stings.
