## Chapter Overview

Han Li’s experiment with the mysterious Green Liquid takes a horrifying turn, confirming that he has stumbled upon something far more destructive than a simple healing tonic. The rabbits don’t just die—they *explode*. This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream principle that in this world, every treasure and opportunity comes with razor-sharp edges. The aftermath forces Han Li to confront the unsettling reality that he may be holding a poison so potent it defies ordinary logic. Meanwhile, his practical transaction with Li Feiyu proceeds smoothly, reinforcing the foundation of their hostage-based friendship: Li Feiyu gets his pain relief, and Han Li secures a grateful ally whose desperation keeps him honest.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li watches in horror as the rabbits swell and burst from the Green Liquid’s effects, barely escaping the blast radius himself.  
- The explosion creates two craters and leaves gore scattered across the herb garden—a visceral demonstration that the liquid is a terrifying poison, not a miraculous drug.  
- Han Li mentally vows never to touch the Green Liquid again, though his later behavior suggests this resolution may erode over time.  
- He delivers the painkiller formula to Li Feiyu at noon, maintaining punctuality as part of his careful character.  
- Li Feiyu receives the medicine with genuine gratitude, but Han Li undercuts the sentiment by noting it’s only a year’s supply pending more ingredients.  
- After cleaning up the carnage, Han Li notices that contaminated soil might have poisoned several herbs and decides to observe rather than act immediately—a small but revealing moment of scientific pragmatism.  
- Returning to cultivation, Han Li hits a wall: the gap between the third and fourth layers remains just out of reach without external aid, leading him to pin his hopes on Doctor Mo’s return.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Green Liquid as Poison Overdrive:** This chapter drives home a common theme in xianxia: that “sacred” treasures or elixirs often have a lethal edge. The Green Liquid accelerates biological growth so aggressively that the rabbits’ bodies cannot contain it—they quite literally burst. This is not just grisly shock value; it establishes the principle that higher-grade spiritual substances are *unbalanced* and dangerous to ordinary flesh, foreshadowing the need for refined consumption methods or greater cultivation levels to handle them safely. The Chinese concept of *yao* (toxin/potion duality) comes into play: what heals immortals can kill mortals.

**The Dantian and Bottleneck:** Han Li’s frustration at being stuck on the third layer without external aid mirrors a core xianxia tension: cultivation is not solely about effort, but about resource access. The “bottleneck” (瓶颈 *pingjing*) is a recurring nightmare for low-level cultivators, and crossing it often requires rare pills, spiritual herbs, or master guidance. This chapter subtly reinforces Han Li’s dependence on Doctor Mo, even as their relationship grows more strained.

### Qi Deviation

A life-threatening condition where a cultivator’s internal energy spirals out of control, often caused by emotional shock or spiritual damage.

## Reading Guide

If you came into *Mortal’s Journey* expecting the hero to find a friendly grandpa in his bottle or a magic potion that instantly makes him OP, this chapter is your reality check. Han Li just discovered that his cheat item is less “miraculous elixir” and more “biological weapon in a jar.” This is peak Mortal Stream: your lucky find may still destroy you if you can’t control it. The rabbit explosion is not gratuitous violence—it’s the novel telling you, in the most graphic terms possible, that nothing here is safe, not even your protagonist’s own assets. The Li Feiyu exchange also shows us Han Li the pragmatist: he doesn’t oversell the drug, doesn’t claim friendship, just handles a transaction with clean edges. That’s our boy.
