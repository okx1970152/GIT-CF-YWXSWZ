## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream's social battlefield. Han Li, having just arrived at the Mo estate, finds himself pitted against the spirited and utterly spoiled Mo Caihuan. The chapter is a delightful, low-stakes clash of wills where Han Li's seasoned pragmatism and immunity to emotional manipulation face off against the girl's arsenal of cuteness, tears, and spoiled demands. We see the protagonist not in a life-or-death struggle, but navigating the “social tribulation” of a mortal family with the same cold, analytical precision he uses for combat. It's a rare moment of dark humor, showcasing Han Li's ability to turn any encounter—even one with a teenage girl—into a calculated game of control and resource management.

## Key Plot Points

- The chapter opens with Mo Caihuan and Han Li locked in a silent standoff in the garden; she has exhausted her tactics of acting cute, begging, and crying, all of which Han Li has passively ignored.
- Han Li reveals his immunity to these tactics, drawing a parallel to Li Feiyu’s similar manipulative performances, which have long since inoculated him.
- Under Han Li’s calm counter-pressure of enjoying her performance and reciting nonsense, Mo Caihuan’s demeanor crumbles from pitiful to resentful.
- To avoid genuine offense, Han Li decides to offer a gift, searching his robe for a suitable item.
- He produces a small bottle of “Radiance Pills”—fragrant pellets originally intended for imperial concubines, which he had made long ago for Li Feiyu to win over a girl.
- Mo Caihuan suspects the pill might be an aphrodisiac or knockout drug, forcing Han Li to endure an awkward, speechless moment.
- She declares she will have the pill tested by her sister before using it, maintaining her cautious front even as she accepts the gift.
- Mo Caihuan promptly declares the transaction a success and offers to help Han Li with any future problems—for a fee.
- Han Li internally writes Mo Caihuan off as a potential romantic interest, concluding that her temperament is far too troublesome for his liking.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The "Three Ultimate Techniques" of a Xianxia Lady.** Mo Caihuan’s arsenal of cuteness, sentiment, and tears is a classic trope in Chinese web novels, particularly among young, spoiled female characters. This isn't just random behavior; it’s a culturally recognized *tactic* of social combat. The protagonist’s ability to see through these performances is a sign of worldly experience and a hardened heart. Han Li’s immunity, forged by Li Feiyu’s similar antics, shows that in the Mortal Stream, even survival skills can be learned through petty human interactions.

**The Radiance Pill (萦香丸).** This chapter provides a perfect example of how Xianxia often uses imperial lore to flavor its world-building. The pill is framed as a rare luxury item, a product of the imperial harem that commoners can’t obtain. Yet to Han Li, it’s a purely functional tool—a leftover from a friend’s romantic scheme, repurposed for mosquito repellent. This contrast highlights his utilitarian mindset. The name "Radiance Pill" evokes exotic fragrance and romantic allure, which is why Mo Caihuan's immediate suspicion that it's an aphrodisiac is both a clever joke and a brilliant piece of character writing. She's cynical enough to know the genre's conventions.

**The Social Tribulation.** While fighting monsters and battling for treasures are the obvious challenges of a Xianxia life, navigating complex human relationships is just as dangerous. This “social tribulation” requires a different kind of calculation. Han Li doesn't defeat Mo Caihuan with force; he out-endures her emotional campaign and uses a minor resource (an unwanted pill) to stabilize the situation. This chapter is a blueprint for how a protagonist in the Mortal Stream must navigate any social hierarchy: with patience, a poker face, and a cold assessment of what each interaction costs and yields.

### Social Tribulation (社会试炼)

A type of conflict in the Mortal Stream where the protagonist must navigate complex human relationships and political hierarchies with the same cold calculation as a physical battle.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for a brilliant character moment that tells you everything about Han Li’s mindset. This chapter has zero combat, zero magic, and zero world-shaking revelations. Instead, it's a clinic on how our protagonist handles being pestered by a pretty girl. His internal monologue is pure gold. Watch how he analyzes the situation, identifies his goal (avoid making an enemy while lodging in the Mo estate), calculates the cost (a useless pill), executes the transaction, and then *instantly and coldly* categorizes Mo Caihuan as “too much trouble.” This is not a guy who gets flustered by a beautiful face. He sees people as problems to be solved or resources to be managed, and his "protection" of his own time and peace of mind is absolute. It’s a quiet but powerful reinforcement of his core character.

## Character Note: Li Feiyu's Ghost

It’s a tiny detail, but Han Li’s comparison of Mo Caihuan’s pleading face to Li Feiyu’s is a brilliant touch. It shows that even though they’ve parted ways, Li Feiyu’s personality left a permanent mark on how Han Li interprets manipulative behavior. In a genre where friends are often quickly forgotten, this small callback keeps their bond alive in the reader’s mind through Han Li’s own mental shorthand.
