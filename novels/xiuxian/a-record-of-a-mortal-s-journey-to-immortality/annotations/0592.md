## Chapter Overview
Buckle up, fellow Daoists—we've officially departed the Chaotic Star Sea and crash-landed into a whole new nightmare. In this chapter, Han Li gets the grand tour of the Netherworld Domain, a mana-sealed pocket dimension where magical power goes to die. Through a conversation with a village elder from the Great Jin Dynasty, we get two competing origin theories for this place: a spatial tear to the underworld, or the belly of the legendary Luohou beast. Neither is comforting. The elder also drops the bombshell about the Spirit Void Eruption—the passive debuff that keeps every cultivator helpless. And just when Han Li thinks he's heard it all, he's told there *is* a way out: climbing Stormwind Mountain. But from the elder's tone and Mei Ning's despair, it's clear this is less an exit strategy and more a death sentence dressed up as hope. Our boy Han Li, however, doesn't flinch. He just stares at the sky and whispers, "I must at least try."

## Key Plot Points
- The village elder explains that most inhabitants were *born* in the Netherworld Domain; outsiders who survive the initial Yin Beast onslaught are rare.
- Two competing origin theories for the domain: (1) a spatial rift between the underworld and the mortal realms, or (2) the belly of the legendary Luohou demon beast—a creature that can swallow the sun and moon and tear through space.
- The elder reveals the Spirit Void Eruption (绝灵之气喷发), a periodic mana-sealing phenomenon that is the *reason* why cultivators lose their powers here; the timing of Han Li's arrival coincided with both a spatial rift *and* an eruption—maximum bad luck.
- The elder himself is from the Great Jin Dynasty (大晋皇朝), the super-empire Han Li has heard of but never visited; his nostalgic description frames Jin as the "holy land of cultivators."
- The only way out is climbing Stormwind Mountain—a method the elder clearly considers suicidal, and which Mei Ning immediately dismisses as hopeless.
- The chapter ends with Han Li inspecting the village's central stone platform, showing his immediate pivot from information gathering to tactical scouting.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
### Netherworld Domain (阴冥之地) — Not Quite Hell, Not Quite Earth
In Chinese folklore, the "underworld" (阴间/阴司) is a structured afterlife with judges, reincarnation wheels, and bureaucratic paperwork. The Netherworld Domain here is a *twisted echo* of that concept—not a true afterlife, but a mana-dead zone where cultivators are reduced to mortals. The elder's two theories (spatial rift vs. Luohou's belly) essentially ask: is this a *geographical accident* or a *living creature's digestive system*? Both are terrifying for different reasons.

### The Great Jin Dynasty — The Superpower You Haven't Met Yet
The elder's gushing about the Great Jin Dynasty is a classic xianxia worldbuilding move: introducing a "bigger pond" when the protagonist has just escaped a smaller one. For T/N readers, this is the first hint that the Heavenly South region and the Chaotic Star Sea are *not* the center of the cultivation world. There's an even larger, more prosperous superpower out there—and it's run by people who actually know what they're doing.

### Luohou (罗睺) — The Eater of Worlds
In Hindu mythology, Rahu is a demon who swallows the sun, causing eclipses. This concept migrated into Chinese Buddhist and Daoist cosmology as Luohou (罗睺), often depicted as a monstrous shadow-being that devours celestial bodies. Using a Luohou as the *physical container* for a sealed sub-realm is a clever twist on the old "world turtle" trope—turning a mythological predator into a *setting*.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a *worldbuilding dump*, but don't let the exposition-heavy tone fool you—every piece of information is a tactical asset. The two origin theories aren't just for flavor; they hint at how the domain's *rules* might be exploited. The Spirit Void Eruption means Han Li can't rely on any of his usual tricks (no Azure Bamboo Swords, no Gold Devourer Beetles, no Heaven Thunder Seeds). He's been reset to *mortal-plus*, and that's a terrifying prospect for a character who has spent his entire life stacking Trump Card #3 behind Trump Card #2.

Pay attention to the way the elder frames the escape method—he doesn't say it's impossible, just that *no one has done it yet*. In a Mortal Stream story, that's not a closing argument; that's a challenge. Han Li's final line ("I must at least try") isn't a naive declaration of hope—it's a cold acceptance that the odds are terrible, and a decision to see what his baseline non-magical abilities can still do. This is the *real* test of his early Jianghu training (Blinking Sword Art, Smoke Step, martial instincts) in a world that has turned off his cheat codes.

Also note: Mei Ning is *not* written off as dead weight here. Her despair is a counterpoint to Han Li's calculation, but she's not screaming or sobbing—she's biting her lip, asking questions, and absorbing information. She's a secondary character, but she's not a background prop. In this translation, her reactions are rendered as contained, pragmatic fear, not melodrama. That's the RMJI way.
