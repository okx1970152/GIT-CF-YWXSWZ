## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to another dose of Jinsei’s eternal hustle! Chapter 360 takes us on a very different kind of treasure hunt—not in a blood-soaked forbidden land, but in the rarefied air of the Stardust Pavilion’s sixth floor. After the chaos of the auction and the bloody trial, we finally get a chance to see Han Li do what he does best in peacetime: spend spirit stones with surgical precision. But this floor holds a surprise that catches even our perpetually cautious protagonist off guard—not a powerful elder, but a mortal girl who treats cultivators like servants. It’s a brilliant subversion of expectation that also serves as a perfect stage for Han Li’s next major acquisition. Get ready for a chapter where the real flex isn’t a flashy spell, but a bank account deep enough to make a Foundation Establishment girl blink.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li, accompanied by Madam Lan, ascends to the exclusive sixth floor of the Stardust Pavilion, expecting to meet an even more powerful cultivator. Instead, he finds a mortal teenage girl named Xiuer.
- The contrast is jarring: Xiuer behaves with the complete entitlement of someone who owns the place, ignoring Han Li entirely while she fawns over Madam Lan, revealing her privileged position within the organization.
- Madam Lan hands over the customer and departs, leaving Xiuer to handle the sale with open coldness. She shows him the two pill formulas and the set of magic tools.
- Han Li examines the formulas: the Spirit-Gathering Pill (useless to him) and the True Essence Pill (perfect for his current needs). He notes the price disparity between ancient and standard formulas.
- He opens the larger box and discovers thirteen red flying needles—the legendary **Red-Line Light-Speed Needles**. This is his first encounter with flying needle magic tools, and he immediately recognizes their rarity and value as assassination tools.
- Xiuer warns him that the needles have an insane requirement: they can only be activated simultaneously, all thirteen at once, which is why no one has bought them yet.
- Han Li, without a word, casually levitates all thirteen needles and weaves them into a crimson net, instantly proving his mastery. Xiuer is visibly stunned for the first time.
- The price is set at 4,000 spirit stones. Han Li negotiates the purchase, and then suddenly shifts the conversation: he asks how the pavilion handles the sale of thousand-year spiritual herbs, signaling a much deeper transaction to come.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The “Shadow Tool” Stigma and Flying Needle Mechanics**
Flying needles (飞针法器) occupy a unique niche in xianxia weaponry. They’re not for flashy, head-on duels. Their small size, high speed, and low profile make them perfect for ambushes and silent takedowns, which is why they earned the unflattering nickname “shadow tools” or “yin tools” (阴器). The cultural bias is interesting: in a world that often glorifies direct, honorable combat (at least in theory), the needle represents the cunning, practical underbelly of cultivation warfare—perfect for a protagonist like Han Li. The technical hurdle—activating all needles simultaneously—is a deliberate design choice in xianxia logic: it creates a high barrier to entry that ensures only those with truly exceptional spiritual sense (like our man Han) can wield them, keeping them from becoming common weapons.

**Red-Line Light-Speed Needles (红线遁光针)**
The name itself is a masterclass in xianxia naming conventions. “Red-line” (红线) refers to the nearly invisible crimson streaks the needles leave in flight—a poetic but deadly image. “Light-Speed” (遁光) suggests teleportation-level velocity, implying these are not just fast, but borderline impossible to track with the naked eye. The purchase of such a set marks a significant upgrade in Han Li’s tactical arsenal, giving him a ranged assassination capability he previously lacked.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s quieter strengths: procurement and positioning. Han Li doesn’t need to draw blood to win here; he needs to read a room, understand a price, and make a cold calculation. Notice how he doesn’t react emotionally to Xiuer’s rudeness—he notes it, files it, and moves on. His display of control over the thirteen needles isn’t bravado; it’s a calculated demonstration of capability that forces the seller to take him seriously. And the final question about thousand-year herbs isn’t idle curiosity—it’s Han Li testing the supply chain for his Heaven Vial’s ultimate profit engine. Pay close attention to the subtle class inversion here: a mortal girl runs the top floor of a cultivator’s pavilion, and a Foundation Establishment cultivator treats her with deference. The Mortal Stream universe isn’t just about who has the most mana; it’s about who controls the right leverage.
