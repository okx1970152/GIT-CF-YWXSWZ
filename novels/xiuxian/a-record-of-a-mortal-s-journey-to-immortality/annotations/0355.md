## Chapter Overview

Forget the diplomatic parlor games of the last few chapters—this one gets straight to the point. Han Li lands at the ruined site of Qi Yunxiao’s residence, finds two Fu Clan cultivators still lurking around, and decides that a conversation is only useful if it serves his intel-gathering. The moment he confirms that only these two are present, the “talk” turns into a three-second execution. There’s no drawn-out magic duel, no dramatic speech about revenge, no last-minute bargaining—just cold, lightning-fast surgery performed with a transparent thread, a handful of puppets, and a feint that would make an assassin proud. The chapter then closes with Han Li arriving at Xin Ruyin’s hidden mountain, where the mist is still intact, but a new voice greets him with suspicion: who is the soulless figure behind him? The tension shifts from external threat to internal trust—a classic Mortal Stream pivot.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li arrives at the destroyed residence and immediately spots the two Fu Clan cultivators lying in wait. His internal calculation clicks into place: these two are connected to the attack.
- The young Fu Clan disciple tries to intimidate Han Li with his family name and almost attacks, but the elder stops him—a sign that the elder recognizes Han Li’s cultivation level and is wary.
- Han Li proposes a prisoner’s-dilemma style Q&A exchange. The elder accepts, but the negotiation is a farce: Han Li confirms Qi Yunxiao is alive, the elder admits the Fu Clan attacked him for killing their members.
- Once Han Li confirms that only these two are present and no backup is waiting, he executes them in a single, coordinated ambush: puppets and the Black Talon draw the elder’s attention, while Han Li himself uses Smoke Step to flank and decapitate both with the transparent thread.
- The narration pauses for a tactical reflection: Foundation Establishment cultivators rarely use full-body defensive barriers because they’re too slow to cast, making them vulnerable to speed-based assassins. Han Li notes this is the same tactic that killed most of the demonic cultivators he fought.
- Han Li loots the corpses, finds one useful coin-shaped defensive artifact, then burns the evidence and flies to Xin Ruyin’s hidden mountain.
- He arrives safely—the mist formation is intact—and sends a sound transmission talisman. The mist opens, but as he approaches the exit, a woman’s voice (not Xin Ruyin’s) challenges him for bringing a stranger (Qu Hun).

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Birth of a Foundation Establishment Meta:** One of the most quietly revolutionary observations in this chapter is Han Li’s realization about Foundation Establishment combat meta. Low-grade *elemental barriers* (五行罩) can’t stop top-grade magic tools, and high-grade barriers are too slow to cast and have no cheap instant-cast talismans available. So the entire Foundation Establishment tier has collectively agreed to rely on *defensive magic tools* (防御法器) instead of full-body shields. This creates a massive vulnerability to speed-based flanking—exactly what Han Li exploits here. It’s a rare example of the novel showing how the cultivation world’s *system* (what’s available, what’s too expensive, what’s a chicken rib) shapes its combat tactics just as much as individual power does.

**Smoke Step + Transparent Thread = Mortal Stream Assassin’s Kit:** Han Li’s signature ground-combat combo is on full display: the *Smoke Step* (罗烟步) closes distance faster than the eye can track, and the *near-transparent thread* (近似透明的丝线) provides an undetectable, blade-sharp killing edge. This isn’t a flashy technique—it’s a low-cost, high-lethality toolset that doesn’t rely on mana consumption or rare resources. It’s the ultimate expression of the Mortal Stream principle: *use what the system overlooks to beat those who rely on the system.*

**The Fu Clan’s Blind Spot:** The young cultivator’s arrogance—“Why would the Qi Clan oppose us over a collateral member?”—is a textbook example of how clan politics in the cultivation world creates predictable behavior. He assumes that his family’s name is a universal shield, not realizing that a rogue cultivator like Han Li has no clan loyalty to exploit.

## Reading Guide

Get ready for some brutally efficient combat, folks. This chapter is a masterclass in how *not* to be a Foundation Establishment cultivator: don’t monologue, don’t let your guard down just because it’s a “negotiation,” and never assume that a low-key rogue cultivator isn’t already calculating your decapitation vector. Han Li’s kill is so clean it’s almost anticlimactic—and that’s the point. The Mortal Stream doesn’t reward flashy showdowns; it rewards preparation, misdirection, and the willingness to strike the second the math checks out.

Also, keep an eye on that new voice at the end. Xin Ruyin’s hideout is secure, but a stranger’s challenge suggests that the “safe house” may not be as safe as Han Li hopes. The tension is no longer about Qi Yunxiao’s fate—it’s about who else knows about this secret mountain, and whether Han Li’s reputation precedes him in ways he doesn’t control.
