## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists! After a tense stretch of decision-making, our favorite cautious mortal finally picks a side, and surprise surprise—he chooses the one with a high chance of death but a glimmer of immortality. The Blood Forbidden Trial, which we heard whispers about in earlier chapters, is now the centerpiece of Han Li’s entire existence for the next six months. But as any veteran of the Mortal Stream knows, jumping into a meat grinder without full prep is pure suicide. So buckle up for a logistics-heavy, strategy-packed chapter where Han Li goes full Quartermaster Mode: picking spells, cultivating illegal herbs, and mentally listing his magical tool inventory like a paranoid survivalist. No flashy fights, but plenty of cold, layered setup. The real battle hasn’t started, but the blueprint is already drawn.

## Key Plot Points
- After agonizing over the choice, Han Li commits to the Blood Forbidden Trial, fully aware that the survival rate is abysmal—maybe one in four, maybe worse. He makes a final trip to Yuelu Hall to confirm there are no hidden loopholes or secret shortcuts.
- Han Li takes stock of his current resources: a couple of decent magical tools (the ring, the flag, the long rope-blade, and the gourd), some stolen talismans from Uncle Ye, and the mysterious sword talisman he is still too scared to investigate openly.
- He decides the fastest power spike comes from talismans and magical tools, not spells—his low talent makes new spells a slow investment. He chooses to learn the Breath Concealment Technique, a practical mid-grade auxiliary spell that hides spiritual fluctuations from other Qi Condensation cultivators.
- He quietly nurtures a risky plan: secretly growing high-grade millennium-old herbs using the Heaven Vial’s green liquid, then selling them anonymously in the market for spirit stones. He takes care to avoid Elder Ma’s schedule and hides the herbs in tucked-away corners of the garden.
- Over four months, Han Li learns the Breath Concealment Technique, produces two thousand-year-old herbs, and finally secures an exit token to head to the market—the famous Yellow Maple Valley market, which attracts even foreigners from Yuanwu Country.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
One thing that makes the Mortal Stream tick is how *logistics* matter just as much as firepower. This chapter is basically Han Li’s supply-chain phase: he’s not power-leveling through a breakthrough, but he’s optimizing his resource pipeline. The green liquid’s ability to force-grow rare herbs is a strategic advantage, not just a cheat code—it lets him convert time into value, which is the closest thing a low-talents cultivator has to a silver bullet.

Also, note the way the market is described: it sits near the border between two countries whose cultivation worlds aren’t at war, so it naturally becomes a hub for rare foreign goods. This is a nice piece of world-building that shows political geography directly shapes resource availability—a small but deliberate touch in the *Mortal Stream* style of making the world feel real.

## Reading Guide
For readers used to nonstop action, this chapter might feel like a breather, but in the Mortal Stream, *preparation is action*. Han Li’s careful calculation of which spells to pick, which herbs to grow, and which talismans to keep hidden is his version of raising the stakes. Every choice he makes here—particularly his decision to secretly grow herbs with the Heaven Vial—is a gamble that could backfire spectacularly if discovered. Also, pay close attention to the mysterious sword talisman he still hasn’t cracked; hints say it’s called a “Talisman Treasure,” which is a serious piece of kit. The stage is being set for something big, and the market trip is just the opening move.
