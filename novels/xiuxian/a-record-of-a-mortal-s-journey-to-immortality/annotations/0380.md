## Chapter Overview
Twenty years vanish in the space of a breath. The chapter opens with Han Li locked in the most grueling marathon of his cultivation life—first speedrunning his re-cultivation to Foundation Establishment with the aid of his Heaven Vial, then enduring a brutal six-year slog through the second layer of the Great Derivative Art that nearly breaks his mind. The payoff? A successful External Incarnation ritual that leaves the normally stoic protagonist walking out of his chamber with unconcealed excitement. But there’s no rest for the wicked (or the paranoid): Han Li immediately pivots to the **Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art**, a radical self-dismantling technique that requires him to voluntarily drop his cultivation level to compress his true essence toward eventual Core Formation. It’s the most extreme example yet of the Mortal Stream principle that shortcuts always carry a price, and progress is paid in pain.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li’s accelerated cultivation: reaching Qi Condensation Layer 9 in just over a year, then re-entering Foundation Establishment two years later via a consecutive triple-dose of Foundation Establishment Pills and his artificially matured herbs. His speed is anomalous but earned.
- The Great Derivative Art second layer takes fully six years—more than double his initial estimate. The mental torture of forcibly expanding his divine sense leaves him with a lingering dread that even his iron will finds hard to shake.
- An old foe’s disciple arrives to challenge Han Li during his third year of derivative training, only to be trapped in the island’s formation for three days. Han Li, barely remembering the grudge, defeats the frustrated challenger just convincingly enough to send him packing without revealing his true strength.
- The External Incarnation ritual succeeds. Han Li performs a soul-splitting technique, dividing a portion of his primordial spirit and injecting it into Qu Hun’s soulless body. The process takes over a month and leaves him weakened but triumphant.
- Han Li immediately begins studying the **Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art**, a self-sacrificial cultivation method that requires him to voluntarily disperse his cultivation from late Foundation Establishment down to early Foundation Establishment, compressing his liquid true essence toward solidity to ease the Core Formation bottleneck. The creator’s theory: three such cycles give a fifty percent chance of forming a Golden Core.
- The chapter ends with a quiet coda: twenty years of uninterrupted seclusion. The island’s mortal population has grown accustomed to their reclusive Immortal Master, whose only interaction with them is the annual automatic delivery of spirit stones for the village’s protective formation. Then, on this day, Han Li emerges at last.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**“Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art” (三转重元功)** — This technique is a perfect example of the Mortal Stream’s core philosophy: *there are no free lunches, only cheaper interest rates*. It doesn’t give Han Li a shortcut to Core Formation—it lets him spread a single insurmountable bottleneck across three painful reset cycles, each requiring him to voluntarily drop his cultivation and rebuild. This isn’t a cheat code; it’s debt restructuring. The original author’s speculation that three cycles would give a “fifty percent” chance of success subtly reinforces the genre’s brutal pragmatism: even the optimal strategy only flips a coin for you.

**External Incarnation (身外化身)** — A classic Xianxia concept where a cultivator splits off a piece of their own soul and implants it into a prepared body or puppet, creating a secondary self that can act independently. In Daoist terms, it’s a literalization of the idea that a sufficiently advanced being can “project” their consciousness into multiple vessels. Here, Han Li uses the soulless body of Qu Hun (formerly his friend Zhang Tie) as the vessel, which adds a quiet layer of emotional weight beneath the cold technical description.

## Reading Guide
This is a “quiet victory” chapter—the kind of time-skip montage that Mortal Stream fiction handles with ruthless efficiency. There’s no dramatic split-second duel, no life-or-death escape, no shocking reveal. Instead, we watch Han Li submit to a six-year headache, a risky soul-splitting ritual, and a decision to *voluntarily destroy his own cultivation progress*. And yet, the chapter hums with a quiet, earned tension: every pill swallowed, every day of torment, every sleepless night of mental training is a calculated investment. The payoff is Han Li walking out of that stone chamber with a genuine, unguarded excitement—a rare emotional crack that makes the victory feel real.

The chapter also showcases a classic Mortal Stream narrative device: the *boring interlude that isn’t boring at all*. Twenty years pass in a single sentence. The island’s mortals treat their immortal neighbor like an eccentric landlord who leaves the rent on the doorstep. And then, with no fanfare, Han Li emerges. Something is about to happen, and the reader is left holding that cold, patient anticipation.
