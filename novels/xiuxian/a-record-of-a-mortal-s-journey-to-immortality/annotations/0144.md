## Chapter Overview

What happens when a nobody with an ancient token walks into the most powerful sect in the region and *takes* a Foundation Establishment Pill away from a well-connected disciple? This chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s brand of systemic politics. Forget fair fights and righteous trials—the allocation of a Foundation Establishment Pill here is settled through family names, inherited privilege, and bureaucratic horse-trading. The Ascension Token isn’t a ticket earned by merit; it’s an institutional IOU that can break the rules of an entire sect’s internal competition. Get ready for a tense, dialogue-driven showdown that lays bare the ugly machinery of a cultivation sect’s power structure. No magic, no flying swords—just pure, concentrated human scheming.

## Key Plot Points

We open with a look at the inner workings of Yellow Maple Valley, hidden behind a grand illusion formation. Sect Leader Zhong Lingdao presides over a tense meeting where the allocation of a single Foundation Establishment Pill has sparked a bitter dispute. The old Ye Clan elder is furious: his great-nephew’s pill has been taken away and given to a wandering cultivator who showed up with an Ascension Token. His opponent, Murong Shan, calmly dismantles his arguments, pointing out the long-term dangers of refusing an Ascension Token bearer. When the Ye elder demands reasons for skipping the two people ranked *below* his great-nephew, the answers are devastating: one is the mortal descendant of a Core Formation elder (Red Whisk), and the other has a wind-attribute mutated spiritual root—both are untouchable. The Ye elder is crushed, his great-nephew’s future seemingly doomed. But Senior Wu, a man known for his cunning, steps in with a plan: instead of fighting the system, buy the pill from the newcomer. He argues that a low-aptitude wandering cultivator with a near-zero chance of breaking through is likely to sell his pill for more practical goods. The chapter closes on this cold, transactional solution.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Foundation Establishment (筑基)** — This is the single most important bottleneck a young cultivator will face. Breaking through to Foundation Establishment is considered the line between a true cultivator and a permanent genius-of-the-low-realms. A successful breakthrough radically increases lifespan, magical power, and status. It is the gate that separates the masses from the elite. A single Foundation Establishment Pill can mean the difference between a glorious future and a tragic, energy-starved death within a few decades. The Ye elder’s despair is not an overreaction.

**Ascension Token (升仙令)** — This is one of the most powerful bureaucratic weapons in the cultivation world. It is not a prize won in combat; it is a political token issued by a great immortal sect to an immortal family as a reward for past service. Whoever holds it is owed a place in the sect *and* a Foundation Establishment Pill, regardless of their own skill. The sect must honor the token, not the bearer. It is a perfect manifestation of the Mortal Stream’s ruling principle: institutional connections and historical debts can rewrite the rules of even the most “fair” competition.

**Spiritual Roots & Genetics** — This chapter lays out the cruel caste system of the cultivation world in stark terms. The Ye elder’s great-nephew is likely a normal cultivator with okay roots. The girl with a Core Formation ancestor is protected by pure, institutional inertia. The wandering cultivator has "False Spiritual Roots"—a term that means he is a *genetic dead end* in the eyes of the system, someone who could cultivate for a hundred years and still never succeed. The Red Whisk descendant and the mutated root bearer are essentially protected classes, while the newcomer is seen as a bargaining chip.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the political infrastructure of a cultivation sect in action. If you love the strategic maneuvering of noble houses and corporate boardrooms, this is your jam. The entire conflict is *unseen* by the wandering cultivator, whose presence has already caused a political earthquake without him even knowing it. Pay attention to how the Ye elder’s emotional plea is met with cold, procedural logic, and how Senior Wu’s solution is not to fight the system but to *work* it. This isn’t just about one pill; it’s a preview of the invisible wars that dictate who rises and who falls in the immortal world. Keep an eye on the mention of that “wind-attribute mutated root” bearer—characters with rare, unique aptitudes are never just background noise in a story this long. And if you’re wondering how the wandering cultivator will react when the elders come to him with a “generous offer”… well, this is *Han Li* we’re talking about. The scheming might just be about to meet its match.
