## Chapter Overview

Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to another masterclass in applied cunning. Forget flashy duels or world-shattering revelations—Chapter 401 is a pure, unadulterated lesson in Han Li’s favorite pastime: *turning the system against itself.* We watch our favorite paranoid cultivator transform from a wandering swordsman into a patient, calculating predator. This isn’t about raw power; it’s about preparation, patience, and weaponizing obscure botanical knowledge. The stage is set on the desolate Red Coral Island, a place that will soon become a kill box. Get ready for a slow-burn chapter where the real action isn't a fight, but the meticulous construction of a perfect trap and the clever exploitation of a neglected corner of the cultivation world’s ecology. It’s a quiet chapter, but the tension is all in the setup.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li selects Red Coral Island in the Condensing Jade Island sea area as his base of operations. He spends two days meticulously surveying the island’s terrain, even diving to the seabed of the ring-shaped center.
- He and Qu Hun deploy a layered defensive system, setting up three major formations—“Reversed Five Elements,” “Heavenly Wind Fury,” and “Illusory Heavenly Net”—to block the three entrances to the island’s center, with additional smaller arrays forming an inner perimeter.
- The chapter’s centerpiece is the introduction of the “Nishang Grass.” We learn its history: a seemingly useless spirit herb that, once every hundred years, unfurls a leaf and emits a scent irresistible to demonic beasts.
- Han Li explains why previous cultivators failed to weaponize this grass—unpredictable leaf-unfurling times and an inability to transplant the plant—which led them to abandon it as a “Demon Luring Grass.”
- Han Li reveals his masterstroke: he uses the Heaven Vial’s green liquid to artificially induce the Nishang Grass’s maturity on demand, bypassing the century-long wait and the transplantation problem.
- Having prepared his inescapable net, he plants a hundred-year-old Nishang Grass seedling, drips a single drop of green liquid onto it, and settles in to wait. His plan is a logistical masterpiece, proving that in the Mortal Stream, victory is achieved long before the first blow is struck.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The ‘Waiting by the Stump’ Strategy (守株待兔):** Han Li consciously compares his plan to this classical Chinese idiom. The original story is a cautionary tale about a farmer who, upon seeing a rabbit accidentally kill itself by running into a tree stump, abandons his farming to wait for another rabbit to do the same. He starves. In the hands of a Mortal Stream protagonist like Han Li, this foolish strategy is transformed into a highly efficient, risk-managed tactic. He doesn’t hope for random luck; he engineers a situation from which a specific outcome is nearly guaranteed. This subversion of a classic fable perfectly encapsulates the novel’s core philosophy: don’t rely on fate, but use your intelligence to create your own luck.

**The ‘Chicken Rib’ (鸡肋) Potential of Knowledge:** The story of the Nishang Grass perfectly illustrates the concept of “Chicken Rib” knowledge in xianxia. The lore was out there, written off as a failed experiment by the broader cultivation world. It was a tasteless bone—the effort required to use it didn’t justify the reward. But for Han Li, whose Heaven Vial is the *one missing ingredient* that makes the whole puzzle work, this seemingly useless knowledge becomes the most valuable asset imaginable. This reinforces the core theme that a protagonist’s unique advantage isn’t just raw power, but the *ability to see value where others see waste*.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a slow, satisfying setup that rewards patient readers. Don’t skip the historical flashback about the Nishang Grass! It’s not just a lore dump; it’s a brilliant piece of narrative economy. It establishes the rules of the world (why this plan hasn’t been done before), showcases the limitations of typical cultivators, and highlights Han Li’s unique genius in exploiting a loophole that only *he* can exploit. The real drama here isn’t in what happens, but in the *preparation*. Watch how methodically Han Li works: surveying, planning, layer upon layer of arrays. The payoffs from these setups are some of the most satisfying in the story. By the end, you should feel a cold sense of anticipation, not for a battle, but for the inevitable, efficient harvest that is about to begin.

## The Lore of the Nishang Grass: A Botanist’s Nightmare

For the curious Daoist, understanding the Nishang Grass is key to understanding Han Li’s genius. In Chinese folklore and xianxia, plants of unusual appearance are often given exaggerated mystical properties (the *Ginseng Fruit*, *Peaches of Immortality*). The Nishang Grass subverts this. It *looks* like a celestial treasure but acts like a common weed with a very specific, localized utility. Its need for a “brightly colored environment” hints at a deeper ecological logic that is never fully explained, a classic technique in xianxia worldbuilding. This gives the world depth without overwhelming the reader with exposition. The grass is a perfect McGuffin—a rare object that drives the plot not because of its intrinsic power, but because of the unique way the protagonist chooses to use it.
