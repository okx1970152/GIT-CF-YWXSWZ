## Chapter Overview

The interrogation of the blue-robed enforcer brings a storm of bad news: the attack on the Seven Mysteries Sect is far larger than anticipated. Li Feiyu emerges from the woods with two critical pieces of intel—one good, one bad—but even the “good” news paints a grim picture of the siege. Han Li, as always, wastes no time on sentiment. He calmly decides their next move: regroup, avoid detection, and kill anyone who sees them. The chapter closes with a parallel scene at Elder Li’s courtyard, where a different kind of power struggle is unfolding—one between a blustering bureaucrat with a golden token and a disciple who knows the price of disobedience.

## Key Plot Points

- The green-robed giant emerges from the forest carrying an unconscious blue-robed enforcer, his robe stained with blood, confirming his lethal efficiency.
- Li Feiyu is visibly shaken by the giant’s presence and retreats defensively, but the giant ignores him and silently returns to Han Li’s side.
- Li Feiyu’s attempt to pry into Han Li’s relationship with the giant is deflected; Han Li shifts the topic to the interrogation, tactfully evading the question while still enlisting Li Feiyu’s help.
- Li Feiyu extracts information quickly: the enforcer was a coward who spilled everything without much pressure.
- The “good news”: the Wild Wolf Gang’s strategy matches Han Li’s earlier prediction—they have only surrounded the other peaks and have concentrated their main force on Sunset Peak, already breaking through several defensive lines.
- The “bad news”: the attack is a coalition including the Iron Spear Society, the Severed Water Sect, and several other mid-sized and small gangs, suggesting the Seven Mysteries Sect is facing a coordinated extermination effort.
- Han Li decides to move immediately: regroup with Li Feiyu’s lover and subordinates while the chaos gives them cover.
- Li Feiyu confirms he has silenced the enforcer, accepting this as standard operating procedure.
- Han Li lays down the tactical principle: avoid enemies when possible, but kill everyone who spots them without hesitation—no survivors means no reinforcements.
- The scene shifts to Elder Li’s courtyard, crowded with defenseless sect dependents, guarded by twenty-odd armed young men.
- Inside the hall, Li’s disciple Ma Rong is arguing with a fat, arrogant official who refuses to send out scouts and brandishes his golden token to assert authority; Ma Rong is forced to submit.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **golden waist token (金腰牌)** appearing in this chapter is a classic symbol of institutional authority in Chinese martial and bureaucratic fiction. It functions as a physical delegation of power: whoever holds it speaks with the voice of a superior, and defiance of the token is defiance of the office itself. In the context of sects and Jianghu organizations, tokens of this kind act as a social trump card, overriding rank and personal judgment. Ma Rong’s reluctant submission—despite his clear strategic instinct that scouting is necessary—demonstrates a recurring tension in these settings: the conflict between practical survival logic and rigid hierarchical protocol. The fat official’s bluster is not just cowardice; it is a weaponized appeal to structure, one that Ma Rong cannot break without openly rebelling. This mechanism parallels the way cultivation hierarchies lock individuals into obedience even when the system is failing around them.

The voluntary destruction of one's own cultivation base, reverting to a mortal state. Often a last resort for those afflicted by irreversible side effects of dangerous techniques.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterclass in pace and contrast. On one side, we have Han Li and Li Feiyu moving through a battlefield with cold efficiency—extracting intel, making snap decisions, and operating on a mutual understanding that mercy is a liability. On the other, we cut to a courtyard full of helpless dependents and a petty authority figure who would rather hide behind his token than act. The fat official is a perfect foil to Han Li: both hold leverage, but one uses it to stall, the other to move. Pay attention to how Han Li’s tactical principle—“kill everyone who spots us”—is stated in the same calm tone he uses to discuss the weather. This is not cruelty for its own sake; it is the logical extension of a world where loose ends get you killed. The chapter also deepens Li Feiyu’s role as the audience’s emotional barometer: he is visibly shaken by the giant’s presence, relieved by Han Li’s composure, and unsettled by the scale of the coalition attack. His reaction keeps the stakes human, even as Han Li’s decisions grow more ruthless.

## Deep Dive: The Interrogation Offscreen

The chapter saves us the spectacle of the interrogation itself—Li Feiyu emerges with results before we can wonder what methods he used. This is a deliberate narrative economy: the important part is not *how* the information was extracted, but *what* it means. The cowardly enforcer’s quick confession reinforces a grim reality of the Mortal Stream: loyalty in this world is cheap, especially when the interrogator knows what he is doing. Li Feiyu’s willingness to silence the man without a second thought mirrors Han Li’s own pragmatism, confirming that these two are on the same wavelength when it comes to survival. The offscreen nature of the violence also keeps the focus on the strategic calculus rather than the gore, allowing the tension of the coalition attack to dominate the reader’s attention.
