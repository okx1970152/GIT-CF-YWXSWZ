## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream combat economy, where a single, perfectly executed gamble flips the table on a seemingly invincible defense. Han Li corners the possessed Qu Hun, but is stunned to find his opponent wielding the power of a genuine Magical Treasure and a mysterious green orb that completely neutralizes his Soul Summoning Bell. What follows isn’t a dramatic, showy victory, but a cold, methodical problem-solving session. Han Li’s layered assault—puppet volleys followed by a calculated Blood Drill snipe on the orb rather than the body—showcases his core doctrine: never fight fair, always aim for the system’s hidden kill switch. The chapter also delivers a bittersweet taste of the higher realms: Han Li finally claims his first real Magical Treasure, the ‘Green煌 Sword,’ only to find it is utterly unusable without a Core Formation base. It’s a hard reminder that in this world, power is locked behind hard statistical gates, not just combat skill. Get ready for a satisfying tactical takedown followed by a cold, sobering glance at the road ahead.

## Key Plot Points

*   **The Green Orb’s Defense:** Han Li’s signature Black Flood Dragon Capture and even his Soul Summoning Bell are completely neutralized by a mysterious, emerald-green pellet spat out by Qu Hun. The narrative establishes this as a desperate, last-ditch measure.
*   **Puppet Barrage & Failure:** Han Li deploys his remaining second-tier puppets for an overwhelming volley, only to find Qu Hun’s green light barrier holds firm. This confirms the absurd defensive gap created by the pellet.
*   **The Surgical Strike:** Instead of targeting Qu Hun’s head or heart (which might not be vital for a zombie), Han Li pivots and uses his Blood Drill to destroy the green pellet itself. The gamble pays off spectacularly.
*   **Soul Extraction & Destruction:** The destruction of the pellet causes Qu Hun immense spiritual trauma, revealing a direct link between the orb and his primordial soul. Han Li coldly crushes the escaping green soul orb, confirming the kill.
*   **First Magical Treasure:** Han Li secures his first true Magical Treasure—the “Green煌 Sword”—but immediately learns its cruel limitation: it is completely unusable to a Foundation Establishment cultivator. It’s a trophy, not a weapon, for now.
*   **Saving Qu Hun:** Despite the charring, Han Li discovers Qu Hun’s core structures are intact. He uses the Soul Summoning Bell to reassert control, confirming his plan to keep the zombie servant as a long-term asset.
*   **Picking up the Pieces:** Han Li carefully collects the shattered fragments of the green orb, suspecting they are remnants of the original Core Formation cultivator’s Golden Core, hinting at future utility or danger.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Magical Treasure vs. Magic Tool: The Uncrossable Line**

This chapter provides a hard, concrete example of a key xianxia hierarchy. A **Magical Treasure (法宝)** is not simply a stronger version of a **Magic Tool (法器)**. It is a different category of existence entirely. Magic Tools are refined from ores and wood, require mana to activate, and are limited by their material construction. A Magical Treasure, however, is forged from a cultivator’s own life force, their natal essence, and can even house a nascent spirit. This is why Han Li’s Black Flood Dragon Capture, a top-grade Foundation Establishment magic tool, is so utterly outclassed by the Green煌 Sword, which was once wielded by a Core Formation cultivator. The power gap is not linear; it is a fundamental difference in qualitative energy density.

**Golden Core (金丹) Fragments**

Han Li’s speculation about the green pellet’s nature is chilling. The ‘Golden Core’ is the perfected, crystallized life force of a Core Formation cultivator. It is not just a storage battery; it is the seat of their power and identity. For a Core Formation cultivator to lose their core, or for it to be shattered, is arguably a fate worse than death. Finding even shattered fragments is a significant, if dangerous, loot drop for a Foundation Establishment cultivator, as they might hold residual power, technique imprints, or be used in forbidden rituals. The mere mention of them in this context reinforces the novel’s dark, resource-scavenging economy.

## Reading Guide

Welcome to the “Mid-Boss” fight that feels like a final boss encounter! This chapter is a perfect example of why *RMJI* is so addictive. It’s not about the flashiest spell, but the smartest application of resources. Watch Han Li run his mental algorithm: the Bell failed, the puppets failed, the direct attack is risky. So he doesn’t keep ramming his head against the wall; he identifies the actual problem—the green orb—and removes it from the equation. That’s peak Mortal Stream problem-solving.

But the best part is the bittersweet victory lap. Getting the Green煌 Sword is a major milestone, but the moment Han Li realizes he can’t use it is pure, concentrated xianxia pain. It’s the system telling the player, “Nice drop. You can’t equip it for 50 more levels.” It’s a motivational stomach punch. This chapter is a fantastic showcase of the series’ core tension: the constant, grinding struggle to reach the next level, where even victory comes with a fresh lesson in your own limitations. Enjoy the tactical genius, and try not to drool over the sword too much—we’re not worthy yet.

## Deep Lore: The Soul’s Fatal Link

The battle also subtly reveals a dark rule of soul manipulation. The green orb was not just a defensive tool; it was the *anchor* for the possessing soul’s connection to Qu Hun’s body. When it was destroyed, the soul was fatally exposed. This reinforces a core principle of xianxia combat: it is always better to attack the *function* than the *form*. Han Li didn’t try to overpower the Green煌 Sword or break Qu Hun’s skin; he targeted the source of the power that made both those defenses work. This is a level of strategic thinking that separates a survivor from a brute.
