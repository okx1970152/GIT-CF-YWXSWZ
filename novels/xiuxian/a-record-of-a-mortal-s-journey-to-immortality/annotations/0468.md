## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a masterclass in Mortal Stream tension—no grand battles, no epic speeches, just a slow, silent, *insidious* death in a black desert. This chapter is a brutal object lesson in why the unknown is always the most dangerous thing in the cultivation world. It also gives us a perfect, chilling portrait of Han Li’s survival doctrine at work: he watches, he calculates, he *refrains* from action, and then he watches a mid-Core Formation cultivator get dismantled by *bugs*. It’s terrifying, it’s precise, and it’s absolutely *chef’s kiss* for anyone who loves the genre’s hardcore resource-management logic.

## Key Plot Points

A sallow-faced Core Formation cultivator—the same one who trembled before the Extreme Yin Patriarch—cautiously scouts a black desert. He uses a precious jade bowl and a green bamboo hat, revealing his wealth and caution. After a failed sweep with a spiritual detection technique, he tests the desert with a disposable spirit beast (a Red Fox Beast). The beast returns unharmed, lulling him into a false sense of security. Believing the coast is clear, he steps onto the black sand. Behind him, unseen, Han Li watches from the hilltop, having narrowly evaded detection. Han Li had considered killing the man for his treasures but decided to let him act as a pathfinder—a coldly rational choice.

The man advances several hundred zhang without incident, lowering his guard. Then, the black sand suddenly rises, transforming into a terrifying swarm of tens of thousands of black flying ants. The man’s ice-shield whirlwind repels them, but the ants are unharmed. His flying blade is instantly overwhelmed and devoured, the bond backlash wounding him gravely. He tries to flee, but the ants coalesce into a massive black spear that pierces through his defenses, leaving ‘speckles of fresh red blood’ on its tip. The chapter ends with the implied death of the Core Formation cultivator, and Han Li, still a Foundation Establishment cultivator, has learned a brutal and valuable lesson about “unknown dangers” from a safe distance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Art of the ‘Pathfinder’ (探路者)**: This is a classic, vicious trope in Mortal Stream xianxia, and it’s one of the most efficient ways to highlight the protagonist’s cold pragmatism. Han Li doesn’t feel guilt or hesitation about letting a stronger cultivator die to test the waters. He simply notes the data point, files away the lesson (“the unknown is the most dangerous thing”), and moves on. The pathfinder is an expendable asset, not a person. The entire scene is a clinical, real-time cost-benefit analysis: “Could I have killed him and taken his treasures? Yes. Did I? No. Why? Because the risk of the unknown location outweighed the value of the known loot.”

**Insect Swarm Tactics (虫海战术)**: This chapter introduces a terrifying variant: black flying ants that are virtually indestructible by common means, can consume a bonded flying blade, and can reform into a single, devastating projectile. This is a recurring motif in xianxia—the ‘insect tide’ (虫潮) that punishes overconfidence and brute force. It’s a systemic check: no matter how strong your magic treasure, if the enemy has a million of *something* that can’t be cut, your power is nullified. The swarm is an ecosystem’s revenge on the individual.

**Bonded Magic Treasures (本命法宝)**: The chapter reinforces the terrible risk of bond-reliant cultivation. When the flying ants devour the man’s flying blade, the spiritual backlash from the bond’s destruction severely wounds him. This is a core Mortal Stream principle: your greatest asset is also your greatest vulnerability. Putting your soul into a weapon means suffering its death as your own.

## Reading Guide

*Black Desert*. It’s the *name* that does it, Daoists. It’s not ‘Yellow Sand,’ it’s not ‘Misty Dunes’—it’s a black, silent, utterly still field of death that looks *wrong* from the start. The chapter’s tension is entirely built on this dread. The sallow-faced cultivator is cautious, rich, and experienced, yet he still falls for the oldest trick in the book: testing a dead zone with a disposable beast. It’s a masterful demonstration of *how the system is designed to kill you through your own methodology*. Han Li, watching from the shadows, is the one who *doesn’t* test it. He simply waits. And that single decision—to refrain, to watch, to learn from someone else’s fatal mistake—is the entire difference between a Foundation Establishment survivor and a Core Formation corpse.

Also, note the transition: Han Li starts the chapter regretting not attacking. He ends it with a cold, silent understanding of *why* he was right to wait. The payout isn’t the loot; the payout is the lesson. And lessons in the Mortal Stream are worth more than most treasures.
