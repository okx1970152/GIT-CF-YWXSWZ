## Chapter Overview

Hold onto your spirit stones, folks—because the gloves are off, and Han Li has just walked into the worst possible scenario. This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream tension: our favorite cautious protagonist, fresh off the teleportation pad of the Blood Forbidden Land, finds himself cornered by two friendly veteran cultivators who treat an eleventh-layer rookie like a quick cash grab. What follows isn’t a flashy duel of epic magic, but a slow, grinding tactical nightmare. Han Li’s internal calculation engine is running at full speed as he measures his trump cards against a coordinated duo, and the chapter sets up a striking contrast between the ambushers’ arrogant confidence and their prey’s cold-blooded resource management. Get ready for the moment the Heaven Thunder Seed enters the negotiation table.

## Key Plot Points

*   Han Li is ambushed at the “One-Sky Pass” entrance, trapped between two Qi Condensation veterans: a thirteenth-layer Spirit Beast Mountain cultivator and a twelfth-layer Skysky Fortress disciple.
*   The terrain is brutally restrictive: sheer cliffs on both sides and suicidal overhead flight, forcing all movement through a single narrow path.
*   The bearded man arrogantly claims Han Li for personal revenge, dismissing him as a mere “rookie,” while his friend stays on standby.
*   Han Li’s internal calculation reveals he has no fear of the power gap; his trump cards (top-grade magic tools, a Talisman Treasure, and the Heaven Thunder Seed) are assets he can deploy to bridge the gap, but he’s reluctant to waste the irreplaceable seed.
*   A crucial reveal: the two ambushers are old friends, well-practiced in coordinated combat, having placed themselves together using a rare “Soul-Melting Talisman” (融灵符).
*   The Skysky Fortress disciple’s internal monologue exposes their cynical plan: they aren’t here for the official trial rewards (the spiritual herbs) but to run a high-profit murder-and-looting operation, hiding until the exit opens.
*   Han Li exploits the Skysky Fortress disciple’s distraction, seizing the opening to strike first. He hurls the Earthen Prison Talisman at the bearded man, successfully trapping his mobility.
*   The chapter ends on a cliffhanger as the yellow cage forms around the bearded man, setting the stage for a two-front battle.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Soul-Melting Talisman (融灵符)** — This isn’t just a clever plot device; it’s a brilliant illustration of the Mortal Stream’s “cooperation is a luxury” principle. The talisman is a high-cost, medium-reliability tool (a 50% success rate and exorbitant price) designed to defy the world’s brutal separation mechanic. The fact that it exists at all tells you that the system is so hostile to teamwork that cultivators are forced to invent rare, expensive workarounds just to have a trusted battle buddy. The Skysky Fortress man’s logic is the perfect encapsulation of Dark Forest economics: “Don’t compete for the prize everyone is fighting over. Instead, prey on those competitors from a position of superior strength.” This is the textbook definition of the “kill the goose” economy—why farm spirit herbs when you can rob the farmers?

**One-Sky Pass (一线天)** — The name literally translates to “Thread of Sky,” a classic Chinese landscape term for a narrow passage between two towering cliffs. In the world of xianxia, such passes are almost never scenic strolls; they are the preferred hunting grounds for ambushes, where a group’s advantage in numbers or terrain knowledge can utterly negate a lone traveler’s evasion skills.

### Blood Forbidden Land

A quadrennial, high-mortality trial zone where various sects compete for resources; flight and teamwork are systemically discouraged.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a masterful escalation of the novel’s core tension. Up until now, Han Li has faced outnumbered fights within the sect on a martial-arts scale. Here, for the first time, he faces a true cultivation-world ambush by a coordinated duo who have experience, camaraderie, and a dedicated plan of action. Pay close attention to Han Li’s moment of “calculated frustration” when he realizes the bearded man has cast a protective spell—it’s a rare moment where his internal “run the numbers” algorithm hits a snag. His immediate pivot to the Skysky Fortress disciple is a textbook example of Mortal Stream resource management: never lock yourself into a losing matchup if a softer target exists. The Heaven Thunder Seed is now a Chekhov’s gun that has been placed very prominently on the table. Every reader knows it’s a game-changer, so the tension isn’t about “if” Han Li can win, but “what price is he willing to pay” to avoid using it. That’s the devilish essence of this world: victory is often less about raw power and more about the strategic acceptance of loss.
