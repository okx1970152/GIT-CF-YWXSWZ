## Chapter Overview

Wake up, Fellow Daoists—we’re diving into the Extreme Illusionary Realm, and this chapter is basically the cultivation world’s version of a trauma-centered funhouse. Han Li strolls through a corridor where every classical fantasy trope (celestial music, dancing cranes, jade-like maidens) gets weaponized against him, escalating from romantic longing to outright seduction to tragic heartbreak. He shrugs it all off with the cool detachment of a man running permanent *Great Derivative Art* anti-cheat software. But the real test comes when he reaches a blood-soaked black hall at the end, where the illusions shift from universal desires to *personal* demons—and a familiar voice calls out his name. The chapter is a masterclass in *Mortal Stream* psychology: Han Li’s defenses are ironclad against generic fantasy, but the moment the illusion tailors itself to his specific history, we see a crack.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li walks a mile-long corridor while the Extreme Illusionary Realm throws escalating auditory and visual seductions at him: first celestial music with blushing maidens, then mournful heartbreakers, then full-on erotic performances. He treats the whole thing as a free show, his eyes cold.
- The *Great Derivative Art* passively filters every wave of charm and emotional manipulation; Han Li’s mind remains iron, and he reaches the corridor’s end without a single pause.
- At the exit, the illusions vanish, revealing a black, blood-drenched hall that exudes an evil, sacrificial aura. Han Li’s confidence visibly tightens.
- Inside the hall, total darkness and a sound-suppressing restriction isolate Han Li’s senses. His magic fire and moonlight stone are instantly nullified—the system is designed to strip him of every crutch.
- A sobbing white-clad woman appears. Han Li tries to shout her away, but her voice triggers a buried memory. When she lifts her face and calls him “Fourth Brother,” he freezes: it’s the face of his little sister, now aged into a married woman. A rare emotional crack forms in his iron composure.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The “Heart Demon” (心魔, Xin Mo) Concept**: This chapter is textbook *Mortal Stream* heart-demon trial design. In xianxia, a “heart demon” isn’t a literal monster—it’s a psychological weakness or emotional scar that can be exploited by illusions, possessions, or tribulations. Han Li’s *Patalocakra* bead and *Great Derivative Art* can shield him from generic charm spells (*媚术, Mei Shu*), but they cannot erase his own memories. The black hall’s trick is brutally elegant: first strip away external tools (light, sound, spiritual sense), then probe for a personal wound. In Chinese folk Daoist psychology, one’s *hun* soul stores emotional attachments; an illusion that mirrors a loved one is considered the hardest to resist because it bypasses rational calculation.

**The “Cultivation World Memory Trap”**: Han Li’s little sister is a rare, deeply buried emotional anchor from his mortal past. In the *Mortal Stream* logic, any surviving connection to the pre-cultivation self is a vulnerability. Higher-level cultivators often sever mortal ties deliberately to avoid this exact scenario. Han Li’s hesitation here is not weakness—it’s a reminder that he still carries a human core beneath the cold computational exterior. The fact that the illusion can *find* this core is what makes the Extreme Illusionary Realm genuinely dangerous.

### Phantom Realm Hall

The black, blood-soaked structure at the end of the Extreme Illusionary Realm corridor, designed to test fear and regret by isolating the cultivator’s senses and probing their most painful buried memories.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *slow-burn horror piece* disguised as a cultivation test. New readers might be tempted to skim the repeated illusion scenes, but don’t—each escalation is a diagnostic test of Han Li’s emotional architecture. The ero-illusion sequence (cranes → maidens → matrons → full-on *Apuleius’ Golden Ass* territory) isn’t fanservice; it’s a deliberate trap to waste time and lower inhibition, and Han Li’s clinical spectatorship tells us everything about his discipline. The real punch lands in the final beat: a single word—“Fourth Brother”—that shatters his momentum. Pay close attention to Han Li’s *silence* after the recognition. In the *Mortal Stream*, the protagonist’s most dangerous state is not rage but stillness. He is processing, recalculating, and deciding whether to engage or sever. That moment of controlled uncertainty is where the true tension lives. If you came for wall-to-wall battles, this chapter is *not* a battle chapter—but it is a setup for an internal one that matters more than any sword clash.
