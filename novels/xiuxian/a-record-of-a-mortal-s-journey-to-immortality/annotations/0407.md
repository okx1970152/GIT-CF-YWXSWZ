## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterclass in the quiet, high-stakes power games of the Mortal Stream. Han Li isn’t fighting for his life against a monster or a demon; he’s sitting in a tea house, negotiating the bulk sale of demon beast materials to a representative of the Sublime Sound Sect. And yet, the tension is palpable. The entire scene is a flawless demonstration of how a low-key protagonist like Han Li operates: he keeps a Core Formation trump card (Qu Hun) hidden, reads his opponent’s intentions before she can strike, and counter-punches decisively when a charm technique nearly takes hold. The chapter ends with the real prize—a lead on Divine Thunder Bamboo, one of the fabled Three Great Sacred Trees—dangled before him like bait. But in this world, nothing is free, and that bait is attached to a very dangerous hook.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li delivers the demon beast materials to Madam Fan and begins the transaction.
- Madam Fan deliberately undervalues the stock by 10%, then voluntarily raises the offer by 5% when Han Li’s silence unnerves her—a classic negotiation gambit that backfires because Han Li is a master of the silent treatment.
- Han Li accepts the final price, and the deal closes cleanly.
- Just as Han Li is about to leave, Madam Fan casually mentions she knows where Divine Thunder Bamboo can be found.
- The mention is coupled with a charm technique aimed at planting a long-term influence in Han Li’s mind.
- Han Li instantly recognizes the technique, counters it with sheer willpower, and reverses the effect—causing Madam Fan to suffer *art backlash* (a rare and painful self-inflicted wound).
- Han Li emerges as the clear winner of the psychological duel, and Madam Fan is forced to play nice.
- She reveals the bait: the bamboo was stolen from the sect en route to auction, and the Sublime Sound Sect is gathering allies to reclaim it.
- The chapter ends on a note of cautious optimism—but the implication is clear: the road to the Divine Thunder Bamboo is paved with blood and betrayal.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Art Backlash (功法反噬)** — In xianxia, pushing a soul-maze or charm technique against a stronger will is not just embarrassing; it can physically damage the practitioner. The energy used to influence the target’s mind is forcibly reversed back into the caster’s own channels, causing internal injury (often manifested as coughing blood). This is a classic way to show that Han Li’s mental fortitude is as dangerous as his cultivation.

**Divine Thunder Bamboo (天雷竹)** — One of the “Three Great Sacred Trees” in the RMJI setting, alongside the Golden Lightning Bamboo and the Myriad Year Spirit Wood. This bamboo is extraordinarily rare and is said to have a natural affinity for lightning-element energy. Just carrying a piece of it can subtly protect the owner from thunder-type tribulations. It is an absolute prize for any cultivator looking to refine a tengalgrade magic treasure.

**Sublime Sound Sect Negotiation Culture** — The Sublime Sound Sect is portrayed here as a politically agile, charm-oriented faction that relies on intelligence networks and social finesse rather than brute force. Fan Jingmei’s “polite, persistent, never directly threatening” voice is exactly how they operate: they will plant a hook, smile, and wait. Only when a direct threat (the charm backlash) forces their hand do they show a crack in the mask.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *must-read* for anyone who enjoys high-stakes negotiation scenes where the real battle is fought with words, gazes, and micro-expressions rather than swords. Pay close attention to Han Li’s silent handling of the price negotiation: his refusal to show eagerness forces Madam Fan to raise her own offer, a textbook example of the Mortal Stream’s “never let them see your hand” rule. Also, note how Han Li keeps Qu Hun hidden until the *after* the transaction, only revealing the Core Formation asset as a silent deterrent when the tension escalates. That’s not paranoia—it’s preparation. For Western readers unfamiliar with xianxia negotiation tropes, think of this as a corporate deal where one party just happens to have a nuclear weapon in their back pocket. And they’re not afraid to let you see it.
