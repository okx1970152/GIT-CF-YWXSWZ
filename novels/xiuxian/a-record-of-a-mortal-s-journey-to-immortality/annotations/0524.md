## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, grab your popcorn—this chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream logistics. Our favorite paranoid herb farmer, Han Li (currently playing the role of “Senior Qu”), has just discovered that the elusive Thousand Leaf Dew he’s been hunting is actually the sap of a Companion Herb that must grow beside an eighth-grade demon beast. That’s right: the ingredient list for his mysterious pill just became a suicide mission. But Han Li being Han Li, he doesn’t throw a tantrum—he files the data, buys a backup material (a beautiful agate horn), and politely declines a fishing-trip invitation from the suspiciously friendly shopkeeper, Li Xun. The kicker? Li Xun had a white-robed watcher hidden in his painting the entire time, and the watcher suspects Han Li knew he was there. The tension is quiet, but it’s *there*. This chapter is all about information warfare, social performance, and the slow burn of setting up a new arc.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li asks Gu Yu about Thousand Leaf Dew. Gu Yu produces an ancient jade slip containing the information.
- Han Li scans the slip: excitement → confusion → bitter realization. The Thousand Leaf Dew is Companion Herb sap from beside an eighth-grade demon beast.
- Li Xun watches Han Li’s changing expressions with growing interest; Gu Yu is relieved his information is correct.
- Han Li compensates Gu Yu generously with a bottle of cultivation pills and a Talisman Treasure—an enormous windfall for a Qi Condensation cultivator.
- Han Li asks about buying an agate beast horn. Li Xun quickly produces one, purchased for 5,000 spirit stones, and sells it at cost.
- Li Xun tries to recruit Han Li for group hunting expeditions. Han Li politely declines, citing upcoming pill refinement and seclusion.
- After Han Li leaves, a white-robed figure emerges from a painting in the room. He reveals he was watching the entire time and suspects Han Li noticed his presence.
- Li Xun defends his strategy: even if Han Li is special, befriending him was the right move. The white-robed figure reluctantly agrees.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Companion Herb (伴妖草)** — This is a classic xianxia concept: certain spiritual herbs are not born from pure qi, but from the ambient demonic energy (妖气) that seeps out of a demon beast’s lair over centuries. They are “companions” to the beast itself. The higher the beast’s grade, the more potent the herb’s properties—but also the more dangerous it is to harvest. In the Mortal Stream, this is a perfect example of the system’s brutal trade-off: the reward and the risk are the same thing.

**Eighth-Grade Demon Beast** — In the cultivation world’s beast hierarchy, grade 8 is a terrifying threshold. Most cultivators in the Outer Star Seas have only heard rumors of grade 7 beasts; a grade 8 is practically a walking natural disaster, roughly equivalent to a late Core Formation or early Nascent Soul cultivator in power. Han Li’s bitter reaction is *extremely* appropriate—this is not a beast you casually hunt; it’s a beast you pray you never meet.

**Spirit Stone Economy** — The agate horn is sold at 5,000 spirit stones, which Han Li pays in mid-grade stones. This reinforces the novel’s economic realism: even a Core Formation cultivator must budget carefully. The horn is not cheap, but it is a necessary ingredient, and Han Li does not haggle because the transaction is also a social test—Li Xun is watching how Han Li handles money.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a *setup chapter*, and if you’re used to Western action pacing, it might feel slow. But do *not* skim it. Every detail here is a planted flag: the eighth-grade beast will become a major plot point later; the agate horn is a long-term asset; Li Xun’s hidden watcher establishes that Han Li is already being observed. Mortal Stream novels earn their payoffs through this kind of quiet groundwork.

Pay special attention to Han Li’s social performance. He plays the part of a grateful, slightly naive Core Formation rogue—“Senior Qu”—perfectly. He accepts the jade slip with enthusiasm but does not demand it; he compensates Gu Yu lavishly but without over-explaining; he declines Li Xun’s invitation with polite delay rather than blunt refusal. Every action is calculated to leave a good impression while revealing nothing real. This is the art of the Mortal Stream: survival is often about managing how others see you, not how strong you are.

Also, note the white-robed figure’s suspicion. If a Nascent Soul-tier spy senses that Han Li noticed him, it means Han Li’s divine sense or paranoia is already operating at an abnormal level. The chapter quietly confirms that Han Li’s caution is so extreme it borders on supernatural—and that’s exactly what keeps him alive.
