## Chapter Overview
Fellow Daoists, settle down—your favorite information-gathering segment has arrived. Fresh out of the deep sea, Han Li stumbles upon three Core Formation cultivators who mistake him for a legendary bogeyman: the dreaded “Insect Devil.” Before the poor souls can soil their robes, Han Li calmly untangles the misunderstanding, frees them from a beetle-collar prison, and—because Han Li never wastes a social encounter—sits them down for a nice, informative chat about what the hell went wrong in the Deep Abyss while he was gone. The chapter is a masterclass in non-combat tension: nobody throws a punch, but the atmosphere is thick with paranoia, suspicion, and the looming shadow of a catastrophically broken frontier.

## Key Plot Points
1. The opening standoff: The three cultivators believe Han Li is the “Insect Devil” and brace for death. Han Li’s questioning confuses them—are they being toyed with?
2. Han Li dismantles the neck-ring restriction, proving his killing intent is zero and that they have indeed mistaken him for someone else, which instantly de-escalates the psychological pressure.
3. But Han Li is no charity worker. He separates the group, sends the Foundation Establishment disciples away, and insists the three Core Formation survivors meet him on the far side of the island for a “friendly chat.”
4. The vulture-faced elder (named Qiu internally) warns his companions to answer honestly and treat Han Li with the solemnity due a Nascent Soul elder, acknowledging the massive power gap.
5. Han Li’s interrogation yields two major intel drops: the Deep Abyss suffered a calamitous demon-beast rampage two years ago that drove even Nascent Soul cultivators out, and the “Insect Devil” is a real, notorious killer who has reportedly killed over a hundred cultivators using insect swarms.
6. The chapter closes with Han Li silently recognizing that the “Insect Devil’s” famous 7-Core-Formation kill from four years ago sounds suspiciously like *his own* battle record, planting a subtle seed of concern.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The “Insect Devil” (虫魔) Trope**: In xianxia worldbuilding, a “Devil” (魔) title is rarely a compliment. It marks a cultivator whose methods are so ruthless, so outside the orthodox code, that their very name becomes a taboo. The “Insect Devil” here is a perfect Dark Forest phenomenon: a single powerful killer with terrifying swarm-tech (万虫大法) haunts a sea region, and any unknown cultivator who also uses flying swords and beetles instantly invites suspicion. It’s reputation as a combat multiplier: even *being confused* with the Insect Devil gives Han Li a psychological edge before he says a word.

**The “Deep Abyss” (深渊) Ecosystem Collapse**: When a cultivation world’s primary resource zone (demon beasts for cores, materials) goes berserk, it doesn’t cause a simple trade disruption—it triggers a refugee crisis of mid-level cultivators spilling into safer zones. This chapter’s mention of Nascent Soul cultivators being driven out is a massive red flag. In RMJI cosmology, the balance of nature between sea and beast is a fragile contract; when it breaks, the human side’s infrastructure (markets, safe zones, aristocratic sects) suddenly looks flimsy and temporary.

## Reading Guide
This is a “peace between storms” chapter, but don’t let the calm fool you. The real tension is in the silent subtext: Han Li is piecing together that someone else out there is wielding *his exact signature arsenal*—multiple azure swords, a young face, Core Formation level, a cloud of devouring bugs. That’s not a coincidence; it’s a doppelgänger problem. Meanwhile, the Deep Abyss lore drop recontextualizes everything Han Li saw on his journey back. The flooding of refugee cultivators into the Outer Star Seas isn’t just an inconvenience; it’s a systemic collapse that will soon force every independent operator to take sides, spend resources defending territory, or flee even further. Han Li is not yet in danger, but he has just received a clean, cold map of the coming chaos. Watch for how he files this intel away.
