## Chapter Overview

In this chapter, Han Li attempts to break open the mysterious bottle using a hammer, but the bottle proves utterly indestructible. Realizing its extraordinary nature, he decides to keep it hidden rather than return it. His motivations are deeply rooted in class resentment: he despises the wealthy disciples and high-status individuals who have mistreated him and others from poor backgrounds. Recalling past humiliations—including a beating by martial-arts-trained rich kids—he feels no moral obligation to return lost property to such people. Instead, he hides the bottle in a leather pouch around his neck, feeling secure for the first time. The chapter reveals Han Li's pragmatic, self-protective worldview and his growing attachment to the mysterious artifact.

## Key Plot Points

Han Li decides to resort to brute force, selecting a heavy iron hammer from the tool shed. He places the bottle on a brick and strikes it with increasing force—five tenths, seven tenths, full strength, twelve tenths—each blow driving the bottle deeper into the brick. Yet the bottle remains flawless, without a single mark. This shocks Han Li and confirms that the bottle is extraordinary. He realizes it must have been lost accidentally, not discarded, and fears the owner might come searching. His resolve to keep it hardens as he recalls the injustices he has suffered: wealthy disciples spending lavishly while he goes hungry, mocking and beating him; authority figures like Protector Wang taking bribes from his uncle; Wu Yan entering the elite Seven Peaks Hall through nepotism. These memories fuel his decision to hide the bottle. He transfers it from his pocket to a leather pouch hanging around his neck, combining it with his lucky boar-tusk amulet. Feeling secure, he wanders the valley until dark.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter offers insight into Han Li's moral framework, which is shaped by his poverty and the harsh realities of sect life. In xianxia and wuxia traditions, protagonists often adhere to a code of honor—returning lost property, respecting elders, and upholding justice. Han Li, however, deviates from this convention. His decision to keep the bottle is not presented as a flaw, but as a pragmatic survival response within a world that has already taught him that the privileged are corrupt and unkind. This early characterization establishes the "mortal stream" ethos: morality is secondary to self-preservation and personal gain. The chapter also demonstrates Han Li's caution—he tests the bottle's hardness before committing to full force, and he conceals it carefully afterward. His methodical approach reflects the calculating nature that defines him throughout the series.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a turning point in Han Li's relationship with the mysterious bottle. Readers should pay attention to the detailed descriptions of his testing process—each incremental strike mirrors his methodical, risk-averse personality. His internal justification for keeping the bottle is one of the earliest explicit statements of his class-conscious worldview. The memories of fighting wealthy disciples and witnessing bribery serve as the foundation for his later moral flexibility. Also note the symbolic act of placing the bottle with his mother's amulet: the bottle becomes a new source of protection and fortune, replacing the talisman's traditional role. This subtle moment foreshadows the bottle's growing importance in his life.
