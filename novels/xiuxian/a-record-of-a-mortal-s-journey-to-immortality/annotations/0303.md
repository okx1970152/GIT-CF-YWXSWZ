## Chapter Overview
Two masked men—clearly the puppet master and his hot-headed field agent—debrief in private after wiping out Old Daoist Wu. The younger one admits to a mana backlash that forced him to drain the Daoist prematurely; the older one calmly dissects the tactical implications. Their target assessment process is a chilling model of operational pragmatism: they identify Han Li as a Foundation Establishment cultivator not through any flashy deduction but by coldly process-of-eliminating lower-tier hypotheses. Far from retreating, the older man sees this as a golden recruitment—the Sect Leader *needs* live Foundation Establishment subjects for a blood sacrifice, and a lone target is a rare logistical windfall. Meanwhile, Han Li lands his Divine Wind Boat in a desolate area, strips the four captives of their masks, confirms they are indeed the Mengshan Five, and sinks into a grim cost-benefit analysis: flee the city and abandon his Qin family protection duty, or stay and play a game he never intended to enter.

## Key Plot Points
- The younger masked man confesses to a premature mana backlash from rushing the Black Fiend Asura Art; his desperate act of draining Old Daoist Wu for refined blood is framed as a survival reflex, not malice.
- The older masked man rejects the idea of retreat; he reveals that the Sect Leader urgently needs Foundation Establishment cultivators for a blood sacrifice ritual, and that a lone, isolated target like Han Li is too valuable to let escape.
- He proposes requesting one or two of the Four Blood Attendants from the Sect Leader to lead the hunt, promising the younger man a share of the credit and even a path to Foundation Establishment as a reward.
- Han Li confirms the captives’ identities as the Mengshan Five (minus the forties woman) and realizes that the Young Prince and Chief Steward Wang are the masterminds behind the recent cultivator disappearances.
- Han Li reproaches himself for his own excessive caution: sending Old Daoist Wu to spy was the action that triggered the chain of events, and he now realizes the trouble is self-inflicted.
- His internal dilemma crystallizes: his duty to protect the Qin family under Li Huayuan’s commission prevents a clean escape, forcing him to consider a stand against an unknown foe within the capital.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Mana Backlash (法力反噬)**: A recurring danger in cultivation fiction where forcibly pushing a technique beyond one’s stable foundation causes internal energy to rebel. The Black Fiend Asura Art (黑煞修罗功) is depicted here as a high-risk, high-return demonic technique whose accelerated growth comes with a ticking clock—early, violent backlash. The older man’s advice to “consolidate your foundation” reflects the orthodox countermeasure: slow down, stabilize, and rebuild internal structure before attempting further breakthroughs.

**Blood Sacrifice (血祭)**: Not religious ritual in the Western sense, but a cold, engineering practice in xianxia logic. Here, the Sect Leader requires live Foundation Establishment captives not for vengeance but as raw materials—their refined blood, cultivation base, and vitality serve as fuel for a dark cultivation technique. The older man’s casual reference to “requiring some for a blood sacrifice” reinforces the Mortal Stream principle: high-level cultivators view lower-tier practitioners as consumable resources, not as moral agents.

**Soul Search (搜魂)**: Though not mentioned directly in this chapter, the older masked man’s confidence in locating Han Li within the vast capital—without ever discussing *how*—strongly implies that a Soul Search of Old Daoist Wu’s spirit has already yielded Han Li’s appearance, aura, or location. In xianxia, Soul Search is the ultimate anti-secrecy weapon: a cultivator can rip memories from a deceased or captured enemy’s soul, making anonymity nearly impossible once a connection is made.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in Mortal Stream pacing—not a single sword wave, but the tension builds through information asymmetry and cold institutional decision-making. Notice that both factions (the masked sect and Han Li) are running the exact same calculation: *what do we know about the enemy, and what assets can we commit?* The masked men decide to escalate by requisitioning Blood Attendants; Han Li’s answer is, conspicuously, withheld. The omission is deliberate—the narrator wants you to feel the weight of his indecision. Also note the structural irony: Han Li blames himself for “excessive caution” in sending Old Daoist Wu, but every step of his logic was sound—he simply didn’t account for a freak mana backlash. In the Mortal Stream, even correct risk assessment can fail you if the dice roll against you. Finally, keep a mental bookmark on the Four Blood Attendants (四大血侍)—this is the first mention of a Foundation Establishment–tier strike force in the sect, a detail that will matter the moment the hunt begins.
