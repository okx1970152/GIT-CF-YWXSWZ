## Chapter Overview
Old friends, the moment of truth has arrived! After all the scheming, bluffing, and side-eyeing in the Void Heaven Palace, Han Li and the undead Profound Bone Saint finally strike a deal—and the legendary Void Heaven Cauldron is about to rise again. But nothing in the Mortal Stream comes cheap: the price is a partnership with a thousand-year-old ghost who has every reason to betray him the second the treasure is in hand. This chapter is a masterclass in cold negotiation, high-stakes timing, and the brutal math of probability (30–40%? Han Li will take those odds). Oh, and that blue ice flame? It still bites like a thousand needles dipped in absolute zero.

## Key Plot Points
- Xuan Gu reveals the Heaven-Mending Pill’s true function: not a generic power boost, but a century-long Spiritual Root wash that raises the chance of forming a Nascent Soul to 30–40% *when combined with the Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng*.  
- Han Li remains skeptical—he demands proof and calls out the obvious betrayal risk. Xuan Gu counters with a fifty-fifty estimate of their actual combat power, a plea of mutual survival logic, and an immediate down payment: the Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng formula.  
- Han Li accepts the deal without further hesitation. The decision is purely tactical—once he commits, he acts with maximum efficiency.  
- Xuan Gu demonstrates Corpse Puppetry on the spot, reanimating the Blood Jade Spider corpse into a temporary puppet. This confirms his claim of being Extreme Yin’s former master and shows the depth of his ghost-path expertise.  
- The two begin the extraction. Han Li suffers under the blue-cold suppression while Xuan Gu manages with his ghost qi, highlighting the stark gap between Core Formation and Nascent Soul.  
- The chapter ends on a cliffhanger: the Void Heaven Cauldron’s form emerges from the icy flames, and Han Li gets his first clear look at the prize.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Heaven-Mending Pill (补天丹):** The name invokes the myth of Nüwa, the goddess who mended the sky after it cracked. In *RMJI*, this legendary pill doesn’t literally repair the heavens—it repairs the cultivator’s “heavenly endowment” (Spiritual Roots). The idea that a pill can fundamentally alter one’s innate talent is borderline heretical in the cultivation world, which is why Xuan Gu had to torture a Star Palace elder to confirm it. Such a rare and specific effect also explains why the pill is useless to anyone without a complementary ingredient (the Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng)—it’s a system key, not a universal booster.

**Corpse Puppetry (炼尸之术):** This is not your typical necromancy. In Chinese xianxia, corpse puppetry is a refined craft—one doesn’t just raise a dead body, but treats it as raw material to be processed, enhanced, and controlled through special techniques (often requiring rare yin materials or soul fragments). Xuan Gu’s ability to instantly reanimate a severed spider corpse showcases his mastery, but the puppet’s short lifespan and diminished power remind us that even top-tier puppetry has limits. It’s a perfect window into the ghost-path cultivation philosophy: nothing is wasted, not even a corpse.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a textbook example of Mortal Stream negotiation dynamics. Both parties know the other is untrustworthy, yet they strike a deal because the mutual gain outweighs the immediate risk of betrayal. Pay attention to how Han Li evaluates the odds: he doesn’t trust Xuan Gu for a second, but he does trust his own ability to *survive* a betrayal long enough to fight back. The thirty-second silence before his decision says more about his character than any internal monologue could. Also, savor the contrast between Xuan Gu’s dry, clinical tone when explaining the pill, and his barely concealed excitement when the cauldron starts moving—a rare crack in his undead mask.

## Heaven-Mending Pill vs. Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng: A Perfect Storm
Fellow Daoists, if you’ve ever wondered how a cultivator with “trash” roots can catch up to the geniuses, this is your answer. The Heaven-Mending Pill alone would be a game-changer—but it’s the *combination* with the Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng that pushes the probability to a workable 30–40%. In the ruthless math of xianxia, that’s practically a sure thing compared to the usual 1–5% chance. The catch? Both ingredients are legendary-tier, and the pill takes a century to fully take effect. In other words, this isn’t an instant power-up; it’s a long-term investment that only pays off if you survive the next hundred years. That’s the kind of bitter realism that defines the Mortal Stream: even a cheat takes time.
