## Chapter Overview
Our boy Han Li finally makes landfall on Starcrest Island—but not before one last round of high-stakes negotiation. Proprietor Gu, desperate to secure inter-island trading rights, dangles a carrot so tantalizing that even our cold-as-ice protagonist pauses to think. The deal? Han Li steps in as a hired gun for a mysterious martial challenge. The reward? A permanent home, a fat share of future profits, and—most crucially—access to the island’s priceless cultivation sites. This chapter is a masterclass in Han Li’s signature move: pretending to be pushed into a corner while calmly crunching the numbers, confirming that even in a brand new continent, the rules of the Dark Forest haven’t changed one bit.

## Key Plot Points
- Han Li firmly rejects Proprietor Gu’s initial request for help, citing his low cultivation and unwillingness to invite trouble on a new island.
- Wang Changqing reveals a key piece of worldbuilding: settling on a major island like Starcrest requires a local guarantor, without which permanent residence, official posts, and access to the Spirit Land Contest are impossible.
- Han Li’s attitude shifts when Proprietor Gu promises a life-changing package: a large Spirit Stone payment, a guarantor for permanent residence, and three years of 30% profit sharing from the Gu family’s trade.
- After weighing the risks, Han Li agrees to help—provided the rival cultivators are not at Foundation Establishment—revealing his belief that he can handle any Qi Condensation opponent with his puppets and tools.
- Four days later, the ship arrives at Starcrest Island’s immense harbor, and Han Li is struck by the sheer scale of the port—hundreds of ships and a sea of people.
- The chapter closes with Han Li’s practical first steps: completing landing procedures and silently observing the new environment, his mind already set on cultivating the Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art and the Great Derivative Art.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Guarantor System (保人/担保):** What sounds like a simple bureaucratic formality is actually the cultivation world’s version of a social credit trap. By requiring a local guarantor for permanent residence, the major islands ensure that powerful cultivators are tied to the local power structures. A wandering rogue who refuses to serve the local lords isn’t just a freeloader—he’s a security risk who can be denied the best spiritual qi and eventually pushed out. This isn’t about paperwork; it’s about control.

**The Spirit Land Contest (灵地争夺大会):** Every ten years, the island holds a tournament where residents compete for cultivation plots. The higher you rank, the better your spiritual qi. It’s a brilliantly brutal system: it pits cultivators against each other in a zero-sum game for the island’s best resources, ensuring the strongest are rewarded while the weak are naturally filtered. No welfare, no handouts. Raw Darwinism with a registration form.

**Port Economy & Transport:** The sheer scale of Starcrest Island’s port—hundreds of ships, the need for entire shiploads of goods, and the fact that Storage Pouches are considered too precious for bulk transport—reveals a cultivation economy that is far more complex and ancient than the Heavenly South’s. This isn’t a village sect world; this is a maritime civilization where logistics, trade routes, and geopolitical alliances matter as much as magical power.

## Reading Guide
Get ready, because we are *officially* leaving the Heavenly South behind. This chapter is a deliberate pivot: Han Li is not just changing locations; he is entering a new economic and political ecosystem. The deal with Proprietor Gu looks like a simple business transaction, but the subtext is Han Li’s first real alignment with a local power structure since leaving the Qixuan Sect. He’s not out of the Dark Forest—he’s just found a new tree to hide under.

Watch how Han Li plays the negotiation. He refuses first, then lets the other party *increase* the offer before reluctantly agreeing. It’s a textbook performance of “giving face” while securing maximum yield. The chapter also plants important seeds: Han Li is now thinking about long-term cultivation strategies (Three Revolutions Heavy Origin Art, Great Derivative Art, External Incarnation) in a place where he can actually settle. The home base arc is beginning.
