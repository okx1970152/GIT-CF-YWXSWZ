## Chapter Overview

Get ready for a masterclass in information warfare, fellow readers! Chapter 97 is a pure, concentrated dose of the ‘Mortal Stream’ we love. Forget flashy battles—this is a quiet, vicious duel of wits between Han Li and Sect Leader Wang. Han Li uses a single, perfectly timed line to probe the sect’s intentions, and the entire power dynamic shifts in an instant. It’s a story about how a perceived rumor can be more terrifying than a thousand swords, and how a clever man can make his enemies defuse themselves.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with a flashback revealing the secret meeting of the Seven Mysteries Sect’s high-ranking members. A violent debate erupts: a faction, driven by greed for Han Li’s techniques, wants to kill him immediately. A more cautious faction opposes the plan, fearing the unknown ‘elders’ who must have trained such a young prodigy. The debate is only settled when a gray-robed elder coldly asks if they are prepared to face those elders’ wrath.

In the present, Han Li’s first line to Sect Leader Wang is a direct probe. Seeing the leader’s reaction, Han Li confirms his suspicion: they did, at some point, consider killing him. However, Han Li remains utterly unbothered. He states that he has no intention of staying, and that he wants to make a “mutually beneficial transaction.”

The true brilliance of the chapter is in the framing. We see the sect’s internal panic, then Han Li’s calm exploitation of it. The chapter ends with a clear victory: at the scheduled meeting time, a composed Sect Leader Wang announces that Han Li has vanished, leaving the rest of the high-ranking members dumbfounded and their greedy schemes completely nullified.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**The Art of the Bluff (炸胡):** This chapter is a textbook example of ‘信息战’ (information warfare) at the street-smart level. Han Li doesn’t have a web of spies; he uses a single, ambiguous statement (“I hear someone is planning to do me harm”) to force Wang Juechu into revealing his own guilt. In the brutal world of ancient China, perception was often reality. A powerful unknown ‘master in the shadows’ was just as terrifying as one that was physically present. Han Li weaponizes the sect’s own paranoia, making them imagine a threat so powerful they censor themselves.

**‘A Good Bird is Put Away When its Arrows are Spent’ (鸟尽弓藏):** This proverb, which Han Li actively reflects on, is a core theme of the Mortal Stream. It describes the tragic fate of a hero who is discarded by their lord once they are no longer useful. Han Li knows that his value to the Seven Mysteries Sect has peaked. By highlighting this internal monologue, the chapter reinforces his core survival trait: he never expects gratitude and always plans his exit before his utility runs out.

**Power Dynamics of a ‘Transaction’:** The chapter’s climax—the unseen deal between Han Li and Wang Juechu—is a classic Dark Forest move. Han Li isn’t demanding a reward; he’s offering a transaction. The true “profit” for Wang is Han Li’s departure. By leaving voluntarily, Han Li removes the sect’s fear of him as an unknown, uncontrollable variable. This makes Wang willing to pay a high price to get Han Li’s promise to simply go away peacefully, rather than fighting a costly war against a boy who might have powerful backing.

A perception-based spell used by cultivators to see spiritual energy and potentially gauge another's power level.

## Reading Guide

We don’t see the details of the transaction itself, which is a brilliant narrative choice. The mystery makes Han Li’s victory feel even more absolute. The chapter confirms a key character rule: Han Li will never fall for the ‘grateful subordinate’ trap. He operates on a purely transactional basis. This chapter is a critical turning point for his character arc. He is no longer just surviving within the sect; he is actively manipulating its leadership and cutting his ties on his own terms. The sight of the high-ranking members sitting in stunned silence, their intricate plots rendered useless by a single, strategic conversation, is one of the most satisfying moments of the early novel.
