## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a turning point in Han Li's early journey. After days of patient observation, the mysterious bottle finally yields its first tangible product: a single drop of emerald-green liquid. Yet Han Li's initial reaction is disappointment—he had hoped for something more immediately useful. The chapter also showcases Doctor Mo's growing understanding of Han Li's psychology: a simple offer of doubled silver for each breakthrough in the oral formula reignites Han Li's flagging motivation. A four-year time jump follows, showing Han Li's transformation into a dark-skinned, silent youth who has reached the third layer of the nameless formula. The bottle is temporarily forgotten as the grind of cultivation takes over.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li's nocturnal experiments confirm that the bottle attracts light threads only in open spaces, and that the process peaks after several nights.
- On the eighth night, the bottle undergoes a dramatic transformation: green light flares, golden ancient characters appear on its surface, and the lid suddenly opens without resistance.
- Inside, a single drop of emerald-green liquid is found. Han Li is disappointed and puts the bottle away, planning to investigate later.
- Han Li's motivation for cultivation plummets after reaching the first layer, leading to Doctor Mo's frustration.
- Doctor Mo discovers the root of Han Li's apathy: lack of financial incentive. He promises to double Han Li's monthly silver for each layer breakthrough.
- The promise works: Han Li throws himself into desperate training. Four years pass, and he advances to the third layer of the oral formula, becoming a dark-skinned, silent, and resolute youth.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The mysterious bottle's behavior—absorbing light specks at night, producing a green liquid, and displaying golden characters—draws on traditional Chinese alchemical imagery. In Daoist internal alchemy (neidan), golden light and characters often signify the appearance of an elixir or a sacred text. The green liquid itself echoes the 'jade liquor' or 'green essence' of alchemical lore, though the novel subverts the expectation of immediate power: Han Li's first reaction is disappointment, reinforcing the mortal stream's anti-romantic stance. The time skip of four years is a common xianxia narrative device to show gradual progress while skipping repetitive training montages. Doctor Mo's use of silver as a motivational tool reflects the grimly pragmatic economy of the cultivation world: even at the lowest levels, resources (spirit stones, silver, pills) are the primary drivers of behavior, not lofty ideals.

## Reading Guide

This chapter establishes two critical narrative mechanisms. First, the bottle's function as a growth accelerator is hinted but not yet revealed—the green liquid's properties remain mysterious, setting up future experiments. Second, Han Li's character is further defined: his motivation is purely material (silver), and he responds best to concrete incentives rather than abstract goals like 'strength' or 'longevity.' Doctor Mo's insight into Han Li's psychology shows that even a manipulative master can be outsmarted by understanding his disciple's true desires. The four-year time skip also compresses the training arc, allowing the story to move toward deeper martial and cultivation conflicts. Readers should note the contrast between Han Li's childhood excitement at the bottle's secret and his later indifference—a sign of his hardening pragmatism.
