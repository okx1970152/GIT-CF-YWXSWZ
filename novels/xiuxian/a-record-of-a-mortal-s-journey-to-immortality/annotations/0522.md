## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, fellow Daoists, to the brutal playground of the Outer Star Seas! This chapter pulls back the curtain on Black Rock City and reveals just how different life—and business—is on the fringes of the known cultivation world. We get a fascinating (and deeply cynical) lesson from our guide Xu Yun on the socio-economic ecosystem of Abyssal Sea Island: a place where self-sufficient factions are slowly building a new frontier, mortals and low-level cultivators eke out a dangerous existence, and the Star Palace is content to let them be as long as they don’t cause trouble in the Inner Star Seas. But the real showstopper is the auction itself. After some tantalizing demon beast eggs, the bidding takes a dark turn with the appearance of human merchandise: female cultivators offered up as “Human Furnaces.” Buckle up—this chapter is a masterclass in the Mortal Stream’s cold, hard reality, where even flesh and blood are just another commodity on the block.

## Key Plot Points
- Xu Yun explains the unique ecosystem of Abyssal Sea Island: its self-sufficient resource base, the resulting influx of families, and the dangerous, lawless frontier life for low-level cultivators and mortals.
- The group learns why demon beast eggs are cheaper here: most can’t survive teleportation, creating a local market that attracts high-level cultivators from the Inner Star Seas.
- A fourth-grade Purple Cloud Eagle egg is auctioned for 3,300 spirit stones, drawing envy and ambition from the group (minus Han Li).
- The auction then shifts to a far more brutal commodity: four Qi Condensation female cultivators auctioned as “Human Furnaces.”
- Xu Yun delivers a calm, matter-of-fact explanation of the system: these women are volunteers, essentially trapped between a lack of talent, no backing, and the dead-end options of the Outer Star Seas.
- The chapter ends with the group processing this grim reality, with the female members showing visible discomfort while the men are more calculative.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Human Furnace Economy (炉鼎经济)** — This is one of the most uncompromisingly dark elements of the *Mortal Stream* genre. The term “Human Furnace” (炉鼎, *Lu Ding*) originates from Daoist alchemy, where a furnace and cauldron are the literal tools for refining pills. But in the twisted reality of xianxia, the metaphor is brutally inverted: a person becomes the vessel to be “refined” for another’s benefit. The system described here is not a villain’s plot, but an *institutionalized*, *voluntary* economic choice born from resource scarcity and rigid social stratification. A female cultivator with poor spiritual roots and no backing in the Outer Star Seas has few paths: dangerous manual labor, suicidal beast hunting, or attaching herself to a powerful male cultivator as a furnace. It’s a transaction—her cultivation base and body for protection, resources, and a chance at a better life. Don’t mistake the novel’s matter-of-fact tone for endorsement; this is the Mortal Stream’s way of showing you a system that grinds people down without a shred of sentimentality. The fact that it’s treated as *normal* is what makes it truly chilling.

**Abyssal Sea Island: A New Frontier** — The chapter draws a direct parallel to the colonization of the Inner Star Seas by the ancestors of modern cultivators. Abyssal Sea Island is a wild, dangerous, and resource-rich frontier where established rules are looser and the potential for gain (or death) is higher. The presence of mortal towns and low-level cultivators is a direct consequence of this self-sufficient ecosystem. It’s a fascinating piece of worldbuilding that shows how cultivation societies expand and evolve, creating their own brutal, survival-of-the-fittest economies far from the more “civilized” centers of power like Heaven Star City.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a classic *Mortal Stream* “quiet worldbuilding” section. On the surface, it’s just exposition at an auction. But pay close attention to the *system* being described. The auctioning of human beings is not a random event; it’s the logical conclusion of a society built on zero-sum resource competition. Han Li’s reaction is key: he is “startled” but quickly masters his shock, his mind already calculating the cold logic of it. This is Han Li’s greatest strength—he observes the ugliest systems, files the data, and moves on. He doesn’t waste energy on pointless moral outrage. This chapter reinforces a core *Mortal Stream* principle: the world is not fair, and your survival depends on understanding its rules, not wishing them away. Also, keep an eye on the demon beast eggs. The group’s excitement, especially the burly man’s, is a classic setup. In this world, a dream of easy power is often just the prelude to a very expensive lesson.

## Deep Dive: The Tao of the Furnace
For the uninitiated, the term “Human Furnace” might sound like pure fantasy horror. But like many concepts in *RMJI*, it has a twisted root in real Chinese philosophy. In Daoist inner alchemy (内丹术, *Neidan*), the practitioner’s own body is the furnace, and their essence (*Jing*), breath (*Qi*), and spirit (*Shen*) are the ingredients to be refined into an immortal “elixir.” The practice of “dual cultivation” (双修, *Shuangxiu*) was a heterodox branch that, in theory, involved partners harmonizing their energies. *RMJI* takes this already murky concept and drags it through the mud of its Dark Forest. It strips away any mystical or romantic pretense and lays bare a system of pure exploitation. The “furnace” isn’t a partner; it’s a resource to be consumed for another’s advancement. It’s a brutal, literal interpretation of what can happen when a philosophy of self-cultivation meets a world of absolute power disparity and institutionalized scarcity. This isn’t just a dark plot point; it’s a statement on how even the most esoteric traditions can be weaponized in a dog-eat-dog world.
