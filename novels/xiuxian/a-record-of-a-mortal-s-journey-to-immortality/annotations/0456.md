## Chapter Overview
On the surface, this is a chapter about hunting a legendary spirit ginseng. But beneath the calm strategizing and technical talk of formations and golden nets, a far darker game is being played. The Profound Bone Saint, outwardly a cooperative senior, has already drawn a line under Han Li’s name—he is marked for death. This chapter is a masterclass in **planned betrayal**, where false generosity is a weapon and every tool handed over carries a hidden blade. For the Mortal Stream reader, the tension is not in *if* the trap will spring, but in watching two cold calculators dance around each other, each waiting for the other to blink first.

## Key Plot Points
- The Profound Bone Saint detects the residual Pure Spirit Qi of the Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng on the stone mountain, confirming its location. He believes only a Nascent Soul-level spiritual sense can see it.
- Han Li, using the Great Derivative Art, secretly confirms he *can* see the faint traces, keeping this advantage hidden from the Profound Bone.
- The Profound Bone produces a specially refined golden silk net, explaining that only pure gold can capture the elusive ginseng, which ignores all other treasures.
- He then produces the Four Symbols Black Tortoise Formation banners, intended to trap the ginseng by hardening the surrounding earth.
- Feigning trust, the Profound Bone hands both items to Han Li to set up, claiming he will scout for the ginseng’s exact location.
- In private, the Profound Bone reveals his true plan to his inner companion: he intends to use the formation to kill Han Li once he enters to capture the ginseng, having already tampered with the banners.
- The companion is revealed to be an old acquaintance who taught the Profound Bone his demon-ghost cultivation methods.
- Their deeper conspiracy is exposed: they aim to seize Han Li’s body for the companion (a forbidden possession) and then use the fatal flaw in Extreme Yin’s technique to kill him as well.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Art of the Double Cross.** The Profound Bone Saint’s maneuver is a textbook example of the “false gift” (假礼) trope in Chinese web novels. He does not attack Han Li directly or refuse him—this would raise alarm. Instead, he hands over the very tools Han Li needs, making the trap self-executing. This mirrors an ancient Chinese strategic principle: “To destroy an enemy, first arm him with the means of his own defeat.” In the Mortal Stream, genuine cooperation is so rare that even a functional alliance can be a prelude to assassination, and a senior’s generosity is often a bill that will be collected in blood.

**Nine-Bend Spirit Ginseng.** This is not just a rare herb. The “Spirit Ginseng” (灵参) is a staple of Chinese folk mythology—a sentient, semi-immortal plant that can flee danger and burrow through solid earth. It is almost always depicted with childlike features and a temperament that avoids capture. The fact that only pure gold can bind it is a direct nod to traditional lore, where certain supernatural beings are invulnerable to all materials except a specific one (e.g., silver for werewolves in Western folklore). Using a golden net is thus not arbitrary but deeply rooted in folk logic.

**Leaving Flaws in a Master Technique.** The Profound Bone’s admission that he deliberately left a fatal flaw in the Extreme Yin Grand Art is a brutal commentary on master-disciple relationships in xianxia. In the Mortal Stream, a master never fully trusts a disciple; the technique itself is a leash. This echoes historical martial arts fiction where a master would withhold the final “killing move” or leave a hidden counter in the deepest layers of the art to ensure lifelong control. It’s not mentoring—it’s institutionalized hostage-taking.

## Reading Guide
Fellow Daoists, sharpen your senses—this chapter is a psychological battlefield packed with subtext. Watch how the Profound Bone speaks of “trust” while planning murder. Note that Han Li’s silent use of the Great Derivative Art is a *deliberate counter-surveillance measure*—he is already lying to the old fiend about his capabilities. The golden-net gift is beautiful, but it is also a transmission belt in a larger trap. The key question hovering over every scene: **Has Han Li figured out the tampered banners yet, or is he walking into the net with his eyes open?** The tension is not in the action—it’s in the silence between the lines. Also, pay attention to the gruff-voiced man’s identity. An “old acquaintance” who taught demon-ghost cultivation and now wants a new body? That name is a time bomb waiting to go off.
