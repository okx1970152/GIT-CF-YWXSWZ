## Chapter Overview
Alright, buckle up, fellow Daoists, because this chapter is a white-knuckle ride where our favorite paranoid mortal gets thrown into a soul-crushing power disparity. Han Li, the cockroach of the cultivation world, faces three Transformation-stage demon beasts, and his bag of tricks—from Emperor's Scale Armor to Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords—suddenly feels like peashooters. The chapter is a masterclass in the *Mortal Stream's* core tension: when all your clever plans and secret weapons meet a wall of sheer, incomprehensible biological superiority, what do you do? You don't panic. You don't rage. You calculate. Even as a Grade 9 Wind Split Beast is about to rip him open, Han Li's survival instinct isn't to scream, but to pivot. This is the Mortal Stream at its most brutal and thrilling—a chess match where your opponent can flick the board into the sun. Get ready for a reality check on the food chain.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with a cascade of failures for Han Li. The poison he successfully administered is being suppressed by the demonic cultivators through sheer willpower and cultivation base. His desperate counterattack with the Azure Bamboo Cloudswarm Swords, both as a swarm and as giant composite blades, fails utterly against the Wind Split Beast's mysterious white light. Even more chillingly, his swords can't wound the Grade 8 Flood Dragon and Turtle Demon *even when they land solid hits*. Han Li is forced to realize the qualitative chasm between a Core Formation cultivator and a Transformation-stage demon; his best attacks are mere annoyances.

In a tactical pivot, he gambles on a two-pronged attack: the Gold Devourer Beetles are sent to devour the other two demons, while he uses the giant swords to attack the furnace's protective barrier, threatening to destroy the Wind Lightning Wings. This move reveals a crucial weakness for the Wind Split Beast, who cares more about the fledgling treasure than the safety of his subordinates. However, this desperate gambit also backfires when the Flood Dragon's final, desperate struggle and subsequent explosion unleashes a "bloody light" of unparalleled toxicity, instantly killing hundreds or thousands of Han Li's precious beetles. The chapter ends with the demonic cultivator Feng Xi, though immobilized, starting to speak, promising a terrible end for Han Li the moment he can move again. The power balance is shattered, and Han Li is facing a failure cascade.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter puts a fine point on the **Cultivation Hierarchy**. In western fantasy, a clever hero can often talk or trick their way out of a fight with a dragon. In the *Mortal Stream* logic of *A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality*, the gap between a Core Formation cultivator and a Transformation-stage demon beast is not a gap; it's a canyon. Core Formation is about consolidating your power into a 'core' (*Jiedan* stage), while Transformation-stage (化形, *Huaxing*) means the demon beast has condensed their power into a humanoid form, gaining access to a whole new tier of intelligence, magical techniques, and physical resilience. The text explicitly states that Han Li's flying swords cannot scratch the Grade 8 demons' hides, which is a direct statement of tier-based immunity. This isn't a case of "the enemy has more hit points"; it's a systemic "your weapon is not meant to hurt this kind of being."

This reinforces the core **Mortal Stream** principle of risk-reward calculation. Han Li's entire strategy is predicated on the assumption that his trump cards (the Azure Origin Sword formation, the Gold Devourer Beetles, the Emperor's Scale Armor) are enough. When they prove insufficient, his primary emotion is not despair but a cold, analytical shock that forces a recalculation. He immediately pivots from a "kill them all" strategy to a "destroy the treasure they are protecting" strategy, using a psychological attack instead of a physical one. This pragmatic shift, even in the face of imminent death, is the hallmark of a survivor in this universe, not a hero.

## Reading Guide
Pay close attention to the *feeling* of this chapter. The author, Wang Yu, is famous for his "plain sketch" style, but here he uses that monotone to create a devastating sense of helplessness. *Your sword hits... and nothing happens. Your bugs swarm... and only a few manage to dim the light. You dodge one claw... and know the next one won't miss.* The horror isn't in the gore; it's in the flat, clinical realization that you, the protagonist, have miscalculated. The true threat isn't the enemy's power, but the fact that your own power is now a rounding error in the equation.

Also, note the introduction of the Wind Lightning Wings (风雷翅) as a motive. Feng Xi's panic at their potential destruction is his one vulnerability. This is a classic *Mortal Stream* trope: the most powerful opponent has a single, exploitable anchor of greed or ambition. Han Li, even when completely outgunned, can still threaten the enemy's "bottom line." The chapter's final horror is the Flood Dragon's self-destruction. This isn't a last stand; it's a final, desperate act of spite that takes a chunk of Han Li's assets with it. In this world, even your victories are pyrrhic, and the costs are paid in blood and resources you can never get back. Read this chapter to feel the pressure of a cold, uncaring cosmos where the only rule is that the bigger fish is always just around the corner.
