## Chapter Overview
This chapter is classic *RMJI*—a bureaucratic dissection dressed up as a sect welcome. Forget heroic anthems; the entire first half is a cold, political tug-of-war over one boy with a special body type. The sect elders don't see *disciples*; they see resource allocations, lineage multipliers, and institutional bargaining chips. For Han Li, the real takeaway isn't the drama—it's the system itself. He's not being *welcomed*; he's being *audited* into a category: outer affairs, expendable labor, with a side of “go learn talisman-making.” By the chapter’s end, he’s been filed into Tianquan Peak—not because he’s special, but because he’s *generic*. And in this world, generic is a survival strategy.

## Key Plot Points
- The group arrives at the Falling Cloud Sect’s main peak, which towers over the surrounding peaks. The base is a bustling, organic market-town—not a mystical paradise, but a working economic hub.
- Yu reports to the Sect Leader (Wei Yiming, a late Foundation Establishment cultivator) in the main stone hall. The seven new “outer affairs” disciples are kept waiting alongside four “inner disciple” candidates, creating an immediate, unspoken class tension.
- Inside, the sect elders argue openly over the “Metal Forging Constitution” disciple: Hidden Sword Peak and Blazing Cloud Peak both claim him. The dispute is cut short by Senior Brother Qiu’s intervention, and the Sect Leader defers the special case.
- A lengthy allocation process unfolds off-screen. The result: Han Li and the bearded man are assigned to Tianquan Peak as outer affairs disciples. The Metal Forging youth is sent to Phantom Stone Peak as a compromise.
- On the return flight, Yu’s tone shifts from cold to informational. He spells out the “generous” deal: complete your tasks, learn the trades (talismans, alchemy), and *maybe* you’ll earn a Foundation Establishment Pill if you perform a miracle. The system isn’t cruel—it’s *efficient*.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**Metal Forging Constitution (煅金之体 / Duan Jin Zhi Ti)**
This is a special physique type, rarer than a single spiritual root but not quite a “Heavenly Root.” It signifies a body born with an innate affinity for metal-element qi—far more efficient at absorbing and channeling metal-attribute energy than a normal cultivator. In sect politics, such a body is a “fixed asset”: it guarantees faster growth in the right lineage, making it a trophy worth fighting over. In *RMJI*, this isn’t about fairness—it’s about optimizing returns on a teaching slot.

**Outer Affairs Disciple vs Inner Disciple (外事弟子 vs 内门弟子)**
Outer affairs disciples are *not* second-class in name, but in practice, they are labor-class cultivators. They perform sect duties (resource gathering, crafting, guard duty) and only cultivate in their spare time. Inner disciples, selected by spiritual root quality, are exempt from labor and expected to focus entirely on breakthrough. The system is a talent funnel: *spiritual roots decide your job, not your ambition*.

**The Six Peaks Economy (六峰体制)**
Falling Cloud Sect’s six peaks function like semi-autonomous departments within a corporation. Each peak has its own specialties (fire-attribute, metal-attribute, talisman-focused, etc.) and competes for resources—namely, *people*. The argument over the Metal Forging disciple is a corporate budget meeting, not a mystical ceremony.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a perfect example of *why* Han Li’s survival path is so distinct from a typical chosen one. Watch how he *processes* this moment: he’s not offended, not inspired, and certainly not daydreaming about future glory. He’s taking mental notes on the power structure—who argued, who deflected, who was deferred. The fact that he ends up in Tianquan Peak (the “mediocre” peak) is a gift: *being overlooked is the best camouflage in the Dark Forest*. If you came here expecting a hero’s welcome, you’re reading the wrong novel. Han Li just got handed a job, a uniform, and a quiet corner to disappear into. That’s a win.
