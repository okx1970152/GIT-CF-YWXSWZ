## Chapter Overview

Our boy Han Li finally gets a proper win this chapter—not a bloody one, but a *quiet victory* in the currency of the cultivation world: knowledge and leverage. After setting his sights on Lei Wanhe as a more promising mark than his own stingy master, he skillfully negotiates an exchange of two rare spirit herbs for two ancient elixir formulas. The plump old Core Formation cultivator, feeling a bit guilty for offloading the obscure recipes, throws in an extra piece of advice about ingredient substitution. But the real jackpot comes afterward: Han Li loots Senior Brother Lin's corpse and discovers a jade slip containing the *Great Derivative Art*—a name that practically screams "game-changing cultivation technique." It's the kind of low-key, high-yield development that makes Mortal Stream readers grin from ear to ear.

## Key Plot Points

- Han Li immediately identifies Lei Wanhe as a better target for his formula-exchange scheme than his own master, Li Huayuan, reasoning that the master-disciple relationship makes extraction harder, not easier.
- After initial refusal citing clan secrets, Lei Wanhe reveals he has *ancient formulas* available for trade—recipes whose ingredients are rare but not extinct, unlike many others.
- Lei Wanhe gives Han Li a crucial piece of advice: if the original ingredients prove impossible to gather, try substituting alternatives. This is both a genuine kindness and a way to ease his own conscience for offloading difficult formulas.
- Han Li selects the Spirit Condensation Pill and Qi Refining Powder from the directory, and Lei Wanhe provides the real recipes in jade slips.
- Han Li learns the location of Li Huayuan's cave dwelling from Lei Wanhe before the latter departs.
- Post-departure, Han Li searches Senior Brother Lin's corpse and retrieves a storage pouch—but finds no puppets, only a few spirit stones and an unremarkable jade slip.
- Upon examining that jade slip, Han Li discovers it contains the *Great Derivative Art*, a discovery that visibly shocks him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

**Ancient Formulas (古方):** In the logic of xianxia alchemy, "ancient formulas" occupy a paradoxical position: they are theoretically superior in quality because they were created when spirit herbs were more abundant and potent, but their practical value is often questionable because the specific ingredients may have become rare or extinct. This creates a *chicken-rib economy* (鸡肋, jī lèi)—"tasteless to eat, yet a pity to discard"—that runs through the entire cultivation economy. Modern formulas are essentially patchwork substitutes invented when ancient recipes became unworkable. For Han Li, who possesses the Heaven Vial's growth acceleration, ancient formulas are *perfectly suited*: he can afford to hunt for rare ingredients and accelerate their growth, while other cultivators cannot. Lei Wanhe's advice about substitution is well-intentioned but misguided when applied to the protagonist's unique advantage.

**Nominal Disciple vs. Personal Disciple (记名弟子 vs. 亲传弟子):** The distinction between these two statuses is a recurring structural element in xianxia. A nominal disciple is a low-commitment arrangement: the master provides minimal guidance, the disciple owes minimal loyalty, and both parties know the relationship is transactional. A personal disciple, by contrast, receives real instruction and resources but also incurs genuine obligations and debts. Han Li's calculation in this chapter—that approaching someone *other* than his nominal master would be easier—is a perfect example of how the Mortal Stream treats master-disciple bonds as strategic liabilities rather than sacred relationships.

**The "Search the Corpse" Reflex:** The fact that Han Li immediately searches Senior Brother Lin's corpse without hesitation, even after a successful negotiation with a Core Formation cultivator, is a core Mortal Stream behavioral marker. In the cultivation world, death is a transfer of assets, and sentimentality about looting is a luxury that leads to early graves. Lei Wanhe's failure to loot the body is either a calculated show of face in front of a junior (leaving the scraps for the beggar) or genuine disdain for the low-tier haul—both interpretations reinforce the predatory nature of the cultivation hierarchy.

### Chicken Rib Economy (鸡肋)

A xianxia term for resources that are valuable in theory but impractical to use; ancient formulas are a classic example.

### Looting Reflex (摸尸)

The default post-conflict behavior in cultivation fiction; a dead cultivator's body is treated as an unowned resource node.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a *textbook example* of the Mortal Stream's "quiet victory" structure: no explosions, no betrayals, no desperate last-stand fights—just a smooth, calculated transaction followed by an unexpected windfall. The genre-savvy reader will feel the setup in their bones: an obscure-sounding technique called "Great Derivative Art" appearing at the exact moment Han Li needs it is not a coincidence, but the novel's natural logic of rewarding preparation. The chapter also showcases Han Li's social engineering skills: he correctly reads Lei Wanhe's personality as more sentimental and less guarded than Li Huayuan, adjusts his pitch accordingly, and walks away with exactly what he wanted. Pay attention to the unstated tension between "ancient formulas are hard to use" (Lei Wanhe's framing) and "I have a magic bottle that accelerates plant growth" (Han Li's hidden trump card)—the reader is now in on a secret the seller doesn't know, and that asymmetry is pure Mortal Stream satisfaction.

## On the Value of Looting

One of the most quietly important narrative conventions in the *Mortal Stream* genre is the **post-conflict loot reflex**. Unlike Western fantasy where looting a corpse is sometimes treated as morally questionable or reserved for "rogue" characters, in xianxia it is a *default survival behavior*—a body without an owner is simply a resource node waiting to be claimed. The reason is structural: cultivation is an economy of extreme scarcity, and any loss of opportunity is a loss of lifespan. Han Li's immediate pivot from joyful formula-acquisition to corpse-searching is not greed but *habitual resource optimization*. The fact that he almost missed the jade slip because he was disappointed about the missing puppets is a delightful narrative irony—his own pragmatic disappointment nearly caused him to overlook the actual treasure.

## Reading Between the Lines: Lei Wanhe's Character

Lei Wanhe emerges from this chapter as one of the *least hostile* high-level cultivators Han Li has encountered so far. He voluntarily warns Han Li about the difficulty of gathering ingredients, he offers a two-formula exchange instead of just one, and his guilt about the deal suggests he still operates with a residual sense of fairness. In a world where Core Formation cultivators routinely cheat Foundation Establishment juniors without a second thought, this makes Lei Wanhe almost saintly by contrast. The attentive reader should note this: Lei Wanhe is not a schemer, just a slightly soft-hearted old man who wants his Essence Augmentation Pill. Such characters are rare in the Mortal Stream, and they usually either get exploited or die early.
