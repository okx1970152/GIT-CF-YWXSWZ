## Chapter Overview
Welcome back, Fellow Daoists, to the financial education segment of our cultivation journey! This chapter is a deep dive into the gritty, nuts-and-bolts economy that makes the immortal world go round. Forget flying swords for a moment; the true power in the cultivation world is the *Spirit Stone*. Our boy Han Li has finally navigated his way into a secret cultivator gathering, and what he finds is a marketplace that is both a treasure trove and a stark reminder of his own poverty. This isn't a chapter of epic battles, but a crucial piece of world-building that explains how power is measured, traded, and hoarded. It sets the stage for all future transactions and power plays.

## Key Plot Points
*   **The Spirit Stone Economy:** The chapter kicks off with a comprehensive lecture on the Spirit Stone, the lifeblood of the cultivation world. It’s used for trading, powering formations, amplifying spells, and quick mana recovery. The scarcity of Spirit Stone veins has made them a universal, regulated currency (Low, Mid, High, Supreme), with elemental variants (Fire, Water, etc.) being even more valuable.
*   **Han Li, The Pauper:** We get a stark confirmation of Han Li’s material status: zero Spirit Stones. This realization frames his entire approach to the market. He is an observer, a scavenger, forced to rely on his wits and whatever goods he can barter instead of having financial firepower.
*   **The Lure of Talismans:** Han Li is drawn to talismans, especially blank talisman paper for his own "Spirit-Fixing Technique." He sees ready-made "Vajra Talismans," "Thunderfire Talismans," and others. He does a price comparison with his own defunct Vajra Talisman, concluding his used copy is worth a third of a fresh one.
*   **The Heavenly Flight Talisman:** The only Primary High-Grade talisman in sight is a "Heavenly Flight Talisman" for 30 Low-Grade Spirit Stones, or trade for equivalent "origin-nourishing pills." The seller, a shrewd straw-hatted youth, is not intimidated by a haggling customer named Ye Bao from the "Qin Ye Ridge."
*   **A Potential Trade:** When the crowd clears, Han Li seizes his chance. He presents his "Yellow Dragon Pills" and "Golden Marrow Pills" to the stall owner, hoping to trade for the flight talisman. The youth carefully evaluates them but, in the end, deems them "not quite enough," refusing the trade.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Economics of Immortality (修仙经济学):** The introduction of the Spirit Stone system is where your standard Western fantasy and Xianxia diverge sharply. In many Western systems, gold is the universal currency. In Xianxia, that's for mortals. The real economy runs on refined, condensed spiritual energy—the Spirit Stone. This isn't just a plot convenience; it’s a brilliant reflection of the world’s core logic. The ultimate resource is the energy needed to live longer and gain power. By making that energy a physical, depletable, and hierarchical currency (Low-Grade to Supreme), the novel introduces a built-in engine for scarcity, class division, and conflict.

**Why Medicines Matter Most:** The seller's willingness to trade a High-Grade talisman for "origin-nourishing pills" is a critical detail for readers. It underscores the 'Mortal Stream' philosophy: in a zero-sum game, your base vitality (health, lifespan, cultivation foundation) is the most valuable asset you own. A flashy flight talisman is useful, but a pill that extends your life or stabilizes your cultivation is priceless. This explains why Han Li’s medical background is his single greatest advantage in this new world.

## Reading Guide
Your perspective for this chapter should be entirely through Han Li's calculating eyes. He is a man walking into a foreign stock exchange with nothing but the shirt on his back. He’s not just sightseeing; he's performing a rigorous market analysis. Note how he instantly assesses value ("My Vajra Talisman is worth a third"), understands the power of leverage (medicine for flight talisman), and recognizes systemic dangers (the "Qin Ye Ridge" name sets off his alarm bells). This chapter is a masterclass in survival through information gathering and economic positioning, even when you have complete zero financial capital.
