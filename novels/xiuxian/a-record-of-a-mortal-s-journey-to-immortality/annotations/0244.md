## Chapter Overview
Oh boy, Han Li just got hit with the biggest unexpected side-quest of his Foundation Establishment career: a marriage arrangement! In this chapter, the political machinations of the cultivation world come crashing down on our cautious protagonist. Uncle-Master Hong Fu, a powerful Core Formation elder, has taken a liking to Han Li and wants him to become her disciple’s dual cultivation partner. What sounds like a dream come true for any other sect disciple is a trap for Han Li, whose secrets and obsessive caution make the prospect of a close partner a nightmare. The chapter masterfully dissects the cynical logic behind such pairings, exploring the role of appearance, politics, and unresolved past traumas in shaping these decisions.

## Key Plot Points
*   Han Li finds himself the object of a sudden praise session by his master Li Huayuan, which immediately triggers his danger sense.
*   The source of his unease is revealed: the red-robed elder, Uncle-Master Hong Fu, has selected him to be the dual cultivation partner of her disciple, a young woman surnamed Dong.
*   Wu Xuan, the handsome seventh senior brother, is revealed to have been rejected for the role and is now casting hostile glances at Han Li.
*   Li Huayuan explains the rationale: Uncle-Master Hong Fu has a deep aversion to handsome men due to past trauma, making Han Li's ordinary appearance a virtue.
*   The young woman, Miss Dong, is described as having mediocre aptitude and only reaching Foundation Establishment through massive resource investment, showing she is her master's spoiled favorite.
*   Han Li's refusal is immediate but cautious. He argues the match is forced, as Miss Dong clearly prefers Wu Xuan.
*   The crisis is deflected by Li Huayuan's wife, who brings up rumors about Miss Dong's past, forcing her husband to take the matter more seriously.
*   The chapter ends with Li Huayuan dismissing the other disciples to discuss the rumors privately with Han Li.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of **"Dual Cultivation Partners"** is a core pillar of xianxia world-building. It's not simply a marriage; it's a strategic alliance for mutual benefit in cultivation. A good partner can accelerate one's progress, and the union often solidifies bonds between powerful masters or sects. However, as this chapter brilliantly illustrates, in the Mortal Stream, romance is often a byproduct of logistics. The pairing is a political deal between Li Huayuan and Hong Fu. Miss Dong's status (a pampered, average-talent disciple of a powerful elder) makes her a prize to be won, not a person to be courted. This cynical framing is classic *RMJI*.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in social tension and political maneuvering within a sect. Han Li’s perspective keeps the reader grounded in his paranoia. Watch how Li Huayuan’s initial joy slowly curdles into frustration as his disciple refuses a “perfect” deal. The real star is Han Li’s internal cost-benefit analysis, which trumps any potential benefits of the union. His silent rejection is driven not by pride but by the hard, logical truth that a close partner means more eyes on his secrets. The chapter is a must-read for understanding how relationships are forged in this world—not with love, but with a calculator.
