## Chapter Overview
Get ready, because *things just got real*. The demonic blood servants are breaking out of their cocoons, and what emerges is not a smooth, heroic transformation—it’s a messy, incomplete, and utterly terrifying half-step into monstrosity. Liu Jing tries a flashy golden-massacre spell, Song Meng unleashes a Talisman Treasure, and Han Li? He releases his beast puppets and stations himself in the back row, conserving mana like a man who knows the real boss hasn’t even shown up yet. But the chapter’s killer moment is pure Mortal Stream ruthlessness: one of their own is murdered mid-cultivation by a demon who can turn invisible. No warning. No dramatic duel. Just a hand through the chest.

## Key Plot Points
- The blood servants’ light cocoons prove immune to conventional attacks, forcing the Yellow Maple Valley team to burn high-grade assets.
- Liu Jing activates a golden talisman that unleashes the Thousand Swords Art, a rare mid-grade metal-element group attack that finally damages one cocoon.
- Song Meng and Chen Qiaojian’s senior sister reveal Talisman Treasures, confirming that only the wealthy or favored can afford such strategic reserves.
- Han Li deploys four beast puppets but deliberately withholds his own trump cards, prioritizing mana conservation for the Black Fiend Leader.
- The Ice Fiend emerges prematurely, half-transformed, with a quickly regenerating shoulder wound; his incomplete form still threatens the party.
- Han Li, ignoring the demon’s threats, orders the puppets to strike first, disrupting the party’s paralysis.
- The Ice Fiend vanishes into stealth using a cold mist escape technique.
- Chen Qiaojian’s senior sister is killed instantly, her chest pierced through; her expression solidifies from terror to unwillingness, then stillness.
- An author’s note breaks the chapter, promising a carefully paced climax and advising readers to rest.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
**The Talisman Treasure Economy**: A Talisman Treasure (符宝) is a fragment of a real magical treasure’s power sealed into talisman form. It is a consumable—once its energy is spent, the talisman is lost. In the Mortal Stream, possessing one is a status marker: either you inherited it from a powerful elder, or you belong to a wealthy clan. Han Li has one too (the gray short-sword), but he hasn’t used it yet. This economy reinforces that high-end combat power in the cultivation world is *inheritable wealth*, not earnable skill.

**Metal-Element Rarity**: The Five Elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) each have their usual spell categories. Metal is notoriously rare in offensive cultivation spells—most metal-element techniques are defensive (Golden Armor, Metal Skin). A mid-grade group attack like Thousand Swords Art is therefore a serious flex, signaling not just power but *institutional access* to lost or elite spell lineages.

**Demonic Transformation (妖化)**: In xianxia, a cultivator can surrender their human form for raw demonic power through a cocoon-like transformation (破茧). This is not a graceful super-saiyan power-up; it’s a brutal, body-warping process that often leaves the subject half-mad or semi-feral. The Ice Fiend’s incomplete emergence is itself a failure mode—he escaped early, retaining some intelligence but still gaining claws, scales, and regeneration. A full transformation would have been worse.

## Reading Guide
**Watch the mana economy**: Han Li is doing something very clever here—he’s using the cheapest options (beast puppets, stored mana) while his teammates burn expensive talismans and personal trump cards. He knows the Black Fiend Leader is the real threat, and he’s not about to exhaust himself clearing the adds. This is textbook Mortal Stream resource management.

**The stealth murder**: The Ice Fiend’s first kill is a perfect demonstration of the Mortal Stream’s lethality. The senior sister is at her most vulnerable—sitting still, channeling a Talisman Treasure, completely exposed. She dies not in a dramatic blade exchange but in a silent, invisible strike. The horror here is not the gore (though there is some) but the *systemic betrayal* of the party’s formation: the healer/damage dealer died because no one was watching the invisible enemy.

**Author’s note**: The chapter ends with Wang Yu’s typical transparent break note—promising a carefully crafted climax, apologizing for the delay, and advising readers to sleep. This is serialization culture in the raw. It costs nothing to preserve; it adds a layer of real-world texture.
