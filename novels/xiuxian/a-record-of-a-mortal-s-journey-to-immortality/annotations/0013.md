## Chapter Overview

This chapter focuses on Han Li’s interactions with Zhang Tie and the first occurrence of a supernatural phenomenon involving the mysterious bottle. Zhang Tie brings dinner to Han Li’s room due to his injured foot. They discuss their cultivation progress: Zhang Tie bitterly complains about the painful Elephant Hide Art under Doctor Mo’s brutal training, while he envies Han Li’s seemingly easy nameless oral formula. After Zhang Tie leaves, Han Li studies the bottle again but falls asleep. He wakes to a cold sensation and witnesses white light threads descending from the skylight onto the bottle, forming countless glowing dots. The bottle appears to draw in these light motes, which behave as if alive. Frightened at first, Han Li recovers his curiosity, touches a dot, and decides to test the bottle outside. In the open, the light dots become far more numerous, creating a large luminous sphere. This chapter establishes the bottle’s otherworldly properties without naming them, laying the foundation for its crucial role ahead.

## Key Plot Points

- Zhang Tie takes care of Han Li by bringing meals to his room, showing their growing camaraderie. During the meal, Zhang Tie vents his frustration with the Elephant Hide Art, describing the painful sessions of medicine baths and wooden beatings that leave him unable to sleep. He envies Han Li’s nameless formula, which requires only meditation. Han Li remains quiet but internally understands Zhang Tie’s fear. After Zhang Tie leaves, Han Li returns to examining the mysterious bottle he found earlier. He becomes drowsy and falls asleep holding it. Suddenly, he is awakened by a chilly sensation. He sees thin strands of white light falling through the skylight onto the bottle, coalescing into bright specks. The bottle is enveloped in a soft white glow. Startled, Han Li throws it away, but after observing no danger, retrieves it. He discovers that the light dots are actively forcing themselves into the bottle, not being pulled in. He touches one and finds it merely cool. Curious, he steps outside and places the bottle on the ground. The light dots vanish when the bottle is packed away, but when exposed, they return in greater numbers, forming a washbasin-sized ball of light. This phenomenon confirms the bottle’s unique ability to gather ambient energy, a trait that will prove invaluable later.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

In xianxia cosmology, the energy of heaven and earth (天地灵气) is often invisible but can be condensed into visible forms near powerful artifacts or blessed lands. The white light threads and dots in this chapter represent such condensed spiritual energy, attracted by the mysterious bottle. This is consistent with the trope of a cultivation treasure that “absorbs the essence of the sun, moon, and stars” (吸收日月精华), a concept rooted in Daoist alchemy where celestial bodies are sources of vital energy. The bottle’s behavior—gathering light only when exposed to the open night sky—implies a connection to the heavens. Note that the novel does not yet explain the nature of this energy; it remains a mystery, adding to the story’s slow-burn revelation style. This scene also echoes the real-world Daoist practice of absorbing qi during specific times, such as midnight. However, in this fiction, the bottle itself does the gathering, not the cultivator.

## Reading Guide

This chapter introduces the first overt supernatural element in the story, so far grounded in martial arts realism. Pay attention to how the narrative handles the unexplained phenomenon: Han Li’s reaction shifts from fear to playful curiosity, typical of his pragmatic but inquisitive character. The description is vivid yet restrained—no overblown fantasy language, just “white light threads” and “cool sensation.” This aligns with the novel’s “white sketch” style. For new readers, note that the bottle’s ability is not named or explained; the author trusts the audience to accept mystery. This sets up a long-term plot device that will unfold over many chapters. Also observe the contrast between Zhang Tie’s physical suffering and Han Li’s meditative cultivation—two extremes of martial training in this world. The outdoor test shows Han Li’s effort to understand the bottle through experiment, hinting at his analytical approach to problems.
