## Chapter Overview

In this pivotal chapter, Xiao Chen reaches a profound philosophical breakthrough that allows him to ascend to the Mortal-Shedding Sixth Heaven. The chapter opens with him feeling the limits of his previous cultivation and realizing that his own pressure had become a cage. Through a meditative experience at dawn, he achieves enlightenment on the cyclical nature of life, growth, and decline, and by releasing all tension, his cultivation barriers crumble. This breakthrough is accompanied by a spectacular supernatural phenomenon: from his five divinized acupoints, four giant dragon shadows emerge—the Eight-Armed Malignant Dragon, the Serpent-Elephant Dragon, the Sword Dragon, and the Lion King Dragon—roaring and circling him. The dragon roars trigger a massive avalanche that buries dozens of greedy cultivators who had come to steal his dragon eggs. The chapter balances serene, almost poetic introspection with sudden, devastating destruction, and Xiao Chen emerges with a new calm, detached authority.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen begins by assessing his cultivation at the peak of Mortal-Shedding Fifth Heaven, aware of a bottleneck caused by his own mental tension. Standing on a snowy peak at sunrise, he enters a state of meditative emptiness and experiences a sudden enlightenment. He recognizes that the pressure he placed on himself had turned from motivation into resistance, mirroring the natural cycle of rise, peak, and decline. By fully relaxing and letting go, his body absorbs primordial energy and breaks through to Mortal-Shedding Sixth Heaven. The breakthrough triggers an unforeseen reaction: his five divinized acupoints emit dazzling light and manifest four distinct dragon shadows formed from pure divine energy. Each dragon corresponds to a beast whose essence he had absorbed—the silver Eight-Armed Malignant Dragon, the dark green Serpent-Elephant Dragon, the jade-like Sword Dragon, and the golden Lion King Dragon. The dragon roars cause a catastrophic avalanche that buries the dozens of greedy cultivators who had rushed up the mountain to loot his dragon eggs, while Xiao Chen remains unharmed on the summit. The three skeletons and Keke observe with curiosity, and Xiao Chen looks down with cold indifference.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of enlightenment (顿悟) is central to xianxia cultivation. Unlike gradual improvement through effort, a sudden insight can break through bottlenecks because it aligns the cultivator's mind with the fundamental principles of the universe. Xiao Chen's realization about the cycle of prosperity and decline (生机、鼎盛、衰败) echoes the Daoist concept of yin-yang alternation and the natural rhythm of all things. The manifestation of dragon shadows from acupoints is a direct result of his unique cultivation method—acupoint divinization. Each dragon shadow represents the life essence of a powerful beast he has absorbed, now awakened as a totemic projection. Avalanches in xianxia often serve as a natural consequence of human greed, a force that punishes without moral deliberation. The crowd's attempts to justify theft with 'shared ownership' reflect a common trope of mob entitlement that ultimately brings self-destruction.

## Reading Guide

Xiao Chen's breakthrough to Mortal-Shedding Sixth Heaven marks a significant power escalation, but his real enemies—Yan Qingcheng, Kailuo, Wang Tong, and their powerful alliances—have not yet committed to battle. The avalanche merely thinned the ranks of opportunistic looters. The dragon manifestation puzzles even Xiao Chen himself, hinting that his acupoint path may have deeper, unexplored consequences. How will the surviving alliance leaders react to this display? Will they become more cautious or more desperate? The chapter also establishes a new philosophical calm in Xiao Chen, suggesting he may approach future conflicts with greater strategic patience rather than raw aggression. The three skeletons and Keke remain loyal companions, but their increasing sentience and curiosity about his power may foreshadow future developments in their own cultivation.
