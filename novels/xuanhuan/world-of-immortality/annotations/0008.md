## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a critical transition from Xiao Chen's solitary survival into a tense social chess match. Two native cultivators of the World of Immortality, Prince Wind and Liu Yue, wash ashore after a disastrous encounter with the Eight-Armed Malignant Dragon. On the surface, they are grateful survivors seeking aid; beneath, they are cold-blooded predators planning to murder Xiao Chen to keep their arrival a secret. Xiao Chen, ever vigilant, sees through their deception via lip-reading, yet plays the part of the obliging host. The mood shifts from the relative calm of lakeside cooking to a taut psychological standoff where every smile conceals a blade. This chapter deepens the novel's atmosphere of calculated danger, showing that in the World of Immortality, even hospitality is a battlefield.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiao Chen encounters Prince Wind and Liu Yue as they emerge from the sea, both clearly shaken by their encounter with the Eight-Armed Malignant Dragon but quickly regaining composure through their cultivation.
- Prince Wind and Liu Yue immediately assess Xiao Chen as a potential threat and agree in hushed tones that he must die—only a corpse can keep their presence on the island secret. They decide to use him temporarily for labor.
- Xiao Chen, from a concealed vantage point, reads their lips and confirms their murderous intent. He makes a split-second decision to feign ignorance and continue his role as a helpful castaway.
- During a meal prepared by Xiao Chen, Prince Wind and Liu Yue reveal their lineage: they are disciples of the legendary Evil King Shi Zhixuan, a name that carries weight even in the World of Immortality.
- Xiao Chen internally registers the shock of the Evil King's reputation but maintains a calm exterior, recognizing the immediate danger while filing away the lore for later consideration.
- The chapter closes with the three characters locked in a fragile equilibrium, each aware of the others' duplicity but unwilling to force a confrontation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- The Evil King Shi Zhixuan is a crossover figure from Huang Yi's wuxia novel "The Twin Swords of the Dragon" (大唐双龙传). In "World of Immortality," he is recontextualized as a historical mortal who achieved Void-Breaking and ascended to this higher plane. His legend as a master of the Unkillable Demon Art (不死魔功) makes his disciples naturally feared.
- The act of lip-reading in cultivation novels often appears as a specialized skill demonstrating a character's heightened perception and strategic thinking. Here, it underscores Xiao Chen's battle-hardened caution—he does not trust even temporary allies.
- The Forbidden Sea surrounding Dragon Island is a staple trope in xianxia: a treacherous boundary that filters out the weak, making survival on the island itself a proof of ability. Prince Wind and Liu Yue's arrival by ship (rather than Void-Breaking) marks them as less formidable than Xiao Chen, but still dangerous.
- The term "邪" (evil/heretical) in the Evil King's title carries nuances of unorthodoxy rather than pure evil, aligning with the Chinese literary tradition of morally ambiguous anti-heroes.

## Reading Guide

- Watch for the power dynamic reversal: Xiao Chen knows they plan to kill him, but they do not know he knows. This asymmetry sets up a classic ambush scenario where the prey is actually the hunter. The coming chapters will likely test Xiao Chen's ability to strike first or escape their net.
- Prince Wind and Liu Yue represent the first direct connection to the wider cultivation society of the World of Immortality. Their allegiance to the Evil King's lineage hints at larger factional conflicts that Xiao Chen may soon be drawn into.
- Pay attention to the subtle cues in their dialogue: Prince Wind's commanding tone and Liu Yue's seductive deflection are both weapons. How Xiao Chen interacts with them will reveal whether he can maintain his mask of innocence while calculating his next move.
- The mention of the Evil King also serves as a lore seed: if his disciples are this ruthless, what kind of being is the master himself? This thread may pay off much later.
