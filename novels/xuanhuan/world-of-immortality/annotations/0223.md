## Chapter Overview
The stage expands to the highest echelon of power in this chapter, as ancient beings and demigods clash over the relics of the Ancestral God Youchao. Tongtian, the Lord of the Four Swords, demolishes the long-standing palace with reckless abandon, exposing two crude artifacts that send shockwaves through the assembled cultivators. Xiao Chen, a spectator near the ground, senses an inexplicable pull from the ruins, drawing him into a destiny far larger than his individual vendettas. The tone is epic and mythic, blending cosmic violence with the serene philosophy of Zhuangzi, who drifts through the carnage as a butterfly.

## Key Plot Points
Tongtian's four divine swords—Slay Immortal, Kill Immortal, Extinguish Immortal, Trap Immortal—strike the ancestral palace, pulverizing it into rubble. The swords then seek out a simple stone dagger and a stone ball, prompting immediate intervention. The Seven Treasures Tree, Kong Xuan's five-colored divine light, and a mysterious butterfly (revealed as Zhuangzi's spiritual form) each challenge the swords. Zhuangzi passes through two swords with ease, embodying his philosophy of detachment. A giant hand from the heavens, belonging to Allah of the Western tribes, seizes the stone ball. Tongtian, the Seven Treasures Tree, and Allah engage in a chaotic battle that shatters space itself. Martial Saint Sun Wu arrives on a heavenly steed, demanding one of the two artifacts for the Eastern lineage, leading to a four-way confrontation that degenerates into primordial chaos. Meanwhile, lesser relics fall from the palace foundations, triggering a bloody free-for-all among the observing cultivators. Xiao Chen's stone drill reacts to a hidden fragment, guiding him to a broken arm buried in the debris—a clue to a greater mystery.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter weaves together figures from multiple mythological systems: Tongtian from Fengshen Yanyi, Zhuangzi from Daoist philosophy, Sun Wu from Chinese military history, and Allah from Islamic tradition. Zhuangzi's butterfly form is a direct reference to his most famous fable about the boundary between dream and reality. The Four Swords of Zhuxian are legendary artifacts said to require four sages to wield them; here Tongtian controls all four, showcasing his supreme power. The crude stone artifacts of Youchao represent a xianxia trope where the most powerful treasures appear ordinary, avoiding detection by divine sense. The battle encapsulating the fusion of Eastern and Western pantheons is a hallmark of this novel's ambitious world-building.

## Reading Guide
Xiao Chen's discovery of the broken arm within the ruins signals a personal stake in the Ancestral God's legacy. This chapter raises the power ceiling dramatically, emphasizing that the current conflict is but a prelude. Readers should note the significance of the stone artifacts—they may hold the key to Xiao Chen's unique cultivation path. The alliance and enmity among the demigods set the stage for future allegiances. Xiao Chen's stone drill, now agitated, hints at an awakening of his own hidden treasures. The next chapter will likely explore the arm's origin and how it connects to the larger conspiracy surrounding the Ancestral Gods.
