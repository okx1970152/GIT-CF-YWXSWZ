## Chapter Overview

Thirty thousand years have passed since the great war, an expanse of time that has ground most of Xiao Chen's contemporaries to dust. In this desolate span, he searches for Heavenly Stele marks to strengthen his path, but what he finds instead are the shattered remnants of old acquaintances. The chapter opens with a jarring reunion: Dugu Jianmo, once a proud swordsman, has become a mindless killing machine driven by the concentrated grudges of the Curse Realm's Ten Thousand Man Pit. Xiao Chen is forced to seal him beneath a Heavenly Stele. He then visits Dragon Island, where ancient survivors like the tree-man and the undying king offer quiet support. But the most shocking discovery lies beneath the earth: an underground laboratory from a lost civilization, where the Otherworld experiments on a captured Heavenly Stele, and where Zhao Lin'er and the little celestial horse are held in grotesque captivity. The tone is one of tragic weariness, punctuated by horror and a faint glimmer of resolve.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen's search for Heavenly Stele marks leads him first to the Curse Realm, where he senses Dugu Jianmo's sword intent. Jianmo emerges as a feral, blood-soaked figure, barely coherent. He begs Xiao Chen to seal him with the seventh Heavenly Stele to keep from losing himself completely. Xiao Chen complies, lowering the stele into the abyss. On Dragon Island, he absorbs a Heavenly Stele mark from a buried stele, then finds that the stele in the Tianzang Valley has been taken by an unknown collector—a troubling sign that someone else is gathering the steles. His investigation takes him deep into the earth beneath the Nine Provinces, where he discovers an ancient underground city converted into a laboratory. There, locked in chains, is Zhao Lin'er, her face half-destroyed, and the pure white celestial horse, both used as experimental subjects. The laboratory is dissecting a Heavenly Stele with mysterious instruments, hinting at a civilization that treated divine artifacts as mere material. Xiao Chen frees them but is left with more questions than answers.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The 'Ten Thousand Man Pit' (千万人坑) is a classic xianxia trope: a site of mass slaughter so concentrated that the grudges and soul remnants form a palpable, corrupting force. Dugu Jianmo's attempt to cultivate there represents a suicidal path—risking his soul to gain power for revenge. The 'Heavenly Stele mark' (天痕) is the imprint left on a cultivator when they bond with a stele; each mark strengthens the bearer's connection to the steles' laws. Xiao Chen now collects these marks to eventually command the full power of the steles. The underground laboratory introduces a rare sci-fi element: an ancient civilization that dissected a Heavenly Stele, treating it as a research object. This suggests the Otherworld's technological sophistication and their total disregard for the sacred. The juxtaposition of high-tech instruments with xianxia artifacts deepens the world's mystery.

## Reading Guide

This chapter plants several seeds for future arcs. Who is the mysterious collector of Heavenly Steles, and why are they gathering them? The underground laboratory implies that the Otherworld (or a faction within it) has a deeper understanding of the steles' structure—possibly enough to replicate or destroy them. Zhao Lin'er's tragic state may soften Xiao Chen's old enmity, but her survival could also reintroduce old complications. Dugu Jianmo's sealing sets up a future battle where he may emerge as a powerful, unstable ally. The little celestial horse's rescue restores a bond from Xiao Chen's early days on Dragon Island. Readers should also note the quiet pledge of the tree-man and the undying king to join Xiao Chen's war—a reminder that not all from the old era have fallen.
