## Chapter Overview

As Xiao Chen and Keke venture deeper into Dragon Island, the narrative tone shifts from cautious exploration to awe and dread. This chapter presents a series of encounters with primordial beasts — a colossal bamboo viper, a fire-plumed mountain-sized bird, and an iron-shelled golden centipede — each more terrifying than the last. The emotional core lies in the contrast between Keke's innocent excitement at approaching her homeland and Xiao Chen's growing realization that her birthplace may be a den of monstrosities. The pacing is tense, with each new beast raising the stakes, while Keke's fearless behavior deepens her mystery. This is a classic discovery chapter, threading danger with revelation.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Xiao Chen and the skeletons fleeing from an immense green snake — a bamboo viper grown beyond all natural measure. The serpent, thick as a house, uses its tongue to snatch an elephant and swallows it whole, a sight that shocks Xiao Chen. He circumvents the snake's territory, but Keke, far from frightened, points excitedly ahead, signaling that home is near. They next witness a colossal fire-red bird, its wingspan rivaling that of a Holy Light Dragon, sweeping over the forest. In a shadowy swamp, a golden centipede ten meters long, with a metallic carapace that can shatter trees and rocks, lurks. Xiao Chen notes that the presence of such ancient beasts confirms his suspicion: Keke's origin may be a primal breeding ground. The three skeletons follow silently, their curiosity piqued. This sequence of encounters escalates the threat level and foreshadows the true nature of Keke's lineage.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

In Chinese mythology and xianxia, the concept of "primordial" (上古) denotes a time when the world was wild and filled with divine beasts. The bamboo viper (竹叶青) is typically a small, deadly snake; its gigantic form here implies it has cultivated for aeons, accumulating spiritual energy and power. The trope of "snake swallowing elephant" (蛇吞象) is a direct reference to a Chinese idiom about greed and the impossible, but here it is literal, reinforcing the super-reality of the World of Immortality. The golden centipede and fire bird similarly follow the pattern of common creatures elevated to mythic status. Keke's complete fearlessness before these apex predators is a strong hint that she belongs to a higher class of being — perhaps a divine beast or a child of the Cosmic Tree itself, a theme common in xianxia when a cute companion is later revealed to be incredibly powerful.

## Reading Guide

The steady escalation of monster encounters in this chapter serves as a warning: Keke's homeland is not a safe haven but a place where the island's most ancient and terrible powers gather. Readers should note the descriptions of the centipede and bird — they are not random threats but likely guardians or inhabitants of the sacred region. Xiao Chen's decision to avoid direct confrontation shows his growing wisdom, but the question remains: how will he deal with a nest of such creatures? The next chapter likely brings them to Keke's birthplace, where the Cosmic Tree stump and possibly the truth of her eggshells await. Also, the skeletons' continued silence and interest may be significant — do they sense something related to the Death Realm? This chapter builds anticipation for a major lore exposition.
