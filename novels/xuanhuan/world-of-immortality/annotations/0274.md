## Chapter Overview
In this monumental chapter, the battle for survival reaches its zenith as a second otherworldly Ancestral God threatens to cross into the Eternal Continent. The half-ancestors, having just destroyed the remaining invaders, face an impossible choice. To seal the spatial fault permanently, they must use the Twenty-Four War Swords, and the only suitable vessel is Xiao Chen—the man who slew them in the Paradise Lost. What follows is a harrowing act of sacrifice: Xiao Chen is impaled by all twenty-four swords and transformed into a stone giant, sealing the rift for generations. This chapter is a study in tragic heroism, hidden history, and the bitter weight of salvation.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the detection of a second otherworldly Ancestral God attempting to breach the Eternal Continent through a spatial fault in the Ancient God Wasteland. The half-ancestors redirect a meteor storm to disrupt his crossing, buying precious time. Meanwhile, they quickly annihilate the remaining otherworld half-ancestors and seal their spiritual imprints. The real crisis is the imminent arrival of another Ancestral God; Shennong is already battling the first one in the void, but there is no guarantee of victory. The half-ancestors realize that only the Twenty-Four War Swords can seal the spatial fault permanently, and they need a physical vessel to anchor them. Xiao Chen, who carries sixteen War Swords within his body and possesses the Ancestral God relics, is chosen. Despite Keke's desperate pleas and attacks, the half-ancestors—including Original, Tongtian, Sun Holy God, and others—act without mercy. They impale Xiao Chen with all twenty-four swords, turning him into a colossal stone giant that fills the spatial fault. Keke weeps and tries to fight, but cannot match their speed. The seal holds. Afterwards, Shennong defeats the otherworld Ancestral God, and the half-ancestors return to glory, their deeds celebrated across the continent. Xiao Chen's sacrifice remains unknown; he is remembered only as a slayer of half-ancestors. The chapter closes on a desolate image: Keke alone, curled against the stone statue, while the world celebrates its deliverance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Twenty-Four War Swords are legendary weapons from another world, first introduced during the Paradise Lost arc. They are tied to the Heavenly Stele and have the ability to suppress even Ancestral God-level power. In this chapter, the swords are used as a sealing mechanism, a common trope in Chinese fantasy where powerful artifacts become keystones of reality. The transformation of Xiao Chen into a stone giant mirrors the fate of many mythical figures who petrify to uphold cosmic order—like Pangu or the Jade Pillar. The spatial fault is a dimensional rift that connects the Eternal Continent to the Otherworld, a typical xianxia concept. The half-ancestors' decision to sacrifice one individual for the greater good reflects the harsh utilitarianism often seen in these stories, where personal morality is secondary to survival. Notably, the narrative criticizes this through Keke's grief and the bitter irony of forgotten heroism.

## Reading Guide
The sealing of Xiao Chen sets the stage for two major threads. First, Keke now has a deep hatred for the half-ancestors who sacrificed her friend; her possession of the Paradise Lost may prove crucial for future vengeance or rescue. Second, Xiao Chen's fate is ambiguous: is he dead, or merely petrified? The stone giant may one day break free, especially given his unorthodox cultivation path. Readers should watch for shifts in Keke's character—from playful to vengeful. Additionally, the half-ancestors' glorified status may be challenged if the truth of Xiao Chen's sacrifice ever comes out. The sealed spatial fault is temporary; otherworld forces will eventually try again. Finally, note that Zhuangzi and the Martial Saint Sun Wu showed concern for Xiao Chen—they may become allies in any future rescue attempt.
