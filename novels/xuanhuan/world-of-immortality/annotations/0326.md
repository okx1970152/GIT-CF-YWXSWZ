## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers one of the most intense and brutal one-versus-many battles in the entire arc. Xiao Chen faces the Six Prodigies of the Cultivation World in the skies above the ancient capital Chang'an. The chapter is a showcase of raw combat power, tactical cunning, and the unforgiving nature of the World of Immortality's martial path. The tone is explosive and unyielding, blending mythic-scale technique displays with cold, personal vendetta. The stakes are absolute: Xiao Chen intends to slaughter every one of his enemies, and he backs up his words with actions that leave the onlookers in awe.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the Six Prodigies—Ye Tian, Shaoyi, Xia Ding, and others—surrounding Xiao Chen, each seeking personal glory. Ye Tian attacks first with the Indestructible Imperial Heavenly Bell, a sacred artifact that produces devastating sound waves. Xiao Chen counters with the Origin Heavenly Sound character 'Om', imprinting it onto the bell and shattering the artifact. Ye Tian uses a 'Nine Deaths Rebirth' technique to reform the bell repeatedly, but Xiao Chen systematically destroys it each time, taunting that he will "kill you a hundred times." Then Shaoyi enters with a legendary bow, firing nine unstoppable blood arrows. Xiao Chen creates eighty-one spatial apertures to trap the arrows, neutralizes them, and charges forward. He severs Shaoyi's arm, tears him apart, and annihilates his nascent soul. The remaining five try to intervene but are blocked by Keke using Paradise Lost. Xiao Chen declares he will kill them all, singling out Ye Tian for immediate execution. Xia Ding challenges him to single combat, but Xiao Chen dismisses them all.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter draws heavily on Chinese mythological combat tropes. The 'Indestructible Imperial Heavenly Bell' echoes the legendary 'Heavenly Bell' artifacts of Chinese lore, often associated with divine emperors. The 'Nine Deaths Rebirth' technique parallels the concept of 'eight-nine arcane art' or similar immortality practices in xianxia. Shaoyi's bow is highly reminiscent of Hou Yi's legendary bow from the myth of shooting down nine suns—its arrows are described as 'blood rainbows' that track their target unerringly. Xiao Chen's 'Origin Heavenly Sound' is a variation of the foundational 'Eight Syllables' taught by his cultivation method, manifesting spoken words as tangible power. The setting of Chang'an as an ancient capital adds historical weight, as it was a center of Chinese civilization. The crowd of martial artists, princes, and commoners watching from below underscores the public nature of this duel, a common trope in wuxia and xianxia literature where reputation and face are paramount.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for a multi-front war between Xiao Chen and the elite of the Cultivation World. With Shaoyi killed and Ye Tian humiliated, the remaining four prodigies are now blood rivals who will stop at nothing to avenge their comrade or restore their own honor. Readers should note the exceptional performance of Keke, who singlehandedly neutralized five top experts with Paradise Lost—her power is clearly growing. The chapter also hints that Xia Ding may be the most dangerous of the group, as he alone seeks single combat after witnessing Xiao Chen's massacre. The final exchange between Xiao Chen and Ye Tian is a psychological blow, shaking Ye Tian's confidence and foreshadowing his demise. This battle is only the beginning of a longer, more brutal campaign that will test Xiao Chen's limits and force him to rely on every technique he has mastered.
