## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges into apocalyptic chaos as the long-suppressed dragons of Dragon Island recover their ancient divine powers and unleash devastation upon the bone sea. The appearance of a sinister dead city and the rising of skeletal armies force Xiao Chen and his companions into a desperate gamble: entering the very heart of death itself. The chapter balances mythic awe with visceral horror, as legendary dragons—Tyrant, Lion King, Eight-Armed Evil, Sword—soar and blast the land, while the Buddha's Dharma Wheel offers a glimmer of sacred light against the encroaching darkness. Xiao Chen's decision to lead his group into the dead city is a turning point, marking his willingness to risk everything for survival.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with Liu Mu's reflection on Time Spiritists, hinting at deeper power dynamics. The bone sea, already ominous, becomes active as skeletons rise and colossal beast bones tremble. A team of cultivators, driven by greed, attempts to scale the dead city's wall to seize the Buddha's Dharma Wheel. They are horrifically killed—first a man turns to bone mid-fall, then the black wall devours the rest—before the Dharma Wheel emits Buddha light that calms the undead. However, a fearsome roar from the city's depths reanimates the skeletons and summons dragons from the distant forest. Over thirty dragons, including apex species, appear in the sky, now wielding their full divine might. They begin bombarding the bone sea, obliterating both skeletons and cultivators. Xiao Chen realizes the dragons are herding them toward the city. With no retreat, he leads Liu Mu, Yizhen, and reluctantly Liu Ruyan (dragging the captive Yan Qingcheng) toward the city gate, where the three skeleton kings have already entered and are facing the ghostly guards.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The chapter introduces the concept of Time Spiritists (时间灵士), a rare and supreme class of cultivator who control the flow of time—a power described as "divine law" (神则) that can trump even a whole realm of difference. This expands the world's magic system beyond spatial manipulation. The Buddha's Dharma Wheel is a potent Buddhist relic, a symbol of enlightenment and salvation, but here it is embedded in a deathly city, suggesting a layered history of conflict between sacred and profane forces. The resurrection of dragons' divine powers breaks the long-standing seal on Dragon Island, indicating that the island's ancient suppression is weakening or being actively challenged. The dead city itself, called the "City of the Dead" or "Underworld Capital," evokes the Chinese mythological concept of Fengdu, the ghost capital, where souls are judged—a place of ultimate peril.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for a major arc shift. The dragons' restored abilities and the emergence of the dead city will likely dominate the next several chapters. Pay attention to the three skeleton kings—their independent actions and mysterious gestures suggest they have knowledge about the city that Xiao Chen lacks. The Buddha's Dharma Wheel may be a key to survival or a lure. Yan Qingcheng's captivity is precarious; her presence adds tension as Liu Ruyan's motives remain unclear. Xiao Chen's decision to enter the city is a bold but necessary move; readers should watch for how he navigates the dangers within, and whether the city holds allies or greater horrors. The mention of Time Spiritists foreshadows future encounters with cultivators who can manipulate time itself.
