## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers a devastating revelation: the gentle Ruoshui is dead, her body stolen by a being of immense power. Xiao Chen, already burdened, confronts the truth that the Green Lotus Heavenly Maiden is a half-ancestor who has been hunting for a perfect human vessel—and she has set her sights on Qingqing. The old man who guided Xiao Chen reveals himself as the legendary Taiwu, a sage of the mortal world, and the two engage in a battle that cracks the sky. The scene shifts from a serene lotus lake to a raging battle over the sea, as four ancient War Swords clash with the Haotian Tower. Xiao Chen is left helpless, witnessing a conflict between beings who view mortals as ants.

## Key Plot Points

The old man, previously mysterious, is revealed as Taiwu, a legendary figure from the mortal world who wields the Haotian Tower—a sacred artifact that once suppressed half-ancestors. He confronts the Green Lotus Heavenly Maiden, who has been masquerading as a celestial maiden. She admits she killed Ruoshui and possesses her body, and she now intends to take Qingqing’s body because it is an even more perfect vessel. The Green Lotus Heavenly Maiden is not a human cultivator but a primordial green lotus that chose to never transform its original body into human form, instead refining its lotus body into a supreme treasure (the Twelve-Grade Lotus Platform) while seeking a human host for its spirit. This path is taboo and reveals her ruthless pragmatism. Taiwu, bound by his new master-disciple relationship with Qingqing, fights to protect her. The battle escalates as both unleash half-ancestor-level power: Taiwu raises mountains from the sea to use as weapons, while the Green Lotus Heavenly Maiden wields four War Swords—part of the legendary set of twenty-four—and her defensive Twelve-Grade Lotus Platform. The conflict tears through dimensional barriers, revealing the outside world. Xiao Chen learns that Taiwu is a unique practitioner—a Qi-Refiner who bridges mortal cultivation and immortal arts—and that the War Swords are tied to cosmic secrets.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Half-ancestors are beings just below the level of Ancestral Gods, possessing dominion over the laws of heaven and earth. The Green Lotus Heavenly Maiden's path is unusual: most cultivators shed their original form to gain a human body, but she kept her lotus form and refined it into an artifact, then used a mortal’s body as a vessel for her spirit. This reflects a perversion of the 'ascension' ideal. The Haotian Tower is a famed artifact from Chinese mythology, said to be one of the first treasures of heaven; its appearance here signals the old man’s ancient heritage. The War Swords are revealed to be connected to the lost twenty-four swords of another world, and the concept of 'Da Yan' (great extension) is used to speculate that there are forty-nine War Swords—one short of the fifty that would complete a primordial cosmos. Taiwu’s fighting style, using natural elements as weapons, aligns with the ancient Qi-Refiner tradition, which predates modern cultivation systems.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up a major plot point: the War Swords are not just powerful weapons but are linked to the ultimate secrets of the cosmos. The Green Lotus Heavenly Maiden’s ambition to collect them suggests there is a grand design at play. Xiao Chen’s grief over Ruoshui deepens his hatred toward those who treat mortals as disposable. The battle’s outcome will determine Qingqing’s fate and Xiao Chen’s next move. Pay attention to Taiwu’s fighting philosophy—it echoes Xiao Chen’s own martial path. The appearance of four War Swords here is a reminder that Xiao Chen possesses sixteen such swords, making him a target. The conflict also highlights the burgeoning chaos as ancient powers stir.
