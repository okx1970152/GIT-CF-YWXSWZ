## Chapter Overview

Chapter 129 offers a rare moment of levity amid the grim desolation of Dragon Island, as Keke's mischievous treasure-hunting instincts lead to a windfall of both material riches and forbidden knowledge. Xiao Chen, initially exasperated by the little beast's theft of the Undying Sect's sacred scripture and a string of precious fire cloud fruits, soon discovers that Keke's find was not a theft from the sect but a rediscovery of a hidden cave. The chapter balances lighthearted banter between Xiao Chen and Keke with the weighty revelation of a legendary cultivation manual. It also plants a seed of subterfuge, as a servant is seen poisoning Xiao Chen's breakfast – a reminder that the politics of the island never rest. The emotional tone oscillates between warmth and wariness, reflecting the dual nature of the World of Immortality where even a moment of comfort can harbor hidden danger.

## Key Plot Points

Keke returns to Xiao Chen's dwelling with the Undying Sect's sacred scripture and a string of fire cloud fruits, visibly proud of her find. Xiao Chen scolds her, fearing she has provoked the powerful sect. However, Keke leads him to a remote stone mountain cave, where the items were hidden in an abandoned ancient dwelling – not stolen from active guards. Inside, Xiao Chen finds more fire cloud fruits growing on the walls and the little defiant dragon relaxing in a hot spring, having gorged on the fruits. Realizing his mistake, Xiao Chen apologizes and examines the sacred scripture. The text reveals two core techniques: the Divine-Demon Undying Seal, which maximizes power efficiency, and the Undying Heavenly Wings, which grant flight at the Mortal-Shedding Fifth Heaven. Xiao Chen commits the entire scripture to memory, intending to integrate its essence into his own cultivation. Later that night, he witnesses a servant surreptitiously poisoning his breakfast ingredients. He tests the substance on a wild rabbit, which becomes crazed with lust, confirming a plot to drug him. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen aware of the threat but choosing to remain silent, likely planning a countermove.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Undying Demon Art (不死魔功) is a legendary cultivation technique created by the Evil King (邪王) of the mortal world. It is infamous for its heretical nature and the danger it poses to readers – merely studying it can cause spiritual collapse. Its two signature techniques, the Divine-Demon Undying Seal (神魔不灭印) and the Undying Heavenly Wings (不死天翼), represent peak martial ingenuity. The Seal allows a practitioner to achieve maximum destructive effect with minimal energy expenditure, enabling a weaker cultivator to defeat a stronger one. The Heavenly Wings are a coveted ability for flight, typically only possible at much higher realms. Fire Cloud Fruits (火云果) are spirit fruits that require thirty years to mature and provide a substantial boost to vitality and energy. The chapter also highlights the precarious social environment on Dragon Island, where even a temporary residence can harbor hidden assassination attempts, reflecting the constant survival pressure.

## Reading Guide

Xiao Chen's decision to memorize the Undying scripture sets the stage for a major upgrade to his combat capabilities. Readers should watch for how he might incorporate the Divine-Demon Undying Seal and the Undying Heavenly Wings into his existing Heavenly Stele Profound Art. The poisoning plot introduced at the end is clearly a precursor to conflict with Qin Yi or other local power players – Xiao Chen's restraint suggests he will not act immediately but will wait for the right moment to expose the scheme. Additionally, Keke's treasure-finding instincts are becoming a recurring and invaluable asset; her ability to sniff out hidden resources will likely play a key role in Xiao Chen's preparation to leave Dragon Island. The little defiant dragon's presence in the cave hints at its growing bond with Xiao Chen. Finally, the fact that the Undying Sect's scripture was hidden in a cave rather than in their active compound suggests deeper mysteries about the sect's history and perhaps the fate of its original practitioners.
