## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a spectacle of transformation and rebirth. The Little Defiant Dragon, once an ugly, overlooked creature, undergoes a metamorphosis through the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree and earthly dragon energy, emerging as a breathtaking white jade Dragon King with dragon horns and tail. The scene is both sacred and surreal—cocoons, seven-colored light, and Keke’s covetousness. Yet the chapter does not linger in wonder; it swiftly pivots to urban intrigue as Xiao Chen marches toward the White Deer Academy, hunting the Rigen scoundrel who dared to attack his companions. The shift from mythic to militant maintains the novel’s signature balance between awe and ruthlessness.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter begins with the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree merging with the Little Defiant Dragon, forming a luminous cocoon that attracts the essence of a dragon vein beneath Heavenly Emperor City. Over a day and night, the cocoon hardens and cracks, revealing a transformed dragon: now pure white jade, with a dragon head, dragon tail, and two horns—unequivocally a Dragon King. Its power surges to the equivalent of Mortal-Shedding Ninth Heaven. Keke, initially tempted to taste the cocoon, is awed. Xiao Chen calculates that the Little Defiant Dragon now rivals, if not surpasses, the eleven young dragon kings of Dragon Island. After the transformation, the Little Defiant Dragon demonstrates a new ability: it can reshape its body into a white jade celestial horse, a useful disguise. Xiao Chen’s wrath, cooled by the dragon’s success, does not extinguish; he sets out for the White Deer Academy in search of the Rigen family’s Ka’osi, who assaulted them. At the academy gates, he is mistaken for a hired thug and directed to the Warm Spring Paradise, where he encounters acquaintances and a new lead. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen wading into the city’s underbelly, vengeance on his mind.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of dragon veins (龙脉) is drawn from Chinese geomancy (feng shui), where the earth’s energy flows in patterns akin to a dragon’s body. Such veins are prized for their ability to nurture imperial destiny or enhance cultivation. Here, the Sacred Tree’s capacity to attract this energy reveals a hidden utility that makes it even more invaluable. The Little Defiant Dragon’s evolution from a form resembling a black-brown puppy to a magnificent white jade dragon is a classic “ugly duckling” motif, common in Chinese mythology to signify the awakening of true potential. Its dragon head and tail align it with the archetypal Ancestral Dragon, the supreme dragon sovereign. The ability to shape-shift into a celestial horse adds a layer of cunning, highlighting that this dragon is not just brute power but also intelligence.

## Reading Guide
This chapter resolves the immediate crisis of the Little Defiant Dragon’s injuries, but it sets the stage for multiple future conflicts. Xiao Chen is now hunting a member of the Rigen family—a direct provocation against one of Heavenly Emperor City’s major noble houses. The prince and the Mand family are also still in play, and the upcoming beast-fighting tournament looms. The Little Defiant Dragon’s new power will be tested in real combat soon. Pay attention to Keke’s growing reactions to the dragon; her own awakening may be foreshadowed. Also note the mention of the escaped white turtle—a seemingly minor detail that often signals a future plot thread. The transformation scene also hints at the deeper mysteries of the Sacred Tree and its connection to dragon origins. Everything is converging toward the tournament and the inevitable clash with the city’s power brokers.
