## Chapter Overview

This chapter blends a timely mortal-world auction with cosmic mystery, as Xiao Chen acquires a third Heavenly Mark from a small stele that had been relegated to a mere curio. Using his visionary arts and the combined power of the ancient scroll and sacred tree, he traces the stele's origin to a legendary ancient castle that has haunted multiple realms. The chapter also delivers darkly comedic relief through Sha Polang's absurd encounters with mortal youths and women, reminding us that even a death-world king can be humbled by everyday life.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen identifies an unassuming stone tablet at a mortal auction as a genuine Heavenly Stele. He substitutes it with a replica and successfully extracts a third Heavenly Mark, bringing him closer to understanding the stone-man path. Using the ‘Po Wang Yan’ (Breaking Illusion Eye), the ancient scroll from the Five Stone Kings, and the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree, he traces the stele's history upstream through time. The vision reveals the stele once resided within a mysterious ancient castle that had appeared over Dragon Island, the Heavenly Burial Valley, the Demon Plain, and the Yellow River. The castle eventually shattered and fell to earth, burying itself deep beneath the present-day mortal village. Xiao Chen resolves to excavate the site to uncover the castle's secrets and its connection to the Heavenly Steles. Meanwhile, Sha Polang's earlier humiliations at the hands of a reckless youth and a mercenary woman are resolved with a characteristic blend of cruelty and humor as he exiles them to remote corners of space.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Heavenly Marks (天痕) are unique imprints left by the Heavenly Steles on a cultivator's soul, acting as keys to deeper mysteries—Xiao Chen now holds three. The 'prophetic eye' technique (破妄之眼) is a staple of xianxia lore, allowing a cultivator to peer into the past by spending great spiritual energy. The recurring appearance of a demonic castle near every major stele site strongly suggests a hidden connective thread among all the Heavenly Steles, possibly part of a larger formation or legacy left by an ancient power. The old goat's earlier antics and Sha Polang's subplot serve to ground the cosmic narrative with mortal humor, a signature tonal shift in the novel.

## Reading Guide

This chapter deepens the overarching mystery of the Heavenly Steles and introduces a buried installation that may hold key answers—likely the same castle seen at Dragon Island. The comedy with Sha Polang reminds readers that even among epic battles, the mundane world can humble cultivators. Xiao Chen's determination to dig hints at a major excavation arc that may reveal more about the stone-man path and the nature of the ancient castle. Pay attention to the castle's appearances across different realms; it may be a mobile nexus or a prison. The upcoming excavation promises danger and discovery.
