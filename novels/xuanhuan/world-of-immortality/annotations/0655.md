## Chapter Overview

This chapter presents a striking fusion of cutting-edge technology and primordial myth, as Xiao Chen observes an Otherworld Ancestral God attempting to decode the mysteries of a Heavenly Stele using advanced scientific instruments. The sterile, metallic atmosphere of the laboratory contrasts sharply with the stele's ancient, living power, which pulses with life fluctuations and a drop of blood bearing human consciousness. When the probing nearly triggers a catastrophic explosion, Xiao Chen intervenes, capturing the researcher and learning that the Heavenly Mark—the stele's essential imprint—has already been stolen by an Otherworld expert. The discovery of a purple jade bracelet from his ancestral village then unleashes an Ancestral Dragon soul, transforming the Yellow River into a stone dragon and summoning three living Ancestral Dragons to the mortal world. The chapter balances clinical precision with mythic grandeur, advancing both the scientific and the legendary threads of the narrative.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen infiltrates a hidden underground laboratory where a silver-haired woman—an Otherworld Ancestral God—uses a battery of complex instruments to scan the Eighth Heavenly Stele. The machines detect life fluctuations, a blood droplet containing human consciousness, and enormous latent energy, but every attempt at deeper analysis triggers violent explosions, forcing the researcher to isolate the stele with spatial portals. After observing the repeated failures and the woman's sacrifice of her own essence blood to empower the scanners, Xiao Chen acts, capturing her in a single move. She reveals that an Otherworld genius has already extracted the Heavenly Mark from this stele, a dire development that forces Xiao Chen to plan a incursion into the Otherworld to recover it. Searching the compound, he finds a purple jade bracelet from his childhood village, which he once used to open world gates. The bracelet shatters in his hand, releasing an Ancestral Dragon soul that plunges into the Yellow River, instantly petrifying the entire river into a miles-long stone dragon. The shockwaves are felt across the land, and three fully grown Ancestral Dragons appear, flying upstream toward the ancestral village. Zhao Lin'er, present with her celestial horse, refuses to leave the underground civilization, determined to resurrect its technology. Xiao Chen also encounters the remains of the guardian Tong Hu, whose skull crumbles to dust after a final recognition.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Heavenly Steles are not mere stone monuments but living entities that contain Dao imprints and Heavenly Marks—sacred patterns that embody the principles of the universe. The attempt to analyze one with mortal technology underscores the inherent limitations of science when confronting the ineffable laws of cultivation. The purple jade bracelet, originating from the Ancestral Dragon Village and linked to the Nine Provinces seal, acts as a key between worlds; its destruction here signals a permanent change in the world's barrier. The transformation of the Yellow River into a stone dragon echoes the ancient curse of the heaven‑defying family and suggests that the dragon bloodline is stirring. The appearance of three simultaneous Ancestral Dragons is unprecedented in recorded history, indicating that the cosmic balance is shifting. The fleeting presence of a human consciousness within the stele hints at a deeper connection between the Heavenly Steles and the primordial ancestors.

## Reading Guide

Pay close attention to the stolen Heavenly Mark—this development introduces a rival who has mastered the same Heavenly Stele Profound Art as Xiao Chen, setting up a future confrontation with profound implications. The awakening of the Ancestral Dragons and the petrification of the Yellow River are not isolated events; they foreshadow a larger revival of the dragon race, which may intersect with the Inverse Dragon King’s destiny. Zhao Lin'er's decision to remain in the underground civilization and rebuild its technology could have far‑reaching consequences, potentially resurrecting super‑weapons from a lost age. Xiao Chen’s loss of the purple bracelet closes one avenue of world‑travel but opens another through the dragon path. The burial of Tong Hu marks the end of yet another ancient guardian, underscoring the theme that the old order is passing away, and a new era—fraught with both danger and possibility—is dawning.
