## Chapter Overview

This chapter delves into the tragic depths of Ge Qian, the 'Human Demon,' as he exacts a devastating revenge upon the Otherworld, crippling nine Stone Man King-level experts by burning the Ancestral Dragon essence they had stolen. His past as the Tenth Son of the Ancestral Dragon, raised on the blood and flesh of his nine slain brothers, is laid bare, imbuing his wrath with a profound, mournful weight. The narrative then shifts to Zhou Wenwang's dire prophecy: a being beyond the laws of this world has appeared, seeking to collect the Heavenly Steles. Compelled by this foreboding, Xiao Chen, bearing the Five Emperor Tower, ventures into the heart of the Otherworld, setting the stage for a confrontation that could determine the fate of all realms.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the aftermath of Ge Qian's devastating strike: five ancient demon cities shattered, nine elite powerhouses crippled, their foundations severed by the mysterious power of the Ancient Lamp. This act, revealed through Lu Zhan's shocked recollection, was long-planned: Ge Qian, the Tenth Son of the Ancestral Dragon, was raised on the flesh and blood of his nine brothers, their essence condensed into nine dragon pearls that were distributed to key Otherworld cultivators. By invoking the Ancestral Dragon's authority through the lamp, Ge Qian burned their stolen power, crippling them at their very moment of ascension. Lu Zhan, enraged, vows to hunt down Ge Qian, but a mysterious ancient lamp shields the Human Demon from detection. On the Yellow River, Ge Qian, melancholic and weary, speaks with Xiao Chen, revealing his identity and his inability to find peace. He confesses that he killed his own brothers, but Xiao Chen understands the manipulation behind it. Ge Qian then departs for the Celestial Realm, intending to fight Stone Man Kings and draw the Otherworld's attention. Soon after, Zhou Wenwang arrives, his divination uncovering a being 'not of this world'—a great disaster born from the mysterious extraction of a Heavenly Mark. He urges Xiao Chen to infiltrate the Otherworld, reclaim the Heavenly Mark, and recall the Heavenly Stele to prevent an unimaginable cataclysm. The Old Stone Turtle and Shi Zhongdi give Xiao Chen the Five Emperor Tower to mask his presence. Xiao Chen crosses into the enemy realm, immediately sensing the oppressive, foreboding aura of the disaster.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter enriches the mythology of the Ancestral Dragon and the Ancient Lamp. The lamp, once a simple artifact from the depths of the Death World, is revealed to be a vessel for the Ancestral Dragon's residual will, capable of transcending spatial barriers to strike at those who bear the dragon's stolen essence. The 'dragon pearls' (long zhu) are a staple of xianxia—condensed life-force of a dragon, often used for cultivation. Here, they serve as a curse in disguise, linking the wielder to Ge Qian's revenge. Zhou Wenwang's appearance recalls the I Ching, the Book of Changes, a foundational text of Chinese philosophy. His 'divination' is not mere fortune-telling but a profound ability to glimpse the threads of fate, a skill that comes with immense cost, as shown by his bloody coughing. The Five Emperor Tower, a legendary stone artifact, is used here to conceal a cultivator's presence from supreme beings, highlighting the necessity of stealth in Tier higher realms.

## Reading Guide

As Xiao Chen steps into the Otherworld, readers should pay close attention to the nature of the 'great disaster.' Zhou Wenwang's prophecy is rarely wrong, and the mention of a being 'not of this world' hints at an entity from beyond the known cosmos—perhaps something related to the sealed being within the Stone Gate or even an Otherworld Ancestral God of unprecedented power. Xiao Chen's mission to reclaim the Heavenly Mark is fraught with peril, as he carries the Five Emperor Tower to mask his presence but cannot rely on it indefinitely. Ge Qian's fate remains unresolved; his attack has crippled the Otherworld but also drawn the attention of the three Supreme Ancestral Gods. The tension between his personal revenge and the larger cosmic threat will likely escalate. Additionally, the Old Stone Turtle and Shi Zhongdi's refusal to go themselves suggests that the danger may be core to the Heavenly Stele's nature—only someone like Xiao Chen, who has bonded with multiple steles, can confront it.
