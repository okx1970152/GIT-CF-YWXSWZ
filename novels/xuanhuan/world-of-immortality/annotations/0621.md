## Chapter Overview
The chapter opens in the sprawling, wall-less city of Han Yue, a hub of exotic races and commerce in the Celestial Realm. The atmosphere is a vivid tapestry of barter and brilliance, from Phoenix eggs to immortal herbs. Amidst this, Sha Polang's absurd quest for black dog meat provides coarse humor, resulting in a swift and humiliating beating from a dog-headed vendor. This comic interlude serves as a foil to the chapter's true weight: an encounter with a mystifying old Daoist who claims descent from the legendary Little Stone Emperor. Through this weathered figure, the narrative unfurls tangible clues about the ninth Heavenly Stele—a relic long shrouded in myth. The tone shifts seamlessly from farce to revelation, grounding the fantastical lore in a memorable, character-driven scene.

## Key Plot Points
Following Keke's trail, Xiao Chen, the Little Defiant Dragon, and Sha Polang arrive at Han Yue City. The market teems with diverse beings—long centipedes, snow demons, bat-like gods—and vendors cry out their wares. Sha Polang's comedic misadventure sets the chapter's initial mood. Later, they come upon an old Daoist beneath a banner proclaiming mastery over fate. Though locals dismiss him as a charlatan, the Daoist speaks with unsettling precision. He identifies the stone brick in Xiao Chen's sleeve—a fragment of a Heavenly Stele—and claims it once struck down his ancestor, the Little Stone Emperor. To prove his lineage, he demonstrates hand seals that Xiao Chen recognizes as belonging to the Heavenly Stele's esoteric techniques, yet distinct from the six steles he has mastered. Xiao Chen refuses to sell the fragment but learns the critical location: the ninth Heavenly Stele may lie hidden in the Little Stone Emperor's tomb. This revelation transforms the group's objective and deepens the mystery of the steles. The chapter concludes with Xiao Chen's resolve to investigate further, now aware that a legendary king once wielded knowledge of the steles.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Little Stone Emperor (小石皇) is a legendary human king of a bygone era, whose name evokes the 'Stone Man' path of cultivation—a route where flesh and blood are petrified into crystalline immortality. The Heavenly Steles (天碑) are primordial monoliths inscribed with the universe's deepest laws. Xiao Chen has mastered six, and a seventh is known in the Death World; the existence of a ninth has been a persistent enigma. The old Daoist's hand seals reflect a variant of the 'Heavenly Stele Profound Art' (天碑玄功), suggesting the Little Stone Emperor indeed encountered a stele and integrated its teachings. Han Yue City's design—curved like a crescent moon, without defensive walls—mirrors the Celestial Realm's reliance on supernatural barriers rather than mortal architecture. The marketplace's barter economy, trading rare treasures like immortal cornelian fruits and divine herbs, illustrates the advanced resource economy of the upper realm, where immortals haggle over items that would shake lower worlds.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a masterclass in tonal contrast, blending coarse comedy with profound revelation. Pay close attention to the old Daoist's hand seals: they are not a bluff, as Xiao Chen verifies their connection to the Heavenly Steles. This confirms that the Little Stone Emperor was more than a conqueror—he was a student of the primordial monoliths. His tomb, therefore, is not just a relic of past glory but a vault potentially holding the ninth Stele. Keep an eye on the Celestial Realm's market dynamics; future exchanges here may yield crucial resources or allies. Sha Polang's humiliation, while humorous, also underscores his impulsive character, a recurring source of both comedy and tension. The chapter subtly advances the greater quest: the gathering of Heavenly Marks and the search for the missing steles. As Xiao Chen now possesses a lead on the ninth Stele, the stage is set for a perilous expedition to the Little Stone Emperor's hidden tomb.
