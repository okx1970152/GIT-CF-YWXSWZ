## Chapter Overview

The Burial Weapon Valley, a place of ancient dread, holds a silent horror: the half skull of the legendary Stone Man, resting above a bottomless abyss. This chapter plunges Xiao Chen and the God of Slaughter Bai Qi into a standoff among sentient divine artifacts and a being whose origins stretch back to the previous cycle of civilization. The tone is one of mounting dread—every character, from the Brass Eight Trigram to the Martial Imprint, treads carefully around the skull, sensing a power beyond even half-ancestors. The mysterious woman, a reincarnation of a fallen Ancestral God, coldly demands the Stone Man's secrets, using lethal force to coerce compliance. Here, the novel's mythic dark fantasy reaches a claustrophobic peak: ancient gods, corpse-like stone relics, and a well that may swallow even Ancestral Gods.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the party confronting the half skull—vast as a house—and the cramped darkness beneath it. Bai Qi suffers crushing injuries but stands. The Martial Imprint and the mysterious woman debate the skull's origin, revealing the Stone Man was split into nine parts by the Ancestral Gods Suiren, Youchao, and Fuxi. The woman, wielding a war sword and a yellow dharma treasure, forces the issue: she hurls the Golden Divine Halberd and Dark-Iron Seal into the abyss, then turns on Xiao Chen and Bai Qi. Xiao Chen chooses to jump into the well rather than surrender his war swords, but his half skull (small, palm-sized) absorbs the Brass Eight Trigram's attack and protects him. The Martial Imprint follows, evading capture. All land in a vast underground realm, a new world of shadows and pale lights. Xiao Chen now holds a key fragment—the small stone skull—that may be crucial to navigating the horrors ahead. The artifact spirits are awed and resentful; the Martial Imprint laments that they have drawn closer to the "most sinister place."

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Stone Man (石人) is a central cosmological artifact in the later arcs of *World of Immortality*. It is said to be a being older than recorded time, shattered into nine parts and hidden by the primordial Ancestral Gods. Each fragment—here a half skull—is inert yet possesses a strange immunity to divine sense. The Ancestral Well (祖井) beneath it is feared as a death-trap even for Ancestral Gods, linking to the deepest layers of the Death World. The mysterious woman, as a reincarnated Ancestral God, commands power comparable to a true Ancestral God, though she is now a weapon spirit. Her use of yin-yang fish (阴阳鱼) as a subduing technique echoes the foundational Daoist symbol of balance. The sentient artifacts (Golden Divine Halberd, Dark-Iron Seal, Brass Eight Trigram) are "ancestral weapon spirits"—souls born from divine weapons, each with their own egos and histories. Their bickering humanizes them, but their power is real. Xiao Chen's mastery of the Eight Aspect Worlds and his strategic decisiveness (choosing to jump rather than be despoiled) mark his growth as a practitioner.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up a major exploration arc in the subterranean realm beneath the Ancestral Well. Xiao Chen's possession of the half skull fragment may grant him unique protection or guidance in the dangerous depths. The Martial Imprint, despite his fears, is now a reluctant ally. Readers should pay attention to the dynamics between the various artifact spirits—their loyalties may shift. The mysterious woman remains a threat, likely waiting above the well for survivors. The coming chapters will test Xiao Chen's resourcefulness in an environment where even half-ancestors fear to tread. Also, note the recurring theme of the Stone Man: its assembly may be key to facing the grand threats of the era. The emotional tone of the chapter is bleak but filled with grim determination—a hallmark of the novel's tragic aesthetic.
