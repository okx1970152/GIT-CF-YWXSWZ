## Chapter Overview

In this chapter, Xiao Chen reunites with old friends from his early days in the World of Immortality, only to learn that five king-level assassins have been dispatched by the Yaksha half-ancestor to end his life. Instead of meeting force with force, Xiao Chen takes a strategic detour into the death world to recruit two eccentric but supremely powerful allies: the seven-colored skeleton king Lian Po and the legendary Living Buddha Ji Gong, a skeletal being who wields the bizarre Great Prophecy Art. The chapter culminates in a shocking and humorous display of power when Ji Gong turns the first Raksha king into a pig, sending the humiliated king fleeing. This sequence establishes that Xiao Chen's alliance now includes beings of absurd, rule-breaking power, signaling that the coming battles will be unconventional.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with a lighthearted reunion as Jin Sanyi, Liu Mu, Niu Ren, Yuwen Feng, and Huo Niao, who have been in seclusion for over a decade, locate Xiao Chen through Liu Mu's spatial tracking. They share a meal and discuss the urgent threat: the Yaksha half-ancestor has sent two Yaksha kings, plus a Raksha king and a Skeleton king, with a second Raksha king on the way—five king-level experts targeting Xiao Chen. Xiao Chen refuses to involve the Pure Land elders or the Phoenix clan, choosing instead to visit Empress Zhao Lin'er. She offers a devastating weapon but also confirms the scale of the threat. Xiao Chen then journeys to the death world, where he recruits the ancient skeleton general Lian Po, who in turn brings him to a ruined temple housing the skeletal Living Buddha Ji Gong. Ji Gong, a master of the "Closed-mouth Zen" that evolved into the Great Prophecy Art, can make his words come true. When the group returns to the mortal world and meets the first Raksha king, Ji Gong laughs and points, declaring "Pig!"—instantly transforming the king into a pig. The utterly humiliated king flees, his dignity shattered. This decisive, comedic setback leaves four remaining kings to contend with, while Xiao Chen's strategy becomes clearer: he will rely on unpredictable, overwhelming allies rather than direct combat.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Ji Gong (济公) is a well-known figure in Chinese folklore, a Buddhist monk with supernatural powers who often appears drunk and eccentric. In this novel, he is reimagined as a skeleton that has cultivated the "Closed-mouth Zen" (闭口禅), a practice of silence that when broken, channels immense power into spoken words—an ability called the Great Prophecy Art. This art allows him to literally speak reality into being, transforming a Raksha king into a pig with a single word. The Raksha and Yaksha are both semi-divine races in Buddhist mythology, often depicted as fierce demons. Their kings (皇) are at the Perfected Man realm, equivalent to the death world's "Emperor" (皇) level. The death world's hierarchy of skeleton kings, colored by their bone quality (purple, yellow, seven-colored), corresponds to cultivation realms like Perfected Man and Earth-Penetrating. The chapter also highlights the power hierarchy: king-level experts are formidable, but a half-ancestor is far above. Xiao Chen's strategy relies on gathering eccentric but powerful allies rather than direct confrontation.

## Reading Guide

The chapter introduces two important allies: Lian Po, a legendary general reincarnated as a seven-colored skeleton, and Ji Gong, the Living Buddha with reality-altering speech. Their eccentricities and overwhelming power promise to make the upcoming conflicts unpredictable and entertaining. The first of the five assassin kings has been neutralized without a fight, but four more remain, including two Yaksha kings and a Skeleton king. The comedy of Ji Gong's antics should not distract from the gravity of the situation—a half-ancestor still hunts Xiao Chen. Readers should watch how Xiao Chen balances his new allies' absurd methods with his own martial resolve. The chapter also hints at deeper mysteries surrounding the death world and the connection between ancient masters from the Nine Provinces and the continent's depths. The Great Prophecy Art, seemingly invincible against king-level foes, may have limitations, and the Raksha half-ancestor's reaction will determine the next phase of conflict.
