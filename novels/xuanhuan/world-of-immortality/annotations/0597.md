## Chapter Overview
After narrowly escaping the dangerous stone-man puppet, Xiao Chen and his companions retreat to Yan Capital, where a shocking discovery awaits. The Ancestral Dragon skin map, plucked from the depths of the ruined palace, reveals far more than mere geography. On its surface, eight and even a faint ninth Heavenly Stele appear, alongside the Yellow Clay Altar, the Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge, the ninety-nine stone steps, ancient lamps, and the great palace of the Chaos Land. These legendary artifacts, scattered across different worlds, now seem to be connected, forming a single, cryptic passage leading toward an unknown destination. The tone of this chapter is one of profound awe and creeping realization—Xiao Chen and his allies sense they have stumbled upon a secret that transcends eras. The sheer scale of the map suggests a plan that involved the ultimate powers of multiple civilizations, and the implications shake even the most seasoned among them. This is not merely a treasure map; it is a schematic of a path that gods may have traveled.

## Key Plot Points
Inside the third palace hall, the group discovers a seemingly broken stone jar. When the Purple Diamond Skeleton Sha Polang carelessly lifts it to his ear, a deafening, painful blast of energy nearly disorients him. Xiao Chen immediately activates his "Void-Breaking Eye" and sees the jar's true form: a blood-and-black‑earth vessel sealed by dense talismanic script. The jar trembles with contained power, and Xiao Chen recognizes it as a prison for an entity of terrifying strength. Using his own power to reinforce the seal, he safely stores the jar within the Primordial Demon City—not daring to keep it in his acupoint space. Moments later, the stone‑man puppet that guards the palace awakens, its battle domain sealing the entire hall. Xiao Chen unleashes the Primordial Demon City, which expands and swallows the puppet with surprising ease. Yet when he checks the city, the puppet is gone—only to appear as a relief image on the surface of the stone jar, bound by the same chain‑like patterns that seal the jar. This reveals that the jar is the key to controlling the puppet. After stowing the entire central palace ruins into the demon city (which undergoes a noticeable evolution), the group returns to their hotel to study the Ancestral Dragon skin map. Under careful examination, they see not only the seven known Heavenly Steles but also the eighth and a shadowy ninth. More striking, the map links the steles with the Yellow Clay Altar, the Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge, the ninety-nine stone steps, ancient lamps, the Chaos Land palace, and even a stone key (longer than the one Xiao Chen possesses). The artifacts seem to be arranged like a trail of signposts or stations on a single, epic road leading to a mysterious ultimate place. The group realizes they have uncovered a record of a grand design—possibly a path left by powers that spanned across worlds and ages.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Ancestral Dragon skin map is a profound artifact in itself: made from the hide of an Ancestral Dragon, it records not just locations but the relationships between legendary relics. In traditional Chinese mythology, maps of this kind often appear in legends as guides to forbidden secrets. The Heavenly Steles (天碑) are central to the cultivation system of this novel—each stele contains ancient methods, and assembling them grants access to supreme power. The map now suggests that the steles are not isolated objects but part of a larger network. The other artifacts—the Yellow Clay Altar (黄泥台), the Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge (通天死桥), the ninety‑nine stone steps (九十九重石台阶)—each have their own mythological weight. For instance, the bridge and steps evoke the idea of a celestial ascent that only the most powerful can attempt. The map’s depiction of these items as stations on a single path implies a grand, perhaps cosmic, purpose that links the major realms of the novel: the Mortal World, the World of Immortality, the Death World, and beyond. The mention of a stone key (石钥匙) longer than the one Xiao Chen possesses hints that the map itself may provide the means to unlock a final gate. This chapter reinforces the xianxia trope of a hidden, world‑spanning legacy left by ancient beings.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up a major narrative arc: the mystery of the Ancestral Dragon skin map and what its connected artifacts reveal. Readers should pay close attention to the map’s details, as they foreshadow Xiao Chen’s future journey—likely toward the location where all these artifacts converge. The sealed stone jar is another ticking bomb; its contents could be either a deadly enemy or a potential ally. The fact that the stone‑man puppet is now bound to the jar offers Xiao Chen a new, formidable weapon, but controlling it without unleashing the sealed entity will be a challenge. The chapter ends on a note of hushed revelation—the map is “madness” and “unimaginable”—which primes the reader for the next phase: Xiao Chen must decide how to use this knowledge. With the Nine Provinces under siege and the Celestial Realm looming, such a grand map may be the key to turning the tide. The coming chapters are likely to explore the origins of this map and the path it charts, potentially bringing Xiao Chen face‑to‑face with the ultimate truths of his world.
