## Chapter Overview

After the apocalyptic night in Heavenly Emperor City—where Keke was killed and the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree fused into his body—Xiao Chen collapses into a coma, drifting into a mystical realm known as the Pure Land. This chapter opens with him awakening in a sea of flowers, disoriented and grief‑stricken. He is found by a pure‑hearted girl named Qingqing, who brings him to a hidden village of reclusive elders. Here, Xiao Chen begins to physically recover, though his emotional wounds remain raw. The chapter balances the serene, almost sacred atmosphere of the Pure Land with the political machinations unfolding in the outside world—most notably the announcement of Hai Yunxue’s marriage to the Tiger Family. A reunion with the spirited Niu Ren and his now‑grown Black Dragon King offers a glimmer of hope.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen first wakes amid a flower sea but soon realizes it is a confusing illusion—the Lost Flower Sea. He uses the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree to break the hallucination and flies five hundred li before collapsing. Qingqing finds him, offers him a divine water, and guides him to her village. There he lies unconscious for nearly a month, his life essence severely drained; the Sacred Tree within him barely sustains his organs. Meanwhile, news spreads that Hai Yunxue will marry the Tiger Family’s seventh son—a political alliance that consolidates the Hai family’s influence. Xiao Chen finally wakes to see Niu Ren, who has grown stronger with a full‑fledged Black Dragon King and claims to have fused five ancient beast souls. Niu Ren offers to help Xiao Chen recover by seeking a supreme beast soul from the Sacred Mountain’s ancient altar. The chapter closes with Qingqing’s grandfather gently warning against careless talk about defying the mountain’s spirits.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Pure Land (净土) is a secluded realm where ancient cultivators retire from worldly strife, protected by the Lost Flower Sea—a natural illusion formation that disorients intruders. Losing life essence is a grave injury that reduces a cultivator’s lifespan and weakens their organs; the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree’s fusion provides a vital anchor. Niu Ren’s mention of “beast soul fusion” reflects a cultivation path where warriors absorb the spirits of primordial beasts to gain power, a common trope in xianxia. The Sacred Mountain altar is a legendary site where one can obtain ancient beast souls—essentially spiritual fragments of long‑dead gods or demons. The Hai‑Tiger marriage is a political maneuver, typical of noble families seeking to strengthen ties across distant regions.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a tonal shift from desperate violence to quiet introspection. Readers should pay attention to the Pure Land’s mysteries—the reclusive elders, the Lost Flower Sea, and the Sacred Mountain—all of which may hold keys to Xiao Chen’s recovery and power growth. Niu Ren’s reappearance and enhanced abilities suggest he will become a key ally. The political marriage between Hai Yunxue and the Tiger Family sets up future conflict, as both families bear grudges against Xiao Chen. His fragile state foreshadows a long road to recovery, but the seeds of a new cultivation path—using the Sacred Tree and potential beast souls—are planted.
