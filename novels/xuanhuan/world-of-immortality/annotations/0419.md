## Chapter Overview
The chapter opens with the devastating aftermath of the Ancestral God corpse poison, which has annihilated over a thousand elite cultivators and ten Perfected Man Heavenly Kings within the Heavenly Burial Valley. Only a handful of top experts survive, including Xiao Chen, Wu Ming, Earth King, Mabaoo, and the Yaksha Heavenly Queen. All are afflicted by the lethal miasma. Xiao Chen, thanks to his supreme martial body, is the first to purge the poison completely. He seizes this fleeting opportunity to hunt down his weakened enemies, setting off a deadly game of cat and mouse across the desolate mountains. The chapter combines visceral horror with tactical cunning, as Xiao Chen must navigate not only the physical threat of the poison but the political machinations of his rivals, all while a half-ancestor battle rages in the depths of the valley.

## Key Plot Points
The Ancestral God corpse poison proves to be a catastrophic equalizer, killing the vast majority of foreign kings and their followers. Xiao Chen, using his unparalleled martial body, purges the poison faster than any other, gaining a critical window of advantage. He immediately pursues Wu Ming, the cunning artifact-refining prodigy who wields the Undying Imperial Heaven Divine Bell and the Heavenly Thunder Hammer. Wu Ming, realizing he is at a disadvantage, attempts to draw Earth King into the conflict by fleeing toward him. However, Earth King, rather than assisting, attacks both indiscriminately. Xiao Chen and Wu Ming briefly cooperate to wound Earth King, then Wu Ming flees again, this time toward War King Mabaoo. Mabaoo, ever calculating, stays neutral, forcing Wu Ming to burn his life essence with a desperate Thunderlight Escape technique. Ye Jing, a female cultivator believed dead, ambushes Wu Ming, severely wounding him but allowing his escape. Xiao Chen then returns to kill the already-weak Earth King with a full combination of the Four Killing Hands. Sensing the Yaksha half-ancestor's fury after being ejected from the valley, Xiao Chen wisely retreats to the Death World.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter demonstrates the deadly nature of Ancestral God-level relics. The corpse poison, derived from a deceased Ancestral God, is so virulent that even half-ancestors can be killed by it. It symbolizes the lingering threat of primordial powers even in death. The concept of 'purifying miasma' is a common xianxia trope, where cultivators must expel external corruption to restore their purity of energy. Xiao Chen's martial body, which he developed by abandoning the path of weapon refinement, proves superior in resilience and recovery. This highlights the novel's theme that pure physical cultivation can surpass artifact-based power. The 'Thunderlight Escape' technique used by Wu Ming is a life-burning escape art, typical of high-stakes cultivation stories where characters sacrifice longevity for survival. Ye Jing's unexpected survival and ambush add a layer of intrigue, showing that the Heavenly Burial Valley's dangers are not the only threat.

## Reading Guide
The rapid chain of events after the poison's release sets the stage for a major power shift among the hundred races. Xiao Chen's decisive elimination of Earth King, and the grievous wounding of Wu Ming, will have ripple effects in the coming conflicts. However, his retreat to the Death World suggests that the fight is far from over. The Yaksha half-ancestor's rage will likely prompt retaliation. Readers should watch for how the surviving kings regroup and whether Wu Ming can recover his strength. Ye Jing's emergence as a wildcard could alter alliances. Additionally, the half-ancestor battle in the valley's depths remains unresolved, and its outcome may drastically affect the mortal world. The use of the Death World as a refuge hints at Xiao Chen's growing connection to that realm, which may become a critical asset in future confrontations.
