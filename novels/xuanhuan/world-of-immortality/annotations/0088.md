## Chapter Overview

In this chapter, the relentless assault of the dragon horde forces Xiao Chen and the surviving cultivators into the enigmatic Dead City—a place that radiates ancient menace and forbidden power. The city's dark glow offers temporary shelter from the draconic onslaught, but its streets are lined with ancient soldiers who stir but do not attack, and its houses conceal ravenous horrors that devour the unwary. Amidst the chaos, the monk Yizhen receives a divine blessing from a Buddha relic, entering a profound meditative state that elevates him to a higher spiritual plane. The chapter masterfully balances external threat with internal mystery, creating a claustrophobic atmosphere where every shadow might conceal death.

## Key Plot Points

The dragons, having driven most cultivators to the brink, turn their fury upon the Dead City itself. Xiao Chen is among the first to enter, accompanied by the three skeletons, Liu Mu, Liu Ruyan, and the captive Yan Qingcheng. Once inside, they witness hundreds of celestial soldiers and shadow soldiers performing ritualistic drills—ancient beings seemingly detached from the living world. A Buddha relic on the city wall erupts with divine light, enveloping Yizhen, who had been grieving his brother's death. The relic transforms him: he levitates within a giant Buddha image, his aura shifting from sorrow to serene transcendence. Meanwhile, the dragon attacks continue, partially blocked by the city's eerie black radiance. Desperate cultivators scatter into the streets and houses, seeking shelter. Screams echo from the houses, accompanied by crunching sounds of consumption—unseen entities feeding on the fallen. Xiao Chen and his group retreat from these death-traps. They later witness a duel between two powerful experts: a man shrouded in black mist wielding a spatial domain, and a woman encased in multicolored clouds who commands a slowing field. Their fight underscores that even in mortal danger, old feuds persist. The chapter ends with the group navigating deeper into the city, aware that the dragons are merely the first layer of threat.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Dead City (死城) is a classic xianxia trope—a forbidden zone that exists between life and death, often a remnant of an ancient civilization or a containment site for undead beings. The celestial soldiers (天兵) and shadow soldiers (阴兵) are common motifs from Chinese mythology: heavenly warriors and ghostly soldiers, here portrayed as both guardians and slaves of the city. The Buddha relic (佛陀法轮) is a powerful artifact from an enlightened being; in Buddhist cosmology, such relics can trigger spontaneous enlightenment or grant divine protection to those with affinity. Yizhen's transformation mirrors the ideal of sudden awakening (顿悟) in Chan Buddhism, where a practitioner transcends grief and attachment in a flash of insight. The dual-domain battle between the black mist man and the cloud woman introduces the concept of territorial control in high-level combat, an advanced technique beyond standard martial exchanges.

## Reading Guide

This chapter deepens the mystery of Dragon Island's central structure. The Dead City clearly holds secrets that even the dragons fear to fully provoke. Readers should watch for: what sleeps in the ancient houses, the identity of the two experts fighting, and whether Yizhen's newfound power will become a game-changer. The city's dark glow and its effect on the soldiers suggest a controlled malice—the city is not merely ruins but a living entity. Additionally, the dragon's behavior is suspicious: they are driving cultivators into the city deliberately, as if experimenting with the city's defenses. Future chapters will likely explore the city's core, the source of the Buddha relic, and the nature of the residents within those blood-red doors. Will Xiao Chen and his group find a way out, or will they become permanent residents of this tomb-like metropolis?
