## Chapter Overview
Xiao Chen and Liu Mu venture deeper into the forbidding Dead City, a realm of death and ancient power. This chapter unveils the city's core—a blood-red river crossed by stone bridges, leading to a central square dominated by a colossal divine stele. The stele, identical to the one in the Bone Sea, radiates an overwhelming spiritual pressure that forces weaker cultivators to their knees. Amidst the exploration, a sinister black cat with a necklace of skulls lurks, and the Three Skeleton Kings discover skulls bearing lotus imprints—signs of fallen undead royalty. The tension escalates when Yizhen, transformed by the Buddha's Dharma Wheel, appears and challenges a mysterious, sword-like figure named Dugu Jianmo, setting the stage for a clash of transcendent power.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with Xiao Chen and Liu Mu witnessing a duel between a man shrouded in black mist and a woman veiled in rainbow radiance, but the scene is a temporal echo from the past. Xiao Chen's vigilance is rewarded when he catches a nearly invisible flying dagger—a relic of the legendary Li Xunhuan's technique—launched by an unseen assailant. Liu Ruyan's playful banter briefly lightens the mood before the group presses onward. They cross the blood river, a river of actual blood that exudes a slaughterhouse stench, and enter the central square. There, the hundred-meter divine stele stands, surrounded by a lethal field that reduces a reckless cultivator to a pool of blood. The square's fence consists of black stone tablets engraved with ancient cultivation techniques, including lost martial arts like "God-Slaying Hand" and "Rebellious Disorder Three Styles." Liu Mu discovers spatial spiritist techniques among them. As the survivors study the engravings, Yizhen arrives, serene and transformed, and addresses the black-mist figure as Dugu Jianmo, declaring a karmic settlement. Dugu Jianmo emerges as a cold, sword-like man, hinting at a legendary lineage.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Dead City is reminiscent of the Chinese mythological "Underworld Capital" (阴都), a realm of the dead governed by the Ten Kings of Hell. The lotus imprints on the skulls connect to the earlier evolution of the Three Skeleton Kings, marking them as potential Undying Kings—entities that even gods fear. The divine stele here is a counterpart to the Yellow River Stele and the Dragon Island stele, suggesting a network of heaven-fallen monuments that seal or suppress worlds. The flying dagger technique is a direct reference to "Little Li Flying Dagger" from Gu Long's wuxia novel, transposed into this xianxia universe as a legendary martial art capable of killing gods. Dugu Jianmo's name evokes Dugu Qiubai, another wuxia legend of the solitary sword seeker, implying a lineage of supreme swordsmen. Yizhen's transformation via the Buddha's Dharma Wheel shows how the Buddhist concept of sudden enlightenment (顿悟) can manifest as a cultivation breakthrough in this world.

## Reading Guide
The impending duel between Yizhen and Dugu Jianmo is the chapter's central hook—what karma ties them, and how will Yizhen's Buddha-enhanced power fare against a legendary swordsman? The divine stele's secrets remain veiled; its connection to the other steles suggests a cosmic pattern Xiao Chen must decipher. The black cat and the skulls with lotus imprints hint that the dead city holds dangers beyond mere traps—perhaps an active undead royalty. Liu Mu's recovery and spatial insights could prove crucial. With the flying dagger attack, the hostility of successors like Wang Tong and Kailuo becomes palpable. Readers should watch how the power dynamics shift as more ancient forces awaken on Dragon Island.
