## Chapter Overview
This chapter unveils a daring theft at the heart of Dragon Island, where Xiao Chen and his undead companions exploit a moment of vulnerability to claim an ancient divine treasure. The narrative balances awe and cunning: the majestic Light Sacred Dragon is discovered in a state of worship before a radiant, palm-high sapling—the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree. Rather than confront the dragon, the three skeleton kings orchestrate a scheme that frames the sacred beast as the thief, showcasing their growing intelligence and shifting loyalty. The tone oscillates between reverence for the sacred tree’s mythic beauty and the grim, calculated humor of the skeletons’ pretense, deepening the dark-fantasy atmosphere of the island.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the three skeletons—Qin Guang, Yan Luo, and Wheel—boldly leading Xiao Chen into a stone forest where they discover the Light Sacred Dragon in an unexpected posture: it is not resting but kneeling in devout worship before a tiny, magnificent tree. The tree, only a handspan high, bears two leaves of black jade and white jade, and is encircled by a seven-colored halo. Recognizing its extraordinary nature, the skeletons immediately motion to dig it up. Xiao Chen hesitates, fearing damage, but eventually relents under their insistence. With careful excavation, he secures the sapling and flees back to the Death Swamp.

Once there, the skeletons reveal further familiarity with the tree, guiding Xiao Chen to attach its roots to a large tree trunk, where the roots miraculously burrow into the living wood. Simultaneously, cries of fury erupt from the direction of the valley—the Light Sacred Dragon has discovered its loss. In a display of cold cunning, the three skeletons instantly collapse among the bones, feigning death to avoid suspicion. Xiao Chen realizes they had planned this all along: the dragon was meant to be the scapegoat. This revelation underscores the skeletons’ growing sentience and strategic depth, transforming them from mere undead pets into calculative allies.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Seven-Colored Sacred Tree embodies a classic xianxia motif: the divine spiritual object that draws reverence from even the mightiest beasts. Its two leaves—one dark as ink-jade, one milky-white as mutton-fat jade—symbolize the interplay of yin and yang, death and life, which is central to many cultivation philosophies. The tree’s ability to parasitically root into a host tree without killing it hints at the concept of symbiosis in nature, a deeper metaphysical relationship often explored in Chinese mythological tales.

The Light Sacred Dragon, a demigod-level creature, kneeling in worship before the sapling reflects the hierarchy of spiritual treasures: even supreme beings are drawn to objects of primordial power. The skeletons’ feigned death and cunning manipulation echo strategies found in classical Chinese military texts like The Art of War, where deception is a cornerstone of survival. This chapter reinforces the idea that intelligence, not just strength, governs the World of Immortality.

## Reading Guide
This chapter marks a turning point in Xiao Chen’s relationship with his undead followers. The skeletons have moved from neutral beings to scheming allies, and their bond will likely deepen as they face the consequences of their theft. Keep an eye on the Light Sacred Dragon: its rage has been provoked, and it will not forget who stole its treasure. Meanwhile, the Sacred Sapling’s ability to absorb life essence from trees suggests it may become a key resource for cultivation—or a liability that attracts greater predators. The contrast between the serene Death Swamp and the dragon’s roaring fury sets the stage for a shifting power dynamic on the island. Readers should also note Xiao Chen’s growing wariness of his companions’ hidden agendas; trust in this world is never absolute.
