## Chapter Overview

Chapter 97 of *World of Immortality* delivers a pivotal revelation as Xiao Chen finally glimpses the long-sought continuation of the Heavenly Qi-Refining Diagram on the ancient divine stele. Amid the desperate flight through the City of the Dead, the Purple Dragon King leads the survivors to a mysterious sacrificial altar, where life and death hang in the balance. Meanwhile, the chapter takes an unexpectedly chaotic turn as Keke reappears, stirring up a hornet's nest of ancient horrors, including a headless golden-winged angel. The tone oscillates from mythic awe to tense action to almost comic chaos, making this a rich and multi-layered installment.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen's desperate observation of the divine stele yields a monumental breakthrough: he captures the continuation of his cultivation diagram and identifies four supreme techniques, which he names Slay God, Reverse Chaos, Suppress Demon, and Collapse. These techniques are the origin of lesser killing arts he encountered before, now revealed in their original, profound form. Simultaneously, the Purple Dragon King leads the surviving cultivators to a blood-soaked sacrificial altar, a potential escape route from the City of the Dead. However, as the dead spirits close in, Keke's sudden appearance turns the situation into a frenzy. The little beast, seemingly in a playful mood, dashes through ancient houses, awakening a headless golden-winged angel and other fierce spirits. The chaos she creates is both awe-inspiring and dangerous, as the ancient entities begin to break free from their confinement. Keke eventually leaps to the altar, reuniting with Xiao Chen, but the forces arrayed against them are now far more formidable.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter deepens the novel's cultivation mythology with the introduction of four ultimate techniques—Slay God, Reverse Chaos, Suppress Demon, and Collapse—which represent the source of many lesser killing arts. The description '大道至简' (the great Dao is simple) underscores a core xianxia philosophy: the highest truths are expressed in the simplest forms. The sacrificial altar, surrounded by blood rivers and carved with scenes of dragon sacrifice, evokes the ancient practice of blood sacrifice (血祭), a ritual from Chinese shamanic traditions. The headless golden-winged angel is a fusion of Western divine imagery with Chinese ghostly horror, suggesting that 'gods' can fall and become malevolent spirits. Such fallen deities are a recurring motif in Chen Dong's work, emphasizing the impermanence of power and the thin line between the sacred and the profane.

## Reading Guide

With the revelation of the four techniques, Xiao Chen now has a clear path for his cultivation, but can he master them in time? The sacrificial altar presents a tantalizing possibility of escape, but the gathering dead spirits, now reinforced by a headless angel, suggest a deadly price. Keke's presence adds a wild card—her fearless antics may have inadvertently triggered a crisis, or she might hold the key to survival. Readers should watch for the interplay between the ancient forces and the altar's purpose. The tension is building toward a climactic confrontation that will test Xiao Chen's newfound knowledge and the resilience of the alliance. Stay tuned for the next chapter's explosive resolution.
