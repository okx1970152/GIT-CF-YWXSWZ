## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a pivotal turning point in Xiao Chen's cultivation journey. After seizing the flesh and blood of an Otherworld Ancestral God, he embarks on a desperate holy sacrifice – a forbidden martial technique that burns the very essence of a divine corpse to elevate his own form. But raw power alone cannot breach the ultimate threshold. In a moment of profound clarity, Xiao Chen confronts the truth: he can never become an Ancestral God in the traditional sense. The weight of that title demands a reverence for the cosmos and a responsibility toward all life that he does not possess. Instead, he accepts a different destiny – that of a War Ancestor, a being whose incomparable physical might can shatter gods but who will never wear the crown of divinity. This is not defeat, but a conscious choice to walk a path of pure, unyielding combat. The chapter also weaves in the cunning creation of a sleeper agent within the Otherworld, and a return to the Death World's God Village where Keke awakens from a decades-long slumber, hungry and full of cryptic dreams.

## Key Plot Points

The holy sacrifice reaches its climax as Xiao Chen feeds the departing Ancestral God flesh into his own body through the forbidden ritual. For three days and nights, divine radiance pours into his frame, yet the threshold to Ancestral God remains impassable. In a flash of insight, he realizes that ultimate transcendence requires not just power, but understanding of the world's fundamental laws – a comprehension he cannot borrow. He accepts the path of the War Ancestor, focusing on physical transcendence alone. His body becomes a vessel of terrifying strength, each of the 365 main acupoints glowing like stars, with countless minor points awakening. The Ancestral God flesh is consumed, leaving a husk that still contains residual power. Xiao Chen then plants a second personality within that husk – the shattered remnants of the Otherworld god Chou Tian, cleansed of dangerous memories. This sleeper agent is meant to infiltrate the Otherworld's grand celebration. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen smashing a king's city with bare hands in the Death World, then returning to the God Village where Keke wakes after thirty-two years, dreaming of a family treasure in the Nine Provinces. Plans are laid to return to the mortal realm and rescue the captive cultivators.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of "holy sacrifice" (圣祭) in this chapter draws on a central paradox of Chinese cultivation philosophy: that enlightenment cannot be forced. The Ancestral God (祖神) is not merely a pinnacle of power, but a being who embodies the will and faith of a civilization. In contrast, the War Ancestor (战祖) is a martial counterpart – one who achieves comparable combat ability through pure physical refinement and combat will, without the civilizational burden. The human body as a microcosm of the universe is a classic Daoist theme: the 365 acupoints correspond to stars, and awakening them mirrors the creation of a personal cosmos. The technique of planting a second personality (第二人格) recalls ancient narratives of soul duplication and possession, here used as a spy tactic. The shattered remnants of the Otherworld god are reformed into a controlled avatar, a risky but brilliant strategy that echoes the 'Shatter Demon, Plant God' (碎魔种神) method seen earlier.

## Reading Guide

As Xiao Chen transitions into a War Ancestor, watch for how his new physical form interacts with the divine artifacts and techniques he wields – especially the incomplete Immortal-Slaying Formation diagram and the thirty War Swords. The sleeper agent Chou Tian sets the stage for a major infiltration arc during the Otherworld's once-in-ten-thousand-years celebration. Keke's family treasure, mentioned in her dream, hints at a deeper lineage and a possible power-up for the white beast. The planned rescue of the captured cultivators from various held islands will likely be the next major conflict. The chapter also deepens the philosophical undercurrent of the novel: that true strength comes from self-awareness and acceptance of one's limitations, not blind ambition. The mood is both triumphant and somber – Xiao Chen has grown immeasurably, but he has also accepted a permanent ceiling on his cultivation path.
