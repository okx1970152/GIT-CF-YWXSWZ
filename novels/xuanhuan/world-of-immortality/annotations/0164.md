## Chapter Overview

This chapter intertwines brutal combat among the young elites of the Eternal Continent with a deep philosophical debate on cultivation. Xiao Chen stands at the center of a dark, claustrophobic field formed by the collective pressure of top experts, exchanging fierce blows with adversaries like Dugu Jianmo and Yuwen Feng. After the skirmish, he engages Hai Yuntian in a tense discussion about using spirit medicines to break through to the Spirit-Treasury realm. The chapter culminates in the acquisition of a legendary ginseng king, linked to the mysterious white turtle—Little Thief—and Keke’s irrepressible greed. The emotional tone shifts from epic violence to intellectual intensity, then to warm, mischievous domesticity, preserving the mythic dark fantasy aesthetic.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with a darkness that robs sight and hearing—a ‘field’ generated by the conflicting wills and latent potential of several peak Mortal-Shedding Ninth Heaven practitioners. Within this field, Xiao Chen is assaulted in rapid succession: first Dugu Jianmo tests his strength with a mighty iron sword blow, then Yuwen Feng charges like a mountain, his fist meeting Xiao Chen’s in a thunderous clash. A spiritist’s ‘annihilation light’ is shattered by Xiao Chen’s blade-light, proving the equal caliber of all participants. The field collapses as swiftly as it formed, leaving Xiao Chen alone with the two little beasts, a faint trace of blood at his lips—promptly healed by his training body’s power. He muses grimly that his rivals are all at the brink of the Spirit-Treasury realm, and that the race to break through first is more intense than ever.

Hai Yuntian, visiting Xiao Chen, is aghast when he learns Xiao Chen plans to consume spiritual medicines as a shortcut. He warns of the ancient taboo—external power seals off one’s own potential. Xiao Chen defends his method with a river-and-dam metaphor: when the flood of accumulated power crashes through the first barrier, it sweeps away subsequent obstacles, creating a cascade of breakthroughs. Hai Yuntian calls it heresy, but Xiao Chen insists on walking a different path from his predecessors. Undeterred by opposition, he commissions Zhuge Liang to gather rare herbs. The merchant delivers a hoard of forest treasures, including a five-thousand-year-old ginseng king that cost five thousand gold coins—and was unearthed alongside the white turtle that is now Xiao Chen’s own ‘Little Thief’. Before Xiao Chen can examine it, Keke snatches the ginseng, takes a blissful bite, and the air fills with the scent of colored light.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

In xianxia tradition, the term ‘field’ (场域) describes a region where a cultivator’s internal energy and will manifests as an external force. When multiple peer-level warriors clash simultaneously, their undeveloped field instincts overlap, producing the disorienting darkness that blinds and deafens ordinary cultivators. This phenomenon underscores how the young elites are so powerful that their mere existence disrupts normal perception.

The debate over ‘external objects’ versus ‘self-training’ is a perennial theme in Chinese cultivation fiction. Hai Yuntian represents orthodox caution: relying on heavenly treasures to break through a major realm is considered a dead end because the external energy does not truly belong to the cultivator and will form a bottleneck that cannot be overcome. Xiao Chen’s counterargument—that if the internal river is strong enough, it will shatter any dam, even an external one—is unorthodox but reflects a philosophy of total self-absorption and syncretism. This aligns with his Dao of encompassing heaven and earth, not separating inside from outside.

The ginseng king’s connection to the white turtle (Little Thief) is significant: in Chinese herb-gathering lore, finding a white guardian creature like a turtle or snake beside an ancient ginseng proves the herb has reached a high spiritual grade. The fact that the white turtle is actually a baby dragon (as Xiao Chen knows) elevates the ginseng to an almost legendary status.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up several threads: Xiao Chen’s cultivation breakthrough attempt is imminent; the race to Spirit-Treasury among the young experts (Dugu Jianmo, Yuwen Feng, etc.) will soon produce a victor who will dominate the next stage of the story. The ginseng king’s power, now partly consumed by Keke, may have unexpected consequences. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen’s river-dam theory holds up in practice, and whether the alliance with Hai Yuntian deepens. The white turtle’s secret is also left tantalizingly open. The next few chapters may shift to the beast-fighting tournament or to Xiao Chen’s closed-doors cultivation.
