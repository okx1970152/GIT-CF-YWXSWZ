## Chapter Overview

After an absence spanning three entire civilization cycles—long enough for worlds to crumble and stars to fall—Xiao Chen returns, having reached a level comparable to a Stone King while preserving his living flesh and blood. This unique achievement sets him apart from the petrified sovereigns of old. His journey takes him first to the Eternal Continent, where he visits the submerged Ancestral Dragon Village and discovers a few precious survivors: the child of Bo Shi and Miao Chi (a little monk), along with Linglong and Tutu, still locked in ageless sleep. Then he steps into the Celestial Realm, entering the bustling trade city of Purple Wind City. There, under the glowing twilight of the Sunset Tower, he witnesses a tense confrontation between younger-generation descendants of old friends—the white-haired scion of Zhuge Pangzi, the golden-haired heir of Totti, and a fiery phoenix youth—and the arrogant progeny of his ancient enemies: Zixiao Wang, the Stone Corpse, and the Otherworld. Rather than intervening directly, Xiao Chen chooses to operate from the shadows, subtly taking control of the fat youth’s hand and turning him into an instrument of devastation. What follows is a cascade of slaughter that climbs the ranks of the enemy’s hierarchy, culminating in the shocking humiliation of Lang Ya, a true Stone King of the Otherworld, who is casually sent flying by a single palm. This chapter marks Xiao Chen’s official return as a hidden mover of events, signaling that the long age of waiting is over.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with a summary of Xiao Chen’s cultivation progress: after three civilization cycles—each the rise and fall of an entire era—he has forged a hybrid body that matches a Stone King in power yet remains warm flesh and blood. This achievement carries profound implications for the martial path, as it transcends the traditional stone-body limitation. He returns to the Eternal Continent and locates the now-floating Ancestral Dragon Village, using the Purple Jade Bracelet to access it. Inside, he finds a young monk hanging upside down in sleep—the son of Bo Shi and Miao Chi, wearing a long-life lock with Yi Zhen’s relic. Nearby, Linglong and Tutu are tangled together, unchanged after countless millennia. Xiao Chen searches their memories and learns that many of his old comrades—including Chu Xingkuang, Jue Dao, Zhao Zhongyang, and Snow Dance—were saved by Yan Huang and escaped the great purge. He leaves the three youngsters sleeping for their own safety, then departs for the Celestial Realm. In Purple Wind City’s Sunset Tower, a trade hub where even Stone Kings occasionally barter, he observes a confrontation: a white-haired fat youth (clearly a descendant of Zhuge Pangzi), a golden-haired lecherous youth (descendant of Totti), and a red-haired phoenix youth are being pressured by descendants of Zixiao Wang and the Stone Corpse, accompanied by an Otherworld woman. Xiao Chen decides to interfere by subtly seizing control of the fat youth’s right hand. One by one, the fat youth’s hand slaps down every enemy: first the immediate aggressors, then a middle-aged manager of the Sunset Tower, then a series of stronger adepts from Zixiao’s lineage, culminating in a semi-stone being who is crushed, and finally Lang Ya himself—a full Stone King—who is sent flying, his body cracking. The crowd is stunned; the fat youth is horrified, not understanding why his hand acts on its own. The chapter ends with Lang Ya struggling to reform his body, demanding to know who is hiding.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of a ‘civilization cycle’ (文明史) is a hallmark of high-level xianxia: it denotes not just a dynasty but the complete birth, maturity, and collapse of an entire cultivation world, often lasting hundreds of thousands or even millions of years. Xiao Chen’s three-cycle cultivation period emphasizes both his immense patience and the monumental power he now commands. The Stone King path traditionally leads to complete petrification—the body becomes an indestructible stone shell, sacrificing vitality for invulnerability. Xiao Chen’s retention of flesh and blood indicates a hybrid breakthrough, possibly combining the Stone Man and Upper Ancestral God paths, which the old stone turtle and Shi Zhongdi hinted at earlier. The Celestial Realm (天界) is the apex arena in the World of Immortality cosmology, where the most powerful beings from all planes gather. Purple Wind City’s Sunset Tower is described as a structure crystallized from evening glow, a typical xianxia marvel where architecture is forged from natural phenomena and divine power. The presence of descendants carrying the same techniques and mannerisms as their ancestors—the fat youth’s gluttonous cultivation art from the Zhuge family, the golden youth’s sun-based arts from Totti, the phoenix clan’s flame powers—shows how bloodlines and martial legacies persist across ages, even as individuals fade.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as a dramatic reintroduction of Xiao Chen after a long time-skip. Pay close attention to his method of intervention: he does not appear in person or announce himself; instead, he puppets a descendant of an old friend, creating a mystery for both the enemies and his own former companions. This sets up a classic ‘hidden hand’ narrative where the protagonist operates from the shadows, gathering intelligence and letting others wonder at the source of the power. The reappearance of the little monk, Linglong, and Tutu in the Ancestral Dragon Village reminds us that some characters were preserved outside the flow of time. Their awakening may become significant later. The conflict with Zixiao Wang’s lineage and the Otherworld foreshadows a larger war: Lang Ya’s humiliation will not go unanswered, and alliances between the Otherworld and local Celestial Realm powers like Zixiao Wang are forming. Xiao Chen’s unique flesh-and-blood Stone King body may be the key to surpassing the limitations of both paths. Watch for how he will use his disguise and the Five Emperor Tower (given earlier) to continue scheming. Finally, the mention of the Ancestral Dragon Village possibly being from the ‘Only True Realm’ deepens the mystery of that legendary place and ties it to the novel’s endgame.
