## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges deeper into the desolate world of the dead, where Xiao Chen exists as a blue diamond skeleton—a form that commands both power and dread. The central discovery is the life-source liquid, a miraculous substance that can refine the fire-seeds of undead beings, stripping away deathly miasma and leaving only pure, sacred essence. For the first time, Xiao Chen learns that creatures of this deathly realm may have a path toward true life, a revelation that ignites a fragile hope amidst the eternal gloom. The chapter balances tactical cunning with mythic horror, as Xiao Chen first shows mercy, then coldly eliminates a treacherous opponent, and finally begins mapping the dangerous territory ahead.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen, moving through the Plain of White Bones at night, encounters a human-faced eagle fire-seed creature in a bone-shaped temple. The creature is in agony, dripping a glowing green liquid into its own fire-seed, causing it to shrink and then become more refined. After a brief confrontation, Xiao Chen interrogates it and learns that the liquid is life-source liquid, extracted from living beings deeper in the continent. It can purify fire-seeds and even enable undead to become true living bodies. True to his word, Xiao Chen spares the creature and departs. However, the creature, now stronger after refining its fire-seed, hunts him down. Xiao Chen had foreseen this betrayal, set a trap by hiding his fire-seed in an ordinary skeleton, and ambushes the attacker, killing it and absorbing its purified fire-seed. This boosts his power by a noticeable margin. He then continues his exploration, discovering that the region is divided into territories ruled by three ancient castles, each housing an unimaginably powerful entity. He exercises caution, avoiding direct conflict, and concludes the chapter with a shocking sight: a living, blood-and-flesh human emerging from one of the castles.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces key mechanics of the death world. The 'fire-seed' (火种) is the essence of an undead being, akin to a soul—a flickering flame within the skull. Higher-grade fire-seeds allow evolution and greater power. The 'blue diamond skeleton' (蓝钻骨体) is a rare and supreme skeletal form, granting natural dominance over lesser undead. The life-source liquid (生命源液) is reminiscent of alchemical elixirs in xianxia, but with a unique twist: it refines the yin/death energy out of the fire-seed, leaving only pure yang/life essence. This process is painful and dangerous but can lead to true rebirth—a concept that echoes the Daoist pursuit of transcending death. The three ancient castles (古堡) are akin to forbidden zones, guarded by beings of near-ancestral power, setting a new tier of threat. The appearance of a living human hints that the world of the dead is not purely dead; there may be hidden sanctuaries where life persists.

## Reading Guide

The chapter sets up a long-term goal: the search for true life in the death world. The three castles are clearly major obstacles or potential sources of knowledge. The living human from the castle raises many questions: Is it a native? A prisoner? A guide? Xiao Chen's strategic patience here is crucial—he is learning to navigate a world with its own hierarchies and politics. Note the refinement process: the creature's fire-seed became stronger after being compressed, much like Xiao Chen's own cultivation method. The life-source liquid may become a key resource, but obtaining it requires confronting living beings or powerful undead. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen balances his desire for growth with the need to avoid drawing the attention of the castle masters. The scene of the living human is a classic narrative hook, promising a shift in the arc's direction.
