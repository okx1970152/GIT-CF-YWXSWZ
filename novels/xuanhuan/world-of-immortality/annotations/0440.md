## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges the reader into a perilous expedition through a dark palace in the Death World, where Xiao Chen, Tianwaitian, Renwairen, and Yingxiong confront a series of undying horrors. The atmosphere is thick with dread and desolation, echoing the tragic mythic tone of the novel. Yet, amidst the darkness, a breathtaking garden of sacred Yin-Yang trees emerges, offering not only respite but legendary treasures. The chapter masterfully balances visceral horror with the awe of discovery, advancing both the protagonists' cultivation and the overarching mystery of the oracle fragments.

## Key Plot Points

The group penetrates deeper into the ancient palace, encountering a grotesque rotting corpse that moves with unnatural speed and attacks without any detectable energy. A death knight on a nether steed follows, wielding a deathly spear that can wound even half-ancestors. An undead spirit casts a deadly soul curse that nearly overwhelms them. Crucially, these adversaries cannot be permanently destroyed; they simply fade, leaving behind only foul traces. The tide turns when the group discovers that both the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree and the bone oracle fragments can dispel the undead curses. After surviving multiple ambushes, they emerge into a radiant, self-contained garden, untouched by the deathly miasma. There, three magnificent Yin-Yang trees stand, bearing fruits that swirl with black and white energies—products of fifty-thousand-year-old yin wood that has evolved into a higher state. Tianwaitian, in a rare moment of nostalgia, recalls stealing such fruits from Shennong himself. On a stone table beneath one tree lies another oracle fragment, hinting at a previous visitor who studied it and left it behind.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Yin-Yang tree (阴阳树) represents a transformative evolution from the Purple Diamond Yin Wood (紫钻阴木) after an epoch of growth. Its fruits, the Yin-Yang fruits (阴阳果), contain the primordial duality of yin and yang, making them supreme treasures for cultivation, far surpassing the ordinary yin wood ginseng fruits. The mention of Shennong (神农氏), the Ancestral God of agriculture and medicine, roots the scene in deep mythological history. The oracle bone fragments (甲骨图) are recurring artifacts tied to the Heavenly Stele and the Martial Imprint; their ability to counter undead curses suggests they carry profound life-affirming power. The undead entities themselves—the corpse, death knight, and spirit—may be remnants of the failed underworld forged by Suiren, unable to be truly slain because they are manifestations of death itself.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up several threads for future development. The undead's ability to vanish and reappear suggests they may be guardians of a deeper secret within the palace. The newly discovered oracle fragment is likely the next piece of the martial inheritance Xiao Chen seeks, potentially unlocking new techniques. The garden itself raises questions: who cultivated these legendary trees, and what else lies ahead? Readers should also note Tianwaitian’s offhand comment about stealing from Shennong—a character detail that adds depth to his roguish persona. As the group proceeds deeper, anticipate greater dangers but also greater rewards. The contrast between the deathly halls and the luminous garden hints at the dual nature of the Death World itself.
