## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a fundamental shift in Xiao Chen's cultivation philosophy. After millennia of relying on divine artifacts, the Old Stone Turtle and the Stone Emperor confront him about his dependency on external treasures. They argue that the Heavenly Stele Profound Art requires true internal mastery, not the crutch of sacred weapons. Xiao Chen resolves to cast aside everything, sealing the Five Emperor Tower and dedicating himself to pure self-cultivation. Over two thousand years of solitary meditation, he finally achieves a breakthrough in perception, allowing him to locate a Heavenly Stele in the Curse Realm. The retrieval of the stele is accompanied by a startling discovery: sword energy that echoes the legendary Dugu Jianmo, hinting at unresolved threads from the past.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Xiao Chen reflecting on the critical importance of the seventh Heavenly Stele. The Old Stone Turtle and the Stone Emperor immediately challenge his approach, comparing him to a child wielding a weapon—powerful only as long as the weapon is in hand. They insist that true mastery of the Heavenly Stele Profound Art requires immense courage, will, and self-reliance. Xiao Chen recognizes the truth in their words: he has become overly dependent on the Divine Diagram, the ancient scroll, and the Five Emperor Tower. In a decisive act, he seals the Five Emperor Tower and entrusts it to the Stone Emperor, committing to a path of internal cultivation. The narrative then jumps forward two thousand years, during which Xiao Chen immerses himself in the celestial furnace of the island, purifying his understanding of the stele methods. He emerges with heightened spiritual perception, able to sense the locations of other Heavenly Steles. Traveling to the Curse Realm, he locates an abyss and extracts an eight-foot-tall stele. But as he pulls it free, devastating sword beams erupt from below, carrying the unmistakable aura of Dugu Jianmo—a sword hero who vanished ages ago. The chapter closes on this haunting note, suggesting that the Curse Realm holds secrets of lost warriors.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Heavenly Stele Profound Art is Xiao Chen's foundational cultivation system, derived from the mysterious steles that appear across the realms. In xianxia tradition, reliance on external treasures (法宝) is common, but often a barrier to true enlightenment. The Old Stone Turtle's analogy of a child with a weapon echoes the Daoist principle that ultimate power must come from within, not from tools. The Five Emperor Tower is a legendary artifact associated with ancient sovereigns; sealing it represents a significant sacrifice. The Curse Realm (咒界) is one of the four worlds in the cosmology—alongside the Martial, Qi, and Soul Realms—known for its formations and curses. Dugu Jianmo (独孤剑魔) belongs to the legendary Dugu clan of swordsmen, whose iron sword style once dominated the martial world. His reappearance here suggests he may have been trapped or transformed in the curse world, and his sword intent carries a weight of history that connects to Xiao Chen's earlier adventures.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a philosophical pivot for Xiao Chen. Readers should note his decision to abandon the Five Emperor Tower—this is not a mere plot device but a deliberate cultivation choice that will likely unlock new levels of strength. The time skip reinforces the novel's theme of profound patience and the cost of true mastery. The appearance of Dugu Jianmo's sword energy promises either a reunion or a confrontation that could reveal crucial lore about the Curse Realm and the fate of the Nine Provinces' heroes. Pay attention to the Old Stone Turtle's warnings about over-reliance on artifacts; this theme may resonate in future battles. The chapter also subtly advances the larger mystery of the scattered Heavenly Steles and the Otherworld's schemes. Readers can look forward to Xiao Chen's next steps as he follows the trail of the steles and seeks to understand the truth behind Dugu Jianmo's presence.
