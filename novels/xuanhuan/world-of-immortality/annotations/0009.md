## Chapter Overview

This chapter deepens the atmosphere of paranoia and hidden danger that defines Xiao Chen’s early days on Dragon Island. Forced to feign gratitude toward Wang Zifeng and Liu Yue, two disciples of the legendary Evil King, Xiao Chen walks a knife’s edge between survival and discovery. The narrative unfolds like a slow-burn thriller, with every polite exchange concealing lethal intent. The discovery of a young celestial horse injects a moment of mythic wonder, but the chapter’s true weight lies in the aftermath of an unseen battle between two primordial beasts—a confrontation that reshaped the land itself. The tone remains desolate and resigned, reminding readers that in this world, even the strongest leave only scars and shattered trees.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen’s precarious position becomes explicit when he lip-reads Wang Zifeng and Liu Yue’s whispered discussion to kill him after using him as bait. This moment crystallizes his survival strategy: play along, gather information, and wait for an opening. The duo reveals that their master, the Evil King, is an immortal being who transcends eras, and they invite Xiao Chen to join their lineage—a hollow offer Xiao Chen must accept with feigned enthusiasm. When the three travelers spot a young unicorn (a celestial horse) hidden in a tree, the creatures’s swift escape dashes their hopes of taming it, though its presence hints at the island’s deeper mysteries. As they venture deeper, they encounter the battleground of the Eight-Armed Malignant Dragon and an unidentified ancient foe—trees leveled, blood and scales scattered, the ground scarred. The two disciples grow visibly unsettled, realizing they have entered the territory of a dangerous creature. Xiao Chen, meanwhile, uses the silence to observe and calculate, his inner resolve hardening.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Evil King (邪王) mythos is a classic xianxia trope—a transcendent figure who has achieved a form of deathless immortality through a dark or unorthodox path. The Undying Demon Art (不死魔功) suggests a technique that defies normal cultivation laws, granting near-eternal life but often at a moral cost. The unicorn or celestial horse (独角圣兽/天马) is a staple of Chinese mythology: a pure creature that bonds only with virtuous maidens and grows into a flying mount of immense power. Its appearance underscores the island’s status as a realm where legendary beasts still roam. The scale of the battle between the Eight-Armed Malignant Dragon and another ancient beast is a reminder that even apex predators are not safe in this world—a Darwinian hierarchy where the strong devour the strong. The ancient forest (原始老林) itself is a character, filled with millennium-old trees and hidden dangers, a setting that amplifies the sense of isolation and mortality.

## Reading Guide

Readers should pay close attention to the dynamic between Xiao Chen and the Evil King’s disciples. Wang Zifeng’s calm, calculating demeanor and Liu Yue’s impatient cruelty will likely clash with Xiao Chen’s cold efficiency when the inevitable confrontation occurs. The celestial horse, though only glimpsed, may return as a key ally—its affinity for pure-hearted individuals raises questions about Xiao Chen’s own moral standing. The unnamed ancient beast that fought the dragon is a looming threat; its territory now encloses the group, and its identity remains unknown. The chapter also plants early seeds about Dragon Island’s forbidden nature and the divine stele that seals it, a mystery that will unfold over many chapters. Expect a rapid escalation in tension as the group moves deeper into danger, and watch for Xiao Chen to exploit any opportunity to turn the tables.
