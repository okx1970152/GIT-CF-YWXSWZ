## Chapter Overview

This chapter brings Xiao Chen face-to-face with one of the most cunning and dangerous beings in the Death World: the original body of Hongjun, a false god who once wreaked havoc in the mortal realm. Now revealed as a peerless Stone King, this being is attempting to steal the very Dao imprints from the Eighth Heavenly Stele through a forbidden technique of self-immersion and substitution. Xiao Chen observes from the shadows, learning both the terrifying power of the Stone King and the profound truth about the Heavenly Steles: they cannot be simply plagiarized but must be earned through prior cultivation. The chapter masterfully blends high-stakes tension with deep philosophical insight into the nature of Dao, setting up a critical turning point in Xiao Chen's cultivation journey.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen, who has been sealed inside the stone jar world for centuries, senses the presence of a powerful Stone King near the Eighth Heavenly Stele. This Stone King is none other than the original entity behind Hongjun, a being who once absorbed vast quantities of mortal faith and claimed supremacy in the Nine Provinces. The Stone King is meticulously studying the stele's inscriptions, using his divine power to make hidden characters and diagrams appear. He employs an extraordinary technique: transforming his body into a Yin-Yang diagram, temporarily merging with the stele's Dao imprints to absorb them directly. This method—self-immersion into the Dao rather than comprehension—is both brilliant and dangerous. Xiao Chen watches as the Stone King nearly loses his mind to the stele's overwhelming power but recovers, eventually stealing the seal technique imprint. Xiao Chen then uses the Five Emperor Tower to approach the stele himself. He attempts to study the diagrams but quickly realizes that the Eighth Heavenly Stele requires mastery of the previous seven. He also discovers that the seal techniques shown are strikingly similar to those used by the mysterious female corpse he encountered earlier. On the reverse side of the stele, he finds crucial textual clues about the locations of the first seven steles and speculation about the steles' origin. This chapter not only advances Xiao Chen's understanding but also introduces a potent antagonist who will likely play a major role in the coming conflict.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of 'stealing the Dao' (窃道) is a recurring theme in xianxia literature, where a cultivator bypasses personal enlightenment by directly appropriating heavenly principles. The Stone King's method—transforming into a Yin-Yang diagram and merging with the stele—is a clever narrative device that highlights his cunning but also underscores a fundamental truth: the Dao cannot be truly stolen; it must be walked. The Eighth Heavenly Stele's requirement that the cultivator possess the previous seven steles' teachings mirrors the incremental, cumulative nature of cultivation in many Chinese myths. The mention of Hongjun (鸿钧) is a direct reference to a popular figure from the novel 'Investiture of the Gods' (封神演义), where Hongjun is the teacher of the Three Pure Ones—a supreme Daoist deity. Here, Hongjun is reimagined as a false god whose original body is a Stone King, adding layers of mythological subversion. The female corpse's seal techniques being linked to the steles hints at a vast, interconnected history spanning multiple worlds.
