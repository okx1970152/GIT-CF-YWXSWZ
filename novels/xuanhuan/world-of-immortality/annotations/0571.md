## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges Xiao Chen and his irreverent ally, the old goat, deeper into the Sun Star Palace, a realm of treacherous ancestral gods and legendary treasures. They encounter Lian Wang, a projection of a peerless stone lotus king, whose mere presence silences their pursuers. The chapter masterfully weaves political cunning with desperate survival: the old goat exploits Lian Wang's authority to intimidate a powerful enemy, then leads Xiao Chen to a miraculous discovery—a broken stone sword, once the weapon of a stone man king. What follows is a deadly scramble for artifacts, with ancestral gods turning on each other as the goat sows discord with discarded fragments. The chapter culminates in Xiao Chen's ruthless pursuit and elimination of the White Ancestral God Bai Zushen, using an ancient scroll that channels the power of five legendary beings. A distant bell tolls, announcing that the ultimate treasure is within reach, setting the stage for a climactic confrontation.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with Lian Wang standing like a statue, her presence so overwhelming that even the arrogant Bai Zushen retreats. The old goat audaciously uses her to berate Bai Zushen, a gamble that pays off when Lian Wang merely steps on the goat instead of annihilating him—suggesting she recognizes something in him. After she departs, the duo discovers a chaotic space behind a displaced king statue, where a broken stone sword floats in a pavilion. A greedy intruder tries to seize it but is repelled by the sword's residual power. The old goat correctly deduces that the king statue is the key to safe retrieval, and they claim the artifact.

Bai Zushen, humiliated and vengeful, gathers seven or eight other powerful beings to ambush them. The old goat's counter is brilliant: he breaks the already-fractured sword into pieces and scatters them among the attackers, inciting chaos. The ancestral gods turn on each other, allowing the duo to escape. A strange, cannibalistic creature then descends upon the hall, slaughtering most of the survivors. Bai Zushen barely escapes but is pursued by Xiao Chen, who unleashes his ancient scroll—a treasure depicting five towering figures. The scroll engulfs Bai Zushen's body and soul, and a final rotten wood arrow annihilates him entirely.

The old goat returns, revealing he witnessed the scroll's power and now covets it. But a resonant bell from the depths of the Sun Star Palace draws their attention: someone has reached the central chamber where the ultimate treasure is sealed. The stone corpse (Shi Shi) and a mysterious beast called Huan Shi are already contested.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Stone man kings (石人王者) represent the pinnacle of physical cultivation in this cosmology—beings who have refined their bodies into indestructible stone and command supreme authority. Lian Wang, the Stone Lotus King, is one such figure, and even a projection of her carries the weight of an entire celestial realm. The ancient scroll Xiao Chen uses is a relic from a previous civilization era, depicting five beings who were the greatest enemies of the Otherworld. This scroll embodies the legacy of fallen ages, a device of immense narrative and combat power. The rotten wood arrow, despite its humble name, is a forbidden weapon capable of slaying ancestral gods; its appearance here underscores the desperation and lethality of the conflict. The Sun Star Palace itself is a sacred site built over the tomb of a stone man king, its corridors lined with traps and remnants of forgotten gods. The broken stone sword, though shattered, retains the essence of a stone man's finishing step—a symbol of incomplete transcendence.

## Reading Guide
The tolling bell signals the arrival at the story's next major crucible: the ultimate treasure. Xiao Chen now possesses a broken stone sword, an ancient scroll, and the fading but potent rotten wood arrow. However, he faces competition from the stone corpse (Shi Shi) and the bestial Huan Shi, both of whom have already reached the core. The old goat's true identity remains tantalizingly veiled—his claims of kinship with Shi Zhongdi may be boast or truth, but his cunning and knowledge are undeniable. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen's new artifacts will shift the balance of power, and whether the ancient scroll's five spirits can be summoned again. The chapter also deepens the lore of the stone man path, hinting at the 'final step' and the sacrifices required to ascend. The confrontation with Lian Wang was brief, but her potential as an ally or threat lingers. The political acumen displayed here—using presence to cow enemies—will be essential in the battles ahead.
