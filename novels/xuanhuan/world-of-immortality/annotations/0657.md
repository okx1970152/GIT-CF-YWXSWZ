## Chapter Overview

This chapter delves into the tragic history of the Ancestral Dragon of the Nine Provinces—her nine sons, all War Ancestors of the Ninth Heaven, who sacrificed themselves fighting the Otherworld gods. It reveals how she summoned the Heavenly Stele, annihilated the invading gods, but was fatally wounded and ultimately became the Yellow River. The chapter also shows Xiao Chen's return to the Ancestral Dragon Village after 30,000 years, his poignant reunion with his still-sleeping parents, and his determined departure. Finally, we see the melancholy figure of Renmo Ge Qian, reciting a poem by the Yellow River, as the Heavens themselves begin to shatter.

## Key Plot Points

The ancestral dragon's nine sons—Yazi, Pixiu, Suanni, and others—are revealed as War Ancestors at the Ninth Heaven, who had hidden from the Otherworld for ages to buy time for their mother's breakthrough. On the day of battle, they emerged and fought with devastating power, killing nearly all Upper Ancestral Gods and below, but they themselves perished. In her grief, the ancestral dragon summoned the Heavenly Stele at the cost of her own lifeblood, achieving the Stone Man King realm in the process, and annihilated every god present in the Nine Provinces. However, an Otherworld powerhouse used forbidden techniques—channeling the resentment of a dozen destroyed worlds—to turn the Heavenly Stele's power back upon her through the law of karma. Before Pangu King could intervene, she transformed into the Yellow River, leaving only a remnant soul sealed in a purple jade bracelet. Xiao Chen returns to the village after 30,000 years, finds his parents sleeping as if no time had passed, and is overwhelmed with emotion but forces himself to leave, knowing the village must remain hidden. Outside, he encounters Renmo Ge Qian standing on a five-story tower by the Yellow River, reciting a melancholy poem and asking, "Is it time now?" The chapter ends with cataclysm: three stone cities in the Otherworld shatter, five primordial demon cities in the new otherworld collapse, and an Upper Ancestral God of flesh and blood has his forehead crack open, blood streaming down.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter deepens the lore of Dragon Island and the Ancestral Dragon lineage. The nine sons of the dragon—Yazi (wrathful son), Pixiu (wealth-attracting son), Suanni (lion-like son), etc.—are legendary mythical beasts from Chinese folklore, here reimagined as Ninth Heaven War Ancestors. The concept of "War Ancestor" (战祖) is distinct from "Ancestral God" (祖神), emphasizing martial combat prowess over divine law mastery. The Ancestral Dragon's sacrifice to summon the Heavenly Stele and the use of "karma" as a weapon by the Otherworld introduce deeper metaphysical elements, showing that even supreme power can be turned against its wielder through the accumulated suffering of destroyed civilizations. The purple jade bracelet as a vessel for a remnant soul is a classic xianxia trope, linking Xiao Chen's family heirloom to cosmic history. Finally, Renmo Ge Qian's poem evokes classical Chinese poetry's melancholic tradition—the eternal river contrasting with fleeting human pride—and his question "Is it time now?" suggests that a long-awaited prophecy or plan is reaching its culmination.

## Reading Guide

This chapter provides crucial backstory for the Dragon Clan's war against the Otherworld, explaining the origins of the Yellow River and the Ancestral Dragon's legacy. It also establishes the deep link between Xiao Chen's hometown and the ancient dragon, making the revelation that his family has been preserved in a hidden village all the more poignant. The reappearance of Renmo Ge Qian, reciting a poem of loss and waiting, signals that major events are unfolding. The synchronized destruction of multiple demonic cities and the injury of an Upper Ancestral God at the chapter's end hint that a grand convergence or counterattack is underway. Readers should pay close attention to Ge Qian's characterization—he is no simple villain but a tragic figure caught between duty and despair. The chapter also emphasizes Xiao Chen's emotional grounding in his mortal family, setting up personal stakes for the coming battles. The phrase "time is up" echoed in the heavens suggests that the long-simmering conflict is about to erupt into a final, decisive war.
