## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a profound philosophical and metaphysical turning point in the narrative. Xiao Chen, guided by a mysterious figure who reveals himself as the Undying Evil King Shi Zhixuan, experiences a vision of the world's true nature. He sees sleeping multitudes, legendary heroes, and the rise of half-ancestors, all culminating in the descent of the Heavenly Steles. The Undying Evil King then delivers a shocking doctrine: many of the revered half-ancestors—including Original, Tongtian, Buddha, and others—are illusory beings, born from the dreams of the real world, while the true Ancestral Gods such as Suiren, Fuxi, and Nuwa are the genuine foundation. The chapter ends with the re-emergence of Dragon Island near the mortal world and the terrifying discovery that the Dead City is materializing beneath Xiao Chen's ancestral village, setting the stage for a cataclysmic convergence.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiao Chen's divine consciousness is guided into a vision where he witnesses countless sleeping souls, then sees the half-ancestors (Original, Tongtian, Zhunti, etc.) and legendary mortals (Dugu Qiubai, Li Xunhuan, Shi Zhixuan) appearing. The Heavenly Steles appear, causing cosmic upheaval.
- The mysterious figure identifies himself as the Undying Evil King (石之轩). He declares that most half-ancestors are illusory (虚), born from the collective unconscious, while the true Ancestral Gods (燧人氏, 有巢氏, 伏羲, 女娲) are real. He claims that Hongjun is the strongest false god, comparable to a true Ancestral God.
- The Undying Evil King explains his desire to shatter the illusion and restore reality, seeing hope in Xiao Chen. He warns that the worst outcome would be the emergence of the 'Primordial Celestial Realm' (洪荒天界).
- After departing, Xiao Chen senses that Dragon Island is reconnecting with the mortal world. Keke confirms the familiar aura.
- The Yellow Clay Altar (黄泥台) flies Xiao Chen back to his ancestral village, where he observes the Dead City (死城) from Dragon Island slowly rising beneath the land, causing intense foreboding.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

- This chapter introduces a meta-cosmological concept: the distinction between 'real' Ancestral Gods (true primordial beings who left historical evidence) and 'illusory' half-ancestors (beings that emerged from the collective dreams of sentient beings but have no true foundation in reality). This echoes Daoist and Buddhist ideas of illusion (maya) and the ultimate nature of existence.
- The Undying Evil King's statement about Hongjun (鸿钧) being the strongest false god is a radical reinterpretation of a figure revered in many xianxia works as the supreme Daoist teacher. It challenges entrenched lore.
- The reappearance of Dragon Island and the Dead City marks a major plot development: the separation between worlds is breaking, and forces from the World of Immortality are manifesting in the mortal realm.
- The Heavenly Steles are revealed to be connected to the fundamental structure of reality, their appearance signaling world-shaking change.

## Reading Guide

- Pay close attention to the Undying Evil King's philosophy—it may foreshadow the ultimate conflict of the series: the battle between the real and the illusory, and the need to 'kill' false gods without slaying true Ancestral Gods.
- Xiao Chen's vision of sleeping multitudes and the later re-emergence of Dragon Island suggest that the boundaries between worlds are thinning. This hints at a massive convergence of realms.
- The hanging question of whether the Dead City will emerge fully from beneath the village sets up immediate tension. What will happen when it does? Will it bring the horrors of the Dead City into the mortal world, or serve as a gateway?
- The absence of Yellow Emperor, Chiyou, Laozi, and Buddha in Xiao Chen's vision of half-ancestors may indicate that they are 'real' or have transcended the illusion, setting them apart from the illusory half-ancestors Xiao Chen has been fighting.
