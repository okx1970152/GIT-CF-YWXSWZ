## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks Xiao Chen's first moments in the World of Immortality, a realm that feels both savage and sacred. After the harrowing vision of the Death Castle and Bone Sea, he awakens on a tropical island alive with primordial beasts. The stark contrast between the deathly aura that swept the island and the vibrant, dangerous jungle underscores the duality of this new world. Xiao Chen's immediate struggle is survival—thirst, injury, and the emotional weight of being permanently separated from his family. His internal monologue carries a tragic, desolate tone, as he performs a quiet ritual of farewell to his parents across the void. Yet even in grief, he forces himself to act, beginning cultivation with the Heaven-Bestowed Qi-Refining Diagram. This chapter establishes the foundation of his resolve and hints at the vast mysteries awaiting him.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen regains consciousness on a sun-scorched beach, the lingering chill of death still in his bones from the passing of the ancient castle. He drags himself to a coconut grove, quenches his thirst, and forces himself to assess his situation. He wonders about Lan Nuo's fate and whether legendary figures like Dugu Qiubai truly inhabit this world. With solemn deliberation, he kneels to the direction of his homeland and bows to his parents, knowing he will never see them again—a moment of restrained but profound grief. He then turns to cultivation, drawing on the Heaven-Bestowed Qi-Refining Diagram that he discovered as a child on the Yellow River Divine Stele. This diagram, which emerged during a drought and bore the inscription "Ever-Pacify the Yellow River," has been his sole cultivation method for ten years. The chapter emphasizes his loneliness and the immense burden of starting anew, but also his unyielding determination to survive. The savage cries of beasts around him remind us that this island is no sanctuary.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The "Heaven-Bestowed Qi-Refining Diagram" is a classic example of a xianxia "heaven-granted" technique, often discovered by chance and tied to divine artifacts. The Yellow River Divine Stele, inscribed with "Ever-Pacify the Yellow River," draws on the legendary Xia dynasty hero Yu the Great, who controlled floods. In Chinese mythology, such steles often carry the power to seal or suppress natural forces. Xiao Chen's practice of kneeling and bowing to his parents across space reflects the deep Confucian value of filial piety, even in a world of immortals. The separation between mortals and cultivators is a recurring theme: once one enters the World of Immortality, the mortal world becomes unreachable. This chapter also introduces the concept of "Breaking the Void" (from the previous chapter), which Xiao Chen himself has not achieved but was drawn into by Lan Nuo's ascension. The cultivation process described—absorbing the essence of heaven and earth through a breathing method—is fundamental to xianxia, emphasizing harmony with nature.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for Xiao Chen's long-term struggle: his cultivation technique is incomplete, and he suspects it may not match legendary methods. Readers should watch for how he adapts and whether he discovers new cultivation insights on Dragon Island. The island itself is a character—filled with primordial beasts and hidden dangers, but also ancient mysteries like the Death Castle and the Bone Sea that may reappear. The mention of Lan Nuo and legendary figures like Dugu Qiubai plants seeds for future encounters or revelations. Xiao Chen's emotional farewell to his parents is a key character moment that grounds his motivation; he is not a carefree adventurer but a reluctant exile. The tension between his grief and his pragmatism will shape his decisions. Additionally, the divine stele's connection to the island's secrets hints at a larger cosmic design. Expect the immediate challenges to involve finding food, shelter, and allies—or avoiding enemies.
