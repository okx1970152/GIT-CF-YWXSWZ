## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges deeper into the oppressive, horror-laden atmosphere of the Dead City. Xiao Chen, Liu Mu, Yan Qingcheng (her power sealed), Liu Ruyan, and the three skeletons navigate the dark alleys, pursued by unknown entities. The tension ratchets up as they realize the city is alive with ancient, murderous beings. A particular ghostly figure stalks them, and they are forced to confront a tangible horror: a child-like corpse that is neither living nor dead. The chapter balances creeping dread with explosive combat, showcasing Xiao Chen's and Liu Mu's coordinated skills.

## Key Plot Points
The group hears distant screams and discovers that yin soldiers, previously passive, are now actively killing cultivators. They witness the aftermath of a fight involving the lone swordsman Dugu Jianmo, who is forced to retreat wounded, establishing the terror of the city's inhabitants. Navigating through narrow alleys to avoid large creatures, they are stalked by a mysterious clacking sound, like wooden clogs. The stalker is revealed to be a ghostly child in ancient clothing, emitting corpse stench and yellow fluid. Xiao Chen and Liu Mu engage it: Liu Mu uses spatial confinement to immobilize it, while Xiao Chen attacks with his Big Dipper light screen and a crescent-shaped light blade. The ghost proves resilient, its body like metal, but they eventually sever its limbs and head, then destroy it entirely. The encounter raises questions about the nature of ghosts in this world, as the child shows no soul force, only malevolent energy. The chapter ends with the group still trapped, the city's horrors closing in.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Dead City is a forbidden zone filled with ancient formations and spectral beings. The yin soldiers and ghosts draw from Chinese folklore where souls of the dead linger due to unresolved grievances or powerful cultivation. This novel distinguishes between 'undead' (bones with soul-fire) and 'ghosts'—corrupted corpses animated by deathly energy, lacking a soul. The child ghost's skin like fish scales and metal-like body suggests a corpse transformed by prolonged exposure to the city's deathly qi, echoing the Daoist concept of 'jiangshi' or hopping corpses. Such entities are often bound to a location by curse or formation, making them predictable but dangerous. The city's design seems to confuse intruders, a common trope in xianxia where ancient formations trap trespassers in an endless maze.

## Reading Guide
The Dead City continues to reveal its layered horrors. Readers should note the emerging patterns: the yin soldiers are now active, and the city's layout seems deliberately disorienting. The introduction of a ghost as a physical corpse rather than a wandering spirit hints at deeper curse mechanics. What other entities lurk? The previous mention of government offices (yamen) and the clacking sound suggest there may be more intelligent and organized forces at work. Xiao Chen and Liu Mu's combat synergy improves, but Yan Qingcheng and Liu Ruyan remain liabilities—one sealed, one fearful. The three skeletons are silent but alert. The chapter ends with the ghost destroyed but the city's trap unbroken; the next chapter likely continues their desperate search for an exit while evading even greater threats. Tension is mounting, and survival seems uncertain.
