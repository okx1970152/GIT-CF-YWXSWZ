## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges Xiao Chen and his allies into a high-stakes trap at Lie Tian Valley, where the cunning Otherworld Stone Man King Lu Zhan has laid two primordial killing formations to refine them. The atmosphere is one of grim strategic deployment and desperate survival, as the protagonists are caught in a vice of ancient arrays designed to slay Stone Man Kings. The revelation that their guide—a seemingly harmless old Daoist—bears the seven-starred palm mark of the legendary Little Stone Emperor adds a layer of ominous mystery, hinting at powers far beyond the immediate fight. The tension escalates when a third, even more cataclysmic formation erupts from the valley itself, ensnaring both the would-be refiners and the trapped, turning the battlefield into a murderous free-for-all.

## Key Plot Points

- The little old man displays his left palm, revealing seven red star moles—the unmistakable birthmark of the Little Stone Emperor. He insists he is only a descendant, but the Stone Crow is skeptical, almost attacking before the old bone intervenes.
- After heated debate, the Stone Crow and old bone decide to investigate Lie Tian Valley regardless, believing the tomb may contain dangerous relics that must be secured.
- Using the Pangu Stone Token to mask their presence, Xiao Chen, the Stone Crow, and the old bone approach the valley, only to be instantly sealed by two interlocking primordial arrays: the Demon-Suppressing Soul-Locking Array and the White Bone Absolute Palace Array.
- Lu Zhan’s projection appears, revealing that he suspected spies and deliberately named the valley as bait. His trap is precise, designed to refine even a Stone Man King.
- Under the crushing weight of the arrays, Xiao Chen unleashes his full arsenal: the ancient scroll, the king-level divine city, the heavenly marks, a heavenly stele, and the stone-man king puppet, shaking the formations and astonishing his allies.
- Before a breakthrough can be forced, seven colossal black stars blast upward from the valley floor, forming a third, over-arching formation that entraps not only Xiao Chen’s group but also Lu Zhan himself.
- Lu Zhan recognizes the power as the innate mark of the Little Stone Emperor, proving the true tomb is elsewhere and that the valley holds an even deadlier secret.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Primordial Killing Fields (太古杀场) are ancient arrays engineered in an era when Stone Man Kings warred directly. The two arrays employed here—the Demon-Suppressing Soul-Locking Array (炼魔锁神阵) and the White Bone Absolute Palace Array (白骨绝宫阵)—are infamous for combining 108 hellish realms with 108 heavenly palaces, each generating a full spectrum of killing forces that grind even supreme beings to essence. The seven black stars are the natural emblem of the Little Stone Emperor, a sovereign of the Primordial Era who unified part of the Otherworld. The appearance of these stars without a visible controller hints at a lingering will or an unsuspected tomb beneath the valley. The Pangu Stone Token (盘古石令), used for concealment, is a relic of the primordial king Pangu, capable of hiding from ancestor-level perception, underscoring the high risk of the mission.

## Reading Guide

The chapter ends in a perfect stalemate: the third formation now threatens both the trapped party and the trapper. Readers should watch for how Lu Zhan will react to this reversal—whether he can withdraw or is forced into a desperate alliance. The little old man’s identity remains the pivotal wildcard; his bloodline may be the key to controlling or dispelling the seven-star array. Xiao Chen’s display of multiple supreme treasures marks his emergence as a genuine threat to Stone Man Kings, but the primordial power of the Little Stone Emperor raises the stakes to an entirely new level. The parallel to Keke’s imprisonment in seven demon cities is striking, suggesting a deeper pattern in the Olden World’s use of such formations. The next chapter will likely resolve the immediate crisis through a combination of the little old man’s heritage and Xiao Chen’s cunning, but the cost may be severe.
