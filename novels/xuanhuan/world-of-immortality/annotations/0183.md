## Chapter Overview

This chapter weaves together the tragic burden of dragon kingship, the raw power of ancient artifacts, and the political undercurrents of Heavenly Emperor City. The Little Defiant Dragon’s victory in the beast arena is not a triumph but a sorrowful step on a bloody path – only one dragon king can survive to become the Ancestral Dragon. Xiao Chen heals the grievously wounded dragon with the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree, only to face a crude challenger who seeks to claim the dragon by force. In a desperate gambit, Xiao Chen channels the power of the Dark-Iron Seal, shattering his opponent. The intervention of an Undying Sect elder hints at deeper entanglements, while Keke’s reckless theft of the sacred tree adds tension and humor.

## Key Plot Points

The Little Defiant Dragon defeats the Golden Dragon King in the arena, but the victory carries immense emotional weight – the dragon must kill its own kind to walk the path toward Ancestral Dragonhood. Xiao Chen rushes the bleeding dragon home and uses the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree, borrowed from Keke, to heal it through a profound spiritual connection. A middle-aged man from a rival family (likely the Ligen clan) storms Xiao Chen’s residence, demanding the dragon and attacking with lethal intent. Xiao Chen, unable to control the full power of the Dark-Iron Seal or the Golden Divine Halberd, briefly manifests the seal’s might in his left hand, smashing the man’s palm and breaking his chest. The man’s defeat is swift but not fatal – Xiao Chen prepares to execute him when an elder of the Undying Sect, Liu Qingfeng, intervenes, citing a common origin with the attacker’s lineage and warning against killing. The elder leaves with the defeated man, and Zhuge Liang quips that the Undying Sect owes Xiao Chen a wife (a nod to Yan Qingcheng). Meanwhile, Keke, incensed by the merchant’s price gouging, sneaks off to steal the sacred tree – and returns with it, much to everyone’s shock.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The dragon kings’ fate is rooted in the novel’s mythic framework: only one Companion Dragon King can survive to gather the Ancestral Dragon qi and break the seal on Dragon Island. This Darwinian path is not a choice but a racial duty, giving each kill a tragic, sacrificial dimension. The Dark-Iron Seal is an ancient sacred artifact that once attacked the divine stele in the City of the Dead. Xiao Chen cannot yet control it, but proximity has infused his hands with traces of its power – enough to wound a Spirit-Treasury realm expert in a single blow. This demonstrates how relics of the primordial age can tip the scales even against higher cultivation. The Undying Sect’s elder Liu Qingfeng reveals that the attacker’s lineage shares origins with the Undying Sect through the ancient Demon Sect, a reminder of the continent’s tangled history. The appearance of Zhuge Liang’s joke about the ‘Romance of the Three Kingdoms’ (passed from the Mortal World) adds a rare moment of cultural crossover humor.

## Reading Guide

The chapter deepens the Little Defiant Dragon’s emotional arc – its loneliness and sorrow humanize it beyond a mere combat beast. Xiao Chen’s growing ability to channel the Dark-Iron Seal and the Golden Divine Halberd (mentioned but unused) foreshadows his potential to harness these god-slaying relics. The intervention of Elder Liu Qingfeng introduces a faction that may become either a shield or a leash; the line about the Undying Sect “owing Xiao Chen a wife” suggests a future confrontation over Yan Qingcheng’s fate. Keke’s theft of the sacred tree may draw unwanted attention from the auction house and the families controlling it. Readers should watch for the consequences of this reckless act, and the inevitable rematch with the defeated challenger’s clan.
