## Chapter Overview
This chapter explores the political and social aftermath of the revelation that Xiao Chen's backing from dragon kings and sacred beasts was a masterful deception by Long Teng. The Hai family, forced to proceed with a wedding they now bitterly regret, becomes a microcosm of the hypocritical power structures of the Southern Wastes. Xiao Chen, trapped in a ceremonial farce, confronts the deep humiliation of being treated as a pawn while maintaining a cold, analytical resolve. The tension between public performance and private desperation drives the chapter, culminating in a solitary, sincere toast from Dugu Jianmo and Xiao Chen's decision to enter the bridal chamber with bitter laughter.

## Key Plot Points
The Hai family and other major families learn that the dragon kings and sacred beasts were taken by Long Teng, shattering their belief in Xiao Chen's backing and leaving them feeling duped. Patriarch Hai Fanyun, to avoid public shame, decides to continue the wedding ceremony but secretly sends word to the assassins Hu Nu and Hu Zun, hoping they will kill Xiao Chen during the event. Xiao Chen deduces this scheme but is forced to participate under the watch of a Hai family elder. The wedding banquet is filled with hypocritical well-wishers, and Xiao Chen feels like an actor on stage, performing a role he despises. As evening falls and the assassins fail to appear, Hai Fanyun grows increasingly anxious. Guests push for the couple to enter the bridal chamber, and Hai Fanyun cannot delay further without losing face. Dugu Jianmo approaches Xiao Chen and offers a genuine toast, the only honest moment of the entire day. Xiao Chen, resigned and defiant, decides to play the game to the end and enters the bridal chamber with a cold laugh.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter vividly illustrates the concept of 'face' (mianzi) in xianxia politics – the Hai family prioritizes public reputation over individual happiness, honor, or justice. The wedding ritual, though sacred, is treated as a tool for political maneuvering, with all parties aware of the hypocrisy. The presence of Dugu Jianmo, a descendant of the legendary sword immortal Dugu Qiubai, highlights the weight of clan lineages in the Eternal Continent. His solitary and aloof demeanor contrasts sharply with the scheming nobles, representing the ideal of the wandering cultivator who values martial honor over social games. The mention of Long Teng's rampage thirty years ago connects to the history of Dragon Island and the power of ancient dragons, reinforcing the cyclical nature of conflict in this world. The toasting scene also carries cultural weight – toasting is a sign of respect or enmity, and Dugu Jianmo's act is a rare honor.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up a critical turning point for Xiao Chen's relationships in the Southern Wastes. The Hai family's scheme to have Xiao Chen killed during the wedding may fail, but the political damage is done – Xiao Chen is now isolated and vulnerable. Readers should watch for how the assassination attempt unfolds and whether Xiao Chen will use the bridal chamber as a stage for escape, confrontation, or further manipulation. The thread with Hai Yunxue, a proud and beautiful woman now forced into this sham marriage, promises future complexity and potential resentment. Dugu Jianmo's gesture of respect may foreshadow a future alliance or rivalry, given his reputation for cold indifference. The appearance of the two assassins, Hu Nu and Hu Zun, will test Xiao Chen's cultivation at the peak of Mortal-Shedding. This arc explores the theme of identity and performance – how much of Xiao Chen's cold exterior is real, and how much is armor against a world that sees him only as a tool.
