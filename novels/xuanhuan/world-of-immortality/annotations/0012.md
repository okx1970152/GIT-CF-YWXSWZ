## Chapter Overview
This chapter pits Xiao Chen in a desperate nocturnal flight through a rain-lashed forest, hunted by the relentless Zhao Lin'er. Wounded and on the brink of collapse, he makes a calculated gamble—leading his pursuer into the territory of an ancient horror: the legendary Ancient Tyrant Dragon. The scene crackles with a grim, mythic tension as the storm, the sword-light, and the beast's primeval presence converge into a life-or-death standoff. This is a chapter of pure survival instinct, where human vendetta meets the indifferent savagery of a world far older.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen, grievously wounded from his earlier battle with Prince Zifeng, is caught by Zhao Lin'er in the rain-swept forest. Despite his valor, his injuries prevent him from matching her speed and technique; the Rainbow Sword Art of the Royal Celestial Maiden forces him into a desperate retreat. Recognizing that he cannot outrun her, Xiao Chen deliberately leads her into the lair of the Ancient Tyrant Dragon—the same beast that had earlier enabled him to kill Prince Zifeng and Liu Yue by its sudden appearance. His gamble succeeds in forcing Zhao Lin'er to retreat, but it also places him in immediate proximity to the fifty-meter-long dragon. The chapter vividly describes the Tyrant Dragon's appearance, linking it to the ancestral dragon lineage and emphasizing its god-like power. Xiao Chen's tactical cunning is on full display: he uses the environment as his weapon, turning an overwhelming threat into an escape route. Zhao Lin'er's caution and survival instinct ultimately outweigh her vengeance, showcasing her as a pragmatic adversary. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen alone in the rain, facing the beast but having bought himself a moment's respite.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Ancient Tyrant Dragon (暴龙) is introduced as a "royal branch" of the dragon lineage, a concept deeply rooted in Chinese mythology where dragons are divided into noble bloodlines with varying power. The description of its abilities—controlling wind and rain, flight, and god-like strength—echoes classical depictions of dragons as weather deities and supreme beings. The term "暴龙" is a xianxia invention, combining "violent" and "dragon," but it draws on the traditional image of the dragon as a natural force. Zhao Lin'er's Rainbow Sword Art (彩虹剑诀) is a typical high-grade cultivation technique that manifests as colored beams of energy, a common trope in xianxia representing the refinement of elemental power into weaponized light. Her movement technique, the Floating Light Shadow-Chasing Illusory Step (浮光掠影神虚步), is ranked alongside the Bodhi Leaf Drifting Art, indicating its transcendent speed—a reminder that in this world, martial arts are not mere combat but paths to immortality.

## Reading Guide
This chapter layers tension masterfully: the personal enmity between Xiao Chen and Zhao Lin'er, the primal threat of the Tyrant Dragon, and the elemental fury of the storm all collide. Readers should pay close attention to Xiao Chen's decision-making—his willingness to risk mutual destruction reveals a core part of his survival philosophy. Zhao Lin'er's retreat shows that she is not ruled by emotion; she will be a more dangerous enemy for it. The Ancient Tyrant Dragon's appearance also sets up future significance—its presence on the island is not coincidental, and its role in the larger dragon mythology of the World of Immortality is just beginning to unfold. The chapter also deepens the world's sense of scale: even the most powerful cultivators are insignificant before such ancient beings.
