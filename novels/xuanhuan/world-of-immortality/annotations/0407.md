## Chapter Overview

This chapter opens with an outrageous scene: Tianwaitian and Renwairen, two human Half-Ancestors with a street‐punk demeanor, are laying siege to the Celestial Paradise of the Angel God. Their vulgar taunts and overwhelming power shatter the sacred calm, stunning even the observing Chen Xiao (Xiao Chen) who watches from Paradise Lost. The mood is defiant and chaotic—these legendary figures have truly returned to the mortal world with a vengeance. Yet beneath the rowdy comedy, the chapter plants the seeds of a dark new order: the Hundred Races are gathering, and the Half‐Ancestors of the non‐human clans are forming a formidable bloc to dictate the rules of the coming spiritual trials. Chen Xiao’s own position becomes precarious when four foreign Half‐Ancestors openly threaten to kill him and Keke if they dare to bring the Paradise Lost artifact into the battlefield. The emotional tone shifts from humorous outrage to grim determination as Chen Xiao decides to bide his time and fight the younger generation instead of the old monsters. The chapter closes with a chilling reminder of the cruelty of the new age: an innocent boatman is casually murdered by a Rakshasa, and Chen Xiao retaliates with swift, absolute force, setting the stage for the coming war of elites.

## Key Plot Points

Tianwaitian and Renwairen have returned to the mortal world and are confronting the Angel God of the Celestial Paradise, accusing him of killing their disciple thousands of years ago. They demolish a portion of the holy temple with raw power, forcing the Angel God to retreat into negotiations. The arrival of the Skeleton King—an astonishing Half‐Ancestor who reveals that he has already achieved flesh‐and‐blood rebirth but deliberately retains his bone form—calms the situation and brokers a truce. However, the Rakshasa King arrives, and together with the Angel God and the Skeleton King, they face down the two human Half‐Ancestors. Tianwaitian drops a bombshell: many human Half‐Ancestors, including the Yellow Emperor, have disappeared in the depths of the cosmos, and only Tianwaitian and Renwairen have returned. He then delivers the pivotal news that the Nine Provinces and the Four Worlds will become a battlefield for a great trial, with the ultimate winners being granted the chance to become Half‐Ancestors themselves—a breathtaking gamble that pits life against life for supreme power. The foreign Half‐Ancestors then turn their attention to Chen Xiao and Keke, barring them from entering the Celestial Temple and explicitly threatening to kill them if they use Paradise Lost in the trials. Chen Xiao, after an initial standoff, retreats with Keke and resolves to crush the younger generation of the foreign races instead. The chapter ends with a brutal incident in a riverside town: a Rakshasa destroys a boat, killing an innocent old man, and Chen Xiao annihilates the murderer on the spot, demonstrating his fury and his commitment to protecting the weak. The Rakshasa King watches from the distance but does not intervene, setting up a future confrontation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The phrase “Half‐Ancestor” (半祖) refers to cultivators who have reached a realm just below the ultimate Ancestral God, commanding staggering power and near‐immortality. These beings are the de facto rulers of the world. The appearance of Tianwaitian and Renwairen—human Half‐Ancestors—signals that the human race is not without its own ultimate champions. Their names translate to “Heaven Beyond Heaven” and “Person Beyond Person,” respectively, titles that convey their transcendence above ordinary classification. The concept of a great trial (试炼大战) where the ultimate victors can be elevated to Half‐Ancestor status is a classic xianxia mechanism for intensifying conflict: it forces all factions to commit their best talents, and the promise of cultivatinal ascension turns life into a high‐stakes tournament. The “Paradise Lost” (失乐园) carried by Keke is a legendary sealed realm that can even trap Half‐Ancestors; it is no wonder the foreign kings fear it. The angelic paradise described (天堂圣地) is likely the same sacred location seen in earlier arcs, now under siege. Chen Xiao’s current state—a Yellow Diamond skeleton from the Death World—makes him an anomaly among the living, and his refusal to back down despite the threats of four Half‐Ancestors shows his characteristic defiance. The casual murder of a mortal boatman by a Rakshasa highlights the brutal hierarchy of the Hundred Races era: ordinary humans are treated as insects by the powerful. Chen Xiao’s instant vengeance reasserts his moral code and foreshadows his role as a protector of the weak in the coming chaos.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as a massive status check for the post‐sealing era. Readers should pay close attention to the new power structure being negotiated by the Half‐Ancestors: the foreign kings (Angel God, Skeleton King, Rakshasa King, etc.) are consolidating their rules, while only two human Half‐Ancestors have returned so far. The mention of other human sages—Buddha, Laozi, and the Yellow Emperor—disappearing into the starry void suggests that deeper cosmic threats loom. Chen Xiao’s decision to avoid direct confrontation with the Half‐Ancestors and instead focus on their younger disciples is a classic underdog strategy that will probably lead to spectacular showdowns in the upcoming trials. The Rakshasa King’s silent observation of Chen Xiao’s execution of the Rakshasa murderer is laden with tension: the king did not lift a finger to save his clansman, which may indicate that he is either indifferent or is saving his own strength. Also, watch for Keke’s role—the foreign Half‐Ancestors’ explicit prohibition of Paradise Lost in the trials suggests that the artifact is their biggest fear. Chen Xiao’s promise to give the foreign kings “something to cry about” during the contest of the younger generation hints that he already has a plan. The chapter ends with Chen Xiao traveling the mortal world with Xiao Tianya and Keke, savoring a peaceful moment before the storm. This calm before the battle is likely the last quiet scene for a long time. The boatman’s death is a grim reminder that in this new age, no life is safe, and Chen Xiao’s response—absolute destruction—sets the tone for his war against the oppressors.
