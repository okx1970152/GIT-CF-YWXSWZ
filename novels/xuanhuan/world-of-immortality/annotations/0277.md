## Chapter Overview

Xiao Chen has returned to his home village in the mortal world after eight years of struggle in the World of Immortality. This chapter juxtaposes the quiet intimacy of family reunion with the harsh reality of the nation's forced labor under the state religion. As Xiao Chen heals his aging parents and confronts local bullies, he cannot help but notice the colossal constructions along the Yellow River—the Ancestral Dragon Platform and the Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge. A sense of unease grows within him, leading him to investigate the river's ancient secrets. This is a chapter of reflection, resilience, and revelation, where personal ties and cosmic mysteries intertwine.

## Key Plot Points

Under the serene moonlight, Xiao Chen stealthily enters his parents' room and channels life essence into their bodies, restoring their vitality. He then drifts through the night, reflecting on the past eight years and his return. At dawn, he accompanies his parents to the forced labor site, where a local thug tries to intimidate him. Xiao Chen's mere gaze sends the man to his knees, revealing his overwhelming power. He reunites with old acquaintances—now turned overseers—and learns that several childhood friends have died at the hands of bullies from neighboring counties. In a chilling display of his might, Xiao Chen makes those bullies jump one by one into the Yellow River, never to surface. He leverages his notorious reputation to secure the exemption of his entire village from the labor tax. He then explores the river, observing the giant platform and bridge under construction, and begins to suspect that the Yellow River itself might be an Ancestral Dragon. He remembers the Heavenly Stele that gave him his cultivation method and resolves to investigate it. The chapter closes with him diving into the river's depths.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of the Yellow River as an 'Ancestral Dragon' is rooted in Chinese mythology, where the river is often seen as a dragon that needs to be tamed. The 'Ancestral Dragon Platform' (祖龙台) and 'Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge' (通天死桥) are mystical constructions that likely serve a grand, perhaps sacrificial, purpose. Xiao Chen's ability to heal his parents by channeling life essence is a classic xianxia trope—a returning cultivator nurturing his mortal family with his power. The oppressive state religion that forces villagers into heavy labor reflects the dark side of mortal world politics, a common backdrop in xianxia narratives. Xiao Chen's use of pure intimidation, based on his cultivation reputation, shows how a powerful cultivator can dominate the mundane world without resorting to overt violence.

## Reading Guide

This chapter provides a well-needed respite from the high-stakes battles of the World of Immortality, but it is far from a mere break. The mysteries planted here—the purpose of the grand constructions, the true nature of the Yellow River, and the fate of the Heavenly Stele—will likely pay off in later arcs. Pay close attention to the contrast between the peaceful village life and the underlying tension of the state religion's schemes. Xiao Chen's emotional state is subdued; he is haunted by the possibility that Qingqing may have been swept into the space sea. This personal worry may influence his decisions going forward. The chapter also reaffirms Xiao Chen's moral compass: he protects his own, punishes bullies, but does not overturn the system. His investigation of the stele is a thread to follow.
