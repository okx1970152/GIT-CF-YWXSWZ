## Chapter Overview

This chapter shifts from the epic battle between the ancient human ancestor Taihao and the supremely powerful Qinglian Tiannü to Xiao Chen's desperate plan. Witnessing Taihao struggle against Qinglian Tiannü's overwhelming collection of sacred artifacts—four war swords, nine green jade lotus leaves, and the twelve-grade lotus platform—Xiao Chen realizes the depth of the threat. Grief for Ruoshui surfaces powerfully, and he resolves to take drastic action: return to Dragon Island through the sealed portal at Lushan, using the jade bracelet as a key. With Taihao's aid, he breaches the gateway, arriving back in the savage, power-suppressed world of Dragon Island. The chapter sets the stage for a high-stakes rescue mission and a confrontation with the forces oppressing the Nine Provinces.

## Key Plot Points

- The battle reveals that Qinglian Tiannü possesses multiple supreme artifacts, making her nearly invincible even against Taihao's Haotian Pagoda, which once sealed three half-ancestors.
- Xiao Chen throws the Golden Divine Halberd and Dark-Iron Seal to Taihao, but they are damaged and useless against Qinglian Tiannü's weapons.
- Xiao Chen's suppressed grief for Ruoshui erupts; he realizes he never truly cut her from his heart. This emotional pain fuels his determination to kill Qinglian Tiannü.
- He devises a plan to go to the World of Immortality to borrow troops. He needs Taihao's help to force open the Lushan portal, guarded by the mysterious Tong Hu.
- Using the jade bracelet, Xiao Chen successfully enters the space tunnel and emerges on Dragon Island, where his cultivation is suppressed to the Spirit-Treasury Ninth Heaven.
- He reaffirms his dual goal: rescue Qingqing (the captive girl) and, more importantly, rescue the little white beast Keke from its lonely fate.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The distinction between Taihao and Fuxi is a significant piece of Chinese mythological scholarship. Historically, pre-Qin texts treat them as separate figures: Taihao was the leader of the Eastern Yi tribes, while Fuxi was a later cultural hero. The conflation began with Liu Xin of the Western Han for political reasons. In this novel, Chen Dong uses this ambiguity to create a powerful human ancestor who is not a full Ancestral God but a mortal sage with immense cultivation. The Haotian Pagoda is a legendary artifact from Chinese myth, said to suppress three half-ancestors. Qinglian Tiannü's artifacts echo Buddhist and Daoist relics (twelve-grade lotus, war swords). The suppression of divine power on Dragon Island is a recurring mechanic; even a half-god like Xiao Chen falls to the realm of Spirit-Treasury. The jade bracelet as a key to the World of Immortality reflects the classic xianxia trope of a secret passage.
