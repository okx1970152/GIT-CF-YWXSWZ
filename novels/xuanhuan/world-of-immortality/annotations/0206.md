## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a devastating emotional climax in the Dragon Island arc. Keke, the innocent and playful white beast who has been Xiao Chen's constant companion, is killed before his eyes by the combined might of six demigod-level experts from the Tiger and Sea families. The scene is one of relentless, brutal despair: Keke repeatedly tries to carry Xiao Chen to safety, only to be struck down again and again until it has no strength left. The raw grief that Xiao Chen experiences—tearing a scream of agony from a man who rarely shows emotion—marks a turning point in his character. Yet even in this nadir, the chapter hints at cosmic forces at play. Keke's death triggers unnatural phenomena: flower rain, Buddhist chants, apocalyptic hymns, and sacrificial dirges. These omens suggest that Keke is far more than an ordinary spirit beast, and that the Tiger family may have unleashed a catastrophe upon themselves.

## Key Plot Points
Six demigods—four from the Sea family and two from the Tiger family (the Tiger slave and Tiger attendant)—have trapped Xiao Chen and Keke in a sealed domain. Keke refuses to abandon Xiao Chen, using its rainbow light to shield him while repeatedly charging the barrier. Each time it is thrown back, wounded more grievously. Xiao Chen, himself severely injured, begs Keke to escape alone, but the little beast stubbornly refuses. After numerous failed attempts, Keke is completely exhausted. With a final, tender glance at Xiao Chen, it launches itself at the Tiger slave, who cuts it down with a palm strike. Keke's body disappears in a burst of seven-colored light, and the world responds: a rain of flower petals falls, Buddhist chanting and apocalyptic hymns fill the air, and sacrificial sounds echo. This phenomenon is said to occur only when beings on the verge of half-ancestor status are slain. Xiao Chen, shattered, makes a desperate vow: he offers his own soul to any ancestral god, willing to endure a thousand lifetimes of suffering in exchange for Keke's life. The Sacred Tree, which Keke had carried, descends into Xiao Chen's hands, pulsing with light.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The appearance of celestial phenomena at a spirit beast's death is extraordinarily rare in xianxia cosmology. Here, the combination of Buddhist chanting, apocalyptic hymns, and sacrificial dirges signals that Keke's origin is tied to the highest echelons of the divine—a level associated with half-ancestors like Buddha or Laozi. The Tiger family's demigods are not ordinary cultivators; they are at the Mortal-Shedding Ninth Heaven, which allows them to manipulate domain fields and exert crushing pressure. Xiao Chen's vow—a soul-offering to an unnamed ancestral god—is a dangerous and desperate act in cultivation lore, as such pacts can bind one's fate across reincarnations. The Sacred Tree's descent to Xiao Chen after Keke's death suggests a transfer of loyalty or a last gift, a common motif in xianxia where a bond transcends death.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for a major shift in Xiao Chen's motivations. His grief and vow are not just emotional; they are a narrative promise that Keke's death will have consequences. The Tiger family's fear after the celestial omens suggests that Keke's origin may involve forces they cannot contend with, potentially drawing in higher powers. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen's vow manifests—will it grant him power, or bring a terrible price? The presence of Dugu Jianmo observing from a distance hints that other factions are aware of the event. The Sacred Tree's new role in Xiao Chen's hands may unlock new abilities. The arc is moving toward a confrontation with the Tiger family and perhaps a revelation about Keke's true nature. The tragic tone underscores that in this world, even the most innocent can fall, but their sacrifice can reshape destiny.
