## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges into one of the most harrowing cultivation gambits in the novel. Xiao Chen, having stolen the divine fruit of the Otherworld Ancestral God Qiu Tian, now faces the monumental challenge of assimilating the power without losing himself. Rather than merging with the alien flesh, he dares to execute the forbidden Sacred Sacrifice technique from the Martial Ancestor's True Scripture—reversing the natural order of cultivation by offering a temporary 'self' (the Ancestral God's body) to empower his own true form. The process is depicted with ritualistic dread, as the ancestral god's corpse seems to awaken, and a rival Ancestral God, Al, senses the anomaly from afar. The tension is twofold: the internal peril of the sacrifice and the external threat of discovery.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen decisively rejects merging with Qiu Tian's flesh through the traditional Shatter Demon, Plant God method. Instead, he invokes the Martial Ancestor's forbidden Sacred Sacrifice, which involves a life-for-power exchange: he projects his consciousness and life essence into the Ancestral God's body, making it his temporary 'self'. Then, he begins to ritually burn that body, channeling its vast divine power into his original, now-empty shell. This process is graphically described—the ancestral god's body glows with sacrificial fire, ancient chants fill the dimension, and the power is transferred like a river of light. Meanwhile, in the real world, the Otherworld Ancestral God Al, ever watchful of his rival, senses something wrong. He sends a massive divine hand to tear open the sealed dimension, but the ninth-heaven half-ancestor guarding the seal delays him, buying Xiao Chen precious time. The chapter ends with the sacrifice ongoing and the external danger mounting.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Sacred Sacrifice (圣祭) is a stark departure from common cultivation logic. While most methods seize power from external sources and refine it, the Sacred Sacrifice gives power away—to an 'external object'—only to reclaim it transformed. It is described as 'reverse cultivation,' aligning with the Daoist principle of wu wei (effortless action) turned on its head. The technique requires a supremely powerful temporary vessel to sacrifice; here, the Ancestral God's body serves that role. This echoes the ancient Chinese concept of 'jisheng' (sacrificial offering) where a surrogate is offered to appease fate. The ritual details—ancient chants, burning divine light, the nine-syllable mantra—draw from Buddhist and Daoist liturgical traditions, reinforcing the novel's syncretic mythology. The tension between Al and the half-ancestor also illustrates the strict hierarchy of the Otherworld: even a ninth-heaven half-ancestor is no more than an 'ant' to an Ancestral God.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up a pivotal power transition for Xiao Chen. The Sacred Sacrifice is a high-risk, high-reward gamble; if he succeeds, he will likely reach a new level of cultivation, perhaps approaching Half-Ancestor. However, the unfinished sacrifice leaves him vulnerable, and Al's intervention is only a matter of time. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen stabilizes his new body and whether the transferred power is truly his own. The confrontation between Al and the half-ancestor also hints at deeper political games among the Otherworld gods. Additionally, the mention of the Martial Ancestor's forbidden techniques suggests that Xiao Chen's martial path is becoming increasingly unorthodox, straying from both the conventional and the divine. The next chapters will likely test the fruits of this sacrifice against real battle.
