## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks the long-awaited full introduction of Keke, the mysterious white beast who has been hinted at in earlier chapters. The tone shifts from Xiao Chen's grim survival struggles to a mix of wonder and unease as he encounters a being whose power far exceeds his expectations. The sacred tree, which Xiao Chen had stolen and cultivated, is reclaimed by its rightful owner—a tiny, adorable creature that proves to be anything but helpless. The scene balances childlike innocence with startling violence as Keke dismantles the three skeletons and hurls Xiao Chen around with telekinetic force. The chapter is both charming and unsettling, deepening the mythic mystery of Dragon Island.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen returns to the swamp with the newly rescued white beast, only to find the three skeletons acting strangely—standing rigid as if terrified. The skeletons' fear is immediately linked to the tiny creature, though Xiao Chen dismisses this possibility at first. The white beast, previously appearing pathetic, springs into action: it grabs the Little Sacred Tree, pulls it from its host, and begins sucking energy from its jade leaves, revealing its true ownership of the tree. After sating its hunger, the beast turns on the skeletons, dismantling them limb by limb with eerie precision and interrogating them with animated gestures. The skeletons, caught betraying their master, point at Xiao Chen to shift blame. In response, the beast uses a telekinetic white light to repeatedly toss Xiao Chen into the air despite his attempts to stabilize. The sequence reveals the beast's immense power and intelligence, terrifying the skeletons and shocking Xiao Chen. This encounter reshapes Xiao Chen's understanding of the island's hierarchy—he is no longer the apex predator in this region. The sacred tree's true owner has arrived, and the power dynamic on Dragon Island shifts.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The 'Little Sacred Tree' is a classic xianxia trope: a miraculous spiritual plant that cultivators fight over for its concentrated life essence. In this chapter, the tree has two jade leaves of different colors, each containing vast energy. The white beast's method of 'suckling' the leaves is reminiscent of how infant divine beasts absorb primordial energy in Chinese mythology. The three skeletons, though undead, display complex social behaviors: they feign death, show fear, and even conspire to betray Xiao Chen. Their reaction to the white beast suggests that Keke possesses an innate authority over the dead or a power that threatens their existence. The beast's ability to dismantle them without harming the bones themselves indicates precise control. This juxtaposition of cute appearance and devastating power is a hallmark of high-level divine beasts in xianxia literature—never judge a spirit creature by its size.
