## Chapter Overview

This chapter offers a poignant contrast between lighthearted bonding and bone-chilling mystery. After the harrowing battle on the snowy peak, Xiao Chen finds Keke asleep atop a magnificent snow lotus, its spiritual radiance illuminating the peak. The playful interplay between Xiao Chen and Keke—over the little beast’s bloated belly and its refusal to allow the sacred tree inside it—provides a moment of warmth and humor in the grim world of Dragon Island. However, the mood shifts drastically when Xiao Chen decides to bury dragon eggs near ancient ruins and instead unearths three identical ice coffins, each containing a wrinkled, hollow human skin. The discovery of fresh blood in the third coffin leaves a lingering sense of dread and raises unsettling questions about the island’s hidden history.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen searches the snowy mountain for Keke and the three skeletons. He finds them on a peak dominated by a house-sized snow lotus radiating divine light, with Keke sleeping at its center. Upon waking, Keke, still bloated from life nectar, digs out a fist-sized snow lotus heart—a crystalline treasure—and reluctantly gives half to Xiao Chen for safekeeping. Xiao Chen offers to help Keke absorb excess nectar using the sacred tree, but Keke vehemently refuses. They then explore ancient palace ruins on a distant peak, deciding to bury the dragon eggs there. While digging, Xiao Chen uncovers three ice coffins aligned neatly. The first two contain identical wrinkled old skins with the same features and birthmarks. The third also holds an identical skin but with a few drops of seemingly fresh blood. The uncanny repetition and lack of bodies suggest something far more sinister than a simple burial ground. Xiao Chen is left deeply unsettled, as the mystery of the skins and the blood hints at dark secrets connected to the ancient ruins and perhaps to the island's past cultivation civilization.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The snow lotus (雪莲) is a classic ingredient in Chinese xianxia and traditional medicine, revered for its life-extending and spiritual properties. Here it is portrayed as a massive spiritual treasure, its heart a crystalline essence that refines vitality. The sacred tree (宝树/小圣树) functions as a spiritual tool that can be absorbed into a cultivator’s body, forming energy channels with acupoints—a concept rooted in Daoist internal alchemy, where the body’s meridian system is used to refine and circulate qi. The ice coffins and preserved human skins evoke the Daoist practice of “shijie” (尸解) or liberation from the corpse, where an immortal sheds the mortal shell like a cicada. However, the identical skins defy explanation and suggest an artificial or forbidden technique, possibly tied to the island’s ancient sealing formation.
