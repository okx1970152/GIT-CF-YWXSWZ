## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks a significant personal milestone for Xiao Chen as he completes his cultivation of the Undying Wings at the Eighth Heaven of Mortal-Shedding, granting him unparalleled speed and aerial supremacy. Yet the chapter is not solely about martial achievement; it is layered with subtle humor, poignant observations of the bond between him and his divine companions, and a sharp turn toward crisis when the little divine beast Keke's rampant thievery from the Undying Sect's herbal garden forces Xiao Chen to consider departure. The tone balances the solemn pride of accomplishment with the warmth of domestic scenes and a stark undercurrent of political complication, culminating in a stunning proposal from the Undying Sect's patriarch that leaves Xiao Chen and the reader equally staggered.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen emerges from three days of meditation having fully mastered the Undying Wings, a pair of crystalline purple energy wings that can also be used as formidable weapons. With them, he can fly with incredible speed, rendering many spellmasters and spiritists powerless against him. He observes the little defiant dragon training fiercely with the adult winged dragon, learning ancient dragon combat techniques and being tempered through brutal but controlled sparring. Keke returns after days of absence, carrying stolen treasures from the Undying Sect's sacred herbal garden—including rare ginseng, purple crystal pears, and golden lotus—and reveals she has been raiding the garden systematically, treating it as her personal larder. Xiao Chen is exasperated by this discovery and concludes they must leave the sect before the theft is discovered. He decides to return a sacred scripture he found to the sect leader, explaining his cultivation of the Undying Wings. The Undying Patriarch, unsurprised and unfazed, thanks him and unexpectedly offers to appoint Xiao Chen as an external protector of the sect. The chapter closes with the patriarch dropping a thunderbolt: he asks Xiao Chen whether he would take Yan Qingcheng as his wife.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Undying Wings, a crystallization of the Undying Demon Art, represent a pinnacle of movement-based divine technique. In xianxia cosmology, the ability to fly—especially with such a manifestation of pure energy—places a practitioner in a different tier of combat, overcoming the advantage that aerial mages once held. The cultivation process involves not only energy accumulation but also comprehension of the art's essence, which Xiao Chen achieved through insight rather than brute force. The scene of the little defiant dragon sparring with an adult dragon who takes human form and precisely inflicts wounds to temper the youngling's body illustrates a training method common in ancient beast lineages: deliberate injury to stimulate latent bloodline powers and refine physical resilience. Keke's theft of herbs underscores a recurring trope: spirit beasts (particularly those of divine or primordial lineage) possess an innate attraction to celestial medicines, often consuming them like common food, which both amuses and alarms their human companions.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a turning point for Xiao Chen on Dragon Island. With his newfound flight ability, his tactical options expand dramatically. However, the contented atmosphere is a lull before a storm: Keke's theft will inevitably lead to conflict with the Undying Sect, and Xiao Chen's decision to leave is wise but may be complicated by the patriarch's marriage proposal. The proposal itself is a shocking development that intertwines Xiao Chen's fate with Yan Qingcheng's—a woman whose pride he has repeatedly crushed and who now, as his captive and potential bride, presents an unpredictable dynamic. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen navigates this diplomatic trap: does he refuse and offend a powerful clan, or accept and bind himself to a resentful enemy? The seeds of future alliances and enmities are sown in this quiet chapter.
