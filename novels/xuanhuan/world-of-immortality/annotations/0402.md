## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges back into the eerie depths of the King Ship—the colossal skull-vessel that drifts across the golden Forbidden Sea. As the Five-Colored Angel King and his warriors attempt to probe its interior, they encounter a silent, absolute annihilation that claims three powerful Angel Generals without a trace. Meanwhile, Keke, emboldened by the safety of the Lost Paradise, guides Xiao Chen and the God Tribe elders into the heart of the skull. Within the void-like space, they discover a withered corpse of unimaginable antiquity—a being whose mere presence corrupts the divine realm of the Lost Paradise and destroys any artifact that touches it. This chapter builds a palpable atmosphere of dread and mystery, revealing that even in the death world, some horrors transcend the known hierarchy of kings and half-ancestors.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter begins with the Five-Colored Angel King and his retinue entering the golden Forbidden Sea through a spatial portal. They witness the enormous black skull-ship (the King Ship) and feel its crushing aura. The Angel King sends a warrior to investigate; the warrior boards the skull but vanishes instantly upon entering the interior. Two more are sent; one vanishes silently, the other lets out a brief cry before being swallowed. The Angel King and his forces realize the King Ship is lethally dangerous. Xiao Chen, meanwhile, remembers that Chiyou, Laozi, and the Buddha had all traveled on this ship without exploring its interior, hinting at its peril. Keke recalls a childhood incident when she was frightened by something unclean inside the ship. To demonstrate, Keke uses the Lost Paradise to approach the skull's interior. Xiao Chen, Li Mu, Zhao Ying, and the Angel King follow within the protective realm. Inside, they find a vast, empty darkness that instantly disintegrates a probe sent from the Lost Paradise. A mummified corpse floats at the center, seemingly ageless and emitting a chilling aura. When an Angel General attempts to cut the corpse's hair with a diamond bone sword, the sword turns to dust and the warrior himself disintegrates. The corpse remains untouched, radiating a power that can corrupt even the sanctified space of the Lost Paradise. The chapter ends with all present recognizing the corpse's terrifying nature, realizing they have ventured into a domain that far exceeds their understanding of life and death.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The King Ship (君王船) is a recurring artifact in World of Immortality, first introduced as a vessel capable of crossing the sealed Forbidden Sea. It is built from the bone of an Ancestral Dragon and wood of the World Tree, but its true origin and purpose remain mysterious. In this chapter, its skull-form is explored as a possible tomb or prison for an entity that surpasses even the 'king' (君王) level. The concept of 'transcending the king realm' (超越君王) refers to a cultivation level above the ordinary king-tier among fire-seed beings in the death world. The Five-Colored Angel King is a Perfected Man (至人) realm being, yet even he is cautious. The Lost Paradise (失乐园) is Keke's inherited divine realm, capable of sealing away laws and divine techniques. That the corpse can still exert influence inside it suggests the corpse's power may rival or exceed that of an Ancestral God. The scene where the Angel General's diamond bone sword (彩钻骨刀) disintegrates upon touching the corpse's hair illustrates the concept of 'absolute qi' or 'doctrine of supreme authority'—a being's residual power can annihilate anything that does not equal it in rank. This chapter deepens the novel's themes of mortality and the unfathomable scale of ancient powers.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up a major mystery: Who is the mummified corpse in the King Ship? It is clearly a being of extraordinary might, likely an Ancestral God or something even older. The fact that Keke's Lost Paradise can be affected hints that the corpse may be connected to the primordial secrets of the Nine Provinces and the cycle of life and death. Readers should pay attention to Xiao Chen's growing understanding of the death world's hierarchy and the hints that the King Ship is more than a mere vessel. The chapter also reinforces Keke's bravery and resourcefulness; she is no longer just a cute pet but a proactive explorer. In upcoming chapters, the mystery of the corpse may drive the plot toward the next major conflict. Also, note that the Angel King and his forces are present; their reaction to the corpse may influence their intentions toward Xiao Chen and the God Tribe. The atmosphere of dread and the absolute power of the corpse suggest that the story is moving toward a revelation about the nature of true death and transcendence.
