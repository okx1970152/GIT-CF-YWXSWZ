## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks a significant expansion of the Death World's lore as Xiao Chen, accompanied by the half-ancestors Tianwaitian and Renwairen, ventures deeper into the unknown territories. The tone balances the desolate, mythic atmosphere of the bone plains with moments of dry humor provided by Jin Sanyi and Shapolang. The central mystery revolves around a fearsome evil corpse—an entity of Ancestral God-level power—that has been visiting sovereign cities to seize fragments of an ancient oracle bone diagram. This revelation sets the stage for a potentially world-altering discovery hidden in the Death World's heart.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen arrives at the Divine Village with Jin Sanyi, who immediately pairs up with the purple-diamond skeleton Shapolang in a comic display of mutual lechery. Tianwaitian and Renwairen recognize Shapolang as a long-lost disciple, though they choose not to restore his memories. The two half-ancestors decide to aid him by having the blood-corpse worm's essence purified for his cultivation.

After a half-year journey, the group reaches the sovereign city of Lian Po, the seven-colored skeleton king. There, they learn that the evil corpse has been appearing across multiple sovereign cities, searching for something. Further investigation reveals that the corpse nearly killed a sovereign king to steal a fragment of an oracle bone diagram, a treasure of unknown origin but clearly of immense importance. This development sends Xiao Chen and his companions deeper into the continent, tracing the corpse's path.

Tianwaitian and Renwairen also observe that the Death World contains remnants of a Great Wall, and they speculate that this world may be a 'cage built from worlds.' They continue their journey for two more years, eventually sensing an overwhelming, destructive power from the plains ahead, suggesting an imminent confrontation or discovery.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The oracle bone diagram (甲骨图) is a concept rooted in ancient Chinese divination, where oracle bones were used for prophecy. In this chapter, it is a fragment of a mystical map that holds secrets powerful enough to attract a quasi-Ancestral God-level threat. The evil corpse itself is a classic undead trope in Chinese mythology—a powerful being that has transcended death but retains a singular, obsessive purpose.

The 'cage built from worlds' notion echoes themes from Daoist cosmology and the concept of the Primordial Celestial Realm. Tianwaitian and Renwairen, as half-ancestors, represent the highest echelon of mortal cultivation, their dialogue hinting at cosmic truths about the nature of reality. The Death World's hierarchy is further fleshed out with sovereign-level kings (君王) and emperors (皇), illustrating a clear power ladder from fire-seed beings to near-ancestral gods.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a pivotal lore-building segment, setting up the Death World's deepest secrets and the evil corpse as a major force. Pay attention to the oracle bone diagram—it may lead to the true origins of the Death World or even the path to the Primordial Celestial Realm. The evil corpse's actions suggest a central mystery that will drive future arcs.

Character dynamics are also key: Jin Sanyi and Shapolang provide comic relief, but their bond may become significant. Tianwaitian's decision to not restore Shapolang's memories indicates a philosophical depth: the past matters less than the present path. Xiao Chen's cultivation continues in the background, but his main role here is as an observer and seeker of knowledge.
