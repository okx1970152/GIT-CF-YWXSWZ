## Chapter Overview
This chapter drops the reader into the heart of Heavenly Emperor City's pleasure quarter, where the simmering conflict between Xiao Chen and the wild warrior Yu Wenfeng finally erupts. The setting—the opulent, sensory-overloaded Drunken Beauty Palace and its tranquil Bright Lake—provides a stark contrast to the brutal, no-holds-barred martial confrontation that follows. Xiao Chen, having recovered from his injuries, initiates the duel, driven by the imperative to face an opponent who had previously humiliated him at the city gates. The scene crackles with tension, moving from elegant wine cups and courtesan music to earth-shattering punches and flying blood. It is a chapter that showcases the core ethos of the World of Immortality: battle is the crucible of growth, and pride must be carved out with one's own strength. The emotional tone is one of grim determination and primal fury, as both fighters unleash their full power in a contest that will reverberate through the city's young elite.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen, having healed and deliberately sought out Yu Wenfeng, confronts him at the Drunken Beauty Palace. The confrontation is immediate and direct: there is no negotiation, only the clear purpose of battle. Yu Wenfeng, a bare-chested, beast-like fighter, is confident and openly states he had intended to hunt Xiao Chen down anyway. The two agree to a duel at the nearby Bright Lake, with the courtesans Bing Qin and Huo Wu offering to provide musical accompaniment, a ritualized backdrop for high-stakes combat. The fight begins with explosive force: Yu Wenfeng's raw power is immense, each punch causing the ground to crack and the lake to surge. Xiao Chen matches him with calculated ferocity, employing the Heaven-Execution Step (tian sha bu), a dangerous technique that distorts space with each step. Yu Wenfeng is driven into the earth but recovers swiftly. The battle moves onto the water, with both fighters using lotus leaves as footholds, engaging in a near-flight-level exchange. Desperate to gain the upper hand, Yu Wenfeng unleashes his Blood Domain, a field of murderous energy infused with bestial souls, a rare ability for a Mortal-Shedding cultivator. In response, Xiao Chen counters with his own defensive field—the Big Dipper light screen, which solidifies into ancient armor. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen fully armored, poised to meet the monstrous onslaught, leaving the outcome uncertain. The fight is framed as a collision of two unstoppable wills: Xiao Chen's relentless advance versus Yu Wenfeng's savage, beast-driven power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter illustrates several core concepts of xianxia cultivation. The 'field' (chang yu) is a spatial phenomenon that manifests the cultivator's innermost power; it is typically associated with the Spirit-Treasury realm but can emerge earlier in exceptional prodigies. Yu Wenfeng's Blood Domain, formed by training his Ten Thousand Beasts Demonic Art among real primordial beasts and even slaying ancient beast souls, demonstrates how a cultivator's environment and choices shape their unique abilities. Xiao Chen's counter-field, the Big Dipper light curtain, is derived from his foundational Heavenly Stele Profound Art, emphasizing the importance of a solid, ancient foundation. The Heaven-Execution Step is a publicly known but extremely difficult technique; its mastery is a testament to Xiao Chen's physical control and tactical acumen. The duel setting—a pleasure palace with a lake—highlights a common xianxia trope where extravagant social venues double as arenas for martial pride and deadly competition. The music played by the courtesans is not mere entertainment; in such settings, it often reflects or intensifies the emotional and energetic flow of battle, a practice rooted in ancient Chinese literary traditions.
