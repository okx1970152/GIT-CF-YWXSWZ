## Chapter Overview

This chapter takes us deep into the underworld of the World of Immortality, where Xiao Chen pursues a mysterious red diamond skeleton—a sentient undead being with a human consciousness and an infuriatingly arrogant personality. The chapter balances desperate combat with darkly comedic dialogue, introducing a memorable new character while advancing Xiao Chen's cultivation. The tone is a blend of mythic desolation and wry humor, as the Red Diamond King's antics provide a stark contrast to the grimness of the dead world. Beneath the comedy, the chapter reinforces the theme of fragmented identity: the Red Diamond King, like many beings in the underworld, has lost most of his memories, clinging only to a vague sense of having been from the Nine Provinces.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen's pursuit of the Red Diamond King spans most of the chapter. The red skeleton proves to be an elusive and powerful opponent, matching Xiao Chen in speed and combat skill. Their initial confrontation is a long chase punctuated by brief exchanges—the Red Diamond King tries to bargain, boast, and even philosophize, while Xiao Chen remains coldly determined to extract information. After a prolonged struggle, Xiao Chen disassembles the skeleton without breaking its bones, forcing it to talk. The Red Diamond King claims to remember only that he was from Jingzhou (a province of the ancient Nine Provinces) and that he was drawn into a lamp-lit underground ruin where he died and awoke in this world. Suspicious but unable to extract more, Xiao Chen spares him and proposes an alliance. To prove his worth, the Red Diamond King leads Xiao Chen to a crumbling fortress held by three powerful fire-seed creatures: a death angel, a bone scorpion, and a giant skeleton. Together they defeat the trio, and Xiao Chen absorbs one of their fire seeds, finally gathering the thirty seeds needed to break through to a new realm. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen's triumphant ascension—his skeleton blazes like a blood-red sun—and his resolve to return to the mortal world to confront his enemies.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of 'fire seeds' (火种) is central to the underworld cultivation system in this novel. These are the condensed life force/soul essence of powerful undead beings. Collecting thirty same-tier fire seeds allows a cultivator to advance to the next level. This mirrors the 'spirit core' or 'inner core' mechanics common in xianxia, but here it is tied explicitly to the death realm's unique ecology. The Red Diamond King's mention of being drawn into a lamp-lit ruin connects to the Nine Ancient Lamps of the Nine Provinces—an artifact series introduced earlier that can trap souls. The chapter also highlights the theme of memory loss: in the underworld, most beings have only fragmented recollections of their past lives, making the Red Diamond King's partial recall significant. His boasting and dramatic mannerisms evoke the classic 'rogue' character archetype from wuxia, providing levity in an otherwise grim setting.

## Reading Guide

This chapter deepens the lore of the underworld while setting up Xiao Chen's imminent return to the mortal world. The Red Diamond King is clearly intended to be a recurring character—his unique sentience, humor, and mysterious past make him a valuable ally or potential wildcard. Readers should watch for how his loyalty develops. Xiao Chen's breakthrough marks a crucial power-up; his next steps will likely involve using the bone well to cross into the living world with a more substantial manifestation. The chapter also teases the Nine Ancient Lamps and the subterranean ruins, hinting at a larger connection between the Nine Provinces and the dead realm. The contrasting tones—Xiao Chen's grim determination versus the Red Diamond King's flamboyance—create a dynamic that enriches the narrative. Pay attention to the Red Diamond King's occasional slips of seriousness; they suggest depths he is not yet ready to reveal.
