## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks one of the most emotionally devastating turning points in the entire narrative. The tone is one of profound, inescapable sorrow—a tragic farewell under the shadow of inevitable departure. The chapter opens with Xiao Chen's inner turmoil as he struggles against the seven demonic diagrams that seal off the Primordial Ancient Village, cutting him off from his parents and the chance to alter fate. After a failed attempt to harness the power of the Dead City and the Yellow Clay Altar, he must accept reality. The bulk of the chapter is set at the Yellow Crane Tower, where Xiao Chen meets old friends for a final gathering before the Ancestral Dragon Ship arrives to carry the chosen away. The mood is heavy with the weight of three hundred years of history, lost love, and the certainty that most of those present will never see each other again. The chapter ends with the ship taking the elites away, the words 'See you in thirty thousand years' echoing with a hollow promise.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Xiao Chen standing atop the Dead City, attempting to use the power of the demonic well and the yellow-clay altar to tear open the seven demonic diagrams and reach the Primordial Ancient Village. His efforts fail spectacularly—the power of seven Otherworld Ancestral Gods is too overwhelming, and his third eye cracks, causing blood to flow. Realizing the futility, he descends from the city in despair. Later, the horn of the Ancestral Dragon Ship sounds across the land, and a list of those chosen to leave is prepared. Xiao Chen, along with Jin Sanyi, Bo Shi, Bai Qi, Fatty Zhuge, Totti, Wu Ming, and others, leaves the village to wander the Nine Provinces and the Four Worlds one last time. Three days later, Xiao Chen, the Inverse Dragon King, Liu Mu, and Niu Ren head toward the Nine Provinces and stop at the Yellow Crane Tower. There, they meet many current and former enemies from the Hundred Races, including the Earth King and Sea God King, but Xiao Chen's presence is now so overwhelming that none dare to attack. The farewells are heart-wrenching: Liu Mu recites a modified poem by Li Bai, implying his own death is certain; Niu Ren asks Xiao Chen to remember him with a drink of wine poured on the ground. Then Chen Fang, Xiao Chen's old friend from the mortal world, arrives with his son Chen Ziteng, having become mortal again after two hundred years. Chen Fang's words about enduring friendship despite imminent death break Xiao Chen's composure, and he leaves the tower drunk, weeping and singing in sorrow as he walks the Yangtze River. Finally, the Ancestral Dragon Ship appears, sweeping chosen cultivators away. Xiao Chen is among them. As the ship sails beyond the heavens, two Ancestral Gods on the bow declare that they will return in thirty thousand years.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter is steeped in classical Chinese cultural motifs. The Yellow Crane Tower is a famous historical and poetic site, immortalized by the Tang dynasty poet Li Bai, whose farewell poem is paraphrased by Liu Mu. The poem captures the essence of eternal separation, making it a poignant choice for this scene. The concept of the 'Ancestral Dragon Ship' is tied to earlier lore—a vessel built from the bones of an Ancestral Dragon and World Tree wood, capable of crossing the sealed Forbidden Sea. Now it serves a new purpose: evacuating elites to a distant realm for thirty thousand years. The 'seven demonic diagrams' represent the power of Otherworld Ancestral Gods, emphasizing the overwhelming might of the alien invaders. Xiao Chen's use of the Dead City's power connects back to his earlier cultivation of the Heavenly Steles and the Stone Man. The chapter also highlights the theme of '三万年后再相见' (meet again in thirty thousand years), a staggering time span that underscores the tragic scale of the novel's conflicts.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a crucial emotional reset before the narrative transitions into a new phase. Pay close attention to the farewells—each character's parting words carry immense weight. Liu Mu's poem foreshadows his likely death, as he is only at the Perfected Man realm and not chosen for the ship. Niu Ren's simple request for remembrance reminds us that even the strongest bonds will be severed by time and death. The reappearance of Chen Fang after two hundred years highlights the cost of Xiao Chen's long cultivation—most of his mortal friends are long gone. The Ancestral Dragon Ship's departure marks the end of the 'current era' of the story. What awaits Xiao Chen and the other elites in the void for thirty thousand years? The phrase 'three thousand years' earlier becomes 'thirty thousand', emphasizing a much grander and more desperate scale. This chapter sets up a colossal timeskip, and readers should prepare for a completely transformed world when the story resumes. Keep an eye on Xiao Chen's emotional state—his drunken grief and despair may foreshadow a changed protagonist.
