## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers a high-stakes duel between Xiao Chen and the scheming artifact refiner Wu Ming, showcasing the devastating power of Xiao Chen's newly mastered second Origin Heavenly Sound, the 'Duo' tone. The clash is not merely a test of strength but a negotiation through violence—both warriors know they cannot permanently destroy the other with their current arsenals, yet each seeks to establish dominance before entering a fragile alliance. The fight crackles with tactical brilliance as Xiao Chen exploits the synergy between the 'Weng' and 'Duo' sounds, forming a barrier of crystallized syllables that repels the legendary Heavenly Thunder Hammer. Wu Ming's vaunted Indestructible Imperial Heavenly Bell is repeatedly pierced and shattered, forcing him to acknowledge Xiao Chen's supremacy. The chapter balances brutal combat with political calculation, as the two agree to a temporary partnership aimed at eliminating the Five Element Heavenly Kings, while secretly planning future betrayal. Meanwhile, the Five Element Heavenly Kings themselves conspire to use a woman—possibly a doppelgänger of Qingqing—as bait to lure Xiao Chen into a trap.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Xiao Chen's half War Sword piercing Wu Ming's Indestructible Imperial Heavenly Bell, a shocking breach of the bell's legendary defense. Wu Ming's surprise quickly turns to desperation as Xiao Chen presses the attack, using the War Sword to carve through the bell repeatedly. Wu Ming merges fully with the bell to gain near-immortality, but this only subjects him to the agony of continuous shattering and reformation. Xiao Chen unleashes the 'Duo' sound alongside the 'Weng' sound, manifesting dozens of materialized syllables that form an impregnable barrier around him, deflecting the Heavenly Thunder Hammer—a half-ancestor treasure—and eventually driving it back. The tide of battle shifts completely; Xiao Chen physically overwhelms Wu Ming, delivering a humiliating beating that includes a kick to the stomach, a palm strike to the head, and a stamp on the chest. Wu Ming attempts a blood-escape technique to flee, but Xiao Chen halts him with a proposal for cooperation. After tense negotiation, they agree to temporarily ally to eliminate the Five Element Heavenly Kings (Gold King, Wood King, Sea God King, Fire Spirit King, Earth King). Xiao Chen returns Wu Ming's captive niece as a gesture of goodwill. Separately, the Five Element Heavenly Kings plan to use a resurrected woman (presumably an illusion of Qingqing) to lure Xiao Chen into a fatal ambush, revealing their awareness of his weakness.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The 'Duo' sound (咄字天音) is the second of the Eight Origin Sounds, representing the interplay of Yin and Yang—the duality of light and dark, life and death, solid and void. Its mastery marks a significant step in Xiao Chen's cultivation of the pure martial path, as these sounds predate even Buddhist mantras and are tied to the primordial laws of the universe. The Indestructible Imperial Heavenly Bell (不灭皇天神钟) is a classic xianxia trope: an artifact that can reform itself after damage, granting its user a form of pseudo-immortality. However, as seen here, such immortality has limits—repeated destruction causes immense pain and consumes spiritual essence. The Heavenly Thunder Hammer (天雷锤) is a half-ancestor sacred artifact of the Artifact Refining Sect, demonstrating the sect's mastery of forging. The Five Element Heavenly Kings represent the five classical elements (Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, Earth) and are powerful Perfected Man realm beings who command corresponding natural forces. Their targeting of Xiao Chen via a loved one is a recurring tactic in xianxia narratives, exploiting the protagonist's emotional bonds.

## Reading Guide

Xiao Chen's temporary alliance with Wu Ming is fragile—both expect to betray each other after the Five Element Heavenly Kings are dealt with. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen plans to overcome the numerical and elemental advantage of the five kings, especially given his limited War Sword count. The woman the kings intend to use as bait is likely the Qingqing lookalike from the Heavenly Burial Valley, raising questions about her true identity and whether she will be a genuine weakness for Xiao Chen. Meanwhile, Keke's location remains unknown, but her absence may become significant if the trap involves her. The chapter also hints at deeper cultivation levels—Xiao Chen's mastery of two Origin Sounds already threatens half-ancestor treasures; future breakthroughs with additional sounds could be world-shaking. Expect upcoming chapters to explore the delicate balance of power among the Heavenly Kings, Wu Ming's hidden schemes, and Xiao Chen's counter-strategies.
