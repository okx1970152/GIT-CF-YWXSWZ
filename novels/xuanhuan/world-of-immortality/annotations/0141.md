## Chapter Overview
This chapter transitions from the wilderness of Dragon Island to the bustling, politically intricate Heavenly Emperor City. Xiao Chen, accompanied by Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon, navigates the city's beast markets, where he acquires a mysterious white turtle. This seemingly trivial purchase draws the attention of a powerful local family, the Manders, who attempt to recruit or intimidate him. The chapter culminates in a public bet between Xiao Chen and a spoiled heiress of the Mander family, resulting in her humiliating loss and subsequent enrollment as his reluctant follower. The tone balances lighthearted market banter with underlying political tension, showcasing Xiao Chen's cunning and adaptability in this new urban arena.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen spots Keke's intense interest in a small white turtle, which the seller dismisses as a mediocre fighting beast. Feigning desire to cook it, Xiao Chen haggles down to five gold coins and purchases it. Upon closer inspection, he discovers an unbreakable black chain binding the turtle, with minuscule characters etched on it, hinting at the creature's extraordinary nature. Sensing he is being followed, Xiao Chen evades a summons from an unidentified powerful family. Later, a blue-haired girl from the Mander family confronts him, accusing him of arrogance. She challenges him to a bet: if she wins, she gets Keke; if she loses, she forfeits her colorful crane. Xiao Chen easily defeats her at the Mortal-Shedding Fourth Heaven, taking the crane and immediately selling it in the market. The girl's subsequent loss in a second bet (details undisclosed) results in her becoming his follower, much to her family's chagrin. This incident establishes a foothold for Xiao Chen within the city's complex web of young elites, and the white turtle remains a compelling mystery.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces the concept of 'beast fighting palaces' (斗兽宫) and the social hierarchy within the World of Immortality's cities. Major families, like the Manders, control such establishments and wield considerable influence. The 'unwritten rule' that older generation cultivators cannot directly target younger ones is a recurring trope in xianxia, providing a framework for protagonist growth and conflict. The white turtle's unbreakable chain and inscribed characters echo classic treasure-seeking plots, where seemingly mundane objects conceal profound secrets. The bet and its consequences, especially the girl becoming a follower, reflect a common narrative device where personal loss forces character development and potential alliance shifts. The colorful crane (彩鹤) is a type of spirit beast, and its sale for thirty gold coins underscores the high-value beast trade in the city.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up several threads for future development: the mystery of the white turtle and its chain, the budding enmity or potential alliance with the Mander family, and the new follower (the blue-haired girl) who may become a recurring character offering comedic relief or unexpected loyalty. Readers should note the political undercurrents—other powerful factions like the Undying Sect are watching Xiao Chen's actions. The relaxed market scenes contrast sharply with the earlier life-or-death struggles on Dragon Island, indicating that while the city offers safety, it also brings a different kind of danger involving politics, reputation, and intrigue. Xiao Chen's strategic use of the family protection rule shows his growing maturity in navigating the immortal world's power structures.
