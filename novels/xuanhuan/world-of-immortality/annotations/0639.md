## Chapter Overview
In this harrowing chapter, Xiao Chen finds himself sealed within a stone gate world, confronted by a spectral shadow of immense power. The shadow is fixated on destroying a mysterious stone jar that clanks with iron chains, while Xiao Chen struggles to escape an impossible spatial loop. Every attempt to flee is thwarted by a force that returns him to his starting point, no matter how far he travels. The oppressive atmosphere of this desolate ruin is punctuated by horrifying discoveries: a fire burning stone arms, a pyre of five stone skulls, and a seated corpse that may hold the key to this realm's secret. Xiao Chen's analytical mind grapples with the nature of this spatial cage, while the shadow's relentless efforts to break the jar suggest that something far more dangerous than either of them is bound within. The chapter closes with a spine-tingling sensation of a hand on his shoulder, leaving the reader in dread anticipation.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with Xiao Chen facing the shadow, which ignores him to seize the stone jar. The shadow tries to destroy the jar by crushing it underfoot, but the jar proves indestructible, emitting curses and mysterious light. Xiao Chen takes the opportunity to flee, but after racing ten thousand li, he finds himself back at the starting point—the spatial laws of this realm enforce a "return to origin." He experiments with flight and running, but every attempt beyond a few hundred zhang results in instantaneous return. Even severed hairs fly across vast distances only to reappear in his hand and reattach to his head. Desperate, Xiao Chen cuts himself, but the blood flows back and the wound heals, confirming the pervasive "return to origin" effect. He searches the immediate area and discovers a ruined stone house where an eerie fire burns not wood but several stone arms. The shadow follows, smashes the jar into the flames, and begins to refine it with star-fire. Xiao Chen then ventures into the thickest black fog and finds a great hall. There, another fire blazes—this time consuming five stone skulls. Behind the fire, a figure sits motionless: a humanoid with flesh and blood. Xiao Chen approaches, touches the shoulder, and finds the body ice-cold, concluding it is a corpse. But as the fire leaps higher, he feels a warm hand on his own shoulder, mirroring his gesture—implying the corpse may not be as dead as it seems.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of "return to origin" (复归原点) is a powerful spatial law that prevents escape by resetting any displacement. In xianxia cosmology, such laws often govern sealed realms created by supreme beings to imprison threats. The stone jar is a classic enigmatic artifact—indestructible and containing a bound entity, common in myths where powerful beings are sealed in vessels. The use of stone limbs and skulls as fuel for eternal flames evokes themes of sacrifice and the lingering power of fallen cultivators. Stone men, who have cultivated to the peak of physical transcendence, leave remains that are nearly indestructible; burning them requires equally supernatural fire. The seated corpse raises questions about the nature of life and death in the cultivation world: even at the Stone Man King level, existence is not eternal, yet some remnants may retain a spark. The shadow's obsession with the jar suggests that the sealed entity is a threat even to it, hinting at a power struggle that predates the current conflict.

Expect revelations about the nature of this realm, the jar's contents, and possibly a new ally or enemy in the corpse.
