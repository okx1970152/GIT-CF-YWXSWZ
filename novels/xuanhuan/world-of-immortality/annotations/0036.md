## Chapter Overview
This chapter functions as a crucial world-building pivot, providing the first comprehensive explanation of the cultivation hierarchy that governs the World of Immortality. Xiao Chen learns the formal structure of the Five Great Realms and their Nine Heavenly Tiers, anchoring his own progress and ambitions. The exposition is framed not as a dry lecture but through the observations of a protagonist who is beginning to grasp the scale of the world he has entered. The chapter also advances the narrative tension: the hunt for the Companion Dragon escalates as multiple cultivators converge, and a dragon’s roar from a nearby valley sets the stage for the next major encounter. This is a chapter of measured pacing, where knowledge is as valuable as combat.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with a systematic breakdown of the Five Great Realms: Mortal-Shedding, Spirit-Treasury, Sky-Mastery, Nirvana, and Deathless Longevity. Each realm is divided into Nine Heavenly Tiers, establishing a precise power scale. Xiao Chen learns that he himself has entered the Mortal-Shedding realm, placing him among the lower tier of true practitioners. Yizhen the monk provides a poignant perspective, musing that even those who achieve Deathless Longevity may find it only a new beginning in the grand scheme of a lost mythological age. The scene then shifts to the immediate dangers of Dragon Island: Xiao Chen detects hostile cultivators tracking him, likely drawn by the tracks of a young Companion Dragon. Using tactical cunning, he and the three skeletons lead the pursuers toward Yan Qingcheng and Rand, forcing the rival factions to clash. During this diversion, a powerful dragon roar erupts from a nearby valley, startling all parties into a temporary retreat. Xiao Chen, ever calculating, decides to investigate the valley, hoping to find an opportunity—perhaps a dragon egg or some other treasure.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces the central cultivation framework of the novel. The Five Great Realms—Mortal-Shedding (蜕凡), Spirit-Treasury (识藏), Sky-Mastery (御空), Nirvana (涅槃), and Deathless Longevity (长生)—follow a classic xianxia progression but with Chen Dong’s unique emphasis on desolate, mythic grandeur. The division of each realm into Nine Heavenly Tiers (九重天) is a common trope, but it gains weight here through the novel’s tragic tone: even at the highest realms, cultivators remain trapped by the ancient sealing of Dragon Island. The mention of ‘Demigod’ (半神) as a colloquial term for Sky-Mastery experts underscores the reverence—and fear—such power commands. Yizhen’s wistful reference to a ‘lost ancient mythological world’ taps into a recurring theme in Chinese web novels: the decline from a golden age of gods and heroes. The cameo mentions of legendary figures like Dugu Qiubai and Li Xunhuan serve as Easter eggs for readers familiar with wuxia classics, linking this world to a broader literary tradition.

## Reading Guide
Xiao Chen’s decision to explore the dragon valley is a classic narrative hook: it promises danger, discovery, and the potential for a major power-up. Readers should note the strategic positioning of Yan Qingcheng and Rand as rivals who may become future allies or enemies. The three skeletons’ sudden display of personality (Yanluo doing a backflip) hints that they are evolving beyond mere undead minions—this thread will likely bear fruit in the conflicts ahead. The chapter also subtly reinforces Xiao Chen’s growth: he is no longer a fleeing fugitive but a calculating survivor who uses the environment and other factions to his advantage. Pay attention to the geography being established—the valley, the waterfall, the forest—as these locations will reappear in future skirmishes. Finally, the cultivation realm system is now fully laid out; from here, every battle will carry clearer stakes in terms of tier advancement and acupoint divinization.
