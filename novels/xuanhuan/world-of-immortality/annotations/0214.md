## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a profound turning point in the World of Immortality's exploration of death and cosmology. Xiao Chen's desperate attempt to summon Keke's soul using an ancient barbarian sacrificial rite spirals into a harrowing descent into the Heaven-Earth Furnace – a failed underworld created by the Ancestral God Suiren. The atmosphere is thick with dread and mystery as the protagonists confront ancient spirits, learn the bitter truth about the nature of reincarnation in this world, and receive a shocking message that suggests Keke may not be truly lost. The chapter balances theological revelation with personal grief, all while deepening the mythic scale of the series.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and Fatty Niu Ren conduct a summoning ritual on a crumbling altar, but instead of calling Keke's soul, they attract a horde of ferocious spirits. The ritual accidentally opens a dark passage into the Heaven-Earth Furnace itself, and the altar sinks underground, sealing them within the furnace's interior. They encounter an ancient spirit who reveals that the Heaven-Earth Furnace is Suiren's failed attempt to create a true underworld – a realm of pure darkness and yin energy, from which return is nearly impossible. This shattered project is the origin of the legends of the Ten Courts of Hell and reincarnation, now passed down as distorted myths. The spirit explains that only those who reach the Deathless Longevity realm can hope to traverse these dark domains. After seven days of wandering a desolate landscape of bones, Xiao Chen and Niu Ren discover a ruined hall where a skeleton claiming to be King Chujiang of the Ten Kings sits upon a throne of bone. The skeleton tosses them a letter sealed with a small paw print – unmistakably from Keke.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter delves into one of the most profound cosmological concepts in Xianxia: the nature of the afterlife and reincarnation. The Heaven-Earth Furnace (天地铜炉) is portrayed not as a literal furnace but as a failed underworld – a dark realm that Suiren, the primordial fire-drilling ancestor, tried to forge as a proper cycle of rebirth. His failure explains why the 'Ten Kings of Hell' are known as legends rather than active rulers; they are echoes of that incomplete project. The distinction between 'yang' (living) and 'yin' (death) energy is central here – pure yin realms are lethal to living cultivators, but beings like Keke, who survived a forced reincarnation earlier, demonstrate extraordinary resilience. The old spirit's remark that only those at Deathless Longevity or higher can move between these realms sets a high benchmark for Xiao Chen's future growth. The letter from a Yama King suggests that Keke is alive and perhaps being held, linking directly to the mythological structure of the Ten Courts.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a major lore drop that recontextualizes many earlier events. The 'reincarnation' that Keke escaped during the golden lion king battle now carries far darker implications – she broke out of a genuine dark domain. Her letter from King Chujiang suggests she not only survived but has reached a place of power or captivity within the failed underworld system. Xiao Chen's next steps will likely involve learning to navigate these deathly realms, possibly seeking the aid of the three skeleton kings (Qinguang, Yanluo, and Wheel Kings) who were originally named after the Yama Kings. The closed passage means Xiao Chen must find another way to rescue Keke or meet the sender's conditions. The coming chapters will likely explore the underworld's geography, the remaining Yama Kings, and the mystery of who used Keke's paw print to send a message.
