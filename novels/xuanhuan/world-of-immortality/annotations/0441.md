## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges Xiao Chen and his companions into the heart of the death world's most dangerous territory: the central palace of a long-dead Ancestral Lord. After thirty years of cultivation on yin-yang fruits, they are ambushed by an unprecedented army of over twenty king-level undead—corpse kings, death knights, flame emperors, and more. The siege is brutal and nearly fatal, saved only by the purifying light of the seven-colored sacred tree. Reaching the central hall, they discover a chilling prophecy: a book predicting their arrival and foretelling that only one of the seven will survive. The chapter masterfully blends overwhelming action with a sinking sense of doom, setting a tragic, foreboding tone.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter begins with the four companions examining bone fragments and discussing the map to the depths of the death world. They settle in a garden of three yin-yang trees, spending thirty years cultivating on the spiritual fruits to restore their strength. Xiao Chen makes remarkable progress: all 365 major acupoints are divinized, countless minor acupoints are activated, and he gains greater control over his 27 war swords. When they proceed deeper into the palace, they are ambushed by a host of king-level undead beings—including the corpse, death knight, terror spirit, flame emperor, bone dragon, lich king, and nine-headed evil ghost—over twenty in total. The four are severely outmatched and nearly killed. They only escape by using the seven-colored sacred tree's light, which the dark beings fear. Reaching the central hall, they find a patch of fresh blood and three broken war swords, indicating that even half-ancestor artifacts can be shattered. Then they discover a book titled "Doomsday Prophecy," which predicts their journey in detail and ends with the chilling line: "Seven of you entered together, only one will leave alive." This revelation introduces a cosmic, fatalistic threat far beyond the immediate dangers of the death world.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The yin-yang tree (阴阳树) is a rare spiritual plant that produces fruits embodying the dual energies of yin and yang. Cultivating on such fruits can replenish vitality and aid in acupoint divinization, but for those at the king level (君王级), they provide no further realm advancement; instead, they store energy for future use. The concept of 'king-level' beings in the death world corresponds to the Perfected Man (至人) realm in the living world, making them formidable opponents. The broken war swords are significant: they are the same type of legendary weapons Xiao Chen wields, and their destruction implies the involvement of a power beyond half-ancestor—possibly a true Ancestral God. The 'Doomsday Prophecy' book is a classic trope in xianxia, where a sage foresees future events with uncanny accuracy, adding a layer of fatalistic tension to the narrative.

## Reading Guide
This chapter raises the stakes considerably. The prophecy that only one of the seven will survive (note: there are only four in the party now, but the prophecy says 'seven') hints at future characters or a hidden meaning. The revelation that the twenty-plus king-level undead were once sealed in stone pillars and later turned into puppets by an Ancestral Lord suggests that the central palace may have been a battlefield of ancient gods. Readers should watch for the identity of that Ancestral Lord and how his remains or treasures might affect the party. The broken war swords also raise the question: what force could break such divine weapons? The prophecy itself may not be absolute—xianxia often includes the theme of defying fate. Xiao Chen’s growth in controlling the war swords without the stone statue is a key power-up, but the coming trials will test his limits.
