## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges Xiao Chen deeper into the desolate underworld, where the boundaries between the living and the dead blur. As he and Niu Ren approach the Nine Nether Platform in search of Keke, they encounter a ghostly old monk who leads them to a forgotten Buddhist temple—a last bastion of sacred light in a realm of darkness. There, Xiao Chen's Suiren Fire-Drill unearths a terrifying relic: a stone arm belonging to a mysterious fragmented being. The chapter weaves together cosmic dread, Buddhist paradox, and the tantalizing mystery of the stone man, a puzzle that may connect to the greatest ancestral gods. The atmosphere oscillates between hushed reverence and spine-chilling revelation, as Xiao Chen realizes that the stone fragments within his body are not mere artifacts but a meditating entity, slowly awakening.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and Niu Ren travel through the underworld's barren blood-red plains for fifteen days, guided by the skeleton king's directions. Their journey is interrupted by faint Buddhist chanting, which draws them to a radiant temple complex—an oasis of sacred light in hell. Before they can investigate, an emaciated, skin-and-bones ghost monk appears behind them, revealing he died two centuries ago and now shelters in Kṣitigarbha's temple, the last pure Buddhist ground in the underworld. He requests a few drops of dragon blood to fortify the temple's defenses against rampaging demons. After receiving it, he insists on guiding Xiao Chen and Niu Ren to the temple, claiming a prophecy foretells that a treasure linked to them will be unearthed. Once inside, Xiao Chen's Suiren Fire-Drill flies from his body, collapses several ancient shrines, and returns with a half-finger-length stone arm. Two living monks of Kṣitigarbha's lineage reveal staggering lore: the stone man was divided into nine parts, three of which fell into hell. They recount how the legendary Lord Tongtian once challenged Sovereign Youchao and later left his Immortal-Slaying Sword in hell, unable to retrieve it—the sword fell precisely where the stone man fragments lay. The ghost monk, a master of the Grand Prophecy Art, clarifies that the stone man predates Suiren's death, dismissing the idea that Suiren himself is the stone man. However, he cites an ancient scripture suggesting that Suiren, Youchao, and other ancestral gods once divided a shattered stone man and several stone artifacts among themselves. In a startling development, the stone legs and arm within Xiao Chen's body now sit cross-legged in a meditation pose, with the Suiren Fire-Drill floating in the statue's palm, indicating the fragments are gradually reassembling a mysterious being.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter draws heavily on Chinese mythological and xianxia conventions. The Immortal-Slaying Sword (诛仙剑) is one of the four swords from the legendary Immortal-Slaying Formation in the classic novel _Fengshen Yanyi_, often associated with Lord Tongtian (通天教主). Its mention as having been sealed in hell highlights the underworld's terrifying depth, where even such a world-shaking artifact struggles to break free. The stone man (石人) concept alludes to the idea of a primordial being shattered and scattered—a theme reminiscent of ancient myths where gods are dismembered to create the world. The ancestral gods Suiren (燧人氏, the fire-driller) and Youchao (有巢氏, the nest-builder) are culture heroes from Chinese prehistory, credited with bringing fire and shelter to humanity. Their involvement suggests the stone man predates even these foundational figures. Kṣitigarbha (地藏王) is a bodhisattva who vowed not to achieve Buddhahood until hell is emptied; his temples in the underworld are a common motif in Chinese Buddhist cosmology. The Grand Prophecy Art (大预言术) is a rare divination skill that grants glimpses of the future, adding a layer of fate and mystery.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up a major long-term mystery: the identity and purpose of the stone man forming inside Xiao Chen. Readers should pay close attention to the fragmented lore about supreme beings dividing the stone man and the lingering power of the Immortal-Slaying Sword. The revelation that the stone man's pieces are converging with Xiao Chen's body hints at a destiny far beyond mere cultivation. The ghost monk's prophecy suggests that the temple's treasure was meant for Xiao Chen, implying that his path is being guided by unseen forces. As he approaches the Nine Nether Platform, the central question intensifies: What has happened to Keke, and how will the stone man's awakening affect the rescue? The tension between sacred Buddhist light and the suffocating darkness of hell creates a powerful backdrop for the trials ahead.
