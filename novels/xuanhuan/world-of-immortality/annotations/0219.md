## Chapter Overview

This chapter strikes a delicate balance between tender reunion and mythic awe. Xiao Chen learns that Keke's soul survives, trapped in the dark domain, but her physical body is gone—a bittersweet revelation filled with both hope and sorrow. The narrative then shifts to the desolate underworld, where the legendary Peacock King Kong Xuan tears across the sky with his fabled Five-Colored Divine Light, carrying a mountain of spiritual treasures. The contrast between Keke's childish determination and Kong Xuan's unstoppable power underscores the vast scope of this world, reminding readers that even as personal stakes rise, cosmic forces are stirring.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen reestablishes contact with Keke's soul in the dark domain. The little beast's body has been destroyed, but its soul cannot be extinguished, and it has been accumulating spiritual essence by consuming eight Purple Diamond Yin Wood Ginseng fruits. A ghost official previously sent a distress signal, not for Keke, but because an invincible beast like Keke had wandered into his territory and refused to leave, causing chaos. Xiao Chen ascends from the underworld with help from the treasure tree and the mysterious stone drill, which calms the torrent of souls. Meanwhile, Niu Ren has been forcibly fused with a Bull King war soul, sprouting small horns that will fade as his cultivation deepens. As they approach the exit, the legendary Kong Xuan appears, wreathed in Five-Colored Divine Light, carrying a mountain of yin wood. He effortlessly cuts the mountain to fit through the passage, displaying power that leaves Xiao Chen and Niu Ren awestruck. Xiao Chen reflects on the emergence of ancient legends—the Sword Immortal, now Kong Xuan—and feels an urgent need to become stronger.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of the dark domain (九幽十八狱) draws from Chinese folk religious ideas of a layered underworld where souls are judged and punished. The Purple Diamond Yin Wood (紫钻阴木) is a rare spiritual material that grows only in death-aligned environments; its ginseng fruits accumulate various forms of qi, aiding Keke's return. The Bull King war soul (牛王战魂) references the Ox-Head (牛头) guardian of hell, a common figure in Buddhist-influenced Chinese mythology. Kong Xuan's Five-Colored Divine Light (五色神光) originates from the Ming dynasty novel Investiture of the Gods (封神演义), where it is an invincible technique that sweeps away all things. This chapter also hints at power scales: war souls, divine light, and the Stone Drill (石钻), which appears to be a tool of Supreme Ancestral God Suiren.

## Reading Guide

Keke's survival opens the door for her eventual return, but the price—her physical body—hints at a transformation. Niu Ren's new war soul may make him a formidable ally, though his comic frustration suggests a long adjustment. The appearance of Kong Xuan signals that legendary figures are stirring, and the convergence of such beings may lead to cataclysmic events. Xiao Chen's sense of urgency is palpable; he has glimpsed the height of power and knows he must accelerate his growth. Readers should watch for how Keke's soul accumulates enough essence to manifest, how Niu Ren's bull horns affect his identity, and whether the ancient artifacts within Xiao Chen—like the stone drill—will play a larger role in facing these looming threats.
