## Chapter Overview

This chapter introduces a pivotal new character—the mischievous, irresistibly adorable yet terrifyingly powerful Keke—and tests Xiao Chen's moral compass in an unexpected way. After stealing a mysterious sacred tree, Xiao Chen finds himself at the mercy of its original owner, a snow-white beast with a peculiar binding art that renders his cultivation useless. The tone balances absurdity and gravity: Keke tosses Xiao Chen around like a toy while munching on roasted goat leg, yet the scene also lays bare Xiao Chen's personal code of conduct. Beneath the playful surface, alliances shift, the skeletons betray their loyalty, and the first hints of a deeper bond between Xiao Chen and Keke take root. The chapter also sets a darker plan in motion as Xiao Chen resolves to strike at Treefolk Valley.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen is humiliated by Keke's binding art, unable to break free despite his superior cultivation. The three skeletons, after being reassembled from their earlier disassembly, attempt to flee but are forced back by Keke. Through their gestures, Xiao Chen learns that Keke is the original owner of the Little Sacred Tree. Despite the indignity, Xiao Chen feels no resentment—he acknowledges their theft and recognizes Keke's innocence. Keke shows intelligence and curiosity, using the tree to demonstrate its resilience. When Xiao Chen decides to leave, Keke follows, hiding at first then openly riding on the Reincarnation King's skull. Meanwhile, Xiao Chen's internal reasoning reveals his ethical boundaries: he is no sage, but he draws a line at harming those who trust him. He also resolves to attack Treefolk Valley with fire, a ruthless but calculated plan. The chapter ends with Keke officially joining the group, wearing the sacred tree like a hat.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Keke's binding art is a rare divine ability that does not rely on raw power but on a mysterious law of immobilization, reminiscent of classic xianxia techniques that bypass typical cultivation defenses. The skeletons' reaction to Keke—fear and subservience—hints at the little beast's transcendent origin, possibly connected to the primordial World Tree. Xiao Chen's decision not to retaliate against Keke reflects a core xianxia theme: the importance of "heart-mind" (xin) and personal dao. Even in a brutal world where strength dominates, maintaining one's principles is part of cultivation. The scene where Keke demonstrates the Little Sacred Tree's resilience by stomping on it underscores the tree's divine nature—it is no ordinary plant but an artifact of immense hardness, akin to a treasure that cannot be damaged by normal means. Additionally, the term "binding art" (禁锢术) appears in many xianxia novels as a supportive crowd-control technique, but here it's shown as almost casually overwhelming, marking Keke as an unorthodox powerhouse.

## Reading Guide

Readers should watch how Keke's relationship with Xiao Chen develops: this chapter establishes mutual respect and trust despite the awkward beginning. The decision to burn Treefolk Valley signals Xiao Chen's proactive ruthlessness, but the actual execution will require careful planning and allies. The skeletons' cowardice adds a light touch, but their genuine loyalty to Xiao Chen remains. As Keke joins the group, her mysterious origins and true power level become a key question: what exactly is this creature? And what does her attachment to the Little Sacred Tree mean for the larger mysteries of Dragon Island? Future chapters will test Xiao Chen's evolving strategy and his newfound alliance with the most adorable—and fearsome—beast on the island.
