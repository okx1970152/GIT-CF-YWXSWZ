## Chapter Overview
After thirteen years trapped in the grim Death World, Xiao Chen and Keke finally return to the vibrant mortal realm. Greeted by the sights and smells of a bustling spring day, their first instinct is not conquest but an overwhelming craving for food. This chapter opens with a deceptively lighthearted tone, as the duo's quest for delicacies leads them into the heart of Yanjing. Yet beneath the comedy of a hungry sacred beast and a grimy protagonist lies a powder keg of political tension. Xiao Chen's casual walk soon erupts into a violent confrontation with the local nobility, revealing that even in a moment of hunger, he is a force that the Hundred Races must reckon with.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen and Keke leave the Death World and arrive in the mortal world, both ravenous after years of deprivation. They head straight for a garden where scholars and maidens are enjoying a spring outing. Xiao Chen brusquely drives them away, and he and Keke feast on the scholars' snacks before indulging in roasted crane, using a precious guqin as firewood. After leaving spirit fruits as compensation, they travel to the ancient capital of Yanjing, where Xiao Chen senses a dense purple dragon qi rising from the earth—an indicator of a powerful earthly dragon vein that could accelerate cultivation. While walking the streets, a mounted procession of the Xiaoyao Hou's retainers nearly tramples an old woman, prompting Xiao Chen to intervene. He brutally cripples the horsemen and dismantles their weapons with a single finger. The Xiaoyao Xiaohouye arrives, furious, but his guards are overwhelmed. Xiao Chen humiliates the young noble by stepping on his face repeatedly. A winged-beast-drawn carriage descends, carrying the niece of Wu Ming—the Duobao Tianwang. Xiao Chen effortlessly disables her twenty escorts with a wave of his hand. When the middle-aged woman demands his identity, Xiao Chen reveals his intent: to seize Wu Ming's bell and hammer treasures. The crowd is horrified, and Xiao Chen boldly states that Wu Ming would flee if he knew Xiao Chen had arrived.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter reinforces the xianxia concept of 'earthly dragon veins' (祖脉) and 'geomantic qi' (紫气). Ancient capitals like Yanjing are built over massive spiritual ley lines that make them superior cultivation sites even to famous mountains. Xiao Chen's ability to see purple qi indicates his profound cultivation. The 'Death World' (死亡世界) is a parallel realm where souls of the deceased become fire-seed beings; Xiao Chen spent thirteen years there, emerging as a Perfected Man realm expert. Wu Ming, also known as Duobao Tianwang, is a master artifact refiner from the Cultivation World; his 'Undying Imperial Heavenly Bell' is a legendary treasure. The 'Xiaoyao Hou' title references the feudal nobility system revived under Empress Zhao Lin'er's reign, showing how the Hundred Races have integrated into mortal politics.

## Reading Guide
Xiao Chen's open declaration against Wu Ming sets the stage for a major conflict. Readers should note the careful balance of power: Wu Ming is a feared Heavenly King, but Xiao Chen's casual confidence suggests he has grown beyond Perfected Man Seventh Heaven. The chapter also introduces the geopolitics of Yanjing under the Xiaoyao Hou, hinting at deeper entanglements with the Hundred Races. Keke's hunger and mischief will likely continue to provide comic relief, but her role in battles could be crucial given her mastery of Paradise Lost. The upcoming planting of the Sacred Tree in Yanjing may alter the city's spiritual landscape, drawing even more attention. Questions linger about Wu Ming's true power and whether he will accept Xiao Chen's challenge.
