## Chapter Overview

This chapter strikes a rare balance between serene domestic life and the brooding tension of a gathering storm. Xiao Chen, having returned to his ancestral village in the mortal world, uses the divine Source Eight Sounds—specifically the primordial 'Weng' character—to transform the frozen hamlet into a lush, spiritual oasis. Yet the tranquility is deceptive; outside the village, a legion of enemies from the Sun Church, the Jie Sect, and various noble families waits to strike. Xiao Chen’s deliberate calm and his cultivation atop the Yellow Mud Altar mask a deeper purpose: he is probing the earth beneath the village, where an ancient underground city and an eternal lamp await discovery. This chapter bridges the mundane and the mythic, reminding us that the greatest secrets often lie buried under the soil of home.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Xiao Chen immersed in cultivation on the Yellow Mud Altar, the 'Weng' tone of the Source Eight Sounds resonating through his soul. Despite the heavy snowfall and the encirclement of powerful cultivators—including Hai Yunxue, Fang Tianqi, and Ye Tian—he remains unmoved for three days, achieving a state of harmony with heaven and earth. On the fourth day, he awakens and dismisses his enemies with contempt, then turns to revive the sleeping villagers. By planting spiritual trees and using the life-giving aspect of the 'Weng' sound, he makes the entire village bloom in winter, a feat that astonishes the surrounding cultivators. More importantly, this act of cultivation triggers an unexpected reaction: the village’s spiritual energy surges, revealing a massive underground city buried deep beneath the earth. Xiao Chen, through the Yellow Mud Altar, discerns an eternal lamp floating within that city, its light reaching directly into his heart. This discovery suggests that the ancient village is far more than it appears—a nexus of forgotten power tied to the very foundations of the mortal world.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The 'Source Eight Sounds' (本源八音) are primordial tones said to embody the fundamental vibrations of creation. In Chinese cosmology, sound is a creative force; the Daoist concept of 'zhen yan' (true words) holds that certain syllables can shape reality. Xiao Chen’s use of 'Weng' to both kill and nurture reflects the dual nature of such primal sounds. The Yellow Mud Altar (黄泥台) is a sacred artifact associated with the ancestor gods, often used for meditation and divination. Its ability to reveal the underground city underscores its role as a bridge between the mortal and the mythic. The scene where Xiao Chen plants spiritual trees and causes the village to bloom is reminiscent of the legendary 'immortal peaches' and 'golden grains' that nourish the blessed—a xianxia trope linking divine favor to earthly abundance. Finally, the eternal lamp (神灯) suspended in the underground city evokes the lanterns of the underworld from Chinese folk religion, where a light that never extinguishes marks a soul’s eternal vigil or a seal of cosmic power.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a turning point in the mortal world arc. Xiao Chen has now demonstrated a mastery of the Source Eight Sounds that goes beyond combat—he can use them to quicken life itself. The discovery of the underground city and the eternal lamp hints at a hidden layer of the Nine Provinces seal, possibly connected to the Ancestral Dragon and the Heavenly Steles. Readers should pay close attention to Xiao Chen’s deliberate cultivation method: by integrating his martial arts with everyday acts like planting trees, he is refining his Dao in a way that is both profound and practical. The gathering of enemies outside the village—including Hai Yunxue’s vengeance and the prodigies Fang Tianqi and Ye Tian—foreshadows a major confrontation. Yet Xiao Chen’s calmness suggests he is preparing for something far larger than a mere duel. The eternal lamp may be a key to awakening greater powers within himself or the world. Watch for how the lamp and the underground city connect to the broader mythos of the Heavenly Steles and the fate of the Nine Provinces.
