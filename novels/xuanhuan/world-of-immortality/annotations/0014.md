## Chapter Overview
This chapter takes a contemplative turn as Xiao Chen, still recovering from his wounds, immerses himself in the natural world to accelerate healing. The narrative slows to a meditative pace, focusing on the subtle exchange of wood essence between the forest and his body, and the tentative bond forming with the young celestial horse. The calm is broken by the arrival of a troop of giant apes led by a formidable four-armed blood ape. What could have been a bloody confrontation is defused through a surprising cultural bridge: cooked meat. The chapter closes with a revelatory image of the apes standing under the moonlight, absorbing its essence—a reminder that even beasts tread the path of cultivation in this world. The tone is one of cautious hope and renewed vitality, a quiet interlude before the inevitable storm.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen utilizes the prolific wood essence of the forest to heal his internal injuries, marking a shift from passive recovery to active cultivation. The green life-energy visibly gathers around him, and he experiences a profound union with nature, feeling the pulse of the trees and grass. The little celestial horse, now less fearful, shares in the spiritual energy and accepts fruit from Xiao Chen, signaling a growing trust. However, the arrival of a troop of giant apes—three-meter-tall black-furred beasts led by a four-meter crimson-furred, four-armed alpha—introduces a new kind of threat. Rather than fight, Xiao Chen employs a tactical gamble: he roasts game meat and offers it to the apes. The alpha cautiously samples the roasted meat, then devours it greedily, followed by the rest of the troop. This simple act of food-sharing dissipates their hostility. Xiao Chen continues to cook for them, and the apes leave peacefully. The chapter’s critical revelation comes at night when Xiao Chen observes the same apes standing in the stone forest, absorbing moonlight—a behavior that confirms they are not mere beasts but spirit creatures on a path of cultivation, hinting at deeper wisdom and potential alliances to come.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Wood essence (草木精气) is a staple of xianxia cultivation representing the life-energy of plants. In Daoist thought, nature is a reservoir of qi, and absorbing it is a fundamental method for healing and strengthening. The depiction here elevates the concept to a visceral level—green light visible to the naked eye—emphasizing the abundant vitality of the World of Immortality. The giant apes’ ability to absorb moonlight essence (月华) aligns with classical Chinese folklore, where spirits and beasts cultivate under the moon to gain intelligence and power. Four-armed mutations often signify exceptional cultivation progress or ancient bloodlines. The celestial horse (天马), a unicorn-like beast, is a divine steed from mythology, naturally drawn to pure spiritual energy. The use of cooked meat as a peace offering reflects a universal survival tactic, but in the xianxia setting it also demonstrates Xiao Chen’s adaptability—his conflict resolution through gift-giving rather than violence shows a nuanced intelligence beyond mere combat.

## Reading Guide
This chapter plants several seeds for future arcs. Xiao Chen’s improved relationship with the celestial horse may eventually lead to a true bond, potentially offering him a mount or divine advantage. The giant apes, now appeased by food, could become either allies or neutral observers in his future struggles. Their ability to cultivate suggests they are not random beasts but possibly guardians of a treasure or territory. Xiao Chen’s reflection on his own incomplete technique hints at a long-term need for a complete cultivation method. Readers should watch for how his healing and rising vitality affect his fighting ability, and whether the peace with the apes holds when the inevitable conflict with other cultivators arrives. The chapter also reinforces the theme that in the World of Immortality, strength is not only measured in force but in wisdom, patience, and the ability to find common ground with the wild.
