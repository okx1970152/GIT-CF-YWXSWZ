## Chapter Overview

This chapter transitions from exploration to poignant revelation. Xiao Chen follows Keke into a region teeming with dragon roars, only to discover a fallen divine tree of unimaginable scale—the Cosmic Tree, Keke's birthplace. The atmosphere shifts from awe at the tree's majesty to sorrow as Keke's playful demeanor collapses into grief upon seeing the desolate stump. The chapter masterfully contrasts the epic scale of primordial destruction with the intimate pain of a lost home, deepening Keke's character beyond a mere cute companion.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and his companions approach a mountain range from which multiple dragon roars emanate, confirming they have entered a den of primordial beasts. Keke leads them directly toward the source, revealing her familiarity with the area. Upon cresting a ridge, Xiao Chen sees a sight that defies belief: a massive black, jade-like ridge stretching across several mountains, which he gradually realizes is not a mountain but the trunk of a colossal fallen tree. Following the trunk, they find its stump, which still stands 1,500 meters high and is as broad as a mountain. The top of the stump is perfectly flat, as if severed by a single, impossibly sharp blow. Keke climbs to the top and is found wandering aimlessly, her bright eyes clouded with confusion. She then curls up and emits soft whimpers, like a lost child, for the first time showing profound vulnerability. The three skeletons communicate through jaw movements, pointing and gesturing. Xiao Chen stays by her side, offering quiet comfort. The chapter ends on this emotional note, without any combat, focusing entirely on the revelation of Keke's tragic origin: she was born alone on this destroyed Cosmic Tree, and this is all that remains of her home.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of the Cosmic Tree (通天神树) draws from ancient Chinese mythology, particularly the Jianmu (建木), a world tree that connected the mortal realm to heaven. In xianxia lore, such trees are often the birthplaces of divine beings or treasures. The tree's perfect, clean cut implies an act of supreme power, hinting at a cataclysm that parallels Dragon Island's own history of divine sealing. Keke's emergence from a rainbow jade egg on this tree aligns with the motif of lone, orphaned divine beasts—creatures born from extraordinary circumstances, isolated from their kind. This chapter also reinforces the desolate, mythic aesthetic: the dragon roars in the background, the dead silence of the tree stump, and Keke's grief all contribute to the sense of a world haunted by its own vanished grandeur.

## Reading Guide

This chapter offers a rare emotional pause in the relentless survival struggle on Dragon Island. Readers should note the gentleness Xiao Chen shows toward Keke—a stark contrast to his cold efficiency in battle. This moment deepens their bond and hints at Xiao Chen's humanity beneath his hardened exterior. The mystery of the Cosmic Tree's severing remains, pointing to a greater power or event that shaped Dragon Island. The multiple dragon roars in the surrounding mountains suggest that Keke's birthplace is still guarded or contested by powerful beasts. Future chapters may reveal more about the tree's history and its connection to the island's seal, as well as how Keke's origin ties into the larger struggle for the island's treasures.
