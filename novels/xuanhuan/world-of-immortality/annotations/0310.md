## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a thunderous declaration of mortal defiance as the ancient warrior god Xing Tian emerges from eons of sealing and immediately makes good on his vow to slay a Half-Ancestor for the mortal world. The confrontation on the Eastern Sea against the Sword Master of Shu Mountain is a masterful display of unadorned Martial Body supremacy against the intricate spellcraft of the cultivation world. Xing Tian's technique—the Hand of the Supreme Being—shatters half-ancestor artifacts as if they were paper, underscoring a fundamental truth: in a world of flying swords and divine treasures, the purest martial path remains unconquered. Xiao Chen witnesses this battle with burning insight, as the principles of his own martial cultivation crystallize before him.

## Key Plot Points
Xing Tian, newly freed from sealing, strides across the Eastern Sea with Chiyou, his killing intent unshrouded. The Sword Master of Shu Mountain, a Half-Ancestor of the cultivation world, intercepts him. Xing Tian's opening act is to annihilate several arrogant cultivation disciples with a flicker of green light, establishing his absolute contempt for those who defile the mortal realm. The battle is fought within a Martial Realm domain Xing Tian creates to contain the devastation. He engages the Sword Master bare-handed, shattering one semi-ancestral divine sword after another with his bare hands—the Hand of the Supreme Being technique. The Sword Master dons full battle armor, wields a divine spear, and unleashes a torrent of artifacts, but Xing Tian's martial body is invulnerable. With three focused punches, Xing Tian destroys the Sword Master's body and armor, then kicks the nascent soul into dust, erasing him completely. The death of a Half-Ancestor shocks the gathered witnesses, affirming that the ancient warriors of the Nine Provinces will not tolerate foreign dominion. Xiao Chen watches with rapt attention, recognizing that Xing Tian's martial path is identical to his own Martial Imprint cultivation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter draws heavily on the Chinese myth of Xing Tian, the headless god who danced with shield and axe after being decapitated by the Yellow Emperor. Here he is presented as a pure martial artist—a stark contrast to the spell-slinging cultivators of the "Cultivation World" (修真界). The "Hand of the Supreme Being" (上苍之手) is a legendary martial technique that embodies the ultimate refinement of physical combat, capable of destroying divine artifacts through pure force. The "Martial Realm" (武界) is a domain created by a martial artist to isolate battle, preventing collateral damage. This reflects the xianxia trope of "worlds within worlds" where power is contained. The Sword Master (剑主) represents the Shu Mountain lineage, a prominent sword-cultivation school in Chinese mythology, whose earthly disciples include the famous Longmei Patriarch. The battle underscores the philosophical divide between martial body refinement and artifact reliance.

## Reading Guide
Xiao Chen's observation of this battle is a pivot point for his own martial understanding. The principles of the Hand of the Supreme Being align precisely with his Martial Imprint cultivation, which he received from the last pure martial artist's skeleton. Readers should note how Xing Tian's combat style mirrors Xiao Chen's own potential future: absolute physical dominance that renders magic and artifacts meaningless. The death of the Sword Master will have cascading consequences—the cultivation world has lost a Half-Ancestor, and other invading powers like the Green Lotus Celestial Maiden must now reckon with Xing Tian's presence. The chapter also sets up a confrontation between Chiyou and Da Yu over the mortal world's resources. Watch for how Xiao Chen integrates these insights into his own martial evolution and his quest to rescue Qingqing from the Green Lotus Celestial Maiden.
