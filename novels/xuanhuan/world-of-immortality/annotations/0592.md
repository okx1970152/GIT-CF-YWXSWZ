## Chapter Overview

After more than ten thousand years of seclusion in the Yellow River—the transformed body of the Ancestral Dragon—Xiao Chen and Xiao Juelong (the Inverse Dragon King) finally emerge into a world utterly transformed. The mortal realm has cycled through another technological civilization, its cities glittering with skyscrapers and its weapons reduced to firecrackers in the hands of a War Ancestor. This chapter opens with the four survivors—Xiao Chen, Xiao Juelong, Sha Polang, and the Nine-Headed Snake—walking through a modern capital, their long robes and flowing hair drawing stares as they sense a familiar artifact: the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree. The tree bears no trace of Keke, however, and Xiao Chen’s hope flickers and fades. Then a War Sword calls to him from an antique shop—a lure. The moment he approaches, an ancient teleportation formation seizes them, hurling them across space to a sea beyond the mortal world. There, an ancient demon city hangs four War Swords from its gates, and within its black mists drift the corpses of Ancestral Gods—and a stone statue uncannily shaped like Keke. The chapter ends on a note of grim mystery and gathering dread, as Xiao Chen stares at the mockery of his beloved companion.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiao Chen has spent over ten thousand years in meditation beneath the Yellow River, reaching a state near petrification comparable to the upper Ancestral Gods of the Eternal Unknown. Xiao Juelong has similarly become a War Ancestor, his dragon horns now fully mature.
- Sha Polang and the Nine-Headed Snake survived the great calamity and were rescued by Xiao Juelong; they remain loyal but comic figures.
- The group surveys the modern world, noting its technological sophistication but spiritual barrenness. Xiao Chen detonates a nuclear weapon in his palm without injury, underscoring the chasm between mortal and immortal power.
- Xiao Chen detects the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree buried in an ancient battlefield; it has evolved further but holds no trace of Keke’s presence, deepening his sorrow.
- Sensing a nearby War Sword, Xiao Chen tracks it to an antique shop. The sword proves to be bait—an ancient formation triggers, attempting to teleport them to a distant location. Xiao Chen breaks free mid-transfer but chooses to follow the coordinates.
- They arrive at a forbidden sea realm where an ancient demon city looms, its four gates each adorned with a suspended War Sword. Within the city, black mists roil, revealing the floating corpses of ancestral gods—including the old farmer and Mo Xiaowang—and a stone statue that mirrors Keke’s form.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter reinforces the cyclical nature of civilizations in the *World of Immortality* cosmology: the mortal realm has risen from the ashes of the Otherworld invasion and rebuilt a technologically advanced society, mirroring a previous cycle described in earlier arcs. The Yellow River, once the physical manifestation of the Ancestral Dragon, serves as Xiao Chen’s training ground—an example of how landscape and legend fuse in Chinese myth. The War Swords, each a fragment of the Heavenly Stele’s legacy, are often used as bait or traps because they resonate with those who hold Heavenly Marks. The demon city itself is a classic xianxia trope: an ancient fortress imbued with spatial laws, serving as a deadly arena for supreme beings. The presence of ancestral god corpses inside suggests that a powerful enemy has been harvesting the remains of fallen gods, possibly to forge a new weapon or complete the Stone Man path.

## Reading Guide

The cliffhanger of Keke’s statue raises immediate questions: Is it a replica meant to provoke Xiao Chen, or does it signal that Keke herself has been captured or petrified? The answer will likely drive the next arc. Xiao Chen’s cultivation has plateaued at the War Ancestor level; his next breakthrough may come from gathering the remaining Heavenly Marks or confronting the being behind this trap. The four War Swords hanging on the city gates are an obvious lure—but for whom? Several factions (the Otherworld, the Celestial Realm, the cursed Heaven-Defying clan) could be involved. Readers should also watch for the Inverse Dragon King’s role: his growth alongside Xiao Chen suggests they will face the coming threats together. The modern world setting may seem a brief diversion, but its mention of ancient weapons and hidden battlefields hints that remnants of previous cycles still lie buried.
