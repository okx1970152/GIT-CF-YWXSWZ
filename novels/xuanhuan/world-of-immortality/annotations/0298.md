## Chapter Overview
This epic chapter sees the full might of the Eternal Continent's semi-ancestors—Laozi, Buddha, Chiyou, Original, Tongtian, Zhunti, and Sun Wu—united in a desperate assault on the Heavenly Stele of Dragon Island. As their combined power shatters part of the ancient castle and forces open the dreaded Dead City, the land is drenched in ghastly blood rain. Amid the cosmic upheaval, personal reunions unfold: the four dragon kings take human form and meet once more, while the white-shelled turtle receives a precious dragon horn and departs. Yet the shadows of old enemies lengthen, as Hai Yunxue cunningly surrounds Xiao Chen with Tiger clan experts, determined to thwart whatever opportunity he senses.

## Key Plot Points
The semi-ancestors, driven by the need to reclaim artifacts lost within the Dead City, launch a coordinated strike against the Heavenly Stele. Their gathered power partially demolishes the ancient castle, causing a torrent of blood to cascade upon the stele and revealing the phantom city gates. Original, Tongtian, and Zhunti press forward under immense pressure, while Buddha focuses on retrieving a colossal Dharma wheel embedded in the gate. Sun Wu's stone dagger—a weapon of an Ancestral God—proves crucial in breaching the blood curtain. Meanwhile, on the edge of the bone sea, Liu Mu, Yizhen, and Niu Ren reunite with their dragon kings, now in human form as youths. The white-shelled turtle, recognizing the dragon horn gifted by the Old Dragon of the Southern Wastes, departs with gratitude. Keke flees from the overbearing little girls Linglong and Tutu, who have their own designs. Hai Yunxue seizes the moment, encircling Xiao Chen with Tiger clan forces, but Xiao Chen remains steadfast, sensing that a monumental opportunity is about to arrive.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The semi-ancestors here are legendary figures drawn from Chinese mythology and classical fiction: Laozi (Daoist sage), Buddha, Chiyou (ancient war deity), Original (Yuanshi Tianzun, head of the Chan Sect), Tongtian (founder of the Jie Sect), Zhunti (a Buddhist sage from Fengshen Yanyi), and Sun Wu (the Martial Saint, author of The Art of War). The Four Immortal-Slaying Swords, the Seven Treasures Wonderful Tree, and the stone dagger are iconic artifacts from these myths. The Heavenly Stele that seals Dragon Island represents cosmic law and suppression; the Dead City, linked to the steles, is a recurring locus of ancient secrets. The Pure Land (极乐净土) is the Buddha's paradise realm, here opened to release his followers. The reappearance of Jieyin (接引) from the Dharma wheel hints at a deeper Buddhist subplot connected to the island's sealing.

## Reading Guide
This chapter intensifies the central mystery of Dragon Island: what lies within the Dead City that the semi-ancestors so desperately seek? Their reckless assault may inadvertently break a seal that could change the entire continent. Xiao Chen's intuition of a looming opportunity suggests he is about to obtain a significant power or artifact, but Hai Yunxue's ambush raises the stakes dramatically. The reunion of the four dragon kings—each loyal to different masters—foreshadows a future conflict or alliance. Keke's flight from the little girls may seem comedic, but her hidden importance to the Lost Paradise realm cannot be ignored. Readers should watch how the semi-ancestors' gamble unfolds, and whether Xiao Chen can overcome the Tiger clan's interference.
