## Chapter Overview
This chapter bridges profound philosophical introspection with cataclysmic supernatural warfare. Xiao Chen, still reeling from the loss of Keke and his own near-death, is drawn into a metaphysical dialogue with the legendary sage Zhuangzi, who appears as a spectral butterfly. The exchange forces Xiao Chen to confront the nature of the Dao itself—what it means, where it lies, and whether inherited wisdom can truly be his own. While Xiao Chen grapples with these eternal questions, the heavens above erupt in a desperate scramble for ancestral god relics, with Lord Tongtian, the Buddha's Seven Treasures Tree, Allah, and Sun Wu locked in a chaotic battle. Below, fearsome sacred beasts like Yazi and Kong Xuan carve through the ranks of opportunistic cultivators. The chapter's dual narrative—inner stillness versus outer chaos—culminates in Xiao Chen's sudden, violent breakthrough from Spirit-Treasury Third Heaven to the Sixth Heaven, a leap fueled not by external treasures but by the crystallization of his own unique path.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen's stone arm and leg fragments from earlier chapters fly into him, completing the stone man within his body—a subtle but crucial step in his physical transformation. Zhuangzi's butterfly leads him into a void and asks a deceptively simple question: 'What is the Dao?' Xiao Chen's initial reply (quoting the Dao De Jing) is dismissed as borrowed. Zhuangzi pushes him to find his own answer. After a period of dark confusion, a flash of insight strikes: the Dao is under his feet—the path he has already walked—and also embodied in those he must overcome. This personal realization shatters his stagnation, unleashing a flood of life essence as his nine divine acupoints connect like stars, forming divine meridians (神脉). The cultivation barrier breaks with a sound like shattering glass, elevating him from Spirit-Treasury Third Heaven all the way to the Sixth Heaven, recovering the 200-year lifespan he had lost. Meanwhile, the battle in the sky intensifies: Lord Tongtian's Four Immortal-Slaying Swords (Zhu Xian, Jue Xian, Lu Xian, Xian Xian) protect a stone sphere and escape into the void. The Seven Treasures Tree, Allah's giant hand, and Sun Wu contend for a stone dagger. Below, Yazi (the dragon's son) tears through fallen angels, and Kong Xuan uses his Five-Colored Divine Light to annihilate a winged dragon and dozens of other cultivators, creating a blood-soaked no-man's land.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Zhuangzi (庄子), one of the foundational Daoist philosophers, is depicted here as a 'Perfected Man' (至人) whose spiritual realm approaches that of an Ancestral God. His famous butterfly dream (庄周梦蝶) is inverted: here the butterfly is real, and it leads Xiao Chen to question the nature of reality and Dao. The exchange echoes classic Chan (Zen) gong'an (koans), where a master forces a student to discard secondhand knowledge. Xiao Chen's final answer—'The Dao is under my feet' and 'I step over you to enter the Dao'—is a radical, iconoclastic stance that mirrors Zhuangzi's own heretical sayings like 'Sages do not die, great thieves do not stop.' The breakthrough mechanic is distinctive: rather than accumulating power, Xiao Chen's nine divine acupoints resonate and form 'divine meridians' (神脉), a rare internal structure that channels life essence directly. This is not a standard cultivation path; it is the fruit of his unique Heavenly Stele Profound Art. The battle above involves figures from multiple pantheons: Lord Tongtian (from Fengshen Yanyi), the Seven Treasures Tree (a Buddhist-Daoist hybrid artifact), Allah (from Islamic tradition), and Sun Wu (the historical military sage). This syncretism is a hallmark of Chen Dong's world-building, treating all mythologies as coexisting cosmic forces.

## Reading Guide
This chapter marks a major inflection point for Xiao Chen. His philosophical breakthrough restores his confidence and sets a new course for his cultivation: he will no longer merely imitate or absorb others' insights but will tread his own path, even if it means crushing the great ones underfoot. Readers should watch for how this new mindset affects his combat style and decisions in the coming arcs. The recovery of his lost lifespan is also critical—his earlier sacrifices had left him vulnerable, and now he is back to full strength. However, the chapter ends with the stone man's completion still incomplete, and the battle for ancestral god relics continues; the stone dagger and the sphere remain unclaimed. The presence of Zhuangzi (and his sudden departure) suggests that other legendary sages may be watching Xiao Chen's progress. The carnage below foreshadows that while Xiao Chen has broken through, the world around him remains a slaughterhouse—he cannot afford to rest. The next chapters will likely see him re-enter the fray, perhaps targeting the Hu and Hai families for their role in Keke's death, now backed by his renewed power.
