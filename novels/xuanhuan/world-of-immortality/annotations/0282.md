## Chapter Overview
Xiao Chen's return to the mortal world brings him face‑to‑face with a past he tried to destroy. Meeting his old friend Chen Fang, he learns of a woman named Ruoshui whom he has inexplicably erased from his heart. This brutal act of self‑severance leaves Chen Fang furious and bewildered, yet Xiao Chen remains unnervingly calm, insisting that moving forward requires cutting away the past. The reunion is steeped in melancholy and unresolved grief, as fragments of Ruoshui's devotion surface through Chen Fang's bitter recollections. The chapter then shifts to the ancient Hua Shan Sword Forum, a once‑legendary gathering now attended by mortal cultivators clinging to faded glory. The arrival of arrogant flying‑sword cultivators from overseas threatens to shatter the assembly—until two unlikely masters emerge from the crowd: a mad old man who destroys a sword with a wad of phlegm, and a child eating a sugar‑coated haw stick who commands the old man as his disciple. The chapter blends raw emotional torment with breathtaking displays of hidden power, reminding readers that the mortal world still holds secrets and strength.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen's reunion with Chen Fang immediately turns hostile when Chen Fang realizes Xiao Chen no longer remembers Ruoshui. Chen Fang's anger reveals the depth of the bond Xiao Chen severed. The third‑eyed cultivator Qingyun uses her heart‑penetration art to confirm that Xiao Chen deliberately excised the imprint of his former lover from his soul, though she notes a bleeding heart beneath his calm exterior. Chen Fang recounts Ruoshui’s devotion: she learned needlework for Xiao Chen, once gave him her life essence at great cost, and after his disappearance, waited years in silence. Xiao Chen listens without visible emotion, but tears escape against his will. He philosophically defends his choice as necessary for new beginnings, but Chen Fang is not mollified. The group then travels to Hua Shan for the legendary sword forum, now a gathering of mortal cultivators. Six overseas flying‑sword practitioners arrive to mock and attack the assembly. A mad old man rises from the ground, shatters their swords with a spit of phlegm, and then kneels before an eight‑year‑old child eating a sugar stick. The child scolds him, and the six invaders flee in terror. The chapter ends with the mortals in awe, realizing that beings of unimaginable power still walk among them.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Hua Shan Sword Forum is directly inspired by the legendary "Hua Shan Discussion of Sword" from Jin Yong's wuxia novel The Legend of the Condor Heroes, where the five great martial artists compete for supremacy. Adapting this to a xianxia setting, the author reimagines it as a gathering of cultivators, highlighting the decline of the mortal world and the lingering memory of its past glories. The act of "severing the heart's shadow" (斩心影) draws on Buddhist and Daoist concepts of cutting attachments to reach higher spiritual states, but Chen Dong presents it as a tragic, surgical necessity that leaves lasting scars. The pair of hidden masters—a mad old man and a child—is a classic trope in Chinese fantasy: the most unassuming figures often wield the greatest power, a device used to subvert expectations and emphasize that true mastery is not about appearance. The overseas cultivators come from islands rich in spiritual energy, reflecting a common setting in Chinese mythology where the immortals live on mystical isles like Penglai.

## Reading Guide
This chapter marks Xiao Chen's definitive return to the mortal world, and his emotional state will be critical to the coming arcs. His decision to sever Ruoshui from his heart is not presented as a triumph but as a bitter wound—readers should watch for how this scar affects future relationships. The sudden appearance of the mad old man and the child suggests that the mortal world is not as defenseless as it seems; these hidden masters may become important allies or wild cards. The Hua Shan Sword Forum is a narrative device to bring together many factions, and the clash with overseas cultivators foreshadows larger conflicts between civilizations. Pay attention to the child's authority: a master of such a powerful old man must have a backstory tied to the ancient peak of mortal cultivation. This chapter also subtly reinforces Xiao Chen's loneliness: his power has grown, but he has paid with his memories and connections.
