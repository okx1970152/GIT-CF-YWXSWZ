## Chapter Overview

In this explosive chapter, Keke unleashes its full fury upon the four sacred beasts that dared to harm the Little Defiant Dragon. The white beast's wrath is absolute, striking down the White Tiger, breaking the Golden Ape's immortal lives, humiliating the Peacock King, and wounding the Golden Lion King. Yet beneath the divine violence lies a poignant undercurrent: Keke's innate mercy, its hesitation before spilled blood, and a final act of healing that restores life from death. The chapter balances brutal combat with moments of tenderness, reinforcing the duality that defines Keke as both a terrifying force and a compassionate guardian.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Keke in a state of unrestrained fury. The White Tiger, one of the four sacred beasts, is instantly killed as Keke's seven-colored divine light sweeps across it. The Golden Ape (Gold), a descendant of a Heavenly God, loses six of its nine immortal lives in rapid succession, its arrogance shattered. The Peacock King, wielding the legendary Chaos Green Feather, attempts to intervene but is swiftly subdued and stripped of its plumage. The Golden Lion King, known for its formidable third eye and astonishing regenerative powers, withstands multiple attacks but is gradually worn down—its recovery strength weakening with each assault.

Keke's strategy shifts from direct destruction to a grim experiment: it forces the Peacock King's Chaos Green Feather against the Golden Lion King's third eye, hoping to break both at once. The collision generates a terrifying dark void and apocalyptic energy, but neither artifact is destroyed, hinting at their incredible resilience. Keke's frustration grows as it realizes it cannot easily destroy these sacred relics. Yet the sustained assault leaves the Golden Lion King nearly crippled, its body repeatedly torn open and healing slower each time. The Peacock King is reduced to a ragged state, its colorful feathers scattered.

Then comes a turning point. The Golden Ape, despite knowing it cannot win, charges forward to protect its childhood friend the Peacock King. Their mutual sacrifice prompts a change in Keke: its rage subsides, and it stays its hand. Two elderly guardians break the arena rules to plead for mercy, and six official protectors arrive, allowing the conflict to end. Keke takes the White Tiger's corpse and the Golden Lion King, flies to the Little Defiant Dragon, and uses the White Tiger's life essence to heal its wounds. The chapter concludes with an old woman attacking Keke in rage, but Keke easily sends her tumbling, showing that even human cultivators cannot match its divine power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of 'nine immortal lives' or 'nine destinies' appears here with the Golden Ape, a direct reference to Chinese folklore's 'nine-tailed fox' and similar motifs where divine beings possess multiple lives. The Chaos Green Feather, traced back to the legendary Peacock King of antiquity, draws on the myth of Kong Xuan who could 'brush away all things' with five-colored light. The third eye of the Golden Lion King is a classic xianxia trope, often representing a hidden divine power that can see through illusions and unleash devastating energy. The healing process using the life essence of a slain sacred beast reflects the principle of 'using one's enemy to strengthen oneself', common in cultivation stories where a practitioner absorbs the power of defeated foes. Keke's hesitation toward bloodshed and its innate kindness, despite possessing overwhelming power, aligns with the archetype of a divine beast that has not lost its pure-hearted nature—a contrast to the ruthless human cultivators often seen in the genre.

## Reading Guide

This chapter showcases Keke's power at a new peak, but also reveals its emotional depth—a key development for the white beast's character arc. The Little Defiant Dragon's healing sets up its recovery and future growth, potentially leading to a breakthrough. The old woman who attacks Keke after the fight is likely connected to the slain White Tiger, hinting at future retaliation from its clan or owners. The other sacred beasts (Golden Lion King, Peacock King, Golden Ape) survive but are deeply wounded, meaning their factions may hold grudges or seek revenge. Keke's mercy might create future allies in the Peacock King and Gold, who witnessed compassion amidst carnage. Readers should watch for how the Little Defiant Dragon's power evolves after receiving the White Tiger's essence. The tension between Keke's gentle nature and its capacity for destruction will continue to play a role in shaping Xiao Chen's group's reputation on the Eternal Continent.
