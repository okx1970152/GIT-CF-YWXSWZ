## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a quiet, introspective interlude following the devastating losses Xiao Chen suffered in Heavenly Emperor City. He lingers in a hidden village of retired experts, physically broken and spiritually hollow, mourning the death of Keke and the betrayal of friends like Hai Yuntian. The idyllic, rustic setting contrasts starkly with the bloodshed and scheming of his recent past. Yet beneath the surface tranquility, Xiao Chen's grief hardens into cold resolve. The chapter focuses on his slow recovery, his encounters with the village's reclusive elders, and his acquisition of rare cultivation techniques—including the legendary Vase Seal. It culminates in a fateful decision: to risk everything on a perilous journey to the Barbarian Sacred Mountain, where he hopes to summon Keke's lost soul. This is a chapter of reflection and rebuilding, setting the stage for Xiao Chen's next, desperate gambit.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen spends four months recovering in a secret village inhabited by retired masters of the Forest and Barbarian clans. His physical condition stabilizes through the life essence stored in his nine divine acupoints, but his spirit is deeply wounded. He reflects on his failures—the loss of Keke, the betrayal of Hai Yuntian, and his own naivety—and resolves to become more cunning without losing his core principles. The village elders, including Turtle Elder and Wang Xianpo, teach him exceptional techniques: a turtle-style breathing method that refines life essence, and the Vase Seal, a supreme hand seal art that Wang Xianpo created by fusing Buddhist principles with natural law. Xiao Chen also learns that the Barbarian Sacred Mountain is the transformed 'Heaven-Earth Furnace' of the ancestral god Suiren, a place where the souls of fallen powerful beings gather. Despite warnings of extreme danger, Xiao Chen decides to go there with the young barbarian Niu Ren to attempt summoning Keke's lingering soul. The chapter ends with his departure, carrying the grief of loss and the weight of a desperate hope.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Vase Seal (宝瓶印) is a hand-seal technique inspired by Buddhist iconography, where the 'vase' symbolizes the perfection of wisdom and the containment of boundless power. In xianxia lore, hand seals (手印, shouyin) are physical gestures that channel spiritual energy and divine law. Wang Xianpo's version combines Buddhist concepts with natural spiritual patterns, making it a rare, self-created supreme art. The Barbarian Sacred Mountain is mythically tied to Suiren (燧人氏), the primordial ancestor who drilled wood for fire. It is described as a 'Heaven-Earth Furnace' (天地铜炉) that was once used to refine the cosmos, but later became a repository for the souls of fallen strong practitioners. The idea of a sacred mountain as a repository of ancestral spirits is common in Chinese mythology—similar to Kunlun or Mount Buzhou. The chapter also touches on the concept of 'life essence' (生命精元) stored in acupoints, a core xianxia principle where the body's hidden energy centers preserve vitality even when a cultivator appears near death. Finally, the retired masters in the village represent the archetype of 'hidden experts' (隐世高手), common in wuxia and xianxia, who have transcended worldly ambitions and now live simply, passing on their wisdom to worthy successors.
