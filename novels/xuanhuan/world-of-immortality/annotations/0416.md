## Chapter Overview

The Heavenly Burial Valley, a forbidden zone that has claimed countless lives over millennia, awakens in this chapter. The ancient stele that seals the valley rises into the sky, drawing cultivators from the Mortal World and foreign kings alike into its depths. Xiao Chen, still in his yellow-diamond skeleton form, sees an opportunity to trap his pursuers by luring them into the valley. But what unfolds is not a simple trap—the valley holds treasures, ancient artifacts, and unspeakable horrors. The chapter masterfully blends the tension of a deadly dungeon crawl with the shock of familiar faces appearing in unexpected places, leaving readers questioning reality and identity.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen leads the enemy kings—Yaksha King, War King Mabao, and others—toward the Heavenly Burial Valley, hoping to turn the gathering cultivators against them. At midnight, the giant stele rises from the valley, tangible and immense, followed by black mist that reveals ancient palace complexes. The valley, once lethal to enter, appears safe, and hordes of cultivators rush in to claim treasures. However, as they delve deeper, a pattern emerges: fallen cultivators bleed into the ground, their flesh vanishes, and only bones remain. Shadowy monsters begin attacking, killing indiscriminately. Xiao Chen uses the Weng sound to defend himself. In the depths, Wu Ming appears with the Indestructible Imperial Heavenly Bell, killing a foreign king before retreating. After passing through forty-nine palaces—a number echoing the Great Derivation—the survivors reach a cliff face with three massive dark caves. From the left cave, three familiar white skeletons peek out: Qin Guangwang, Yanluowang, and Lunhuiwang. From the central cave emerges a woman bearing Qingqing's exact face, crowned and dressed in rainbow battle armor. The third cave reveals a monstrous, armored claw. These visions shake Xiao Chen to his core.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The number forty-nine—the number of palace halls traversed—is rooted in the "Great Derivation" (大衍之数) from the I Ching, where fifty is the number of total transformations, but only forty-nine are employed; one is left to represent the unmanifest. This concept appears multiple times in the novel, such as with the War Swords (forty-nine plus one formation diagram). Here, it suggests that the palace complex is a cosmic formation or sealing array. The Heavenly Burial Valley itself is a legendary site in Chinese xianxia lore, often a place where ancient gods and heroes were interred. The reappearance of the three skeleton kings and a figure resembling Qingqing—who was last sacrificed to seal the Abyss of Evil—raises profound questions. Is this the real Qingqing, reborn or transformed? Or is the valley projecting illusions? The giant armored claw hints at an even greater power sealed within.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a turning point that reunites Xiao Chen with key figures from his past, though in ambiguous forms. Watch for how the skeleton kings—now in the Death World's depths—interact with Xiao Chen in future chapters. The woman who looks like Qingqing may be an ally or a threat; her crown and rainbow armor suggest she commands great authority. The third cave's claw hints at a sealed entity that could be a supreme danger. Xiao Chen's strategic use of the valley to turn the tables on his enemies is classic, but the chapter's real weight lies in these revelations. The stele that rose may also connect to the Yellow River stele and Dragon Island stele, hinting at a larger cosmic plan. The next few chapters will likely involve confrontations with these mysterious beings and the pursuit of the valley's ultimate secret.
