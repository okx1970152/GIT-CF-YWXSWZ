## Chapter Overview
This chapter pivots from direct action to strategic revelation, as the devil’s emergence reshapes the hunt for dragon kings. The alliance has gathered five, but the devil reveals there are eleven total, with three already in the hands of older experts. Xiao Chen, ever cautious, declines to let Keke participate further, fearing long-term enmity with future sovereigns. The winged dragon king’s escape underscores that not all power is brute force—mobility and cunning matter. Meanwhile, the scarred black dragon’s return, wounded from battling a superior, resonates with the novel’s theme of indomitable will. The chapter’s tone is somber and calculating, as the cost of leaving Dragon Island becomes clearer.

## Key Plot Points
The devil’s ultimatum forces Xiao Chen to confront the possibility of being stranded on Dragon Island forever if he does not act soon. The revelation that eleven dragon kings exist—three already captive—means the nine required for the divine ship are nearly within reach, yet the remaining targets prove elusive. The winged dragon king, able to fly from infancy, evades five dragon kings with ease, introducing a new variable: mobility as a decisive advantage. Xiao Chen’s refusal to involve Keke in the hunt demonstrates his protective loyalty and long-term vision; he will not sacrifice a companion’s safety for immediate gain. The reappearance of the solitary black dragon, scarred and defiant after battling a winged dragon king, deepens its mystique. Its survival against the odds echoes the protagonist’s own journey. The devil’s discourse on the ‘sovereign’ level clarifies the power hierarchy: even the Undying King of the Death Swamp is far from true sovereign status—only beings that cannot be killed, only sealed, earn that title. This positions the three skeleton kings as potential future powers.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The term ‘sovereign’ (君王) denotes a transcendent level of power in this world, comparable to dragon kings among dragons. The devil’s dismissal of the Undying King as unworthy of that title emphasizes that true sovereigns are nearly invincible, a concept rooted in Chinese mythological hierarchies where kings and emperors represent different cosmic degrees. The companion dragon kings (伴生龙王) are born alongside an ancestral dragon (祖龙), following the logic that supreme beings attract lesser sovereigns. The winged dragon king’s innate flight breaks the usual pattern of juvenile dragons being grounded; flight typically requires Sky-Mastery realm cultivation. This anomaly suggests special bloodlines or blessings, hinting at deeper world mechanics where exceptions define greatness.

## Reading Guide
Readers should note the devil’s cryptic remarks about the sovereign level—they foreshadow the three skeletons’ evolution and the importance of the undead path. The winged dragon king, having escaped, will likely reappear as a major obstacle or potential ally. Xiao Chen’s restraint in not hunting suggests he may seek alternative means to gain a dragon king, perhaps by earning the black dragon’s loyalty through compassion. The revelation of three dragon kings already captured implies the older generation of cultivators will soon enter the stage as competitors. The chapter closes with Xiao Chen observing the wounded black dragon, setting up a possible bond that could grant him a dragon king without force. Watch how this thread develops—it may provide the key to the divine ship.
