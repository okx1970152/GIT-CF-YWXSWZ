## Chapter Overview
In this poignant and explosive chapter, Xiao Chen returns to his mortal home, only to find that the Yellow River Divine Stele – the very foundation of his cultivation – has vanished. The emotional weight of reunion with his parents and childhood friend Su Ying is immediately overshadowed by the appearance of a new, predatory force: cultivators from the so-called 'Cultivation World' (修真界). These arrogant beings view mortals and traditional warriors as beneath them, and their presence signals a far deeper conspiracy. The tone is one of tragic nostalgia mixed with cold fury, as Xiao Chen is forced to confront the reality that even his home is no longer safe from the machinations of otherworldly powers.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen dives into the Yellow River but finds no trace of the Divine Stele, a monument that had stood unmoved for millennia. A pair of Xiuzhen cultivators – a man and a woman – confront him with contempt, demanding his identity. When pushed, they admit they come from a 'Cultivation World' and are searching for the stele. Rather than reveal their mission, they self-destruct, showing their masters value secrecy over their lives. Returning to his village, Xiao Chen learns that the Xiuzhen have been actively searching the river for months, questioning locals. Later, while visiting the Grand Dragon Terrace (祖龙台), he spots Su Ying – a childhood friend who once loved him – now forced into labor and being harassed by thugs. The Xiuzhen fly overhead but ignore her cries for help, yet they react instantly when the thugs are in danger. Enraged, Xiao Chen slares several Xiuzhen disciples and reveals his Space Divine Law (空间神则), a power they call the 'king of divine principles'. The chapter ends with a direct confrontation between Xiao Chen and two mid-ranked Xiuzhen masters, setting the stage for a new conflict.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The term 'Cultivation World' (修真界) is introduced here as a separate realm of cultivators distinct from the World of Immortality (长生界). Historically, the mortal world once had many cultivation paths, including the 'Xiuzhen' (修真) tradition, which was considered superior to martial arts (武者). The Xiuzhen cultivators use flying swords (飞剑) and possess esoteric techniques like blood-forged swords and binding ropes. Xiao Chen's Space Divine Law (空间神则) is a legendary ability that controls space itself, putting him on a level beyond ordinary martial arts. The conflict between the martial path and the Xiuzhen path mirrors a deeper theme in Chinese fantasy: the tension between innate physical cultivation and external magical arts. The Xiuzhen's arrogance and disregard for mortal life highlight their moral decay, a common critique in xianxia.

## Reading Guide
This chapter marks a major turning point: the introduction of a new world system (Cultivation World) that will likely play a key role in upcoming arcs. The disappearance of the Yellow River Stele is a mystery that ties back to the larger stele narrative from Dragon Island and Paradise Lost. Xiao Chen's connection to Su Ying and his parents creates emotional stakes that may be exploited by his enemies. Pay attention to the Xiuzhen masters' reaction to Xiao Chen's space power – they acknowledge it as 'kingly', but still believe their path is superior. This sets up a philosophical clash between the martial and Xiuzhen traditions. The chapter also deepens Xiao Chen's character: he is gentle with his family, ruthless with evil, and fiercely protective of the innocent. His fury at the Xiuzhen's indifference to Su Ying's plight shows that the mortal world still shapes his moral compass.
