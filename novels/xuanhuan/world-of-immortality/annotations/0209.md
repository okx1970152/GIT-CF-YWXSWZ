## Chapter Overview
This chapter opens with Xiao Chen's unrelenting vengeance. He wields the Golden Divine Halberd and Dark-Iron Seal to slaughter half-gods from the Tiger and Hai families. The tone is cold, brutal, and ultimately hollow. After the slaughter, he flees south, escaping pursuit by Long Teng and the Tiger family expert Hu Lie. He crashes into a pristine, nearly Edenic landscape that triggers a heartbreaking vision of his mortal home—his aging parents and a weeping woman by a river. The chapter closes with Xiao Chen weeping, believing he has returned to his homeland. It is a chapter of visceral action giving way to profound emotional devastation.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen impales and destroys the half-god Tiger Servant (Hu Chuan) with the Golden Halberd, then drives off the Tiger Slave. He kills three Hai family half-gods, including a giant and a mist-form cultivator. The Tiger Servant and Tiger Slave are both killed. He is about to attack the Hai mansion when the Dark-Iron Seal warns him of a deeper threat sleeping there. He hurls the seal into the Hai family compound, destroying half the mansion, then flees. Long Teng intercepts him, demanding the Dragon Sacred Tree, but Hu Lie of the Tiger family arrives, also demanding it. Xiao Chen uses the two divine artifacts to escape at incredible speed, leaving both pursuers behind. Exhausted, he collapses in a beautiful, tranquil valley that resembles his mortal homeland. There, he has a vivid vision—his parents by the village gate and a grieving woman by the river. He wakes crying, believing he has returned home.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The chapter involves 'half-gods' (半神), which in this world's cultivation system are experts at the Sky-Mastery realm. Xiao Chen's ability to defeat multiple half-gods stems from his new Spirit-Treasury realm cultivation and his use of the legendary Golden Divine Halberd and Dark-Iron Seal. These artifacts have their own wills and can be bribed with lifespan. The 'Soul-Sacrifice Vow' mentioned in the previous chapter echoes the xianxia trope of trading lifetimes for power. The vision of home incorporates classic Chinese imagery: parents waiting by the village road, a woman weeping by the willow-lined riverbank—a reference to the longing of a wife or lover. This evokes the novel's early themes of Xiao Chen's forced separation from the mortal world.

## Reading Guide
This chapter concludes the Heavenly Emperor City arc. Xiao Chen's revenge is savage but empty—Keke is still dead (or seemingly dead). His flight into the Southern Wastes opens a new geographical and emotional zone. The vision of home signals that his connection to the mortal world remains a driving force. The mysterious 'old man' behind Long Teng and the Tiger family's Hu Lie are new antagonists. The divine artifacts' warnings hint at much greater powers on the Eternal Continent. The next chapters likely explore the Southern Wastes and Xiao Chen's struggle to recover emotionally and cultivate further. Readers should watch for whether Keke's essence within the Sacred Tree will be revived, and how Xiao Chen will navigate the power politics now that he is a fugitive.
