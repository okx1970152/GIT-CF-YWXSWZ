## Chapter Overview

This chapter unfolds in the oppressive silence of the Death World, where Xiao Chen and his companions—Babra, Sha Polang, and the Little Defiant Dragon—journey along the black, thundering River of Death. The narrative balances two stark registers: the cold, analytical hunt for power, and a sudden descent into raw, personal grief. A cataclysmic migration of white bone creatures draws them to a controversial discovery—a partially petrified Otherworld expert named Ling Hu Sha, who has achieved a dangerous simulacrum of the Stone Man realm through forbidden means. This encounter offers Xiao Chen a new path, but the price of that knowledge is a memory of devastation: a glimpse of Keke, the little white beast, crushed under the attacks of Ancestral Gods on the Ancestral Lord Warship. The chapter closes with Xiao Chen standing before the Death Heavenly Palace, its gray mist swirling around a broken warship stained with Keke's bloodied paw prints—a haunting image that merges cultivation lore with tragic intimacy.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and his group follow the River of Death deeper into the Death World, witnessing a massive flight of bone creatures driven by a distant event. They discover a small stone city with a suspended humanoid figure—Ling Hu Sha—who has achieved a partial, artificial Stone Man state by refining a stone corpse and demon city ruins through a secret method. Xiao Chen captures him easily using the Stone Man King puppet from the jade bottle. Through soul torture, he extracts the forbidden technique, which involves collecting stone remains from shattered demon cities to force a premature petrification. However, the true emotional blow comes when Xiao Chen searches Ling Hu Sha's memories: he finds a vision of Keke, the white beast, bathed in rainbow light, charging against a phalanx of Ancestral Gods on the Ancestral Lord Warship, only to be struck down by their combined divine power. Xiao Chen shatters Ling Hu Sha's head, performs a sacred sacrifice to absorb his essence, and moves forward with grim determination. He then leads the group into the gray mist that periodically conceals the Death Heavenly Palace, where they spot the broken half of the Ancestral Lord Warship—its deck marked with tiny, blood-soaked paw prints.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Stone Man path in this novel is one of the most formidable yet perilous cultivation routes. A true Stone Man is nearly invincible, with a body that rivals Ancestral God artifacts. Ling Hu Sha's method represents a heretical shortcut: rather than undergoing the long, natural evolution of a stone city, he uses the residual energy of a dead Stone Man's corpse and the ruins of a demon city to artificially trigger partial petrification. This yields an incomplete but powerful state between Ancestral God and Stone Man—a kind of 'half-Stone Man' that is both fragile and terrifying. The Death Heavenly Palace is a recurring mystery: a floating complex of ancient halls that appears only when the gray mist—the lingering death-essence of fallen Ancestral Gods—rises. It is said to hold the fates of those who entered before, such as Tianwaitian and Renwairen. Keke's death, if confirmed, would be a profound loss: she was a half-ancestor beast of the cursed heaven-defying clan, and her Seven-Colored Sacred Tree was an artifact of immense potential. The bloodied paw prints on the warship serve as a visceral reminder of the cost of the Otherworld war.

## Reading Guide

This chapter forces Xiao Chen into a pivotal decision: will he adopt the dangerous, artificial Stone Man method to gain power quickly, or will he seek a more natural path? The vision of Keke's death is deliberately left ambiguous—no final outcome is shown, only a frozen frame of struggle. This opens the question of whether Keke survived, perhaps through some hidden fragment of the Lost Paradise or a last-moment intervention. Readers should watch for the next appearance of the Death Heavenly Palace, as it may hold not only the answer to Keke's fate but also the fates of other missing allies. The half-stone man technique, while tempting, carries the risk of becoming a failed Stone Man—a cautionary tale for Xiao Chen. The tension between the urgency to avenge Keke and the need for stable cultivation will define the chapters ahead.
