## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a poignant study in contrasts: the savage Darwinism of Dragon Island’s royal dragons, the fierce pride of a dying little beast, and the sudden emergence of a mysterious divine aura that hints at powers beyond mortal reckoning. Xiao Chen watches a vicious fight between young dragon kings and a stubborn ordinary dragon, then attempts a mercy that is spurned. Later, a valley suffused with golden light and a cottage radiating a godlike presence raises the possibility of a true divine being on the sealed island—and thus a potential weapon against the demon that has been pursuing him. The tone is tragic, desolate, yet threaded with quiet wonder.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen observes the Sacred Mountain at night and discovers three young dragons battling: two are infant dragon kings (one red, one gold) and the third is a common, undersized dragon with no horns and a scruffy black-brown hide. Despite being utterly outmatched and mortally wounded, the little dragon repeatedly charges into the fight, refusing to yield. After the two dragon kings leave, Xiao Chen finds the little dragon near death. Keke, moved by empathy, offers the Sacred Tree’s leaves, and Xiao Chen tries to feed it a snow lotus heart, but the dragon stubbornly rejects all aid—spitting out the lotus and rolling away from the healing paste. Moved by its unyielding pride, Xiao Chen forces a piece of the lotus into its mouth and departs, knowing it will likely die by dawn. Three days later, while traveling toward the outer island, Xiao Chen encounters a beautiful valley where a hut emits waves of golden light and a peaceful, sacred aura. The demon appears and declares it “the aura of a god,” shocking Xiao Chen—if a living god exists on Dragon Island, it might be able to destroy the demon. The golden radiance fades, and the demon relaxes, recognizing the phenomenon as a “divine awakening” rather than a fully manifested deity.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The hierarchy of dragons on Dragon Island is a brutal meritocracy: ordinary dragons, dragon kings (伴生龙王), and the legendary Ancestral Dragon. Dragon kings are born with innate superiority, able to use dragon magic, while common dragons are physically and spiritually weaker. The little dragon’s refusal of help reflects a core xianxia virtue—pride in one’s own path, even to death. In Chinese cultivation lore, accepting aid can be seen as a loss of face or a karmic debt; a true warrior would rather die than owe a stranger. The “divine awakening” the demon senses is likely a being undergoing the transition to the Deathless Longevity or Nirvana realm—a state where the flesh and spirit are reborn, emitting overwhelming sacred radiance. The golden light and serenity mark a being far above Mortal-Shedding, hinting at the island’s hidden depths.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up two major threads: the fate of the stubborn little dragon (will it survive via the snow lotus or die alone?) and the mysterious figure in the valley. The demon’s reaction suggests this divine presence is real but not yet fully mature. Watch for whether Xiao Chen seeks out this being as a potential ally against the demon. Also note the Sacred Mountain’s strange aura—it may hold more secrets about Dragon Island’s sealing and the missing gods. The little dragon’s pride may foreshadow its future significance; a creature that refuses to die might one day become a formidable force. Tensions are high as Xiao Chen balances survival, compassion, and the desperate need for power against an immortal enemy.
