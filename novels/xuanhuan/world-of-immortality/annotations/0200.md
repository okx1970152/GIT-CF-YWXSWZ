## Chapter Overview

This chapter pivots from the brutal duels and beast-fighting spectacles of recent chapters into the quieter but equally treacherous realm of aristocratic politics. Hai Yunxue, the peerless beauty of the Southern Wastes, openly reveals her family’s marriage scheme to Xiao Chen during a moonlit walk on Wangdi Mountain. Her candor strips away the customary veneer of courtly negotiation, exposing the cold calculus behind the great families’ sudden warmth. Yet beneath her vulnerability lies a sharp strategist who knows her worth. Xiao Chen, still simmering from his recent triumphs and wounds, listens without commitment, aware that these alliances are built on sand. The chapter masterfully contrasts the serene beauty of Heavenly Emperor City with the bitter reality of familial obligation. It sets the stage for a high-stakes game of marriage proposals that will tie Xiao Chen to two powerful women, forcing him to navigate a labyrinth of expectations he never sought.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter unfolds across two interwoven strands: the intimate conversation atop Wangdi Mountain and the fallout that follows. Hai Yunxue initiates the dialogue with refreshing honesty, admitting that her family ordered her to lure Xiao Chen into marriage. She confesses her dread of being bartered like livestock, yet finds solace that Xiao Chen is not a brute like Hoffman or a lecher like the Zhuge heirs. This confession humanizes her, making her more than a beautiful pawn. Xiao Chen remains noncommittal, offering only vague promises to help her avoid a forced union, knowing that his own supposed connections to the Southern Wastes will soon be exposed as a misunderstanding. The second strand erupts when news of the Undying Sect’s approval of Xiao Chen’s marriage to Yan Qingcheng spreads through the city. The Hai family counters swiftly, fabricating a prior betrothal between Xiao Chen and Hai Yunxue, claiming their wedding will occur in half a month. Xiao Chen finds himself ensnared in a web of competing claims, unable to laugh as the absurdity turns oppressive. Concurrently, the mysterious elder Long Teng continues to visit Xiao Chen publicly, reinforcing the false impression that Xiao Chen is backed by a South Barbarian powerhouse. The chapter closes with Xiao Chen feeling a chill of entrapment, realizing that his craving for autonomy is being used against him.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter illuminates the role of marriage alliances in the World of Immortality’s aristocratic culture. Families like the Hai, Undying Sect, and Zhuge use marital ties to secure loyalty from rising cultivators. Hai Yunxue’s lament that she cannot choose her own path reflects a common trope in xianxia: the tension between personal freedom and familial duty. Her reference to her aunt, who married a noble wastrel and died in despair, adds tragic depth. The setting of Wangdi Mountain—a legendary peak said to be a woman turned to stone while waiting for the Heavenly Emperor—parallels Hai Yunxue’s waiting for a worthy partner. Culturally, the chapter also highlights the importance of face (mianzi) in these societies; the great families scramble to be first to bind Xiao Chen, not because of genuine affection, but because losing the race would be a public humiliation. The sudden fabrications of betrothals are typical of political maneuvering in xianxia settings, showing how personal bonds are co-opted for power.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a calm before the storm. With two competing marriage claims now official, Xiao Chen’s quiet life in Heavenly Emperor City is about to implode. Watch how he maneuvers between the Undying Sect and the Hai family—his decisions will ripple across the South Wastes. Hai Yunxue’s honesty may foreshadow a genuine alliance, but Yan Qingcheng’s hidden feelings remain unexploded. Long Teng’s involvement also deepens; his persistent favor raises questions about his true motives. The upcoming beast tournament will serve as a crucible where all tensions—romantic, political, martial—may erupt. Readers should note Xiao Chen’s growing unease: he is no longer just a fighter but a pawn in a larger game. The chapter also subtly hints that the marriage to Yan Qingcheng may sever the thread of resentment between them, or twist it into something more dangerous. Prepare for a convergence of personal and political stakes in the chapters ahead.
