## Chapter Overview

In this chapter, Xiao Chen reunites with the cunning Wu Ming and learns of the Martial Ancestor True Scripture – a sentient tome that has emerged in the ancient capital of Yan. Xiao Chen recognizes it as the Martial Imprint he once encountered, now seeking a worthy inheritor. The chapter delves into the nature of cultivation breakthroughs, contrasting those who soar after Nirvana with those who fade. Seeking to repair his broken War Swords, Xiao Chen visits the Artifact Refining Sect, where the elders deliver devastating news: the swords cannot be conventionally repaired. Yet one elder’s hesitation reveals a deeper secret – the swords may be forged from a legendary substance called Eternal Light, formed from the deaths of at least ten Ancestral Gods. Xiao Chen’s ruthless interrogation uncovers this world-shaking revelation, setting a new path forward.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen meets Wu Ming on a scenic terrace in Yan. Wu Ming confesses his bitter frustration: the Martial Ancestor True Scripture, a living entity of green light, rejected him despite his genius, stating that only a pure martial artist might qualify. Xiao Chen realizes this is the same Martial Imprint that once dismissed him as having ‘twenty-year talent.’ He reflects on the transformative power of the Nirvana realm: some rise, some fall. He then asks Wu Ming to borrow the Artifact Refining Sect’s forging manuals to repair his three broken War Swords. At the sect’s divine peak, four Earth-Penetrating realm elders examine the swords and declare them irreparable without an Ancestral God’s power. One elder mutters something about the swords being ‘innate’ – a clue Xiao Chen seizes. He feigns departure, then secretly infiltrates the sect, discovering the elder frantically researching ancient texts. He confronts him and learns the truth: the War Swords are forged from Eternal Light, a substance created when at least ten Ancestral Gods die together, their souls merging into an undying radiance. This revelation stuns Xiao Chen, who realizes the swords’ origin is far more ancient and terrifying than imagined.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Nirvana realm (涅槃境界) is a pivotal threshold in cultivation, where a practitioner’s true potential may emerge or diminish. The novel illustrates this through examples: Chu Xingkuang rose after Nirvana, while many former ‘Ten Prodigies’ faded. This concept emphasizes that early talent is not destiny – profound changes occur within the crucible of rebirth. The ‘Great Derivation’ number (大衍之数五十，其用四十九) is a Taoist cosmological principle applied here to the War Swords: the number forty-nine represents the manifest world, while the unused ‘one’ is the variable, the primordial chaos. This hints that the War Swords are not artificial creations but manifestations of cosmic law. Eternal Light (永恒之光) is a theoretical substance from the Artifact Refining Sect’s highest scripture – the death-essence of multiple Ancestral Gods fused into an inextinguishable radiance, capable of forging weapons that kill gods. This ties the War Swords to the lost epoch of the Ancestral Gods.

## Reading Guide

The discovery of Eternal Light reshapes the stakes for Xiao Chen. His broken War Swords may not be irreparable after all – if he can locate a site where many Ancestral Gods fell, the swords might be reforged. This sets up a quest into the deepest mysteries of the Death World and the forgotten history of the Nine Provinces. Xiao Chen’s brutal interrogation of the elder also signals his willingness to cross ethical lines for power, deepening his morally grey character. Wu Ming’s scheming nature suggests he will not be a passive ally – he too seeks the Eternal Light. Readers should watch for the appearance of the Martial Ancestor True Scripture in Yan, which may attract other ancient beings. The chapter also reinforces that Xiao Chen’s cultivation is now at the Earth-Penetrating realm, allowing him to intimidate even sect elders. Tensions between the human world and the foreign races continue to simmer, with Xiao Chen at the center of the storm.
