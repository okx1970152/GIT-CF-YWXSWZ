## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers an emotional gut-punch as the Ancestral Dragon Ship departs for the long voyage of exile, carrying the chosen elites to safety while Xiao Chen makes the heart-wrenching decision to stay behind. The central theme is the unbearable weight of separation from loved ones and the choice between pragmatic survival and the bonds that give life meaning. Xiao Chen, Keke, and the Little Defiant Dragon abandon their chance at escape, preferring to face an uncertain and likely apocalyptic future with their families and friends. The chapter juxtaposes this solemn departure with a comedic interlude from Jin Sanyi and a rescue mission that introduces a mysterious, god-like old farmer, hinting that even in the darkest hour, unseen forces may be at work.

## Key Plot Points

The Ancestral Dragon Ship departs with a final cry of “See you again in thirty thousand years,” setting a tragic tone as the elites leave their mortal loved ones behind forever. Xiao Chen, unable to bear the thought of returning to a world without his family, leaps from the ship, followed immediately by Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon. Two Ancestral Gods seal the ship to prevent others from following, emphasizing the finality of the departure. Back in the Nine Provinces, Jin Sanyi comically laments his own exclusion from the ship, only to be struck by lightning and bombarded by falling meteors that herald the opening of an Otherworld Ancestral God passage. Xiao Chen and his companions set out to find missing allies, encountering Dugu Jianmo’s daughter, Dugu Shanshan, who tells them that her mother, Ah Bing, and siblings are under attack by Otherworld cultivators. They rush to the rescue, only to find the attackers already dead—frightened to death by a strange old farmer who had passed by. Xiao Chen gives chase but cannot catch the seemingly slow figure, realizing he is a being of incomprehensible power. The old farmer’s cryptic words hint that the situation is not entirely hopeless and that ancient races like the Celestial and Dragon races survived similar purges.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of “thirty thousand years” as a cultivation epoch reflects the novel’s vast timescale, where entire civilizations rise and fall between major conflicts. The departure of the Ancestral Dragon Ship mirrors the archetypal xianxia trope of a chosen exile, where the most talented cultivators are sent away to preserve a bloodline or doctrine, often returning only after the world has been transformed. The number three thousand or ten thousand years is a common motif in Chinese mythology for cycles of destruction and rebirth. The “God of Slaughter” Bai Qi, referenced in earlier chapters, and the mysterious old farmer are examples of hidden masters—beings of immense cultivation who live simply, a staple of the genre. The old farmer’s ability to kill with a glance and move at an unreachable pace despite appearing slow exemplifies the principle that supreme cultivation often transcends visible displays of power.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a crucial turning point: Xiao Chen has rejected the safe path and chosen to fight alongside his loved ones, anchoring his character development in the themes of loyalty and home. Readers should note the arrival of the Otherworld passage—this signals that the invaders are now preparing a full-scale assault, and the time for hiding is over. The mysterious old farmer is likely a key figure; his words about ancient races surviving suggest that the Nine Provinces may have hidden champions yet to emerge. Pay attention to the fate of Dugu Jianmo’s family and whether Ah Bing and the children will become liabilities or catalysts for Xiao Chen’s next moves. The appearance of Jin Sanyi with the Doomsday Heavenly Halberd also hints at that artifact’s continuing significance. The tension is building toward a confrontation where every choice will have weight.
