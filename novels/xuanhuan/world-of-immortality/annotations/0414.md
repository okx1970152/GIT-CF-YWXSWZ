## Chapter Overview
The chapter opens with the awe-inspiring return of the Dragon Clan's War Dragon—the Lion King Dragon, a being who forsook the path of the Ancestral Dragon to evolve solely for combat. Its presence sends shockwaves through the Half-Ancestors and foreign kings, especially when it reveals itself as the mysterious mentor who taught the white beast Keke its divine arts. The War Dragon takes Keke back to Dragon Island, signaling the island's unsealing and the beginning of the trial war. Meanwhile, the Yasha Half-Ancestor, nursing a personal grudge, manipulates the teleportation to trap Xiao Chen in a fatal encirclement of multiple Heavenly Kings. Outnumbered and without his divine altar, Xiao Chen chooses a desperate gambit: instead of retreating to the Dead World, he shatters the void and leads his pursuers into an unfamiliar realm—the Cultivation World. There, he turns predator, vowing to make it their graveyard.

## Key Plot Points
The Lion King Dragon's revelation as the War Dragon of the Dragon Clan reshapes the power dynamics among the Hundred Races. Its casual defiance of the Yasha and Asura Half-Ancestors, and its declaration that the trial war's date was altered by its own hand, demonstrates its unmatched authority. The War Dragon’s protection of Keke, whom it taught secret dragon techniques before her birth, deepens the mystery of her origin. In stark contrast, Xiao Chen’s situation descends into extreme peril. The Yasha Half-Ancestor transparently attempts to murder him by teleporting him into the midst of four Heavenly Kings (Sea God King, Earth King, Yasha Queen, War God King) and fifteen Fish-Leap realm experts. Xiao Chen reacts instantly, killing two of his enemies with combined Eight-Aspect Worlds and Heavenly Stele techniques before shattering the void. He emerges in the Cultivation World (Qi Realm), a realm of sword-immortals and flying artifacts. Realizing he is no longer surrounded, he ambushes a lone foreign king and declares the Cultivation World the burial ground of his enemies. The chapter ends on a note of grim defiance, with local cultivators recognizing Xiao Chen and exclaiming his name.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Lion King Dragon represents a rare deviation from the standard dragon evolution path. In xianxia, most dragons aim to become Ancestral Dragons, the supreme sovereigns of dragon-kind. The War Dragon instead forgoes that goal to become a pure combat guardian – a "War God King" of the Dragon Clan. This echoes the xianxia theme of unique, heterodox cultivation paths that can rival the orthodox. The "trial war" is a classic tournament arc where young elites compete for a chance at half-ancestor ascension, but here it is subverted by half-ancestor politics and personal vendettas. The Yasha Half-Ancestor's blatant manipulation of the teleportation array demonstrates the ruthlessness of the senior generation. The Cultivation World (Qi Realm) is one of the four great worlds – Martial, Qi, Curse, and Soul – mentioned earlier in the cosmology. It is a realm where practitioners refine qi, nurture artifacts, and fly on swords, resembling the classic xianxia setting. The mention of "blood tear bamboo" (血泪竹) as a top-ten divine wood for crafting flying swords is a direct reference to immortal cultivation lore, grounding the new setting in traditional Chinese myth.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a pivot point in the arc. The War Dragon's departure with Keke removes a protective presence, leaving Xiao Chen dangerously exposed. Simultaneously, the shift to the Cultivation World opens a vast, unfamiliar theater of conflict. Readers should pay close attention to how Xiao Chen adapts his strategies – his Eight-Aspect Worlds, Origin Sounds, and pure martial techniques – in a realm where qi refinement and artifact combat are dominant. The half-ancestor Yasha’s open hostility guarantees further traps, and the presence of foreign kings like War King Mabaoo and Yasha Queen promises future confrontations. The isolated kill Xiao Chen executes is just a prelude to a prolonged asymmetrical campaign. The recognition by local cultivators raises the question: does Xiao Chen’s legendary status from the Nine Provinces still carry weight here, or will it bring unwanted attention? The fragment of the blood tear bamboo scene hints at rare treasures that could accelerate his flesh-and-blood rebirth. The chapter’s cold, defiant closing – Xiao Chen’s declaration that the Cultivation World will be the enemy’s burial ground – sets a tone of desperate, relentless determination. This is not a retreat; it is a strategic repositioning for a war of attrition.
