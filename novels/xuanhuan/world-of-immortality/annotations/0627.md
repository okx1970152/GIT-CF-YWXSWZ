## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges Xiao Chen deeper into the enigmatic mausoleum of the Little Stone Emperor, a place where death and mystery converge. After crossing the lethal Black Styx River—a barrier that can kill even Ancestral Gods—the group encounters a stone skeleton guardian, a giant tombstone with a chilling inscription, and seven colossal tombs that challenge all expectations. The atmosphere is thick with dread and the sense of being manipulated. A fleeting shadow, the legendary Moon Shadow Magic Blade, and an imperious figure in dragon robe and crown suggest that the Little Stone Emperor's legacy is far more complex than a simple burial. The chapter masterfully builds tension through cryptic clues and autonomous responses from Xiao Chen's own artifacts, hinting at a larger, unseen design.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen, Keke, and the old Daoist cross the Black Styx using the stone jar to absorb its curses, only to find their return blocked by a stone skeleton that later engages an arriving Stone-Man King (Lishibeast). Forced forward, they discover a massive tombstone inscribed with the Little Stone Emperor's achievements and the ominous final words: 'Welcome home.' This phrase, combined with the earlier 'debate in life, rule in death,' suggests the tomb is a trap for all powerful beings. The group then encounters seven mountain-sized tombs, not a single one, casting doubt on who is buried here. A shadowy figure wielding the Moon Shadow Magic Blade attacks, its projections almost overwhelming Xiao Chen's Divine Diagram. Suddenly, a majestic figure in dragon robe and crown appears, walking past them without acknowledgment. In response, the stone jar's puppet, the five Stone King imprints, and the Otherworld Stone King's Dao imprint all autonomously move to sit before this figure, as if answering a summons. Xiao Chen realizes the situation is spiraling beyond his control, and the old Daoist's knowledge is increasingly suspect.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Black Styx River is a classic xianxia trope—a deathly river that dissolves souls and bodies, often used as a boundary between worlds. Here it is said to be formed from the waters of a Dead Lake where primordial Ancestral Gods fell, imbuing it with curses that can kill even nine-heaven Ancestral Gods. The 'Moon Shadow Magic Blade' is a legendary stone weapon of the Little Stone Emperor, capable of projecting countless shadows, with only one true blade that can cut through space. The seven tombs echo the ancient Chinese practice of multiple burial mounds for a single imperial tomb to confuse grave robbers, but here they may represent different aspects or lives of the Little Stone Emperor. The phrase 'Welcome home' is deeply unsettling, implying that all who enter are considered part of the tomb's collection. The autonomous movement of the stone artifacts suggests they are attuned to the will of the tomb's master, indicating the Little Stone Emperor's influence persists even after death.

## Reading Guide
This chapter raises more questions than answers. Pay close attention to the old Daoist's reactions—his wooden expression and detailed knowledge imply he may be more than a simple guide. The seven tombs are likely a major puzzle; their significance will unfold in subsequent chapters. The dragon-robed figure's identity is murky: is it the Little Stone Emperor's remnant will, a projection, or something else? The fact that the stone puppet and the five King imprints respond to him suggests a deep connection to Xiao Chen's own destiny. The Moon Shadow Magic Blade will probably be a recurring threat. Readers should watch for further clues about the 'home' inscription and what it means for the countless powerhouses who have entered before. The tension is building toward a confrontation that may alter Xiao Chen's cultivation path.
