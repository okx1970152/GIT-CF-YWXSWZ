## Chapter Overview

After the desperate chase and brutal confrontation with Guluo, Xiao Chen finally tastes victory. Wounded but alive, he spends the night cultivating under the moonlight, replenishing his depleted life-force alongside the three enigmatic skeletons whose soul-light seems to flicker with forgotten memories. The chapter opens with a visceral escape scene, then transitions into a rare moment of peace and reflection. The morning brings a stunning encounter: a white-robed monk walking across the sea on a single wooden beam, like a banished immortal from ancient legend. This meeting is not merely a new alliance; it is the key that unlocks the tragic history of Dragon Island and the vanished gods of the World of Immortality.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen lands two decisive sword strikes on the fleeing Guluo, the second piercing his chest and nearly killing him. Guluo escapes only by clinging to his flying scroll, but Xiao Chen has turned the tide emotionally and strategically. Under the moonlight, he cultivates the essence of the moon to restore his nearly dry life-force, while the three skeletons—Qin Guangwang, Yanluowang, and Lunhuiwang—absorb crystals, their bones growing jade-like. The following dawn, Xiao Chen prepares to pursue Zhao Lin'er and Guluo, but is interrupted by the arrival of Yizhen, a young monk who walks on water atop a wooden beam. Yizhen reveals that this island is Dragon Island, sealed in primordial times by a divine stele that stripped all dragons of their divine powers. He further recounts that after the dragons were sealed, the ancestral gods Suiren, Youchao, Fuxi, Laozi, Buddha, and others vanished without a trace. No one knows what happened to them, but the island's mysteries are only the beginning of a much greater cosmic enigma.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces a cornerstone of the World of Immortality's mythic history: the divine stele that seals Dragon Island. The concept of a heavenly monument that suppresses an entire race echoes the myth of the Yellow River Stele from Xiao Chen's childhood, tying his personal destiny to a wider cosmic pattern. The vanished gods—Suiren (who brought fire), Youchao (who built the first shelters), Fuxi (the culture hero), Laozi, the Buddha, and others—are canonical figures in Chinese mythology. Their disappearance frames the setting as a fallen age: once home to supreme beings, now a realm where even dragon kings are reduced to beasts. The monk Yizhen, who speaks without Buddhist formalities and walks across the sea, embodies a transcendent mastery that harkens back to the legendary Bodhidharma's "one reed crossing the river," blending martial achievement with spiritual detachment.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a pivot from pure survival to world-spanning mystery. Readers should pay close attention to Yizhen's words: the fate of the vanished gods is the central riddle of the novel, and Dragon Island is but the first clue. Watch how Xiao Chen's personal struggle against enemies like Zhao Lin'er and Guluo will now intertwine with this ancient history. The three skeletons' growing sentience and their pale moonlight memories hint at a connection to the past that may prove crucial. The chapter also subtly reinforces the theme of cycles—Xiao Chen has just escaped one predator only to encounter a larger, cosmic threat. The calm before the lore storm is a moment to breathe, but the stillness is deceptive. Great revelations carry great dangers.
