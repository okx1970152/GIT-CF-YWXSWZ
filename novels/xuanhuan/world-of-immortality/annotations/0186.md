## Chapter Overview

The beast tournament reaches its climax as six formidable young sacred beasts qualify for the final melee. The Golden Lion King demonstrates overwhelming power by crushing a Black Tortoise—renowned for its defense—and devouring its essence. Xiao Chen observes this deadly competition with growing concern, knowing his own Little Defiant Dragon will soon face these predators. Meanwhile, the White Tiger reveals its divine markings, the Red Dragon King proves its savage pedigree, and a golden ape descendant of a Heavenly God secures its place. Two dragon kings mysteriously withdraw, casting an uncertain shadow over the final contest.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the Golden Lion King’s decisive victory: its red light shatters the Black Tortoise’s legendary defense, a feat that stuns the audience. It consumes the fallen beast’s essence, underscoring the brutal law of the arena—victors grow stronger by absorbing the defeated. That evening, Fatty Zhuge arrives with a portion of the tortoise meat, and a small group including Hai Yuntian, Kanasi, and her brother Qilao share a forbidden feast, blending camaraderie with the grim reality of the tournament. The next day, the White Tiger faces a four-imagery beast (a chimera of lion, bear, horse, and tiger) and, after a prolonged defense, unleashes its true power: divine patterns (神纹) that detach and form a luminous tiger, tearing the opponent apart. This establishes the White Tiger as a top contender. The Red Dragon King, tamed by Dugu Jianmo, crushes its opponent through sheer ferocity. The golden ape Gold advances effortlessly. The Winged Dragon King and Evil Dragon King withdraw, their mysterious owners apparently fleeing trouble, disappointing the crowd. The six finalists are set: Golden Lion King, Red Dragon King, White Tiger, Gold, Peacock King, and Little Defiant Dragon.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Beast essence consumption is a key mechanic in the World of Immortality. When a sacred beast dies, its vital essence (精气) can be absorbed by the victor to enhance its life force and potential—not an instant boost but a long-term improvement in vitality and latent power. This reflects the novel’s theme of survival of the fittest. The White Tiger’s "divine patterns" (神纹) are ancient, heaven-granted symbols with mystical power. They are described as a form of "god script" (神文) that contains ultimate truths; only rare beings manifest them. This concept ties into Chinese mythology where patterns on creatures or objects are seen as inscriptions of cosmic law. The four-imagery beast is a variant of the legendary "Four Unlikes" (四不像), a chimera often associated with the divine beast Qilin. The Peacock King is confirmed as a descendant of a Heavenly God, aligning it with the golden ape Gold and the mysterious woman in black mist.

## Reading Guide

With the final six revealed, the next rounds promise chaotic free-for-all combat. Watch for the Little Defiant Dragon’s growth—it has recovered and strengthened, now one and a half meters long with jade-like scales. The withdrawals of two dragon kings hint at larger forces at play; their owners sensed danger or were called away, possibly connected to the Phoenix Saintess or the mysterious power behind this tournament. The golden ape Gold and the Peacock King both carry divine bloodlines, and their confrontation with Keke (who defeated Gold earlier) may be foreshadowed. Xiao Chen’s concern for his dragon is plain—the Golden Lion King’s power is formidable, and the White Tiger’s divine patterns make it a terrifying opponent. The final battle will likely test every sacred beast to its absolute limit.
