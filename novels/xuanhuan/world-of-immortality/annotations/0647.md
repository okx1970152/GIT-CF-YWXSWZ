## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges Xiao Chen into a dire predicament: trapped as a mark on the ancient stone jar, he can do nothing but watch as the terrifying demon shadow refines the vessel. The demon shadow, a malevolent entity that even a coalition of Stone-Man Kings could not destroy, has discovered the new imprint. Xiao Chen is paralyzed, vulnerable to a fatal attack at any moment. Desperation forces him to adopt a fraught solution: take control of the peerlessly beautiful female corpse—a vessel of immense but alien power. The transformation is jarring, but survival trumps all. With guidance from a fading Light Point, Xiao Chen learns to wield the female corpse's strength and the legendary Five Emperor Tower, setting the stage for a desperate counterattack against an almost invincible foe. The chapter balances claustrophobic dread with the explosive release of forbidden power.

## Key Plot Points
- Xiao Chen, reduced to a mark on the stone jar, barely survives the demon shadow's violent attempts to erase him. The jar's indestructibility shields him.
- A fading Light Point (the remnant of the Stone-Man King puppet) reveals the method to escape: Xiao Chen must mentally inhabit the female corpse, gaining control of her body.
- After observing that the demon shadow cannot maintain a stable form indefinitely, Xiao Chen seizes the moment when it re-forms. He commands the female corpse to swing the Five Emperor Tower, shattering the shadow.
- Despite the initial success, Xiao Chen knows he cannot truly kill the shadow with raw power alone. The female corpse lacks the Dao-brand mastery needed for a permanent victory.
- The Stone-Man King puppet sacrifices its remaining essence, burning its Dao-prints to empower the Five Emperor Tower. This allows Xiao Chen to briefly unleash supreme might, suppressing the demon shadow within the tower.
- The Five Emperor Tower's inner carvings respond to the sacrifice, generating a sealing force. Xiao Chen pours the female corpse's boundless divine energy into the tower to sustain the seal.
- Weakened and pursued, Xiao Chen flees toward the deepest part of the stone-gate world, leaping into a pitch-black abyss.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter exemplifies the theme of "borrowing a corpse to reanimate the soul" (借尸还魂), a common trope in Chinese fantasy where a cultivator inhabits another's body. Here, Xiao Chen reluctantly controls a female corpse—a vessel of incredible power but lacking her own Dao awareness. The Five Emperor Tower (五帝塔) and Five Emperor Seal (五帝印) are likely references to the legendary Five Sovereigns (五帝) of ancient China, who brought order to the world. Such artifacts in xianxia are often sentient or contain the imprint of past owners. The demon shadow's periodic dissipation and re-formation echoes the instability of undead entities in Chinese mythology, which often rely on external souls or energies to maintain cohesion. The Stone-Man King's self-sacrifice, burning its Dao-prints, reflects the ultimate price of cultivating a personal Dao—giving up one's very essence for a final act.

## Reading Guide
Xiao Chen's leap into the abyss signals the end of this intense clash but opens a new phase of vulnerability. What lurks in the depths? The demon shadow, though suppressed, is not destroyed—it will undoubtedly return. The female corpse's power is fading as its inner energy is drained into the Five Emperor Tower. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen will replenish his strength and whether the female corpse's body can be a long-term asset or a liability. The Five Emperor Tower has shown sentient properties; its full nature remains to be explored. The sacrifice of the Stone-Man King puppet carries emotional weight, as Xiao Chen's bond with his allies deepens even as his isolation grows. The next chapters likely shift to survival and exploration in an unknown realm.
