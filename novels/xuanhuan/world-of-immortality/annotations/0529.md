## Chapter Overview
In this chapter, Xiao Chen confronts the mysterious seventh Heavenly Stele, a being of near-sentience that demands immense life force and the unique ‘heavenly marks’ from previous steles to unlock its secrets. The wise but infuriating old stone tortoise reveals that the legendary Martial Ancestor once attempted the same path but lacked the necessary marks, achieving only partial understanding. This revelation deepens the mythic significance of the Heavenly Stele Profound Art, framing it not as a mere martial technique but as a universal essence bridging all cultivation systems. The chapter builds toward a desperate gamble: Xiao Chen uses a massive Otherworld Ancestral God corpse as a vessel for the forbidden Saint Sacrifice, hoping to ignite enough power to activate the stele’s inheritance. The tone is one of tragic determination, balancing the old tortoise’s sardonic jabs with the weight of Xiao Chen’s resolve.

## Key Plot Points
- The old stone tortoise, an ancient being of unknown depth, reveals that someone before Xiao Chen has similarly studied the Heavenly Stele method: the Martial Ancestor. He did not possess heavenly marks from the other steles and could only glean fragmentary insights.
- The tortoise clarifies that the Heavenly Stele Profound Art is not purely martial—it is a condensation of supreme Dao, adaptable by any cultivation system. Xiao Chen realizes his earlier assumption was limiting.
- The seventh stele is fundamentally different: its inscriptions have become sentient, able to form living dragon-script creatures that attack intruders. Xiao Chen’s thirty War Swords hold them at bay.
- To unlock the stele, two conditions are required: sufficient life energy and the sacred ‘heavenly marks’ imprinted by the previous six steles. Xiao Chen possesses two such marks—from the Yellow River stele and the Dead City stele.
- Xiao Chen decides to perform the Saint Sacrifice (a ritual to extract pure essence from a powerful corpse) using the fallen black Ancestral God as fuel. He merges his life essence and consciousness into the corpse, awakening its dormant power.
- The sacrifice commences: the black giant sits, his body ignites with divine fire, and ancient chanting fills the realm. Xiao Chen’s first heavenly mark activates, summoning a phantom of the Yellow River stele from afar, linking the two steles in a cosmic alignment.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
- **Saint Sacrifice (圣祭)**: A cultivation ritual that consumes the life essence of a supreme being—here an Ancestral God corpse—to fuel the practitioner’s breakthrough or unlock a treasure. It is described as ‘going against heaven’ and is accompanied by ancient, resonant chanting that mimics both Buddhist and demonic hymn, emphasizing its transcendent and borderline taboo nature.
- **Heavenly Marks (天痕)**: Indelible imprints left on a cultivator’s soul by a Heavenly Stele when it departs. They act as keys to access later steles. Xiao Chen carries marks from the Yellow River and Dead City steles, making him uniquely qualified to approach the seventh.
- **The Martial Ancestor**: A legendary figure who synthesized his own supreme martial arts by drawing from many sources, including the Heavenly Steles. His inability to fully comprehend the seventh stele underscores the difficulty of the task and the rarity of Xiao Chen’s opportunity.
- **The sentient stele**: Unlike the first six, the seventh stele’s symbols have become animate, capable of attacking. This suggests the stele has accumulated enough power over ages to develop a form of consciousness, guarding its teachings with lethal tests.

## Reading Guide
- Watch for the outcome of the Saint Sacrifice: will Xiao Chen succeed in unlocking the seventh stele, or will the ritual fail catastrophically? The black giant’s body is already beginning to burn, but the process is dangerous and may attract unwanted attention.
- The old stone tortoise’s true nature remains veiled. His casual knowledge of the Martial Ancestor and the steles suggests he is far more than a mere relic. Pay attention to his cryptic remarks—they may foreshadow greater trials ahead.
- The connection between the two heavenly marks Xiao Chen possesses may be crucial. The chapter ends with the Yellow River stele phantom manifesting, hinting that the seventh stele’s activation might draw on the power of all previous steles. This could have implications for the wider plot, including the siege on the Nine Provinces.
- Finally, note the emotional undercurrent: Xiao Chen’s impatience with the tortoise’s sarcasm yet his respectful engagement reflects his growth—he is willing to learn, even from a maddening teacher. His decision to sacrifice a fallen enemy’s body shows his pragmatism has not dulled his reverence for power.
