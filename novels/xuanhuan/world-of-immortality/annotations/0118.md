## Chapter Overview

This chapter shifts from the desperate pursuit of escape to a quieter, more introspective confrontation with the island's ancient secrets. Stranded on Dragon Island after missing the Ancestral Dragon Ship, Xiao Chen and Yan Qingcheng seek the Demon for knowledge of the alternative King Ship. The Demon reveals a startling truth: to safely sail the King Ship, they need a yin sacred object—specifically, Suiren's burial shroud, said to lie at the base of the divine stele. The tone is desolate yet charged with a flicker of hope, as the reality of their captivity sinks in. Amidst the grim planning, a subplot of relational warmth emerges: Keke communicates with the stubborn little defiant dragon, hinting at a deeper bond and a possible miracle.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and Yan Qingcheng, accompanied by Keke and the three skeletons, visit the Demon in his snowy mountain lair to inquire about the King Ship. The Demon explains that the King Ship, also called the Ghost Ship, is sealed with the remnants of a king's soul and would kill ordinary passengers without a protective yin sacred object. He dismisses the possibility of using Keke's sacred tree, as it is a life-oriented treasure that might clash with the ship's death energy. The only known yin sacred object on the island is Suiren's burial shroud, located at the base of the divine stele in the Bone Sea—a place even the Demon cannot approach without powerful sacred artifacts like the Buddha's Dharma Wheel or the Golden Divine Halberd. Xiao Chen recalls the shroud they encountered in the City of the Dead, but the Demon clarifies that was merely a cloth stained by a powerful god's blood, not a true sacred object. Reflecting on their predicament, Xiao Chen voices the grim possibility of being trapped forever, which leads to a tense yet oddly familiar exchange with Yan Qingcheng. The chapter culminates with the reappearance of the little defiant dragon, now stronger with a budding horn, and a surprising development: Keke communicates with it, and later the little dragon arrives at the Bone Sea carrying something in its mouth, suggesting a potential turn of fortune.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter introduces the King Ship (君王船), a vessel distinct from the Ancestral Dragon Ship (祖龙神船). While the Ancestral Dragon Ship requires nine dragon kings and is a safe passage for living beings, the King Ship is a darker alternative—built for the dead or those willing to risk ghostly possession. The Demon's explanation of yin sacred objects (阴性圣物) highlights a key xianxia concept: the balance of yin and yang energies. A yang (life-filled) object clashes with death aura, while a yin object aligns with it, granting protection. The reference to Suiren (燧人氏) is monumental: Suiren is the primordial Ancestral God who discovered fire, a foundation of human civilization. That his burial shroud might exist on Dragon Island, at the base of the sealing stele, ties the island's mystery directly to the lost age of the Nine Provinces' ancestors. The divine stele's lethal barrier, which disintegrates all physical matter that approaches, echoes the life-forbidden zones characteristic of high-level cultivation worlds. Lastly, Keke's ability to communicate with the little defiant dragon hints at universal animal-speech or soul-level understanding, a trait often reserved for divine beasts.

## Reading Guide

The little defiant dragon's arrival with an unknown object sets up a crucial turning point. Readers should watch for what it brings—could it be the very shroud they need, or another artifact that opens a path to the divine stele? The bond between Keke and the little dragon deepens, suggesting the little defiant dragon may become a key ally. Additionally, the Demon's timeline (months to years) creates tension: Xiao Chen is unwilling to wait long, so any shortcut must come soon. The discussion of the King Ship and its dangers foreshadows a high-stakes departure, potentially more perilous than the Ancestral Dragon Ship. The unresolved tension between Xiao Chen and Yan Qingcheng continues, but their forced proximity may lead to a grudging partnership. Finally, the mention of Suiren's shroud connects to the larger narrative of the Nine Provinces' ancestors and the sealing of Dragon Island, hinting at revelations about the island's true purpose and the fate of the primordial gods.
