## Chapter Overview
In this chapter, Xiao Chen continues his rampage through Heavenly Emperor City, now confronting Chu Xingkuang, one of the legendary Four Heroes of Yindu. The chapter opens with Xiao Chen effortlessly dispatching Hu Zifeng, a seventh heaven Mortal-Shedding expert of the Tiger clan, using a combination of fierce combat techniques including the Lion's Roar Seal and the Vairocana Bottle Seal. The crowd is electrified. But the true challenge arrives when Chu Xingkuang steps forward, deploying the legendary Destiny Twin Sons divine ability, which splits him into two beings wielding the Heavenly Buddha Wheel and Hades' Death Scythe. These twins cannot be killed—they regenerate endlessly. Xiao Chen must find a way to break this immortal cycle before he is overwhelmed.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter begins with Xiao Chen (as Xiao Shishui) having killed Hu Zifeng. Hu Zifeng attempts a desperate self-destruction but is stopped by Xiao Chen's Vairocana Bottle Seal, which obliterates his lower body. Xiao Chen finishes him with the Immovable Wisdom King Seal. The crowd is stunned. Then, the Chu family's three death warriors attack—all Mortal-Shedding fourth or fifth heaven. Xiao Chen uses the Lingxi Sword Wave, creating an eighty-meter death zone that reduces them to dust in moments. Chu Xingkuang enters the arena. He uses the Destiny Twin Sons divine ability, splitting into a benevolent son and a malevolent son. The twins are intangible—they can be destroyed but instantly reform. Xiao Chen tests several seals: the Lion's Roar Seal and the Perfect Vase Seal shatter them but they return. Chu reveals his ultimate weapons: the Hades' Death Scythe (malevolent) and the Heavenly Buddha Wheel (benevolent). Xiao Chen is struck and bleeds from the mouth. The crowd now believes Xiao Chen cannot win. But Xiao Chen, recalling Chu's boast that the twins can only be killed by sealing the evil son in heaven and the good son in hell, declares he will do exactly that, setting up the next battle.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Destiny Twin Sons divine ability is a rare and fearsome technique that splits the practitioner into two personas: good and evil. In Chinese mythology, this mirrors the concept of dual nature—yin and yang, good and evil—existing within one person. The ability to endlessly regenerate makes it nearly unbeatable; Chu claims it can only be defeated by sealing the evil son in heaven and the good son in hell, a paradoxical feat. This ties into Buddhist cosmology, where heaven (deva realm) and hell (naraka) are opposite extremes. The Heavenly Buddha Wheel and Hades' Death Scythe are mythological weapons representing ultimate good and ultimate evil, often seen in Buddhist and Hindu iconography. Xiao Chen's counter-strategy shows his deep understanding of opposing forces and his ability to think beyond brute force.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up the climax of Xiao Chen's conflict with the Four Heroes. Chu Xingkuang's Destiny Twin Sons ability is perfectly countered by Xiao Chen's stated plan to suppress the twins into heaven and hell, but how will he accomplish this? Readers should watch for whether Xiao Chen will use the Buddha's Dharma Wheel (from the Dead City) or his newly acquired dragon-clan divine arts (Binding and Sweeping) to achieve this separation. The chapter also deepens Xiao Chen's reputation; he has now killed a seventh heaven expert and three fourth-to-fifth heaven practitioners with contemptuous ease. The next chapter promises to resolve the standoff with the Destiny Twins and possibly reveal more of Xiao Chen's hidden techniques.
