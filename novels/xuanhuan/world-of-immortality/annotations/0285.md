## Chapter Overview

This chapter cuts to the bone of Xiao Chen's tragic past. A chance glimpse of a purple jade bangle on a young cultivator's wrist reignites a connection he thought he had severed: the memory of Ruoshui, the woman he loved in the mortal world. What follows is not a negotiation but a slaughter. Xiao Chen moves through the young cultivators like a scythe, his detached efficiency masking the emotional weight of reclaiming a family heirloom. The chapter balances harrowing combat with a poignant reminder that even in the World of Immortality, mortal bonds leave indelible scars. The three old masters—Tianwaitian, Renwairen, and Shanwaishan—also clash with the older cultivators, setting the stage for a confrontation far larger than a single trinket.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Chen Fang's panicked cry, pointing out the bangle on a white-clad young cultivator. Xiao Chen immediately recognizes it as his family's heirloom, given to Ruoshui. When questioned, the young cultivator is flippant, claiming it was a gift from a lover. Tensions escalate as Tianwaitian, Renwairen, and Shanwaishan confront the older cultivators. Tianwaitian, despite being sealed by the Nine Provinces' restrictions, unleashes a devastating burst of power, tearing an old cultivator apart with his bare hands. This display shocks both sides. The remaining eight old cultivators deploy the Iron Blood Flag formation, trapping the three masters. Inside the formation, Tianwaitian sends a private message to Xiao Chen, urging him to retrieve the bangle and hinting it may be tied to the Nine Provinces' seal. Xiao Chen then turns his attention to the younger cultivators, who attack en masse. He wields the Eight Aspect Worlds at extreme speed, combined with ancient martial techniques, and methodically kills over a dozen of them, shattering their weapons with his bare hands. He finally corners the white-clad youth, demanding the truth about the bangle and Ruoshui's fate.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The purple jade bangle is a classic xianxia token: a family heirloom passed down through generations, often used as a betrothal gift. In Chinese cultivation culture, jade bracelets can be enchanted as spatial storage rings or defensive treasures. The young cultivator wears it as a mark of a romantic relationship, which angers Xiao Chen. The concept of 'cutting someone from the heart' (斩灭心间) is a cultivation mental technique to sever emotional attachments for spiritual progress. However, this chapter shows that such severance is never absolute, as the bangle revives memories. The Iron Blood Flag formation (铁血大旗) is an advanced sealing technique, using eight banners to create a localized realm of suppression, seen often in epic battles between high-level cultivators. Tianwaitian's ability to kill an old cultivator despite the Nine Provinces' seal hints at the terrifying latent power of ancient martial artists, a recurring theme in the novel.

## Reading Guide

This chapter deepens the mystery surrounding Ruoshui and the true nature of the Nine Provinces' seal. The young cultivator's evasiveness suggests Ruoshui may be in grave danger or has been used by the cultivation sects. Xiao Chen's emotional state is fragile despite his cold exterior; the bangle may become a major plot device linking the mortal and immortal worlds. Tianwaitian's hint about the bangle's connection to the seal will likely drive future conflicts. Readers should pay close attention to the young cultivator's sect affiliation—it may belong to a faction with ancient ties to the seal's suppression. The chapter also showcases the gap between sealed and unsealed power: Tianwaitian's brief rampage demonstrates what the three masters are capable of when unrestrained.
