## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a critical shift from the savage wilderness of Dragon Island to the structured, treacherous politics of a great sect. Xiao Chen, accompanied by Yan Qingcheng and his mysterious companions, enters the heart of the Undying Sect—a lineage tracing back to the legendary Evil King. The tone is one of cautious tension: the serene, paradise-like surroundings belie the deadly undercurrents of interrogation and rivalry. Xiao Chen must maintain a delicate cover story about Yan Qingcheng's enslavement and his killing of her uncle, Wang Hao, while facing the oppressive scrutiny of the sect leader—a figure whose very presence seems to exist beyond normal space. The chapter layers political maneuvering with mythic intrigue, especially when the sect leader's attention turns to Keke, the little defiant dragon, and the three skeletons wearing the demon's shed skins, leading to an astonishing revelation.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and Yan Qingcheng arrive at the Undying Sect's sacred land, a realm of flying dragons, guardian beasts, and immortal-like scenery. They are met by Qin Yi, a senior disciple whose polite words carry veiled threats about the "Crushing Demon Seed" cultivation method. Xiao Chen deliberately provokes Qin Yi by feigning romantic interest in Yan Qingcheng, a move that spreads gossip and deepens the rivalry. The sect leader, a seemingly ancient being at the demigod level, summons Xiao Chen for a private interrogation. With immense psychic pressure, he questions Xiao Chen about Wang Hao's death and the slave rumor. Xiao Chen holds firm to the prearranged story, barely enduring the leader's near-crushing aura. The leader then insists on seeing Keke and the little defiant dragon. Upon viewing the three skeletons clad in the demon's shed skins, the leader is visibly shocked, muttering about their impossible identity—a moment that hints at vast, hidden lore connecting these undead beings to the world's ancient history.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The "Crushing Demon Seed" (碎魔种神) technique is a classic xianxia trope: a cultivator uses a mortal as a "cauldron" or crucible, often a hated enemy, to sharpen their will and break through cultivation bottlenecks. Yan Qingcheng's choice of Xiao Chen—a far stronger cultivator—is unconventional and laden with risk. The Undying Sect's lineage from the Evil King (邪王) taps into Chinese martial arts lore, specifically the character Shi Zhixuan from the novel "The Smiling, Proud Wanderer." This cross-referencing enriches the world. The sect leader's ability to appear "in another space" reflects higher cultivation realms (Spirit-Treasury and above), where one's presence becomes elusive and reality-warping. The three skeletons wearing the demon's shed skins harks back to earlier arcs involving the demon's cyclical molting; the leader's shock suggests these skeletons might be reincarnations of ancient undying kings, a major lore payoff.

## Reading Guide

Yan Qingcheng's cooperation is fragile, and the bond of the Crushing Demon Seed may become a double-edged sword. The chapter ends on a note of revelation—the skeletons' past might be linked to the City of the Dead and the Undying King, deepening the overarching mythos. The next chapter will likely explore these revelations and their implications for Xiao Chen's survival and escape from the island.
