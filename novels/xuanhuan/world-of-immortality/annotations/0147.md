## Chapter Overview
Xiao Chen is unceremoniously summoned to the mansion of Prince Sun Miao, a visiting royal from the Tianluo Kingdom, but he refuses to be treated as a spectacle for the prince's amusement. After a series of escalating provocations, Xiao Chen decides to confront the prince directly, crashing the banquet and seating himself among the noble guests. This chapter crackles with political tension, as the prince's attempt to assert dominance collides with Xiao Chen's unyielding pride, while the watchful heirs of the Southern Wastes' great families gauge the outcome. The poem 'A Beauty in the North' sets the atmosphere, framing the delicate balance between desire, power, and defiance that runs through the scene.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens at a banquet where Prince Sun Miao hosts several influential young heirs from the Southern Wastes, including Yan Qingcheng, Hai Yunxue, Zhuge Ming, and the carefree Kanasi. The prince, frustrated by being ignored by Heavenly Emperor City's lord, shifts his focus to the handsome women present, but his true intention is to test Xiao Chen. When he asks Yan Qingcheng about Xiao Chen, her evasive response reveals past humiliation on Dragon Island, intriguing the other guests. The prince then proposes to have his own experts duel Xiao Chen for entertainment. Most of the guests agree, with Kanasi openly enthusiastic, while others see an opportunity to probe the prince's strength. Meanwhile, Xiao Chen beats up the prince's soldiers for a third time, infuriating Sun Miao. Hai Yuntian warns Xiao Chen and offers to rally city allies, but Xiao Chen chooses to face the prince directly. He strides into the banquet hall without announcement, ignores the prince, and sits next to Kanasi, calling her 'little sidekick.' This brazen act shocks everyone, turning the banquet into a stage for his defiance and setting up an inevitable confrontation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The opening lines quote a classic Chinese poem, 'A Beauty in the North,' from the Han dynasty, which describes a peerless beauty capable of toppling cities and kingdoms. In the Xianxia tradition, such poetry is often used to evoke the tension between personal desire and political power. The banquet scene illustrates how noble families use beautiful women as diplomatic assets—their marriages and alliances are tools for consolidating power. The Beast Fighting Palace and noble houses like the Rigen and Mander families represent the interwoven interests that dominate the Southern Wastes. Prince Sun Miao's status as a 'Prince of the Realm' (亲王) carries weight in Tianluo Kingdom, but in the free city of Heavenly Emperor City, his authority is limited, creating friction. Xiao Chen's act of seating himself without permission is a direct challenge to the prince's face (面子), a concept of social honor critical in Chinese culture. The term '蛮夷之地' (barbarian land) is used dismissively by the prince's men, reflecting a civilizational hierarchy that Xiao Chen flouts.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for a major political conflict in Heavenly Emperor City. Xiao Chen's open defiance makes him a target for Prince Sun Miao, who will likely escalate his response. The subtle reactions of the other guests—Yan Qingcheng's guardedness, Hai Yunxue's silence, Zhuge Ming's calculation—hint at deeper schemes. Readers should watch for how the noble families align themselves: will they back the prince, support Xiao Chen, or exploit the chaos? The mention of Yan Qingcheng's past humiliation on Dragon Island also suggests unresolved threads from the earlier arc. The coming chapters promise a clash between raw personal power and imperial authority, with the Beast Fighting Palace and the city's true rulers lurking in the background.
