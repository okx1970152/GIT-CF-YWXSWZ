## Chapter Overview

Xiao Chen ventures deeper into the death world and stumbles upon a scene of utter devastation. A mysterious, flesh-and-blood entity has single-handedly wiped out an entire castle of fire creatures, leaving only shattered bones and stolen fire seeds. Taking advantage of the carnage, Xiao Chen collects the leftover fire seeds and advances his own cultivation, his blue crystalline bones now flecked with blood-red specks. But this destroyer is no random killer — he hunts castles that have refined his race's flesh into Life Source Liquid, a forbidden act. Soon, a Red Diamond King intercepts him, and the two clash with world-shaking force. As Xiao Chen watches from a distance, a little boy — flesh-and-blood, innocent, and utterly out of place — appears silently behind him, raising the stakes of the chapter.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen arrives at a massive red-stone castle wreathed in black mist. A powerful, flesh-and-blood figure emerges, casts a blood-red glance his way, and then departs at incredible speed. Inside the castle, Xiao Chen finds every fire creature killed — skulls crushed, fire seeds scattered except for the castle master's. This is a total massacre. He collects nine same-tier fire seeds and absorbs them, strengthening his bones with red crystalline veins. He then follows the destroyer's path to a second castle, encountering the same carnage and gathering ten more fire seeds. At a third castle, he witnesses the destroyer confronting the Griffin Castle Master, accusing it of refining his race. The destroyer crushes the griffin with contemptuous ease, then methodically slaughters the remaining fire creatures. The Red Diamond King arrives to challenge him, and the two clash, sending debris and dust across the plain. As Xiao Chen watches, a little boy of about five or six appears directly behind him — a flesh-and-blood child with no warning. The chapter ends on this unsettling note.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter expands the death world's hierarchy. Flesh-and-blood beings are rare and powerful in this realm, contrasting with the common fire creatures. The 'Red Diamond King' represents an elite undead with crystalline bones, a sign of high cultivation in death-world physiology. The destroyer's wrath stems from the taboo of refining a race's flesh into Life Source Liquid — a practice that violates the natural order and echoes the ethics of ancient cultivation sects. Xiao Chen's absorption of fire seeds is the primary cultivation method in the dead world: collecting the energy cores of fallen undead to strengthen one's own fire. The sudden appearance of a living, innocent child subverts the setting's grim logic, hinting at deeper connections between life and death, possibly tied to reincarnation, powerful avatars, or beings that can cross realms. His silent approach signals a level of power or concealment that far exceeds ordinary detection.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a turning point. Xiao Chen has witnessed beings far beyond his current level: the flesh-and-blood destroyer and the Red Diamond King. Their conflict could reshape the local power balance. More importantly, the mysterious boy opens a new narrative thread. Is he a fragment of a greater being, a native of the death world, or a messenger? His appearance may lead Xiao Chen to crucial information about the death world's rulers, the path back to the living world, or the truth about the fire seed hierarchy. Readers should note the ethical justification behind the destroyer's rampage — it suggests deep-seated racial conflicts that may reflect larger cosmic struggles. Xiao Chen's rapid advancement through fire seeds also signals that the death world offers unique growth opportunities, but at great risk.
