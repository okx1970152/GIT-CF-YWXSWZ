## Chapter Overview
In this chapter, the group ventures deeper into the City of the Dead, only to encounter horrors that test their combined strength. The desolate, oppressive atmosphere of the dead city is palpable as they flee from lesser undead only to be cornered by a true terror: a shrouded wraith wrapped in a winding sheet of black blood. Xiao Chen, Liu Mu, and their allies must fight for their lives in a narrow alley, where teamwork and desperation become their only weapons. The tone is one of grim survival, with the mythic horror aesthetic fully realized—decaying bodies, bone-chilling winds, and a foe that regenerates from near-fatal wounds. This chapter highlights the escalating danger of the dead city and the necessity of unity among the living.

## Key Plot Points
After dispatching a minor undead, the group attempts to flee the narrow alley but is intercepted by gray-winged humanoid skeletons that fly overhead. The true threat emerges when a tall, shrouded figure appears, wrapped in a winding cloth stained with black blood—a fierce corpse wraith of immense power. Xiao Chen leads the attack, combining his Big Dipper Sealing Divinity and crescent moon blade into a single devastating light blade. Liu Mu supports with spatial imprisonment techniques, folding the environment to restrain the wraith. Despite their coordination, the wraith proves extraordinarily resilient: its ghost claws can parry divine light, and its shroud emanates a deathly domain that suppresses all power. The wraith regenerates a shattered hand instantly and breaks free from Liu Mu's spatial cage. Xiao Chen lands a powerful palm strike that embeds his hand in the wraith's bones, but the shroud suddenly wraps around his arm, threatening to engulf him. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen trapped, the shroud's black mist overwhelming his divine radiance, and the outcome uncertain.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter showcases the unique combination of techniques between Xiao Chen and Liu Mu. The "Big Dipper Sealing Divinity" is based on the seven stars of the Big Dipper, a constellation deeply significant in Chinese cosmology, often associated with fate and celestial power. Liu Mu's spatial techniques are a rare branch called "Space Spiritist" arts, considered kingly among same-realm cultivators because they manipulate the fabric of space itself—here used to fold and imprison the wraith. The wraith itself is a "凶灵" (vengeful spirit), a type of undead that retains a carnal body and immense resentment, often wrapped in its own burial shroud. Such shroud artifacts are sometimes refined into powerful evil tools in xianxia lore, absorbing death energy and protecting the undead. The corpse miasma and ghostly aura are common elements of Chinese folk horror, emphasizing the danger of corrupt, death-infused environments.

## Reading Guide
This battle is not over; Xiao Chen is in grave danger as the shroud attempts to absorb him. Readers should watch how the other characters—especially Liu Mu and the skeletons—will react to rescue him. The wraith's intelligence is questionable; it acts on instinct and fury, but its artifact shroud hints at a deeper, more sinister origin. The dead city's mysteries deepen: what created such powerful undead? The group's survival depends on their ability to work together across their distinct cultivation styles. The coming chapter likely tests their cohesion and resourcefulness. Also note that Yan Qingcheng and Liu Ruyan are present but have not fully committed to combat—their true intentions and value as allies remain ambiguous.
