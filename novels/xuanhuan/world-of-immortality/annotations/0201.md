## Chapter Overview
This chapter pivots from the raw combat of recent duels to the treacherous currents of political theater in Heavenly Emperor City. Xiao Chen finds himself trapped not by an enemy’s sword, but by the web of alliances, ambitions, and misunderstandings that his association with the mysterious dragon elder Long Teng has woven. As powerful families like the Hai and Undying Sect vie to claim him as a son-in-law or leverage his perceived connections, the very friendships he has built become instruments of pressure. The emotional core of the chapter is the sudden and wrenching separation from Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon, orchestrated by Long Teng under the harsh doctrine that true growth demands severing all bonds. This is a chapter of strategy, loss, and the cold logic of cultivation.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen spends three weeks navigating the political fallout of his victories. The Hai family maneuvers to force a marriage between him and Hai Yunxue, spreading rumors to make the union seem inevitable. The Undying Sect’s Liu Qingfeng pushes for Xiao Chen to wed Yan Qingcheng, while other families offer concubines or bribes. Xiao Chen sees through the schemes—he is a ‘bubble’ inflated by Long Teng’s presence, a trap for the greedy families. Long Teng confirms this by laughing that he has been ‘playing with the families,’ then coldly springing his real plan. He seals Xiao Chen, Keke, and the two dragon kings, declares that the bond must be severed for them to reach their full potential, and declares he will not kill Xiao Chen but will leave him to ‘live or die’ in the city. He transforms into black mist and flies toward the Southern Wastes, taking Keke, the Little Defiant Dragon, and the Cyan Dragon King. Xiao Chen is left alone, realizing the dragon elder’s game is over, and now the families he snubbed will likely turn on him. The chapter ends with the arrival of Hai family servants summoning him to the wedding, and Xiao Chen bracing for the fallout.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The political structure of the Eternal Continent is clarified here: Heavenly Emperor City is a hub of noble families like the Hai, Mande, Zhuge, and Ligen, each controlling beast-fighting arenas and wielding influence. The concept of a ‘forced marriage’ in xianxia is often a plot device to create ties between a rising cultivator and an established family. Here, it backfires because Xiao Chen’s ‘backer’ (Long Teng) is a phantom. The Hai family’s cultivation art, the Lotus Sutra (莲花宝典), is a version of the infamous Sunflower Manual (葵花宝典) from mortal legend—a powerful but self-castrating technique. This dark allusion underscores the cost of power in this world. Long Teng’s lesson about cultivating ‘ruthlessness’ echoes a common xianxia trope: that emotional attachments weaken a cultivator’s spirit. His method, however, is brutal and hypocritical, serving his own agenda.

## Reading Guide
This chapter marks a major turning point. Xiao Chen loses his two most powerful young allies—Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon—and his political cover. The impending marriage farce will either be a trap or an opportunity. Watch for how the Hai family and the Undying Sect react now that Long Teng has vanished. The mention of the tiger clan expert ‘Tiger Lie’ (虎烈) approaching from the Central Plains foreshadows a new threat. Xiao Chen’s ability to survive alone, without his beast companions, will test his true strength. The ‘pit’ Long Teng dug for the families may collapse on Xiao Chen, or he may escape it through his own cunning. The emotional hollow left by the parting will likely fuel his determination to grow stronger.
