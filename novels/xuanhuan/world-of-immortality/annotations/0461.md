## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges into the suffocating darkness of a thousand-foot cavern, where Xiao Chen faces a nightmare born of putrid waters and ancestral sin. The corpse river—a toxic yellow flood of death and decay—flows through the earth like a wound, and from its depths emerges a rat-headed giant with spider-like bone spears and a scorpion tail. This semi-ancestor has feasted on the flesh of an otherworldly ancestral god, granting it a regenerative undying body and unmatched ferocity. But the true horror lies upstream, where a figure called "the great one" stirs, its mere existence the source of the entire corpse river. The chapter balances visceral combat with creeping dread, as Xiao Chen fights not just for survival, but to uncover what darker power taints this subterranean realm.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen descends into the deepest part of the underground palace, following a tide of millions of black rats that feast on ancestral god remains. There he discovers a yellow river of corpse water so corrosive it vaporizes even the rats that consumed ancestral god flesh. From the river emerges a rat-headed humanoid giant, bound by massive blood-red iron chains—which it claims it used to restrain itself for cultivating with the river's power. The monster wields four bone spears on its sides and a hooked scorpion tail, all capable of regenerating instantly after being damaged. It recognizes Xiao Chen as a strong opponent and declares its intent to devour him and take his war swords. Xiao Chen engages in a ferocious battle, using his ancestral god battle suit to withstand the monster's deadly bone spears, while his war swords and the seven-colored sacred tree hold the creature at bay. The monster reveals that by feeding on the ancestral god's body, it learned techniques and achieved an undying form. During their fight, a mournful wail echoes from upstream, causing the monster to show fear and speak of a "great one" whose existence alone created the corpse river. It suggests that offering Xiao Chen's flesh to this entity would please it. Xiao Chen is shaken, realizing that if a semi-ancestor monster trembles at that presence, the river's source must harbor a being of unimaginable power—perhaps beyond the semi-ancestor level altogether.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The corpse river (尸河) is a classic xianxia horror motif—a corrupted waterway formed from the decay of supreme beings, embodying death and impurity. Its corrosive nature reflects the concept of "death qi" as an elemental force that erodes life and divine power. The monster claims to have cultivated by binding itself in the river with iron chains, a self-imposed trial to absorb the river's lethal essence and transmute it into power—a common trope of using extreme environments to temper the body. "Semi-ancestor" (半祖) denotes a cultivator on the threshold of ancestral godhood, possessing immense power but still bound by mortal limits. The undying body (不死身) achieved by consuming ancestral god flesh is tied to the idea that devouring a higher being's essence grants a fragment of its immortality. The "great one" (大人) upstream hints at a being perhaps at the ancestral sovereign (祖君) level or beyond, a silent terror whose mere presence reshapes the land around it. This aligns with the novel's motif that the deepest layers of the Death World hold entities that defy the understanding of even mighty cultivators.

## Reading Guide

This chapter raises the stakes significantly by suggesting that the underground complex Xiao Chen has entered is merely the outer hall of a far more dangerous domain. The corpse river's source introduces a potential antagonist of terrifying scale—one that even a semi-ancestor dreads. Readers should watch how Xiao Chen handles this new threat: will he retreat, press forward, or find a way to exploit the tension between the monster and the unseen entity? The mention of the "great one" also deepens the mystery of the Death World's ancient secrets and the fate of the ancestral gods who fell here. Future chapters are likely to reveal more about this being and perhaps connect to the broader arc involving the War Swords and the seal on the Nine Provinces.
