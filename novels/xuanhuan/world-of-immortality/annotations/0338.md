## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a cataclysmic turning point as Xiao Chen, fused with the broken stone man and wielding the Yellow Clay Altar, unleashes devastating power against the half-ancestors within the Dead City. The battle reaches mythic proportions as he personally slays the Sun Holy God and the White Tiger Saint Emperor using the eight primordial sounds and the Eight Aspect Worlds. Simultaneously, the death knell of the illusory half-ancestors sounds across the Nine Provinces, with cosmic forces clashing in the void above—Shennong and the Ancestral Dragon confront an otherworldly entity over the fate of the real and the illusory. The chapter is a fusion of visceral combat and philosophical revelation, driving home the theme that the false gods born from mortal belief are now being unmade.

## Key Plot Points

Fused with the stone man, Xiao Chen becomes a force capable of battling half-ancestors. He wields the four war swords and uses the Yellow Clay Altar's protection to withstand attacks. The eight primordial sounds, each tied to a phase of the Eight Aspect Worlds, resonate to shatter the Sun Holy God's body and soul, marking the first half-ancestor killed by Xiao Chen's own hand. He then turns on the White Tiger Saint Emperor, trapping him in the Eight Aspect Worlds and using eight ancient weapons (including the stone drill, stone ball, golden halberd, and four war swords) to crush him repeatedly until his soul fades. The Three Infant Grand Matriarch is wounded but fights on. Meanwhile, the death knell of the Dead City destroys illusory half-ancestors across the worlds—even those hiding overseas are dragged to the city and annihilated. Shennong, manifested as a dim light, confronts a being of ultimate illusion in the void, arguing over the worth of mortal lives. The Ancestral Dragon appears, declaring that he has stayed neutral for too long and now joins the battle. The true half-ancestors—Laozi, Buddha, Chiyou, and others—prepare to sever their illusory counterparts in the Dead City, signaling a final reckoning between reality and illusion.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Yellow Clay Altar is revealed to be formed from the flesh of Ancestral Gods, serving as a vessel for the Eight Syllables of Origin—eight primordial sounds that predate Buddhist mantras and embody the power of creation and destruction. The Eight Aspect Worlds technique, derived from Fuxi's trigrams, here merges with the eight sounds to create an inescapable killing realm. The concept of 'illusory half-ancestors' elaborates on the novel's central theme: that many beings worshipped as gods were born from the collective belief of mortals and the expansion of a 'virtual world' in ancient times. The death knell tolls for these false gods, returning them to nothingness. Shennong's sacrifice mirrors the real Ancestral Gods who died protecting their descendants, contrasting with the self-serving illusory powers. The Heaven-Earth Furnace, an artifact of Suiren, is mentioned as the object of the otherworldly being's scheme—a reminder that even supreme treasures can be perverted for destruction.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is the climax of the Dead City arc and a turning point in the novel's cosmology. Readers should pay close attention to the distinction between 'true' half-ancestors (like Laozi, Buddha, and Chiyou) who are preparing to sever their illusory selves, and the 'false' half-ancestors that Xiao Chen kills. The cosmic battle between Shennong and the otherworldly figure sets up a larger war between the real Nine Provinces and the illusory worlds. Xiao Chen's fusion with the stone man hints at his potential to become an Ancestral God-level being, but the cost of this power—and the mysterious figure wearing Suiren's burial shroud—remains unresolved. The death knell's effect on half-ancestors scattered across the worlds suggests that the destruction of the virtual realm is accelerating, and Xiao Chen's actions are directly tied to the fate of reality itself.
