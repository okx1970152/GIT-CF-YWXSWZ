## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers a cataclysmic, blood-soaked climax. Xiao Chen, guided by Keke's divine tree, systematically executes every half-ancestor trapped within Paradise Lost—including Buddha, Tongtian, the Original, the Sun God, Laozi, Kong Xuan, and a dozen others. The narrative pulses with tragic inevitability: these once-revered figures are stripped of their divine powers by the mysterious realm, reduced to mortals, and judged by a man who refuses to bow to any god. The chapter is a relentless cascade of decapitations, each blow shaking the foundations of the Eternal Continent. Blood rains from heaven, the earth trembles, and the world awakens to the terrifying truth: the age of half-ancestors is ending in a single, horrifying dawn.

## Key Plot Points

An ancient voice within Paradise Lost reveals its true nature: it suppresses all divine abilities, leaving even half-ancestors powerless as mere mortals with strong bodies. Xiao Chen, wielding the bloodstained War Sword, begins his purge. He first confronts the Buddha, who has shown greed for Keke's sacred tree, and cleaves his golden body in two. The crowd outside explodes in fury and disbelief, but none dare enter the realm—those who try dissolve into blood. Xiao Chen moves methodically: he beheads the Great Demon (first patriarch of the Demon Palace), decapitates the founder of the Cihang Sword School, strikes down the Daoist holding the Seven Treasures Wonderful Tree (likely Zhunti), kills Satan of the Western pantheon, and bisects the Sun God. He then slays Kong Xuan, the Peacock King of legend, with a single blow. Finally, he confronts Laozi. Though Laozi briefly breaks free of Keke's binding and speaks—claiming he will truly appear in the world—Xiao Chen's sword falls, severing his head. All twenty powerful beings in Paradise Lost are dead. The chapter ends with the haunting refrain: "Sages never die, thieves never cease; half-ancestors perish, heaven and earth restart."

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The term "half-ancestor" (半祖) refers to supreme cultivators who stand just below the level of Ancestral Gods—divine beings who shaped primordial civilizations. Figures like Buddha, Laozi, Tongtian, and Kong Xuan are drawn from Chinese mythology and the Ming novel *Investiture of the Gods*. Their appearance here as flawed, greedy entities challenges the traditional reverence for such sages. Paradise Lost is a unique spatial domain that nullifies all supernatural power, reducing the mightiest to ordinary flesh—a stark reminder of the novel's theme that true strength lies beyond cultivation. Xiao Chen's speech denouncing these 'self-proclaimed gods' for lacking the civilizational contributions of Suiren, Youchao, or Fuxi echoes the Daoist critique of institutional religion. The blood rain and thunderous omens across the continent signal that the deaths of half-ancestors are cosmic events with world-altering consequences.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up an irreversible transformation of the Eternal Continent's political and spiritual landscape. Xiao Chen has become the nemesis of virtually every major sect, cult, and lineage. Readers should watch for the fallout: how will the disciples of the slain half-ancestors retaliate? Can Xiao Chen survive outside Paradise Lost with the entire world hunting him? The mysterious words of Laozi—"If I truly appear in the world"—hint that the real Laozi may still be alive, adding a layer of uncertainty. Keke's role continues to deepen; her sacred tree was the catalyst for this massacre. The power vacuum will likely draw out even older, darker forces. The chapter's closing butterfly (Zhuangzi's incarnation) suggests that the cycle of life and death now teeters on the brink of a new age.
