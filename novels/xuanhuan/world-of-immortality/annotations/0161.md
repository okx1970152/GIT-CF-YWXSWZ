## Chapter Overview

Xiao Chen lingers on a floating divine island, his heart heavy with the sudden loss of Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon, who were taken by a mysterious black figure. In his solitude, the seemingly ordinary burial shroud of Ancestral God Suiren begins to move of its own accord, leading him to a nondescript stone wedge buried in the ruins. This humble object, later revealed as the legendary Suiren Fire-Drill, marks a turning point in Xiao Chen's fortunes. The chapter closes with a stunning reunion: Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon return safely, and they have brought three young dragon kings from the primal mountains—an event that reshapes the landscape of Xiao Chen's journey and hints at the boundless wonders of the Eternal Continent.

## Key Plot Points

After the abrupt disappearance of his two companions, Xiao Chen stays on the divine island, hoping against hope for their return. The first major event is the spontaneous activity of the burial shroud, which has been inert since its acquisition on Dragon Island. The shroud leads him to the ruins at the island's center, where it forcefully ejects a small, gray-brown stone wedge from the foundation. At first, Xiao Chen dismisses it as an ordinary stone, but careful examination reveals it to be oddly light, almost like petrified wood. The true revelation comes when he sees a primitive tribe using a similar implement for fire-making. In a flash of insight, he realizes this is the Suiren Fire-Drill—the very tool used by the ancestral god who discovered fire. The chapter then pivots to a desperate rush as dragon roars echo from the primal forest. Xiao Chen finds Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon safe, and they are accompanied by three young dragon kings, creatures of immense potential. These dragon kings are clearly not from Dragon Island, indicating that the two little beasts have not only survived but forged alliances with powerful entities in the deeper wilderness.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Suiren Fire-Drill is profoundly significant in Chinese mythology. Suiren (燧人氏) is one of the Three Sovereigns, credited with inventing fire-drilling, which marked the dawn of civilization. In the world of xianxia, such ancestral tools are often hidden sacred relics, containing the essence of the ancestor's enlightenment. That this drill has remained hidden for eons, mistaken for a common stone, underscores the theme that true power often lies in humble forms. The burial shroud's behavior suggests it is a yin-aligned sacred object, capable of sensing the drill's location. Additionally, the appearance of three dragon kings from the primal mountains expands the story's scope: these are not merely beasts but beings of kingly lineage, hinting at a larger, untamed world beyond the city. Their alliance with Keke and the defiant dragon elevates both characters' status, suggesting they are destined to gather powerful allies.

## Reading Guide

This chapter resolves the emotional cliffhanger of Keke and the defiant dragon's disappearance, but it also opens new mysteries. The Suiren Fire-Drill is now in Xiao Chen's possession—a relic of a founding ancestor. Its purpose and power remain unclear, but it will likely become a key cultivation tool or artifact in future conflicts. The three dragon kings are a major power boost, but they also signal that Xiao Chen's group is attracting attention from higher-level beings. Readers should watch for how the drill integrates with Xiao Chen's cultivation path, and whether the dragon kings will follow him or merely cooperate temporarily. Additionally, the mention of a beast-fighting tournament in Heavenly Emperor City sets up a stage for these new companions to be tested. The narrative also hints at the 'madman' who took Keke and the dragon; his identity remains a looming threat that Xiao Chen must eventually confront.
