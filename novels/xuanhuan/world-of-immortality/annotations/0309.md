## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a pivotal moment in the mortal world arc, as Xiao Chen and Chi You attempt to breach the ancient seal on Changyang Mountain. The mountain, steeped in myth as the birthplace of the Yan Emperor and connected to the Yellow Emperor, is revealed to hold the severed head of Xing Tian, the indomitable war god of the Nine Provinces. The chapter blends tragic desolation with epic rebirth—Xing Tian's awakening is a testament to unyielding will, and his first words defiantly challenge the encroaching cultivators from other realms. The tone is solemn, awe-inspiring, and filled with the weight of ancient history.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen meets Chi You at Changyang Mountain, where Chi You has been trying to break the seal for days. He reveals the seal is far stronger than expected, reinforced by a mysterious new power. The mountain is not just stone but the petrified body of a primordial being—one of the twelve great powers of the age of ignorance: the Goat (Wei Yang). Chi You seeks knowledge from a half-ancestor and returns with the legend: Changyang Mountain is the corpse of the Goat, a being near the Ancestral God level. The seal cannot be broken by force alone.

Two mischievous little girls, Linglong and Tutu, arrive with a gift from their father: the Xuanyuan Sword. They guide Chi You to a sword hole on the mountainside. When Chi You plunges the sword in, blood-light erupts, and the mountain begins to split. A cataclysmic upheaval follows—the entire mountain range for five hundred li collapses into a golden desert, leaving only the splitting Changyang Mountain. Chi You calls out, and Xing Tian's head rises, bathed in starlight, his body reconstructed from celestial energy. Xing Tian awakens calm and aware, immediately senses spying cultivators from other realms, and declares that those with no root in the mortal world must leave or be expelled. He and Chi You vow to unite old comrades and wage war again.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

Xing Tian (刑天) is a legendary figure from Chinese mythology, a fearless warrior who fought the Yellow Emperor after his head was severed. The line “刑天舞干戚，猛志固常在” is a famous quote from Tao Yuanming, praising his indomitable spirit. In this novel, Xing Tian is portrayed as a war god near the Ancestral God level, sealed for ages but now awakened.

The revelation that the twelve zodiac animals (Zi Shu, Chou Niu, Yin Hu, etc.) were once twelve supreme beings during the age of ignorance is a creative expansion. Each is said to rival an Ancestral God in power. This connects the familiar earthly zodiac to cosmic mythology, enriching the worldbuilding.

The Xuanyuan Sword (轩辕剑) is the legendary sword of the Yellow Emperor, a divine artifact that here serves as the key to break the seal. Its presence underscores the complex relationships between ancient figures—Chi You and the Yellow Emperor were enemies, yet the latter’s sword aids in freeing Xing Tian.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets the stage for the mortal world’s defense against invading cultivators. Xing Tian’s awakening introduces a new powerhouse aligned with the Nine Provinces. Readers should watch for how Xing Tian and Chi You will rally other ancient beings. The mysterious new sealing power on Changyang Mountain hints at a hidden hand — possibly connected to the Heavenly Steles or the Otherworld. Xiao Chen’s role as an observer and inheritor of the martial imprint will be tested as the conflict escalates. The little girls, Linglong and Tutu, remain enigmatic; their father is clearly a figure of immense power. Their playful demeanor contrasts sharply with the grave events, a signature tonal shift of the novel.
