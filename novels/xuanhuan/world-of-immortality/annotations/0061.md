## Chapter Overview

Yan Qingcheng's cold declaration of a lawful death hunt sets the stage for a new phase of conflict on Dragon Island. But Xiao Chen, true to his character, refuses to play by her rules. Instead of waiting for the hunt, he plunges deeper into the island's unknown reaches, driven by a pragmatic need for power and a growing sense of the island's mysteries. This chapter is a study in contrasts: the political scheming of the alliances versus the solitary wonder of exploration, the serene forest versus the shocking expanse of a bone sea, and the domestic familiarity of Keke's sacred tree versus the alien process of it merging with Xiao Chen's body. The tone shifts from tense confrontation to quiet awe as Xiao Chen stands at the edge of a vast, desolate battlefield.

## Key Plot Points

Following a psychic clash with Yan Qingcheng, she announces a legitimate ten-day death hunt by the Undying Alliance, fabricating a story to make it official. Xiao Chen, dismissive of the threat, returns to his valley and borrows the sacred tree from Keke to recover his energy. During cultivation, the tree mysteriously enters his body, connecting to his four previously divinized acupoints, forming a unique energy circulation. Despite repeated attempts, he cannot replicate the absorption. Deciding to train deeper on the island, he sets out with Keke and the three skeletons. Yan Qingcheng, furious at being ignored, spreads false news of Xiao Chen's acceptance of the hunt and leads her own pursuit. Meanwhile, Xiao Chen traverses a dead-silent forest with ancient trees, then emerges to a breathtaking sight: a vast sea of white bones stretching to the horizon, punctuated by a massive stele wreathed in black clouds. To the west, inexplicably, snowy mountains rise above the tropical landscape.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The sacred tree's spontaneous entry into Xiao Chen's body is a classic xianxia motif of a spiritual treasure recognizing a worthy master. The tree's connection to his four divinized acupoints—Shangqiu, Zhongting, and Shoulder-Well—suggests his unique acupoint cultivation path resonates with the tree's nature, hinting at a deeper compatibility. The bone sea and colossal stele evoke the legend of the Yellow River Divine Stele, suggesting this island's seal is part of a larger divine arrangement. The stark contrast between a tropical jungle and snowy mountains reinforces that Dragon Island is a place where the natural order has been warped by ancient forces. Such geographical anomalies often indicate the presence of powerful formations or suppressed relics in xianxia lore.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as a crucial transition. The fabricated death hunt by Yan Qingcheng introduces a new layer of political danger, but Xiao Chen's deliberate disregard signals his growing confidence and his prioritization of power over politics. Readers should watch for the stele in the bone sea—it is almost certainly linked to the divine steles mentioned earlier, and Xiao Chen's origin story with the Yellow River stele suggests a fateful connection. The snowy mountains also promise new challenges and resources. The sacred tree's partial bonding raises questions about whether this is a one-time event or the beginning of a permanent fusion. The death hunt will force Xiao Chen to return to the populated zones eventually, setting up a confrontation with his many enemies.
