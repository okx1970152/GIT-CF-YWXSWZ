## Chapter Overview

This chapter presents a profound philosophical crossroads for the characters of World of Immortality. A mysterious gate called 'Return' (回) appears, offering the chance to revisit past regrets and potentially alter history. Many characters, including the usually carefree Jin Sanyi and the ever-innocent Keke, are drawn to it, hoping to undo personal failures or sorrows. Yet Xiao Chen stands apart. Despite harboring deep regrets, he refuses to enter, arguing that the past cannot be truly changed and that dwelling on it only prolongs pain. Instead, he chooses the Gate of Hope (希望), which promises a glimpse into the future. His journey through this gate reveals a desolate, war-torn world and the ominous floating city of the Heavenly Emperor, where a voice cries out, 'The Heavenly Emperor has regret!' Xiao Chen discovers a pile of petrified stone-man bones, hinting at an ancient tragedy. The chapter is a meditation on fate, time, and the courage to move forward rather than look back.

## Key Plot Points

The central event is the appearance of two mystical gates: the 'Gate of Return' and the 'Gate of Hope'. The Return gate allows a being to travel back to a pivotal moment and attempt to rewrite history. Characters like the mysterious old farmer, Jin Sanyi, Fatty Zhuge, and even Keke enter it, each seeking to correct a past regret. Jin Sanyi, for instance, laments his sister's untimely death and wants to prevent it. Keke's mother pushes her through the gate despite the little beast's protests. Xiao Chen, however, remains behind. He admits he has many regrets but insists that revisiting the past cannot fundamentally alter the great tragedies—deaths, losses, and the course of civilizations. To him, 'the past cannot be changed; if you walk through it again, you only relive the pain'. He then asks about the other gate. The Gate of Hope is said to be far more dangerous but offers the chance to gain 'five hundred years' of borrowed time. Xiao Chen resolutely steps into it, seeking power rather than nostalgia. Inside, he is drawn irresistibly across space-time to a grim battlefield where a lone figure fights against a barrage of ancestral god weapons. He then arrives at the Heavenly Emperor City, now floating in the clouds and abandoned except for a haunting cry, 'The Heavenly Emperor has regret!' Inside the empty city, he finds a massive pile of petrified stone-man bones, connecting to the primordial mystery of the stone men. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen alone in the city, the voice of regret echoing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of correcting past regrets via a mystical gate is a powerful trope in xianxia fiction, tapping into the universal human longing for 'what if'. However, Xiao Chen's rejection of this opportunity reflects a Daoist or Buddhist understanding of acceptance: the past is fixed and clinging to it hinders spiritual progress. His choice to enter the Gate of Hope instead aligns with the proactive nature of cultivation—seeking future power rather than atoning for past failures. The cry 'The Heavenly Emperor has regret' alludes to the idea that even supreme beings are not immune to sorrowful mistakes. The stone-man bones are a recurring enigma; stone-men are a post-ancestral cultivation path that mortals can achieve, and finding their remains hints at the fall of once-great cultivators. The vision of a lonely warrior fighting ancestral god weapons suggests Xiao Chen's possible future role or a parallel timeline. The 'five hundred years' borrowed from heaven is reminiscent of classic tales where cultivators seek to extend lifespan or gain respite from heavenly constraints.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up a major narrative divergence: while many side characters are exploring their pasts via the Return gate, Xiao Chen is now ahead in the timeline, glimpsing a future full of destruction and mystery. As you read on, pay attention to whether the characters who return from the past emerge unchanged or altered—this will reveal the rules of this universe's time travel. Xiao Chen's vision of the lonely hero and the Heavenly Emperor City may recur, so note the details. The stone-man bones are a crucial clue to the ancient war against the Otherworld; their presence in Tian Di City implies that city was once a seat of power. The voice of regret may belong to a celestial being who failed in his duty. The Gate of Hope is extremely dangerous, so Xiao Chen's journey will likely bring him face-to-face with overwhelming challenges. The promise of 'five hundred years' suggests he may gain enough time to reach the Ancestral God level. Watch for the reappearance of the mysterious woman (Keke's mother) and the fate of those who entered Return.
