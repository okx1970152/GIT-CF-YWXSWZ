## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers one of the most poignant and desolate moments in the entire arc: Xiao Chen returns to the mortal world, only to find himself a red-bone skeleton, a mere wisp of lingering怨念. The tragic, mythic tone is established immediately as he materializes before the Dead City, the very site where his stone-body perished. The Golden Lion King appears—now a towering young man fully wielding the power of reincarnation and destruction—and cruelly dismisses Xiao Chen as nothing but a fading phantom. Through the overheard words of soldiers, we glimpse how the world remembers him: a taboo name spoken in whispers, a legend of half-ancestor slaughter. The chapter is steeped in loss, isolation, and the cruel passage of time, yet it also plants seeds of hope, as Xiao Chen’s indomitable will refuses to fully fade.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen descends through the ancient well beneath the Dead City and passes through a spatial tunnel, emerging in the mortal world near the Dead City. His physical body is nowhere to be found; instead he exists as a red-bone skeleton that cannot touch solid objects. The Golden Lion King appears, recognizing the skeleton as a lingering怨念, not a living being. He tests this by striking with his fist—the blow passes through harmlessly—then opens his Reincarnation Eye to send Xiao Chen to the cycle of rebirth, but the乌光 also passes through without effect. The Golden Lion King concludes that Xiao Chen has long since灰飞烟灭 and that only怨念 remains. He departs, declaring that the world’s remaining strong warriors will serve as his whetstones on his path to becoming lord of the earth. Xiao Chen, stunned, wanders toward his ancestral village, but finds the area sealed by soldiers. The soldiers gossip about the current strongest beings: the Golden Lion King, the three dragon sons (睚眦、狻猊、貔貅), the infamous Jin Sanyi (the ‘猥琐王’), and others. One old soldier mentions the name ‘Xiao Chen’ but is quickly silenced, for it is taboo in the court of Empress Zhao Lin’er. Xiao Chen listens in sorrow, reflecting on his past glory and the friends and enemies he once knew.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Golden Lion King (三头黄金狮子王) is a rare sacred beast whose three eyes each hold a unique power: the left eye controls reincarnation, the right eye controls destruction, and the sealed third eye on its forehead is said to possess a terrifying, unknown power. In this chapter he is a fully matured young man, wielding the ‘Upper War Body’ (上战体), a supreme physique for martial combat. His ability to casually open the Reincarnation Eye and attempt to send Xiao Chen into the cycle of rebirth draws upon Buddhist cosmology, where the gate of reincarnation (轮回门) is a pathway to a new life after death. Xiao Chen’s state as a red-bone skeleton that cannot be touched or attacked is a classic depiction of an incorporeal怨念—a residual consciousness clinging to the world due to unresolved emotions. In Chinese mythology,怨念 can linger long after death, but as the Golden Lion King notes, even怨念 eventually dissipates. The soldiers’ discussion of the current powerhouses reflects the political landscape: dragons sons (睚眦, etc.) are mythical beasts known for their ferocity, and Jin Sanyi (金三亿) is a legendary assassin with a dysfunctional speech pattern. The mention of Zhao Lin’er as Empress of the Nine Provinces confirms her rise to power, and her taboo on Xiao Chen’s name signals his controversial legacy as the slayer of half-ancestors.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a major emotional and narrative low point for Xiao Chen, but it also sets the stage for his eventual return. The fact that he exists as a怨念 rather than a fully deceased soul suggests that his story is not yet over. Pay attention to the interaction with the Golden Lion King: even the King’s supreme Reincarnation Eye could not send him away, hinting that Xiao Chen’s怨念 is anchored by something deeper—perhaps the incomplete Martial Imprint or the stone man still within him. The soldiers’ gossip introduces the current generation of young powerhouses, many of whom will become allies or foes in the coming arcs. The reappearance of city guards and the imperial court system under Zhao Lin’er indicates that the mortal world has restructured politically. Xiao Chen’s emotional state—grief for the lost past, loneliness, and a spark of indomitable will—will drive him to find a way to regain a physical form. The chapter closes with his longing for friends and enemies, foreshadowing future reunions and conflicts as he re-enters the world he once shaped.
