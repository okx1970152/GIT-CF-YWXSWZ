## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a seismic reversal in the conflict between Xiao Chen and the combined forces of half-ancestor disciples, holy artifacts, and the ancient cultivation world. Trapped in a valley and besieged by an overwhelming array of semi-ancestor-level artifacts and the legendary Nine Heavens Ten Earths God-Slaying Demon-Exterminating Annihilation Formation, Xiao Chen faces certain death. Yet through the mysterious mark on his forehead—a living rune that seems to connect him to the Dead City itself—he tears open a passage to that deathly realm. What emerges is not the city in full, but a corps of hundreds of ancient celestial and underworld soldiers, the living war effigies, who turn the tide with savage, unstoppable force. The chapter is a brutal, cathartic display of Xiao Chen's growing connection to the primordial powers of the World of Immortality and the tragic cost of his path. The tone is one of cold fury and grim necessity, as the slaughter becomes a flood that washes away his enemies.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen, trapped in a sealed valley, uses the demonic mark on his forehead to open a black spatial passage, unleashing mist that shields him from the combined assault of numerous sacred artifacts (Three-Infant Divine Lamp, White Tiger Sacred Mark, Seven-Colored Fierce Sword, Sun Sacred Scripture, etc.). The Yellow Clay Altar, sensing his peril, emits a burst of Xuanhuang qi that temporarily repels the divine light of Luoyang and stabilizes the killing formation. Xiao Chen then summons the stone drill from the stone man within him, which destroys the blade mountain and absorbs the sea of fire, then pierces through dozens of enemies before returning. He follows with the stone ball, shattering the hellish space of the formation and killing more opponents. However, the deadly formation persists, supported by eight half-ancestor artifacts and the Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge, which channels the divine power of Luoyang. As Xiao Chen's strength wanes, the mark on his forehead bleeds and opens the passage again, revealing a towering figure shrouded in black mist, wearing the burial shroud of Suiren. This figure's gaze alone repels the holy artifacts and sends the Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge reeling. The divine light of Luoyang then merges with the Yellow Clay Altar, and the altar begins to absorb the city's power, triggering the Eight Syllables of Origin. The formation collapses. Xiao Chen, now on the altar, commands hundreds of living war effigies—celestial and underworld soldiers from the Dead City—who charge into the enemy ranks, cutting through everything. The combined forces of the Tiger clan, Sun Church, Jie Sect, and others are routed, leaving fields of corpses in a thousand-li pursuit.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter draws deeply on Chinese mythological motifs of the underworld and celestial armies. The 'living war effigies' (活兵俑) are akin to the Terracotta Army but on a supernatural scale—ancient warriors preserved by deathly power, bound to the will of one who can command the Dead City. The Dead City itself (死城) is a recurring location tied to the sealing of Dragon Island and the failed underworld of Ancestral God Suiren. The Heaven-Reaching Death Bridge (通天死桥) is a legendary artifact that connects realms; here it acts as a conduit for the divine power of the historical city of Luoyang, a real ancient capital in China. The Eight Syllables of Origin (本源八音) are primordial sounds that predate Buddhist mantras, associated with the Yellow Clay Altar, which is said to be formed from the flesh and blood of Ancestral Gods. The altar's ability to absorb the power of a capital city reflects the concept of 'feng shui' and the accumulation of spiritual energy in places of historical significance. The stone drill and stone ball are relics of the Stone Man, a being of immense importance who appears to be a perfect martial form that can absorb divine artifacts.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as a major turning point in the arc. Xiao Chen now has a direct connection to the Dead City and can summon its ancient soldiery, a power that will likely grow as he masters the mark. The enigmatic figure in the burial shroud—who is clearly not hostile to Xiao Chen—remains a mystery: why does Suiren's shroud cover him, and what is his relationship to the ancestors? The Yellow Clay Altar's absorption of Luoyang's divine light hints that other ancient cities may hold similar power. Meanwhile, the routed enemies—the Tiger clan, Sun Church, Jie Sect, and others—will regroup and likely escalate their efforts. The death of so many elite cultivators will send shockwaves through the cultivation world, and the half-ancestors may now view Xiao Chen as a primary threat. The romance and friendship threads (Qingqing, Zhao Lin'er, Hai Yunxue) are poised for further complexity as Xiao Chen's power grows. Keep an eye on the mark on Xiao Chen's forehead—it may be a key to the ultimate fate of the Dead City and the illusory nature of reality hinted at by the fortune teller.
