## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a masterful blend of brutal martial action and mythic revelation. Xiao Chen, now wielding the terrifying power of a true Martial Body, demonstrates his absolute dominance over armed cultivators, shattering their artifacts with his bare hands. Yet the true weight of the chapter lies not in the violence but in the discovery that his ancestral purple jade bracelet is no mere heirloom—it is a key to the ancient seal of the Nine Provinces. The appearance of the mysterious guardian Tong Hu at Lushan waterfall and the vision of a primordial world of dragons and savage beasts opens a new dimension of mystery, linking Xiao Chen‘s personal journey to the cosmic fate of the Mortal World itself.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen’s supreme hand (上苍之手) crushes all opposition: he snaps flying swords, shatters a golden pagoda, and kills a dozen cultivators in quick succession. The white‑robed cultivator’s defensive jade bracelet proves surprisingly resilient, forcing Xiao Chen to use restraint once he realizes it is his own family heirloom. After sparing the man on the condition of information, he learns the bracelet was found at Lushan waterfall. The three old monsters—Tianwaitian, Renwairen, and Shanwaishan—break free from their seal and identify the bracelet as a potential key to the Nine Provinces seal, with a golden river symbolizing the Yellow River within its depths. Xiao Chen returns to his home village, Ancestral Dragon Village, to ensure its safety, then proceeds to Lushan. There, the guardian Tong Hu reveals that a man previously brought the bracelet to the waterfall and chose to enter an illusory world, effectively dying in the mortal realm. When Xiao Chen’s bracelet reactivates, the waterfall tears open to reveal a vision of a barbaric land with wingless dragons, eight‑armed evil dragons, and ancient mountains—a direct glimpse into the World of Immortality or a similar sealed domain.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter introduces the concept of the ”Martial Body“ (武体) as a pure physical cultivation path that rejects reliance on external energy or divine arts. Xiao Chen’s supreme hand is a manifestation of this path, allowing him to break sacred relics with his bare flesh—a rare and terrifying ability. The purple jade bracelet functions as a ”key“ to the Nine Provinces seal, a common trope in xianxia where artifacts unlock hidden realms or cosmic barriers. The three old monsters (Tianwaitian, Renwairen, Shanwaishan) represent ancient guardians of the seal, their names evoking the idea of ”heaven beyond heaven, man beyond man, mountain beyond mountain“—a layered cosmology. The guardian Tong Hu, with his cryptic words about ”god“ versus ”man,“ reinforces the theme that the Mortal World is but one layer in a vast multiverse of sealed realms.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up several crucial threads: the jade bracelet’s true nature as a seal‑key will likely become central to Xiao Chen’s quest to return home. The vision of the barbaric dragon world—identical to the Dragon Island seen earlier—suggests that the illusory world Tong Hu mentioned is in fact the World of Immortality itself, and the man who entered may hold significance. Keep an eye on the identity of that mysterious figure; his fate may mirror Xiao Chen’s own. The chapter also deepens the emotional stakes: Xiao Chen’s home village is now under the protection of an old monster, but the bracelet’s link to the seal means his personal history is inextricably tied to the fate of the Mortal World. The brutal efficiency of Xiao Chen’s kills reminds us of the tragic, unforgiving nature of the cultivation path—every step forward is soaked in blood.
