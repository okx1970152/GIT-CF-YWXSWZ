## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges the reader into the raw, unforgiving law of the jungle on Dragon Island. Xiao Chen witnesses the aftermath of a brutal beast war, a clash driven by primal greed for a hidden treasure. The chapter balances the visceral horror of nature red in tooth and claw with the quiet, almost sacred process of cultivation. It is a testament to Xiao Chen’s pragmatic survival instinct: he does not indulge in pointless heroics but seizes opportunity from chaos. The tone is desolate and mythic, underscoring that in this world, strength and cunning are the only currencies that matter.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen stumbles upon a scene of carnage: dozens of dead beasts, their bodies torn apart in a struggle for access to an ancient sealed cave. The victor is a magnificent Three-Eyed Divine Wolf, a blue-furred, winged creature with a third, baleful eye. Though grievously wounded, it is still lethally dangerous. Xiao Chen, recognizing the wolf is at its last gasp, ambushes and kills it with a single calculated strike. He then enters the cave to discover a massive dragon skeleton—a Sword Dragon—and a colossal head-sized Dragon Crystal, the source of the conflict. Understanding the value and danger, he quickly collects the crystal and a handful of lesser Beast Crystals from the corpses, then flees to a secure location. There, he spends the next day painstakingly absorbing the Dragon Crystal’s pure essence, channeling it into his right foot’s Shangqiu acupoint, which becomes fully divinized like its counterpart. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen contemplating the grand ambition of divinizing all 365 major acupoints.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces the concept of Beast Crystals (兽晶), a common trope in xianxia where powerful monsters condense their life essence into a crystalline core. Here, the coveted prize is a Dragon Crystal (龙晶), a far rarer and more potent form found in the remains of ancient dragons. The absorption process is described with clinical yet reverent language, emphasizing the sacred transmutation of raw energy into a personal cultivation foundation. The Three-Eyed Divine Wolf (三眼神狼) draws on Chinese folklore where three-eyed creatures often possess supernatural sight or power—a third eye symbolizing heightened perception or a connection to the divine. The methodical, acupoint-based cultivation (穴道神化) is Xiao Chen’s unique path, diverging from the more common realm-based progression seen in other practitioners. It requires immense, concentrated essence to transform a single acupoint into a luminous ‘light point,’ a node of condensed power.

## Reading Guide

The stage is set for a period of relative stability as Xiao Chen consolidates his gains. Having now divinized two acupoints (both Shangqiu points on his feet), he has taken a major step forward, but the road to 365 is long. The chapter hints at the sheer scale of resources needed—each acupoint likely requires a treasure comparable to a Dragon Crystal. This frames his future goals as he continues exploring the island. The mysterious Sword Dragon skeleton raises questions about what other dangers and opportunities lie in the deeper regions. Watch for how Xiao Chen will use his increased vitality and power in the coming conflicts. The brutal logic of the beast war also mirrors the human politics on Dragon Island; readers should expect similar ruthlessness from the cultivators seeking treasures and supremacy.
