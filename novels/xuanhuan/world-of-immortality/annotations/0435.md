## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a climactic showdown as Xiao Chen fully awakens the power of the War Swords, turning the tide against a coalition of half-ancestors. The atmosphere shifts from desperate defense to overwhelming offense, with Xiao Chen wielding twenty-seven primordial stone swords that tear through the fabric of reality. The chapter also provides sharp tonal contrast through the Buddhist monk's grotesquely comedic transformation of enemy kings into beautiful women, a moment of levity that underscores the mythic dark fantasy aesthetic. At its heart, this is a chapter about the cost of power and the desperation of trapped gods.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen triggers the War Swords by manifesting the Heavenly Stele diagrams on his stone body, causing the swords scattered among the half-ancestors to rebel. The Yaksha Half-Ancestor, already wounded from previous battles, is struck repeatedly by the swords and forced to flee through his illusory world. Three remaining half-ancestors—the Skeleton King, Angel Holy God, and Asura Half-Ancestor—join forces to seal the sky and crush Xiao Chen under the combined will of heaven and earth. Xiao Chen's stone body cracks under the pressure, but the true forms of the War Swords emerge from their stone shells after the outer layer shatters. With twenty-seven true swords, he breaks the sealing domain and engages the Asura Half-Ancestor in a focused assault, wounding him severely. The chapter's turning point comes when Xiao Chen retreats into the King Ship and unearths the corpse from Paradise Lost. When the half-ancestors attack the corpse, it absorbs their divine power and opens its eyes, locking all half-ancestors in place with an aura of absolute dread. Meanwhile, the monk known as the Buddha of Laughter uses his transformation art to turn five Rakshasa kings into pigs and an elderly Yaksha king into a beautiful woman, causing the allied forces to flee in panic.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The War Swords (战剑) are primordial artifacts tied to the Heavenly Stele and the Great Derivation number (fifty, of which forty-nine are used). Xiao Chen's ability to control them stems from his mastery of the Heavenly Stele Profound Art, which imprints the stele's diagrams onto his body. The half-ancestor realm represents the pinnacle below Ancestral Gods, where cultivators can merge their will with the natural world to suppress enemies. The Eight Syllables of Origin, particularly the 'Weng' and 'Duo' sounds, are central to Xiao Chen's cultivation, representing life-death and yin-yang duality. The 'King Ship' is a death-associated vessel that can cross the Forbidden Sea, and the corpse within is that of an Ancestral God or transcendent being, capable of neutralizing half-ancestor attacks through passive absorption.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up a dramatic power shift. The half-ancestors are now vulnerable, and the awakened corpse promises further escalation. Readers should watch for how the other half-ancestors, especially the Yaksha and Skeleton kings, regroup after this humiliation. Xiao Chen's control over the War Swords will likely grow, but the cost—his stone body cracking—suggests limitations. The comic subplot with the monk's transformation art masks a deeper truth: even kings can be reduced to absurdity. The next chapter will likely explore the corpse's origin and the half-ancestors' next move, possibly drawing in the mysterious female sovereign of the Death World.
