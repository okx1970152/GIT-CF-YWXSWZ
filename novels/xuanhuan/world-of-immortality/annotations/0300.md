## Chapter Overview
This chapter stands as a monumental turning point in the epic narrative of World of Immortality. The ancient dragon, who for countless ages bore the dead city on its back, makes a final, heartrending sacrifice using the supreme divine art of Six Paths of Reincarnation. As the steles rain from heaven one after another, Xiao Chen, on the verge of spiritual collapse, manages to imprint all six Heavenly Stele diagrams into his being, completing his foundational cultivation method at last. The return to the mortal world is marked by the emergence of the half-ancestors—Primordial, Tongtian, Zhunti, Chiyou, and others—who have retrieved their legendary treasures from beneath the dead city. Their gratitude to the fallen dragon and their debt to Xiao Chen set the stage for a decisive confrontation against the encroaching Otherworld forces. The chapter reveals the hidden cosmology of four worlds beyond the mortal realm, and hints at an even greater Primordial Celestial Realm. It is a chapter of awe-inspiring sacrifice, revelation, and the gathering of supreme power.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the old dragon struggling to hold up the dead city as more heavenly steles descend. Primordial, Tongtian, Zhunti, Chiyou, and other half-ancestors desperately try to excavate their lost artifacts from the city's foundations—Tongtian's bronze shield, Chiyou's iron halberd, Zhunti's seven-colored evil sword, and Primordial's ancient staff. Just as they seem unable to lift the city, the old dragon unleashes the Six Paths of Reincarnation, an ultimate divine art that shakes the cosmos. The steles' power is temporarily sealed, allowing the half-ancestors to retrieve their treasures. The old dragon then sacrifices itself, shattering its body to clear the way for the next Ancestral Dragon, erasing the character 'dragon' from the stele inscription, and laying the stele across the demon well. Blood rain and falling petals mourn the dragon's passing. Xiao Chen, meanwhile, endures mental torture to record the diagrams of six steles into his mind. After three days of mourning, the group discovers that a mysterious doppelganger of Xiao Chen had left from Dragon Island twenty days earlier via the King Ship. The half-ancestors then escort the group through the Lushan passage back to the mortal world. There, Xiao Chen learns from Chiyou and Laozi about the four worlds that encircle the mortal realm: the Martial World (now the Eternal Continent), the Curse World, the Qi World (Spiritual Realm), and the Soul World, all derived from four original cultivation systems. They also hint at a Primordial Celestial Realm above them, the possible destination of Ancestral Gods after death. Finally, Xiao Chen requests the help of ten half-ancestors to rescue his homeland and confront the Green Lotus Celestial Maiden.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Six Paths of Reincarnation (六道轮回) is a supreme divine art often mentioned in Chinese mythology and xianxia fiction, said to be powerful enough to destroy worlds. Here it is used by a declining Ancestral Dragon, its potency diminished by age. The Heavenly Steles (天碑) are monumental stones bearing cultivation diagrams that appear throughout the story—each stele is both an artifact of power and a seal. Xiao Chen's method of imprinting the diagrams without understanding them is a classic cultivation trope: the seed of insight planted for future growth. The revelation of four cultivation systems—Martial (武), Curse (咒), Qi (气), and Soul (魂)—maps onto the historical development of xianxia subgenres. The idea that Ancestral Gods (祖神) do not truly die but merely pass into a higher realm (Primordial Celestial Realm, 洪荒天界) is a profound metaphysical concept that recontextualizes all their sacrifices. The old dragon's final act—erasing the 'dragon' character from the stele—symbolizes a breaking of ancient seals and a liberation of the dragon lineage, a deeply mythic moment.
