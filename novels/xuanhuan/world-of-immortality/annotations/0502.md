## Chapter Overview

In this pivotal chapter, Xiao Chen ventures into the legendary Heavenly Emperor City—a relic of a bygone age that has hung suspended above the clouds. There he discovers a massive petrified stone skeleton and hears the mournful lament of an unseen presence: "The Heavenly Emperor has regrets." What follows is a desperate negotiation between Xiao Chen and the city's ancient life force, a being that offers him a forbidden chance: five hundred years of accelerated cultivation time in exchange for the souls of three Otherworld Ancestral Gods. The city then seals itself, tears free from the mortal plane, and flees beyond the cosmos, pursued by three supreme Otherworld Ancestral Gods wielding legendary weapons. Xiao Chen, trapped within the stone ribcage, begins an agonizing training of repeated self-destruction and rebirth to forge himself into a war-tier half-ancestor. The chapter is a turning point—desolate yet hopeful, steeped in tragic grandeur and the mythic weight of ancient debts.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen finds the Heavenly Emperor City floating above the clouds, its streets covered in bone dust. A gigantic petrified stone skeleton lies at its center—possibly the remains of a Stone Man. A spectral life force, the lingering will of the Heavenly Emperor, speaks of infinite regret and proposes a pact: Xiao Chen may borrow five hundred years of cultivation time, but the price is three Ancestral God souls to be rendered upon his eventual breakthrough to that realm. Xiao Chen accepts, knowing that his era demands supreme power. The city immediately seals itself, vibrating with energy, then launches beyond the heavens into absolute void.

Three Otherworld Ancestral Gods—wielding the Immortal Shield, the Demon Blade, and the thirty-three-layer pagoda—attempt to intercept the fleeing city but fail. Their weapons shatter star systems, yet the city escapes entirely from their time-space continuum. The life force reveals that the emperor's attempt to transcend one step further led to an abyss, and that the bargain may be the last chance for both parties. Once the city is safe beyond reach, Xiao Chen begins his training: within the stone cage, he repeatedly destroys and rebuilds his own body, striving to become a half-ancestor of explosive combat power rather than a mere practitioner of balanced growth.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of "borrowing five hundred years" is a classic xianxia trope—a desperate cultivator trades future obligations for immediate growth time. The Heavenly Emperor City itself evokes the myth of the Jade Emperor's celestial capital, but here it is a tragic ruin. The three weapons that attack the city—the Immortal Shield, the Demon Blade, and the thirty-three-layer pagoda—are archetypes of divine artifacts from Chinese mythology. The shield was once wielded by the Otherworld Ancestral God Babula, a figure previously encountered; the demon blade weeps like a ghost, echoing the legend of cursed swords; the pagoda that absorbs starlight recalls the Buddhist concept of a world-devouring tower. Xiao Chen's training method—self-destruction and rebirth—mirrors the Nirvana realm's death tribulations, but applied at a lower level to harden his body and will for future half-ancestor breakthroughs. The chapter also reinforces the fatalistic tone: great power demands great sacrifice, and the debt of three Ancestral God souls will eventually come due.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a setup for Xiao Chen's long-term power progression. The bargain effectively isolates him from the current conflicts in the Nine Provinces, Suspending him in a time-dilated space. Readers should note the specificity of the deal: it is not a general power boost but a focused path toward becoming a combat-oriented half-ancestor. The three Ancestral God souls are a ticking debt—future confrontations with Otherworld powers will be framed by this obligation. The escape of the Heavenly Emperor City also signals that the Otherworld Ancestral Gods are aware of Xiao Chen and consider him a variable worth eliminating. The training method of repeated self-destruction suggests that Xiao Chen's growth will be steep but brutal, and his eventual emergence will likely coincide with a major crisis. Keep an eye on the fate of the city: its lament and the emperor's regret hint at deeper secrets about the Stone Man legacy and the true nature of the heavens.
