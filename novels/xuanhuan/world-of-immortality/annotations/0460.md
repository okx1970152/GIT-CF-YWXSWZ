## Chapter Overview
In this chapter, Xiao Chen ventures deeper into the Death World's interior, entering a desolate red-stoned mountain range that bears the scars of an ancient battle where an Otherworld Ancestral God fell. The land itself seems oppressive, hindering movement and flight. Here, he encounters swarms of rats that have consumed the flesh of the dead Ancestral God, granting them terrifying regenerative abilities and near-immortality. The tone is one of mythic horror and desolate mystery, as Xiao Chen realizes the rats are not mere beasts but products of corrupted divine essence. The stakes rise when he discovers the Ancestral God's own skeleton, still animated by residual battle instinct and powerful enough to withstand his War Swords. This chapter deepens the lore of the Death World and sets up a confrontation with an unknown entity lurking deeper in the cave.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen finds a broken stele marking the site of an Otherworld Ancestral God's death. He discovers a field of skulls, including one from a Half-Ancestor, confirming the scale of the ancient battle. Entering a red-stoned mountain range, he is attacked by a black rat with unnatural speed and regeneration—a creature that has fed on the Ancestral God's corpse. Thousands of rats swarm him, including four golden rats near the Earth-Penetrating realm. Xiao Chen realizes the rats' immortality comes from the Ancestral God's blood; he can kill them by drying their blood with sacred fire. He captures a golden rat and absorbs its energy, then follows the retreating horde into a deep cave leading to an underground palace. There, he is ambushed by the Ancestral God's skeleton, which possesses spider-like bone legs and a scorpion tail. The skeleton has no soul-fire but acts on pure battle instinct, striking with lethal precision. Xiao Chen's War Swords cannot damage it, but his Ancestral God War Armor protects him from the scorpion tail's piercing attack. He uses a formation of twenty-seven War Swords and the Sacred Tree to temporarily suppress the skeleton. He discovers a jade book on the skeleton, likely an Otherworld scripture. He also senses a massive, powerful beast deeper in the cave, indicating greater danger ahead.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The undying rats illustrate a common xianxia trope: consuming the flesh of a supreme being can grant immortality or immense power, but often with side effects such as corruption or dependency on the source's energy. The rats cannot permanently die unless their blood is fully purified, symbolizing how Ancestral God essence is both a blessing and a curse. The Ancestral God skeleton acts on residual battle instinct—a concept where powerful warriors leave imprints of their will that persist after death, making their corpses dangerous for eons. The jade book is a classic method of preserving knowledge across ages, often containing cultivation techniques or historical records. The oppressive mountain domain is a result of the Ancestral God's death, where residual divine power warps the land. This chapter also reinforces that the War Swords, while formidable, are not yet complete; they cannot single-handedly kill an Ancestral God-level being, emphasizing the need to gather all forty-nine swords.

## Reading Guide
This chapter raises several mysteries: Who killed the Otherworld Ancestral God? What is the massive beast in the depths of the cave—a rat king or something else? The jade book may contain crucial information about the Death World's history or a technique Xiao Chen can use. His inability to destroy the Ancestral God skeleton suggests he will need to either escape, find a way to seal it, or awaken the Sacred Tree's full power. The presence of the skeleton also hints that the rats may be serving a larger master. In the next chapter, Xiao Chen will likely confront the hidden entity and perhaps find a way to claim the jade book. His Ancestral God War Armor proves vital here; without it, the scorpion tail would have been fatal. Note also that the golden rats can transform into human form, indicating high intelligence. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen uses the information from the jade book and whether he can secure the Ancestral God skeleton as a treasure for his allies.
