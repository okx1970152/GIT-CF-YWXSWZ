## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges deep into the desolate underworld, a realm of ancient horrors and forgotten relics. Xiao Chen and the young Niu Ren continue their exploration, stumbling upon a giant stone sword that radiates a killing intent so fierce it feels like a blade at the throat. The sword is none other than the legendary Zhuxian Sword, a weapon stained with the blood of countless gods and demons. The discovery triggers a chain of supernatural events: the Suiren Fire-Drill reacts, the sword shatters its stone shell and flies away, and a second stone leg emerges from the depths. The chapter balances grim underworld exploration with a mythic revelation that expands the novel’s cosmology, introducing the figure of Tongtian, a name that strikes terror into the hearts of gods. The tone remains tragically epic, blending awe with the weight of ancient, unforgotten power.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and Niu Ren, accompanied by the Black Dragon King, traverse the chaotic underworld, avoiding dozens of ferocious souls. After thirteen days, they enter a vast blood-colored desert and see a towering, sword-shaped peak in the distance. The oppressive killing aura grows stronger as they approach, and they realize the peak is a three-thousand-meter stone sword. Inscriptions on the blade reveal the name “Zhuxian Sword.” Niu Ren panics, recognizing the name of Tongtian’s supreme weapon. The Suiren Fire-Drill suddenly emerges from Xiao Chen and buries itself at the sword’s base, causing the sword to tremble violently. The stone shell cracks, revealing a radiant true blade that erupts with world-shaking energy. The sword tears through space and vanishes. In its wake, a second stone leg is left beside the first, perfectly matching the one found earlier at the ancient temple. Xiao Chen now possesses both legs of what appears to be a mysterious stone body. The chapter also cuts to a celestial island where the handsome but terrible Tongtian retrieves the Zhuxian Sword, declaring his intention to send disciples into the world. The Golden Divine Halberd and the Dark-Iron Seal stir from their slumber, sensing the power of the sword.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Zhuxian Sword is one of the four legendary swords from Chinese mythology, central to the *Fengshen Yanyi* (Investiture of the Gods). In that epic, Tongtian Jiaozhu—the master of the Zhuxian Sword Formation—wields four invincible swords: Zhuxian, Luxian, Juexian, and Xianxian. Here, the novel integrates this myth, positioning Tongtian as a primordial figure who once challenged the Ancestral God Youchao and beheaded countless immortals. The underworld, or Hell (地狱), is depicted not as a mere realm of punishment but as a layered domain where ancient cultivators and fierce souls make their lairs—a place ruled by power rather than justice. The Suiren Fire-Drill’s response to the sword underscores its sacred nature as a relic of primordial civilization. The stone legs, seemingly crude but causing such upheaval, hint at a greater mystery: perhaps a stone man or a primordial deity being assembled piece by piece, tying into the novel’s themes of lost ages and indomitable will.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a pivotal lore expansion. Readers should pay close attention to the stone legs—their origin is clearly tied to the underworld and the Suiren Fire-Drill, suggesting they are parts of a primordial construct. The awakening of the Zhuxian Sword and Tongtian’s reappearance signals that the stage is expanding beyond Dragon Island and the Eternal Continent. Tongtian’s decision to send out disciples foreshadows new factions and conflicts. The brief appearances of the Golden Divine Halberd and the Dark-Iron Seal indicate that these artifacts are not dormant but aware of cosmic shifts. Xiao Chen’s cultivation path now includes the secret of the stone legs, which may offer a path to powers that rival legendary weapons. The underworld’s dangers remain, but so do its hidden treasures. Keep an eye on the number of stone limbs collected; the full set may unlock something of world-shattering consequence.
