## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers a high-stakes duel that embodies the tragic, mythic tone of *World of Immortality*. Xiao Chen, newly revealed in the Yin capital, takes a stand against an overwhelming coalition of Great Shang and Roman Empire elites to protect his friend, the Buddhist monk Yizhen. The centerpiece is a clash with Jue Dao, the successor of the legendary Li Xunhuan and wielder of the Little Li Flying Dagger—a technique said to never miss and to kill even gods. The tension is palpable as Xiao Chen's Eight-Aspect Worlds, his self-created divine art, are pitted against a perfected, inherited killing technique that carries the weight of centuries. Beyond the physical conflict, the chapter explores the politics of alliance and enmity in the Eternal Continent, setting the stage for a confrontation that blurs lines between friend and foe.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen intervenes as a group of twelve experts—six from Great Shang and six from the Roman Empire—encircle Yizhen. Recognizing Xiao Chen from past encounters, they demand he step aside. Xiao Chen refuses, arguing that Yizhen is no foreigner but a fellow Eastern cultivator. The leader, Jue Dao of the Little Li Flying Dagger, prepares to strike. The atmosphere grows suffocating as Jue Dao exploits a flash of sunlight to launch his flying dagger, a technique that summons ghostly phantoms and deathly energies. Xiao Chen narrowly blocks the dagger with his fingers, sustaining serious injury from the piercing sword-qi. He then closes the distance, using his Eight-Aspect Worlds to trap Jue Dao in close-quarters combat, preventing a second throw. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen unleashing the eight realms—Earth, Fire, Wind, Water, and more—against his opponent, a desperate but calculated gambit to survive the legendary technique.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Little Li Flying Dagger is a legendary technique from Gu Long's *The Legendary Twin Flying Daggers*, adapted here as a peerless divine art that can tear through space and bypass defenses. Its use here highlights the xianxia theme of inherited versus self-created techniques: Jue Dao wields a perfected, lineage-based art, while Xiao Chen's Eight-Aspect Worlds are his own invention, derived from Fuxi’s trigrams. This contrast underscores a core theme of the novel—the value of personal enlightenment over rote tradition. Additionally, the political configuration mirrors the real-world Silk Road tensions: Great Shang (a Chinese-analogue) and the Roman Empire (a Western-analogue) are allied against Fan Kingdom (a Buddhist-analogue). The appearance of Roman spellmasters and knights alongside Eastern cultivators adds a layer of exoticism and stakes, showing that the world’s power struggles span entire continents.

## Reading Guide

This chapter raises several urgent questions: Can Xiao Chen's Eight-Aspect Worlds truly counter the Little Li Flying Dagger's legendary power? The fight is far from over; Jue Dao may yet have an ace. Watch for how Xiao Chen's body-refinement and his fused artifacts (the Dark-Iron Seal and Golden Divine Halberd) might play a role. Also note Yizhen's status as a potential Buddha successor—his safety is a priority for Xiao Chen, and his fate could influence broader alliances between the Pure Land and the southern tribes. The arrival of Zhao Lin'er and her Sun Knights in earlier chapters hints that her faction may also weigh in. As the battle rages, tension builds not only from the duel but from the political quagmire: Xiao Chen has made enemies of both the Tiger Clan and now the Great Shang elite. This fight is a crucible that will test the limits of his newfound power and his resolve.
