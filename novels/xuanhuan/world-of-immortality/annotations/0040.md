## Chapter Overview

This chapter thrusts Xiao Chen into the brutal reality of Dragon Island's ruthless competition. After witnessing the savage murder of cultivators by the barbarian youth Kai'o, Xiao Chen faces open provocation from Kai'o and the forest tribe youth Yarode. Yarode's scornful comparison of Xiao Chen to a bird before a dragon epitomizes the contemptuous hierarchy among practitioners. Xiao Chen suppresses his anger three times, recognizing that conflict is inevitable on an island where everyone fights over the Ancestral Dragon's legacy. But Kai'o refuses to let him retreat, pursuing and attacking. At last, Xiao Chen reaches his limit and resolves to fight — a decision that transforms his passive survival into active defiance. The chapter crackles with tension, contrasting Xiao Chen's disciplined restraint with the feral aggression of his enemies.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen follows the blood-red ape to the edge of a dangerous mountain range, where the ape stops and leaves. Pushing forward, he discovers fresh corpses of cultivators torn apart. The killer is revealed to be Kai'o, the barbarian youth from earlier, who is slaughtering practitioners with bestial savagery — using his tiger tail as a blade to bisect a man. Yarode watches calmly. They detect Xiao Chen and surround him. Yarode's insult — comparing Xiao Chen to a bird before a dragon — is a calculated humiliation meant to provoke or demean. Xiao Chen clenches his fist three times and releases it, choosing to walk away. But Kai'o, driven by predatory instinct, follows and attacks from above, crashing through treetops like a human beast. Xiao Chen realizes that on Dragon Island, compromise is impossible — everyone is a potential enemy in the scramble for dragon treasures. He reaches his moral limit and steels himself for battle: "There is nothing to say. Only slaughter could stop slaughter."

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces the stark tribal hierarchy of Dragon Island: races like the Barbarian Clan (with sealed beast souls) and the Forest Clan (with plant affinity) view themselves as superior. Yarode's metaphor of 'bird before dragon' reflects a worldview where strength dictates rank. The concept of a 'human-shaped beast' is common in xianxia — warriors who embrace primal instincts rather than refined cultivation. Kai'o's beast soul likely enhances his physical power and aggression. Yizhen the monk is mentioned as a figure whose favor grants temporary safety, showing how alliances and personal connections can deter violence even here. The repeated image of Xiao Chen clenching his fist three times and releasing it is a classic xianxia trope for suppressing anger, displaying emotional discipline — a key trait of a mature cultivator who avoids needless conflict but will not be cowed.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets the stage for Xiao Chen's first major confrontation with powerful rivals on Dragon Island. Watch how the fight unfolds — Kai'o and Yarode represent two different combat styles: brute force versus tactical control. Xiao Chen's unique acupoint-divinization cultivation and his skeletal allies may give him an edge, but he is outnumbered. The narrative also hints at the larger political landscape: alliances and enmities are forming rapidly, and Xiao Chen's fight will attract attention. Pay attention to the theme of 'bottom line' — Xiao Chen has a clear moral code: suffer humiliation if necessary, but never allow his dignity to be trampled. This principle will guide his choices throughout the arc. The chapter raises the question: can Xiao Chen survive and grow stronger while making enemies of the most powerful factions on the island?
