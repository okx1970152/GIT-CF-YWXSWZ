## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a breathtaking clash between two demigod-level beings—a Fallen Angel and a Light Holy Dragon—showcasing the terrifying scale of power that exists beyond mortal cultivation. Through Yizhen’s quiet exposition, the reader learns of the ancient, hunted Fallen Angel race, adding layers of mythic history to Dragon Island. Amidst the awe and dust, Xiao Chen endures a subtle but sharp social slight: the stronger cultivators dismiss him entirely. His internal reaction is not bitterness but a cold, resolute determination to prove himself through action. The chapter then pivots to mystery as the three skeletons lead Xiao Chen to a hidden valley where the defeated Light Holy Dragon slumbers, setting the stage for a dangerous opportunity.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the conclusion of the aerial battle between the Light Holy Dragon and the Fallen Angel. The Fallen Angel wounds the dragon with an energy sword and dark light, forcing it to crash onto Dragon Island where its divine powers will be permanently sealed. Yizhen explains that Fallen Angels are an ancient, nearly extinct race worshipped as gods by weaker races, hunted by other deities. This lore enriches the world’s depth. After the battle, the powerful cultivators—Kael, Arod, Rand, and Yan Qingcheng—depart in pairs, ignoring Xiao Chen entirely. His self-deprecating smile masks a fierce resolve; he knows strength is the only currency that matters here. Gathering salt, he leaves Yizhen to pursue Guluo and Zhao Lin’er before the island becomes even more chaotic. The three loyal skeletons then guide him to a valley outside his exploration range, where the massive silver Light Holy Dragon lies sleeping, its very presence radiating a powerful dragon aura.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Fallen Angels (堕落天使) are a rare and potent example of a recurring Xianxia trope: ancient races that once rivaled gods. Their depiction as winged, energy-wielding beings echoes Western mythology but is filtered through a cultivation lens—they are mortal-descended beings who reached divinity through cultivation, not divine creation. The Light Holy Dragon (光明圣龙) is another such being, representing a dragon lineage that can achieve human form and wield holy light. The critical worldbuilding element here is Dragon Island’s sealing effect: any dragon entering the island is stripped of its divine abilities and regresses to a bestial state. This explains why the dragon crashes rather than retreats. The social hierarchy among cultivators is also on display; those with lower cultivation are invisible to the powerful. Xiao Chen’s resolve in the face of this slight is a classic protagonist moment—proving oneself through growth rather than complaint.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up multiple threads. Xiao Chen’s discovery of the sleeping Light Holy Dragon presents a high-risk, high-reward opportunity. Will he attempt to steal the dragon’s treasures, or will he use this knowledge for a strategic advantage? The dragon’s presence also implies that other ancient beings may be drawn to the island. Meanwhile, the snub by Rand and Yan Qingcheng plants a seed of future conflict; Xiao Chen is not the type to forget or forgive. The alliance between Rand and Yan Qingcheng will likely complicate his plans. The arrival of more cultivators in the coming days promises to turn Dragon Island into a battlefield for the Ancestral Dragon. Xiao Chen’s priority remains eliminating Guluo and Zhao Lin’er, but the escalating scale of events may force him to adapt. Watch for how he leverages the skeletons’ loyalty and his growing acupoint cultivation in the chapters ahead.
