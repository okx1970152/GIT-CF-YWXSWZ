## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges Xiao Chen and his group into the very heart of the Death World, a realm of desolate grandeur and cosmic secrets. After bypassing the awe-inspiring Sixth Heavenly Stele, they traverse a soundless desert filled with the projections of worlds that have perished and those on the verge of annihilation. Here, they encounter a being reduced to a single white jade skull—a remnant consciousness of an ancient powerhouse whose true essence escaped long ago. Through this spectral guide, the chapter unveils staggering revelations: the Martial Ancestor’s mad scheme to sacrifice an entire world, the revitalization of a dead world by the goddess Nuwa, and the existence of the River of the Future, a temporal current that may allow glimpses into what lies ahead. The tone is one of tragic grandeur, balancing the awe of discovery with the weight of ages.

## Key Plot Points

The group first notices that the area around the Heaven Stele is a true forbidden zone, but they manage to circle around it and enter the deepest part of the Death World—a vast, silent Gobi desert. Scattered black vortexes appear, which the ancient skull later explains are the lingering projections of worlds that have already been utterly destroyed. Shortly after, they see bright, softly glowing orbs that are the “death throes” of worlds still in the process of perishing. The skull, concealed beneath the sand near Keke, introduces himself as a fragmentary remnant of a being who lost his body eons ago. He confirms that his true soul escaped and likely started anew elsewhere. He leads them to a world teeming with life—a world that had been dead but was revived by Nuwa, who now sits in meditation there. The skull then recounts the terrifying history of the Martial Ancestor: this figure extracted eleven ancient demon cities and planned to use them to sacrifice an entire other world (the Otherworld), but was persuaded by the skull to hold back. Instead, the Martial Ancestor sacrificed those demon cities and even himself to refine his own body into a supreme weapon, a method Xiao Chen finds deeply inspiring. Finally, the skull shows them the River of the Future, which flows from a stone gate representing the past into another gate that leads to the future. He warns that traversing it is deadly without the Oracle Bone Diagram. At the chapter's end, they glimpse the absolute edge of the Death World—a boundless, dark void—and the skull mentions the legendary Heavenly Stairway.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of world projections as “death throes” (回光返照) reflects a deep xianxia philosophical view that even universes have life cycles. The River of the Future, connected to the Oracle Bone Diagram, ties into Chinese lore of fate and oracles—the Diagrams are legendary artifacts capable of mapping destiny. Nuwa, the serpent goddess who mended the sky, here appears as a world-reviving cultivator, a fitting role for a creator deity. The Martial Ancestor’s actions—uprooting demon cities and attempting to sacrifice a world—embody the xianxia theme of defying heaven at all costs. His method of self-sacrifice to forge a body of ultimate power is a classic trope of the genre. The mention of the “Heavenly Stairway” (通天阶梯) hints at a final path to transcendence, often a goal for protagonists in such epics.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as a major lore dump, setting up crucial elements for the endgame. The River of the Future could become a key tool for Xiao Chen—perhaps to retrieve lost War Swords or to learn vital information about the Otherworld's plans. The ancient skull's connection to a past life (possibly the stone turtle or the old goat) is worth tracking. The Martial Ancestor's legacy offers Xiao Chen a potential cultivation path: sacrificing powerful remnants to forge his own transformation. Pay attention to the description of the Death World's edge and the mention of the Heavenly Stairway; these will likely be the location of the final confrontation. The chapter deepens the sense of cosmic inevitability, and Xiao Chen's determination is reinforced by the knowledge that such audacious feats are possible.
