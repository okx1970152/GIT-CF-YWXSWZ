## Chapter Overview

This chapter masterfully balances comedic relief with rising tension, as Xiao Chen grapples with the unruly Divine Tribe youths and then boldly ventures into the angelic realm of Heaven. The tone shifts from exasperated humor—as the seven young immortals bribe Keke with promises of exotic delicacies and scatter across the Nine Provinces—to a solemn, awe‑inspiring atmosphere when Xiao Chen retrieves his colossal stone statue from the Eastern Sea. The final act plunges into the heart of Heaven itself, where a mysterious voice hurls defiance at the resident half‑ancestor, suggesting that Xiao Chen is not the only one with a score to settle. The chapter reinforces the brutal law of the Hundred Races era while offering a glimpse of the colossal power Xiao Chen may soon command.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen returns to the Nine Provinces with seven young Divine Tribe immortals, expecting a valuable fighting force. Instead, they prove to be a chaotic bunch, each with bold ideas: one wants to taste angel wings, another to capture a divine king as a bride. Their greed proves infectious, and Keke—lured by promises of ten pairs of bear paws, five pairs of angel wings, three dragon legs, and two phoenix wings—secretly releases all but two. The remaining pair consists of the youngest, Tianya, who cannot resist the spirit fruits in Paradise Lost, and the hulking Qier, who dreams of abducting a Perfected Man‑level heavenly king as a wife. Disgusted but resigned, Xiao Chen sends Qier away as well, keeping only Tianya.

Turning to more urgent matters, Xiao Chen and Keke retrieve the colossal stone statue from the Eastern Sea. The statue, still pierced by the twenty‑four War Swords, has developed cracks but is slowly absorbing ambient spiritual energy, hinting at a possible restoration. This prize gives Xiao Chen renewed confidence. Deciding to track down the five escaped youths before they cause a disaster, he learns they planned to feast on angel wings, so he sets course for Heaven, the floating paradise of the angel race. Using Paradise Lost to shield their presence, they observe Heaven’s stunning landscape—serene meadows, busy lesser angels, and formidable martial angels training on sacred peaks. Deeper still, they sense the oppressive auras of nine sun‑like palaces and finally a plain ancient palace at the center. There, a brazen voice curses the “bird god,” the angelic half‑ancestor, demanding satisfaction. The chapter closes on this explosive cliffhanger.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Divine Tribe introduced here originates from the Death World’s God Village, a reclusive community of fire‑seed beings who have partially reversed death and now possess flesh‑and‑blood bodies. Their youths are immensely talented but sheltered, leading to a naive recklessness that Xiao Chen must manage. The statue retrieved from the sea is Xiao Chen’s own petrified body from the Paradise Lost arc, impaled by the twenty‑four War Swords; its gradual absorption of spiritual energy suggests that the swords themselves may be repairable or reclaimable. Heaven, in this setting, is not merely a celestial reward but the militaristic home of the angel race, who were allegedly created as war tools by an unknown supreme being. The term “Perfected Man” (至人) refers to a cultivation realm above Fish‑Leap, the level of many foreign kings, indicating the scale of power that now governs the world.

## Reading Guide

The mysterious challenger at Heaven’s core—who dares to openly insult a half‑ancestor—is a critical new thread. This figure’s identity and grievance will likely force Xiao Chen to choose sides or exploit the chaos. Meanwhile, the five escaped Divine Tribe youths are somewhere in the Nine Provinces, potentially walking into traps set by hostile kings. Their fate may test Xiao Chen’s responsibility as their guardian. The stone statue’s restoration is a long‑term asset, but its condition raises questions about whether the War Swords can be fully absorbed. As the Hundred Races era intensifies, Xiao Chen’s need for absolute power grows ever more urgent.
