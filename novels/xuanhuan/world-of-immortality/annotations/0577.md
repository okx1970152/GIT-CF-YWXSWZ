## Chapter Overview
This devastating chapter marks the end of a civilization cycle as Xiao Chen returns from the Celestial Realm to find all his worlds reduced to ash and silence. The tragic, desolate tone reaches its apex when he discovers that even the God Village in the Death World has been obliterated, leaving him the sole survivor among familiar faces. The chapter balances raw grief with a hauntingly beautiful stillness, symbolized by a single cherry blossom tree that outlasted the slaughter.

## Key Plot Points
- Shi Zhongdi (Old Goat) vanishes after speaking of another 30,000-year cycle, his fate sealed by the Celestial Realm giants. His final words, 'see you in 30,000 years,' echo the novel's recurring theme of cyclical time.
- Xiao Chen traverses the Nine Provinces, the Eternal Continent, the Soul World, and the Curse World, finding only ruins and corpses. Every living being has been wiped out by Otherworld forces.
- He glimpses a future version of himself fleeing toward the Heavenly Emperor City, a brief but powerful reminder of the Hope Gate's influence.
- In a burst of rage, he kills the ancestral gods Lan Yufeng and Lu Lopes using the ancient scroll and formation diagram, but the victory feels hollow.
- Retreating to the Death World, he discovers the God Village annihilated, with only a single cherry blossom tree—planted by Yan Qingcheng and Keke—still standing amid the desolation.
- The chapter ends with Xiao Chen's crushing solitude and an author's note reassuring readers that the story will conclude properly, not hastily.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of 三万年 (30,000 years) reappears as a significant cycle, reminiscent of Buddhist kalpas. The phrase '三万年后再相见' echoes earlier promises of reunion across eons, reinforcing the novel's epic scale. The cherry blossom tree symbolizes both beauty and impermanence in East Asian culture; here it stands as the last remnant of happier times shared with Yan Qingcheng and Keke. The destruction of entire worlds (九州, 长生界, 咒界, 魂界) reflects the overarching theme that civilizations rise and fall, and even half-ancestors cannot guarantee eternal survival. The author's note directly addresses the reader, a rare break of the fourth wall, to reassure that the ending is being carefully prepared.

## Reading Guide
This chapter functions as a narrative reset, stripping away all supporting characters and leaving Xiao Chen utterly alone. Readers should prepare for a final arc focused on his personal resolution and counter-attack. The appearance of a future self suggests time-manipulation abilities that may be explored further. The author's note signals that the ending is being carefully prepared; pay attention to how the remaining plot threads—the hope gate, the stone man path, and the Heavenly Emperor City—will be resolved. Xiao Chen's psychological state is at its lowest; future chapters will likely show him rebuilding his resolve from absolute despair.
