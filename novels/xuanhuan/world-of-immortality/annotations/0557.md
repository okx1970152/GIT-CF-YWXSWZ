## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a profound, meditative exploration of loss, sacrifice, and legacy. Xiao Chen enters the Great Palace within the most evil place and discovers the tragic fate of the Stone Man Kings—mighty beings who gave their all to protect the Nine Provinces. Through the Divine Diagram, he witnesses ancient battles where stone warriors fell, their bodies shattered, yet their wills remained unbroken. The palace itself becomes a sanctuary of memory and inheritance. Unlike previous action-packed chapters, this one slows to a contemplative pace as Xiao Chen spends fifty years absorbing five mysterious stone carvings, each embodying the essence of a fallen king. It is a chapter of quiet reverence that deepens the mythic weight of the story.

## Key Plot Points
1. Xiao Chen stands in the Great Palace, surrounded by the shattered remains of Stone Man Kings. He learns through the Divine Diagram that these beings once sacrificed themselves to complete a world-saving diagram, shattering their bodies and souls.
2. The palace reveals that the Nine Lamps, once used to refine the Divine Diagram, have left residual power in their stone bases. This power briefly rekindles to sustain the Divine Diagram and reveal a vision of the past.
3. Vision: The Otherworld won a bloody victory, but all Stone Men were lost. The surviving Stone Man Kings used their final moments to send their fragments back to the palace, while their residual souls lit the Nine Lamps to sustain the Nine Provinces.
4. The Stone Man Kings left behind five stone carvings on the altar. Xiao Chen, recognizing their profound value, sits before each for ten years, finally imprinting the fifth after fifty years.
5. The carvings are not techniques but the very essence of the kings—their divine bearing, their will, their place in the Dao. To absorb them is to channel a fragment of their indomitable spirit.
6. As Xiao Chen completes the fifty years, a near-crumbled Stone Figure stirs beneath the palace, revealing a living—or barely living—Stone Man may still exist.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of the 'Stone Man' is a unique cultivation path in *World of Immortality*, distinct from typical xianxia realms. Stone Men are beings who have achieved a state of near-invulnerability and cosmic connection, often rivaling Ancestral Gods. Their 'final step' represents a complete transformation into a being of primordial stone. The 'Nine Lamps' are a recurring motif—sacred artifacts that can refine and preserve. The act of imprinting a master's essence through prolonged meditation is a classic xianxia trope, emphasizing patience and spiritual resonance. The 'Divine Diagram' here is a fragment of a larger, world-essential artifact, suggesting that Xiao Chen's accumulation of Heavenly Stele imprints is part of a cosmic plan.

## Reading Guide
This chapter rewards patience and an appreciation for world-building over fast action. Readers should pay close attention to the visual descriptions of the stone carvings—the divine bearing of each fallen king—as they may foreshadow Xiao Chen's own eventual cultivation path. The mention of a living Stone Man beneath the palace is a major development; keep this in mind as the plot advances. The fifty-year time skip also marks a significant maturation for Xiao Chen's character, deepening his connection to the ancient heroes. The aftermath of this chapter will likely see him emerge with a new, unstoppable resolve, perhaps carrying the legacy of the Stone Man Kings into battle.
