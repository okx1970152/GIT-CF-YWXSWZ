## Chapter Overview

This chapter transports readers to the heart of the Great Shang Kingdom, one of the five hegemonic states of the Eternal Continent. The setting is a prestigious establishment where aristocratic youths from the Central Plains openly mock cultivators from the Southern Wastes, dismissing them as barbaric and weak. The tension culminates in a duel between Yu Wenfeng, a proud Southern Wastes expert, and Yin Feng, a spiritist wielding the sinister black lotus flame. Yu Wenfeng is defeated, highlighting the gap in technique and cultivation resources between regions. Yet, beneath this surface conflict, a darker undercurrent flows: the chapter subtly reveals that Xiao Chen, widely believed dead after his legendary night of slaughter in Heavenly Emperor City, is alive and present, observing the scene with cold detachment. The narrative thus balances immediate martial rivalry with the lingering shadow of a figure who once shook the foundations of the Southern Wastes.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with biting insults from several Central Plains aristocratic youths, who dismiss Southern Wastes cultivators as unworthy of attention. The boasts of these young elites are not empty: their cultivation is genuine, backed by family resources and ancient techniques. Yu Wenfeng, unable to tolerate the disrespect, challenges Yin Feng, one of the most prominent among them. The duel takes place within the confines of the establishment, with the participants agreeing not to damage the historic site. Yu Wenfeng unleashes his Ten Thousand Beasts Demonic Art, manifesting a crimson beast domain and countless spectral beasts. However, Yin Feng's dark lotus flame proves superior—it burns through the beast domain and devours the summoned souls, forcing Yu Wenfeng to retreat. The aristocratic youths mock him openly, while bystanders like Yan Qingcheng and Qilao watch with frustration. A Shui and A Bing, siblings from the Western Frontier, intervene to prevent further humiliation. Throughout this, the narrative cuts to Xiao Chen, seated quietly nearby, observing everything without intervening. The chapter ends by noting his presence, leaving readers to wonder when he will act and what his survival means for the fragile status quo.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The divide between the Central Plains and the Southern Wastes reflects the hierarchical nature of the World of Immortality. The Great Shang Kingdom is one of the five dominant states, each with deep-rooted aristocratic families that control cultivation resources and ancient scriptures. In contrast, the Southern Wastes are considered a backwater, though they have produced occasional legends like Long Teng and Xiao Chen. Yin Feng's black lotus flame is a rare spiritist ability that has evolved into a full divine art, capable of burning both physical matter and spiritual entities. Such techniques are typical of the Central Plains' inherited bloodline secrets. The chapter also references a past event—a night when Xiao Chen killed over a dozen Spirit-Treasury experts and five demigods before vanishing. This massacre, still fresh in memory, underscores the latent power of the Southern Wastes even as its current young generation struggles against Central Plains arrogance.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as a crucial worldbuilding pivot, moving the story from the insular politics of Heavenly Emperor City to the broader continent of the Eternal Continent. Readers should note the introduction of the Great Shang Kingdom and its aristocratic culture—it sets the stage for future conflicts between regional powers. The appearance of A Shui and A Bing from the Western Frontier hints that other regions are also engaged in this competition. Most importantly, Xiao Chen's silent presence signals that his legend is far from over. The chapter builds a quiet tension: the Central Plains youths believe the Southern Wastes are weak, but they have no idea that the man who once fought demigods is watching them. This contrast between perceived vulnerability and hidden strength will likely erupt in subsequent chapters. Pay attention to Yin Feng's technique—the black lotus flame may become a recurring threat or power to overcome.
