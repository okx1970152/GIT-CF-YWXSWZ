## Chapter Overview
This chapter opens with Xiao Chen uncovering a chilling secret: three ice coffins containing ancient human skins, one of which appears freshly shed. The implication—a being undergoing cyclical rebirth—casts an ominous shadow over the snowy region. Quickly departing, Xiao Chen witnesses Yan Qingcheng emerging from the snow with a dramatic breakthrough to the Mortal-Shedding Sixth Heaven, her Undying Demon Art proving its fearsome capability. After a brief pursuit, he meets Liu Mu, the Space Spiritist, in a tranquil valley. Their conversation bridges cultivation insights and the island's volatile politics, setting the stage for future alliances and confrontations.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen examines three ice coffins: two ancient with blackened blood, one recent with fresh blood and a fragment of new skin. He deduces a terrifying cycle of death and rebirth, possibly an ancient being shedding its skin like a cicada. Disturbed, he reburies the coffins and leaves the snowy region. On his way out, he sees a purple light and recognizes Yan Qingcheng, who has survived being buried under an avalanche and broken through to the sixth level of Mortal-Shedding. Xiao Chen considers pursuing but she flees with her newly formed light wings. Exiting the snow, he finds a lush, peaceful mountain with a lake where Liu Mu is fishing. Liu Mu senses Xiao Chen's bloodthirsty aura from the recent massacre. They acknowledge both have broken through to the Sixth Heaven. Liu Mu proposes a rematch but Xiao Chen declines, citing his need to stay at peak form to deal with current enemies. They share cultivation insights: Xiao Chen's dawn enlightenment, Liu Mu's lesson about letting go of pressure. They discuss the nature of Spiritists versus Spellmasters—Spiritists are born with innate powers, while Spellmasters channel energy from external deities through chants. Finally, they touch on Dragon Island's shifting dynamics and the threat of Yan Qingcheng and others.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of cyclical rebirth through shedding the body like a cicada echoes Daoist and Buddhist notions of reincarnation and transcendence. In xianxia, such transformations often accompany techniques like the Undying Demon Art, which allows a practitioner to advance through near-death experiences. The contrast between Spiritists and Spellmasters highlights different magical systems common in xuanhuan: innate talents versus external divine gifts. The description of space spiritists as "kings among same-level cultivators" underlines their feared status. The peaceful lake setting juxtaposed with Xiao Chen's recent violence embodies the series' signature contrast between serene nature and brutal cultivation reality.

## Reading Guide
Xiao Chen now faces a landscape of rising powers: Yan Qingcheng has broken through, the mysterious ice-coffin entity lurks, and the alliances are regrouping. His refusal to fight Liu Mu preserves his strength, but also hints at a potential future showdown. The discussion on cultivation insights suggests that mental state is as crucial as physical energy—a theme that will likely deepen. Watch for how Xiao Chen navigates the increasing threats while consolidating allies. The revelation of the ice coffins may foreshadow a major antagonistic force tied to Dragon Island's secrets, perhaps connected to the undying kings or ancient beings. The narrative tension between survival and ambition continues to drive Xiao Chen's decisions.
