## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges the reader back into the desolate heart of the Death World, where Xiao Chen, Tianwaitian, and Renwairen have been trapped in a deadly gray mist for decades. The mist is no ordinary fog—it contains a terrifying 'World Power' that can dissolve even half-ancestors. The narrative shifts from desperate survival to shocking encounters: a crumbling skeleton king who attacks them, a mysterious bone map that offers a possible path, and finally a devil king who has survived alone for a thousand years on a floating black rock. The chapter culminates in the appearance of a colossal heavenly palace drifting toward them, leaving the question of escape tantalizingly open. The tone is one of oppressive dread and eerie wonder, as time itself seems meaningless in this deathly void.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the three protagonists fleeing a gray mist that destroys mountains and shatters sub-dimensions. Tianwaitian and Renwairen struggle to resist the corrosive fog, but it soon overwhelms them. Xiao Chen merges with the cracked stone statue, and using the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree, he manages to protect all three. A sudden attack from a skeleton king forces them to fight back—they trap him in a sword array and after a brutal struggle, the king's body is destroyed, leaving only his skull and a bone fragment. Under interrogation, the king reveals that the gray mist is a primordial death material, and that the bone fragment is part of a map sought by even transcendent beings. They kill the king and absorb his fire seed, but Xiao Chen finds his cultivation has plateaued—the fire seed can no longer raise his realm, only deepen his latent potential. The next forty years are a monotonous march through the fog until they discover a floating black rock island. On it lies a nearly spent devil king named Yingxiong, who has been trapped for a thousand years. He greets them with bitter curses, then tosses them another bone map fragment. As they prepare to rescue him, the earth trembles—a massive heavenly palace looms out of the mist, far larger than their island.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The gray mist is described as containing 'World Power' (世界之力), a concept in xianxia where the fundamental force of the world itself can annihilate even immortal beings. This elevates the Death World from a mere afterlife to a primeval realm where the laws of existence break down. The bone map (甲骨图) evokes ancient Chinese oracle bone inscriptions—fragments that hold cosmic secrets. Such maps are a recurring trope in cultivation novels, often pointing to hidden inheritances or ultimate treasures. The cultiation plateau Xiao Chen experiences—where external resources (fire seeds) no longer advance his realm—reflects a common xianxia theme: after a certain point, only personal enlightenment and accumulation can fuel growth. The term '君王' (king) in the Death World refers to powerful undead rulers, equivalent to the 'Perfected Man' realm in the living world.

## Reading Guide

The bone map fragments are the key to escaping the gray mist and possibly to the deepest secrets of the Death World. The approaching heavenly palace may hold answers—or greater dangers. Pay attention to the devil king Yingxiong: his thousand-year survival hints at hidden resilience and knowledge. The fact that Xiao Chen absorbed only part of the skeleton king's fire seed and felt his potential increase suggests that latent power will be critical in future breakthroughs. The relationship between the two half-ancestors and Xiao Chen also deepens during these decades—their cooperation is now instinctive. Readers should wonder what lies beyond the mist and whether the palace is a refuge or a trap. The slow passage of time here foreshadows that the overarching plot may leap forward once they escape.
