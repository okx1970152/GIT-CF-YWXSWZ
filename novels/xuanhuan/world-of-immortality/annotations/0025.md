## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a concentrated dose of brutal, tactical combat in the Death Swamp. Having stolen a precious Life Radiance scroll and healed his wounds, Xiao Chen is no longer the hunted—he becomes the hunter, cutting through Guluo's forces with cold efficiency. But Guluo, a scion of a powerful family, is not so easily broken. Using rare magic scrolls, he turns the tide with devastating precision: Deep Blue Ice freezes the three skeleton kings solid, and a petrification scroll encases Xiao Chen in stone. Zhao Lin'er watches from the sidelines, her elegant praise masking her calculation. The chapter closes with a splinter of hope: Xiao Chen's stone prison cracks, hinting at a breakout that will shake the swamp. The tone is unrelentingly grim, with short, violent bursts of action punctuated by moments of tense standoff.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen, now fully healed by the Life Radiance, immediately seizes the offensive. He kills several of Guluo's elite guards with devastating blows—splitting a man in half, kicking through another's chest—as the three skeletons hold Guluo at bay. This aggressive push is a calculated move to reduce enemy numbers before the main threat can retaliate. Guluo, enraged and humiliated, unleashes his trump cards: first a Deep Blue Ice scroll that summons a massive energy bird and blankets the area in freezing light, trapping the three skeletons in ice. Then, as he charges Xiao Chen, he deploys an earth-attribute scroll that conjures an eight-meter energy lizard. After a desperate chase through the trees, Xiao Chen is caught in the yellow light and petrified into a statue. Zhao Lin'er, ever the political player, showers Guluo with flattery while he mourns his fallen men—a performance that strengthens his authority among the survivors. The chapter ends with a crack appearing in Xiao Chen's stone shell, signaling his imminent liberation and the continuation of this bloody struggle.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The magic scroll system presented here is a key feature of the World of Immortality's combat variety. Scrolls (卷轴) are one-time artifacts that store powerful spells—here, elemental attacks or sealing techniques like petrification and ice binding. They represent a resource-based economy of power: rare, precious, and often decisive. The Three Skeletons—now named Qin Guangwang, Yanluowang, and Lunhuiwang—are named after the Ten Kings of Hell from Chinese underworld mythology. This naming not only gives them identity but also foreshadows their potential evolution, as each Yama King presides over a specific judgment of the dead. In the broader xianxia tradition, petrification is a common sealing technique, often used to neutralize threats temporarily. Breaking free requires immense internal energy or a sudden breakthrough, which Xiao Chen's previous cultivation of the Heavenly Stele Profound Art may enable.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for a dramatic reversal: Xiao Chen's breakout from the stone seal will likely be explosive, and Guluo has now exhausted his most valuable scrolls. The power dynamic is shifting. Readers should watch for how the Three Skeletons recover from their icy prison—their loyalty and combat effectiveness will be critical in the next confrontation. Zhao Lin'er's role as a political manipulator also deepens; she is not merely a vengeful adversary but a strategist who lets others bleed for her goals. The scroll-based magic system adds a layer of tactical depth: Xiao Chen may need to acquire or counter such artifacts in future battles. Finally, note the thematic tension between life and death: the Life Radiance that healed Xiao Chen came from the same source as the killing scrolls, reminding us that in the World of Immortality, creation and destruction are two sides of the same coin.
