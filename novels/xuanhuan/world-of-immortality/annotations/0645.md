## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges deeper into the desolate prison island, where ancient sealed beings have gone mad from eons of isolation. The focus shifts to the central stone prison, revealed to be part of the legendary Five Emperor Pagoda. The female corpse, a figure of immense power and mystery, strides toward it, shattering buildings and absorbing soul shadows. When a stone sword on the prison door wounds her with a rainbow beam, she responds with a divine seal that resonates with the Heavenly Stele techniques, shattering the weapon. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen being pulled into the pagoda, where he discovers five engraved figures on the inner walls—a potential key to a new cultivation path. The tone is oppressive and tragic, blending horror with the solemn weight of ancient secrets.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the aftermath of the prison break: ancient criminals, reduced to mad soul shadows, dissipate as they are absorbed by the female corpse. She moves purposefully toward the central prison, a towering stone structure that dwarfs the surrounding peaks. A stone sword hanging on the prison door shoots a rainbow beam that pierces her chest, demonstrating that the prison is guarded by a force capable of wounding even her. Unfazed, she begins to form a complex hand seal. Xiao Chen notices that the seal bears a striking resemblance to the Heavenly Stele techniques he knows, but is vastly more profound. The seal's power shatters the stone sword into sand. However, the door itself emits a dazzling light that repels her. Xiao Chen then realizes the prison has the shape of a two-story pagoda—the legendary Five Emperor Pagoda, used as a cage for a terrible entity. He is drawn inside by a spatial vortex, where he finds himself in the pagoda's first floor. The walls bear five carved figures in different postures, presumably depicting an ultimate technique. The chapter balances horror, mystery, and cultivation lore effectively, revealing that the female corpse may have a direct connection to the creator of the Heavenly Steles.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Five Emperor Pagoda (五帝塔) is a reference to the mythological Five Emperors of ancient China, often associated with supreme power and order. In xianxia, such pagodas are common as artifacts used for sealing or training. The female corpse's divine seal being similar to the Heavenly Stele Profound Art suggests that the steles and the corpse share a common origin—perhaps from the same primordial master. The prison island concept, where ancient criminals are sealed and go mad over eons, is a staple of Chinese fantasy, emphasizing the terror of immortality without purpose. The rainbow beam from the stone sword is reminiscent of the Five-Colored Divine Light used by Kong Xuan, but here it's a weaponized law of heaven, a 'divine principle' condensed into light. The five engraved figures on the pagoda walls likely represent the highest martial or spiritual truths of the Five Emperors, a technique that could rival the Heavenly Stele methods.

## Reading Guide

The chapter also deepens the mystery of the 'demon shadow' that orchestrated the escape. With Xiao Chen trapped inside the pagoda, the next chapters will likely explore its interior and the being within. The contrast between the female corpse's unstoppable power and the prison's ancient defenses heightens the stakes, setting the stage for an escalation in the cosmic conflict.
