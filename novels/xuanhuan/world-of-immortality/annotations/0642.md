## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks the violent end of the Otherworld as a known realm, as the allied supreme beings of the Nine Provinces—Pangu King, Wu Zu, Fuxi, Keke's father, and the Stone Crow—unleash their full might. The battle is apocalyptic: worlds burn, ancestors fall, and the very fabric of the Otherworld is sacrificed in a ritual of holy fire. Yet the victory is profoundly hollow. The four founding ancestors of the Otherworld retreat before the final blow, taking with them unseen reserves and a deeper truth: the world destroyed was only a shell. The chapter closes millennia later, when the Ancestral Dragon Ship returns with all aboard dead, a grim testament to the perpetual cost of war. This is not an ending, but a pause—a moment of exhausted breath before the next upheaval.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with Pangu King engaging Lu Zhan in a clash that shatters the Otherworld's sky. Wu Zu sits motionless in the void, igniting the Saint Sacrifice that will burn the entire realm to nothing. Keke's father intercepts Lang Ya, while the Stone Crow and Keke's great-grandfather occupy two other ancestors. When the Eternal King Meng Degang arrives with True Wood Female Saint, they are met by Fuxi, who kills Meng Degang with the Eight Origins Sounds and, assisted by the Stone Crow, dispatches the Female Saint. Lu Zhan, seeing no path to victory, orders the ancestors to flee. The world collapses into chaos and fire. Afterward, Pangu King warns that the Otherworld was merely a front; its true strength lies elsewhere. A time skip of thousands of years follows. The Ancestral Dragon Ship—sent away three decades ago—returns, crashing into the Eternal Continent. Aboard it are only corpses, all passengers dead, their fate a mystery.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter draws heavily on Chinese mythological figures. Pangu, the primordial creator, is here reimagined as a Stone Man King, wielding a colossal axe that represents the power of opening heaven and earth. Fuxi, the serpent-tailed god of early Chinese mythology, is portrayed as a supreme martial artist who commands the Eight Origins Sounds—primordial vibrations that govern creation and destruction. The Saint Sacrifice (圣祭) is a forbidden technique where a cultivator uses the life essence of an entire world as fuel, a method so extreme it borders on self-annihilation. The Ancestral Dragon Ship, first seen earlier in the novel, was built to carry chosen cultivators away from danger; its return with only corpses underscores the cruel irony of fate. The 'Thirty Thousand Year Pact' referred to highlights the ephemeral nature of mortal schemes against cosmic timelines.

## Reading Guide
This chapter concludes a major arc—the destruction of the Otherworld as a tangible realm—but opens far larger questions. Where did the four founding ancestors flee? What deeper power do they serve? More chillingly, what happened to the passengers of the Ancestral Dragon Ship? Their corpses suggest a catastrophe that occurred beyond the known worlds. The sealed stone door atop the remaining stone steps, previously locked by the Three Sovereign Mirror, may become a focal point. Readers should brace for a new phase: the Otherworld's true masterminds remain hidden, and the cost of victory will only grow. The deaths of Meng Degang and True Wood Female Saint are significant—they were the strongest beneath the Ancestral Gods—but their loss does little to diminish the ominous aftertaste of this hollow triumph.
