## Chapter Overview
This chapter plunges Xiao Chen and his companions into the depths of chaotic spacetime, where time and distance become meaningless. They encounter a mysterious stone monument left by a supremely powerful being, whose childish scrawls hide a mocking test. The Martial Imprint, already bearing the scars of this being's earlier mischief, is forced into a humiliating act of veneration, only to discover it was all a ruse. The chapter culminates in a stunning vision: a white beast identical to Keke but utterly different in demeanor — aloof, sunglasses-sporting, and exuding a timeless arrogance. This doppelgänger's casual words hint at a being who has transcended the known realms, leaving Xiao Chen and the Martial Imprint to reckon with a power that toys with half-ancestors for amusement.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen, the Martial Imprint, Bai Qi, and the sentient weapons drift through disordered spacetime, witnessing ancestral god-level attacks that miss by eons. They discover a prehistoric island with vibrant life, and atop a peak find a large green stone engraved with childish but imperious writing. The message requires an ancestral god-level cultivator to prostrate for three days to open an exit. Despite the Martial Imprint's fury, the group convinces him to try. After he secretly performs the ritual, the stone reveals another mocking line, proving it was all a trick. The Martial Imprint is left seething. Weeks later, they glimpse a parallel spacetime channel where a white beast identical to Keke appears, but this one is cool and arrogant: it wears black crystal glasses, has slicked-back hair, and speaks of loneliness and boredom. The Martial Imprint explodes with rage, identifying it as the same entity that carved on him and devoured ancestral god spirit essences. This vision confirms that Keke's lineage involves a being of near-ancestral god power who roams across worlds.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces chaotic spacetime, a realm where normal laws of time and space break down, often appearing between worlds in xianxia cosmology. Only supreme beings can navigate safely. The stone monument's demand for ancestral god-level veneration reflects cultivation hierarchy: only the highest echelons can interact with certain ancient inheritances. The prehistoric island is a fragment of a bygone era, a common trope representing preserved power. The being behind the monument is implied to be Keke's relative or future self, connecting to earlier hints about Keke's divine origin. The wry humor in the writing mirrors Keke's playfulness, suggesting a familial resemblance. The Martial Imprint's fury highlights the humiliation of a half-ancestor being tricked by a being that treats ancestral gods as playthings.

## Reading Guide
Readers should note the significance of the white beast's appearance: it is likely a future version of Keke or a parent, and its casual mention of visiting other worlds or the Primordial Celestial Realm foreshadows larger cosmic conflicts. The chapter reinforces that Keke's true nature is far grander than a simple spirit beast. The Martial Imprint's reaction deepens the mystery of the stone monument writer, who may become a recurring figure. Pay attention to spatial distortion – time flows differently in chaotic spacetime, which could explain Keke's transformed demeanor. The next chapter may explore how Xiao Chen and his group escape this limbo and whether they will encounter the real Keke again.
