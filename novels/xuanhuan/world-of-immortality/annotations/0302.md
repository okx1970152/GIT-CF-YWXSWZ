## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a spectacular clash of titans as the mysterious Qinglian Tiannü engages Jieyin Daoren in a battle that tears through dimensions and redefines space itself. The confrontation is not merely a contest of power but a philosophical duel over the nature of reality—Jieyin's "Realm of Reception" versus Qinglian's absolute mastery of the War Swords. The scale is cosmic: oceans vaporize, space fractures for thousands of li, and half-ancestors watch from the sidelines with growing unease. When Jieyin is defeated but reveals a Fate Twin Sons technique, the tension ratchets higher as Tongtian Jiaozhu steps forward, wielding the legendary Four Immortal-Slaying Swords. Amid this, Keke's sudden appearance on Xiao Chen's shoulder adds an intimate, almost tender note, reminding readers that the protagonist is still present—an observer in a war of gods.

## Key Plot Points
The battle begins with Qinglian Tiannü unleashing her four War Swords against Jieyin Daoren's Dharma Wheel and iron whisk. Jieyin counters with his signature "Realm of Reception," a space that renders attacks illusory. Qinglian destroys the space and reorders it, forcing Jieyin to regenerate multiple times. She wounds him, extracting a price of three drops of her blood for thirty thousand souls. Jieyin retaliates with "Three Thousand Buddha Lands" and "Ten Thousand Demon Realms," but Qinglian's swords and twelve-grade lotus platform prove superior. She even claims the right to behead Jieyin. However, Jieyin survives by using the Fate Twin Sons technique, dividing himself into two entities—himself and the Buddha—though the Buddha does not join the fight. Tongtian then enters the fray, stating that he will prove himself against Qinglian. He deploys the Four Immortal-Slaying Swords and a bronze shield, but Qinglian dismisses his swords as inferior copies of the true War Swords. The chapter ends with the tension unresolved, and Keke arrives on Xiao Chen's shoulder.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The term "Realm of Reception" (接引世界) is a direct reference to the Buddhist concept of 接引 (reception into the Pure Land), but here it is weaponized as a spatial domain that negates attacks. Qinglian's "Twelve-Petal Lotus Throne" (十二品莲台) is a classic Buddhist treasure symbolizing purity and invulnerability, often associated with Guanyin or the Buddha. The "Fate Twin Sons" (命运双生子) is a divine art that splits one being into two independent bodies, making the user nearly impossible to kill—a technique previously seen in Chu Xingkuang. Tongtian's "Four Immortal-Slaying Swords" (诛仙四剑) are legendary from the Fengshen Yanyi; in that myth, Tongtian's swords formed an invincible killing formation. Here, Qinglian claims they are inferior copies, elevating the status of her War Swords. The swords are named: Zhuxian (Slay Immortal), Luxian (Kill Immortal), Xianxian (Trap Immortal), Juexian (Extinguish Immortal). The bronze shield is reminiscent of defensive artifacts from the same mythology.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up a cascade of half-ancestor battles. Readers should track the power hierarchy: Qinglian has defeated Jieyin and now faces Tongtian. The outcome will likely affect the broader conflict between the Eternal Continent forces and the Otherworld. Keke's arrival signals that Xiao Chen's group is not far behind; the presence of the white beast may tip the scales. Pay attention to the language about "true War Swords" versus copies—this may foreshadow the ultimate role of the twenty-four War Swords Xiao Chen collected. Also, the Fate Twin Sons technique links Jieyin and the Buddha; if they unite, their combined power could challenge even Qinglian. The next chapter likely continues this confrontation, with Tongtian deploying his sword formation.
