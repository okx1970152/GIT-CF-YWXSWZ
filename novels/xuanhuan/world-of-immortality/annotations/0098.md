## Chapter Overview

This chapter shifts from the claustrophobic horror of the Dead City to the enigmatic beauty of the Sacred Mountain. The group's escape is disorienting, and they quickly realize their new surroundings are equally treacherous. Keke's reappearance injects both levity and wonder, as her antics with the Purple Dragon King reveal her fearless nature. But the true tension emerges when members of the group begin vanishing without a trace, hinting that the Sacred Mountain is a labyrinth of invisible death traps. Xiao Chen's leadership and Keke's mysterious rescue abilities become the group's only hope.

## Key Plot Points

The blood altar transports the survivors to the Sacred Mountain, a location familiar to Xiao Chen from his earlier near-death experience. The group quickly discovers that the mountain is riddled with spatial anomalies that cause individuals to vanish, likely sending them back to even more dangerous parts of the Dead City. Xiao Chen volunteers to lead the descent, relying on Keke's unique spatial manipulation ability to pull him back when he disappears. Keke rescues him six times, but her power is waning. The Purple Dragon King, observing Keke's technique, replicates a weaker version, revealing that Keke's ability is actually a dragon race divine art. Yizhen provides a crucial insight: Keke may belong to one of the few ancient species that can learn any race's abilities, explaining her extraordinary versatility. The group's progress is halted by the increasing density of ancient palaces near the mountainside, which correlate with greater danger. They must find a way to descend safely or risk being trapped forever.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Sacred Mountain is a recurring location tied to the primordial sealing of Dragon Island. Its spatial anomalies are likely remnants of ancient formations or battlefields. The concept of "spatial translocation" is a common trope in xianxia, often used to traverse between realms or escape danger. Here, the blood altar serves as a crude teleportation array, but the destination is unpredictable and fraught with peril. Keke's ability to learn dragon techniques is significant: many xianxia stories feature divine beasts that can mimic the powers of other species, often as a sign of supreme lineage. The fact that even the Purple Dragon King, a future dragon sovereign, is inferior to Keke in this art suggests her origins may be tied to the primordial age when dragons shared the world with equally powerful races.

## Reading Guide

This chapter deepens the mystery of Keke's identity. Her ability to use a dragon's innate spatial binding art—and surpass the Purple Dragon King in its execution—raises profound questions about her lineage. Readers should watch for how this revelation affects Xiao Chen's treatment of Keke and the reactions of other cultivators. The Sacred Mountain itself remains a hazard; the group has not yet reached the base, and with Keke's power weakening, their descent will become increasingly perilous. Additionally, the connection between the Dead City and the Sacred Mountain suggests that these locations are part of a larger, trapped network on Dragon Island. Future chapters may explore this network and the forces that created it. Pay attention to any new abilities Keke might display, as her repertoire could be the key to surviving the island's deadliest regions.
