## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a monumental turning point where the celestial steles vanish, the Dead City fully descends, and the ancestral past bleeds into the present. Xiao Chen, guided by a fortune teller revealed to be King Wen of Zhou, steps into the Dead City to confront the half-ancestors. The epic 'Ancestral Song' echoes across the Nine Provinces, a lament for the forgotten heroes Suiren, Shennong, and Nüwa. The chapter is saturated with tragic grandeur: the corpse of Ancestral God Youchao crumbles, merging with the Yellow Clay Altar, while Xiao Chen's stone man stands and fuses with his body. The half-ancestors are summoned by the death knell, and the battle for reality versus illusion begins.

## Key Plot Points
King Wen of Zhou appears as a fortune teller, delivering a prophecy: 'Go to the Dead City, summon the heroic spirits, and the hundred thousand death army will slay the false gods.' Xiao Chen witnesses the Yellow River Stele and the stele from the Dead City fade into the void. The Dead City, previously a distant anomaly, now fully manifests in the mortal world, triggering a global upheaval of spiritual energy. Xiao Chen's forehead mark opens a passage, and he arrives on the city walls. The city is filled with ancient warriors and monstrous beasts; the Demon Well at its center emits a pillar of dark light and a death knell that summons half-ancestors from across realms. The White Tiger Saint Emperor and Sun Holy God attack Xiao Chen, but the Yellow Clay Altar shields him. The corpse of Youchao is ejected from the Demon Well; the half-ancestors attempt to seize it, but it crumbles, its flesh merging with the altar. The ancient song pierces the hearts of all, and Xiao Chen weeps for the forgotten ancestors. Within him, the broken stone man rises and fuses with his limbs, awakening a new power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The 'Ancestral Song' is a central poetic element that references the primordial ancestors: Suiren (who brought fire), Shennong (who tasted herbs), Nüwa (who mended the sky), and the Yellow Emperor and Yan Emperor (Yan and Huang). It criticizes the present age for forgetting these founders while praising false gods. King Wen of Zhou, author of the I Ching, appears here as a sage who can read fate. The lore of the 'Post-Heaven Eight Trigrams' (后天八卦) is tied to King Wen, distinct from Fuxi's 'Pre-Heaven Eight Trigrams'. The Dead City is also called the 'Abyss of Evil' (罪恶深渊), a prison that once held the twenty-four War Swords and the corpse of Youchao. The fusion of the broken stone man with Xiao Chen's body signifies the awakening of a 'Stone-Man' path, a primordial cultivation method lost to ages.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for the climactic conflict in the Dead City. Pay close attention to the 'Ancestral Song'—its verses hint at the true history of the Nine Provinces and the nature of the half-ancestors as illusions. Xiao Chen's emotional vulnerability (tears, grief) marks a rare moment of humanity before the final battle. The fusion of the stone man suggests Xiao Chen is becoming a vessel for the will of the ancestors. The half-ancestors' desperation to seize Youchao's body and their summoning by the death knell indicate that the Dead City is a fatal trap. The coming chapters will likely reveal the fate of the 'hundred thousand death army' and the true meaning of 'shattering the false gods'. Readers should watch for the role of the Yellow Clay Altar and the nine eternal lamps, which seem to guide Xiao Chen's path.
