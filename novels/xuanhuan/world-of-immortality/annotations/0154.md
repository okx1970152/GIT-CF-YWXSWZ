## Chapter Overview
This chapter offers a rare moment of stillness for Xiao Chen amidst the turmoil of Dragon Island and the Eternal Continent. The hot spring setting creates a false sense of peace, but beneath the surface, Xiao Chen's mind churns with memories of the mortal world and the implacable timeline he has set himself. It is a meditation on loss, ambition, and the cost of the immortal path, culminating in a deliberate provocation of the local nobility. The blend of poignant introspection and the first sparks of direct conflict in Heavenly Emperor City makes this a pivotal chapter for character and setting alike.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen, relaxing in the hot springs of a luxury venue, allows himself a moment of vulnerability. He reflects on his family back in the mortal world, the unlikelihood of ever returning, and the painful brevity of mortal life. He resolves to reach the Deathless Longevity realm within twenty-four years—a nearly impossible goal—before he is too old to see his loved ones again. This personal vow steels his resolve. He sends a servant to summon the Rigen family scion Hoffman, whom he intends to confront for the earlier attack. Hoffman arrives with three other nobles: Luogu Ao, Zhuge Kun, and Li Dongbo. Xiao Chen coldly forces them to identify themselves and immediately slaps Li Dongbo when he becomes aggressive. Meanwhile, Keke, tipsy from stolen wine, stumbles into a neighboring pool where several Southern Wastes beauties, including Yan Qingcheng, are bathing. Keke's playful escape from their attempts to capture her adds a moment of levity, though Yan Qingcheng recognizes her and smiles knowingly. The chapter ends with the tension at the boiling point, Xiao Chen facing the four young masters with cold contempt.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The chapter illustrates the profound emotional conflict inherent in xianxia: the pursuit of immortality requires the practitioner to sever ties with the mortal world, yet the heart remains bound. Xiao Chen's 24-year deadline mirrors the real-life pressure of cultivation timelines common in Chinese web novels, where time is both a resource and an enemy. The hot spring venue itself is a classic trope—a neutral ground where social tensions simmer beneath polite facades. The "beauty-outshining palace" and its baths represent the decadence of Heavenly Emperor City's elite, while the ritual of summoning servants and the hierarchy of noble families reflect the rigid social structures of this world. Keke's accidental intoxication ties into the concept of spiritual beasts not being immune to mortal weaknesses, adding unpredictability.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up two major threads: Xiao Chen's personal cultivation deadline and his escalating feud with the Rigen family. The confrontation with Hoffman is likely a prelude to a larger clash, especially with the nearby presence of Yan Qingcheng, whose allegiance remains ambiguous. Watch how Xiao Chen's cold efficiency in dealing with the nobles will ripple through the political landscape of Heavenly Emperor City. Meanwhile, Keke's encounter with Yan Qingcheng may signal a future alliance or complication. The twenty-four-year vow gives the entire story a sense of urgency—every chapter now carries the weight of Xiao Chen's internal clock. The relaxed opening also makes the impending violence more impactful, a classic narrative contrast.
