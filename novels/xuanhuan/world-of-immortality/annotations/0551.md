## Chapter Overview
This chapter deepens the mystery of the death world's ancient inhabitants and their connection to the divine diagram. Xiao Chen confronts a being who once projected himself as Tongtian Jiaozhu, but now reveals his true identity as Xu Tian, a death world entity of immense age and power. The encounter shifts from a test of strength to a tense negotiation, as Xu Tian offers cooperation against a supreme enemy — the one whose projection was the false god Hongjun. Xiao Chen, ever pragmatic, demands Stone Man cultivation secrets and the very demonic city that Xu Tian commands. The chapter blends epic combat with lore revelations, setting the stage for a dangerous alliance.

## Key Plot Points
The Taoist who blocks Xiao Chen is not the real Tongtian but an ancient death world being named Xu Tian, whose projection once imprinted the 'Tongtian' image into mortal worlds. He has been waiting for Xiao Chen's arrival — or more precisely, for the reappearance of the divine diagram that the war swords now carry. Xu Tian tests Xiao Chen's power with four immortal-slaying swords, which are shattered by Xiao Chen's thirty-three war swords. He then reveals a stone spear — the same one that flew from the ruined city beneath the Nether River — and uses his own primordial demonic city as a weapon. Xiao Chen, refusing to be coerced, counters with conditions: he will cooperate only if Xu Tian imparts the secret method for cultivating to the Stone Man realm and gives him the demonic city. Xu Tian unexpectedly agrees, demonstrating his desperation or his true need for the divine diagram.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The concept of 'projection' (投影) is central to this chapter. Powerful beings in the death world can scatter fragments of their consciousness across realms, which may then coalesce with the faith of mortals to create 'fake gods' — as seen earlier with figures like Tongtian and Hongjun. Xu Tian explains that most such originals have perished over the eons, but he remains. The stone spear and the movable demonic city both tie to the Stone Man path, a rare cultivation route that culminates in a being of petrified flesh nearly immune to divine law. Xiao Chen's demand for the 'complete Stone Man method' signals his intention to walk this path. The phrase 'the one that fled' (遁去的一) comes from the I Ching's great derivation (大衍之数五十, 其用四十有九), referring to the missing element that the broken formation diagram embodies — a key to defeating even Supreme Ancestral Gods.

## Reading Guide
Xu Tian's sudden agreement to Xiao Chen's steep demands should raise questions. What is the true nature of the enemy he fears — the being whose projection was Hongjun? And can a death world creature of such ancient malice be trusted? Xiao Chen's growing ambition to become a complete Stone Man is now supported by a potential teacher, but the price may be his future involvement in a war beyond the Nine Provinces. The demonic city and the stone spear are new assets, but they may carry hidden dangers. Readers should watch for how Xu Tian's cooperation unfolds, and whether the alliance holds when the supreme enemy makes its move.
