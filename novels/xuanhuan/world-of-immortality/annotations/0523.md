## Chapter Overview

The forging of Keke's supreme treasure reaches its climax in this chapter, aided by the ancient stone statue and the mechanical warrior of the divine city. As the Seven-Colored Jade Eggshell is refined into a dazzling small bowl with heavenly thunder and divine source material, the artifact shows its power by contending with three supreme ancestral god weapons. However, a greater mystery unfolds when a corner of the broken formation diagram appears with five War Swords, only to remain forever out of reach, lost in the past. The chapter ends with the approach of a colossal yellow-earth giant bearing a stone head, forcing their retreat to the mortal world. Xiao Chen's understanding of the stone man deepens, and the boundaries between past and present blur.

## Key Plot Points

Keke's long-awaited treasure forging process succeeds spectacularly. The Seven-Colored Jade Eggshell, after being tempered by mountain-sized heavenly thunder and infused with the essence of countless divine artifacts, transforms into a small, warm jade bowl with seven-colored light. The bowl bears Keke's unique paw print and a miniature carving of the little beast. The stone statue's nine main vines and the mechanical warrior's guidance prove crucial. Following the forging, the blood figure formed from three Supreme Ancestral Gods' blood attacks but is instantly destroyed by a main vine. The three supreme treasures—Demon Blade, Immortal Shield, and Thirty-Three-Story Pagoda—are then used to test the bowl, and with the vines' help, the bowl absorbs their divine power. Meanwhile, a corner of the broken formation diagram appears in the sky accompanied by five War Swords. Xiao Chen attempts to capture it using his own formation diagram and thirty War Swords, but it vanishes, lost in time. The mechanical warrior explains it cannot be obtained. Finally, a giant yellow-earth figure with a stone head strides toward the city, and the nine vines open a spatial passage to send Xiao Chen, Keke, and their companions back to the mortal world. Xiao Chen reflects on the nature of the stone man and the stone statue, sensing they are distinct, and realizes that the battle at the end of time may impact the present.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The forging of divine artifacts through heavenly thunder is a classic xianxia trope, symbolizing heavenly punishment and refinement. Here, the thunder not only tempers the material but also carries the essence of a thunder spirit, which is actively captured and fused into the treasure. The concept of a treasure bonding with its owner through personal marks—like Keke's paw prints—is also common. The formation diagram lost in time is a profound idea: some supreme artifacts exist in a temporal state beyond reach until the right conditions align. The stone man path, a unique cultivation system in this novel, distinguishes between stone statues (immobile, power-concentrated forms) and true stone men (living beings of peerless physical might). The giant yellow-earth figure with a stone head suggests a being at that ultimate stage, raising the stakes for the coming conflict.

## Reading Guide

This chapter showcases the birth of a new supreme artifact that could rival the greatest weapons in existence. Readers should note how Keke's treasure bowl interacts with the three supreme Ancestral God weapons—it already displays world-suppressing potential. The loss of the formation diagram is a setback, but it confirms that a complete diagram exists, possibly to be gathered later. The approach of the yellow-earth giant heralds a powerful new adversary or ally; its stone head implies it may be connected to the stone man lineage. Xiao Chen's growing insight into the stone man's nature will be crucial in the battles ahead. The transition back to the mortal world suggests that the consequences of the end-of-time events are about to ripple into the present. Keep an eye on Keke's role: her treasure may become decisive.
