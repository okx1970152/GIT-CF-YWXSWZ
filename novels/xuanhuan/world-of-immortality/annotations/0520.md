## Chapter Overview

This chapter plunges the five Otherworld Ancestral Gods into a nightmare journey through a primordial landscape where the boundaries of time have turned treacherous. They are ambushed by the legendary Kunpeng, a beast of myth that should be long dead—yet it fights with suicidal ferocity, seriously wounding one of their number. As they press onward, a chilling realization dawns: they are traveling backwards through the ages, and the fallen heroes of the past are being "borrowed" by an unseen power to oppose them. Meanwhile, Xiao Chen glimpses a fleeting fragment of the broken formation diagram, and Keke grows inexplicably excited, sensing the pulse of her own bloodline in the ancient air. The chapter culminates in the discovery of a colossal, dread-filled city that sends even the Ancestral Gods into trembling terror.

## Key Plot Points

The five Otherworld Ancestral Gods—Lu Lopes, Lan Yufeng, Ai Er, and two unnamed others—traverse a savage ancient mountain range, killing an aggressive primeval bird that attacks them. This act awakens the Kunpeng, an avian ancestor of unimaginable power. Despite their combined strength, the Kunpeng fights without any regard for its own life, self-destructing its body and burning most of its soul to grievously wound Ai Er. The Ancestral Gods finally destroy it with the aid of the upper Ancestral God's thirty-three‑layer pagoda. However, they are shaken by the realization that the Kunpeng was already dead in the real world—it has been resurrected through the power of a mysterious family, which is using the fabric of time itself to weaponize ancient history. Faced with the choice of stagnation or progression, they decide to march forward, hoping to find a way out. Elsewhere, Xiao Chen and Keke follow at a distance. Xiao Chen sees a corner of the broken formation diagram carrying five War Swords flash past; he cannot catch it. Keke becomes very agitated, certain that her family's power is near. Finally, the group arrives at a vast valley centered on an immense ancient city, eerily similar to the City of the Dead and the Heavenly Emperor City. The Ancestral Gods stare in dread, recognizing that in the forgotten past, someone from Keke's clan achieved a state that rivaled—or perhaps surpassed—the Heavenly Emperor. The chapter closes on the terrible, unfinished exclamation: 'The city has grown a—'

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Kunpeng is one of the most famous primordial beasts in Chinese mythology, derived from the Zhuangzi's parable of a colossal fish that transforms into a bird of equal enormity. Here it is depicted as a 'war ancestor' with combat power that can threaten even true Ancestral Gods. The concept of 'borrowing' a dead being from the past is a unique xianxia device, tied to the mysterious 'family' (now clearly linked to Keke's bloodline). Their ability to reach across time and resurrect fallen heroes makes them a terrifying opponent. The ancient city that appears at the end strongly resembles both the Dead City (from Dragon Island) and the Heavenly Emperor City, suggesting a shared origin or a cyclical pattern of supreme civilization. This deepens the lore about Keke's origins and the true nature of the 'most evil place' in the Death World.

## Reading Guide

The chapter leaves us with a cliffhanger: what has the ancient city 'grown'? Could it be a living weapon, a sealed ancestor, or a passage to another realm? Pay close attention to the fragment of the formation diagram that Xiao Chen failed to capture—it may reappear as a key element. Keke's excitement suggests that her family's hidden bastion is nearby, and the impending encounter may reveal long‑kept secrets about her lineage and the fate of the Primordial Celestial Realm. The Ancestral Gods are now thoroughly terrified, hinting that the next chapters will bring a confrontation with an entity beyond their worst fears. Readers should watch for the convergence of Xiao Chen's War Swords, the broken diagram, and Keke's awakening powers.
