## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a somber, introspective turning point for Xiao Chen. After returning to the mortal world and encountering the remains of the last pure martial artist, he resolves to fully embrace the Martial Imprint. But before he can step onto that path, he must first sever the deepest emotional tie of his former life: his love for Ruoshui. The chapter is a slow, painful journey through memory, as Xiao Chen visits every place they shared and systematically erases her from his heart. The tone is tragic and desolate, heavy with the weight of loss and the cold necessity of cultivation. It is the death of his former self—a brutal, necessary sacrifice for the martial path.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Xiao Chen standing in a hut where a poem is carved, reminding him of Ruoshui. He then travels to Emei Mountain, where he witnesses a group of powerful cultivators (likely from the mortal world's cultivation circles) breaking open a sealed mountain. Inside, they find a stone platform with a crystal coffin containing the skeleton of a pure martial artist—a ‘former martial artist’ who cultivated the same path Xiao Chen now pursues. The skeleton briefly animates, revealing that the martial artists were exterminated by an alliance of all other cultivation systems after their Ancestral God fell. The skeleton’s residual consciousness transfers its blood to Xiao Chen, igniting his ‘war blood’ and affirming the supremacy of the martial path. After burying the skeleton, Xiao Chen decides to fully cultivate the Martial Imprint. This requires a ruthless purification of his heart: he must cut away all emotional ties to his past. Over the next months, he travels to every place he shared with Ruoshui—Nanjing’s Qinhuai River, Beijing’s snack street, the northern grasslands, and finally the Yellow Crane Tower. With each visit, he forcibly erases her memory from his mind, using his will to sever the bonds. The process culminates in a meeting with old friends, including a woman with a third eye who perceives the pain of his self-inflicted severance. At the end, Xiao Chen asks, ‘Who is Ruoshui?’—the final affirmation that he has cut her out completely. The chapter closes with his transformation: he feels empty, but also filled with power and purpose to pursue the ultimate martial way.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter draws on the Daoist concept of ‘cutting off attachments’ as a necessary step on the path to immortality or supreme power. In xianxia, a cultivator must often sever ties to the mortal world, including romantic love, to avoid emotional distraction. Xiao Chen’s method—visiting each significant site and willfully erasing the memory—is an extreme form of this practice, reflecting the uncompromising nature of the ‘Martial Imprint’ path he has chosen. The skeleton of the last martial artist delivers a crucial warning: pure martial artists were persecuted by an alliance of all other cultivation schools because they were so powerful. This echoes the history of disputes between cultivators in Chinese mythology, where secret traditions were sometimes suppressed through collaboration. The woman with a third eye is a nod to characters with spiritual perception who can glimpse events beyond ordinary view; her third eye represents a common trope of divine insight in xianxia. Finally, the phrase ‘人生若只如初见’ at the chapter’s opening is a famous line from a poem by Qing dynasty poet Nalan Xingde, lamenting the fading of initial romance. Its use here frames the entire chapter as a farewell to innocence and love.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a quiet, emotional interlude after intense action and conflict. Readers should pay close attention to the tone—it sets the stage for Xiao Chen’s new resolution and the power he will gain. The encounter with the martial artist’s skeleton is a major lore drop: it suggests that the ‘Martial Imprint’ path is not just an individual choice but part of a suppressed tradition. Xiao Chen’s decision to sever his love is not portrayed as triumphant; it is haunting and ambivalent. The woman’s comment about ‘a man who makes your heart ache’ hints that this transformation may have costs. Looking forward, this chapter foreshadows Xiao Chen’s future cultivation breakthroughs, but also raises questions about whether cutting off all emotion is truly strength or a form of self-mutilation. The reappearance of characters from his mortal past (Chen Fang) and the mysterious third-eye woman may set up future encounters. Readers should expect Xiao Chen to return to the Eternal Continent with a colder, more focused mindset, ready to confront enemies with renewed purpose—but also with a lingering hollow inside.
