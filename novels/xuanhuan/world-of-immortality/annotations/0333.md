## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a pivotal shift from the claustrophoric horrors of the Dead City to a larger meditation on civilization, memory, and the cost of forgotten history. The mysterious figure wreathed in the Burial Shroud dominates the first half, his mere presence shaking the Dead City and scattering half-ancestors with casual might. Then the scene transitions to the mortal world's capital, where a seemingly ordinary fortune teller delivers a devastating critique of humanity's ingratitude toward its own founding ancestors. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen taking direct, violent action against the imperial palace, calling out Zhao Lin'er and signaling a decisive phase in his homecoming. The chapter balances mythic terror, philosophical weight, and raw vengeance in equal measure.

## Key Plot Points

In the Dead City, Xiao Chen, Qingqing, and Keke encounter a towering figure draped in the Burial Shroud. Though no energy fluctuations emanate from him, his presence crushes the soul. He reaches out and teleports Keke into his grasp, causing the little beast genuine fear for the first time—her divine light and Paradise Lost fail entirely. After studying her, he releases her unharmed and vanishes. Days later, he reappears on a rooftop, staring west, then casually reaches into the sky to obliterate a hidden divine island, gravely wounding the Three-Baby Matriarch who was spying from within. The sheer scale of his power leaves Xiao Chen awestruck. Unable to comprehend the Dead City's secrets with such a being present, they depart.

Meanwhile, changes ripple across the Nine Provinces: mysterious ancient lamps rise over each province, and phantom images of lost lands—War Clan valleys, Dragon Island, floating villages—grow increasingly distinct, portending their materialization. In the bustling capital of the Great Shang Empire, a cultured fortune teller catches Xiao Chen's attention. He accurately reads Keke's nature and then declares Xiao Chen will soon die, though great men will be buried with him. Pressed to speak of heaven and earth, the fortune teller delivers an impassioned lament: the ancestors (Fuxi, Nüwa, Suiren) blazed the path of humanity through blood and sacrifice, yet later generations prefer the false comfort of 'divine melodies' over their true inheritance. He insists that the inversion of the illusory and the real is what plagues the world. A thunderbolt aimed at him forces him to depart, but not before warning Xiao Chen of upheaval. Taking the prophecy as his deadline, Xiao Chen flies over the imperial palace, shatters its halls with the Dark-Iron Seal, and demands Zhao Lin'er show herself.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The fortune teller's speech echoes a core theme in Chen Dong's works: the conflict between authentic ancestral sacrifice and hollow doctrine. The mention of 'Great Dao' (大道) as something that can enslave or override the ancestors reflects a Daoist critique of institutional dogma. The lamps rising over each province are reminiscent of the 'nine lamps' motif common in Chinese legend, often tied to the Nine Provinces as a unified territory. The phantom lands—War Clan valleys, Dragon Island, floating divine villages—are drawn from earlier arcs in the novel and from mythological geography, grounding the narrative in a layered cosmos. The fortune teller's passionate tears and the thunderbolt attempt to silence him evoke the story of the historian Sima Qian or even the philosopher Qu Yuan, where truth-tellers are punished by heaven or state. His line about 'the last five thousand years' alludes to cycles of civilization, a familiar theme in Chinese historical philosophy.

## Reading Guide

The fortune teller's prophecy that Xiao Chen's death is imminent creates a countdown tension that will likely drive the next several arcs. Xiao Chen's response—to act boldly rather than cower—indicates a shift from reactive survival to aggressive resolution. Keep an eye on the phantom lands materializing: this suggests the boundaries between the Mortal World, the Cultivation World, and the Longevity World are dissolving. The mysterious figure in the Dead City is almost certainly Suiren in a corrupted or incomplete state, which may relate to the Burial Shroud. The lamps over the provinces and the fortune teller's talk of inverted reality hint at a larger crisis of memory and identity. Zhao Lin'er's reaction to Xiao Chen's assault will test her position as Solar Saintess and possibly reveal more about the Sun Religion's collapse. This chapter sets the stage for both personal vengeance and a cosmic reckoning.
