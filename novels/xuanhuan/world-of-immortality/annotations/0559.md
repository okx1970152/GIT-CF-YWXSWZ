## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a pivotal shift in scale as Xiao Chen stumbles upon a hidden passage to the legendary Celestial Realm (天界). However, the path is not a simple road to salvation. It is a tomb—a prison where six Ancestral Gods and a failed Stone Man have rotted for eons, their desiccated corpses holding a grim secret. The discovery of a divine scroll left by five Stone-Man Kings and the chilling words "The Heavens are not reliable" cast a long shadow over the hope of refuge. The sudden, unnatural resurrection of the six corpses, their bodies reanimated by an unseen force from across the void, injects a note of dread. Xiao Chen emerges from the passage into a paradise of breathtaking beauty, but the lingering horror suggests this new world may be far more dangerous than it appears.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen cleaves through a sealed passage in the chaotic void, only to find a long corridor fortified with divine metals—a prison for supreme beings. Within, he discovers the remains of six Ancestral Gods and a semi-petrified Stone Man, all utterly decayed by time. A desperate engraving on a stone wall screams "The Celestial Realm is not reliable!" revealing that the very realm he sought for aid once betrayed its own defenders. He then retrieves a magnificent ancient scroll bearing the imprints of the five Stone-Man Kings, a treasure that seals the exit. As the scroll is removed, a supernatural wind rises, and the six ghostly Ancestral God corpses reanimate, their flesh swelling and their faces twisted in demonic fury. They do not attack Xiao Chen but are swiftly summoned away by a black bridge of magic extending from tens of thousands of li away, drawn by an entity of terrifying power. Finally, Xiao Chen steps out of the passage into a pristine landscape of sapphire lakes, white swans, and floating celestial mountains, realizing he has truly entered the Primordial Celestial Realm.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Primordial Celestial Realm (洪荒天界) is a foundational concept in this cosmology—a realm above the Four Worlds, often depicted as the ultimate paradise and source of supreme power. The ruined prison within its passage subverts this ideal. The Ancestral Gods (祖神) are beings who have achieved supreme enlightenment and eternal life, yet here they are shown as fragile husks. The Stone Man (石人) is a rare cultivation path that sacrifices divine abilities for an indestructible body; the failed Stone Man at the prison's end could not complete his transformation and died. The scroll left by the five Stone-Man Kings (石人王者) is a supreme artifact, perhaps a map or seal, now obtained by Xiao Chen. The resurrection of the six corpses into undead (尸变) echoes the novel's recurring theme that "death is not the end"—but here it is a horrifying corruption, not rebirth. The invisible hand that drags them away hints at a power that predates even the Ancestral Gods of this age.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a lore bomb that redefines the arc. Readers should watch for clues about who or what pulled the corpses—it may be the same presence that corrupted the Celestial Realm in ages past. The phrase "The Heavens are not reliable" sets up a major twist: Xiao Chen's journey may not end in help but in a deeper conflict. The divine scroll he now carries will almost certainly become a key artifact in future battles. The serene beauty of the Celestial Realm is almost certainly a facade; given the horror behind the passage, the paradise ahead is likely host to ancient grudges and sleeping monstrosities. Keep an eye on the fate of the six reanimated Ancestral Gods—they may reappear as puppets or merged into a single abomination.
