## Chapter Overview
This chapter delivers a monumental convergence of power as ten half-ancestors—including Laozi, Tongtian, Buddha, and others—travel with Xiao Chen to the Shu Mountain Immortal Island to confront the formidable Blue Lotus Heavenly Maiden (Qinglian Tiannu). The atmosphere shifts from the serene, misty sea journey to a tense, almost apocalyptic confrontation. The half-ancestors, who had remained hidden and patient, finally reveal their presence, and the Blue Lotus Heavenly Maiden, who had been arrogantly controlling the situation, is shocked to her core. Xiao Chen steps forward with a demand for the release of Qingqing and Ruoshui, setting the stage for what promises to be a earth-shattering battle among supreme beings. The chapter crackles with the weight of ancient legend and impending violence, a masterclass in building mythic tension.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the half-ancestors traveling across the Eastern Sea, their combined might calming the turbulent waters. They note the floating Penglai Immortal Island and discuss foreign treasures, though they do not pursue it. Chiyou senses that old acquaintances like Yu the Great and Houyi may soon reappear. As they approach Shu Mountain Immortal Island, the peaceful scene is shattered by a massive explosion: Fuxi (Taihao) is blasted out of the void, barely protected by his Heaven-Reaching Tower. The voice of the Blue Lotus Heavenly Maiden rings out from dimensional space, threatening anyone who interferes. When she finally emerges, adorned with a Twelve-Grade Lotus Platform and surrounded by four war swords, she immediately realizes the horrifying truth—she faces ten half-ancestors, not one or two. Her shock is palpable; she had dismissed Xiao Chen as a mere demigod, never imagining he could summon such backing. She throws insults, but the half-ancestors remain calm. Buddha and Tongtian step forward, eyeing her lotus platform and swords, but it is Ksitigarbha who demands satisfaction. The sea freezes for three thousand li as she unleashes her full killing intent, and the standoff intensifies.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The term 'half-ancestor' (半祖) refers to beings who have reached the ultimate threshold below the Ancestral God (祖神) level, possessing world-shaking power. The simultaneous appearance of ten such figures is unprecedented and underscores the magnitude of the crisis. The chapter draws heavily on Chinese mythological figures: Laozi, the founder of Daoism; Tongtian, the master of the Jie Sect; the Buddha; Ksitigarbha (Jieyin Daoren), a Western Buddhist sage; and Fuxi, a primordial culture hero. Their diverse origins enrich the world's tapestry. The Shu Mountain (蜀山) is a fabled realm of immortals in Chinese lore, often a setting for epic battles. The Twelve-Grade Lotus Platform is a supreme Buddhist treasure, capable of both defense and attack, while the Four Immortal-Slaying Swords are legendary weapons from the Fengshen Yanyi, associated with Tongtian. The freezing of three thousand li of sea demonstrates a half-ancestor's ability to alter the environment on a colossal scale.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up a powder keg of conflict. The next few chapters will likely feature an intense battle between the Blue Lotus Heavenly Maiden and one or more of the half-ancestors, as personal honor and the pursuit of treasures drive them forward. Readers should pay close attention to the dynamics among the half-ancestors—Buddha's interest in the lotus platform and Tongtian's desire for the swords may create friction. Xiao Chen remains in a precarious position, relying on his allies but still vulnerable. The fate of Qingqing and Ruoshui hangs in the balance. Additionally, the mention of Chiyou's old acquaintances and the appearance of Penglai Island suggest that even more legendary figures may soon emerge. The tension is masterfully built, and the resolution will have far-reaching consequences for the Eternal Continent.
