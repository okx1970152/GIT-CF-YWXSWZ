## Chapter Overview

This chapter presents a desperate confrontation as an Otherworld Ancestral God attempts to descend through a portal, only to be repelled by Xiao Chen's Heavenly Stele, the Divine Diagram, and the Ancient Scroll. The battle is intense, with the menacing black claw demonstrating terrifying power. However, the tide turns when Xiao Chen reinserts the Heavenly Mark into the stele, causing it to regain its full might and shatter the claw. The sacrificial altar is destroyed, aborting the summoning. Yet the chapter does not end in triumph; it transitions into a journey deeper into the Death World's ancient forbidden zone, where the eighth Heavenly Stele stands—a blank stele devoid of inscriptions, raising new mysteries. The tone shifts from explosive combat to ominous exploration, hinting at deeper secrets and dangers.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen's initial assault with the Heavenly Stele is nearly thwarted by the Otherworld Ancestral God's black claw, which grabs the stele. Meng Degang and the True Wood Female Saint attack but are restrained by the black tide from the stele. Xiao Chen uses the third Heavenly Mark to empower the stele, driving back the claw. The altar shatters, ending the descent. Afterward, Xiao Chen recovers and, with Keke and the skeleton kings, advances into the forbidden zone. They face increasing pressure and lightning but use their treasures to proceed. They find the eighth Heavenly Stele, which is blank—no text or diagrams. This contrasts with previous steles that contained cultivation methods or inscriptions. The blank stele suggests something unique or incomplete. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen bypassing it to see what lies at the deepest end.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Heavenly Steles are primordial artifacts tied to the fate of the Nine Provinces and the martial path. Each stele previously provided Xiao Chen with cultivation insights and Heavenly Marks. The discovery of a blank eighth stele subverts expectations; it may represent a stage beyond form and text, aligning with Daoist concepts of the nameless origin. The Otherworld Ancestral God's black claw resembles descriptions of primordial demonic entities in Chinese mythology—its scaled, clawed form evokes ancient dragons or demons. The sacrificial altar and summoning ritual mirror classic xianxia tropes of evil cults attempting to bring forth a dark god. The 'black tide' from the stele's depths suggests the stele's autonomous protection, possibly a law of the Death World forbidding combat near the deepest secrets.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up a major mystery: what is the blank eighth Heavenly Stele? Xiao Chen's decision to bypass it rather than engage suggests he is conserving strength for greater challenges ahead. Readers should watch for how the blank stele's purpose unfolds—it may be a key to the final step of the Stone Man path or a gateway to the ultimate truth of the Death World. The brief appearance of the black tide as a protective mechanism hints that the deepest part of the forbidden zone holds something that even the Otherworld Ancestral Gods fear. The chapter's pacing balances action with exploration, and the cliffhanger regarding the blank stele promises revelations in subsequent chapters.
