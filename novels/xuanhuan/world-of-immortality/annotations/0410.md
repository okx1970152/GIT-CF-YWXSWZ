## Chapter Overview

This chapter offers a welcome respite from the relentless battles of the Hundred Races era. It opens with the Yecha King's bitter humiliation at the hands of Tianwaitian, a sharp reminder that even the most fearsome kings can be caught off-guard by cunning and audacity. Xiao Chen returns from the Death World only to find himself in the midst of a warm, sometimes comedic reunion in Hangzhou. Old friends, rivals, and allies gather as if drawn by fate, their surprise at Xiao Chen's survival underscoring how far he has traveled from the brink of death. The tone balances the lingering gravity of the Death World with the lighter, more human moments of shared meals, playful insults, and the pure joy of seeing familiar faces.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter begins with the Yecha King's public humiliation: Tianwaitian mocks him, forcing him to retreat even though he is a Perfected Man realm king. This demonstrates that even the highest cultivation does not guarantee invulnerability to political embarrassment. Xiao Chen then returns to the Death World, where he learns from the Divine Tribe about their near-fatal adventures in the central islands and the existence of a fearsome female Yecha king. Departing for the mortal world, he heads to Hangzhou to meet Keke. He finds Keke and the boy Tianya in a food street, stealing treats like classic mischief-makers. After reprimanding them, he repays the shopkeepers and the group runs into Toti, who was sold into a black coal mine by Jin Sanyi and is now a wealthy mine owner. Jin Sanyi appears with his stuttering humor, and soon the reunion expands: Niu Ren (now Bull Demon King), Liu Mu, and a revitalized Yuwen Feng appear, followed by the Inverse Dragon King, Azure Dragon King, Black Dragon King, and a host of other familiar faces—Chen Hangjin, Jue Dao, Chu Xingkuang, Huo Niao, Yao Yao, A Shui, and Samo. All are stunned to see Xiao Chen alive and well.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The term '烧饼' (baked cake) is used as a recurring insult throughout this chapter, roughly meaning 'dimwit' or 'blockhead.' It is a playful yet stinging rebuke, and the Divine Tribe members wield it with enthusiasm. Jin Sanyi's signature stutter, rendered with word repetition and ellipsis, provides comic relief without breaking the mythic register. The Yecha King's humiliation by Tianwaitian highlights an important facet of the Hundred Races political landscape—saving face is critical, and clever taunts can be as effective as weapons. The gathering of so many young experts from earlier arcs (Chu Xingkuang of the Fate Twins, Chen Hangjin of the Nine Nether King Wheel, Jue Dao of the Little Li Flying Dagger) gives the reader a sense of how much time has passed and how each has evolved.

## Reading Guide

This chapter primarily serves as a breather before the next storm. The Yecha King's retreat concludes his immediate threat, but the mention of a female Yecha king suggests the danger is far from over. The massive reunion of Xiao Chen's old allies and rivals creates an electric atmosphere—something big is brewing. Keep an eye on the Divine Tribe members, whose unique abilities and outsider perspective may prove crucial in the coming conflicts. The reappearance of Yuwen Feng, now immensely powerful after merging with a horse-king soul, hints that he may finally shed his 'eternal loser' reputation. This is a chapter for savoring connections before the epic clashes that surely await.
