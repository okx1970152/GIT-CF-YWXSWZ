## Chapter Overview

In this chapter, Xiao Chen, gravely wounded by Prince Feng's self-destructive attack, becomes the prey of an ancient Tyrant Dragon. Facing near-certain death, he refuses to surrender. More remarkably, the young Celestial Horse, which had previously kept its distance, voluntarily appears and risks being torn apart to lure the dragon away, displaying courage and compassion beyond instinct. Xiao Chen escapes with his life, but collapses unconscious in a tree from his injuries. When he wakes, the rain has stopped; he finds a flowery peak as a temporary camp and begins healing using the Wood Essence of the abundant vegetation. After the tense flight and rescue, the chapter shifts into quiet recovery and reflection, as Xiao Chen ponders the mystery of the Tyrant Dragon's lost divine powers, seeding future exploration.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen's desperate flight from the Tyrant Dragon is a harrowing sequence: the dragon's footsteps shatter trees, its roar nearly deafens him, and its claws gouge the earth inches from his body. Just when escape seems impossible, the Little Celestial Horse appears, flashing through the treetops to draw the dragon's attention. This brave act allows Xiao Chen to flee the predator's territory. However, his injuries from Prince Feng's explosion force him to collapse after running several miles. He slips in and out of consciousness, glimpsing the Celestial Horse's concerned form through the rain. When he fully awakes at noon, he finds a safer perch—a small, flower-covered hill with a clear spring—and begins recuperation. He also reflects on the strange fact that the ancient Tyrant Dragon, which legends say should command wind and lightning, displayed only brute ferocity on this island. Connecting this to Prince Feng's earlier remark about a divine stele suppressing Dragon Island, Xiao Chen starts to suspect that some greater seal is stripping primordial beasts of their supernatural abilities. This insight marks a turning point in his understanding of the world. Meanwhile, he resolves to recover fully within thirty days, after which he will hunt down Zhao Lin'er, who orchestrated this chase. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen using the Wood Essence of the forest to heal his wounds, guided by the profound art of the ancient stele.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The **Tyrant Dragon** (暴龙) is one of the legendary "king lineage" dragons, said to control wind and lightning and soar through the skies. Yet on Dragon Island, it behaves as a mere beast—a deliberate suppression of divine abilities that Xiao Chen attributes to the mysterious **Divine Stele** mentioned by Prince Feng. This concept of a heavenly seal neutralizing supernatural powers is a recurring motif in xianxia, where ancient formations or artifacts limit the capabilities of powerful beings. The **Little Celestial Horse** (小独角圣兽), also called the Little Unicorn, is a divine creature with a jade horn that absorbs moonlight. Its willingness to risk itself for Xiao Chen hints at a deep bond forming, despite its earlier timidity. Xiao Chen's healing method, drawing on **Wood Essence** (草木精气) from the forest's life force, is a classic cultivation technique: utilizing the vitality of nature to restore his body and spirit. His foundational art, the **Heavenly Stele Profound Art** derived from the Yellow River Divine Stele, enables this efficient absorption. The thirty-day recovery period establishes a countdown tension: Xiao Chen must remain hidden and heal completely before Zhao Lin'er's allies can track him down.

## Reading Guide

This chapter showcases Xiao Chen's resilience and the beginning of a meaningful bond with the Celestial Horse. Keep an eye on this foal's appearances—it may become one of Xiao Chen's most trusted companions in the wild. The mystery of the Divine Stele's suppression of ancient powers is introduced subtly; later chapters will expand on how this seal shapes the entire Dragon Island arc. Xiao Chen's thirty-day recovery countdown sets up a strategic pause before his inevitable counterattack against Zhao Lin'er and her faction. Readers should also note the contrast between the savage environment and moments of quiet introspection—this rhythm of violence and reflection is a hallmark of the novel's style. The tactical insight about the Tyrant Dragon's lost divine arts suggests that many legendary beasts on the island are mere shadows of their true selves, a fact that will influence Xiao Chen's approach to future threats.
