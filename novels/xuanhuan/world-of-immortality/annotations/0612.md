## Chapter Overview

The chapter plunges deeper into the Death World's ancient battlefield, a realm of perpetual gloom and lethal curses. Xiao Chen's group stumbles upon a small petrified beast that closely resembles Keke's Heaven-Defying Clan lineage, evoking a poignant response from Keke. However, the encounter proves to be a cunning trap: the beast is a decoy crafted from stone man remains, placed to lure them into a massive curse array controlled by a hatred‑charged resentment entity. The tension escalates as the entity unleashes hundreds of curses, but the mysterious stone jar absorbs them all, annihilating the entity. Afterward, they discover a withered root—possibly a fragment of the legendary Dream Flower—that greedily drains life essence from Paradise Lost without reviving. Pressing onward, they witness apocalyptic phenomena: blood‑colored lightning splitting the sky, blood rain, and fleeting visions of the Ninety‑Nine Stone Steps and multiple Heavenly Steles, hinting at the unspeakable horrors guarding the deepest forbidden zone.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen's group moves through a desolate battlefield littered with decaying ruins. They find a small petrified beast missing an arm, acting dazed and vulnerable. Keke, believing it might be a relative, is moved to tears, but Xiao Chen senses a trap and holds Keke back. Upon closer inspection, the beast is revealed to be a cleverly crafted false body made from the remnants of a stone man, lacking true vitality. As the group collects it, a resentment entity—formed from curses scattered across the ancient battlefield—activates a terrifying curse array, intending to destroy them. The curses manifest as tangible waves, but the stone jar, which has been resting quietly, suddenly flares up and absorbs all the intertwined curses, dissipating the entity entirely. This confirms the stone jar's nature as a "source of all evil" that devours curse energy. Later, Keke finds a half‑withered root belonging to the Dream Flower, a celestial treasure marked on the Ancestral Dragon skin map. When planted in Paradise Lost, it greedily absorbs a massive amount of spiritual essence from the garden's divine trees, yet shows no sign of revival, remaining dry and inert. Undeterred, Xiao Chen leads the group toward the source of a powerful Heavenly Stele aura. As they advance, the environment grows increasingly hostile: blood‑red lightning bolts hundreds of li thick split the sky, blood rain falls, and ethereal corpses float past. Most startlingly, Xiao Chen catches a glimpse of the Ninety‑Nine Stone Steps crossing the heavens, followed by several enormous Heavenly Steles—visions that suggest the ultimate secrets of the Death World are converging.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter introduces several profound cultivation concepts. The "resentment entity" (yuan ling) is a malevolent consciousness born from the lingering hatred of a slain supreme being; existing only as a mass of curses, it can endure for ages and manipulate supernatural phenomena. The stone jar, here called the "source of all evil" (wan e zhi yuan), demonstrates its ability to absorb and neutralize all forms of curse energy, making it an invaluable artifact against death‑attribute attacks. The "Dream Flower" is a legendary celestial treasure said to grant extraordinary enlightenment or rebirth; even its withered root retains the ability to absorb immense life force, hinting at its latent potential. The Ninety‑Nine Stone Steps are a pivotal element in the Stone Man path—the staircase a would‑be stone king must climb to take the final step. Their appearance alongside multiple Heavenly Steles indicates that the deepest part of the Death World serves as a convergence point for the most ancient forces in the cosmos, far exceeding the dangers of the outer regions.

## Reading Guide

This chapter functions as both a prelude to greater horrors and a deepening of the novel's core mysteries. The destruction of the resentment entity resolves a minor plot thread from earlier arcs, but the stone jar's true origins remain a tantalizing loose end. The Dream Flower root's stubborn absorption of life force suggests it will eventually revive, potentially yielding an incredibly rare treasure. However, the true focus falls on the apocalyptic visions of the Ninety‑Nine Stone Steps and the Heavenly Steles—these are directly tied to the Otherworld's highest powers and the ultimate fate of the Nine Provinces. Readers should pay close attention to any future references to these elements, as they signal that the Death World's innermost sanctum will soon become a central battlefield. The escalating danger also raises the question: who or what is controlling these visions? Is the Death World itself a living prison? The chapter primes us for a confrontation that could break the boundaries between worlds.
