## Chapter Overview

This monumental chapter shifts the narrative back to the mortal world, where the legendary Yellow River manifests its true identity as an Ancestral Dragon. The heavens and earth tremble as the dragon's colossal head rises from the East China Sea, stirring both mortal cultivators and immortal practitioners into a frenzy. Amid the cosmic spectacle, Xiao Chen returns to his ancestral village to uncover cryptic lore about a jade bracelet, then ventures to the sea where he reunites with a long-missing companion, Qingqing. The chapter balances epic world-altering events with intimate character moments, deepening the mythic scope of the novel while reaffirming personal bonds.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the stupendous revelation that the Yellow River itself is an Ancestral Dragon, a being of primordial power whose head has manifested in the East China Sea. The river of mud and water now surges through the sky, its roar echoing like a dragon's cry. Cultivators from both the mortal world and the Eternal Continent race to witness the event, drawn by the promise of ancestral dragon essence. Xiao Chen, however, takes a different path: he returns to his village to question the elders. They speak of ancient legends—that the river once coiled near the village, giving it the name 'Ancestral Dragon Village'—and mention a heirloom jade bracelet, though its significance remains unclear. After bidding his family farewell, Xiao Chen heads to the sea with his friend Chen Fang. There he sees the impossible: an enormous golden dragon head, as large as a mountain range, floating in the waves, alongside a divine stele that seems to suppress it. The stele, identical to the one from his childhood, now bears faded inscriptions, suggesting the seal is weakening. Xiao Chen senses a familiar energy and discovers Qingqing approaching through the mist. She reveals that she too was pulled into the mortal world by the spatial vortex and has been exploring the seas, even stirring trouble among the local cultivators. Their reunion is cut short when the dragon's soul bursts forth, sending thousands of cultivators charging into the deep sea to claim its power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of the 'Ancestral Dragon' (祖龙) carries heavy mythological weight in Chinese culture, often associated with the primordial dragon that embodies the very essence of dragonkind. In the novel, the Yellow River—a symbol of Chinese civilization—is reimagined as a sleeping Ancestral Dragon, its awakening tied to the removal of divine seals. The 'divine stele' (天碑) continues its role as a cosmic seal, echoing earlier appearances from Dragon Island and the Yellow River's history. The village elders' mention of a 'jade bracelet' (玉镯) invokes classic xianxia tropes where seemingly ordinary objects hold hidden power or clues to greater mysteries. Qingqing's remark about 'sects' and 'cultivation systems' reflects the diversity of paths in the World of Immortality, contrasting the martial arts of the Eternal Continent with the more scholarly Daoist traditions of the mortal world.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets the stage for a massive conflict over the Ancestral Dragon's soul, which will likely draw all major powers into a bloody scramble. Xiao Chen's reunion with Qingqing after several months of separate experiences is significant—she brings knowledge of the mortal world's cultivation networks and may become a key ally in navigating the coming chaos. The fading divine stele hints that the seal on the Ancestral Dragon is about to break completely, which could unleash a force that reshapes the world. Readers should also note the unresolved mystery of the jade bracelet: it may connect to Xiao Chen's family lineage or to the broader mythos of the Ancestral Dragon. With tens of thousands of cultivators converging on the East China Sea, the next chapter promises a war for a prize that could elevate any practitioner to demigod status.
