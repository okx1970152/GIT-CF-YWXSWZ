## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks the apex of Xiao Chen's bloody crusade and the beginning of his final farewell. Leading an unstoppable army of ancient celestial and underworld soldiers, he carves a path through the coalition of half-ancestor disciples, leaving battlefields soaked with blood and strewn with corpses. The world trembles before him—yet at the peak of his power, a deep premonition of death shadows every step. The chapter contrasts the epic, brutal spectacle of conquest with intimate, poignant moments of farewell. Zhao Lin'er's suspicious death and Xiao Chen's withdrawal from those he loves set the stage for a solitary confrontation with fate. The prose is both grandiose and elegiac, weaving the tragedy of a man who must become a monster to destroy monsters, knowing he will not survive the purification of the world.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen and his ancient soldiers rout the joint forces of half-ancestor disciples, slaughtering them for a full day and night until the coalition scatters. The celestial and underworld soldiers then return to the Dead City, leaving Xiao Chen alone to pursue his enemies. He fails to find Su Ying among the fallen, deepening his sense of loss. The chapter reveals the fundamental truth behind the conflict: the reversal of illusion and reality, where the false gods born from human hearts must be eliminated even if it costs Xiao Chen his life. Keke gleefully loots the defeated cultivators, amassing a mountain of magical artifacts, which Xiao Chen then sacrifices to feed the damaged Black Iron Seal and Golden Divine Halberd, using the Indestructible Imperial Heavenly Bell as a power source after shattering it multiple times. Confronting Zhao Lin'er in the imperial palace, he finds her dead under mysterious circumstances, though he suspects deception. Breaking through to the Seventh Heaven of Nirvana, he then systematically destroys all the religious shrines across the Nine Provinces, hunting down half-ancestor disciples with relentless fury. For three months, he is unchallenged—yet he knows his time is short. He leaves quietly, unable to bear the sorrow of farewell, carrying only the vow to 'send the illusory half-ancestors to their doom.'

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter delves into the philosophical core of the novel: the 'reversal of illusion and reality' (虚实逆转). Many beings revered as half-ancestors—such as the Buddhas, Daoist sages, and Western deities—are revealed to be constructs born from collective human faith, not true primordial entities. This concept draws on Buddhist and Daoist ideas of emptiness and the power of belief. The 'nine ancient lamps' that illuminate the Nine Provinces are connected to the realignment of the mortal world with the original, primeval reality. The sacrificial feeding of artifacts—destroying one magical weapon to empower another—is a recurring trope in xianxia, symbolizing the harsh calculus of cultivation where even treasures are consumed for survival. Xiao Chen's 'premonition of death' is a classic narrative device in tragic hero stories, often signaling the final sacrifice for a greater good. The celestial and underworld soldiers (天兵, 阴兵) are drawn from Chinese mythology, representing the armies of heaven and hell. Their appearance here as Xiao Chen's instruments of justice blurs the boundary between order and chaos.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets the stage for the final climax of the Mortal World arc. Pay close attention to the philosophical dialogue about illusion and reality—it recontextualizes every previous battle with half-ancestors as a war against false gods. Xiao Chen's decision to leave everyone behind, including Keke and Qingqing, signals that he expects to die in the coming confrontation. The mysterious circumstances surrounding Zhao Lin'er's death are worth noting; given her cunning nature, she may have faked her death to escape the coming purge. The replenishment of the Black Iron Seal and Golden Divine Halberd foreshadows that these artifacts will play a crucial role in the battles to come. The chapter builds tremendous tension: Xiao Chen has achieved near-total dominion, but the price is his isolation and awareness of his own mortality. His solitary departure, without any of his companions, evokes a tragic, mythic resonance—a lone hero walking toward certain annihilation. Readers should anticipate a final, decisive battle that will determine the fate of both the mortal world and the illusory realms.
