## Chapter Overview

The long-awaited reunion between Keke and her mother finally arrives in this chapter, but not in the way anyone expected. The mother, a stunning blue-haired woman of unfathomable power, is so deeply lethargic from ancient battle wounds that she can barely stay awake. Her comedic struggle to remain conscious provides a stark contrast to the gravity of what she reveals: two legendary gates—one called Regret, the other Hope—that can allow a single foray into the past. The chapter balances a warm, humorous family moment with the weight of cosmic power, as the old farmer with the Destruction War Spear is the first to step toward the Gate of Regret, driven by a battle that has haunted him for ages.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens as the mysterious gate behind Keke's jade box crumbles, revealing a seemingly empty void. Keke rushes in, followed by Xiao Chen and the old farmer. They find a surreal scene: a floating island with golden beach, blue sea, and a lazy woman who is Keke's mother. Keke's mother is incredibly powerful but perpetually sleepy due to injuries from fighting Otherworld Ancestral Gods. She repeatedly falls asleep mid-conversation, to the absurd frustration of everyone. Eventually, she reveals two gates: the Gate of Regret (回门) and the Gate of Hope (希望之门). These are incomplete treasures 'borrowed' from the Otherworld, capable of allowing a person to go back in time once to change a single personal regret. However, the power is limited—one cannot change major history without risking death. The old farmer, whose identity is hinted to be a legendary figure, immediately enters the Gate of Regret, shouting about an unfinished battle. Xiao Chen and others consider their own regrets. Keke's mother explains that the gates were set up by Keke's father as a final gift, but they are imperfect and dangerous. The chapter ends with several characters walking toward the gates, each carrying a burden of past failure.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of revisiting the past to correct regrets is a powerful trope in xianxia, but here it is carefully constrained. The author establishes that even Ancestral Gods cannot truly reverse history—time reversal in combat is limited to a brief moment, and changing the past would undo the present. The Gates of Regret and Hope are 'borrowed' (i.e., stolen) treasures from the Otherworld, implying that other universes possess even more potent laws. This touches on the novel's theme of 'illusion vs. reality'—if the past can be changed, what is real? The gates operate under strict rules: only one use, limited to personal change, and the user's father's restrictions protect them from fatal paradoxes. This is a fascinating middle ground between the wish-fulfillment of time travel and the hard consequences of altering fate.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets up a pivotal choice for every character. Which regret will Xiao Chen address? Will he try to save Qingqing or Ruoshui? The old farmer's past battle is intriguing—his urgency suggests he is a warrior from an age of lost gods. Pay attention to the subtle hints about Keke's father, who is never seen but clearly a being of immense power and cunning. The gates are not fully perfected, so any trip carries risk. The coming chapters will likely show flashbacks or alternate timelines, so keep track of each character's entry. Keke's mother, despite her laziness, is a deep well of knowledge—her offhand remarks about the Otherworld and the war are lore gold. Also, note that the gate of Hope is mentioned but not explored; it may allow a character to glimpse the future rather than the past.
