## Chapter Overview

This chapter presents a terrifying display of instinct-driven power as the female corpse—an ancient supreme being's preserved body—effortlessly destroys a stone-man-level guardian and his beast, then advances into the heart of the stone jar's inner world. Xiao Chen watches in awe as she shatters a giant clam that attempts to consume her, and then systematically demolishes cliffside palaces, each containing an imprisoned soul that she absorbs. The ethereal cries of 'out… kill out' and an ancient voice calling for release deepen the mystery, leading Xiao Chen to a chilling realization: the 'Source of All Evil' is not a being but a prison for ancient criminals, and the female corpse is being directed by the stone jar to devour these trapped souls. The chapter balances epic combat with lore revelation, setting the stage for the confrontation with the central prison's mysterious occupant.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the female corpse locked in battle with a strange winged guardian wielding a stone halberd and riding a stone beast. With a casual flick of her finger, she deflects the halberd and absorbs the beast's law-weaving attacks. The guardian's charge is rendered useless, and the female corpse, moving with preternatural grace, shatters the halberd with her bare hands, then tears apart the guardian's stone body and the beast alike, leaving them sinking into the sea. Xiao Chen is stunned—her power rivals that of a stone man king, yet she acts purely on instinct. She proceeds toward a large island, where the haunting voices grow louder. There, she encounters a fetid lake that is actually a giant rotten clam. The clam snaps shut, trapping her in its corrosive flesh, but she bursts it apart, absorbing its essence. Moving on, she enters a series of palaces built into a cliff. Each palace holds a faded soul shadow that lunges at her; she effortlessly destroys palace and soul, absorbing their energy. An ancient voice cries out, claiming to have been trapped for eons and demanding release, but the female corpse continues her methodical march. Xiao Chen deduces that the 'Source of All Evil' is a prison system within the stone jar, and the female corpse is being used to eliminate these prisoners. The clanking of chains from a massive central stone prison hints at an even greater captive within.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of 'Source of All Evil (万恶之源)' is reinterpreted here: rather than a personification of evil, it is a sealed prison realm within the stone jar—a microcosm that traps ancient criminals and forbidden beings. This aligns with the novel's recurring theme of 'illusory gods' and 'false half-ancestors' that were products of faith. The female corpse is a prime example of a 'preserved supreme being'—a body left behind by a peak cultivator who may have attempted the final step or was slain, yet whose flesh retains residual instinct and immense power. Such bodies are often sealed with 'decaying corpse techniques' (馈尸大法) to prevent them from causing harm or being resurrected. Her effortless destruction of a stone man-level guardian demonstrates that even a body without a soul can dominate lesser supreme beings. The mention of 'Three Sovereign Mirror and Five Emperor Pagoda (三皇镜与五帝塔)' as needed artifacts for passage suggests that the Source of All Evil is a controlled facility, requiring specific keys to access deeper layers. This chapter also evokes the xianxia trope of 'imprisoned ancient evils' and the peril of releasing them—a common motif in primordial cultivation stories.

## Reading Guide

This chapter significantly advances the 'Source of All Evil' arc by clarifying its nature as a prison rather than a single entity. Readers should pay close attention to the imprisoned souls: are they remnants of the Otherworld's enemies, fallen stone man kings, or something else? The ancient voice that speaks with more clarity may be a key figure—perhaps a sealed ally or a greater threat. The female corpse's role is ambiguous: she is being directed by the stone jar (魔影) to devour these prisoners, but does she have her own will? Her origin—whether she is a creation of the Little Stone Emperor's era or a survivor from an earlier civilization—remains a tantalizing mystery. Xiao Chen now possesses the Three Sovereign Mirror, which may be crucial for entering the central prison. The clanking chains in the distance promise a climactic confrontation with an entity that might be the true 'source of all evil.' This chapter sets the stage for a major revelation about the stone jar's purpose and the ancient wars that shaped the Death World.
