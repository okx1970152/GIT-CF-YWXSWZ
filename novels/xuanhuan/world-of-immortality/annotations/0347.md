## Chapter Overview

In this chapter, Xiao Chen continues his exploration of the Death World, now as a Blue Diamond Skeleton. His journey takes an unexpected turn when he encounters a young boy named Tianya, who claims to be a god from a village called Nüwa. Tianya is lost and naive, possessing superhuman speed but the innocent mind of a child. Xiao Chen decides to escort him home, leading them through structured territories of the Death World—from isolated castles to a city with patrolling angel skeletons. Upon reaching the lush oasis of Nüwa Village, Xiao Chen is welcomed by the village elder, who reveals that their ancestors were gods who achieved the reversal of death. The chapter ends with a stunning revelation: a treasure mirror showing scenes of the Nine Provinces, hinting at deep connections between the Death World and the mortal realm.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiao Chen, in his Blue Diamond Skeleton form, is approached by Tianya, a five- or six-year-old boy who inexplicably appeared at his side. Tianya says he is a god, over one hundred years old, but has only been awake for five.
- A battle between a Red Diamond Skeleton and a living corpse (a being with flesh and blood) erupts nearby; the living corpse is defeated and flees.
- Xiao Chen decides to help Tianya return home, learning that the village called Nüwa is far to the interior.
- They travel through the Death World, passing a castle where Xiao Chen fights and interrogates the master, learning the direction of Nüwa Village.
- They avoid a large city ruled by powerful fire-seed beings, observing organized society with patrolling angels.
- After journeying three thousand li, they reach an oasis—a green plain with life—where Nüwa Village stands.
- The village elder, Tianya’s grandmother, thanks Xiao Chen and orders a young man to harvest a Red Diamond-level fire seed as a reward.
- The elder explains that their ancestors were powerful beings who reversed death, becoming living gods. The deepest part of the continent holds unimaginable power.
- Nüwa herself is said to have revived in this world and taught their ancestors.
- The elder shows a treasure mirror that displays scenes of the Nine Provinces, stunning Xiao Chen and reinforcing the connection between worlds.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces the concept of "Fire Seeds" (火种) as the source of power for skeletal beings in the Death World. Fire seeds come in different qualities (e.g., blue diamond, red diamond), and absorbing higher-quality seeds allows evolution—a key mechanism for advancement in this realm. The existence of organized cities and social hierarchy among fire-seed beings mirrors human society, suggesting that even in death, civilization can emerge. The most crucial idea is the "reversal of death": powerful beings can transition from death back to life, becoming living gods with flesh and blood. This is embodied by the villagers of Nüwa Village, who are descendants of such beings. The name "Nüwa" directly references the Chinese goddess Nüwa, creator of humanity and mender of the sky. Her revival in the Death World implies that Ancestral Gods may exist across multiple realms. The treasure mirror showing the Nine Provinces bridges the Death World with the mortal realm, foreshadowing future inter-world conflicts or journeys.

## Reading Guide

Xiao Chen's exploration of the Death World deepens considerably. The interior of the continent is hinted to hold immense power and danger, with rumors of beings that rival gods. The encounter with Nüwa Village suggests that other legendary figures from Chinese myth may have revived here, potentially becoming allies or antagonists. Tianya, the child god, may become a recurring character due to his unique background and connection to Xiao Chen. The treasure mirror linking to the Nine Provinces raises questions: Can the Death World be used to access the mortal realm? What role will Xiao Chen play in this crossroads? This chapter expands the novel's cosmology beyond the Eternal Continent and Dragon Island, setting the stage for higher-level conflicts involving Ancestral Gods and the nature of life and death itself.
