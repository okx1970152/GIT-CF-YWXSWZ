## Chapter Overview

Xiao Chen confronts the past as the jade bracelet reveals Dragon Island and a lonely Keke. The chapter bridges mortal and immortal realms, blending nostalgia with ominous portents. The Yellow River's supernatural reversal signals cosmic shifts, while Tong Hu's cryptic warnings deepen the mystery of the sealing. Emotional stakes rise as Xiao Chen weighs his bonds to family against Keke's plight, and a new figure—a man resembling him—enters the Longevity World under a woman's piercing gaze. This chapter is a turning point: a somber farewell to old chapters and the rumble of something ancient awakening.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen uses the jade bracelet to scry Dragon Island, glimpsing Keke alone on the Cosmic Tree stump, crying and confused. Tong Hu reveals the bracelet is a key to the Nine Provinces seal, allowing passage to the Longevity World, but cannot be taken there. Xiao Chen attempts to force entry but is rebuffed by Tong Hu's ancient martial prowess, suffering injury. Tong Hu mentions a man resembling Xiao Chen recently entered the Longevity World, accompanied by a woman with terrifying eyes that match the portrait's eyes. This shocks Xiao Chen, implying someone from his lineage or a doppelgänger is involved. Later, a tremendous roar from the Yellow River shakes all Nine Provinces; the river's waters rise to the sky, flowing eastward above ground. In the ancestral dragon village, elders speak of the "Ancestral Dragon turning over," a sign of great change. A messenger arrives from the East Sea, reporting that the dragon head has materialized and a colossal divine stele has manifested, connecting to Xiao Chen's own Heavenly Stele inheritance.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The "Ancestral Dragon turning over" (祖龙翻身) draws on Chinese mythology where the Yellow River's course is tied to dragon veins and the fate of the land. The Yellow River is often linked to the Ancestral Dragon, a cosmic entity. The divine stele emerging at the East Sea echoes the earlier steles from the Yellow River and Dragon Island, part of the novel's "Heavenly Stele" lore. Tong Hu's statement that the Longevity World is "illusory" (梦幻空花) reflects a Buddhist/xianxia theme of reality as dream. The jade bracelet as a key to seals is a common trope. The appearance of a doppelgänger introduces doppelgänger lore, often tied to fate and ominous omens.
