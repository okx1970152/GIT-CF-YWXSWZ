title: "Chapter 1 Guide: The Ascension and the Accidental Immigrant"
---

The novel opens with a stark, philosophical question: "Who in this world can ever escape death?" This sets the tragic, desolate tone that defines the entire work. We are immediately grounded in a world where even the most beautiful and powerful—from peerless beauties to emperors—ultimately become dust. Yet, legends of immortals like Laozi, Zhuangzi, and Bodhidharma whisper that perhaps death is not absolute.

Then comes the miracle: on a full moon night at Kunlun's Red Dust Peak, the celestial maiden Lan Nuo prepares to break the void and ascend to the World of Immortality. Over a hundred thousand witnesses gather in hushed reverence. The scene is solemn, almost sacred—the moonlight, the silent crowd, Lan Nuo in white like a goddess. She hesitates twice before finally severing her mortal ties and stepping into the void.

But the chapter’s real hook is the desperate, blood-soaked chase unfolding simultaneously. Xiao Chen, our protagonist, is fleeing for his life from the vengeful Royal Celestial Maiden Zhao Lin'er and her elite pursuers. His path leads him straight to Red Dust Peak. In a shocking twist, as Lan Nuo breaks through the void, Xiao Chen and Zhao Lin'er are both swept along, accidentally entering the World of Immortality.

Xiao Chen’s reaction is not triumph but profound grief. He is torn from his family, his world, everything he loves. This is no glorious ascension for him—it is an exile. The stark contrast between the sacred purity of Lan Nuo's departure and the violent desperation of Xiao Chen's forced entry establishes the core tension of the novel: immortality is not a gift, but a burden.

This chapter masterfully combines mythic grandeur with personal tragedy, introducing a world where the price of eternity is the loss of everything human.
