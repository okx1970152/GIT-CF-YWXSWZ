## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a powerful set piece that reasserts Xiao Chen's mythic presence in the mortal world. The colossal stone statue of Xiao Chen, pierced by the legendary Twenty-Four War Swords, becomes a battlefield as cultivators seek the treasures within. The Little Defiant Dragon, now a fully grown dragon king, defends the statue with desperate courage. Xiao Chen's own spirit arrives as a crimson bone skeleton, witnessing the loyalty of his old companion. The chapter culminates in a jaw-dropping moment as the stone statue itself awakens, pulling a war sword from its chest and wreaking vengeance upon the aggressors. This is a chapter of reunion, sacrifice, and the rekindling of a legend.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with the stone statue dominating the sea, drawing a crowd of cultivators. The Twenty-Four War Swords are sensed as divine treasures, causing a chaotic scramble to break the statue. The Little Defiant Dragon stands alone against the mob, declaring that the statue represents Xiao Chen's sacrifice in sealing the spatial rift – the 'twenty-four swords piercing through the body to hold back the ancestor gods.' He fights the Nine-Headed Silver Serpent and three powerful Qi Refiners (Ye Jiuzhong et al.) simultaneously. Despite his formidable dragon techniques, he is overwhelmed. Suddenly, Xiao Chen's spirit appears as a red-bone skeleton, urging the Little Defiant Dragon to retreat. But before he can act further, he is teleported away. Just as the mob prepares to destroy the statue, it begins to move – an earthquake of cosmic significance. The statue pulls a stone sword from its chest, and with a single swing, kills many of the attackers. It gently lifts the wounded Little Defiant Dragon onto its shoulder, then continues its rampage, throwing the war sword to slay the two Qi Refiners beside Ye Jiuzhong. The chapter ends with the Nine-Headed Serpent and Ye Jiuzhong fleeing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Twenty-Four War Swords are legendary weapons of another world, mentioned earlier in the Paradise Lost arc. They were used by Xiao Chen to seal a spatial rift, allowing the half-ancestors to escape but trapping him in stone. The concept of a stone statue containing soul power is rooted in ancient myths of petrified heroes awaiting revival. The Little Defiant Dragon's 'Reverse Dragon Seven Steps' and dragon race divine powers are classic xianxia tropes for dragon beasts. The red-bone skeleton form of Xiao Chen's spirit is a unique state – a skeletal body that remains indestructible, hinting at his incomplete physical resurrection. The Nine-Headed Silver Serpent is a reference to the Hydra-like monsters in Chinese mythology, often representing chaos and evil.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a major turning point. The stone statue's awakening signals that Xiao Chen's power is not lost; his spirit is active, and the war swords still resonate with him. Readers should watch for the fate of the Little Defiant Dragon, now on the statue's shoulder. The battle is far from over – the Nine-Headed Serpent and Ye Jiuzhong escaped, and they will likely rally more forces. Moreover, the teleportation of Xiao Chen's spirit suggests another force is at play, possibly the Yellow-Clay Altar or the Evil King. The next chapters will likely follow the statue's rampage and Xiao Chen's efforts to regain full physical form. The tension between illusion and reality introduced by the Evil King remains unresolved, and this chapter may be a step toward breaking the illusion.
