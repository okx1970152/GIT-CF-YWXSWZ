## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a turning point as the Ancestral Dragon's soul, a colossal golden mountain of pure energy, plunges into the Eastern Sea, triggering titanic waves and worldwide tremors. The Heavenly Stele that once bore the inscription "Eternally Settle the Yellow River" suddenly loses those characters, leaving only "Eternally Settle," before rocketing into the sky. Xiao Chen, accompanied by the pure-hearted Qingqing, witnesses these cosmic events with awe and analytical curiosity. The stele's disappearance and the fragmentation of the dragon soul set the stage for a massive scramble among cultivators for the resulting treasures. However, Xiao Chen chooses a different path: instead of joining the bloody fight for the dragon pearl, he and Qingqing plan to raid the overseas immortal caves while their owners are distracted. The chapter also introduces a dangerous new rival, Deng Yu of the Shu Mountain sect, who tests Xiao Chen's strength.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the Ancestral Dragon soul—a golden mountain of pure energy—rushing into the deep sea, causing violent storms and lightning. Cultivators from all factions rush to claim it. Xiao Chen and Qingqing remain near the Heavenly Stele, observing the fading inscription. The "Yellow River" characters completely disappear, suggesting the ancient seal on the Yellow River has been lifted. Qingqing speculates that the stele may be suppressing the entire Nine Provinces, not just the river. Xiao Chen recalls seeing the stele's phantom in the Dead City on Dragon Island, hinting at a deeper connection. The Nine Provinces tremble, but beams of light from various locations stabilize the Yellow River. Then the Heavenly Stele itself flies into the sky. Xiao Chen follows, entering a state of unity with heaven and earth, and briefly glimpses a shattered world in the sky with the stele at its center. The stone man inside Xiao Chen reacts, and a stele diagram is imprinted on it. Returning to the sea, Xiao Chen learns from Qingqing about the fate of the half-ancestors in the great war against the Otherworld: the first wave died, but the second wave survived. The dragon soul then fragments into countless golden beams that sink into the ocean floor, puzzling everyone. Only one dragon pearl remains, sparking a bloody battle. Xiao Chen and Qingqing decide to exploit the chaos by raiding the immortal islands. They encounter Deng Yu, the strongest young talent of Shu Mountain, who tests Xiao Chen with a sword finger. Qingqing deflects it, and Deng Yu leaves. They then set their sights on the Shu Mountain island, with Qingqing suggesting they frame the Taiyin Sect.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of the Ancestral Dragon is central to Chinese mythology as the progenitor of all dragons, often associated with the Yellow River and the origins of civilization. Here, the Ancestral Dragon soul is a massive golden energy body that dissolves into the land, nourishing the spiritual veins of the overseas islands—a common trope in xianxia where legendary beings sacrifice themselves to empower the world. The Heavenly Stele, bearing the inscription "Eternally Settle the Yellow River," is a powerful cosmic artifact that seals something immense; its fading characters imply that the seal on the Nine Provinces is breaking. This ties into the novel's larger mystery of the Nine Provinces' imprisonment and the struggle for freedom. The battle among cultivators for the dragon pearl echoes classic jianghu treasure hunts, where greed leads to mutual destruction. The introduction of Shu Mountain and Taiyin Sect references actual Chinese mythological sects from folklore and later xianxia literature, grounding the story in familiar cultural touchstones.

## Reading Guide

This chapter advances several key threads: the nature of the Heavenly Stele and the Nine Provinces seal becomes more complex; Xiao Chen gains a new stele imprint on his stone man, which will likely unlock further powers; and the Ancestral Dragon soul's dissolution sets up future opportunities for spiritual cultivation. The appearance of Deng Yu from Shu Mountain signals that Xiao Chen's reputation is spreading, and he will need to contend with the younger generation of the orthodox sects. Xiao Chen's decision to raid the immortal caves rather than fight for immediate power shows his strategic cunning and long-term thinking. Readers should note the hints about the broken world in the sky—this likely ties into the larger cosmology and the true nature of the World of Immortality. The forthcoming confrontation with Shu Mountain and the Taiyin Sect's scapegoating promises intrigue and action.
