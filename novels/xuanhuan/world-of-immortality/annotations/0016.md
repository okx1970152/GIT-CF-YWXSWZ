## Chapter Overview
This chapter pivots from the violent standoff at the stone forest to a quiet but revelatory scene of recovery and discovery. Zhao Lin'er is snatched from the jaws of the four-armed blood-red ape by none other than the young celestial horse, cementing a bond that adds new dimensions to the island's conflicts. Xiao Chen, forced to retreat from the hostile apes, comes across a strange red crystal among the bones of a tyrant dragon. His tentative absorption of its energy leads to a startling revelation about his unusual cultivation path—acupoint divinization—and deepens the mystery of the island's latent power. The tone shifts from frantic survival to reflective observation, but the underlying tension of rivalries and unknown forces remains.

## Key Plot Points
Zhao Lin'er's life is saved by the young celestial horse, which bursts from the forest and knocks aside the massive four-armed blood-red ape with surprising strength. The horse then carries her away, easily outrunning the pursuing apes. This rescue confirms that Zhao Lin'er has formed a bond with the celestial horse during its two-day absence, a privilege Xiao Chen knows is reserved for pure maidens. The four-armed blood-red ape, after its failed attack, recovers by extracting a crystalline substance from its fallen kin and consuming it, visibly restoring its energy. This event sparks Xiao Chen's curiosity about such crystals. Later, while searching for a safer retreat, Xiao Chen finds a thumb-sized red crystal among the bones of a tyrant dragon's kill. By using his Heaven-Bestowed Qi-Refining Diagram, he absorbs the crystal's pure energy with surprising ease, though most of it disappears into his left foot's Shangqiu acupoint, where the silver light point from the dragon egg resides. He realizes that his cultivation follows a unique path of divinizing acupoints rather than merely refining the body. After moving to a new location, he hears a fierce battle among beasts and goes to investigate, finding a field of corpses from various large predators.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The young celestial horse (or unicorn) is a mythical creature deeply rooted in Chinese lore, often associated with purity and virtue. The text notes that only pure maidens can approach it, a trope common in xianxia where innocence or spiritual purity determines affinity with sacred beasts. The beast crystal (晶石) Xiao Chen discovers functions similarly to an inner core or dantian in many cultivation systems—a concentrated repository of life essence that can be absorbed to accelerate cultivation. Here, however, the absorption does not directly heal his injuries but instead nourishes his acupoints, highlighting the unique, non-standard nature of Xiao Chen's foundation. The concept of 'acupoint divinization' (穴道神化) is introduced: instead of simply gathering energy to break through realms, Xiao Chen's body treats each major acupoint as a vessel that can be 'divinely transformed' into a light point, granting superior power and vitality. This marks a departure from the standard realm progression detailed in earlier chapters.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets up several threads for future development. First, Zhao Lin'er's alliance with the celestial horse makes her a more formidable and mobile enemy—she now has a steed that can outrun almost anything on the island. Second, the discovery of beast crystals as a viable cultivation resource opens a new avenue for Xiao Chen to rapidly advance his acupoint divinization. The four-armed blood-red ape's consumption of a crystal suggests that even beasts use this method, hinting at a broader ecosystem of energy exchange. Third, the mysterious fight between multiple large beasts suggests a territorial conflict or a powerful predator that Xiao Chen may have to face. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen will balance the need for crystals against the dangers of hunting them, and how his unique cultivation path will evolve as he accumulates more of these energy stores.
