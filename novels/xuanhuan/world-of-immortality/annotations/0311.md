## Chapter Overview

This chapter is a meditative reprieve from the relentless battles of the recent arcs, as Xiao Chen returns to his ancestral village by the Yellow River and immerses himself in deep cultivation. The opening lines announce the emergence of Xing Tian, a primal war god, whose presence alongside Chi You shifts the cosmic balance. Yet the narrative quickly narrows to Xiao Chen's personal journey: he seeks not just power but understanding, integrating the martial imprint and divine arts into a unified path. The discovery of a mysterious yellow clay platform in his village—an object that resonates with ancient, primordial sounds—adds a layer of profound mystery, hinting at secrets that tie the mortal world to the very foundations of the cultivation realms. The chapter balances serene village life with the underlying tension of hidden threats, as three cultivators arrive seeking the village, only to be repelled by the old madman, Shanwaishan.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen achieves a major breakthrough in cultivation by harmonizing his martial body with the spatial divine principle, overcoming the destructive conflict that previously threatened to tear him apart. His training method shifts from raw power accumulation to a meditative state of 'sinking' and 'comprehending', where he experiences vivid mental recreations of past battles and a trance-like union with nature. This culminates in a spiritual vision that seems to transcend time and space, touching the very edge of the Dao. The arrival of three cultivators—a man of plain appearance but remarkable aura, and two female attendants—reveals that Xiao Chen's village is no ordinary place. Shanwaishan, the eccentric old madman, forcibly expels them, confirming that the village is an ancient flood village with ties to the Ancestral Dragon and the Nine Provinces seal. He then presents Xiao Chen with a yellow clay platform he unearthed nearby, an artifact so indestructible that even his power cannot damage it. Village elders, shaken, identify it from ancestral legend as a sinister object, but Xiao Chen keeps it and later, while in a deep meditative state, hears from it primordial sounds resembling but predating the Buddhist six-syllable mantra. Keke reappears with the two little girls Linglong and Tutu imprisoned in the Lost Paradise, a playful subplot. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen sitting on the platform, feeling as though he is perched above a demonic abyss.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter is rich with mythological references. Xing Tian and Chi You are two of the most fearsome war gods in Chinese mythology: Xing Tian, who was decapitated by the Yellow Emperor but continued to fight using his nipples as eyes and navel as mouth, and Chi You, a bronze-headed, iron-forearmed warrior often cast as a rebellious titan. Their reappearance signals a major upheaval. The six-syllable mantra (嗡嘛呢叭咪哞) is a core Buddhist invocation associated with Avalokiteshvara, here presented as a derivative of more ancient primordial sounds. This aligns with the novel's recurring theme that the World of Immortality's 'gods' are later constructs masking a deeper, more brutal history. The yellow clay platform (黄泥台) evokes the idea of a primitive altar or vessel for primal forces, a common motif in xianxia where crude, unrefined objects contain immense power. The concept of a 'flood village' (洪荒古村) ties into the primordial age (洪荒), suggesting the village has existed since the dawn of time, possibly as a nexus for the Nine Provinces seal and the Ancestral Dragon.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a quiet but crucial setup for future revelations. The yellow clay platform is clearly no ordinary relic; its ability to withstand Shanwaishan's power and emit the primordial sounds suggests it may be a key to the Heaven-Earth Furnace, the six Heavenly Steles, or even the Lost Paradise world. Xiao Chen's successful fusion of divine and martial paths marks a milestone in his cultivation—his earlier struggles with body-splitting are now overcome, positioning him for greater challenges. The three cultivators' interest in the village hints at a larger conspiracy, possibly tied to the National Religion or the Cultivation World, and their retreat is likely temporary. Readers should watch for the platform's role in upcoming conflicts, as well as the fate of the two little girls in Keke's prison. The reappearance of Xing Tian and Chi You promises an escalation in the half-ancestor war, and Xiao Chen's connection to both the village and the platform may place him at the center of that storm.
