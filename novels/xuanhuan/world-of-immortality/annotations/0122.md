## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks a pivotal departure from Dragon Island as Xiao Chen and his companions board the King Ship, guided by the demon. The atmosphere is bittersweet, with the little defiant dragon tearfully parting from the ten ancient dragons. Aboard the ship, the danger of the Forbidden Sea is underscored by a simple test: a coconut disintegrates instantly beyond the ship's protective aura. The real drama, however, unfolds in Xiao Chen's confrontation with Yan Qingcheng. In a tense negotiation, she proposes a false narrative of alliance to escape the Undying Sect's retribution for her uncle's death. Xiao Chen must decide between killing her or trusting a fragile, self-serving pact.

## Key Plot Points
- Keke confronts the ten dragon kings, arguing with them about the little defiant dragon's departure, and astonishingly wins them over—they show affection instead of hostility.
- The little defiant dragon says a tearful farewell to the ten dragons, revealing its deep emotional bond.
- Xiao Chen's group boards the King Ship using the demon's blood ritual and the ancestor's burial shroud for protection.
- The King Ship moves slowly across the Forbidden Sea, encountering resistance that suggests a conscious force opposing its passage.
- Xiao Chen tests Yan Qingcheng's loyalty by threatening to kill her now that they have escaped the island. She argues for a unified front, claiming their enmity can be spun as a ruse to mislead the Undying Sect regarding her uncle's death.
- Yan Qingcheng swears an oath and proposes to secure testimonies from Yizhen, Liu Mu, and others to corroborate their alliance story.
- Xiao Chen, outwardly unconvinced, allows her to live for now, understanding the political utility of a living witness.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The King Ship (君王船) is a legendary vessel that can traverse the Forbidden Sea, distinct from the Ancestral Dragon Ship summoned by nine dragon kings. It is summoned through blood sacrifice and requires a protective artifact like the ancestor's burial shroud to safeguard passengers. The Forbidden Sea itself is a realm of annihilation: any unprotected matter touching its golden waters is obliterated instantly, echoing the 'void that devours all' from Xianxia cosmology. The demon's warning to avoid the ship's cabin hints at ancient, sealed dangers that linger even on such vessels. This chapter also showcases the deep intelligence and emotional capacity of dragons, particularly the ten kings' affection for the little defiant dragon, a common trope in Chinese mythology where divine beasts are sentient and noble.

Watch for hints of the King Ship's mysteries—the demon's warning about the cabin suggests a future plot point. Additionally, Keke's influence over the dragon kings raises questions about her true origins and power. The slow pace of the crossing implies that dangers may arise before they reach land, testing the fragile truce between Xiao Chen and Yan Qingcheng.
