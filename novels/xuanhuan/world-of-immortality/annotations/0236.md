## Chapter Overview
This chapter offers a welcome shift in tone, blending dark fantasy gravitas with moments of lighthearted comedy and character reintroduction. Xiao Chen, still reeling from recent betrayals and losses, seeks respite in the mundane rhythms of a city tea house. His quiet contemplation is interrupted by an utterly unexpected figure: a stuttering, wildly unkempt assassin named Jin Sanyi, who claims to be a future killer-king. The chapter carefully balances the absurdity of Jin's speech and mannerisms with the serious weight of his proposal: to join forces and slay the demigod-level Tiger Servant. This tonal interplay is a hallmark of the novel, reminding readers that even in a world of immortal strife, humanity and humor persist.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen settles into a fleeting moment of peace at a street-side tea house, observing the ordinary life of the city. His solitude is shattered by the arrival of Jin Sanyi, a member of the legendary assassin organization 'Reward Three Billion.' Jin's extreme stutter and lecherous demeanor initially seem comedic, but beneath the surface lies a deadly purpose: he proposes a joint assassination of the Tiger Servant, the demigod who killed Keke. Before the plan can be fully discussed, a commotion draws them to a crossroads where Niu Ren, the bull-horned friend from Dragon Island, has erected a banner challenging 'Xiao Shishui'—a clear invitation to Xiao Chen. Niu Ren reveals that his bull horns have grown uncontrollably and he has come seeking a solution. The trio then decides to visit the opulent Sinks the Fish and Startles the Swan Palace, where Liu Ruyan is said to reside. On the way, they encounter Yan Qingcheng, Princess Yin Ying, and A Bing, leading to an awkward and hilarious exchange where Jin's stutter inadvertently creates scandalous nicknames.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The assassin organization 'Reward Three Billion' references a common xianxia trope where a bounty is placed on powerful beings, and the number symbolizes the astronomical reward. Jin Sanyi's name cleverly merges the word 'gold' with 'three billion,' marking his identity. The Sinks the Fish and Startles the Swan Palace is a pleasure palace whose name derives from classical Chinese idioms describing breathtaking beauty—'fish sink, swans fall'—now repurposed as a venue for elite entertainments. Niu Ren's bull horns are a physical manifestation of his beast-blood heritage, common in xianxia where bloodlines awaken and cause bodily transformations. The stuttering characterization of Jin is unusual for a deadly assassin, playing against type and adding a layer of unpredictability to his threat level.

## Reading Guide
This chapter serves as a breather before a likely storm. The plan to assassinate the Tiger Servant promises a high-stakes conflict that will require Xiao Chen to confront the demigod who killed Keke. Meanwhile, the reunion with Niu Ren and the mention of Liu Ruyan hint at a growing network of allies—or at least co-conspirators—gathering around Xiao Chen. Pay attention to Jin Sanyi's combat prowess, hinted at by his instantaneous vanish and invisible movement. The chapter also seeds future tension: Yan Qingcheng's brief but furious appearance suggests unresolved feelings and potential complications. Readers should prepare for a shift toward more direct action in the coming chapters, while enjoying the current blend of humor and camaraderie.
