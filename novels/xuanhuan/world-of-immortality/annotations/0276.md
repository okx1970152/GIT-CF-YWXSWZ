## Chapter Overview
This chapter marks a profound tonal shift from the cosmic battles of the Immortal World to the intimate tragedy of Xiao Chen's mortal homecoming. After eight years of separation, twenty-eight-year-old Xiao Chen returns to his village on the Yellow River—only to find his parents aged into white-haired figures, his boyhood friends dead from forced labor, and his homeland groaning under the oppressive heel of a newly risen National Religion. The narrative strips away the grand cultivation conflicts to focus on raw, aching humanity: the tears of a mother, the calloused hands of a father, and the silent graves of childhood companions. It is a chapter of grief, guilt, and the quiet kindling of righteous fury.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen is pulled back to the Mortal World through a spatial vortex. He immediately flies to his home village by the Yellow River, overwhelmed by nostalgia and pain. He encounters his elderly great-uncle and great-aunt, who reveal that his parents are forced to labor on a mysterious construction project—either the Ancestral Dragon Platform or the Tongtian Death Bridge—along with nearly every able-bodied villager. The National Religion, a newly dominant sect, has conscripted all adults from fifty to children into this deadly work, causing numerous deaths. Xiao Chen rushes to his home and finds it meticulously kept by his mother, filled with tokens of her longing—his childhood wooden swords by their bedside. At sunset, he reunites with his weary parents and childhood friends, learning that three of his closest friends have died in the labor camps. His father confesses that without the help of the younger generation, they would have perished. In the quiet of the night, his parents reveal that Ruoshui, the woman he loved, waited for him for seven years before disappearing last year, leaving a fortune behind. This triggers a complex grief in Xiao Chen—gratitude for her devotion, guilt for his absence, and sorrow for what might have been. The chapter closes with Xiao Chen vowing to free his village, but recognizing the need for careful planning to protect those he loves.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The chapter introduces the concept of a 'National Religion' (国教) in the Mortal World—a state-backed cult that has rapidly risen to power, wielding both political and supernatural influence. In xianxia tradition, such organizations often serve as local antagonists, contrasting with the transcendent powers of the Immortal World. The forced construction of the Ancestral Dragon Platform or Tongtian Death Bridge echoes ancient Chinese mythology where colossal projects were built by corvée labor, often linked to imperial ambition or supernatural sealing. The Yellow River, deeply symbolic in Chinese culture as the cradle of civilization, carries ancestral weight here: it is both Xiao Chen's homeland and a site of exploitation. Xiao Chen's cultivation at the Sky-Mastery realm (御空, demigod) grants him true flight and greatly extended lifespan, yet he must exercise restraint because his family and neighbors remain mortals vulnerable to retaliation. This creates a poignant gap between his personal power and his ability to protect. The eight-year separation also grounds the cultivation timeline in real human cost—parents age, loves fade, and childhood friends die while the cultivator journeys through higher realms.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a crucial emotional reset for Xiao Chen after the relentless high-stakes battles of the Immortal World. It reconnects him to his mortal roots and provides a personal motivation for his future actions. Pay attention to the simmering anger beneath Xiao Chen's controlled exterior—the National Religion's cruelty will not go unanswered. The mention of Ruoshui is a poignant thread; her disappearance may have deeper implications tied to the cultivation world. The mysterious construction projects (Ancestral Dragon Platform / Tongtian Death Bridge) hint at a larger scheme that may connect to the legends of Dragon Island or the Heavenly Steles. Readers should note the contrast between Xiao Chen's overwhelming power and the mundane tyranny he faces—a reminder that the greatest conflicts in xianxia often begin with the suffering of ordinary people. The chapter sets up a new arc where Xiao Chen must balance his cultivation ambitions with his duties to his family and home, a tension that will define his next steps in the Mortal World.
