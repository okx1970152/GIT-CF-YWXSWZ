## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a high-stakes chase sequence that sees Xiao Chen seize the legendary Three Sovereign Mirror and become the target of nearly every Stone Man King in the Celestial Realm. The tone is one of breathless desperation and strategic cunning, as Xiao Chen must use every tool at his disposal—the ancient scroll, the divine diagram, the king-tier divine city, and Keke's support—to survive the combined pursuit of nine ancient kings. The chapter balances awe-inspiring displays of power with Xiao Chen's narrow escapes, culminating in a daring plan to flee to the Otherworld and use the Ninety-Nine Stone Steps as a battleground where even kings fear to tread.

## Key Plot Points
As the seven great tombs of the Little Stone Emperor collapse, the kings scramble to claim the remaining treasures. Xiao Chen, still carrying the Three Sovereign Mirror, becomes the primary target. The mirror sticks to a Heavenly Stele, making it difficult to detach, and Keke struggles to pry it free. Meanwhile, Stone Crow, Lu Zhan, and others seize tombs and treasures. Xiao Chen uses the ancient scroll and the five king imprints to block a devastating mental attack from a Stone Man King, but is wounded by the shockwave. Realizing he cannot outrun nine kings alone, he turns the Three Sovereign Mirror against Lu Zhan, reflecting a claw strike and wounding the king. However, the mirror drains immense divine power; Keke and others pour their strength into it to sustain its use. Old Skeleton and Stone Crow reveal they cannot openly help, as other hidden kings watch. Cornered, Xiao Chen decides not to abandon the mirror but instead to flee the Celestial Realm entirely. He breaks through world barriers, seeking the Otherworld's Ninety-Nine Stone Steps, where he hopes the kings' fear of that cursed place will give him an edge. Lu Zhan sends a warning ahead to the Otherworld, but Xiao Chen presses on, determined to use the stone steps as a stage for the final confrontation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The Three Sovereign Mirror (三皇镜) is described as the third-greatest war treasure in all the heavens, a stone artifact of immense power. Its ability to reflect attacks is tied to the three faint figures carved on its reverse, suggesting a link to ancient sovereigns or the Three Sovereigns of Chinese mythology (Fuxi, Shennong, and the Yellow Emperor). The mirror's activation requires vast divine essence—Xiao Chen loses a third of his power just holding it—and it can only deliver a few full blows before needing recharging. This reflects a common xianxia trope: supreme artifacts are as much a burden as a boon. The Ninety-Nine Stone Steps (九十九重石台阶) are a recurring forbidden zone in the novel, associated with the Otherworld's deepest secrets and taboo to even Stone Man Kings. Xiao Chen's plan to lead the kings there leverages the superstition and fear tied to that location, a classic gambit of using a dangerous environment as a weapon.

## Reading Guide
Readers should pay close attention to the tactical details of Xiao Chen's escape—each use of the mirror, the scroll, and the divine diagram depletes resources, and the tension is palpable. The hidden kings that Old Skeleton mentions hint that the Celestial Realm's power structure is even more layered than shown. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen crossing into the Otherworld; the coming confrontation at the stone steps promises to be a turning point, possibly revealing more about the Little Stone Emperor's legacy or the mysterious origins of the Heavenly Steles. Keke's role also evolves, as she actively channels power and strategizes with Xiao Chen, showing growth beyond mere comic relief.
