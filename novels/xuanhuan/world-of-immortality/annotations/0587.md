## Chapter Overview

This chapter delivers the long-awaited cataclysm: the Ninety-Nine Stone Steps, the supreme anchor of the Otherworld's authority, are shattered. For eons, these steps stood as an unassailable barrier, allowing the three Otherworld Stone Man Kings to revive and dominate. Here, the combined forces of the Nine Provinces—Xiao Chen merged with the fragmented Stone Man, Keke's father, Ge Qian, Ke Zu, and the ancient turtle—face absolute annihilation. Just as the kings prepare to end the rebellion, a transcendent power arrives: Pan Gu, the primordial ancestor, drives his divine palace into the steps, cleaving them apart with his stone axe. The Eternal Unknown collapses, and for the first time, the celestial realm's giants glimpse the forbidden secrets beyond. The chapter is a thunderous turning point, a moment when desperate sacrifice and primordial legend converge to break an ancient seal.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen, now fused with the fragmented Stone Man and bearing the divine diagram and ancient scroll, desperately engages the Stone Man King Lu Zhan. Despite wielding the Five Great Dao Imprints and the stone turtle's integration, he is overpowered. Lu Zhan alone is a peerless martial being; his finger shatters the Annihilation War Spear, and he repeatedly evades the divine diagram. The disparity is crushing: not a true king, Xiao Chen cannot channel the war treasures' full force. Meanwhile, Keke's father and Ge Qian combine their newly realized divine laws, but they too are broken by the second king. Ke Zu is blasted bloodily aside. The steles and ancient lamps cannot turn the tide. Lu Zhan coldly declares that ants cannot wield divine weapons. At the brink, the Ninety-Nine Stone Steps tremble. A colossal divine palace pierces through world barriers, bearing Pan Gu himself. His stone axe simultaneously strikes at all three kings, and the palace crashes into the steps, splintering them. The Eternal Unknown—the hidden realm of the supreme ancestral gods—crumbles, exposing its heart to the heavens. The celestial realm's giants recoil in awe. This chapter marks the end of an era; the ultimate seal is broken, but at vast cost to the Nine Provinces' champions.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The Ninety-Nine Stone Steps are a recurring symbol of transcendent authority in Chinese mythological cultivation lore—a stairway to the ultimate realm, guarded by supreme beings. Here, they represent the Otherworld's grip on reality, their destruction a shattering of cosmic order. Pan Gu, the primordial creator in Chinese myth, is reimagined as a peerless Stone Man warrior who sacrificed his own chance at divinity to seal the Otherworld. His divine axe, a direct echo of the creation myth, becomes a weapon of ending rather than beginning. The chapter also crystallizes the concept of 'Dao Imprints': the residual will of fallen kings that can empower a successor. Xiao Chen's five imprints—from the Ancestral Gods of the Nine Provinces—are not techniques but the embodied essence of their martial paths. That they cannot overcome Lu Zhan alone underscores that true kingly power must be self-earned, not borrowed. The ‘Ancient War Triple Seal’ and ‘Soul-Slaying Spear’ techniques evoke the martial sophistication of a bygone age, now turned against the Otherworld.

## Reading Guide

The destruction of the Ninety-Nine Stone Steps is a major inflection point. Readers should watch how the collapse affects the broader power balance: the three kings are wounded but not dead, and the Eternal Unknown's exposure may draw new players. Xiao Chen has yet to achieve true Stone Man status; his path remains one of cultivation through crisis. The appearance of Pan Gu suggests that other legendary hidden figures may yet emerge. The chapter also leaves the fates of Keke's father, Ge Qian, and Ke Zu grievously injured. Their survival and recovery will be critical. The scattered fragments of the steps may themselves become treasures or tools. Moreover, the celestial realm's giants—Stone Corpse, Lotus King, Lishi Beast—now have a direct window into the conflict; expect their interference to escalate. This is not a victory, but an opening. The war for the Nine Provinces has entered a new, more direct phase.
