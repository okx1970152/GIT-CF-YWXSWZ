## Chapter Overview
This chapter shifts from direct combat to exploration and mystery, focusing on the Sacred Mountain that has been hidden in plain sight on Dragon Island. Xiao Chen, having completed his seclusion, ventures into the dragon-haunted depths and makes a staggering discovery: a colossal, time-worn Sacred Mountain that only appears from certain vantages. The presence of three king-tier dragons—the Tyrant Dragon, the Lion King Dragon, and the Sword Dragon—coexisting calmly at its base suggests forces far greater than mere territoriality are at play. When the Lion King Dragon lets out a moon-defying roar and charges into the mountain, it vanishes without a trace, leaving Xiao Chen and the reader with a chilling sense of the unknown. The tone is one of awe, tension, and mounting dread, as the island’s ancient secrets begin to surface.

## Key Plot Points
- Xiao Chen observes multiple apex dragons—Tyrant, Lion King, and Sword Dragons—gathered peacefully near the foot of a mysterious Sacred Mountain, an unprecedented sight that defies the brutal nature of these beasts.
- Keke tries to point at the sky, but Xiao Chen initially sees nothing; only after repositioning does he spot the mountain, revealing that it is hidden behind an ancient divine formation that only allows sight from specific angles.
- The Sacred Mountain is described in vivid, desolate terms: no plants or animals, only shattered, majestic palaces that exude an atmosphere of ancient decay and death.
- Xiao Chen deduces that the mountain’s concealment is due to a primordial divine formation, and that all the fierce beasts are there because of the mountain’s influence.
- After nightfall, the beasts disperse to their lairs, and Xiao Chen decides to approach the mountain under moonlight.
- Suddenly, the Lion King Dragon roars with golden radiance and charges up the mountain, then vanishes completely—as if erased from existence—leaving an eerie, dead silence.
- The disappearance of a fifty-meter dragon king underscores the mountain’s otherworldly danger and deepens the mystery of Dragon Island.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
In Chinese mythology, sacred mountains (圣山) often serve as dwelling places for immortals or as seal points for ancient powers. The concept of a mountain being hidden by a divine formation—visible only from select locations—echoes the trope of "immortal's cave" or "hidden realm" commonly found in Xianxia lore. Such formations are typically set by supreme beings to conceal treasures, prisons, or knowledge. The fact that even apex dragon kings cannot resist the mountain's call suggests that its origin predates the current era of Dragon Island’s seal. The dead silence and ruined palaces hint at a violent history, possibly linked to the ‘Divine Stele’ that suppressed the dragons. The snapping of the Heaven-Reaching Divine Tree (通天神木) noted earlier in the chapter is also a classic symbol of a fallen age—a world-tree that once connected realms now broken, foreshadowing the cosmological scales at play.

## Reading Guide
This chapter sets the stage for one of the most pivotal mysteries on Dragon Island. Readers should pay close attention to the Sacred Mountain’s nature: it is not merely a location but a narrative device that will likely unlock deeper truths about the island’s purpose, the disappeared gods, and the lost civilization of the Nine Provinces. The instantaneous disappearance of the Lion King Dragon is a major hook—did it enter another realm, die, or trigger something? Xiao Chen’s decision to approach despite the danger shows his growing hunger for answers. The presence of three dragon kings in proximity also suggests that the mountain may be the key to the island’s seal or the Ancestral Dragon’s legacy. Coupled with Keke’s unusual sensitivity to the hidden mountain, the coming chapters promise a revelation of cosmic importance.
