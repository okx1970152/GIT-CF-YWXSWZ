## Chapter Overview

After the blood-soaked massacre on the snowy peak, Xiao Chen's reputation as a ruthless killer is cemented. But his victory is short-lived. Almost immediately, the genius illusionist spiritist Kailuo unleashes a torrent of near-real attacks, blurring the line between reality and fantasy. Yan Qingcheng, the beautiful heir of the Undying Evil King, joins the fray with her graceful but deadly Immortal Devil Art. And beneath it all lurks the legendary Little Li Flying Dagger, its unseen bearer waiting for the perfect moment. This chapter shifts from raw brutality to psychological warfare, forcing Xiao Chen to rely on his instincts and tactical wisdom rather than mere strength.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the aftermath of Xiao Chen's one-man massacre: the snowy peak is drenched in blood, and the surviving crowd watches in stunned silence. But the tension immediately reignites as a storm of energy blades descends upon Xiao Chen—the opening salvo of Kailuo's illusion assault. Xiao Chen recognizes the attacker instantly: Kailuo, the genius illusionist spiritist of the Sixth Heaven of Mortal-Shedding. Yan Qingcheng then reveals herself, her purple light-wings carrying her into the air as she unleashes the Undying Demon Art. Despite being outnumbered and facing a sixth-heaven illusionist, Xiao Chen remains calm. He analyzes the battlefield, noting that Kailuo's illusions mix real attacks with false ones. After a prolonged exchange, Xiao Chen detects a subtle flaw—a heartbeat—and charges forward to break through the illusion. But at the critical moment, a blinding point of light streaks toward him: the Little Li Flying Dagger. It shatters his divine sword instantly, forcing Xiao Chen to make the strategic decision to retreat and fight his enemies separately. The chapter ends with the chilling implication that Wang Tong, the flying dagger's holder, has already claimed a victory of intimidation.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The "Little Li Flying Dagger" is one of the most famous weapons in Chinese wuxia literature, originating from Gu Long's novel "The Legendary Swordsman" (小李飞刀). In the World of Immortality, its legend has been elevated to myth: its first wielder, Li Xunhuan, supposedly slew a god upon ascending. The technique is passed down through bloodlines, making Wang Tong a direct inheritor of this deadly art. Also notable is the "Genius Illusionist Spiritist" Kailuo. Spiritists are rare cultivators born with innate spiritual powers; illusionists are a specialized subset who manipulate sensory perception. Unlike spells that require incantations, illusionists can create environments that feel utterly real. Finally, Yan Qingcheng's "Undying Demon Art" is a signature technique of the Undying Evil King lineage, a cultivation path that blends demonic ferocity with refined elegance—fitting for a character who is both beautiful and lethal.

## Reading Guide

This chapter sets the stage for a major confrontation arc. Xiao Chen's tactical retreat is not a sign of weakness but a calculated move to divide his enemies. In future chapters, expect him to pick off Kailuo, Yan Qingcheng, and Wang Tong one by one, likely using the environment and his growing powers. The introduction of the Little Li Flying Dagger raises the stakes significantly: no one in the Mortal-Shedding realm is safe from a single, perfectly aimed strike. Readers should pay attention to how Xiao Chen might counter such an attack—perhaps through his sealing divinity or the mysterious dragon shadows. Additionally, Kailuo's illusion arts will require a different kind of solution, possibly involving his heightened senses or the skeletons' perception. The coalition against Xiao Chen is formidable, but his survival instincts have never been sharper.
