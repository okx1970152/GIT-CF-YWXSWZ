## Chapter Overview

The concluding chapter of *World of Immortality* brings the epic saga to a close with a cosmic denouement that redefines the entire conflict. After countless cycles of war, sacrifice, and hidden machinations, the true nature of the struggle is revealed: the ancient sages—Pangu, Nüwa, Suiren, Fuxi, Shennong, the Yellow Emperor, and the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors—laid a grand plan spanning eons to lure all would‑be destroyers into a trap. The arrival of the Five Heroes of Chaos (Luangu Wuxiong) presents a seemingly invincible threat, but their victory is hollowed by the revelation that they have been walking into a sealed cage. The chapter is a masterful blend of tragic heroism and philosophical resolution, finally answering the question that has haunted the narrative: can a world of peace ever be built upon the bones of its ancestors?

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with the Five Heroes of Chaos shattering the last defenses and decimating the incarnations of Pangu, Nüwa, and the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors, whose bodies have long been decaying. Yet, as the Five Heroes attempt to enter the Primordial Gate to seize control of the Sole True Realm, a massive hand appears—the combined will of all the ancestors—and seals the gate, trapping the Five Heroes and the strongest emperors from the Sole True Realm inside. The ancestors reveal their millennia‑old deception: they engineered the very concept of a ‘Sole True Realm’ to draw all destructive emperors into one space, then planned to use the power of all worlds to refine that realm, eliminating the threat of emperor‑level cultivators permanently. In a blaze of sacred fire, Pangu, Nüwa, and all the ancestral gods sacrifice their remaining essence to ignite the refining process. Though the gate strains as the trapped emperors fight to break free, the countless heroes of the Nine Provinces—including historical figures like Sunzi, Zhang Sanfeng, and countless ancestors—sacrifice their bodies and souls to seal the gate with the power of blood and faith. The Three Sovereigns Mirror, the Five Emperor Tower, and the Nine Heavenly Steles all play their part. Finally, Xiao Chen wields the complete Forty‑Nine War Swords to annihilate the last three surviving emperors. The aftermath sees the merging of all worlds into a single realm, named the World of Immortality. Five thousand years later, the world is peaceful, but cultivation has declined so that no one can ever again become an emperor. The epilogue shows Xiao Chen, Keke, and a few old friends living in a restored village, with the song of the ancestors still echoing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter directly invokes several foundational Chinese myths: Pangu the creator, Nüwa who mended the sky, and the Three Sovereigns (Fuxi, Shennong, Suiren) and Five Emperors (Yellow Emperor, Zhuanxu, Ku, Yao, Shun). In the novel’s cosmology, these figures are not merely mythological but historical ancestors who sacrificed themselves to protect humanity. The concept of ‘using all worlds to refine a single true realm’ is a variation of the Daoist idea of refining chaos into order. The ‘Gate’ at the end of the Ninety‑Nine Stone Steps represents the boundary between the mortal realm and the divine. The final refinement process, fueled by the blood and souls of countless heroes, echoes the Chinese virtue of collective sacrifice for the greater good. The ‘Ancestral Song’ (祖神谣) recited by the characters is a poetic lament that reminds readers never to forget the deeds of the forefathers—a theme central to Chinese filial piety and ancestral worship.

## Reading Guide

This is the final chapter, so there is no next chapter, but readers should reflect on the entire journey. The chapter resolves all major threads: the fate of the ancestral gods, the destiny of Xiao Chen, and the ultimate question of whether a peaceful world can exist. The author, Chen Dong, uses the ending to deliver a poignant message about memory and forgetting: the new World of Immortality eventually forgets its founders, and later generations write disrespectful histories about the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors. This is a bitter irony that the protagonist must accept. Readers who have followed the story from the beginning will find the emotional payoff in the final reunion of Xiao Chen, Keke, Qingqing, and the three skeleton kings. The closing lines of the Ancestral Song serve as a perfect epitaph for the entire novel. For those interested in the novel’s themes, the ending suggests that true heroism lies not in personal glory but in ensuring future generations can live in peace—even if they forget the price that was paid.
