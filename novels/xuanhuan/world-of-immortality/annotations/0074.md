## Chapter Overview
After the eerie retreat from the Sacred Mountain, Chapter 74 pivots from the supernatural to the strategic. Xiao Chen, Keke, and the three skeletons settle by the Cosmic Tree stump, a brief respite from the island's terrors. But Xiao Chen's mind is already calculating the next move: the dragon eggs. The chapter crackles with tension as they navigate a night-time menace—a golden centipede—and then execute a daring daylight heist against some of the most powerful dragons on the island. The tone is a blend of cautious adventure and calculated risk, laced with the sly satisfaction of a successful burglary.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with a retreat from the inexplicable Sacred Mountain, a place so sinister that even Xiao Chen's curiosity bows to survival. A giant golden centipede, a ten-meter horror with venomous breath, forces them to flee to the relative safety of the Cosmic Tree's severed roots. Morning brings a reprieve: the island's beauty reasserts itself with birdsong and dew-kissed flowers. Xiao Chen, ever analytical, ignores the idyllic scene and turns to the problem of power. He decides to steal dragon eggs—specifically from the king-lineage nests that dot the nearby valleys. The plan is elegant in its simplicity: he and the three skeletons will each enter a different dragon's lair and take a single egg, leaving one behind to preserve the bloodline. The execution is swift and silent. Xiao Chen claims a golden Lion King Dragon egg, while King Qin Guang brings back a cyan Tyrant Dragon egg, King Yan Luo a jade Sword Dragon egg, and King of Reincarnation a purple Thunder Dragon egg. The four eggs gleam with otherworldly light, a treasure hoard that promises immense cultivation power.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Dragon eggs in the World of Immortality are not mere offspring; they are concentrated repositories of life essence, capable of directly boosting a cultivator's power. The eggs from king-lineage dragons—Lion King, Tyrant, Sword, and Thunder—are particularly potent, each containing the primal energy of a specific dragon bloodline. The Lion King Dragon, for instance, is a dominant predator whose egg radiates golden light, hinting at solar-like vitality. Xiao Chen's theory that such eggs might hatch into a Companion Dragon King underscores their value: a dragon born alongside an Ancestral Dragon, nearly as powerful. Yet the theft is not without moral dimension. Xiao Chen insists on taking only one egg per nest, a nod to the xianxia principle of leaving a path for life—a small mercy in a world of constant predation. The chapter also highlights the skeletons' growing sophistication; they are no longer mere undead but cunning thieves capable of navigating dragon territory.

## Reading Guide
As Xiao Chen and his skeletal crew admire their glittering prize, the reader's mind inevitably turns to the consequences. Dragon mothers are not forgiving, and these are king-lineage dragons—among the most dangerous creatures on the island. The stolen eggs thrum with power, but they also mark Xiao Chen as a target for every covetous cultivator and enraged dragon. How will he use them? The chapter sets up a crucial resource-gathering phase, but also a ticking clock. The next chapters may explore the cultivation breakthrough these eggs enable, or the wrath that follows. The careful balance of risk and reward is a recurring theme, and this heist raises the stakes for everyone on Dragon Island.
