## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks a pivotal escalation as the Paradise Lost world—a realm where all cultivation is nullified—finally opens after nine seals are broken. Ancient powers such as Tongtian, Laozi, Buddha, and the Sun Holy God vie for the legendary war swords that emerge from the space channel. Keke’s childish voice announces the breaking of each seal, culminating in the revelation of a world where even demigods become mortals. Xiao Chen, along with his companions, enters the Paradise Lost, only to be suddenly impaled by three war swords that drag him toward a colossal heavenly stele, leaving his fate in grave jeopardy. The chapter blends cosmic mythology with brutal physical consequence, raising the stakes to an unprecedented level.

## Key Plot Points

The reincarnation gate opens, drawing ancient beings into the underworld. Tongtian, Laozi, Buddha, and others enter a space channel that holds the Paradise Lost world. Keke’s voice announces the breaking of the fourth to ninth seals, each releasing powerful phenomena: a dragon king soul that disintegrates upon exit, and five massive war swords that the ancient powers immediately fight over. Tongtian uses his four immortal-slaying swords to secure one war sword; the others are captured by Laozi, Buddha, Primordial, Sun Wu, and the Sun Holy God. An otherworldly strongman protests but is ignored. After the ninth seal breaks, an overwhelming force drives even the greatest beings back. The Paradise Lost is revealed: a radiant, beautiful realm where all magical power is suppressed, leaving cultivators as mere mortals. Xiao Chen, Yizhen, and others enter, but immediately three war swords fly toward Xiao Chen with impossible speed. Unable to evade due to the loss of his powers, he is pierced through the chest and abdomen, blood spraying. The swords then drag him away, and he falls into a dying state. In his fading consciousness, he sees a towering heavenly stele and hears someone say, 'This is Paradise Lost, sealed by the heavenly stele.' The chapter ends with Xiao Chen's life hanging by a thread.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The concept of a realm where cultivation is suppressed—the Paradise Lost (失乐园)—is a classic xianxia trope, often used to level the playing field and force reliance on physical strength or mental resilience. The war swords (战剑) are an ancient set of weapons that predate even the great powers, hinted to be connected to Tongtian's immortal-slaying sword formation, a nod to the Chinese classic *Investiture of the Gods*. The heavenly stele (天碑) returns from earlier arcs (Dragon Island, the Yellow River), symbolizing cosmic order and sealing power. The reincarnation gate (轮回门) and the underworld (地狱) draw from Buddhist cosmology, while the intervention of figures like Laozi and Buddha weaves Daoist and Buddhist mythology into the narrative, creating a syncretic mythological tapestry.

## Reading Guide

This chapter leaves Xiao Chen in a life-threatening crisis, pierced by three war swords and being dragged toward an unknown destination. Readers should watch for the meaning of the Paradise Lost world—why would ancient powers covet it if their powers are suppressed? The presence of Keke, who has been breaking the seals from within, suggests she may hold the key to the realm's secrets. The war swords themselves are clearly objects of immense importance, linked to the immortal-slaying swords. How Xiao Chen survives (if he does) may involve the stone man fragments or the heavenly stele's power. The next chapters will likely reveal more about this new world and the fate of the young cultivators caught among ancient titans.
