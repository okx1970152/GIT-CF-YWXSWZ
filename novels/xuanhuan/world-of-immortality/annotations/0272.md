## Chapter Overview

This chapter catapults the reader into a surreal and desolate desert, where the boundaries between reality and illusion blur. Xiao Chen witnesses the emergence of three extraordinary children—a little monk, a little Daoist, and a child of killing intent—who are in truth the reincarnated avatars of the legendary Buddha, Primordial, and Tongtian. A haunting voice declares that this world is but a projection of a true reality, casting doubt on everything Xiao Chen holds true. What follows is a cataclysmic clash between these reborn half-ancestors and a fleet of golden chariots from the Otherworld, a battle that shakes the very heavens and heralds the return of long-vanished powers. The chapter is a masterclass in mythic scale and tragic beauty, fusing existential dread with explosive divine combat.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiao Chen explores the ancient desert and finds three structures: a temple, a Daoist temple, and a divine island. Three children, each barely three or four years old, emerge with eerily mature bearing.
- The little monk (Buddha), the little Daoist (Primordial), and the murderous child (Tongtian) walk into the desert, ignoring Xiao Chen entirely. They move at impossible speeds and vanish into a spatial rift.
- A vast voice declares the world is an illusion and will soon collapse. The children respond by launching their temples and island into the sky to strike the golden chariots of the Otherworld half-ancestors.
- The battle escalates: more children appear—Sun God, Satan, the three Western saints, Zhunti, the first palace master of the Demon Palace, the first matriarch of the Swordmere Palace, and the White Tiger Holy Emperor—all reincarnations of the half-ancestors Xiao Chen had previously beheaded.
- Xiao Chen's sacred artifacts fly away one by one to their original owners, leaving only the Seven-Colored Sacred Tree above him.
- A mysterious voice laments that this is a failed gambit: “Those who should have come did not; those who should not have come have arrived.”
- A terrifying presence from a spatial rift announces the arrival of an Otherworld ancestral god, but suddenly an equally formidable aura from the Eternal Continent emerges, shocking everyone.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter draws heavily on the “Fengshen Yanyi” (Investiture of the Gods) tradition, with Tongtian, Zhunti, and the four Immortal-Slaying Swords referenced explicitly. The reincarnation of beheaded half-ancestors as children reflects the xianxia trope of nirvanic rebirth after catastrophic defeat. The concept of “illusory world” is a profound philosophical theme in Chen Dong’s work, echoing Zhuangzi’s butterfly dream—a point reinforced by the appearance of Zhuang Zhou himself. The “Twenty-Four War Swords” are a recurring artifact from earlier arcs, now tied to the otherworldly invasion. The “failed gambit” suggests that the half-ancestors planned to lure their enemies into the illusory world but were betrayed or outmaneuvered. The sudden emergence of an ancestral god from the Eternal Continent hints at hidden powers that were previously thought extinct.

## Reading Guide

This chapter recontextualizes many earlier plot threads, especially the beheading of the half-ancestors—their rebirth as children suggests an ongoing cycle of conflict beyond the Dragon Island arc. Pay close attention to the identity of the mysterious voice that declares the world an illusion; it may be linked to the “real” world’s ancestral gods. The arrival of an ancestral god from the Eternal Continent raises the stakes entirely: if such beings still exist, the balance of power has shifted dramatically. Future chapters will likely explore this cataclysmic war between worlds. Readers should note which artifacts Xiao Chen has lost and what that means for his cultivation path. The involvement of Zhuangzi also hints at higher philosophical mysteries that may affect the ultimate nature of reality in the story.
