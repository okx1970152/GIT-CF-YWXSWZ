## Chapter Overview
This chapter is a masterful blend of brutal action and profound, aching emotion. Xiao Chen, now a half-god (Sky-Mastery realm) returned from the World of Immortality, unleashes his fury upon the foreign cultivators who have enslaved the people along the Yellow River. He cuts down multiple half-gods, destroys their grand constructions, and demonstrates the devastating power of his Space Divine Law and Eight Aspect Worlds. But the heart of the chapter lies elsewhere. After settling his blood debt and freeing a village, Xiao Chen embarks on a solitary pilgrimage to the abandoned mountain valley where his lost love Ruoshui once lived. There, he discovers a heart-rending inscription left by her years ago: 'If life were only as at first sight, why the autumn wind to fan the parting grief?' This simple line, echoing the classical poem, shatters his stoic armor and brings him to tears. The chapter reminds us that beneath the cold warrior lies a man haunted by love, memory, and the price of his immortal journey.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with Xiao Chen flying along the Yellow River, slaughtering cultivators without mercy. He kills three half-god practitioners with his Golden Divine Halberd, then demolishes their vast palace complex. When five more cultivators respond, he effortlessly shatters their flying swords with the halberd and beheads three younger ones with the lightning speed of his Eight Aspect Worlds. Two middle-aged half-gods are crushed by his Space Divine Law, their bodies and artifacts ground to dust. He continues upstream, killing several more half-gods, and learns that the foreign cultivators are building massive structures for an unknown purpose. He witnesses a confrontation between a blue-robed young cultivator who condemns the oppression and an older cultivator who defends it. Xiao Chen coldly executes the older man, then systematically burns four or five of the construction sites, sending a shockwave through the mortal world's power structure. The Imperial Religion mobilizes, but Xiao Chen vanishes. He visits the village of Su Ying, a woman from his past, but does not reveal himself—only ensures her village is freed from labor. The chapter's true climax occurs when he travels to a valley in the Emei Mountains where Ruoshui once stayed. The hut is abandoned and dusty. On a table, he finds her inscription: '人生若只如初见何事秋风悲画扇' (If life were only as at first sight, why the autumn wind to fan the parting grief?). He reads it aloud, his voice choking with sorrow.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The inscription is from a famous poem by Nalan Xingde, a Qing Dynasty poet. It mourns the loss of a relationship's initial perfect beauty, contrasting it with the inevitable decline of love over time. In Chinese culture, leaving a poetic message (题字) at a place of significance is a way to immortalize emotion. For Ruoshui, this is her final farewell to Xiao Chen. The chapter also highlights the unique powers of the foreign cultivators: they can forge 'Spatial Thrust Swords' (穿空剑) designed to counter space-based divine abilities, showing that the cultivation worlds are locked in an arms race. Xiao Chen's Sky-Mastery (half-god) cultivation is so far above most mortal practitioners that he can effortlessly kill multiple half-gods, but the Imperial Religion's deeper reserves remain unseen. The mention of a 'blue-robed youth' who opposes the enslavement indicates that not all cultivators are corrupt, setting up potential allies in the struggle.

## Reading Guide
This chapter deepens Xiao Chen's emotional core and sets his motivations for the next arc. His grief for Ruoshui is now confirmed—she is lost to him, likely married or dead, and the inscription suggests she understood the tragedy of their separation. This sorrow may fuel either a desperate search for her or a darker acceptance that pushes him toward recklessness. The Imperial Religion is now actively hunting him, and the scale of their construction projects hints at a grand, ominous plan. The blue-robed cultivator who opposes them may become a key figure. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen's personal grief influences his war strategy, and whether the mysterious mention of 'Ruoshui' will lead him into even greater dangers.
