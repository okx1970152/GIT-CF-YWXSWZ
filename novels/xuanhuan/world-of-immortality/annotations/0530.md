## Chapter Overview
In this pivotal chapter, Xiao Chen harnesses the power of the holy sacrifice ritual to elevate his physical form to the War Ancestor realm, achieving a body nearly indestructible to gods. Simultaneously, the seventh heavenly stele imparts profound truths, linking his marks across the six previous steles. Yet the underworld is not without its perils: a terrifying beast from the black lake attacks, testing Xiao Chen's newfound might. Amidst the revelations, the ancient stone turtle—revealed to be a being from a forgotten age—drops enigmatic hints about the true nature of the heavenly steles, hinting at a primordial origin far beyond mortal understanding. This chapter blends intense self-transformation with dark, mythic world-building.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with Xiao Chen immersed in the holy sacrifice, using the flesh of a black giant to refine his own body. The seventh heavenly stele shines brightly, and ancient diagrams imprint into his divine source. His physical shell is transformed to the War Ancestor level, granting him peerless physical power but leaving his spiritual cultivation still three steps below. He gains a crucial insight: the first heavenly mark now functions as a miniature stele in his hand. Meanwhile, a monstrous creature from the black lake attacks, its horn unleashing chaotic light that pierces even the six steles. Xiao Chen defends with the stone skull and his miniature stele, managing to break the horn's tip. After the battle, the old stone turtle—formerly thought to be just a tortoise—reveals itself as a true stone being from eons past. It tells a cryptic tale: once a turtle kept a man, and then tells that the heavenly steles 'grew.' This revelation recasts the steles as organic, world-born artifacts rather than crafted weapons, hinting at a deeper connection to the fabric of the cosmos.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
The holy sacrifice (圣祭) is a rare and dangerous cultivation method that allows one to extract the essence of a powerful corpse to strengthen oneself. The War Ancestor (战祖) stage is distinct from a true Ancestral God: the body reaches perfection, but the soul and understanding of cosmic principles lag behind. In Chinese mythology, turtles are symbols of longevity and wisdom; the stone turtle here is a remnant from an ancient age, petrified yet conscious. The concept that the heavenly steles 'grew' rather than being forged suggests they are living artifacts, possibly born from the universe itself—a notion that aligns with Daoist ideas of natural, spontaneous creation. The black lake monster, with its chaos-attuned horn, represents the raw, untamed forces that still lurk in the forgotten corners of the world.

## Reading Guide
With Xiao Chen's body now at War Ancestor level, his next challenge is to elevate his spiritual cultivation to match. The stone turtle's cryptic remark about the steles having grown opens up a whole new layer of world-building: what else might be alive in this world? The black lake creature, though driven back, is a reminder that even a half-ancestor peak is not yet invincible. Readers should keep a mental note of the turtle's story about the man it kept—it may be a clue to the identity of the original creator or guardian of the steles. As Xiao Chen continues his journey through the underworld, expect more encounters with ancient beings and deeper secrets about the origins of cultivation itself.
