## Chapter Overview
This chapter pivots sharply from high-stakes combat to comedic social farce, as Xiao Chen finds himself ambushed in a hot spring by a cadre of Southern Wasteland beauties. The scene crackles with sharp banter and escalating absurdity, from Kanasi's brazen intrusion into the men's bath to the women's feigned assault on Keke as a pretext to embarrass Xiao Chen. Yet beneath the laughter, the chapter underscores Xiao Chen's unyielding audacity and his refusal to be cowed by social convention or royal politics. His ultimate response—standing naked to retrieve his gold notes—is both a practical measure and a deliberate shock tactic, reaffirming that he will not be shamed by anyone.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen finishes collecting debts from the sons of powerful families: seventy-nine thousand gold from Zhuge Kun and eighty-three thousand from Hoffman, plus IOUs from Li Dongbo and Luo Guao. He prepares to leave when Kanasi storms into the men's hot spring, followed by four Southern Wasteland beauties including Hai Yunxue and Yan Qingcheng. They use the pretense of reclaiming Keke as a stray beast to attack Xiao Chen, while their real aim is to punish him for his arrogance. When the women grab his clothes—which hold the gold notes and IOUs—Xiao Chen rises from the water completely naked, shocking everyone and causing the women to flee. He retrieves his garments and later dons a bathrobe, but the psychological blow to the pride of the assembled nobles is complete. The chapter also reveals that the women are connected to the defeated young masters: Hai Yunxue is Li Dongbo's cousin, and another beauty is related to Zhuge Kun. This interweaves family honor into the ongoing conflict, setting up future political complications.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
Hot springs are a common setting in xianxia for relaxation and political intrigue, often serving as neutral ground where alliances and grudges simmer beneath the surface. The practice of writing IOUs (欠条) is a recurring trope in Chinese web novels, representing a legally binding social contract even among cultivators—violating such a promise carries serious face consequences. The women's bold intrusion into the men's bath is deliberately provocative, intended to humiliate Xiao Chen by violating social norms; in xianxia culture, such boundary-breaking is both scandalous and a display of power. Xiao Chen's countermove—rising naked—is an extreme form of social defiance, using shamelessness to regain the upper hand. This reflects the xianxia theme that strength and audacity often override propriety.

## Reading Guide
This comedic interlude lightens the narrative after the intense battles with Lu Muda and the political tension of the prince's banquet. Pay close attention to the network of family connections revealed: Hai Yunxue, Kanasi, and the women are all tied to the young men Xiao Chen has humiliated, meaning this conflict is far from over. The chapter also subtly reinforces Xiao Chen's resourcefulness: his priority is securing the gold notes, not personal pride. The coming chapters are likely to escalate as these families retaliate—perhaps through the Beast Fighting Tournament or direct confrontation. The white-shelled turtle (Little Thief) and Keke's role remain threads to follow. Expect a shift back to martial action soon.
