## Chapter Overview

This chapter masterfully blends dark humor with mythic gravitas as Xiao Chen's group uncovers a stone coffin containing the body of a legendary fire crow, a primordial baleful bird of dire omen. The old skull's horrified recognition sets the tone: this creature is a true terror of the ancient world. Yet Xiao Chen's audacious threat to cook and eat the crow—and Keke's spirited cooperation—injects a startlingly comic element into the confrontation. The scene crackles with tension as the crow's spirit appears, furious and demanding retribution. But the encounter pivots toward negotiation when Xiao Chen displays his Divine Diagram and Heavenly Marks, forcing the crow to reconsider. The chapter closes on an uneasy alliance, with the crow borrowing the Divine Diagram for its failed cultivation, hinting at deeper secrets and setting the stage for future conflict or cooperation.

## Key Plot Points

1. The group discovers a three-foot stone coffin containing the body of a legendary fire crow, known across the ages as the 'baleful bird' that brings death in its wake. The old skull's alarm underscores the creature's fearsome reputation.
2. Xiao Chen decides to carry the coffin, provoking an icy dread that unnerves Sha Polang and signals the crow's lingering power despite its corpse-like state.
3. At the flame cave, the crow's spirit manifests, enraged by the theft. The old skull mediates, revealing the crow is the local demon tribe's ancient ancestor and has been slowly self-destructing due to a cultivation mishap.
4. Xiao Chen escalates by threatening to cook the crow, pulling out a Heavenly Stele as a 'brick' to smash the corpse. Keke playfully builds a fire, adding to the absurdity.
5. The crow's fury peaks, but Xiao Chen counters by displaying the Divine Diagram and Heavenly Marks, shocking the crow into a more conciliatory stance.
6. The crow demands to borrow the Divine Diagram, suggesting its power is far greater than commonly known. An uneasy truce is formed, with Xiao Chen agreeing to the loan.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The fire crow embodies the classic xianxia trope of the 'baleful bird' (丧门星), a creature whose very presence is an ill omen. Its stone body with sprouting feathers (石体生羽) illustrates a rare and perilous cultivation attempt: merging the Stone Man path with flesh-and-blood rebirth. This parallels the dangerous 'holy sacrifice' techniques seen earlier. The Divine Diagram (神图) and Heavenly Marks (天痕) are recurring mystical concepts, with the Diagram being an artifact of immense, layered significance—here recognized even by a primordial beast. The transactional nature of immortal alliances is on full display: the crow's demand for the Diagram is framed as a loan, reflecting the complex etiquette of power negotiation among ancient beings. The old skull's role as mediator highlights the value of ancient wisdom in resolving conflicts between titans.

## Reading Guide

This chapter introduces a major new element: the fire crow's interest in the Divine Diagram. Readers should note that the crow, though severely weakened, remains a fearsome entity whose full strength could be catastrophic if restored. The alliance is fragile; the crow's true intentions are unclear, and its request for the Diagram may have unforeseen consequences. The Heavenly Marks and Diagram are clearly central to many of the series' deepest mysteries. Pay attention to the old skull's reactions—he recognizes the crow and seems to know more about the ancient past than he lets on. Keke's playful participation in the threat shows her growing confidence and bond with Xiao Chen. The chapter brilliantly balances dark humor with tension, maintaining the novel's signature mythic dark fantasy style.
