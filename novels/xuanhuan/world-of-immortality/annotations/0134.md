## Chapter Overview

This chapter shifts the focus from the desolate, mythic struggles of Dragon Island to the bustling, mercantile heart of the Eternal Continent. Xiao Chen, having barely escaped the sealed island, encounters a rotund, silver-tongued merchant named Zhuge Liang—a name that carries its own cross-world literary weight. Through their lively exchange, readers are introduced to the continent's dominant subculture: beast-fighting (斗兽). The tone is lighter, laced with humor and the shrewd calculation of a born salesman, yet it never sheds the novel's underlying gravity. Zhuge Liang's enthusiasm for the Little Defiant Dragon and Keke hints at the immense value these creatures hold, while his revelations about the Dragon Island's previous openings and the secret dragon populations on the mainland expand the world's scope dramatically. The chapter balances earthy comedy with crucial lore, reminding us that power in the World of Immortality takes many forms—including gold and influence.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen’s group encounters Zhuge Liang, a portly merchant who immediately fixates on Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon. Despite a clumsy collision with the proud little dragon, Zhuge Liang remains undeterred, even excited by its strength. His servants’ dogs and ‘yellow-earth dragons’ (large lizard-like sub-dragons) are effortlessly defeated by the Little Defiant Dragon, confirming its extraordinary bloodline. Zhuge Liang, revealing himself as a professional beast-tamer from the prestigious Zhuge family, explains the massive beast-fighting industry on the Eternal Continent. He claims his family operates arenas in the Heavenly Emperor City, the southern mainland’s largest metropolis, and that top-tier fighting beasts command enormous prices and prestige. He reveals a stunning secret: previous Dragon Island openings, though rarer, have allowed captured young dragons to be brought to the mainland, where they grow and even breed. These dragons are held by colossal powers—imperial families, wealthy syndicates, and cultivation sects—and are often the crown jewels of beast-fighting arenas. Zhuge Liang tries repeatedly to buy or contract the Little Defiant Dragon, but Xiao Chen refuses, wary of binding agreements. The chapter ends with Zhuge Liang reluctantly accepting a verbal agreement, while Keke and the Little Defiant Dragon glare at the obnoxious merchant.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces the concept of beast-fighting (斗兽) as a major cultural and economic pillar of the Eternal Continent. In Chinese xianxia tradition, spirit beasts and divine animals often serve as mounts, guardians, or companions to cultivators, and their trading is a common plot device. However, the formalized industry of arenas (斗兽宫) elevates this to a system of prestige and wealth. The term ‘斗兽宫’ literally means ‘beast-fighting palace,’ suggesting a grandiose, almost imperial scale. The revelation that past Dragon Island openings have allowed young dragons to be brought to the mainland solves a narrative puzzle: how can dragons exist outside the sealed island? It also establishes that the Eternal Continent's powers have long exploited the island's periodic unlocks. The mention of ‘暴龙’ (tyrant dragon) as a fighting beast implies that even suppressed dragons retain immense physical power, enough to rival demigods. This layer of world-building contrasts sharply with the island’s raw, untamed nature, showing that on the mainland, such primal forces are commodified and controlled.

## Reading Guide

This chapter is a breather after the intense Dead City arc, but it plants seeds for future conflicts. Pay close attention to Zhuge Liang’s character—his name is no coincidence; he is shrewd and likely knows far more than he lets on. The Little Defiant Dragon's performance against the yellow-earth dragons and the dogs hints at its latent power, which will draw unwanted attention. The fact that previous island openings allowed dragons to be taken suggests that many powerful factions already have an interest in Dragon Island’s creatures. Xiao Chen’s refusal to sign a contract shows his cautious, independent nature, but the verbal agreement with Zhuge Liang may come back to haunt him. Keke’s reaction to the merchant—annoyed but restrained—foreshadows that she may have her own opinions about the mainland’s business. The chapter also sets up a key tension: will the Little Defiant Dragon thrive in the arena, or will its pride lead it into deadly conflicts? Readers should watch for how the beast-fighting industry connects to the larger political and cultivation hierarchies on the Eternal Continent.
