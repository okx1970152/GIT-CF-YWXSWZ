## Chapter Overview

This chapter marks the explosive return of Xiao Chen to the Nine Provinces after a long absence in the Death World. No longer a skeleton, he appears in the Rain and Mist Tower in Chang'an, instantly establishing his dominance by casually defeating a Perfected Man realm Tiger clan expert. His identity is swiftly deduced by the crowd, and the legendary 'Demon King' title is resurrected. The chapter combines breathtaking displays of power – the suppression of Little Rakshasa, the capture of Hu Xifeng, and the utter annihilation of the Tiger clan's mountain stronghold with twenty-seven War Swords – with a quieter, more ominous investigation into a century-old rumor. The tone shifts from the tense, curious atmosphere of the capital to a cataclysmic display of martial might, then settles into a determined resolution to journey westward. A brief, humorous coda with Jin Sanyi and the Divine Tribe youths provides a light contrast before the impending conflict.

## Key Plot Points

Xiao Chen's entrance into the Rain and Mist Tower is immediately confrontational. He effortlessly tosses a Perfected Man realm Tiger clan warrior down the stairs, drawing the attention of all cultivators present. Recognizing the old man who muttered his identity, Xiao Chen confirms he is the legendary figure from 150 years prior. He then systematically humiliates the younger generation: he suppresses Little Rakshasa's bloodthirsty attacks and forces him to kneel, demonstrating complete superiority over a Heavenly King trained by Cold Hanbing. The real target is Hu Xifeng, the Tiger clan's third-generation leader. Xiao Chen demands answers about a century-old rumor that the Tiger clan used a woman as bait to lure him out. Under pressure, Hu Xifeng attempts to escape using space scrolls, but Xiao Chen effortlessly tears through space to drag him back, crushing his defenses. Admitting the rumor was a trap, Hu Xifeng reveals nothing more. Xiao Chen then delivers an ultimatum: a satisfactory explanation, or the Tiger clan will be destroyed. He follows through with terrifying speed, flying west to the Tiger clan's mountain base. There, he unleashes twenty-seven War Swords, leveling dozens of peaks and pulverizing the ancient castle into dust. The chapter ends with Xiao Chen resolved to travel to the far west, where the Tiger clan has fled with the Sun Church, while a separate scene shows Jin Sanyi, Tianya, and Qier exploring the western lands on Christmas Eve, adding a touch of seasonal humor.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter reinforces the cultivation hierarchy: the Perfected Man (至人) realm is the level of a Heavenly King, such as the Tiger clan's enforcer who is effortlessly defeated. Xiao Chen's ability to tear space and intercept teleportation demonstrates his mastery of the Eight Aspect Worlds and spatial laws, which surpasses ordinary teleportation techniques. The War Swords are legendary weapons first introduced in the Dragon Island arc; wielding twenty-seven of them simultaneously is a staggering display of power, indicating Xiao Chen has fully integrated the Heavenly Stele teachings. The 'Demon King' (大魔王) title reflects his terrifying reputation among the Hundred Races. The Christmas Eve setting in the western lands is a clever metafictional nod, grounding the fantastic narrative in a familiar calendar event, and the banter about 'not making mistakes' is a playful caution to readers.

## Reading Guide

This chapter serves as a major power showcase and a pivot toward a new arc. Xiao Chen's ruthless efficiency confirms that his time in the Death World has only sharpened his edge. His investigation into the woman used as bait is likely connected to Ruoshui or another figure from his past, and this unresolved thread will drive his conflict with the Tiger clan and their allies. The destruction of the Tiger clan's base is a declaration of war, not a final victory – the core of the clan has relocated to the western lands with the Sun Church. This sets up a likely confrontation with both organizations. Meanwhile, the brief appearance of Jin Sanyi, Tianya, and Qier suggests they will be important in the western storyline. The humorous Christmas scene lowers expectations for a direct assault, allowing the tension to simmer. Readers should watch for how Xiao Chen's quest for the truth intersects with the gathering of his old allies and the movements of the half-ancestors, who are all reacting to his reemergence.
