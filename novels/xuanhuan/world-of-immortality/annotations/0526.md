## Chapter Overview

Xiao Chen descends into a bottomless abyss beneath the shattered mountains, drawn by the faint trace of a Heavenly Stele. What begins as a reconnaissance quickly escalates into a gauntlet of horrors: half‑ancestor beasts, a blood‑red bovine colossus that may be the lost Bovine War Ancestor, and swarms of undying dragon‑shaped inscriptions that tear through space. The path forces Xiao Chen to unleash his full arsenal—six Heavenly Steles, thirty War Swords, and the incomplete formation diagram—before he reaches the abyss floor. There, he finds a black, deathly lake and an island crowned by a massive stele, its carved patterns still alive with murderous intent. Around it lie the broken bodies of an Ancestral Dragon and several Ancestral Gods, their blood still gleaming after untold ages. The scene crackles with the desolate, mythic weight of a battlefield where even supreme beings have fallen, and Xiao Chen is left to wonder what power could have wrought such carnage.

## Key Plot Points

- Xiao Chen descends into a deep abyss and is immediately attacked by a half‑ancestor‑level black‑scaled bird, which he kills with his bare hands. The bird dissipates into energy, revealing it was not a living creature but a manifestation of some force.
- A colossal blood‑red bull with a single horn and great wings charges at him. Its body is nearly indestructible; even Xiao Chen's War Ancestor‑level fists cannot break it. He suspects it may be the lost Bovine War Ancestor, now mindless or merely a corpse animated by residual power. He uses six Heavenly Steles to drive it down into the depths.
- A thick swarm of black, dragon‑shaped creatures, each about a foot long with transparent wings, emerges. They can transform into black daggers and their collective attacks are powerful enough to threaten even a true Ancestral God. They shatter the six condensed stele shadows and pierce Xiao Chen's Eight‑Aspect Worlds.
- Xiao Chen is forced to use the thirty War Swords and the incomplete formation diagram, grinding the swarm to powder seven times before they finally retreat. He follows them and discovers they are not beasts but living markings carved into a Heavenly Stele—the stele's own patterns that have taken form. As long as the stele endures, they cannot be permanently destroyed.
- Reaching the abyss floor, Xiao Chen sees a vast black lake of deathly stillness and an island at its center bearing a single enormous stele. On the island lie the corpses of a true Ancestral Dragon, its body severed into six pieces, and the remains of several Ancestral Gods—one crushed into pulp, another reduced to a head. Their blood still glows with divine light. The scene implies a cataclysmic battle that even these supreme beings could not survive.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter introduces a crucial piece of Xianxia lore: the Heavenly Steles are not merely inert monuments. The markings on this new stele (distinct from the six Xiao Chen already possesses) can animate and attack as independent entities. This concept—a sacred inscription taking physical form—echoes themes of the Dao giving birth to myriad things. The undying nature of these creatures, tied to the stele's own permanence, underscores the danger of facing an object that is itself a divine artifact. 

The fallen Bovine War Ancestor (牛祖) is one of the Twelve Zodiac War Ancestors, a group of twelve primordial beings whose combat power rivaled that of true Ancestral Gods. Their origin is tied to the ancient war between the Nine Provinces and the Otherworld. The presence of one here (even if only a corpse or corrupted remnant) suggests that this abyss was a battlefield of the highest order. The Ancestral Dragon corpse—with its skull pierced by a claw and its soul extracted—points to an enemy that could kill a dragon sovereign with terrifying ease. Such details deepen the mythic horror of the setting.

The black lake offers no explanation, but its deathly stillness and the oppressive aura emanating from it hint at a connection to the deepest reaches of the Death World or even the Primordial Celestial Realm. The fact that the stele is used to suppress this location reinforces the classic Xianxia trope of steles as seals against ancient evils.

## Reading Guide

This chapter marks a significant escalation in the power scale Xiao Chen faces. No longer are half-ancestors the peak threat; even they can be torn apart by the forces lurking here. The revelation that this stele is different from the six he carries—and that its patterns can manifest as living killers—sets up a new layer of mystery. Readers should note the line about the stele's markings being "as if alive" and consider how this might relate to the origin of all War Swords and the Great Derivation number (forty‑nine). The depth of the abyss and the presence of such powerful corpses suggest that the true final battleground between the Nine Provinces and the Otherworld may be closer than expected. Keep an eye on the fallen Ancestral Dragon: its ring seal on a horn (mentioned but not elaborated) may become a plot item. The incomplete formation diagram proves its worth again, but Xiao Chen's reliance on it while his personal cultivation still lags behind half-ancestors is a vulnerability waiting to be exploited.
