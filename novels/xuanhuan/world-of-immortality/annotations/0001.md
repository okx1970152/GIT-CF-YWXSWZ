## Chapter Overview
This opening chapter immediately establishes the tragic, desolate tone that defines World of Immortality. It begins with a philosophical meditation on death and the futility of worldly glory, then plunges into the pivotal event that sets the entire narrative in motion: the ascension of Lan Nuo. Against a moonlit Kunlun, a silent crowd of thousands witnesses a miracle, but the true protagonist is not the transcendent deity but the desperate fugitive Xiao Chen, bloodied and unwillingly drawn into the immortal realm. The chapter masterfully contrasts sacred ritual with brutal violence, and the triumphant miracle of void-breaking with the personal grief of a man forced to abandon his family.

## Key Plot Points
The chapter opens with a sweeping reflection on death and immortal legends, invoking figures like Laozi and Bodhidharma to frame the eternal human longing for transcendence. The scene then shifts to the fifteenth night of the lunar month at Kunlun's Red Dust Peak, where over a hundred thousand people have gathered to witness Lan Nuo's third attempt to break the void. After two previous hesitations, she finally severs her mortal ties and steps through a shattered portal. In the climactic moment, Xiao Chen, fleeing a death warrant from the Royal Celestial Maiden Zhao Lin'er, charges up the peak and is unwittingly swept into the void alongside Lan Nuo. Zhao Lin'er also ascends accidentally, setting up a parallel journey. Xiao Chen's internal monologue reveals his profound reluctance: he does not see this as an honor but as a forced exile from everything he loves, a heavy departure filled with grief and longing.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter introduces the foundational concept of "breaking the void" (武破虚空), the apex achievement for mortal cultivators in Chinese xianxia, symbolizing transcendence from the mortal world to a higher realm. The setting of Kunlun Mountains carries deep mythological resonance as a sacred axis mundi in Daoist and folk traditions. The character Lan Nuo embodies the archetype of the celestial maiden who must sever "red dust" – a poetic term for worldly attachments – to gain immortality. The act of "cutting karmic ties with the wisdom sword" (慧剑斩尘缘) is a classic Daoist and Buddhist metaphor for the sacrifice required for enlightenment. The crowd's silent reverence and the moonlit atmosphere evoke the solemnity of a cosmic ritual, emphasizing that ascension is both a personal and public spectacle.

## Reading Guide
As the first chapter of an epic xianxia, this chapter lays the emotional groundwork for Xiao Chen's character. Readers should note his profound reluctance and grief—this is not a hero eager for adventure but a young man torn from his family. This initial vulnerability will shape his future hardness. The presence of Zhao Lin'er, now also in the World of Immortality, promises ongoing conflict. The philosophical questions about death and immortality posed here will echo throughout the novel. Watch for how Xiao Chen's unwillingness to embrace this new world contrasts with the ambitions of other characters, and how his love for the mortal world becomes a source of both pain and strength.
