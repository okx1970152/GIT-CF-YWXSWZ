## Chapter Overview

In this introspective chapter, Xiao Chen stands at a crossroads. After weeks in the teeming political cauldron of Yin Capital, he resolves to leave before his true identity is fully exposed. But before his departure, he chooses to act with unrestrained freedom, setting the stage for a final confrontation with the Tiger clan. The chapter weaves together Xiao Chen's unyielding character, Zhao Lin'er's unexpected rise to power as Sun Saintess, a deep reflection on cultivation philosophy and emotional avoidance, and a mysterious nine-day meditation at a petrified tree linked to Ancestral God Youchao. The tone shifts from tense political maneuvering to intimate soul-searching, culminating in a haunting whisper about the "Heaven-Earth Copper Furnace" that hints at vast, ancient secrets beneath the city.

## Key Plot Points

Zhao Lin'er confronts Xiao Chen in a private garden, urging him to flee Yin Capital to avoid the Tiger clan's demigods. Xiao Chen refuses flatly, unwilling to be coerced. Their exchange reveals Zhao Lin'er's path to becoming the Sun Saintess of the Solar Religion: her pure-blooded celestial horse was revealed as a descendant of the Sun Goddess's own mount, she gained the patronage of the transcendent Lan Nuo and the ancient tree-man who had shed his bark on Dragon Island, and her Eastern origin was leveraged to project racial inclusivity. She boasts that Lan Nuo broke through from Nirvana to Deathless Longevity within a year—a testament to her peerless genius. Xiao Chen, though impressed, remains focused on his own path. Leaving the garden, he contemplates the five great cultivation realms and the transformative opportunity of Nirvana, then returns to his inn where his friend Niu Ren (the Ox King soul host) teases him about his avoidance of romantic attachments. Xiao Chen admits to himself that he has been fleeing emotional intimacy, remembering the pain of loss from his mortal life. Determined to face life fully, he yet finds no one who truly stirs his heart. He then goes to a famous relic: a petrified tree where Ancestral God Youchao was said to have meditated for nine days. Xiao Chen replicates the vigil, sitting motionless for nine days. On the final night, as midnight passes, he hears a faint, disembodied voice: "Forge the Heaven-Earth Copper Furnace…" The cryptic words suggest a cosmic project far beyond mortal understanding.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

The chapter reinforces the five cultivation realms: Mortal-Shedding (蜕凡), Spirit-Treasury (识藏), Sky-Mastery/御空 (also called Demigod), Nirvana (涅槃), and Deathless Longevity (长生). Nirvana is portrayed as a perilous but unprecedented opportunity for rebirth—a second chance to improve one's fundamental talent and root bone. The concept of "heart cultivation" (修心) is also central: Xiao Chen realizes that his refusal to form deep emotional ties stems from the trauma of losing his mortal family, and that true cultivation requires confronting rather than avoiding such bonds. The petrified tree is said to be the remnant of a once-living giant that died and turned to stone, a site where only a handful of cultivators have ever gained insight. The mention of Ancestral God Youchao connects to the overarching mythos of the primordial culture heroes, and his cryptic words about the "Heaven-Earth Copper Furnace" foreshadow a legendary artifact or process tied to the very creation of the world.

## Reading Guide

Xiao Chen's decision to leave Yin Capital is laden with tension—the Tiger clan, the Solar Religion, and the Great Shang court are all closing in. The chapter sets up an imminent clash, though Xiao Chen chooses a last act of defiance and mystery. Pay close attention to the whispered words about the Heaven-Earth Copper Furnace; this is likely a major plot hook tying Youchao's ancient schemes to the present. Also, Xiao Chen's emotional resolution suggests his character may be on the verge of change in how he relates to women like Liu Ruyan and Yan Qingcheng. The coming chapters promise explosive action as Xiao Chen prepares to publicly behead a Tiger clan demigod within ten days—a boast he made earlier and now seems determined to fulfill. Readers should watch for how his new resolve and the mysteries of Yin Capital converge.
