## Chapter Overview

In this pivotal chapter, Xiao Chen follows the mysterious little beast deeper into Treefolk Valley, where he witnesses an astonishing sight—an ancient treefolk undergoing a painful metamorphosis into a humanoid form. The valley's heart holds a pool of life nectar, a legendary substance said to revive the dead. But the little beast has already consumed most of it, leaving only a few crystals. Xiao Chen's awe at the treefolk's transformation is tempered by frustration at the beast's gluttony. The chapter balances wonder and waste, setting up a new dynamic between Xiao Chen and the divine companions he now resolves to keep close.

## Key Plot Points

The chapter opens with Xiao Chen realizing the little beast previously discarded the sacred tree in the swamp, explaining how he easily found it. The beast leads him and the three skeletons into the valley, avoiding treefolk guards. Deep within, they discover a twenty-meter-tall elder treefolk whose chest has split open, revealing a naked young man struggling to break free—a metamorphosis from tree to human. Xiao Chen is stunned by this evolutionary leap. Continuing past the suffering elder, they find a small pool of crystalline liquid: life nectar, nearly drained by the beast and the sacred tree. The little beast casually pulls out the tree and finds three sparkling crystals at the bottom, eating one like candy and tossing another to a skeleton. Xiao Chen is left with a mix of awe and regret, realizing the immense treasure that was squandered. He resolves to bind the beast and the sacred tree to himself, acknowledging his own selfish ambition. The chapter ends with the beast wearing the sacred tree as a hat, utterly indifferent to the cosmic significance of what it has consumed.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes

This chapter introduces two profound concepts: the metamorphosis of treefolk and the nature of life nectar. In Chinese mythological fantasy, ancient trees can awaken consciousness and become treefolk (树人). Here, they take a step further, shedding their wooden shells to be reborn as humanoid forms—an echo of the Daoist principle of transformation toward a higher state. Life nectar is a legendary substance formed from the essence of primeval forests, reputed to bring the dead back to life and regenerate flesh. That Keke can consume it like water and remain unchanged signals its origins far beyond normal spiritual creatures. The sacred tree's ability to absorb half a pool of nectar to produce a single leaf hints at its supreme potential. These revelations deepen the mystery of Dragon Island's hidden ecology.

## Reading Guide

This chapter shifts Xiao Chen's trajectory from pure survival to deliberate acquisition of transcendent assets. Readers should note the elder treefolk's metamorphosis—it may awaken soon and become a major power on the island, possibly grateful or hostile. Keke's casual consumption of the life nectar and crystals suggests its power level is far beyond what has been shown; future chapters may reveal its abilities in crisis. The sacred tree now sports three leaves—a sign that it is growing. The wasted nectar will sting Xiao Chen, but his decision to keep the beast close indicates a growing bond that transcends mere utility. Expect tensions with Zhao Lin'er, who is still hunting him, and the awakening of the treefolk as a potential ally or obstacle.
