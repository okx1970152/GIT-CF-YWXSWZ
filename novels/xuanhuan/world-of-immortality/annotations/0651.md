## Chapter Overview
This chapter opens in mourning. Xiao Chen walks a world that has moved on without him—the Ancestral Dragon Ship, once the last hope of the Nine Provinces, crumbles into the earth. He buries it with a heavy heart, then turns to the living sea only to stumble upon an island of the risen dead. What he finds there is not a threat, but a graveyard of memory: former acquaintances twisted into zombie forms, including Princess Yin Ying, the Peacock King, and the golden ape Gold. The tragedy is not just their deaths, but the hollow echo of what they once were. Xiao Chen’s choice to purify rather than destroy shows a weary mercy. But then the chapter pivots from sorrow to terror—his discovery of the Buddha’s body, a perfect golden vessel, hosts not a sleeping sage but an ancient, megalomaniacal entity that proudly confesses to the slaughter of the Ancestral Dragon Ship’s passengers. With the Five Emperor’s Tower floating over him, Xiao Chen listens as the enemy declares its intent to return. The emotional weight of loss crashes into the cold dread of a new, old threat.

## Key Plot Points
Xiao Chen begins by burying the wasted Ancestral Dragon Ship, then leaves the desolate region to travel across the Eternal Continent. He comes upon an island of the walking dead, where he recognizes the zombie forms of former acquaintances—Princess Yin Ying, the Peacock King, and the golden ape Gold—all now shambling, mindless corpses. He chooses not to destroy their newfound intelligence but purifies the island’s deathly aura, sparing them after cleansing their corruption. This mercy act is bittersweet: it reminds him of all the dead he cannot bring back. Then, venturing into the eastern sea, he detects a powerful presence—the body of the Buddha, a perfect and imperishable golden corpse. Inside that body, a remnant consciousness awakens. This ancient being declares itself the enemy of the Five Emperors, and claims responsibility for the destruction of the Ancestral Dragon Ship, which it used to recover the coordinates of the world. It has now found its path home. Xiao Chen’s Five Emperor Tower shields him from the entity’s overwhelming psychic pressure, but the threat is clear: an ancient evil has fixed its gaze on the world, and it is coming.

## Cultural / Xianxia Notes
This chapter references several deep mythological layers. The Five Emperors Tower (五帝塔) is a legendary stone artifact, said to be one of the supreme treasures of the world, rivaling the Three Sovereign Mirror. Its presence here signifies Xiao Chen’s incredible growth and his stake in primeval conflicts. The ancient enemy that possesses the Buddha’s body is a being from a time before even the Stone Man Kings ruled—an adversary of the Five Emperors themselves. Its claim to have been “exiled into the void” and its subsequent re-discovery of the world’s coordinates revisits the xianxia trope of sealed evils returning. The fate of the Ancestral Dragon Ship is sealed: its passengers, the last hopes of the Nine Provinces, are now confirmed dead at the hands of this ancient foe. The zombie forms of familiar characters like Princess Yin Ying and the Peacock King serve as a grim reminder that even the powerful cannot always escape the cycle of death and corruption. The choice to purify rather than destroy shows Xiao Chen’s compassion even in his grief.

## Reading Guide
This chapter is a turning point—Xiao Chen is finally confronting the scope of his loss, but also being presented with a direct enemy to channel his grief against. The ancient entity in the Buddha’s body is not just a random monster; it is connected to the Five Emperors, the very forces behind some of the world’s most powerful artifacts. Its return is likely to shake the foundations of all realms. Look for Xiao Chen to begin shifting from solitary mourning toward active planning—the Five Emperor Tower is now a major asset, and its connection to the enemy may hold keys to victory. The encounter also raises questions: who else is still alive? Does this enemy have allies? And what other secrets lurks in the depths of the eastern sea? The tone is somber but charged with new purpose. Readers should watch for attempts by Xiao Chen to gather allies or information, and for the first signs of the ancient enemy’s influence spreading.
