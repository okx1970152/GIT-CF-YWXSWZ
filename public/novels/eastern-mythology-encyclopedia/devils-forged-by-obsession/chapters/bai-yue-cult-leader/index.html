Bai Yue Cult Leader

拜月教主

Entry0045 Type魔种包 VolumeDevils Forged by Obsession Updated2026-05-19T18:56:12+08:00

Bai Yue Cult Leader (a Tian Mo driven by the nihilistic denial of true love, whose obsession transformed the world into a laboratory for proving human affection a lie) was not born a monster—he was a man broken by loss, who turned his grief into a cold, calculating war against the very idea of love. His path into Mo was not a surrender to darkness but a deliberate, philosophically rigorous descent into the abyss of nothingness.

拜月教主 / The High Priest of the Moon, Heretic of False Love
堕落之源 / Nihilistic denial of true love and a vengeful urge for annihilation from losing his wife and child
Transformation Era: Late Nan Zhao Kingdom period (approximate, no precise year recorded)
Current Mo Hierarchy: Tian Mo (Heavenly Mo)
Sphere of Influence: The Nan Zhao Kingdom and the entire mortal realm, via his ambition to reset the world through a primordial flood.

The primary site associated with the Bai Yue Cult Leader is the remnant of the Nan Zhao royal temple complex, which he converted into his base of operations. Its underground chambers contain the remains of his alchemical equipment and the sealed containment vessels for his Water Beast. Local folklore marks the area as 'the place where love goes to be tested,' and it is considered an ill-omened zone by nearby villagers. A second site is the river valley where the Water Beast was ultimately sealed—a stretch of water that still, on certain moonless nights, is said to carry an unnatural stillness, as if the river itself is waiting for an experiment to resume.

The Bai Yue Cult Leader's story is intimately connected with several key figures from the legendary chronicle of the Nan Zhao era. His adopted daughter, A-Nu, is the most direct emotional link—she serves as both a living subject in his experiments and the ultimate refutation of his theory. The protagonists Li Xiaoyao and Zhao Ling'er stand as the primary opposition to his worldview, their relationship representing the exact phenomenon he sought to disprove. The Water Beast, a creature of his own creation, is both his most ambitious instrument and the symbol of his final undoing. Within the broader framework of the Mo cosmology, the Bai Yue Cult Leader represents a rare case of a Tian Mo formed not through irrational rage but through hyper-rational despair—a caution against the cold intellect that mistakes proof for truth.

The Bai Yue Cult Leader occupies the rank of Tian Mo, having fully fused his consciousness with a localized strain of Hun Dun Zhuo Qi (Primordial Chaotic Residue) that manifested through his experiments. His transformation took place over several decades, accelerating after he began performing blood-based alchemical rituals on living subjects. At the Tian Mo level, his existence is no longer that of a biological entity but a walking violation of emotional and physical law. The defining characteristic of this stage is that his mere presence, when fully unsealed, destabilizes the emotional and causal fabric of nearby mortal communities—families turn on each other, lovers forget their bonds, and trust dissolves into paranoia. He does not attack the heart; he makes the heart doubt itself.

(1) The cause of his fall was a single, unbearable loss. The Bai Yue Cult Leader was once the Grand Priest of the Nan Zhao court, a respected figure of wisdom and authority. He had a wife and a child. They were murdered by political rivals—not for any crime, but as a message. He found their bodies, and in that moment, something deeper than grief took root. It was the question: if love was real, why did the world allow this? He began to test this question. (2) The critical moment of transformation arrived during his first forced experiment on a living couple. He was not yet a Mo, only a heartbroken man with a dangerous theory. He separated a pair of devoted lovers, sealed each in a separate chamber, and subjected them to extreme emotional pressure to see if their "love" would hold. When one of them broke—betraying the other to save their own skin—he felt not disgust, but a cold, crystalline clarity. His spiritual energy, which had until then flowed in the balanced pathways of a civilized man, reversed direction. It began to cycle backward through his meridians, and each reverse cycle consumed a piece of his capacity for trust, for hope, for the very feeling he was testing. (3) Before his descent, he was a mortal human—a scholar-priest, highly educated in classical texts and astronomical observation. After the reversal, only the scholar's mind remained. The priest's faith, the husband's tenderness, the father's protective instinct—all were burned away as fuel for his experiment.

(1) His obsession is not a singular person or event but a philosophical proposition: "True love does not exist." This is a Zhi Nian Si Jie (Obsession Knot) of the most abstract kind—a fixation not on a person, but on a proof. Every loving glance he witnesses, every sacrifice he hears about, every bond he cannot break with his instruments is an affront to his thesis. He does not hate love; he finds it an intolerable inconsistency in the universe. (2) His sensory world has been warped by this obsession. When he looks at a loving family, he does not see warmth—he sees a data set awaiting disproof. The sound of laughter causes him physical pain, not through malice but through the dissonance it creates with his internal model of reality. The scent of a mother holding her child is, to him, the smell of a lie waiting to be exposed. (3) The drive is irreversible because it is self-reinforcing. Each time he succeeds in breaking a bond, he collects evidence. Each time a bond resists being broken, the frustration strengthens his conviction that love is a stubborn illusion that must be shattered with greater force. The universe is a laboratory. He cannot leave the laboratory; he must finish the experiment.

(1) The Bai Yue Cult Leader's Wu Yun Chi Sheng manifests not as a hunger for blood but as a hunger for emotional data—specifically, the moment a loving bond breaks. He feeds on the anguish of betrayal, the sound of a heart losing its faith, the precise instant when devotion curdles into resentment. (2) The cycle operates on a variable period. After a successful experiment—witnessing the collapse of a claimed "love"—he experiences a brief window of calm, lasting hours to days. Then the emptiness returns, colder than before. The data he has collected, he realizes, is never enough. He needs more cases. More proof. More destruction of the exception that might falsify his theorem. (3) His residual rationality remains fully intact. This is what makes him terrifying: he knows exactly what he is doing. In moments of clarity, he can describe his own hunger with unsettling precision. He is aware that the drive is consuming him. He does not care. The experiment, he believes, is worth the cost of the experimenter.

The Bai Yue Cult Leader has not developed a fully separate Yan Mo-level obsession-entity. His original self and his obsession are not in conflict—they have merged. The scholar who loved his wife and the heretic who rejected love are now one continuous consciousness. This fusion gives him an unnerving calm. There is no internal struggle, no imprisoned original self watching from behind a wall. His transformation was not a hostile takeover by his obsession but a quiet, voluntary surrender of his entire being to a single question. The tragedy is not that he lost himself, but that he gave himself away piece by piece, with full awareness, in the service of an unfalsifiable hypothesis.

(1) The most representative act of his Mo-path was his systematic use of the Nan Zhao court and temple network to conduct large-scale human experiments. He did not merely kill—he dissolved marriages by royal decree, tore children from parents, and subjected prisoners to alchemical transmutations designed to erase their memories of affection. He compiled extensive records of his findings, writing cold, clinical observations of human agony. (2) He was never directly confronted by Heavenly Court forces, as his actions remained within the mortal realm and fell below the threshold that triggers automatic Tian Tiao intervention. However, he was opposed by mortal cultivators from the Shu Mountain sect, who recognized the rising chaos-energy of his work. (3) His most ambitious project was the creation of the Water Beast—a localized manifestation of Hun Dun Zhuo Qi bound to an amphibious vessel. The Water Beast was designed not to attack cities but to trigger a flood of primordial scale, wiping clean the "corrupted" world of emotional entanglements and allowing a new, colder order to emerge from the chaos.

(1) His relationship with the Daoist path is one of inversion. He was never a formal Daoist cultivator, but his role as Grand Priest placed him within a tradition of cosmic observation. His rejection of love is a perversion of Tai Shang Wang Qing—not a transcendence of emotion, but an annihilation of it through intellectual violence. (2) He has no recorded interaction with formal Shen (deity) structures. The local gods of Nan Zhao, if they existed, were either ignored by him or too weak to oppose a mortal acting with deliberate, methodical cruelty. (3) The Buddhist path is the silent counterpoint to his doctrine. His nihilism is the exact inversion of the Buddhist teaching that attachment causes suffering—he concludes that therefore attachment is a lie, whereas Buddhism concludes that attachment must be understood and released, not disproven. (4) He held a cult-like influence over the mortal population of Nan Zhao. Many who served him were not coerced but convinced: convinced that love was a delusion, that the old bonds were chains, and that his flood would bring a kind of freedom. His most tragic relationship was with his adopted daughter, A-Nu, who he raised without love—as a living subject in his experiment.

(1) The Bai Yue Cult Leader was not destroyed by Tian Qian directly. Instead, his plans were thwarted by the very force he denied—genuine love. His adopted daughter A-Nu's loyalty to her biological father figure (the protagonist Li Xiaoyao and Zhao Ling'er's protection of each other) formed a counter-proof to his thesis that he could not refute. In his final confrontation, the protagonists did not defeat him through greater power, but through the stubborn survival of love in the face of his experiment. (2) The nature of Tian Qian, however, would have awaited him had he succeeded. If he had triggered the flood and reset the world, the Dao's self-correction mechanism would have identified him as a universal-level law pollutant and initiated a force that would have erased not only his body and soul but every trace of his data—his notebooks, his experiments, his theories. All would have been wiped from existence as if they had never been. (3) In the cosmic ledger, he remains a cautionary entry: a being who used intellect not to seek truth but to prove a foregone conclusion, and in doing so, turned the mortal realm into a question with only one answer allowed.

Lore Notes

Nan Zhao Kingdom

A historical kingdom in what is now Yunnan, China, functioning as the primary setting for the Bai Yue Cult Leader's activities.

A-Nu

The adopted daughter of the Bai Yue Cult Leader, raised without love as a living test subject for his theory on the falsehood of human affection.

Water Beast

A creature of Hun Dun Zhuo Qi bound to a vessel by the Bai Yue Cult Leader, designed to trigger a primordial flood that would wash away all emotional bonds and reset the world to chaos.

Zhao Ling'er

A key figure in the Nan Zhao legend, her self-sacrificing love for Li Xiaoyao served as a direct refutation of the Cult Leader's thesis.

Li Xiaoyao

The protagonist who opposed the Bai Yue Cult Leader; his bond with Zhao Ling'er represented the phenomenon the Cult Leader spent a lifetime trying to disprove.

FAQ

Was the Bai Yue Cult Leader always evil?

No. He was a respected Grand Priest of the Nan Zhao court who turned to Mo after his wife and child were murdered by political rivals.

Why did he want to flood the world?

He believed human emotions were a lie. The flood, triggered by the Water Beast, was intended to erase all memory of love and reset the world to a cold, order-based state without emotional entanglement.

How was he defeated?

Not by force, but by falsification. His adopted daughter A-Nu and the protagonists' genuine love for each other stood as unshakable counter-proofs to his theory that love does not exist.

Is he a demon in the traditional sense?

No. In the Eastern cosmic system, Mo are not a born species of evil but beings transformed by obsession or contamination. He is a Tian Mo—a being whose existence warps the laws of emotion and causality wherever he extends his influence.

Entry Guide

So. You've never heard of the Bai Yue Cult Leader. Let me set the scene for you. Imagine a man who loved his wife and child so completely that when they were taken from him—murdered by political rivals for a message he never even read—his grief didn't drive him mad. It drove him *cold*. He didn't start screaming. He started thinking. And the thought that consumed him, the one that turned him into a walking catastrophe, was this: *If love is real, why did the universe allow this?* He didn't curse the gods. He didn't seek revenge. He decided to prove—methodically, scientifically, with the rigor of a scholar—that love itself was a lie. That every embrace, every sacrifice, every whispered "I love you" was just a biochemical lie humans told themselves. And he had an entire kingdom's resources to run the experiment.

You might have seen the simplified version. In the game *Chinese Paladin*, he's the final boss—the cult leader in the moon-and-stars robe who summons a giant water monster and wants to drown the world. Easy to write off as just another "evil sorcerer wants to end the world" trope, right? But the story missed the most important part: the logic of his conversion. He's not a demon who was born evil. He's not a power-hungry wizard who cut a deal with dark forces. He's a brilliant, once-decent man who suffered a loss so profound that he decided the whole structure of human emotion was a fraud. In the Eastern cosmic system, that's a specific kind of fall—the obsession-based descent into Mo. Not "I want to be evil," but "I must prove I was right." Let's walk through how that works.

Every Mo has a point of no return. For most, it's a person. For this man, it was an idea. After his wife and child were murdered, he didn't try to resurrect them or take revenge. He asked a harder question: *Was their love ever real in the first place?* That question became his obsession—a Zhi Nian Si Jie, a knot you cannot untie. He began to test it. He started with animals, then moved to criminals, then to anyone he could justify as a "subject." He would separate couples, torture families, watch bonds break. And every time a bond broke, he wrote it down as proof. Every time a bond held, he thought: *The test was not severe enough.* He wasn't becoming cruel for cruelty's sake. He was becoming methodical for the sake of a proof he had already decided was true. That's the most dangerous kind of Mo: the one who believes he's doing science.

Now, the Wu Yun Chi Sheng—the blazing hunger of the corrupted senses. This is where it gets disturbing. His five senses didn't crave blood or flesh. They craved *data*. Specifically, the sound of a heart breaking. He would sit in the shadows of rooms where he'd just ruined a marriage, and he would *listen*. Not for the screams—for the silence after the screams. That silence fed him. It confirmed his thesis. But like any hunger, it grew with feeding. A single ruined marriage would satisfy him for a few hours, maybe a day. Then the emptiness would return, and he'd need a more elaborate experiment. A more dramatic betrayal. A family that had been together for decades, torn apart by a single royal decree. He wasn't a predator in the traditional sense. He was a data vampire. And the data was always, always incomplete.

Here's something unusual about the Bai Yue Cult Leader: he never developed a separate Yan Mo—an Obsession-Entity—living in his skull. Most advanced Mo end up with the original self imprisoned inside, watching helplessly as the obsession takes the wheel. Not him. He merged with his obsession so completely that there was no gap left for conflict. The scholar and the heretic became one person. That's what makes him so calm. When he explains to his victims that love is a lie, he doesn't sound angry or zealous. He sounds like a professor explaining physics to a slow student. He's not possessed. He's convinced. And a convinced man is far harder to shake than a possessed one.

His ultimate plan—the flood—was his way of reaching the Tian Mo level. He didn't just want to kill people; he wanted to *reset the stage*. The Water Beast wasn't a weapon of war; it was an instrument of sterilization. A flood of primordial chaos that would wash away every memory, every bond, every trace of the "lie" called love. That is the mark of a fully realized Tian Mo: you don't just break the local rules. You try to replace them with your own. He would have succeeded, too, if not for one inconvenient variable. He had an adopted daughter, A-Nu. He raised her without love—as a specimen. But she developed love anyway. And when the final confrontation came, it wasn't a sword or a spell that broke his proof. It was a woman who, despite being raised as a data point, chose to protect the people she loved. That choice was a contradiction he could not resolve. It didn't kill him by force. It killed him by falsification.

There's a heavy question at the bottom of this story, and I want you to sit with it for a moment. In the Eastern cosmic order, there are exits for almost every path. Cultivators can ascend. Buddhas can reach nirvana. Ghosts can reincarnate. Even gods who fail can fade into the cycle. But the Mo path has *no exit*. No reincarnation. No redemption. No cycle. Only Tian Qian—total erasure from existence. Why? Because the Mo is not a being that merely opposes the cosmic order; a Mo is a being that *cannot coexist* with the order. Every bond the Bai Yue Cult Leader broke was a wound on the fabric of the world. Every "proof" he collected was a denial of the Dao's most fundamental operation—the interconnection of all things. The cosmic order cannot absorb a failed experiment. It can only delete it. This might be the darkest insight in the myth: that some forms of grief, when processed through enough intellect, can turn a man into something the universe can no longer tolerate. Not because he's evil, but because he has reasoned his way into a corner where he can only survive by destroying the logic of existence itself. That's not a villain. That's a wound that learned to think.
