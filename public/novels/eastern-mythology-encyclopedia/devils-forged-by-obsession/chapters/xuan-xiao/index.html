Xuan Xiao

玄霄

Entry0046 Type魔种包 VolumeDevils Forged by Obsession Updated2026-05-19T18:58:20+08:00

Xuan Xiao (玄霄 / The Fallen Frost of the East) was never born a monster. He was a peerless prodigy of the Qiong Hua Sect, a young man with a heart broad enough to hold both his friends and the world. But the world was not broad enough to hold him. When his own masters, terrified of his power, sealed him alive beneath eternal ice for nineteen years, something inside him froze, then shattered. What emerged was not the man they had betrayed, but a being of pure, crystalline vengeance—a Mo who would burn the heavens to ash just to prove that he had once been innocent.

**玄霄 / Xuan Xiao, The Fallen Frost of the East**
堕落之源：对师门背叛的刻骨怨恨与被囚禁十九年的冤屈 / The burning hatred of betrayal by his sect and the injustice of nineteen years of imprisonment
Transformation Era: The late era of the Qiong Hua Sect's conflict with the demon realm, approximately one century before the events of the Immortal's Tale IV (仙剑奇侠传四).
Current Mo Grade: Tian Mo (天魔)
Domain of Influence: The lower celestial realms near the Qiong Hua Sect's former location; the deepest abyss of the Eastern Sea (东海).

The deepest abyss of the Eastern Sea, under which Xuan Xiao is sealed, is a forbidden zone of absolute cold. No sea creature approaches it; the water around it is stiller than a frozen lake. Cultivators who descend too close report a sensation of spiritual suffocation, as if the hatred in the ice is pressing against their souls. The scar in the sky above the former Qiong Hua Sect's location remains a site of interest, though the mountain itself has fallen.

This entry is deeply connected to the history of the Qiong Hua Sect (琼华派), a now-fallen immortal sect whose pursuit of celestial transcendence led to its destruction. Xuan Xiao's story is also tied to that of his former sworn brother, Yun Tian Qing (云天青), and his friend Su Yu (夙玉), both of whom were caught in the sect's power struggles. The twin swords, Xi He (羲和) and Wang Shu (望舒), are central artifacts in Xuan Xiao's fall and subsequent plan; they represent the yang and yin principles of the sect's forbidden cultivation method. The play also references the Celestial Realm's enforcement of the Tian Di Gang Chang, and the tragic outcome of Xuan Xiao's rebellion is a dark parallel to the stories of other fallen beings in the Mo scroll.

Xuan Xiao's Mo grade is Tian Mo (天魔), a state achieved upon his full emergence from the ice prison. The transformation from a human cultivator to a Tian Mo spanned nineteen years—not a gradual corruption, but a cumulative freeze. For the first few years, he was merely a prisoner, a man waiting for rescue or explanation. By the tenth year, the waiting had curdled into a cold, calculating resolve. By the nineteenth, the last ember of warmth in his heart had been extinguished, and what remained was a being whose very presence warped the local reality of the Eastern Sea ice field. As a Tian Mo, his existence is a violation of the cosmic order: the ice that bound him now follows his will, and the cold that tormented him has become his weapon. He does not need to move to cause destruction; the frost in his soul reaches out to freeze the world.

**Transformation Cause:** Xuan Xiao was a genius of the Qiong Hua Sect (琼华派), possessing an innate talent that the sect had not seen in centuries. He was also a man of deep loyalty—he stood by his friends Yun Tian Qing and Su Yu when they were outcasts, and he took up the Xi He Sword (羲和剑) to fight the demon realm, accepting the burden of its yang fire backlash. That very loyalty and devotion to the sect, however, became the seed of his doom. When the sect elders grew fearful of his power and his volatile state—a side effect of the Xi He Sword's yang fire—they did not attempt to heal him. They judged him a threat. In what they called an act of containment, they struck him down and sealed him beneath a mountain of ice at the bottom of the Eastern Sea's vortex.

**Critical Moment of Transformation:** The moment of reversal was not a single explosion of rage, but a slow, relentless crystallization over nineteen years. The first few months, Xuan Xiao raged against his bonds, calling out to his friends, expecting rescue. The first few years, he reasoned with the darkness, telling himself that his masters would see their error. But the ice is a poor listener. In the long silence, his appeals grew quieter, then stopped. The anger did not vanish—it sank into his marrow, freezing there. The last time he spoke was not a plea, but a whisper: "I understand now. You are all afraid. And you will all pay." The moment his heart accepted that there was no justice to be found from within the ice, his spiritual energy reversed. The pure yang qi of the Xi He Sword, once a tool of righteous destruction, turned cold and dark. He did not descend into Mo—he descended into a perfect, absolute stillness, from which vengeance would emerge as a matter of cosmic inevitability.

**Pre-Mo Identity:** Before his descent, Xuan Xiao was a human cultivator of the Qiong Hua Sect—the most gifted disciple of his generation, a sworn brother to Yun Tian Qing and a close friend to Su Yu. He was known for his straightforward nature, his willingness to protect the weak, and his belief that justice was a simple thing. After the transformation, little of that original self remains. The intelligence and tactical brilliance are intact, but the warmth that once softened his edges is gone entirely. He remembers his friends, but the memory is a cold record—a fact from a life that died in the ice.

**Form of the Obsession:** Xuan Xiao's obsession is not a singular emotion like love or revenge in the conventional sense. It is a complex of two intertwined fixations: (1) the burning, unprocessed hatred of his sect's betrayal, and (2) the desperate, almost cosmic need to prove that his punishment was unjust—that he was innocent, that the world wronged him, and that the world must therefore be corrected through fire. This is not a simple grudge; it is a philosophical wound. He does not just want to kill the elders who imprisoned him. He wants to demonstrate, on a scale that encompasses heaven and earth, that they were wrong to fear him. And the only way to demonstrate that perfectly is to become exactly what they feared.

**Sensory and Cognitive Distortion:** Xuan Xiao's perception of the world is permanently altered by the nineteen-year freeze. He perceives warmth—the warmth of other living beings, of friendly gestures, of affection—as a form of weakness, a flaw to be exploited or a mistake to be corrected. His hearing is attuned to silence; loud, joyful noises feel like an insult to his suffering. Emotionally, he has lost the ability to feel trust. He can simulate it coldly, but he no longer understands why one being would willingly help another without a hidden agenda. The world, to him, has been reduced to a stage of betrayers and betrayed, and he has already chosen his role.

**Irreversibility of the Drive:** The drive is irreversible because it is built into the very structure of his restored power. He fused with the Xi He Sword's yang fire during his imprisonment, but that yang fire has been transformed by the ice into a cold flame—a contradiction that cannot be resolved. To let go of his hatred would be to lose the very energy that sustains him. He cannot return to the Qiong Hua Sect's orthodox path, because that path requires a version of himself that no longer exists. The only direction is forward, into deeper cold and darker flame.

**Sensory Hunger:** In the state of Wu Yun Chi Sheng (五蕴炽盛), Xuan Xiao's hunger is not a biological appetite for blood or flesh, but a metaphysical hunger for vindication. He does not feast on fear—he feasts on the recognition of his power. When an enemy bows before him, when a city crumbles at his approach, when the heavens themselves send their best to stop him and fail, that brief moment of triumph provides a fleeting satisfaction. But it is never enough. Each victory only reminds him that the Qiong Hua elders are still dead and cannot see his glory. Each defeat—and there are few—deepens the cold, and the hunger grows.

**Cycle of Satisfaction and Emptiness:** The satisfaction of a victory lasts perhaps a few hours. In that time, Xuan Xiao experiences a cold, thin pleasure—like a man who has finally found a mirror in which he can see himself as powerful. But the mirror always cracks. The emptiness that follows is not a void of sorrow, but a void of absolute silence, a recurrence of the nineteen-year stillness. It is in that silence that the hatred re-freezes, sharper than before, and he begins planning the next campaign.

**Remnant Sanity:** Xuan Xiao retains full cognitive function. He is lucid, articulate, and capable of long-term strategy. The person who speaks to his allies in the human world is still, in a formal sense, the same mind that once laughed with Yun Tian Qing. But the emotional architecture that connects that mind to the world has been severed. He can remember love, but he cannot feel it. He can recall the concept of mercy, but he no longer understands its purpose. The core of his original self is preserved as a kind of cold archive—a database of memories that no longer inform his decisions.

Xuan Xiao has not reached the stage of Yan Mo (魇魔), where the obsession coalesces into an independent consciousness separate from the original. In his case, the obsession has not become a separate entity—it has *replaced* the original entity. There is no internal war, no original self trapped behind a wall watching a doppelgänger commit atrocities. The original Xuan Xiao who loved his friends and believed in justice is gone, dissolved by nineteen years of cold. What remains is a single, unified being: Xuan Xiao the Tian Mo, whose mind and hatred are one seamless whole. He is not possessed by his obsession; he *is* his obsession.

**Most Defining Act:** Xuan Xiao's most iconic act was his plan to force the Qiong Hua Sect into the Celestial Realm. After escaping the ice, he did not simply slaughter his captors—which would have been a straightforward vengeance. Instead, he seized control of the sect's twin swords—the Xi He and the Wang Shu (望舒剑)—and began channeling their combined energy to lift the entire floating mountain of the Qiong Hua Sect into the heavens, tearing a hole in the boundary between the mortal world and the Celestial Realm. His goal was not mere destruction; it was apotheosis through force. He intended to prove that the heavens were not beyond his reach, and that the judgment of those who had condemned him was worth nothing.

**Conflict with Celestial Authorities:** This plan drew the direct attention of the Celestial Realm (神界). The Heavenly Court dispatched envoys and celestial soldiers to intervene. Xuan Xiao's confrontation with these forces was not a prolonged war—it was a final, desperate battle on the edge of the torn sky. He wielded the frozen yang fire of the Xi He Sword against the divine light of the Celestial Realm's emissaries, fighting with a ferocity born from the knowledge that this was his only chance. He was ultimately defeated, but not broken. The Celestial Realm could not destroy a Tian Mo through ordinary combat; they could only contain him.

**Law Pollution:** Xuan Xiao's prolonged presence near the Qiong Hua Sect's floating mountain caused a localized phenomenon of *Law Pollution* (法则污染). The boundary between the mortal realm and the celestial realm, which the Great Disconnection (绝地天通) had sealed, was reopened by force in that region. Even after his defeat, the scar in the sky remained a forbidden zone where the normal flow of time and space was disrupted for a generation.

**Relation with the Immortal Path (仙道):** Xuan Xiao was a product of the Immortal Path before his fall—a prodigy of the Qiong Hua Sect, which pursued the Dao through the refinement of celestial swords. His relationship with the orthodox Immortal Path is one of betrayal and rejection. He does not view the Dao as worthy of his pursuit; he views it as a system that punishes excellence and rewards mediocrity. He is the living indictment of the Immortal Path's failure to nurture its brightest children.

**Relation with the Divine Path (神道):** The Celestial Realm actively views Xuan Xiao as a threat to cosmic order. His attempt to breach the boundary between realms was a direct violation of the *Tian Di Gang Chang* (天地纲常). The Heavenly Court dispatched forces to neutralize him, and upon his defeat, they did not offer him a path of redemption or reincarnation. They sealed him in the deepest abyss of the Eastern Sea, an eternal prison.

**Relation with Buddhism (佛门):** There is no record of Buddhist attempts to convert or pacify Xuan Xiao. His nature as a Tian Mo driven by crystalline hatred would be fundamentally resistant to the concept of extinguishing attachment—his attachment to his own story of injustice is the very foundation of his existence.

**Relation with the Demon Realm and Mortal Society:** Xuan Xiao never sought to rule the demon realm. His goal was always the vindication of his own life's story, not dominion over others. However, the Qiong Hua Sect's conflict with the demon realm was the context in which he was betrayed, and the demon realm's forces were aware of him. Among mortal cultivators, his name became synonymous with the tragedy of genius betrayed—a cautionary tale told to talented disciples who might become too powerful for their masters' comfort.

**Current State:** Xuan Xiao is currently sealed in the deepest abyss of the Eastern Sea (东海之底), an eternal prison from which there is no escape. The Celestial Realm did not kill him because a Tian Mo of his nature cannot be killed by ordinary divine force—only by *Tian Qian* (天谴), the cosmic obliteration from which nothing returns. The Heavenly Court chose containment over eradication, possibly fearing that even obliteration would leave a scar.

**Nature of the Imminent Cosmic Obliteration:** Xuan Xiao has not yet experienced Tian Qian. His existence continues in the sealed prison, a cold star of hatred and memory frozen at the bottom of the sea. The cosmic order has not yet activated the obliteration protocol, possibly because he has been neutralized and no longer threatens the realms. But should he ever escape again, the Dao's final response would be swift: a pillar of void-light descending not to judge, but to erase.

**Final Position in the Cosmic Order:** Xuan Xiao's ultimate legacy is a scar—a wound in the cosmic order's memory. He is a reminder that the system of *Tai Shang Wang Qing* (太上忘情) has a shadow side: when the demand to release attachments is enforced by the powerful against the brilliant, the result is not enlightenment, but a crystallized being of pure, eternal rage. He has no place in the cycle of reincarnation, no hope of rebirth. He is a permanent, immobile verdict on the failure of those who claimed to be righteous.

Lore Notes

Qiong Hua Sect (琼华派)

A once-renowned immortal cultivation sect that pursued the Dao through the use of twin celestial swords. Its fear of Xuan Xiao's power led to his imprisonment and eventual destruction of the sect.

Xi He Sword (羲和)

The yang-aspect celestial sword that Xuan Xiao wielded. Its pure yang fire was the source of his immense power and, eventually, the fuel for his transformation into a Tian Mo.

Wang Shu Sword (望舒剑)

The yin-aspect celestial sword, paired with the Xi He. Xuan Xiao used both swords in his plan to lift the Qiong Hua Sect into the Celestial Realm.

Nineteen Years of Ice (十九年冰封)

The period of Xuan Xiao's unjust imprisonment at the bottom of the Eastern Sea's vortex, during which his soul was transformed from a hopeful cultivator into a Tian Mo of pure resentment.

Eastern Sea Abyss (东海之底)

The deepest point of the Eastern Sea, where Xuan Xiao is currently sealed. A forbidden zone of absolute cold and spiritual silence.

Immortal's Tale IV (仙剑奇侠传四)

The primary narrative source for Xuan Xiao's story; a classic Chinese RPG that explores themes of betrayal, fate, and the cost of obsession.

FAQ

Is Xuan Xiao a demon in the Western sense?

No. He is a Tian Mo (天魔), a being who became a living violation of cosmic law through extreme suffering, not through an inherent evil nature. He is closer to a fallen titan than a demon.

Why was he imprisoned?

The Qiong Hua Sect elders feared that the yang fire from the Xi He Sword was driving him mad. Instead of helping him, they judged him a potential threat and sealed him under ice for nineteen years.

Can he ever escape his current prison?

The canon suggests he is sealed permanently at the bottom of the Eastern Sea by the Celestial Realm. There is no known path of escape or redemption.

Does he have any allies?

Before his fall, he had close friends in Yun Tian Qing and Su Yu. After emerging from the ice, he allies only with those who serve his goal; he trusts no one.

What is the difference between a Tian Mo like Xuan Xiao and one like Mara?

Mara is a structural Tian Mo born from the cosmic order itself. Xuan Xiao is a made Tian Mo—a human cultivator who was transformed into that state by external injustice and internal obsession.

Entry Guide

Let me tell you about the hardest fall I know in Chinese mythic storytelling. You've heard of fallen heroes—Lucifer, Prometheus, the tragic king who was too good for his kingdom. Xuan Xiao is all of those, and none of them. Imagine the most gifted student in your school—not just academically brilliant, but genuinely good-hearted, the kind of person who would take a punch for a friend who couldn't throw one. Now imagine that the same school, the very teachers who praised him, decided he was too strong to control. They didn't try to help him; they didn't warn him. They waited until his back was turned and buried him alive under a glacier. Not for a month. Not for a year. For nineteen years. And they expected him to come out grateful.

If you've played the Chinese RPG Immortal's Tale IV, you know Xuan Xiao's name. You know him as the final boss, the ice-cold destroyer who nearly tore the sky apart. The simplified version you'll find online calls him a "villain" or a "fallen hero," and that's not wrong, but it's like calling the Pacific Ocean "a bit damp." What those summaries skip is the *why*. In the Western tradition, a hero falls because of a moral choice—pride, lust, ambition. Xuan Xiao's fall is different: it's a mechanical response to an injustice. He didn't choose evil; evil chose to imprison him, and he chose to survive. By the time he emerged, the question of morality had become meaningless. He was no longer asking if he was good or bad. He was asking if he could prove that his existence mattered.

Here's how the transformation actually worked. The obsession path into Mo usually starts with an attachment—love, vengeance, protection. Xuan Xiao's case is unusual because the attachment is not to a person, but to a story: the story of his own innocence. He was wronged. He knows it. The Qiong Hua Sect elders know it. Everyone who reads the history knows it. But knowing is not the same as reversing. In the first years of his imprisonment, he clung to that story—"they will see their mistake, they will let me out, I will forgive them." That attachment kept him sane. But the ice did not freeze only his body; it froze the narrative itself. By the time the ice cracked, the attachment had mutated. The story was no longer "I am innocent, and they will see." It had become "I am innocent, *and they will pay for ever doubting it*." At that moment, the spiritual energy in his meridians reversed. He didn't fall into Mo—he climbed into it, step by frozen step, over nineteen winters.

The Wu Yun Chi Sheng, or "Blazing Skandhas," is usually a condition of ravenous hunger—a Mo whose senses are so distorted that only blood or fear can satisfy them. Xuan Xiao's version is different, and more terrifying. He is not hungry for flesh or terror; he is hungry for *acknowledgment*. Every time he walks into a room and sees the fear in his enemies' eyes, he gets a small dose. Every time he defeats a celestial envoy and watches their divine light dim, he gets another. But the hunger never dies. Because even the most complete victory cannot call back the nineteen years. Even total annihilation of his enemies cannot bring back the boy who once believed in justice. The emptiness he feels after a victory is the same silence he felt under the ice. And that silence is unbearable.

Most Mo at the higher levels experience the Yan Mo stage—an internal split where the obsession births a separate consciousness, a second self that wears the same face. Xuan Xiao never went through this. And that's arguably worse. In his case, the original self—the hopeful, loyal, laughing genius—did not survive to be trapped behind a wall. The ice killed him. What emerged was a unified consciousness, seamless and absolute. There is no civil war inside Xuan Xiao. No good version fighting the bad version for control. There is only one will, cold and complete. He is not two people in one body. He is a man who has been distilled down to a single, perfect note of hatred. And if you have ever been wronged—truly, deeply wronged—you will understand why that is the most terrifying outcome possible.

The ultimate stage of any Mo is the Tian Mo—a being that has fused with Primordial Chaotic Residue until its presence alone warps reality. Xuan Xiao is not a natural Tian Mo in the sense of being born from an ancient shard of chaos. He is a *made* Tian Mo, forced into that state by the severity of his suffering and the purity of his resentment. When he emerged from the ice, the world around him began to respond to him as if it were afraid of his cold. Plants near him did not wither gradually—they froze in a single frame. The air grew silent. His presence was not a scream; it was a deep, cosmic silence that preceded an avalanche. He did not need to gesture to destroy. His hatred was a field that radiated outward, and anything within it began to die. Think of him as a statue carved from the night before the Big Bang. He is not the enemy you fight—he is the weather system that follows you home.

So what is Xuan Xiao's place in the cosmic order? He is the answer to a terrible question: what happens when a good person is locked in a dark room for too long? In Eastern myth, that question is not sentimental—it's a matter of cosmic physics. The Dao demands that attachments be released, that resentment be dissolved, that wrongs be forgiven. But what if the wrong is unforgivable? What if the person who wronged you won't even admit it? Then the Dao has a backup plan: the Mo path. It is not a reward, not a shortcut, not a dark transcendence. It is a fall into a state where the universe itself becomes your enemy. Xuan Xiao will never reincarnate. He will never be rehabilitated. He sits at the bottom of the Eastern Sea, a frozen sun of pure grievance, until the Dao decides to erase him completely. And here is the hardest part: he knows this. He knows the ending. He fought anyway. Because for a man who was buried alive for nineteen years, even a doomed rebellion is better than silence. That is the Mo's final truth: they are not monsters. They are the people the world decided were too heavy to carry, but who refused to stop walking anyway.
